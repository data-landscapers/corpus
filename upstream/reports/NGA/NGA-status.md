# Nigeria: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-06 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/NGA/ledger.csv` (182 systems and instruments, 7 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
In a single year the central bank issued a data-localisation directive, market-concentration caps, an automated anti-money-laundering standard, a real-time FX reporting regime and a three-year payments vision; the presidency issued an executive order on virtual assets; the revenue service issued a tax circular that collects stamp duty in Bitcoin and USDT; and the identity commission got a new statute. Over the same year the grid never reliably exceeded 6 GW for about 230m people and no hyperscaler opened a cloud region.

The National Identification Number now stands at 136m against a 180m target, and the NIMC Act 2026 is being converted into operating agreements ministry by ministry — reaching elections, defence, trade, mining and welfare inside a single fortnight — while the gazetted text of the Act was still not publicly available six weeks after assent.

There were [188m active subscriptions and broadband penetration of 55.67%](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/06/telecom-subscriptions-rise-to-188m-as-broadband-penetration-hits-55-67/) by 2026-04. Population coverage is a separate measure again: [4G LTE reached 84.60% of the population and 5G 13.28% at December 2024](https://ncc.gov.ng/sites/default/files/2025-11/2024-Year-End-Performance-Report.pdf), against a [Spectrum Roadmap target of at least 96% and at least 50% by 2030](https://ncc.gov.ng/sites/default/files/2026-03/Spectrum-Roadmap-for-the-Communications-Sector-2026-to-2030.pdf). 23% of rural communities have internet against 57% urban, and the regulator recorded [19,384 fibre cuts](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/08/nigerias-telcos-hit-by-19000-fibre-cuts-in-eight-months/) in eight months of 2025 and [155,397 in April and May 2026 alone](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/04/nigerias-project-bridge-targets-90000km-fibre-network-by-2030/), more than 54,000 attributed to vandalism. Operators name vandalism, multiple taxation and Right-of-Way as what limits quality. [Deployment of about 3,700 towers](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/08/fg-to-rollout-90000km-fibre-optic-network-tijani/) was announced on 2026-08-04 to start around October 2026.

The Commission has begun publishing [crowdsourced measurement of the networks with Ookla](https://www.ncc.gov.ng/market-data-reports/network-performance-report), which puts 5G use among 5G-capable devices at 31.4% in the Federal Capital Territory and 27.5% in Lagos in February 2026, unchanged since August 2025. [Close to twelve licence-exempt free-space optical links](https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/05/taara-and-liquid-intelligent-technologies-expand-high-speed-wireless-connectivity-across-nigeria/) are reported running across Lagos, needing neither trenching nor a spectrum licence.

The flagship answer is Project BRIDGE: 90,000 km of open-access fibre on a [World Bank-appraised envelope of US$1.6bn](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/04/nigerias-project-bridge-targets-90000km-fibre-network-by-2030/) stated on 2026-08-04, superseding the approximately [US$2bn announced on 2025-05-18](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/05/fg-to-roll-out-2bn-fibre-optic-infrastructure-nationwide-by-q4-2025-tijani/). The special-purpose vehicle was due to incorporate by 2026-07-31 and physical deployment is expected to start in 2027. Against it, [about 101,000 km of fibre has already been built](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/how-nigeria-built-101000km-of-fibre-that-bypasses-130m-nigerians/) and, on the account held, bypasses some 130m Nigerians.

Nigeria has [about 26 data-centre facilities](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/02/26/lagos-is-getting-a-250-million-hyperscale-data-center/), Africa's second-largest market. The grid has [never reliably exceeded 6 GW](https://techmoonshot.com/2026/05/21/africas-data-center-boom/) for about 230m people, roughly 41% availability.

Major operators must report significant incidents to the regulator's response team within four hours. On 2026-08-04 the technology agency [signed two national cloud guidelines and a National Digital Infrastructure Assurance Framework](https://von.gov.ng/nigeria-unveils-sovereign-cloud-regulatory-frameworks/); none of the texts was published that day, so what they require of anyone is unstated.
