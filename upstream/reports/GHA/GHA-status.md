# Ghana: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-06 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/GHA/ledger.csv` (109 systems and instruments, 9 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Ghana has a data-protection statute in force since 2012, an identity credential registering 19.2 million adults and integrated with 262 institutions, a mobile-money market carrying [GH¢492.9bn on 954 million transactions in June 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/mobile-money-transactions-hit-gh%c2%a2492-9bn-in-june/), and a national artificial-intelligence strategy launched in April 2026 at a US$270m headline. The 2026 appropriation carries [GHS 729,947,850 for ICT infrastructure, regulation and capacity building](https://www.mofep.gov.gh/sites/default/files/budget-statements/Appropriation%20%28%20No.%202%29%20Act%2C%202025%20Act%201163_0.pdf). It has no register of licensed digital lenders, no open-data instrument, and no telecom statistics from the regulator later than the third quarter of 2024, and no published text for any of the bills the government has named.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
The exclusive wholesale 5G licence was deleted with effect from 15 July 2026, a shared wholesale network went live in March 2026, and a request for applications now offers eleven spectrum lots on a US$230m aggregate reserve. The regulator's [consolidated response of 31 July 2026](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NCA-Consolidated-Response-5G-RFA-2026.pdf) moves the submission deadline to 27 August, refuses any extension to the 70% population coverage obligation of 6 March 2027 while conceding that obligation can be met in substantial part from existing deployment, and rewrites the dominant operator's rural schedule into fixed dates of 2029, 2032 and 2035, expressly so that buying spectrum does not accelerate a commitment it already carries.

Network quality runs the other way. Operators recorded [more than 8,000 fibre cuts a year](https://www.graphic.com.gh/business/business-news/ghanas-digital-backbone-comes-under-strain.html) as at May 2026, against about 400 a year in the early deployment period. The dominant operator's own half-year accounts show [capital expenditure of GHS 2,100.4m for the six months to 30 June 2026](https://mtn.com.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Scancom-PLC-MTN-Ghana-H1-2026-Results.pdf), down 36.3%, with intensity falling from 29.1% to 14.0%.

The country's biggest facility by committed capacity, at [30 MW designed for expansion to 150 MW](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/investors-pour-into-africas-digital-backbone-as-data-centre-projects-near-900-mw/), is known only from a regional trade round-up with no Ghanaian primary, while an analyst brief written for the EU digital investment facility put the whole commercial market at [about 4.8 MW of live critical IT load across 8 to 10 facilities in 2024](https://cms.d4dhub.eu/assets/Initiatives/Data-Governance-in-Africa/Digital-Investment-Facility/2507_Country-Market-Briefs/Data-Center-Market-Brief-Ghana.pdf). The same brief puts national generation at about 6 GW of which roughly half reaches the end user, and no power arrangement is published for any named Ghanaian site.

The [Cybersecurity Act 2020](https://csa.gov.gh/resources/cybersecurity_Act_2020%28Act_1038%29.pdf) establishes the Cyber Security Authority, provides for designation, registration and compliance audit of critical information infrastructure and licenses cybersecurity service providers, though nothing has been published under those powers; the national response team's [profile](https://csa.gov.gh/cert-gh-rfc) records a 24-hour service for eight sectoral teams and its own policies as under review. Measurement lags the market: the regulator's latest statistical bulletin covers [July to September 2024 and appeared eight months later](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Q3-2024-Statistical-Bulletin-Third-Quarter-Volume-9-Isuue-3.pdf), putting one operator on 75.09% of the market, and its register of authorised providers has [42 of 78 operational](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Q2-2025-Final-LIST-OF-AUTHORISED-ISPS-IN-GHANA-1.pdf).

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Identity moved from a credential to a compulsory verification regime. Amendment regulations came into force on 9 June 2026 making biometric matching against the register the only lawful means of verification and barring photocopying, scanning and retention. Banks, telecoms operators, hospitals, schools, insurers and agencies have [until 2 November 2026](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/banks-telcos-and-hospitals-have-until-november-2-to-stop-taking-ghana-card-photocopies.html) to install readers and integrate, on a sanctions ladder running to suspension of verification access and licence withdrawal. The credential itself cleared a backlog of [more than 640,000 cards by September 2025](https://www.myjoyonline.com/nia-clears-640000-card-backlog-expands-nationwide-and-diaspora-operations/) and now operates in eleven overseas missions. Verification tied to SIM registration is explicitly excluded from that regime, and the biometric SIM re-registration announced for the first quarter of 2026 has still not begun.

Payments are the deepest part of the record and the interoperable share is the finding. Registered wallets rose from 76.4 million to 84.6 million in the year to June 2026 with 26.4 million active at ninety days, and wallet float reached a record GH¢40bn; interoperable transfers ran to [GH¢6.2bn, about 1.3% of total mobile-money value](https://www.myjoyonline.com/mobile-money-transactions-hit-gh%c2%a2492-9bn-in-june/). Almost everything still settles inside a single network. Digital credit acquired a perimeter and then an enforcement phase: the licensing deadline of 30 June 2026 elapsed and the central bank [named twenty unlicensed lending applications on 3 August 2026](https://www.bog.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Notice-No.25-BOG-SEC-GOV-2026-Notice-PUBLICATION-OF-UNLICENSED-DIGITAL-CREDIT-SERVICE-PROVIDERS-ON-THE-BANKS-WEBSITE-SOCIAL-MEDIA-PLATFORMS-AND-OTHER-COMM-CHANNELS-1.pdf), framing the harm first as a violation of customer data privacy. The promised register of licensed providers is not published, so a borrower can check twenty names and cannot check whether the twenty-first is licensed.

Revenue systems are the one area where artificial intelligence is in production. The customs valuation system had [analysed about 366,000 declarations to 17 July 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/publican-ai-system-generates-gh%c2%a2300m-additional-revenue-since-rollout-ato-forson/), 24% of them triggering more than one risk indicator, with collections up 17.5% against a 6.3% rise in declared value — the finance ministry's own figures, unaudited, and contested by importers.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The 2012 Act is unamended, a draft replacement published during 2025 has not been enacted, and the regulator declared 2026 a year of enforcement without publishing a fine, prosecution or registered-controller count. What did arrive is central-bank work: a [cyber and information security directive launched on 26 March 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/ghana-strengthens-financial-sector-cyber-defences-with-new-security-directive-launch/), described as the country's first comprehensive artificial-intelligence governance framework for financial institutions. The enforcement action framed as a data-privacy violation in August 2026 was taken by the central bank, not by the data-protection regulator.

The [Electronic Communications Act 2008](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/NCA-Electronic-Communications-Act-775.pdf) fixes no universal-access levy rate at all: it sources the fund from contributions stipulated in each licence, so the rate cannot be read off the statute, and five of the fund's ten trustees are elected by the industry forum that pays into it.

Parliament's role in the period was fiscal rather than legislative. On 28 July 2026 it [approved a multi-year commitment for three revenue platforms](https://ghanaiantimes.com.gh/parliament-approves-expenditure-for-electronic-revenue-generation-platform/) with published benefit projections and no disclosed cost, duration or termination terms; the opposition [put the cross-border operator's share at up to 3% of collections](https://dailyguidenetwork.com/minority-rejects-passed-3-digital-tax-deals/), about GH¢690m a year.

The SIM re-registration legislative instrument was stated ready to be laid in December 2025 and has not been laid; the identity agency's Act amendment has been before Parliament since August 2025 with nothing on file since.

The strategy was Cabinet-approved in early April 2026 and [launched on 24 April at US$270m](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/ghana-news-president-unveils-270m-national-ai-strategy-250m-computing-centre-20m-implementation-fund-earmarked-to-create-africas-ai-hub.html) — US$250m for a computing centre and US$20m earmarked for implementation — and a [ten-year implementation plan with annual milestones was announced on 29 July 2026](https://thechronicle.com.gh/government-rolls-out-10-year-ai-implementation-roadmap-to-drive-jobs-economic-growth/), built with UN partners. The flagship coders programme, launched in April 2025 against a target of one million trained by 2030, was [embedded permanently in a university curriculum on 29 July 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/yea-partners-ghana-digital-centres-to-train-2000-youth-in-ai-cybersecurity-and-digital-skills/) with 2,500 laptops delivered, and a youth agency signed for 2,000 vendor-certified microdegree places on 14 July — 2,000 of the 3,000 places allocated to the continent, with 600 paid internships. Fifteen months after launch it has published no cumulative number of people trained.

Rural access is financed by an instrument now named for dissolution. The universal-access fund carries the rural telephony project as its top priority, with the last disbursement figure 60.2% as at May 2022; the draft [development fund bill would convert it](https://amchamghana.org/2026/07/30/amcham-ghana-joins-key-stakeholder-talks-on-new-digital-economy-fund/) into a wider digital-economy and innovation fund, with the fate of its rural-access earmark unestablished. No dedicated community-network regime exists, and the regulator told a policy dialogue on 29 July 2026 that [an existing hotspot authorisation could found one](https://www.myjoyonline.com/isoc-ghana-urges-policy-reforms-to-expand-community-networks-and-bridge-rural-digital-divide/) — four such authorisations exist, each capped at three administrative districts, against a [single authorised rural provider that is not operating](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Q2-2025-Final-LIST-OF-AUTHORISED-ISPS-IN-GHANA-1.pdf).

Affordability moved by ministerial pressure rather than by market entry. An unlimited 100 Mbit/s fibre package [fell from GH¢987 to GH¢299 a month in June 2026](https://www.myjoyonline.com/sam-george-announces-major-reduction-in-mtn-fibre-broadband-prices/), a cut extracted from one operator, not a licensing change letting others compete. The demand side has not been measured nationally since 2019, when the regulator recorded [16.8% of households with internet access against 73.8% owning a television](https://nca.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Household-Survey-on-ICT-in-Ghana-Abridged-NRF.pdf) and smartphone ownership at 58.4% urban against 28.1% rural.
