# Zambia: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-06 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/ZMB/ledger.csv` (141 systems and instruments, 55 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Zambia's digital state collects revenue well and identifies people slowly. Over 400 government services are onboarded across the service bus, payment gateway, national portal and payment platform, with more than K8.8 billion collected cumulatively ([Accountant General's office](https://itweb.africa/article/digital-services-strengthen-zambia-finances/JBwEr7n3zk3M6Db2)). Against that, biometric enrolment on the national register stood at about 1.3 million people at the end of 2025, and issuance of the biometric registration card was deferred until after the general election of 13 August 2026 ([ministry account](https://znbc.co.zm/?p=10211)).

The governing instruments moved in one direction only. Enforcement of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 was declared fully operational on 18 July 2026, twenty-six days before that election ([enforcement account](https://africabusinessinsight.com/zambia-begins-enforcing-controversial-cyber-crimes-act-amid-digital-economy-growth/)). The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner has been operational since early 2025 and the base contains no registration count, enforcement decision, fine or breach notification for it at any date.

Only 33.0% of Zambians used the internet in October 2025, unchanged across two annual readings ([compiler analysis](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-zambia)), against government coverage claims of 80% to 94% that state no basis.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Zambia's network figures do not reconcile, and the disagreement is between the state and its own financier. The World Bank appraisal of January 2025 records 10,000 km of fibre ([appraisal document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012025023033676/txt/P505094-630a5ef9-d23a-4221-b7c8-397c94a90b4d.txt)); the Minister of Technology and Science stated in May 2026 that the network had grown from 15,000 km in 2021 to more than 45,000 km, with national coverage at 94% ([ministerial statement](https://techafricanews.com/2026/05/15/zambia-approves-k255-million-to-upgrade-digital-infrastructure-and-network-quality/)). Neither states its counting basis, and the two accounts have not been reconciled.

The same ambiguity attaches to the word connected. In February 2025 the state ICT institute's own provincial officer said site works were complete at twelve Western Province sites but that completion depended on last-mile connections still outstanding; by March 2026 all 116 councils were reported connected and phase one complete ([briefing](https://kalemba.news/local/government-to-track-cdf-transactions-payment-digitally-as-e-council-develops/)). In August 2026 the institute confirmed that four named border posts still have connectivity gaps, with no budget or completion date published ([status update](https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/05/zambia-moves-to-close-border-connectivity-gaps-with-national-network-upgrade/)).

Two builds are real. A government-owned private 4G network for public institutions was launched at Ndola Teaching Hospital on 15 July 2026, with the national electronic health record migrating onto it ([launch account](https://itweb.africa/article/zambia-launches-govlink-private-4g-network/G98YdMLGBZw7X2PD)), and a cross-border fibre interconnector to Mozambique was commissioned on 23 February 2026.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Zambia's revenue systems are the part of its digital public infrastructure that can be measured. Over 400 services are onboarded and more than K8.8 billion has been collected cumulatively across the service bus, payment gateway, national portal and payment platform ([Accountant General's office](https://itweb.africa/article/digital-services-strengthen-zambia-finances/JBwEr7n3zk3M6Db2)); eight of 116 local authorities went live in June 2026 and returned about K21.7 million in their first month ([collection figures](https://zambianbusinesstimes.com/k22-million-collected-from-8-councils-via-electronic-payments/)).

Identity is where the position is weakest and least stable. The base carries three enrolment figures that do not reconcile — 1.5 million in September 2024, about 1.3 million in December 2025, and about 1.5 million of about 20 million in July 2026 — a later source carrying a lower figure than an earlier one with no revision reported by any party ([ministry account](https://znbc.co.zm/?p=10211)). The paper registration card, covering some 94% of adults in 2021, remains the operative credential, and card issuance was deferred until after the August 2026 election against a funded target of 4 million by October 2030 ([appraisal document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012025023033676/txt/P505094-630a5ef9-d23a-4221-b7c8-397c94a90b4d.txt)).

Civil registration is the sharper gap: 14% of births were registered in 2024 and 6% of children held a certificate, against a target of 50% by end-2026 for which no measured mid-point exists.

The exchange layer named at appraisal has not appeared: no source reports it in operation or names an agency connected to it ([appraisal document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012025023033676/txt/P505094-630a5ef9-d23a-4221-b7c8-397c94a90b4d.txt)). What is evidenced runs through the government service bus instead, and the national electronic health record is deployed in approximately 80% of health facilities ([institute account](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/08/smart-zambia-expands-digital-infrastructure-to-reach-every-corner-of-the-country/)).

<!-- narrative: governance -->
Zambia's two cyber statutes of 2025 operate as one regime and the wiki holds only half of it. The Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 is held as gazetted and its investigatory powers are court-gated; the interception mandate a United States embassy alert attributes to the regime belongs to the companion Cyber Security Act No. 3, whose text is not held, so that disagreement cannot be closed from the base.

Enforcement of the Cyber Crimes Act was declared fully operational on 18 July 2026, twenty-six days before the general election of 13 August, with compliance obligations extended to financial institutions, telecommunications operators, digital platforms, mobile money providers and e-commerce businesses ([enforcement account](https://africabusinessinsight.com/zambia-begins-enforcing-controversial-cyber-crimes-act-amid-digital-economy-growth/)). No prosecution, charge or conviction count is published. The Law Association was reported in April 2025 as set to mount a legal challenge; no filing, hearing or ruling appears anywhere in the base in the sixteen months since.

Data protection is the reverse case: an authority that exists and does not appear to act. The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner became operational with a controller registration deadline of 30 April 2025, and the base holds no registration count, enforcement decision, fine, breach notification or annual report for it at any date. Monitoring current to late 2025 records Cabinet planning to repeal the 2021 Data Protection Act; nothing has followed.

Surveillance runs without a visible authorising instrument: research mapping records mass surveillance in Zambia despite no terrorism threat and documents it repurposed against political opposition, while no authorising instrument, oversight body, camera count or retention rule is held ([research mapping](https://www.ids.ac.uk/news/african-nations-spending-1bn-a-year-on-harmful-surveillance-of-citizens/)).

Whether Zambia has a national artificial intelligence strategy is an open question the base cannot settle. The head of the Presidential Delivery Unit said on 17 July 2026 that the strategy was nearing completion and invited stakeholder contributions ([statement](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/17/zambia-eyes-ai-leadership-following-global-robotics-challenge-milestone/)), while a continental review published on 1 July 2026 reads Zambia's national AI strategy alongside six others as an existing text. Zambia leads the continent on one measure: 29% of sampled Zambian startups have at least one female co-founder, the highest in the sample.

Zambia has one domestic digital budget line in the entire wiki. ZMW 255,000,000 was approved for fiscal year 2026 to upgrade digital infrastructure and quality of service, particularly rural; the record is built from press reporting rather than an appropriation document, the stage is unclear, and no funding source is named ([approval account](https://techafricanews.com/2026/05/15/zambia-approves-k255-million-to-upgrade-digital-infrastructure-and-network-quality/)). There is no domestic budget archive for Zambia, where nineteen other countries have one, so no appropriation figure in this report rests on a budget document.

The universal access fund operates without published accounts: no fund size, levy rate, annual disbursement or cumulative tower count is held for any year.

Financial inclusion reached 80.1% of adults in 2025 from 69.4% in 2020, with mobile money use at 76.2% ([survey figures](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/from-rails-to-routine/dgp45vaB8nQ7X9l8)). Internet use did not follow: 33.0% in October 2025, unchanged year on year while user numbers grew with the population ([compiler analysis](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-zambia)). The wiki holds no rural-urban or gender disaggregation of internet use to explain the gap.

External financing carries the programme: a US$100 million grant board-approved on 11 September 2025, of which US$5 million is for digital skills ([appraisal document](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099100625152522349)). Against that, the training the base can evidence is 146 public officers in three districts of one province.
