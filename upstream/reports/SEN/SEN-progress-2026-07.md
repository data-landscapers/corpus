# Senegal: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-06

*Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 102 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 56 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-06, 18 did not, 16 carry no stated baseline, and 12 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 145 sources for this place in the window — 27 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 118 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Civil registration produced the window's clearest movement: deployment of the civil-status software went from [235 of about 400 centres connected in August 2025 to 437 of 629 in July 2026](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/anec-senegal_%C3%A9tat-davancement-au-18-juillet-2026-lanec-activity-7485345706107625472-_yhI) — on a denominator that itself changed — while the [archive position did not move from 20m acts of a 30m universe](https://www.child-identity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PRESENTATIONETATSENEGAL-Aliou-Sall.pdf). The externally funded programme behind it [closed on completion in June 2026](https://aps.sn/le-programme-nekkal-a-permis-dimportantes-avancees-dans-la-modernisation-de-letat-civil-selon-un-responsable/).

The position that worsened is security. [Held state breaches went from one to three](https://www.connectingafrica.com/cybersecurity/senegal-s-free-internet-plan-could-pose-cybersecurity-risks) — the tax authority, the identity directorate and the treasury — with no technical accounting published for any, and the country still has no national response team.

The money moved on paper. The digital-sector appropriation went from [FCFA 16.1bn for FY2024 to FCFA 31.1bn for FY2025](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/senegal-le-ministere-charge-du-numerique-a-un-budget-de-584-milliards-fcfa-en-2025-contre-427-milliards-fcfa-en-2024/) and the ministry vote from FCFA 58.4bn to [FCFA 81.06bn for FY2026](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/0912-51246-senegal-allocates-60-of-2026-digital-ministry-budget-to-investment), but the FY2026 figure is reported as *set* rather than tabled or enacted, and no execution or outturn exists for any year.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
International capacity moved from built to working. The [2Africa landing station went from built-but-not-in-service to in service with sixteen fibre pairs and 180 Tbps](https://techafricanews.com/2026/02/23/sonatel-commissions-2africa-submarine-cable-africas-largest-connectivity-project/), and a [teleport that had held no low-orbit gateway gained sixteen antennas and a Tier III room](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/10/sonatel-expands-gandoul-teleport-with-eutelsat-oneweb-satellite-gateway/). Fixed broadband rose to [1.16m subscribers with more than 640,000 fibre-to-the-home connections](https://techafricanews.com/2026/04/22/sonatel-expands-fiber-and-mobile-data-base-as-ftth-subscribers-top-640000/).

Satellite access went from no authorisation framework and no live service to a [five-year renewable authorisation carrying local-supervision and data-sovereignty clauses](https://aps.sn/starlink-une-autorisation-dexploitation-de-cinq-ans-accordee-au-fournisseur-dacces-a-internet-par-satellite-tutelle/), with the regulator enforcing against unauthorised resale. The state programme built on it slipped: from no free-connectivity programme at all to a [tour distributing a first 200 of 1,000 kits and a one-million-beneficiary target moved from the first half of 2026 to the year's end](https://lesoleil.sn/actualites/technologie/connectivite-universelle-le-senegal-vise-1-million-de-beneficiaires-dinternet-gratuit-dici-fin-2026/), with no beneficiary count published.

Financing appeared where none had been: a [senior facility of up to EUR 270m, EUR 170m committed](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/07/13/ebrd-extends-e270m-financing-to-yas-for-kenya-senegal-network-expansion/), and an [announced US$150m tower-operator intent](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/20/helios-towers-plans-150-million-investment-to-expand-senegals-digital-infrastructure/) with no signed agreement behind it. Domestic hosting did not move at all: the [sovereign-cloud memorandum of October 2024](https://www.ecofinagency.com/telecom/0410-45971-senegal-launches-sovereign-cloud-initiative-with-google), the [Dakar data centre under construction since January 2025](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/paix-data-centres-breaks-ground-on-facility-in-dakar-senegal), the national data centre and the exchange reactivation all hold last year's position.

The one position that regressed is the breach count, against an index score unchanged at [67.17 out of 100 on the 2024 edition](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/senegal-pushes-stronger-cybersecurity-cooperation-to-protect-critical-infrastructure), which predates all three incidents.
