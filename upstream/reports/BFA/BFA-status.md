# Burkina Faso: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/BFA/ledger.csv` (99 systems and instruments, 11 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Burkina Faso's digital state is being built by decree and by executive programme. Two government mini data centres were commissioned in January 2026 at ten times the prior capacity; [120 public platforms hosted outside the country were inventoried](https://www.queenmafa.net/2026/07/26/chantier-zero-donnee-a-lexterieur-120-plateformes-deja-repertoriees/) for the first time on 25 July 2026 with migration begun; and a data-protection law and a merged regulator were voted on 3 August 2026, neither promulgated. Domestic money moved the other way: the six digital budget lines the wiki tracks fell from FCFA 13.56bn in 2025 to [FCFA 9.61bn in 2026](https://budgetouvert.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/loi-de-finances-pour-lexecution-du-budget-de-letat-exercice-2026.pdf), below even the 2024 level. Internet access runs at about [72% urban against 27% rural](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/burkina-faso-issues-600-000-criminal-records-online-but-rural-connectivity-gap-persists).

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Real fourth-generation coverage [fell from 53% to 33% under insecurity](https://burkina24.com/2025/09/30/linclusion-par-le-pylone-comment-le-burkina-faso-veut-gommer-la-fracture-numerique-dici-2027/), on the electronic-communications director-general's statement of September 2025. The white-zone programme stands at service orders signed and equipment on order, with [1,000 localities to be supported in 2026](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/13/burkina-faso-targets-1000-white-zones-in-2026-connectivity-drive/) against nearly 2,000 still uncovered. No regulator or ministry primary states whether the programme counts telecom sites or localities served, or its baseline year.

The [two mini data centres were commissioned on 23 January 2026](https://primature.gov.bf/souverainete-numerique-le-premier-ministre-inaugure-deux-datacenters-2/) as the government cloud's first facilities, to host all public data and repatriate what is held abroad. The Prime Minister's office publishes no figure; the FCFA 16bn cost and the more than 7,000 virtual machines are [trade-press attributions](https://techafricanews.com/2026/01/23/burkina-faso-asserts-digital-sovereignty-with-launch-of-national-mini-data-centres/). A national big data centre is announced for 2028 with no cost, siting or procurement on record, and the [wholly domestic commercial host that launched on 31 July 2026](https://www.aib.media/burkina-souverainete-numerique-ika-cloud-le-premier-hebergeur-100-local-des-donnees/) states no capacity, tier or certification. The localisation rule is carried by a prime-ministerial circular whose date, number and text are not held.

The 2024 information-systems security law acquired part of its machinery: the Council of Ministers [adopted two decrees on 30 July 2026](https://burkina24.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CONSEIL-DES-MINISTRES-N%C2%B025-DU-30-JUILLET-2026.pdf), one classifying critical-infrastructure bodies across energy, water, health, telecommunications and finance and setting their obligations, the other fixing periodic audits and vesting auditor accreditation in the national cyberspace control body. Two further permissions in the same law have no such procedure: [since 9 July 2024 any importation or sale of information-security hardware or software has required a technical approval](https://www.an.bf/storage/libelrapportautres/rXFCav065KRZXiB2PB65xip11Qg8fPpCZ3sjGpsf.pdf), and security products used by critical-infrastructure bodies require homologation, both from a body the law leaves to a decree. There is still no national computer emergency response team — no creating instrument, no named operator and no budget line — and no named instrument for powering rural sites.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
The service layer was built inside the window. A [central services portal launched on 5 May 2026](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/burkina-faso-launches-central-portal-to-streamline-e-services) with 1,672 information sheets, 95 digitised procedures and 183 public institutions; seventeen regional portals followed in June; and pension formalities, official travel, treasury teleprocedures, an estate-agency register, an electronic travel form and a solidarity platform all went live between March and July 2026. The portal's 95 procedures do not reconcile with the ministry's own count of 146 live procedures at end-2025.

Identity moved twice, in opposite directions at once. The regional biometric card has been in mass production since January 2026 and was adopted by a second member state in March; no enrolment or issuance total has ever been published. The foundational electronic identifier went the other way — a failed 2025 tender, then a [decree of 30 July 2026 organising mass registration](https://www.sidwaya.info/burkina-faso-acceleration-de-la-digitalisation-de-lidentite-et-restructuration-de-la-formation-professionnelle/) through committees run entirely by public structures so that fingerprint and iris data stay in-country. Enrolment had not begun as at 5 August 2026, and the decree states no target, budget or supervisory authority.

The newest object is a kind rather than a system. Automated traffic enforcement went live at midnight on 1 August 2026, reading plates against the vehicle database, serving fines by message with a payment link on the national payments rail, and immobilising vehicles for non-payment within a month. It [detected 1,099 offences in its first twelve hours](https://lefaso.net/spip.php?article148326), five of them paid. Neither held account reports a legal basis, retention rule or data-protection authorisation, and the chain went live on the third day of the sitting that voted its would-be supervisor out of existence.

On 3 August 2026 the legislature [voted a data-protection law and an organic law merging the broadcasting regulator with the data-protection commission](https://www.sidwaya.info/surete-aerienne-et-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-lassemblee-legislative-du-peuple-adopte-les-projets-de-lois/). The new law indexes fines to turnover, prohibits processing without consent and prohibits transferring sensitive data abroad. The state news agency [places the merger under the revised Constitution of 20 January 2026](https://www.aib.media/burkina-les-deputes-adoptent-la-loi-portant-creation-de-larcod-issue-de-la-fusion-du-csc-et-de-la-cil/). Neither text is held or promulgated, and the merged authority has no stated commencement, seat or leadership. The merger removes the separation that let one regulator act as a check on the other.

The enforcement decrees under the 2021 law were never issued in the five years it ran, or when its authority was superseded. That authority's 2024 annual report was validated in plenary in June 2025 and was still unpublished as at 25 July 2026, against a statutory duty. The access-to-public-information law and the whistleblower bill are where they were in February 2025, dormant and in preparation.

A decree of 23 July 2026 restricted recourse to the fast-track procurement route to sovereignty, strategic character, threats to public order or service continuity and humanitarian consequences of insecurity — a route the digital ministry has used repeatedly, and one that authorised FCFA 9.11bn for it in April 2026, five times its whole 2026 domestic capital.

The European country programme has ended: the Commission [closed it on 31 December 2024](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/document/download/10d4ba3f-ee66-426e-8c60-bcd89b09fd11_en?filename=ad-mip-2024-c2024-7502-sub-saharan-africa-annex_en.pdf) for want of the conditions for a medium-to-long-term cooperation framework, replacing it with a EUR 238m envelope for actions in countries in complex settings inside the regional programme, shared with three other states. It carried no digital priority area or allocation.

Artificial intelligence went from no national instrument to a validated roadmap and two live deployments in twelve months. The document itself is not held, formal adoption is not established, and the roadmap's own relationship to an action plan for 2026 to 2028 named in September 2025 is unresolved in the record.

Deployment ran ahead of governance. No dedicated artificial-intelligence instrument exists: asked in plenary on 3 August 2026 where the technology sat in the new data-protection law, the justice minister said it was already integrated, notably through the prohibition on transferring sensitive data abroad — a data-residency rule doing service as governance — with the roadmap intended to become dedicated regulation later.

Capacity is being built at the top of the state rather than in the market. About thirty lawmakers were trained in July 2026 and the Prime Minister's office ran two sessions for its own managers. Foreign partnerships have produced no delivery: a Gulf memorandum of September 2025, a United States training initiative announced in January 2026 and a Chinese fibre-and-surveillance project that passed its stated completion date all sit unreported.
