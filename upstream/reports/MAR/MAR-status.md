# Morocco: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/MAR/ledger.csv` (110 systems and instruments, 17 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Morocco has built the systems and not the statute. Fifth-generation mobile service has been commercial since 7 November 2025 on all three networks ([launch account](https://en.hespress.com/125066-what-5g-could-mean-for-moroccos-economy-and-its-people.html)), an electronic identity card is issued at 4.6m a year, a national digital wallet is under construction, and patrol vehicles carrying live facial recognition entered service in Rabat in July 2026 ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)). The governing statute remains law 09-08 of 2009, and the framework bill meant to replace it has been with the general secretariat since November 2025 ([drafting account](https://cadeproject.org/updates/morocco-introduces-digital-x-0-law-to-drive-ai-governance-and-digital-sovereignty)).

The financing is external and the domestic appropriation stops in 2024. A US$250m acceleration programme was approved and signed in June 2026 and is not yet in force ([ministry account](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/07/330823/morocco-to-launch-250-million-world-bank-backed-digital-transformation-program/)); the digital ministry's published budget gives its investment appropriation as MAD 1,758.7m in 2024 and nothing since ([ministry budget](https://www.mmsp.gov.ma/fr/budget-annuel)).

The ombudsman is the one independent measure of whether the digitisation works: 9,958 files opened in 2025, up more than 25% in a year ([report account](https://www.lebrief.ma/administration-mediateur-du-royaume-100161011/)).

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Three operators launched fifth-generation service on the same day, 7 November 2025, on 3.5 GHz and 700 MHz, after a July 2025 tender with one-off fees of MAD 900m and MAD 600m ([tender account](https://www.telecomreviewafrica.com/articles/general-news/12401-morocco-launches-5g-license-tender-to-advance-digital-strategy/)). The regulator reported 3.2m subscribers by March 2026, 8.4% of mobile-internet users. A measurement firm recorded the fifth-generation median download speed down 38% from 261.7 Mbps at launch to about 160-165 Mbps at month six ([measurement account](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026)). Two held accounts of the licence coverage obligation disagree; the specification is not held.

The April 2026 memorandum on the artificial-intelligence platform at Nouaceur gives MAD 12bn over two phases for 36 MW cumulative by end-2027 ([ministry communique](https://techafricanews.com/2026/03/11/ministry-of-digital-transition-and-administration-reform-highlights-ais-role-in-digital-transformation/)) — below the "over 40 MW" the consortium announced for phase 1 alone in June 2025. The last national measurement of the estate is the economic and social council's: 98 MW installed in 2020, of which 14 MW was outsourced ([council avis](https://www.cese.ma/media/2023/10/AvAs71f3-P1.pdf)).

The cloud rules are made and not in force. Decree 2-24-921, in the Bulletin officiel of 20 February 2025, requires vital-importance entities using cloud for sensitive systems or data to use a qualified provider, with level 2 requiring processing, storage and supervision exclusively on national territory and Moroccan majority ownership ([decree text](https://www.anrt.ma/sites/default/files/2025-03/D%C3%A9cret%202.24.921-%20Prestataires%20Cloud-FR.pdf)). By its own terms it enters into force only when the arrêté fixing the qualification standard is published, and neither that nor the list of qualified providers is held.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
The police directorate issued 4.6m electronic identity cards in 2024, ran 80 mobile enrolment units serving more than 130,000 citizens, and operated a trusted third-party verification platform with 17 framework agreements and more than 30 authorised institutions ([annual report account](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2024/12/166797/dgsn-issued-record-4-6-million-digital-ids-slashed-crime-rates-in-2024)). Renewal moved online in May 2026, leaving only biometric capture in person ([renewal account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-digitizes-national-id-renewal-process)).

Eight partnership conventions signed in February 2026 launched a national digital wallet authenticated by the identity card, on a stated six-month build ([launch account](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/1302-52886-morocco-launches-next-generation-e-government-platform-idarati-x-0)). No launch date, service count or user figure has followed.

Law 55.19 has been in force since 28 September 2020, and only procedures published on the idarati.ma portal it created are legally recognised and enforceable on citizen and official alike. More than 2,500 procedures were transcribed in the law's first phase, and the ministry reports a 45% reduction in the documents demanded of users where its simplification method has been applied, on unnamed perimeters ([methodological guide](https://www.mmsp.gov.ma/sites/default/files/publications/GuideMethodologiqueSimplifDigit_10052025.pdf)). An interior ministry circular reported in July 2026 abolished 22 certificates citizens had been required to produce with no legal basis ([circular account](https://en.hespress.com/142557-morocco-abolishes-22-administrative-certificates-to-streamline-public-services.html)).

The electoral organic bill 53.25, tabled in October 2025 with two to five years' imprisonment and a fine of MAD 50,000-100,000 for four AI-enabled offences, was [cleared by the Constitutional Court on 24 December 2025](https://www.chambredesrepresentants.ma/sites/default/files/loi/Projet_loi_53.25_0.pdf), alongside companion organic law 54.25 on political parties; neither has a promulgation dahir on file, and companion law 55.25 on electoral lists is still committee-reported, not adopted. Two bills remain with the legislature or the general secretariat, neither promulgated: a 50-article digital health bill adopted on 22 July 2026 ([bill account](https://medias24.com/2026/07/23/sante-numerique-ce-que-le-projet-de-loi-changer-dans-votre-parcours-de-soins-1728651/)); and the Digital X.0 framework bill, under review since November 2025 ([drafting account](https://cadeproject.org/updates/morocco-introduces-digital-x-0-law-to-drive-ai-governance-and-digital-sovereignty)).

The operative statute is still law 09-08 of 2009, whose text is not held; an analyst writing in The Conversation states that it was [amended in 2023 towards the European data-protection regulation](https://theconversation.com/le-maroc-futur-leader-continental-africain-des-data-centers-et-de-lia-288658), and no amending instrument or supervisory-commission annual report is held. Morocco ratified Council of Europe Convention 108 during 2019; the Council's committee, opining favourably in 2012, named two defects the base holds no record of closing: a carve-out for security and law-enforcement processing, and a definition of sensitive data omitting sexual life and criminal convictions ([committee opinion](https://rm.coe.int/16806fdcb3)).

The commission permitted banks and payment institutions to use it for remote account opening in April 2020 ([banking deliberation](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP_Deliberation-n-D-108-2020_26-03-2020.pdf)) and social-protection institutions for benefit proof of life in July 2020 ([proof-of-life deliberation](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP_Deliberation-n-D-126-EUS-2020_29-07-2020.pdf)), both on prior authorisation. On 21 March 2025 it opened hearings towards a deliberation on video surveillance, treating facial recognition on the public highway as an open question; none is published ([statement](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CNDP-Communique%E2%95%A0%D0%91-Vide%E2%95%A0%D0%91osurveillance-et-protection-des-donne%E2%95%A0%D0%91es-a%E2%95%A0%D0%90-caracte%E2%95%A0%D0%90re-personnel-FR-20250321.pdf)).

A general [case-by-case facial-recognition authorisation regime has applied since December 2020](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CNDP-Dlibration_D-195-EUS-2020-Usage_de_la_reconnaissance_faciale-_20210421.pdf), replacing an earlier moratorium and conditioning future authorisations on a national trusted-third-party identity system; patrol vehicles carrying live facial recognition entered the field in Rabat in the week of 20 July 2026 ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)), but no held account cites a data-protection authorisation reaching them specifically, and law 09-08 does not apply to processing in the interest of national defence and state security.

The national open-data portal runs on a four-volume procedure the digital development agency published to December 2024 ([standards manual](https://www.data.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2025-01/Manuel_Standards_Donn%C3%A9es_Open_Data_1.1.pdf)); its dataset count is not held.
