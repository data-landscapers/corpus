# Zimbabwe: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/ZWE/ledger.csv` (144 systems and instruments, 9 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Zimbabwe has a data protection statute in force and no administrative sanctions under it. The Cyber and Data Protection Act has applied since 2022, with the communications regulator designated as data protection authority ([the Act](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf)); the regulator stated in May 2026 that administrative sanctions are not yet in place, leaving criminal penalties of up to seven years' imprisonment for the accountable chief executive as the only enforcement route ([regulator account](https://businesstimes.co.zw/potraz-plots-crackdown-on-firms/)). Mandatory compliance inspections begin on 1 September 2026 ([notice account](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2026/07/potraz-starts-data-protection-inspections-on-1-september-here-is-what-it-means-for-you/)).

The National AI Strategy 2026-2030, launched on 13 March 2026, provides for an implementation office, a regulatory committee, a data platform, a fusion centre and a sovereign national cloud ([launch account](https://itweb.africa/article/zimbabwe-unveils-five-year-strategy-for-ai-innovation/PmxVEMKELYkvQY85)). All five remain proposals, and a media rights organisation records that the strategy has no legal force ([organisation analysis](https://misa.org/blog/implications-of-zimbabwes-national-ai-strategy-on-freedom-of-expression-privacy-and-access-to-information/)).

The identity system that would underpin any of it has not started. A national digital identity was announced in May 2025 for a July 2025 start and no launch, enrolment or coverage figure is on record ([ministerial account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/southerneye/local/article/200055327/minister-denies-id-backlog-amid-growing-public-outcry)).

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Satellite terminal subscriptions reached 67,057 by the fourth quarter of 2025, up 31.62% on the quarter ([regulator report](https://technomag.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Q4-2025-Abridged-Sector-performance-report-HMed-final-1.pdf)). Two licences were issued to the operator on 6 September 2024, conditional on the regulator clearing every agent and dealer distributing terminals; the widely cited US$575,000 is the trade press's arithmetic on the 2021 fee schedule, not a regulator figure ([regulator statement](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2024/09/potraz-starlink-licence-disruption/)).

The satellite operator was excluded from the regulator's equipped international bandwidth table from the second quarter of 2025 and remained excluded at the fourth, while carrying 35.05% of fixed internet and data traffic ([analysis](https://www.newsday.co.zw/theindependent/business-digest/article/200055624/measured-but-not-counted-how-starlink-broke-potrazs-bandwidth-framework)).

Fifth-generation service reaches 18.9% of the population and 0.0% of the rural population ([regulator report](https://technomag.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Q4-2025-Abridged-Sector-performance-report-HMed-final-1.pdf)). The Harare Internet Exchange Point was built on a US$200,000 African Union Commission grant and housed in the state fixed operator's exchange building; when the regulator reported it to the ITU in October 2020 every Zimbabwean access provider was connected and it had two international peers ([regulator contribution](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Regional-Presence/Africa/Documents/2020/contributions/RI2_POTRAZ-Zimbabwe-IXP_RDFAFR2020.pdf)). The ITU's record of the project to establish a national incident response team, which ran to 28 February 2021, still states that its achievements will be updated on completion ([project record](https://www.itu.int/net4/ITU-D/CDS/projects/display.asp?ProjectNo=9ZIM17008)). The ICT ministry's own 2025 list of priority projects separately names establishment of a national computer incident response team (ZW-CIRT), for monitoring, detecting and responding to cyber threats, alongside a National Data Centre project; neither carries a completion date or operating status ([priority projects](https://www.ictministry.gov.zw/pages/projects)).

None of the commercial storage estate is carrier-neutral: an analyst brief for a European Union facility put about six live facilities at roughly 2 MW of live load, on 2024 estimates ([market brief](https://cms.d4dhub.eu/assets/Initiatives/Data-Governance-in-Africa/Digital-Investment-Facility/2507_Country-Market-Briefs/Data-Center-Market-Brief-Zimbabwe.pdf)). The state fixed operator's own product page states its data centre environment is Tier 3 standard, an undated and uncertified operator claim, vendor- and ICT-provider-neutral, running Microsoft HyperV, VMware, OpenStack and Huawei FusionSphere virtual-machine platforms with active-active redundancy across multiple data centres nationally ([TelOne product page](https://www.telone.co.zw/products/details/data-centre-cloud-services)). A health financing partner reports 1,044 health facilities solarised across all ten provinces, more than 60% of the total, on 11 megawatts of capacity ([case study](https://www.theglobalfund.org/media/qksld2tg/publication_zimbabwe-digital-health_casestudy_en.pdf)).

The central bank reported 511.1 million electronic transactions worth ZiG1.37 trillion in the six months to 30 June 2026, and the same conference heard that of hundreds of thousands of cards in the market only about 20,000 are in use ([conference account](https://bullszimbabwe.com/digital-payments-surge-but-adoption-lags-survey/)). The central bank's own fourth-quarter payment systems report, held for the first time, puts electronic transactions at ZiG736.03bn in the quarter to 31 December 2025, up 7.78% on the third quarter, with mobile money carrying 86.70% of volume but only 10.51% of value against RTGS's 58.29% of value on 1.20% of volume ([RBZ report](https://www.rbz.co.zw/documents/nps/quarterly/2025/NPSD_FOURTH_QUARTER_REPORT_ACTIVITY_DECEMBER_2025.pdf)). The stock exchange and a corporate-capital firm signed a memorandum of understanding in July 2026 to channel growth capital into SMEs through the Zimbabwe Entrepreneurship Exchange (ZEEX), a newly approved venue combining primary-market fundraising, asset tokenisation and secondary-market trading; no capital commitment or listing timetable is stated ([MOU account](https://positiveeyenews.co.zw/2026/07/31/zse-and-lloyd-corporate-capital-partner-to-unlock-growth-capital-for-zimbabwes-smes-through-zeex/)).

The Smart Zimbabwe 2030 master plan, previously known to the base only at second hand, is now held in full: it sets a National Data Centre as a Smart Government outcome and records, as at December 2018, no interoperability between data centres, systems and databases across ministries ([master plan](https://www.ictministry.gov.zw/assets/documents/Smart%20Zimbabwe%202030%20Master%20Plan.pdf)). The Office of the President and Cabinet's E-Government Technology Unit issued Version 2.0 of the government-wide ICT specification standards in May 2025, revising the May 2023 original as the reference standard for all public-sector ICT procurement ([standards](http://zim.gov.zw/index.php/government-documents/category/7-goz-ict-specifications-standards)). ZIMRA is readying a second phase of its Tax and Revenue Management System (TaRMS), integrating third-party stakeholders after a first phase said to have improved data integrity, payment accuracy and taxpayer compliance ([TaRMS account](https://fingaz.co.zw/2026/08/06/zimra-readies-tarms-phase-2-rollout/)). Kwekwe City Council's own account of its finance department states billing runs on the Promun (ERP) package across rates, refuse, sewerage and water charges, with an explicit information-technology and e-governance function ([council page](https://kwekwecity.org.zw/departments/finance/)).

The 2026 monetary policy statement capped cash withdrawal charges at 2% and card terminal charges at 1.5% to a maximum of US$20, and raised transfer limits to ZiG13,000 per transaction with a ZiG50,000 monthly cap ([policy account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200052094/rbz-cuts-limits-bank-charges-expands-mobile-and-zipit-limits)). A named analyst records the governor conceding limited capacity to enforce them ([analysis](https://www.newsday.co.zw/opinion-analysis/article/200055031/zig-is-legal-tender-so-why-cant-you-use-it-online)).

Against about 1.5 million undocumented children and passport backlogs recorded at more than 300,000 in 2022, the home affairs minister told the Senate in May 2026 that he was not aware of any backlog ([Senate account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/southerneye/local/article/200055327/minister-denies-id-backlog-amid-growing-public-outcry)). Civil registration is computerised in all ten provinces and 63 districts but in more than 20 of 209 sub-offices ([registrars' meeting](https://www.zbcnews.co.zw/african-registrars-look-to-replicate-zimbabwes-e-passport-success/)). A mobile exercise issued 3,203,389 registration documents free between February and September 2022 against a target of 2 million ([ministerial statement](https://www.newzimbabwe.com/civil-registry-issues-over-3-million-identity-documents-for-free-ahead-of-july-elections/)).

The national electronic medical record, Impilo, is implemented at 1,254 of a planned 1,900 health facilities, and a health information exchange to connect laboratory systems, logistics management and DHIS2 to it is under development; both are reported by a health financing partner, not the ministry ([case study](https://www.theglobalfund.org/media/qksld2tg/publication_zimbabwe-digital-health_casestudy_en.pdf)).
