# Cote d'Ivoire: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/CIV/ledger.csv` (108 systems and instruments, 10 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Côte d'Ivoire has a personal-data statute in force since 2013, a cyber-security agency operating since February 2025, a national root of trust commissioned in July 2026, and an interoperability platform live with twelve public bodies. The ministry puts telecommunications penetration above 130% of population, mobile coverage at 95%, and about [38,000 km of optical fibre reaching nearly one million homes](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6286/cote-divoire-positions-digital-technology-as-key-driver-of-economic-transformation) (2026-07). It has no artificial-intelligence statute, no published account of the universal-service levy, and no regulator annual report since 2022. The digital ministry's appropriation rose 37% to [FCFA 83.28bn for 2026](https://www.dgbf.ci/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Loi-de-Finances-2026.pdf), while the earmarked operator levies inside it fell and their share of the vote dropped from 65.6% to 42.7%.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Two operators launched geostationary services in January and April 2026, a low-orbit operator [went live by 17 July 2026](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html) on a twelve-month provisional licence, and on 31 July the minister said regional satellite capacity would be routed through the universal-service bodies. Fifth-generation service has missed a second stated launch date: announced for July 2026 in towns above 25,000 inhabitants, with no launch, spectrum award terms or coverage obligation on file.

The emergency phase [connected 500 villages in 2025](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2642807/societe/forum-intech-sante-2026-lansut-presente-sa-feuille-de-route-pour-connecter-les-zones-rurales-et-moderniser-lacces-aux-soins) against a stated 575 localities and 825,675 residents by December, with 300 more announced for 2026; no held source records the shortfall.

The state agency [commissioned the national public key infrastructure](https://www.aip.ci/cote-divoire-aip-lanssi-met-en-service-linfrastructure-a-cles-publiques-nationale/), announced on 23 July 2026, on the legal basis of the 2013 electronic-transactions law and its 2014 and 2016 decrees, giving the country a sovereign root of trust for every certification authority on its territory. Against that, the state agency's [2024 cybercrime report](https://anssi.gouv.ci/documents/90/LE_RAPPORT_2024_SUR_LETAT_DE_LA_CYBERCRIMINALITE_EN_C%C3%94TE_DIVOIRE.pdf) records 12,100 complaints and 338 arrests, and no 2025 edition has been published. No Ivorian data centre publishes a measured electricity load: the only figure held is a design [1.5 MW of information-technology power at the Grand-Bassam carrier-neutral facility](https://www.raxiogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Cote-DIvoire-IVORY-COAST-ESIA-EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.pdf), from a 2022 environmental study.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
The national identification register's own director general [put holders at roughly 11 million](https://digitalmag.ci/identite-numerique-la-cote-divoire-pays-connecte-mais-pas-encore-pleinement-identifie/) in a substantially larger population and named historic duplicate identities inside the register (2026-07-30); the register itself is unchanged. The figure comes from a commentary piece, not an official publication, and carries no as-of date. On-time birth registration, the pipeline feeding the register, [fell to 55.4% in 2024](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2635039/economie/annuaire-statistique-detat-civil-de-lannee-2024-le-taux-denregistrement-des-naissances-dans-les-delais-en-nette-regression) from 56.6% in 2023 and 66.8% in 2021, and no 2025 yearbook is held.

All 39 first-instance tribunals were [connected to the justice platform on 29 June 2026](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2642911/societe/e-justice-les-39-tribunaux-de-premiere-instance-desormais-connectes-au-reseau-numerique-ivoirien), from a two-court pilot in January 2025, with nationality certificates and criminal-record extracts issued nationwide. The interoperability platform is live and populating on a [EUR 387,350 contract for the platform core and five priority services](https://telecom.gouv.ci/new/actualite/146), and the minister told a seminar on 21 to 22 July 2026 that the first services onto it [were chosen largely by volunteering rather than against user priorities](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2643436/economie/gouvernance-numerique-djibril-ouattara-appelle-a-une-meilleure-coordination-pour-accelerer-linteroperabilite), against targets of 100 services online by end-2026 and 700 within three years.

Retail agents have levied [a flat FCFA 100 on every counter deposit or withdrawal since November 2025](https://www.koaci.com/article/2026/07/24/cote-divoire/societe/cote-divoire-taxe-de-100-fcfa-appliquee-sur-les-operations-mobile-money-dans-les-points-de-vente-les-operateurs-complices_198876.html), on top of roughly 1% operator pricing on withdrawals, authorised by no tariff. Regional instant-payment interoperability became compulsory from 30 June 2026, with no account of Ivorian compliance since. The central bank puts the country at [99,649,204 electronic-money accounts opened at 31 December 2024](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2026-03/Rapport%20annuel%20sur%20les%20services%20financiers%20num%C3%A9riques%20dans%20l%27UEMOA%20-%202024.pdf), 40.07% of the union's total, with 26,370,627 active and 34.7% of the union's transaction value.

A working session on 29 July 2026 brought the civil-service ministry's project team together with the information-systems heads of Ivorian banks, through the banking association APBEF-CI, to advance deployment of the e-Administration application, which is to move loan-application supporting documents directly between the ministry and a chosen bank, with paper attestations replaced by electronic-signature-authenticated documents. The reform is scoped to [314,310 civil servants and state agents](https://connectionivoirienne.net/2026/07/31/fonction-publique-vers-la-fin-des-longues-demarches-pour-obtenir-un-pret-bancaire-en-cote-divoire/) and builds on SIGFAE, the existing civil-service management system; no go-live date, cost or contracting party is stated.

The data-protection authority's own communique, dated 3 August 2026, confirms the CERTINUM platform's 2 July 2026 launch and [directs all data controllers, correspondants a la protection and advisory firms to use it for their compliance procedures](https://artci.ci/lartci-invite-les-entreprises-a-privilegier-la-plateforme-certinum/) from that date. The wording invites rather than mandates: no cut-off for paper filings, compliance deadline, fee schedule or provision for requests already filed by the old route is stated.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The 2013 data-protection law is in force and unamended, the telecommunications regulator remains the designated authority, and neither the law's text nor any implementing decree is held. Compliance moved online in July 2026: the regulator's new platform [replaced about a hundred physical files a month](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2642985/economie/ntic/protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-certinum-entre-en-service-pour-accelerer-la-conformite-numerique-en-cote-divoire) with online submission, tracking and traceability for conformity, processing authorisations and video-surveillance authorisations. No post-launch throughput figure has been published.

The regulator's own portal states that it must produce an activity report each year and publish it; [no 2024 edition could be located](https://www.autoritedeprotection.ci/artci/), and the most recent findable anywhere is 2022. The regulation levy that funds the authority was appropriated at FCFA 2.58bn for 2026, down 43.8%, so a shrinking levy has no public account of what it buys.

The 2021 to 2025 cyber-security strategy remains the instrument formally in force; a [draft for 2026 to 2030 was put to stakeholder review on 23 July 2026](https://www.aip.ci/cote-divoire-aip-cybersecurite-lanssi-prepare-une-nouvelle-strategie-nationale-face-a-la-montee-des-menaces-numeriques/), extending scope to artificial intelligence, emerging technologies and cloud, and awaits the Council of Ministers. It is also the only binding-track instrument that puts artificial intelligence inside state scope. A consumer federation [launched a complaints platform on 30 July 2026](https://news.abidjan.net/articles/748737/cote-divoire-lunion-federale-des-consommateurs-lance-my-signal-une-plateforme-numerique-pour-renforcer-la-protection-des-consommateurs) charging FCFA 150 per report.

<!-- narrative: ai-tech -->
Two national strategies were handed to the Prime Minister on 13 March 2025 and neither has a held adoption instrument. The artificial-intelligence strategy is costed at FCFA 912bn over five years; no AI-specific line is identifiable in the 2025 or 2026 finance laws the wiki holds, and the strategy text is not held. Three institutions the strategy provides for — a safe-AI label, a national committee, and a national agency the ministry said in July 2025 was being established — have produced no criteria, membership, decree, budget or staffing in the year and more since. The coordination role has meanwhile been taken up in practice by the cyber-security agency through its draft strategy.

A government assistant went live in March 2026 on messaging platforms and a toll-free line, with no usage, cost, vendor or model published. The United States announced on 16 July 2026 that a contractor had received [formal authorisation for a US$170m export-credit-financed project](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/17/us-expands-cote-divoire-tech-footprint-with-starlink-approval-and-170m-data-centre-project/) covering a sovereign national data centre, a government digitisation platform and a smart-border surveillance system — announced by the United States rather than by any Ivorian instrument, and the figures are US-side approvals rather than Ivorian disbursements. The ministry [launched two accelerator cohorts and a state procurement platform on 29 July 2026](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/la-cote-divoire-lance-deux-accelerateurs-pour-faire-emerger-ses-champions-du-numerique) against a target of FCFA 5 to 10bn mobilised from banks, funds and donors. The FCFA 100bn innovation fund announced in July 2025 has no capitalisation, manager, governance or first close thirteen months on.
