# Benin: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/BEN/ledger.csv` (74 systems and instruments, 4 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
One presidency-attached agency owns identity, the population register, the civil registry and the electoral register as a single integrated system, and its director general is also National Civil Registrar. [Biometric enrolment stands at about 98–99%](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/05/20/transforming-lives-in-benin-a-unique-identification-system). A national data exchange instituted by decree in 2020 has carried [more than 138 million requests](https://statistiques.xroad.bj/), and its published conditions of access make the data-protection authority a step in the technical onboarding path rather than a complaint channel after the fact. That authority [publishes its caseload](https://www.lanation.bj/numerique/protection-des-donnees-personnelles-en-2025-au-benin-lapdp-dresse-un-bilan-sous-le-signe-de-la-conformite), holds a dedicated AI policy, and has made the electoral agency strip fields from the roll before publication.

Against that, the first per-programme execution figures Benin has ever published show the national digital programme at [**0.62% of its credits ordonnancé at 30 April 2026**](https://budgetbenin.bj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Note-de-presentation-du-PLFR-2026.pdf), against 15.43% across all programmes. The ministry's own steering programme ran at 20.12%, so this is specific to the digital line rather than ministry-wide.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
A [second 5G operator](https://techafricanews.com/2026/04/28/benin-enters-the-5g-era-as-moov-africa-benin-launches-high-speed-network-at-novotel-orisha-cotonou/) launched in April 2026 alongside the incumbent's 2024 network; data prices fell from 4,000 to 995 FCFA a gigabyte between 2016 and 2023; more than 3,000 km of fibre underpins the service estate; and free roaming with Senegal and Togo began on 24 April 2026 under an ECOWAS regulation, with free incoming calls for thirty days. Regional financing arrived in March 2026: US$137m for Benin, Liberia and Sierra Leone, aimed at broadband for more than 5m people and digital public services for more than 5m more.

Benin has run a national digital-security strategy since 2020, and in June 2025 published a [first national vulnerability report](https://techafricanews.com/2025/07/03/benin-releases-first-national-cybersecurity-report-highlighting-rising-threats): 878 vulnerabilities over 2021–2024, 23% of them critical, and institutional compliance ranging from 6% to 87%.

The agency reports more than 230 services delivered online and about 40 priority structures under tailored security oversight. An expert roadmap to 2033 recommends a sovereign cloud and a national cyber authority — neither of which exists.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
About 98–99% biometric coverage sits under a register connecting more than 80 institutions at about 200,000 authentications a day, with [at least 75% of identification services accessed digitally](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202602/benin-reports-75-of-identification-services-accessed-through-digital-platforms) in 2025. But [between 2021 and 2025 about 2.5m births were declared against under 1m actually registered](https://www.lanation.bj/actualites/aristide-adjinacou-directeur-general-de-lanip-nous-voulons-faire-de-chaque-maternite-une-veritable-porte-dentree-vers-lidentite-legale). A single-window pilot at ten maternities lifted registration to 76.56% against about 22% nationally, and generalisation slipped three to four months.

The register is also the electoral roll. The agency compiled and managed the list for the 12 April 2026 presidential poll. A civil-society organisation issued more than 130,000 free cards across nine communes, which showed the gap between the older enrolment campaign and card issuance — failed fingerprints and dead enrolment numbers.

The exchange was instituted by decree in 2020 on an X-Road-lineage platform and now reports more than 138 million requests. In July 2026 its operator [published what a body must satisfy before connecting](https://lanation.bj/numerique/interoperabilite-au-benin-les-regles-dacces-a-xroad-bj) — for a private body, data-protection authority authorisation before first connection.

Payments run on the regional rail rather than a national one. Banks and mobile money were fully interconnected by May 2026, and [mobile-money penetration reached 88.09% on 11.65m active accounts](https://www.lanation.bj/numerique/communications-electroniques-lessor-des-usages-numeriques-transforme-le-marche-beninois) at end-2025.

On regional border cooperation, the customs administrations of Nigeria, Cameroon and Benin [adopted a Joint Communiqué on 27 July 2026](https://theinsight.com.ng/nigeria-customs-benin-cameroon-deepen-regional-border-modernisation-at-beitbridge-benchmarking-mission/), following a benchmarking mission to the Beitbridge and Chirundu border posts, committing to a Trilateral Strategic Steering Committee to carry the Beitbridge modernisation and concession model onto the Sèmè-Kraké and Mfum-Ekok corridors.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The data-protection authority was created by the Code du numérique, publishes a numbered annual report, holds a dedicated AI policy with strict purpose limitation, and has required the electoral agency to strip extraneous fields from the roll before publication. It names its own gaps as the absence of algorithmic-audit tooling and budget.

Policy is being rewritten around a new government: on 27 May the digital portfolio changed hands and the ministry was renamed Transformation Digitale et Innovation with an explicit AI-strategy mandate. A decree moved the budget programme codes at the same time, so any Benin budget series keyed on ministry breaks at 24 May 2026.

The incoming programme commits, in its own words, to a data-localisation law, national supercomputers and a state platform monetising locally produced data — and counts a government AI super app among the foundations it already has. Nothing held establishes that the super app exists.

On openness, an [Open Budget Survey 2025 score of 77/100 on transparency](https://fraternite.bj/budget-ouvert-obs-2025-le-benin-se-hisse-parmi-les-references-mondiales-de-la-transparence-budgetaire/), fifth globally, from 1/100 in 2012 — while the annual performance reports its own organic budget law requires have never been published for any year.

<!-- narrative: ai-tech -->
The 2023–2027 national strategy took the country from 145th globally to the sub-Saharan top five on an AI-readiness index in three years.

In June 2026 the digital agency published a [framework for generative AI in the public service](https://asin.bj/doc/137/download) that starts from an admission — public agents already use these tools on personal accounts outside any framework — and orders a census of those users, a binding charter, and contracts forbidding any use of administration data to train models. The framework also states that contractual terms do not close the sovereignty risk, because the model, the infrastructure and the jurisdiction remain another party's.

Two capability programmes are on record, in language and in health. The first voice-to-voice AI in Fon was built for mother-tongue service access — and an [AI, drone and geospatial malaria programme](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/benin-combines-ai-drones-and-geospatial-mapping-in-fight-against-malaria) costing US$2.3m reports incidence down 38% and mortality down 39% across 2022–2024.

The domestic commercial layer is early: a content-verification platform founded in 2025, and a strategy co-author arguing in print for frugal domestic AI against a GPU-scarcity route to AI becoming a luxury good.
