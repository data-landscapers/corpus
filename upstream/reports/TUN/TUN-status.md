# Tunisia: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/TUN/ledger.csv` (61 systems and instruments, 9 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Tunisia's digital state is more built than it is governed. The mobile identity became [compulsory for investment declarations and for the business register from 1 July 2026](https://techafricanews.com/2026/06/26/tunisia-makes-mobile-id-mandatory-for-investment-project-declarations/), a national health identifier [repurposed from the Covid-19 vaccination identifier](https://www.assabahnews.tn/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9/151109) now joins health, civil-status, social-affairs and education records, and the interoperability platform is [extending from five ministries to the business economy](https://cyber.ee/resources/news/phase-2-tunisia-interoperability/). The instrument governing all of that data is still the [2004 organic law](https://en.africanmanager.com/personal-data-protection-tunisian-parliament-moves-to-curb-ai-and-algorithm-risks/); its 123-article replacement is in committee.

Connectivity is the strongest leg: three operators on 5G since February 2025, and the [Medusa cable's Bizerte landing in service from 3 June 2026](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/medusa-s-viatunisa-subsea-cable-now-live).

The state has declared two things it has not done. It has [no adopted artificial-intelligence strategy](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/08/05/intelligence-artificielle-latuge-devoile-son-livre-blanc-pour-la-tunisie/), and [no published allocation for the digital transition](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/08/04/sara-zaafrani-zenzri-devoile-les-grandes-orientations-du-projet-de-loi-de-finances-2027/) in the budget that names it as a structuring axis.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
The Mediterranean position is now a working asset. The [Medusa system's first African landing at Bizerte](https://medusascs.com/news/medusa-submarine-cable-system-lands-in-bizerte/) went into service on [3 June 2026](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/medusa-s-viatunisa-subsea-cable-now-live), with Orange Tunisia owning and operating the landing station and the roughly 1,050 km Tunisian segment, and Tunisie Telecom [buying a dedicated 20 Tbps pair to Marseille](https://medusascs.com/news/tunisie_telecom_joins_medusa/) on it. Both operators' publicity claimed the landing; the distinction is between owning international capacity and renting it.

Mobile is broad in coverage and contested in quality. All three operators have run 5G since February 2025, and the regulator's obligations — a 30 Mbps floor on fixed-wireless access with a 50% contractual-speed guarantee — [took fixed-wireless lines from 9,425 at launch to 319,554 by May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026). The same measurement, cited not absorbed, finds the Tunisian 5G median down 30% over six months and the combined 4G and 5G median below the pre-launch baseline.

Hosting is entirely prospective. Data centres are [a named project line in the statutory development plan](https://www.arp.tn/loi/project/4315) with no budget, sequencing or delivery agency, and the flagship is a [20 MW facility at Bizerte under a non-binding memorandum](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/solecrypt-plans-20mw-solar-powered-data-center-in-bizerte-tunisia/), to be powered from a 60 MW solar site whose base camp opened in November 2025. The health ministry separately [intends to host national health data on Tunisian cloud](https://iafrica.com/tunisia-advances-digital-health-reform-with-ai-and-national-cloud-strategy/), with no provider, amount or date. None of it is operating capacity.

The security posture on record is advisory. The national agency's response to a [live campaign harvesting banking data through fake traffic-fine messages](https://www.assabahnews.tn/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9/151319-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B0%D8%B1-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84-%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%AF%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%B7%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%86%D9%83%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86) was advice and a verification test: government sites use only the national domain. No takedown power or enforcement action appears in the base.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
From 1 July 2026 the mobile identity is no longer optional. Live since August 2022 and legally equivalent to face-to-face identification, it [stood at 200,000 subscribers as of March 2025](https://idtechwire.com/e-houwiya-a-milestone-in-tunisias-digital-transformation-roadmap-2025) with no obligation to hold one; from 1 July both the [investment authority](https://techafricanews.com/2026/06/26/tunisia-makes-mobile-id-mandatory-for-investment-project-declarations/) and the [business register](https://en.africanmanager.com/tunisia-national-business-register-accelerates-digitalization-of-its-services/) made it a condition of access, and no subscriber figure has been published since.

The [national health identifier is the Covid-19 vaccination identifier, repurposed](https://www.assabahnews.tn/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9/151109) and bound to the identity card, the social security number and school identifiers — a join key across four administrations. Coverage cannot be stated: [over 15,000 numbers issued as at 22 June 2026](https://tmo-mag.com.tn/tunisia-finalizes-sahetna-tn-digital-health-portal-as-national-id-rollout-gains-steam/), [over 9,000 patients registered in one region's pilot as at 13 July](https://tmo-mag.com.tn/tunisias-digital-health-revolution-over-9000-patients-registered-in-national-id-pilot-program/), roughly 100,000 medical files linked by 27 July — three figures at three scopes, with no national count or dashboard published against the ministry's end-2026 pledge.

Registers are digitalising fast and documenting themselves thinly. The [business register discontinued paper filing](https://en.africanmanager.com/tunisia-national-business-register-accelerates-digitalization-of-its-services/), the [community-companies register went live in July 2026](https://www.nessma.tv/fr/economie/actu/lancement-du-registre-national-des-societes-communautaires-pour-impulser-le-developpement-local/560812) with only 60 of 230 companies created found operating, and [business tax registration moved online in August 2026](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/08/04/patente-en-ligne-la-direction-generale-des-impots-lance-sa-nouvelle-plateforme-numerique/); none names an instrument, retention rule or supervisory authority for the data held. The [AMEN social registry](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2024/11/14/tunisians-share-how-their-life-changed-through-the-amen-program) runs the other way: over 333,000 households on cash transfers as of December 2023, not updated since.

The [national TUNPAY label](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/tunisia-launches-tunpay-label-to-accelerate-mobile-payment-adoption) obliges banks, payment institutions and the postal service to carry one identity across wallets and acceptance points, and the market is widening through a telco wallet and a [Gulf-backed fintech licence](https://menastartupdigest.com/ooredoo-fintech-secures-license-to-launch-digital-wallet-in-tunisia/). Volume is real — [8.4m mobile transactions worth TND 1,769m in 2025, up 81%](https://trendsnafrica.com/central-bank-of-tunisia-reports-81-surge-in-mobile-payments-as-tunisia-accelerates-its-digital-transformation-in-2025/) — but only through an aggregator, not the central bank's own bulletin.

A second e-invoicing effort is advancing where the legislated one has stalled: the state wholesale-markets company piloted it at [Moknine](https://www.tunisie-tribune.com/2026/08/03/la-sotumag-poursuit-la-mise-en-oeuvre-du-programme-national-de-generalisation-du-systeme-de-facturation-electronique/) and launches at Teboulba on 15 August 2026, with no stated link to the finance ministry's amendment.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The governing data-protection instrument is [the organic law of 2004](https://en.africanmanager.com/personal-data-protection-tunisian-parliament-moves-to-curb-ai-and-algorithm-risks/), which civil society [describes as unfit for the processing the state now does](https://paradigmhq.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Tunisia-Country-Report.pdf). Its replacement — 123 articles across six titles — was under committee review in February 2026 and has not passed. Its sponsors name the gap: the limited role of the supervisory authority, and the absence of rules for the press, surveillance cameras, algorithms and cross-border transfers. The bill would introduce a data protection officer regime, require public institutions to obtain authorisation for any processing, and move enforcement to financial penalties.

The authority that bill would strengthen is named in none of the year's new data-concentrating systems, and no enforcement action or annual report of its own is held.

The [amendment to the compulsory e-invoicing article](https://www.assabahnews.tn/ar/%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%88-%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%84/139940-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%85%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%AD%D8%A7-%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%AD-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9) records that no unified national e-invoicing system exists and that mass e-invoicing raises serious data-protection issues, and mandates a 30-day ministerial report on readiness before any phased timeline.

[Digital Tunisia 2020-2025 lapsed at term](https://pm.gov.tn/fr/decision-gouvernementale/zenzri-lors-dun-conseil-ministeriel-2026-annee-du-demarrage-effectif-de-la) with no extension, repeal or outcomes report held, and its successor is a chapter of the [development plan promulgated on 20 July 2026](https://www.9anoun.tn/fr/kb/jorts/jort-2026-073-5e980/loi-ndeg-2026-16-du-20-juillet-2026-portant-approbation-du-plan-de-developpement-2026-2030-4). The [annex is substantial](https://www.arp.tn/loi/project/4315) — its sectoral volume mentions digital or digitalisation 414 times and artificial intelligence 119 — but every volume held is marked draft, and whether the promulgated annex differs from the version voted is not established.

The [Open Government Partnership](https://www.opengovpartnership.org/documents/tunisia-results-report-2023-2025/)'s independent reviewers found the E-Government Unit delivered a working national public data registry, while a formal National Open Data Strategy still awaits government endorsement.

Tunisia has an artificial-intelligence roadmap and no strategy, and the roadmap was not written by the state. <!-- narrative: finance -->
Tunisia's digital financing is European, and it is two instruments routinely read as one. The [EBRD loan of up to EUR 190m to the state operator](https://www.ebrd.com/home/news-and-events/news/2025/eur-190-million-to-tunisie-telecom.html), announced in January 2026 after board approval in July 2024, is a corporate modernisation programme — a 4G-to-5G upgrade, fibre to 200,000 households, backbone and core work, energy investment, and the operator's connection to the submarine cable. The [landing station itself was built under a separate EUR 9.6m grant](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/viatunisia) inside a EUR 32m project, to a different company, on a different timetable, closing in August 2025. Neither is a cable-financing facility, and neither is the other.

Against that external money there is no domestic figure. The [2027 draft budget names the digital transition as a structuring axis](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/08/04/sara-zaafrani-zenzri-devoile-les-grandes-orientations-du-projet-de-loi-de-finances-2027/) and attaches no allocation, target or programme to it.
