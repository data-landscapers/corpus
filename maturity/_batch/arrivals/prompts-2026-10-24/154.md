### Study interoperabilitylaw

sub_indicator: "law" (class "law")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "standing": "in-force", "draft", "none"
- aspect "duty": "applied", "required", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**The law, decree or regulation that says public bodies may or must share the data they hold with each other**, whatever it is called: a data sharing or data exchange act, an e-government or digital government law, an interoperability decree or regulation, or the part of a digital code or an administrative simplification law that does that job.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Data sharing law | Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law | `gov.legislate--legislation-enabling-data-interoperability` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a data exchange platform itself, an interoperability framework or standard issued as guidance with no legal force, a law for one sector only (health, land, tax), a data protection law, an access to information law, an open data policy, a national identity law, an electronic transactions law that says nothing of public bodies exchanging data, an agreement between two agencies, a digital strategy, a treaty.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there a law or decree on public bodies sharing data with each other, or a draft of one? | A bill, draft decree or draft regulation published, before parliament or cabinet, or passed, or a law, decree or regulation made |
| 2 | Is it in force? | A law promulgated and in force, or a decree or regulation issued and binding, standing alone or as part of a larger law or code. A bill or draft is not a yes |
| 3 | Does it require public bodies to share data, and not only allow it? | A source says the law in force obliges public bodies to exchange data, to connect to the government's exchange platform, or not to ask a person for what another public body already holds |
| 4 | Has it been applied in the last three years? | A dated regulation, decree or order made under it, or a dated source naming public bodies that exchange data because it requires them to |
#### 3. The ladders

**Data sharing law**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no law or decree on public bodies sharing data and no draft of one |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a bill or draft, and nothing in force |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a law, decree or regulation in force |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: it requires public bodies to share |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: it has been applied in the last three years |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a regulation made under a law shows the law is in force.
- **Questions 1 to 3 have no time limit**; a law stands, with its year, until a dated source says it is repealed, replaced or struck down.
- **A statement that there is no law counts for three years.**
- **A decree or regulation counts as well as an act of parliament.**
- **A law for one sector places nothing**, and is noted. This is one change from the row as it stands, which places Kenya at stage 3 on its Digital Health Act; Kenya holds no fact.
- **An e-government or digital government law counts only where a source says it provides for public bodies exchanging data**, or its own text plainly does. One that only sets up an agency or allows electronic documents is noted.
- **An interoperability framework counts only where it is issued as a decree or regulation.** One adopted as a policy or a standard is the interoperability framework row's, and is noted here.
- **A decree that sets up the exchange platform and binds public bodies to use it is a yes to questions 2 and 3 together.**
- **A platform alone places nothing**, however many bodies are connected to it. Bodies connected count for question 4 only where the source ties them to the law.
- **A law passed but not yet in effect is a yes to question 2**, and the summary gives the date it starts.
- **An old law still in force is a yes to question 2 and no more**, until a source says it requires sharing.
- **Whether the law requires sharing is taken from what a source says of it, or from what its own text plainly provides**, where the text held is short.
- **Two more changes from the row as it stands**: its stage 4 asks for implementing acts, register-reuse rules, levels of assurance and a custodian together, with a sharing agreement on record; its stage 5 is the African Union strategy's end state, with mutual recognition of identities between countries. Neither is a fact one story can state.

**The test: is the office itself connected, by a connection provided to it?** Staff working on their own phones or modems are not a connection. A network that *reaches the district* connects nothing until a source puts the office on it.

**Local governments.** The unit is the **basic tier**: the lowest body that administers a locality generally and holds its own budget, whether **elected or appointed**: communes, municipalities, district and town councils, local government areas, counties, woredas. The denominator is the country's own count of them; the short summary names the tier.

**One flag that never changes the stage.** *On the government network*: the offices are on the government's own network.
