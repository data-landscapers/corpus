| `upper-tier` | The networks and platforms of a state, province or region | Noted, not assessed; stated in the long summary |
| `central` | Field offices of a ministry or national agency, and the service centres they run: prefectures beside elected communes, district treasuries, one-stop shops | Out of scope |

**The test: is the office itself connected, by a connection provided to it?** Staff working on their own phones or modems are not a connection. A network that *reaches the district* connects nothing until a source puts the office on it.

**Working online is evidence of a connection.** A source saying local governments enter transactions, at their own offices, in a national system held elsewhere states a `connection`, used for `systems`.

**Local governments.** The unit is the **basic tier**: the lowest body that administers a locality generally and holds its own budget, whether **elected or appointed**: communes, municipalities, district and town councils, local government areas, counties, woredas. The denominator is the country's own count of them; the short summary names the tier.

**One flag that never changes the stage.** *On the government network*: the offices are on the government's own network.
#### 3. Aspects

| # | Aspect | Role | Values |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance, planning, finance | qualifier | Owner: the state, central or local; partner or vendor. Plan: a current strategy or costed plan naming the connection of local governments, or none. Finance: domestic budget line, donor only, or not stated |
| 2 | What the office does over it | coverage | `systems`: staff work in a national or shared system held elsewhere. `internet`: email and the web, or use not stated. `none` |
| 3 | How the office is connected | coverage | `institutional`: provided to the office and paid for by the state or the authority. `personal`: staff's own phones or modems. `none` |
| 4 | Last twelve months | qualifier | Advancing, no change on record, or regressing, with the dated event |
| 5 | Local governments connected | coverage | Share of basic-tier local governments, with numerator, denominator, year and who says so; else `equipped`, a share given equipment or covered by a programme; else a count; else *not published* |

**Aspects 2, 3 and 5 set the stage; 1 and 4 cap or flag it and never raise it. The cap rule and aspect 4 are the health study's, unchanged** (its §3): without the state as owner and a domestic budget line or current plan, stage 4 or 5 is capped at 3 and flagged *externally run*.

**Rural.** The stage reads local governments nationally. Where a dated source says rural ones are unconnected, the cell is flagged *rural gap* and the short summary gives the figure.
#### 4. The ladders

**Office connection**

| Stage | What the office does | How connected | Local governments |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that local government offices have no connection of their own | `personal` or `none` | |
| 2 Preparing | A programme to connect them is being prepared or piloted; or only the capital city or the upper tier is connected | Any, or not stated | Pilot local governments, or none |
| 3 Establishing | `systems` or `internet`: past its pilot and live in some local governments outside the capital | `institutional` | A minority, a count with no total, or not published |
| 4 Operating | `systems` or `internet` | `institutional` | More than half, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | `systems` | `institutional` | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years that gives the rural share |

**Pilot or live, and preparation, are read as the schools study reads them** (its §4). Equipment delivered under a named connection programme is preparation.

**A noted class never places a country above stage 1**, as in the police study (its §4).

**A share *equipped*, *covered* or *computerised* is not a share connected**: it is `equipped` and places at stage 3 only where a connection is live. *All councils*, said by the ministry beside a count of them, is 100 per cent.

**The registry study's R3 fixes hold** (its §4): `institutional` is read from the fact's words, and a law places nothing. A connection with no use named is read as `internet`.

**Fixed at L3** (`ladder-test.csv`, `corrections.csv`): every rung is reached. A share places above stage 3 only where its own source states the `institutional` connection. A network's coverage of areas is `backbone`. A count of offices, centres or sites is a floor without its total. A connection announced is preparation. The basic tier is the one the country counts as its local governments; sub-counties, sectors, cells and wards are sub-offices. A ministry's local sector office is `central`.

**Fixed at L5.** Local governments working in a national system held elsewhere, past any pilot and outside the capital, reach stage 3 with the connection not stated, and no higher. A connection handed over and not said to be in use is preparation.
#### 6. What the review reads and the search asks for

- **Subjects and term list**: in `study.json`.
- **Source types for the briefs**, ranked: annual reports of the ministry of local government and the national ICT agency, for government-network coverage; budget reports and parliamentary answers; audit reports on local governments; donor project documents and evaluations; local government association surveys; dated news, for aspect 4.

### Study opendatapolicy

sub_indicator: "policy" (class "policy")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "standing": "adopted", "ended", "draft", "none"
- aspect "use": "published", "portal", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**The government's own policy that public bodies publish the data they hold for anyone to use and reuse**, whatever it is called: an open data policy, strategy, charter, decree or law, an open government data policy, or the open data part of a larger policy or law where a source says that part sets out that rule.

The study redraws `gov.policy--open-data-policy`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts 33 countries at stage 2, nine at stage 3 and five at stage 1, none at 4 or 5, and leaves seven unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Open data policy | Open data policy: national open data policy | `gov.policy--open-data-policy` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a portal with no policy behind it, a statistics office's data portal, a portal run by a donor or a development bank, an access to information law, a national data policy, one ministry's, one sector's or one city's open data policy, an open data licence, a hackathon, a commitment in an Open Government Partnership plan.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there an open data policy, or one being written? | A policy, strategy, decree or law on publishing government data openly: being drafted, out for consultation, or adopted. A consultant hired or a team appointed to write it counts. A stated intention with no drafting begun is not a yes |
| 2 | Has the government adopted it, and has it not run out? | Approved by cabinet, or launched or issued by the head of state, the minister or the agency responsible, and not ended, replaced or withdrawn. A policy with no end date counts |
| 3 | Is a government open data portal running under it? | A national portal the government runs, opened or reported at work, that a source ties to the policy or that the body responsible for the policy runs |
| 4 | Have public bodies published data under it? | In the last three years: a dated source naming a ministry or agency that has released data on the portal or under the policy, or counting the datasets or the bodies publishing |
#### 3. The ladders

**Open data policy**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no open data policy and none is being written |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a policy being written, or one that has run out with no successor adopted |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a policy adopted and still running |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a government portal is running under it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: public bodies have published data under it in the last three years |

- **Questions 1 to 3 have no time limit**: an adopted policy stands, with its year, until its end date passes or a dated source says it has been replaced or withdrawn.
- **A statement that there is no policy counts for three years.**
- **A draft validated at a workshop, or handed to the minister, is still a draft.**
- **A portal with no policy behind it places nothing.** This is one change from the row as it stands, whose stage 2 counts a portal or a pilot on its own. The portal is noted, and `data.open--use-of-open-data` is the row that reads what is published.
- **A statistics office's data portal is not the portal**, nor is one a development bank or a donor runs for the country, unless a source ties it to the policy.
- **The top stage is data published under the policy, not a law requiring it.** This is the other change from the row as it stands, whose stage 4 asks for a licence, a portal, a duty to publish and a custodian together, and whose stage 5 is agreement with the African Commission's declaration.
- **An access to information law is not an open data policy.** A right to ask for information is noted; a law or decree that a source says requires public bodies to publish their data openly counts.
- **A commitment in an Open Government Partnership plan to write a policy is a stated intention**, and a yes to question 1 only once drafting is reported.
- **Signing the International Open Data Charter places nothing.**
- **A policy for one ministry, one sector or one city does not count.**
- **The open data part of a national data policy or a digital strategy counts** where a source says that part sets the rule; it is named in the summary by that instrument's name, and the data governance and digital strategy rows are not changed by it.
- **A policy adopted but not yet in effect is a yes to question 2**, and the summary gives the date it starts.
- **How good the policy is, how open the licence is and how many datasets there are, are not judged.** A count is given where a source gives one.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `standing` (`adopted`, `ended`, `draft`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, and `use` (`published`, `portal`, `none`) for 3 and 4.

### Study opendatause

sub_indicator: "use" (class "open")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "access": "licensed", "published", "none"
- aspect "breadth": "several"
- aspect "reuse": "reused"

#### 1. What is assessed

**Data a public body publishes online for anyone to download and reuse, and the reuse of it**: datasets on a national open data portal, a statistics office's data portal, a ministry's, a regulator's or parliament's, a geoportal. Whoever publishes counts, under whatever name, with or without a policy behind it.

The study redraws `data.open--use-of-open-data`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts five countries at stage 3, 36 at stage 2 and two at stage 1, none at 4 or 5, and leaves eleven unplaced. It touches no other row: whether an open data policy exists, is adopted and has a portal under it stays with the open data policy study.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Use of open data | Use of open data: use of open data | `data.open--use-of-open-data` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a dashboard or report with no data to download, a portal announced or being built, a portal run by a donor, a development bank or an international body, an international compilation of national figures, a rating such as the Open Data Inventory, an access to information request, a policy or law with no data published under it, a hackathon whose data is not said to be the government's.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does a public body publish data online for anyone to download? | A source from the last three years shows datasets a public body publishes for download, on a portal or its own site, or reports an open data portal at work |
| 2 | Is that data published under an open licence? | A source from the last three years states a licence or terms that let anyone reuse the data, such as Creative Commons or the Licence Ouverte, for at least one body's data |
| 3 | Do several public bodies publish data that way? | A source from the last three years names two or more public bodies publishing data on a portal, or counts the bodies publishing |
| 4 | Is the data reused? | A source from the last three years names an application, a piece of research, a news report or a business built on the government's published data, or gives a figure for its downloads or users |
#### 3. The ladders

**Use of open data**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the last three years says no public body publishes data for anyone to download |
| 2 Published | Yes to 1: data published for download, no open licence on record |
| 3 Licensed | Yes to 2: data published under an open licence |
| 4 Several bodies | Yes to 3: two or more public bodies publish |
| 5 Reused | Yes to 4: reuse of the data on record |

- **A later yes answers the earlier ones**, as the method has it: reuse on record places a country at stage 5 whether or not its licence is held.
- **A statistics office's data portal counts**, and will carry most stage 2 and 3 cells. A portal a development bank or donor runs counts only where a source says the government body publishes on it.
- **A statement that there is no national portal is not a stage 1**: a statistics office may still publish. Stage 1 needs a source that no body publishes data for download.
- **Dataset counts and how open the data is are not judged.** A count is given where a source gives one.
- **Changes from the row as it stands.** Stage 2 counted a portal planned or piloted, and a dashboard: both go to `other`, since nothing is downloadable. Stage 4 asked for several bodies, a licence, bulk download or an interface and reuse together; the interface goes, as it needs the portal itself examined, and reuse moves to stage 5. Stage 5 asked for open data by default, timely, disaggregated and interoperable across the value chain: that is alignment and quality, and goes.

`study.json` holds this as three aspects: `access` (`licensed`, `published`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, `breadth` (`several`) for 3 and `reuse` (`reused`) for 4. **For the drafter**: the portal or site goes in `country_label` with its address, and the body that publishes in the fact.

### Study satellitebroadband

sub_indicator: "satellite" (class "satellite")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "service": "choice", "sale", "licensed", "none"
- aspect "counted": "counted", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**Internet access sold to households or businesses in the country over a satellite link**, whoever sells it and whatever the orbit: a low-orbit service such as Starlink or OneWeb, a geostationary one such as Konnect or YahClick, or capacity on a national satellite resold by a licensed provider.

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is a satellite broadband provider licensed? | A source states that the regulator or ministry has licensed or authorised a provider to sell internet over satellite in the country |
| 2 | Is it on sale? | A source states that households or businesses in the country can buy satellite broadband from a licensed provider |
| 3 | Is it on sale from more than one satellite network? | A source states that services on two different satellite networks are on sale, for instance Starlink and a OneWeb or Konnect service; two resellers of one network count once |

**Satellite broadband licensed and on sale**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the past three years says no satellite broadband provider is licensed, or that its sale or use is banned |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a provider licensed |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 2: on sale to households or businesses |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 3: on sale from two satellite networks |
| 5 Leading | Yes to 4: subscribers counted by the regulator |

- **Only question 4 has a window.** A licence or a service stays in place until a later source says it has been suspended, withdrawn or stopped; a suspension of sales takes a country back to stage 2.
