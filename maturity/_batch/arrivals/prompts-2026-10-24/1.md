| 1 | Governance, planning, finance | qualifier | Owner: the force or its ministry, partner or vendor. Plan: a current strategy or costed plan naming the system, or none. Finance: domestic budget line, donor only, or not stated |
| 2 | Records made digitally | coverage | O, C, or none |
| 3 | How the record is made | coverage | `connected`: at the station, when the report is taken, and held in the national system, online or by synchronising. `local`: at the station, in a system that stays in the station. `keyed-elsewhere`: at headquarters or a regional command, from the station's paper forms, as routine. `paper` |
| 4 | Last twelve months | qualifier | Advancing, no change on record, or regressing, with the dated event |

**Aspects 2, 3 and 5 set the stage; 1 and 4 cap or flag it and never raise it. The cap rule and aspect 4 are the health study's, unchanged** (its §3): without the force or its ministry as owner and a domestic budget line or current plan, stage 4 or 5 is capped at 3 and flagged *externally run*.

**Rural.** The stage reads stations nationally. Where a dated source says rural stations are outside the system, or that the force policing the countryside is, the cell is flagged *rural gap* and the short summary gives the figure.

**Two forces.** Where police and gendarmerie keep separate systems, the cell is staged on the force with more stations, and the long summary states the other. The short summary names the force its figure counts.
#### 4. The ladders

Tested at P3 (`ladder-test.csv`); rung 5 is unreached and stands. **Fixed at P5**: an absence covering one town, region or function leaves the cell unplaced.

**Station records**

| Stage | Records | How the record is made | Stations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that stations record on paper only | | |
| 2 Preparing | A system for stations is being prepared or piloted; or one runs at headquarters or in the capital only | Any, or not stated | Pilot stations, or none |
| 3 Establishing | O or C: past its pilot and live in some stations outside the capital | `connected`, `local` or `keyed-elsewhere` | A minority, a count with no total, or not published |
| 4 Operating | O or C | `connected` | More than half, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | O and C | `connected` | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years that gives the rural share |

**Pilot or live, and preparation, are read as the schools study reads them** (its §4).

**A noted class never places a country above stage 1.** A country holding only noted classes is unplaced, or stage 1 where a source states that stations record on paper; what it holds prints under *Noted, not assessed*.

**A share of districts with a digital station is read as the share of stations.** A share of stations *equipped*, *connected*, *computerised* or *digitised* is not a share recording: it is `equipped` and places at stage 3 only where a `record` system is live. *All stations*, said by the force beside a count of stations, is 100 per cent.

**The registry study's R3 fixes hold** (its §4): `connected` is read from the fact's words, and a law places nothing. A digital record with neither O nor C named is read as O.

### Study publicconsultation

sub_indicator: "consultation" (class "consultation")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "hearing": "answered", "held", "none"
- aspect "standing": "body", "required", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**Whether people outside government get a say in the country's digital laws and policies**: whether the government or a regulator asks them, publishes what they said, is required to ask, and keeps a standing body where they sit.

**People outside government** are civil society, companies and their associations, academics and the public. Donors, United Nations agencies and hired consultants are not.

**A digital law or policy** is a named law, bill, regulation, policy or strategy on telecommunications, the internet, data, digital identity, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, digital government or the digital economy.

The study redraws `gov.discourse--non-governmental-contribution-to-national-policy`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts 24 countries at stage 2 and two at stage 3, none at 1, 4 or 5, and leaves 28 unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation on digital law and policy | Non-governmental contribution to national policy: consultation on digital law and policy | `gov.discourse--non-governmental-contribution-to-national-policy` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a national internet governance forum; a conference or debate with no named law or policy put to it; a consultation on something not digital; a civil-society report or campaign the government did not ask for; a workshop of officials, donors and consultants only; an open government action plan with no digital commitment; a regional or continental consultation.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has the government asked people outside government for their views on a digital law or policy in the last three years? | A named draft put out for comment, or a hearing, workshop or meeting on a named digital law or policy that the source says people outside government took part in. The government, parliament or a regulator does the asking |
| 2 | Has it published what it was told in the last three years? | A published report of a consultation on a digital law or policy, the comments received, or the government's or regulator's written answer to them |
| 3 | Has a consultation on a digital law or policy been held under a rule that requires one, in the last three years? | A consultation that the source says a law, decree or published procedure required. The rule alone, with no consultation held under it, is not a yes |
| 4 | Has a standing body with members from outside government given its view on a digital law or policy in the last three years? | A council, committee or forum set up by law, decree or a minister or regulator, with named members from outside government, that the source says gave an opinion on, reviewed or helped draft a named digital law or policy. A body that has only been set up or has only met is not a yes |
#### 3. The ladders

**Consultation on digital law and policy**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says a named digital law or policy was made without consulting people outside government, or that they were refused a part in it |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: people outside government were asked |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: what they said was published |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a consultation was held because a rule required it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: a standing body with outside members gave its view |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a consultation held under a rule, and a council's opinion on a bill, each show people outside government were asked.
- **Every answer counts for three years.** So does a statement that people were not consulted, and it places a country at stage 1 only where no yes is held inside the same three years.
- **A national internet governance forum places nothing**, and is noted: it meets, and no government is bound to ask it anything. The row as it stands places Togo, South Sudan, São Tomé and Príncipe and Chad on theirs.
- **A rule that requires consultation places nothing until a consultation is held under it**, and is noted. The row as it stands places Tunisia and Angola on the rule.
- **A body that has been set up or has met places nothing until it gives a view on a named law or policy**, and is noted. The row as it stands places Sierra Leone, Cabo Verde and Lesotho on such a body.
- **A consultation on something that is not digital places nothing.** The row as it stands places Gabon on its development plan and Eritrea on water planning.
- **A validation workshop is a yes to question 1 only where the source names someone from outside government in the room.**
- **A regulator consulting the companies it licenses is a yes to question 1.** The summary says who was asked.
- **A hearing held by parliament on a digital bill is a yes to question 1.**
- **An open government action plan is a yes to question 1 only**, and only where the source says civil society helped write it and it carries a commitment on digital government, data or the internet. A report on that plan by the Open Government Partnership's own reviewers is not a yes to question 2: the government did not publish it. *(Reworded in the run; no stage moved.)*
- **A complaint that a consultation was too short or ignored does not undo a yes.** It is noted.
- **Whether the views changed anything, and how open or fair the consultation was, is not judged.**
- **Two more changes from the row as it stands**: its stage 4 asks that outside views be traceable in the adopted text, which needs two texts compared; its stage 5 is the African Charter on Democracy's end state. Neither is a fact one story can state.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `hearing` (`answered`, `held`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2 and for stage 1, and `standing` (`body`, `required`, `none`) for questions 3 and 4.
