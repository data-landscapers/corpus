### Study regionalinfrastructure

sub_indicator: "shared" (class "shared")

#### 1. What is assessed

**A link or facility that carries or holds digital traffic for the country and at least one other African country**, whoever owns it and whatever it is called: a fibre link across a border, a submarine cable that joins two African countries or that a landlocked country reaches through a neighbour, an internet exchange point or data centre that serves more than one country, a satellite owned or used by more than one.

|---|---|---|

#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| 2 | Has building started in the last three years? | A dated source saying a contract was awarded, works began or cable was being laid. A tender opened or money promised is not a yes |
| 3 | Is it in service? | A dated source saying the link was lit, commissioned or opened to traffic, or the facility opened to users from another country. A launch ceremony for works, or a test, is not a yes |
| 4 | Is it reported carrying another country's traffic in the last three years? | Published figures of capacity sold or traffic carried across the border; a named operator or government of the other country reported buying or using it; or a fault on it reported cutting the other country's service. #### 3. The ladders

**Digital links and facilities shared with other African countries**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no digital link with a neighbour, or stands outside the shared links of its region, with none on record |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: agreed, and nothing more on record |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: being built |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: in service |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: reported carrying another country's traffic in the last three years |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a link in service was agreed and built.
- **Question 3 has no time limit**; a link in service stands, with its year, until a dated source says it is cut or closed. Questions 1, 2 and 4 count for three years.
- **A statement that the country has no shared link counts for three years**, and gives way to a dated source naming one.
- **A link counts for each country a source names at its ends.** A country only named as on the route does not take the stage.
- **A landlocked country's route to a sea cable through a neighbour is a link in service** for both, where a dated source says traffic runs over it.
- **A submarine cable counts where a source says it joins the country to another African country.** A landing alone does not.
- **A company's link counts as a government's does.** What is asked is what the link does.
- **A regional programme's financing signed by the country is a yes to question 1** where the source names the cross-border link or shared facility it pays for.
- **An agreement a later dated source says is suspended or cancelled is no longer a yes.**
- **A national facility described as serving the region is not a yes to question 3** until a source names another country using it.
- **The best-placed link sets the stage**; the others are named in the summary.
- **What a link joins and what it carries is taken from what a source says of it.** How good or how dear it is, is not judged.
- **A resolution to pay for a study of a shared satellite is a study**, not a yes to question 1 *(added in the run, for the Northern Corridor states' satellite)*.
- **A link live and carrying traffic for customers who are not named is in service, not a yes to question 4** *(added in the run, for the Goma to Mombasa route)*.

- **A cable owner's announced plan, or a consensus reached at a workshop, is not an agreement** *(added in the run, for Madagascar, Liberia and Sierra Leone)*.

### Study regionallaw

sub_indicator: "harmonisation" (class "harmonisation")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "accepted": "ratified", "signed", "none"
- aspect "effect": "in-use", "in-law", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**The country's taking of digital law agreed among African governments into its own law**, whatever form the shared law takes: a treaty of the African Union, an act, directive or regulation of a regional community, a model law, or an agreement between African governments to recognise each other's licences, signatures or rules.

*Digital* means telecommunications, the internet, data, digital identity, digital government, digital payments, digital trade, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. The two continental texts are the African Union's Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (the Malabo Convention) and the Protocol on Digital Trade of the African Continental Free Trade Area.

The study redraws `gov.regional--regional-legal-harmonisation`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts three countries at stage 3, one at stage 2 and one at stage 1, and leaves 49 unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Regional digital law taken into national law | Regional legal harmonisation: regional digital law taken into national law | `gov.regional--regional-legal-harmonisation` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a United Nations, Council of Europe, Arab League or Lusophone convention or charter; a shared law that is not digital, such as a land, accounting or statistics directive, a free movement rule or a business law act; a regional strategy, declaration or guideline that is not law; a roaming cut, a joint body, a meeting hosted or a seat won, which the regional policy row holds; a national law with no source tying it to a shared one.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has the country signed an African legal text on a digital matter, or is it bound by one? | A source naming the country as a signatory to such a treaty or agreement, or as bound by a named act, directive or regulation of its regional community. Membership of the African Union or a regional community alone, with no text named, is not a yes |
| 2 | Has it ratified one? | A dated ratification or accession: a law or decree approving it, or the instrument deposited. A cabinet approval, a bill before parliament or a promise to ratify is not a yes |
| 3 | Has a national law or rule been passed that gives effect to one? | A dated source saying a national law, decree or regulation was passed or amended to carry out, transpose or bring national law into line with a named shared text. A draft, a consultation or a study is not a yes |
| 4 | Is the shared law reported applied across a border in the last three years? | A published act under it that reaches another country or the regional body: a licence or signature recognised, a request between authorities answered, a ruling that applies it, or the regional body's published finding that the country complies. A launch, a workshop or a statement of intent is not a yes |
#### 3. The ladders

**Regional digital law taken into national law**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has not signed, with no other shared text on record |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: signed or bound, and nothing more on record |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: ratified |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a national law or rule gives effect to it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: reported applied across a border in the last three years |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a national law passed to carry out a community directive needs no ratification; a licence recognised under an agreement shows the agreement is in effect.
- **Questions 1, 2 and 3 have no time limit**; a signature, a ratification and a law stand, with their year, until a dated source says the country has withdrawn or the law is repealed. Question 4 counts for three years.
- **A statement that the country has not signed counts for three years**, and gives way to a dated source naming a signature or another shared text.
- **Not signing one text is not stage 1** where the country has signed or is bound by another. It is named in the summary.
- **A country that has left a regional community is no longer bound by its texts** from the date it left. A national law it passed to carry one out stands.
- **A community regulation that applies in the country without a national act is a yes to question 1.** It is a yes to question 3 when a dated source says the country's own government or regulator has applied it.
- **An agreement that needs no ratification is a yes to question 1 on signature**, and goes on to question 3 by a national rule or question 4 by its use.
- **A text ratified by parliament or cleared by a court, with nothing saying it was deposited, is a yes to question 2**; what is not reported is named in the summary.
- **A national law that a source says is modelled on a shared text is a yes to question 3.** One that a source says falls short of it, or needs amending to match it, is not.
- **What a text covers and whom it binds is taken from what a source says of it**, never from reading its text for the purpose.
- **How good the national law is, or how well it matches, is not judged.** A reported criticism is noted.
- **A blank line for the country in the African Union's register of a treaty is a dated statement that it has not signed** *(added in the run)*.
- **A national law that a source calls inspired by a model law, in part, is not a yes to question 3** *(added in the run, for Lesotho)*. The question asks for a source saying the law was passed to give effect to the shared text.
- **A ratification a dated source reports stands where the register shows none** *(added in the run, for Djibouti and Gabon)*; the summary names the difference.
- **A year alone, in the as-at's own year, stands for the as-at** *(added in the run)*.

**The country's part in making digital policy with other African governments, and in carrying out what they agree**, whatever form it takes: membership of an African body that sets digital policy, an agreement or declaration signed with another African government or with such a body, a meeting hosted or chaired, a seat won, and the thing agreed put into effect at home.

*Digital* means telecommunications, the internet, data, digital identity, digital government, digital payments, digital trade, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. *An African body* is the African Union, one of its regional communities, or a body whose members are mostly African states: the African Telecommunications Union, Smart Africa, a regional association of regulators or of data protection authorities.
