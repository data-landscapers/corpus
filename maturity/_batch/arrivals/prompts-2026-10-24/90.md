### Study internetusage

sub_indicator: "use" (class "use")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "users": a value matching (survey|estimate):\d{1,3}(\.\d+)?

#### 1. What is assessed

**How many of a country's people use the internet: a quarter, half, four in five, or nearly everyone.**

**The figure** is the share of the whole population who used the internet in the last three months, as the International Telecommunication Union publishes it for each country, or as a national household survey or census gives it. **A government press statement that gives the share of people who use the internet counts as a survey** *(Bill, 2026-10-13)*.

The study redraws `infra.connect--internet-usage`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts 15 countries at stage 1, 19 at stage 2, 17 at stage 3 and three at stage 4, with none at stage 5 and none unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| People who use the internet | Internet usage: people who use the internet | `infra.connect--internet-usage` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: internet or mobile-data subscriptions, which count a person once for every line; an operator's or the regulator's count of "internet users" or its "penetration rate", which are counts of subscriptions; DataReportal's yearly country figures; a figure for adults only; households with internet; mobile internet users; social media users; network coverage; the price of data.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does at least a quarter of the population use the internet? | The latest figure held, for 2024 or later, is 25 per cent or more |
| 2 | Does at least half? | That figure is 50 per cent or more |
| 3 | Do at least four in five? | That figure is 80 per cent or more |
| 4 | Does nearly everyone? | That figure is 95 per cent or more |
#### 3. The ladders

**People who use the internet**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | The latest figure, for 2024 or later, is below 25 per cent: fewer than a quarter use the internet |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: at least a quarter |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: at least half |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: at least four in five |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: nearly everyone |

- **The figures Corpus already holds answer all four questions for 51 countries before anything is read**: stage 1 for 11, stage 2 for 21, stage 3 for 16, and stage 4 for Libya, Morocco and Seychelles. None reaches stage 5.
- **Three countries have no figure in the window**: Eritrea's latest is for 2020, South Sudan's for 2019 and Sudan's for 2017. They are *No evidence* unless a story read in the run gives one.
- **The two lower lines are a quarter and a half, where the row as it stands uses 27 and 53 per cent**, thirds of the way to 80. On the held figures that moves Sierra Leone (25.1) and Lesotho (51.8) up a stage and no other country.
- **An estimate by the International Telecommunication Union counts.** It is the latest figure for 45 of the 51; the summary says where a figure is an estimate and not a survey.
- **A national survey or census figure for 2024 or later, where one is held, is used in place of an estimate**, and the newest survey in place of an older one. A government press statement's figure is a survey figure where it is a share of people; one that counts subscriptions or lines is not.
- **A fall is a fall**: the latest figure sets the stage, whatever an earlier one said.
- **A regulator's or operator's count, DataReportal's figure, and a figure for adults only are noted and never used**, even for a country with nothing else. South Sudan's current cell rests on the regulator's count.

`study.json` holds this as one aspect, `users`, each fact carrying `survey:` or `estimate:`, its per cent and its year; the ladder counts the lines the latest figure clears.

### Study nationaldatacentres

sub_indicator: "national" (class "datacentre")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "centre": "two", "tier3", "service", "none"
- aspect "owner": "national", "foreign"
- aspect "state": "hosted", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**A data centre for hire in the country that is owned in the country**: by the state, a state enterprise, or a company owned mostly by the country's own people or firms, whatever it is called. "For hire" is the datacentres study's test: it rents space, power or computing to other organisations.

The study redraws `infra.store--local-data-centre-capacity-national-providers`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts 18 countries at stage 4, 7 at stage 3, 8 at stage 2 and 8 at stage 1, none at stage 5, and 13 without a stage. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Nationally owned data centres for hire | Local data centre capacity (national providers): nationally owned data centres for hire | `infra.store--local-data-centre-capacity-national-providers` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a foreign-owned data centre, which `datacentres` assesses; one announced, financed or under construction; one used only by its owner; a rule that data must stay in the country, which `datalocalisation` assesses.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is a nationally owned data centre for hire in service? | A source states that a data centre for hire in service is owned by the state, a state enterprise or a company owned mostly in the country |
| 2 | Is one of them Tier III or above? | A source states that such a data centre is Tier III or IV, by certification, a TIA-942 rating or the operator's own statement |
| 3 | Are two of them Tier III or above? | Sources state that two such data centres are Tier III or IV |
| 4 | Does the government host its own data with a national provider? | A source states that ministries' or the state's own systems or data are hosted in a nationally owned data centre in the country |
#### 3. The ladders

**Nationally owned data centres for hire**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the past three years says no data centre for hire in the country is nationally owned |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a nationally owned data centre for hire in service |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 2: one at Tier III or above |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 3: two at Tier III or above |
| 5 Leading | Yes to 3 and 4: two at Tier III, and the state's own data hosted with a national provider |

- **No question has a window.** A data centre in service, and a state's hosting, stand until a later source says they have ended.
- **Ownership is the owner's, at the as-at date.** A company bought by a foreign group is foreign from the sale. A joint venture is national where the country's side holds the majority.
- **Stages 2 to 4 change.** The row banded the share of facilities nationally owned, at 25 % and 50 %. A share needs every facility counted, and no story states one; a share of 100 % also put a country with one small state facility above one with five. The study counts nationally owned facilities and their Tier instead, as `datacentres` does for all owners.
- **Stage 5 is the row's own end state**, asked as a fact: the row's wording also required a data classification in force, which needs a law read and belongs to `datalocalisation`.
- **Stage 1 needs a statement of absence**, as in every study. A country whose only data centres for hire are foreign-owned, with no such statement, is unplaced and says so.

`study.json` holds this as three aspects: `centre` (`two`, `tier3`, `service`, `none`) for a facility, `owner` (`national`, `foreign`) for who owns it, and `state` (`hosted`, `none`) for question 4.
