#### 1. The question, and the row it redraws

**Does the country have a national digital strategy in force, and does it publish what the strategy has delivered?** `gov.policy--digital-transformation-strategy` stages 45 cells: 26 at stage 3, 19 at 2, none at 1, 4 or 5, and 9 unplaced. Two things cause that.

- **No settled subject.** Cells hold national strategies, public-service plans, a development plan's pillar, an agency's plan, a ministerial roadmap and a connectivity strategy as one thing.
- **A fourth rung asking for four things at once**: a costed plan, a mandated body, a progress report and budget lines.

**This is the first study of an instrument.** The method's rule that a strategy places nothing above stage 2 was written for systems. Here it becomes: **adoption alone places no higher than 3**; above that the test is published reporting.

**`gov.policy--ict-strategy` is not redrawn.** It stages on the same document as this row in at least 14 countries; that is its own study's to settle.
#### 2. Typology

| Class | What it is | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `national` | **The government's own strategy for digital change across the economy and the state**: adopted, or to be adopted, by the government as a whole | Assessed |
| `government` | A strategy for digitalising the state's own administration and services, and nothing beyond them | Noted, not assessed; flagged |
| `development-plan` | A digital pillar or chapter of a national development plan or vision | Noted, not assessed; flagged |
| `ministry-plan` | A ministry's, regulator's or agency's own strategic plan or roadmap | Noted, not assessed |
| `directive` | A law, decree, ordinance or order to digitalise, with no strategy behind it | Noted, not assessed |
| `programme` | A financed project or programme, donor or domestic | Noted, not assessed; finance it gives the strategy is a `national` fact |
| `sector-ict` | An ICT or telecommunications sector policy | Logged for `gov.policy--ict-strategy` |

**The test: is it the whole government's strategy, and does it cover both the economy and the state?** Read from the text or from a source describing its pillars. A *digital economy strategy*, *digital agenda* or *master plan* is `national` where it passes; the label is the country's.

**The whole government.** Adopted by the cabinet, the head of state or government, or parliament. A ministry's own roadmap is `ministry-plan`, whatever it is called.

| # | Aspect | Role | Values |
|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | Period | coverage | `to:YYYY`, the year its stated period ends, or `open` |
| 5 | Delivery plan | coverage | `costed`: an implementation or action plan stating what it costs. `actions`: dated actions or targets with no cost. `none` |
| 6 | Reporting | coverage | `results-all`: a published report, review or evaluation giving progress against the strategy's own targets or actions, on every pillar. `results`: the same on some. `activity`: implementation claimed, measured against nothing in the strategy. `none` |

**Aspects 2 to 6 set the stage; 1 and 7 cap or flag it and never raise it.**

**The cap rule.** Without a mandated body and a domestic budget line, stage 4 or 5 is capped at 3 and flagged *no budget or body*.

**Flags that never change the stage.** *Government only* and *in the national plan*, for the noted class held instead; *lapsed*; *text unpublished*.

**An open strategy stops at stage 3. Stage 5 has no alignment test**: method §3 rule 5.

**Fixed at D3** (`corrections.csv`):

- **Adoption is the source's own word**: *approved*, *adopted*, *enacted* or *gazetted*; the act named is not required. A launch by a ministry alone is `announced`.

- **A published rate of execution of the strategy's own actions is `results`.**
- **A fact is kept for a named strategy whose scope its document does not describe**; the class comes from the documents that do.

#### 1. What is assessed

**The law that makes a contract, record or signature made electronically as valid as one on paper, and sets the rules for selling online**, whatever it is called: an electronic transactions act, an e-commerce law, an electronic signature law, or the part of a digital code, a communications act or a consumer protection law that does that job.

#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there a law on electronic transactions or e-commerce, or a draft of one? | A bill or draft published, out for consultation, before cabinet or parliament, or passed, or a law made. A law only announced or planned is not a yes |
| 2 | Is that law in force? | A law, ordinance or decree in force that gives electronic contracts, records or signatures legal effect, or sets rules for selling online. A bill or draft is not a yes |
| 3 | Have rules been made under it to put it to work? | A dated decree, regulation or order made under the law: on electronic signatures, on those who certify them, or on those who sell online |
| 4 | Has it been applied in the last three years? | A dated accreditation or licence of a provider of electronic signatures or certificates under it, a registration or licence of an online seller or digital operator under it, or a penalty or order made under it |
#### 3. The ladders

**E-commerce law**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no law on electronic transactions or e-commerce and no draft of one |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a bill or draft, and no law in force |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a law in force |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: rules made under it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: the law applied in the last three years |

**Applied, settled as worded** *(Bill, 2026-10-23)*: the authority the law creates issuing certificates itself is not a provider accredited under it, and licensed providers named with no licence date do not show the law applied within three years.

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a signature provider accredited under the law shows the law is in force.
- **Questions 1 to 3 have no time limit**; a law stands, with its year, until a dated source says it is repealed, replaced or struck down.
- **A statement that there is no law counts for three years**, and gives way to a dated source naming a draft or a law in force.
- **A law on electronic signatures alone is a yes to question 2**: it gives electronic dealings legal effect. The summary says the law covers signatures only.
- **A digital code, a communications act or a consumer protection law counts for the part that does the job**, where a source says it covers electronic transactions or online selling.
- **An old law still in force is a yes to question 2 and no more**, until a source names rules made under it. A bill to replace it is named in the summary and does not lower the stage.
- **A law promulgated but not yet in effect is a yes to question 2**, and the summary gives the date it starts. One passed by parliament and not yet promulgated is a yes to question 1 only *(reworded in the run: drafters read "passed" two ways, for Djibouti)*.
- **A regional act or model law places nothing** until a source says the country has made it its own law.
- **A government office using electronic signatures is not a yes to question 4**; nor is a signature platform launched or a contract to build one. The question asks for an accreditation, licence, registration or penalty under the law.
- **A state body named as the national certification authority is a yes to question 4** where a source says it is named or accredited under the law.
- **What the law covers is taken from what a source says of it**, never from reading its text for the purpose.
- **How good the law is is not judged.** A reported criticism is noted.
- **Changes from the row as it stands.** Most of its 16 unplaced cells lead with something that is not this law. Its stage 4 asks for several things together and its stage 5 for four consistent laws, three of them other rows'; neither is a fact one story can state.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `standing` (`in-force`, `draft`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, and `reach` (`applied`, `rules`, `none`) for 3 and 4.

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a consultation held under a rule, and a council's opinion on a bill, each show people outside government were asked.
- **Every answer counts for three years.** So does a statement that people were not consulted, and it places a country at stage 1 only where no yes is held inside the same three years.
- **A national internet governance forum places nothing**, and is noted: it meets, and no government is bound to ask it anything. The row as it stands places Togo, South Sudan, São Tomé and Príncipe and Chad on theirs.
- **A rule that requires consultation places nothing until a consultation is held under it**, and is noted. The row as it stands places Tunisia and Angola on the rule.
- **A body that has been set up or has met places nothing until it gives a view on a named law or policy**, and is noted. The row as it stands places Sierra Leone, Cabo Verde and Lesotho on such a body.
- **A consultation on something that is not digital places nothing.** The row as it stands places Gabon on its development plan and Eritrea on water planning.
- **A validation workshop is a yes to question 1 only where the source names someone from outside government in the room.**
- **A regulator consulting the companies it licenses is a yes to question 1.** The summary says who was asked.
- **A hearing held by parliament on a digital bill is a yes to question 1.**
- **An open government action plan is a yes to question 1 only**, and only where the source says civil society helped write it and it carries a commitment on digital government, data or the internet. A report on that plan by the Open Government Partnership's own reviewers is not a yes to question 2: the government did not publish it. *(Reworded in the run; no stage moved.)*
- **A complaint that a consultation was too short or ignored does not undo a yes.** It is noted.
- **Whether the views changed anything, and how open or fair the consultation was, is not judged.**
- **Two more changes from the row as it stands**: its stage 4 asks that outside views be traceable in the adopted text, which needs two texts compared; its stage 5 is the African Charter on Democracy's end state. Neither is a fact one story can state.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `hearing` (`answered`, `held`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2 and for stage 1, and `standing` (`body`, `required`, `none`) for questions 3 and 4.

### Study regionallaw

sub_indicator: "harmonisation" (class "harmonisation")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "accepted": "ratified", "signed", "none"
- aspect "effect": "in-use", "in-law", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**The country's taking of digital law agreed among African governments into its own law**, whatever form the shared law takes: a treaty of the African Union, an act, directive or regulation of a regional community, a model law, or an agreement between African governments to recognise each other's licences, signatures or rules.

*Digital* means telecommunications, the internet, data, digital identity, digital government, digital payments, digital trade, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. The two continental texts are the African Union's Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (the Malabo Convention) and the Protocol on Digital Trade of the African Continental Free Trade Area.

The study redraws `gov.regional--regional-legal-harmonisation`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts three countries at stage 3, one at stage 2 and one at stage 1, and leaves 49 unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Regional digital law taken into national law | Regional legal harmonisation: regional digital law taken into national law | `gov.regional--regional-legal-harmonisation` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a United Nations, Council of Europe, Arab League or Lusophone convention or charter; a shared law that is not digital, such as a land, accounting or statistics directive, a free movement rule or a business law act; a regional strategy, declaration or guideline that is not law; a roaming cut, a joint body, a meeting hosted or a seat won, which the regional policy row holds; a national law with no source tying it to a shared one.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has the country signed an African legal text on a digital matter, or is it bound by one? | A source naming the country as a signatory to such a treaty or agreement, or as bound by a named act, directive or regulation of its regional community. Membership of the African Union or a regional community alone, with no text named, is not a yes |
| 2 | Has it ratified one? | A dated ratification or accession: a law or decree approving it, or the instrument deposited. A cabinet approval, a bill before parliament or a promise to ratify is not a yes |
| 3 | Has a national law or rule been passed that gives effect to one? | A dated source saying a national law, decree or regulation was passed or amended to carry out, transpose or bring national law into line with a named shared text. A draft, a consultation or a study is not a yes |
| 4 | Is the shared law reported applied across a border in the last three years? | A published act under it that reaches another country or the regional body: a licence or signature recognised, a request between authorities answered, a ruling that applies it, or the regional body's published finding that the country complies. A launch, a workshop or a statement of intent is not a yes |
#### 3. The ladders

**Regional digital law taken into national law**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has not signed, with no other shared text on record |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: signed or bound, and nothing more on record |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: ratified |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a national law or rule gives effect to it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: reported applied across a border in the last three years |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: a national law passed to carry out a community directive needs no ratification; a licence recognised under an agreement shows the agreement is in effect.
- **Questions 1, 2 and 3 have no time limit**; a signature, a ratification and a law stand, with their year, until a dated source says the country has withdrawn or the law is repealed. Question 4 counts for three years.
- **A statement that the country has not signed counts for three years**, and gives way to a dated source naming a signature or another shared text.
- **Not signing one text is not stage 1** where the country has signed or is bound by another. It is named in the summary.
- **A country that has left a regional community is no longer bound by its texts** from the date it left. A national law it passed to carry one out stands.
- **A community regulation that applies in the country without a national act is a yes to question 1.** It is a yes to question 3 when a dated source says the country's own government or regulator has applied it.
- **An agreement that needs no ratification is a yes to question 1 on signature**, and goes on to question 3 by a national rule or question 4 by its use.
- **A text ratified by parliament or cleared by a court, with nothing saying it was deposited, is a yes to question 2**; what is not reported is named in the summary.
- **A national law that a source says is modelled on a shared text is a yes to question 3.** One that a source says falls short of it, or needs amending to match it, is not.
- **What a text covers and whom it binds is taken from what a source says of it**, never from reading its text for the purpose.
- **How good the national law is, or how well it matches, is not judged.** A reported criticism is noted.
- **A blank line for the country in the African Union's register of a treaty is a dated statement that it has not signed** *(added in the run)*.
- **A national law that a source calls inspired by a model law, in part, is not a yes to question 3** *(added in the run, for Lesotho)*. The question asks for a source saying the law was passed to give effect to the shared text.
- **A ratification a dated source reports stands where the register shows none** *(added in the run, for Djibouti and Gabon)*; the summary names the difference.
- **A year alone, in the as-at's own year, stands for the as-at** *(added in the run)*.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `accepted` (`ratified`, `signed`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, and `effect` (`in-use`, `in-law`, `none`) for 3 and 4.

### Study regionalpolicy

sub_indicator: "cooperation" (class "cooperation")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "part": "active", "member", "none"
- aspect "effect": "in-use", "in-effect", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**The country's part in making digital policy with other African governments, and in carrying out what they agree**, whatever form it takes: membership of an African body that sets digital policy, an agreement or declaration signed with another African government or with such a body, a meeting hosted or chaired, a seat won, and the thing agreed put into effect at home.

*Digital* means telecommunications, the internet, data, digital identity, digital government, digital payments, digital trade, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. *An African body* is the African Union, one of its regional communities, or a body whose members are mostly African states: the African Telecommunications Union, Smart Africa, a regional association of regulators or of data protection authorities.

The study redraws `gov.regional--regional-policy-collaboration`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts 29 countries at stage 3, 14 at stage 2 and one at stage 1, none at 4 or 5, and leaves ten unplaced. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Regional cooperation on digital policy | Regional policy collaboration: regional cooperation on digital policy | `gov.regional--regional-policy-collaboration` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: an agreement with a government or body outside Africa, or with a company; a global body or campaign; an Arab League or Francophonie body; a donor's programme no African body runs; a national law brought into line with a regional model, a shared cable or exchange point, and a rule on sending data abroad, each of which has its own row; a trade or political agreement that is not about digital policy.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the country belong to an African body that makes digital policy? | A source naming the country as a member of such a body, as joining it, or as taking part in its meeting. Membership of the African Union or a regional community alone is not a yes |
| 2 | Has it taken an active part in the last three years? | A dated agreement, memorandum, protocol or declaration on digital policy signed with another African government or an African body; or a meeting of such a body hosted or chaired; or a seat or office in one won. Talks opened, a visit, or a meeting attended is not a yes |
| 3 | Has anything it agreed been put into effect? | A dated source saying a thing agreed has started in the country: roaming charges cut or removed with a neighbour, a joint committee or council set up, a regional guideline or strategy adopted as a national rule or plan, a shared service opened |
| 4 | Is that reported in use in the last three years? | Published figures of its use; a dated decision of the joint body; or a published review of what the agreement has delivered. A launch, a renewal or a statement of intent is not a yes |
#### 3. The ladders

**Regional cooperation on digital policy**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country is suspended from, has left or stands outside the African bodies that make digital policy, with no agreement on record |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a member, and nothing more on record |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: an agreement signed, a meeting hosted or chaired, or a seat won, in the last three years |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: something agreed put into effect |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: reported in use in the last three years |

- **A yes to a later question answers the earlier ones**: an agreement signed shows the country takes part; a joint body's decision shows it was set up.
- **Membership has no time limit**; it stands, with its year, until a dated source says the country has left or is suspended.
- **Question 3 has no time limit**; what was put into effect stands until a dated source says it has ended. Questions 2 and 4 count for three years.
- **A statement that the country stands outside counts for three years**, and gives way to a dated source naming a membership or an agreement.
- **A suspension from one body is not stage 1** where the country is a member of another or has signed an agreement since. It is named in the summary.
- **Chairing, hosting or holding office is a yes to question 2 and no more.** It shows an active part, not a thing delivered.
- **An agreement approved by one side only, or awaiting signature, is not a yes to question 2.**
- **A regional strategy, declaration or guideline that names the country as bound, with no act of the country's own, is a yes to question 1 only.**
- **A national plan that says it follows a regional one is not a yes to question 3.** The question asks for a dated act that a source ties to what was agreed.
- **A donor's regional programme counts where an African body runs it**, and then as that body's: joining it is a yes to question 1, a financing agreement signed under it a yes to question 2.
- **A declaration that lists the country among many signatories, with no act of its own reported, is a yes to question 1 only** *(added in the run, for the Africa Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and its 49 signatories)*.
- **A body created by signature, its statutes and work programme still to be adopted, is not yet a joint body set up** *(added in the run, for the Council of Sahel Regulators)*. A joint commission that has met is.
- **Projects agreed by a joint body are a statement of intent, not a decision in use** *(added in the run, for Algeria and Tunisia)*.
- **A body of countries that share a language is `other`** *(added in the run)*, as the Francophonie is.
- **What an agreement covers is taken from what a source says of it**, never from reading its text for the purpose.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `part` (`active`, `member`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, and `effect` (`in-use`, `in-effect`, `none`) for 3 and 4.
