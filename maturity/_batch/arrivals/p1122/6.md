#### 3. The ladders

**Robustness of system**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the past three years says there is no national ID system, or only a paper one with no unique number. |
| 2 Under way | Yes to 1 |
| 3 In service | Yes to 2 |
| 4 Biometric checks | Yes to 3 |
| 5 Certified | Yes to 4 and 5 |

**Each step of the ladder must be met to progress** *(Bill, 2026-11-09, message 114; method §3.3)*. **Settled as worded** *(Bill, 2026-10-23)*: a minister's statement that an audit was done is not a published audit, so it is no yes to question 4. `study.json` holds this as three aspects: `system` (`inservice`, `underway`, `none`) for stage 1 and questions 1 and 2, `biometric` (`checked`) for 3 and `assurance` (`certified`) for 4.

- **Rung 5 needs the whole population** *(Bill, 2026-11-09, message 108)*: the certified or audited ID must also be issued to all or nearly all the population, which question 5 asks. ### Study internationalbandwidth

sub_indicator: "routes" (class "route")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "routes": "independent", "two", "one", "none"
- aspect "access": "open", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**Fibre that carries the country's internet traffic abroad**, whoever owns it and whatever it is called: a submarine cable landing in the country, or for any country a fibre link to a neighbour's network that its international traffic runs over.

The study redraws `infra.connect--international-internet-bandwidth`, which as at 2026-08-31 puts seven countries at stage 4, eight at stage 3, three at stage 2 and Eritrea at stage 1, none at stage 5, leaves Angola, the Gambia, Seychelles and Uganda unplaced and 31 countries without evidence. It touches no other row.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| Fibre routes to the rest of the world | International internet bandwidth: fibre routes to the rest of the world | `infra.connect--international-internet-bandwidth` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: satellite and microwave links; a cable that passes the country without landing; a cable announced, financed or being laid but not in service; the national backbone inland, which `fibrebackbone` assesses; data centres and exchange points; bandwidth in gigabits, which is reported in the long summary and sets nothing.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the country joined to the rest of the world by fibre? | A source states that a submarine cable lands in the country and is in service, or that the country's international traffic runs over fibre to a neighbour |
| 2 | Does it have two or more such routes? | Two routes, each stated in service, on one source or two |
| 3 | Are two of them independent of each other? | Two routes that share no landing station: two cables landing at different stations, a cable and a land link through a neighbour, or for a country without a coast, links through two different neighbours |
| 3a | Does a high-capacity cable carry its traffic? | A source states that a cable of more than 100 Tbps design capacity is in service and lands in the country, or carries its traffic through a neighbour |
| 4 | Is capacity on them sold to other operators? | A source states that operators other than the owner buy capacity at the landing station or on the cable, or that it runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms |
#### 3. The ladders

**Fibre routes to the rest of the world**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the past three years says the country has no international fibre route: satellite or microwave only |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: one route |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 2: two or more routes, sharing one landing station or one neighbour, or with no high-capacity cable |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 3 and 3a: two independent routes, and a high-capacity cable carries the country's traffic |
| 5 Mature | Yes to 4: two independent routes, a high-capacity cable, and capacity sold to other operators |

- **High-capacity cables** *(Bill, 2026-10-30)*: more than 100 Tbps design capacity, the cable's whole system and not the branch. 2Africa, Equiano and Medusa count; Umoja, Waterworth and Seacom 2.0 count once a source says they carry traffic. Another cable counts where a source states its design capacity above 100 Tbps. `high-capacity.csv` names, per country, the row that shows such a cable carrying its traffic; a stager adds a line when a cell reaches question 3a.
- **A cable a source says the country "already lands" is in service** *(Bill, 2026-11-09, message 82)*: the benefit of the doubt goes to the landing. Gabon's 2Africa answers the high-capacity test.
- **A sale at a point of presence is not question 4** *(Bill, 2026-10-30)*: the capacity must be sold at the landing station, on the cable, or on open-access or regulated terms.
- **No question has a window.** A route in service stays in service until a later source says it is cut or closed; a cut that is repaired is still in service.
- **A route counts for the country it lands in, or whose traffic a source says it carries.** A cable that passes offshore does not count.
- **Question 3 was reworded in the run** to say that a cable and a land link through a neighbour share no landing station; drafters had read it as asking for two landing stations only. Every country was restaged.
- **Question 4 needs question 3.** Open access on routes that are not independent is noted and leaves the country at stage 3.
- **Stages 1 to 4 are the row as it stands.** Stage 5 asked for bandwidth per internet user in Africa's top fifth; Corpus holds that figure for no country, so no country could reach it. Capacity sold to other operators replaces it: the African Union asks for infrastructure to be shared, and a story states it.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `routes` (`independent`, `two`, `one`, `none`) for questions 1 to 3, and `access` (`open`, `none`) for question 4.

### Study internationalstandards

sub_indicator: "international" (class "adoption")

Aspects and their values:
- aspect "standing": "adopted", "member", "none"
- aspect "use": "writes", "required", "none"

#### 1. What is assessed

**Whether the country takes the world's standards for information technology and data as its own**: its standards body's membership of the international standards organisations, the international standards it has adopted as national ones, whether public bodies must meet them, and whether the country helps write them. It counts whatever the body or the rule is called.

| Sub-indicator | Indicator text | Id |
|---|---|---|
| International standards for information technology and data | Adoption of international standards: international standards for information technology and data | `gov.standards--adoption-of-international-standards` |

Anything else the evidence names is `other`, noted and never staged: a system built to an international standard, such as a passport to the aviation specification, payments moved to the ISO 20022 message format or an identity platform on an open-source one; a company's or a data centre's certificate; certification in the country, which the national standards row holds; regional standards and membership of the African or a regional standards organisation; international standards on food, goods or any other field; a regulator's rule for one sector, such as banks; membership of the International Telecommunication Union, which every country holds; a statistical or accounting standard.
#### 2. The questions

Asked in order. Each is answered with a date and a link.

| # | Question | A yes is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the country's standards body a member of an international standards organisation? | The body is named as a member of ISO, the International Organization for Standardization, or of IEC, its counterpart for electrical technology, in any class of membership |
| 2 | Has the country adopted an international standard on information technology or data as a national standard? | A named ISO, IEC or ITU standard on information technology, information security, software or data, adopted by the standards body as a national standard. One out for comment is not a yes |
| 3 | Must public bodies meet a named international standard? | A law, decree, regulation, directive or circular that names an international standard on information technology or data and requires ministries, agencies or government systems to meet it |
| 4 | Has the country taken part in writing international standards on information technology in the last three years? | A dated source naming the standards body or a public body as a participating member, chair or secretariat of an ISO or IEC committee that writes standards on information technology or data. An observer seat is not a yes |
#### 3. The ladders

**International standards for information technology and data**

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country belongs to no international standards organisation |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: the standards body is a member |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: an international standard on information technology or data is a national standard |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: public bodies must meet a named international standard |
| 5 Mature | Yes to all four: the country has taken part in writing such standards in the last three years |

- **Each question must be met on its own evidence** *(Bill, 2026-11-09, message 112; `"later_yes": false`)*: a committee seat or a rule does not show adoption, nor an adopted standard membership.
- **Questions 1 to 3 have no time limit**: a membership, a standard and a rule stand, with their year, until a dated source says they have gone. Question 4 counts for three years.
- **A statement that the country belongs to no international standards organisation counts for three years.**
- **The class of membership does not change the stage.** Full, correspondent and subscriber members all answer question 1, and the summary says which. This is a change from the row as it stands, which reads the class as a rung: a correspondent member can and does adopt international standards, so the class is not a step on the way.
- **Membership of the African or a regional standards organisation places nothing**, and is noted. So do regional standards a country adopts.
- **A catalogue or a count of adopted international standards is a yes to question 2 only where the source names one on information technology or data.** The row as it stands places Zambia on a count.
- **International standards in other fields place nothing**: food, steel, units of measurement.
- **A system built to an international standard places nothing**, and is noted. The row as it stands places Angola, Burundi and Seychelles on payments moved to ISO 20022, and Mali on its passport.
- **A certificate places nothing**, whoever holds it: certification is the national standards row's.
- **A rule that is modelled on a standard is not a rule to meet it.** Question 3 needs the source to say public bodies must comply with, or be certified to, the named standard.
- **A regulator's rule for one sector places nothing**, and is noted: a central bank requiring a security standard of banks.
- **A rule binding one government system places nothing** *(settled as worded, Bill, 2026-10-31, messages-for-bill 56)*: Guinea's transport order requiring its passenger-data system to meet ISO/IEC 27001 is insufficient; GIN stays unplaced.
- **A national committee that follows an international one is a yes to question 4 only where the source says it takes part as a participating member.**
- **A seat on, or a candidature for, a board of the International Telecommunication Union places nothing.**
- **How many standards are adopted, and whether the country favours open or technology-neutral ones, is not judged.**
- **Two more changes from the row as it stands**: its stage 4 asks for a catalogue, committee seats and a mandate together; its stage 5 is the African Union strategy's end state. Neither is a fact one story can state.

`study.json` holds this as two aspects: `standing` (`adopted`, `member`, `none`) for questions 1 and 2, and `use` (`writes`, `required`, `none`) for 3 and 4.
