## Strategies, plans and policies

### Digital transformation strategy · 2 Preparing

The final draft of South Africa's [ICT and Digital Economy Masterplan](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Digital-Economy-Masterplan-22-Feb-2021v1_updated.pdf>) is dated 22 February 2021 and covers 2020 to 2025. [Economist Impact](<https://assets.ctfassets.net/9crgcb5vlu43/X4hhlGuvESvwRgn8tmMeO/660ad2ce4b5894c6d3df3dc953856a5a/ei_report-country_profile-south_africa.pdf>) says the final version is not public, and no source found says it was adopted. The department's 2023/24 plan lists a [Digital Economy Framework and Strategy](<https://static.pmg.org.za/DCDTAnnual_Performance_Plan_2023-24_compressed.pdf>) still to go to Cabinet. No change has been found in the last twelve months. Establishing would need a strategy adopted, published and still running.

### ICT strategy · 3 Establishing

The department issued the National Integrated ICT Policy White Paper in [Government Gazette 40325 of 3 October 2016](<https://pmg.org.za/files/2601001discussionusaasa.pdf>). Its stated purpose is to use ICTs [to eliminate poverty and reduce inequality by 2030](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/digital-sovereignty-in-the-brics-countries/south-african-digital-sovereignty-at-the-crossroad-of-securitization-and-development/08C1119DE09286B84626FA49F6AD0586>). In the last twelve months it was cited and not changed: a [World Bank report of June 2026](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099653207022613768/pdf/IDU-eb3a6d23-36a4-413f-bc1e-458a577684b7.pdf>) lists it among the anchors of South Africa's policy framework. No delivery plan for the White Paper has been found, and no report against its own targets. Operating would need both, with a report published within three years.

### Broadband strategy · 3 Establishing

South Africa's broadband plan is [SA Connect, a policy of 2013](<https://www.gov.za/documents/notices/electronic-communications-act-international-mobile-telecommunications-roadmap-2>) that sets the aim of broadband for all. In July 2026 the regulator ICASA named it as the existing national policy in its [draft IMT Roadmap 2026](<https://www.icasa.org.za/news/2026/icasa-publishes-draft-imt-roadmap-and-implementation-plans>). An [August 2026 article](<https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-08-03-sas-radio-spectrum-revolution-answers-mobile-network-operators-prayers/>) gives its target as universal access at an average of 100Mbps by 2030, with no figure for delivery. No budget line, universal service fund allocation or financed programme tied to the plan has been found for the last three years. Operating would need money committed to it.

### Data storage / cloud strategy · 3 Establishing

[On 31 May 2024 the communications department gazetted the National Policy on Data and Cloud](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202406/50741gen2533.pdf>), covering national and provincial government and organs of state. It says government data shall be stored in unified, cloud-enabled data centres in designated locations. It names the State Information Technology Agency as the body that sources cloud services for government. No change has been found in the last twelve months. No government cloud or data centre that public bodies must use has been found, and no list of approved providers. Operating would need the government to name where public bodies must host.

### Data interoperability framework / roadmap · 3 Establishing

[In a reply to a question of 28 August 2025](<https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/32258/>), the Department of Public Service and Administration told Parliament it had published the Minimum Interoperability Standards. It presents them as guidance for departments. In the last twelve months, [Operation Vulindlela's report for April to June 2026](<https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/Operation%20Vulindlela%20Progress%20Report%20Q1%20-%202026.pdf>) says work progressed on updating them. The update was to be tested in priority digital services by September 2026. No instrument has been found that makes the standards compulsory or names who enforces them, and no department is reported following them. Operating would need public bodies to be required to follow the framework.

### AI strategy · 2 Preparing

South Africa has no adopted national AI strategy. The communications department put a draft AI policy out for comment in April 2026, and [withdrew it that month after fabricated references were found in it](<https://www.lawyershub.org/news/post/africa-forward-summit-2026-launching-the-africa-europe-ai-governance-report>). [In October 2026 the minister said an independent panel is preparing a replacement](<https://techcabal.com/2026/10/15/south-africa-ai-policy-hallucinations/>), which Cabinet is to finalise before the end of March 2027. [In November 2026 President Ramaphosa announced a national AI action plan within 90 days](<https://dirco.gov.za/remarks-by-deputy-minister-thandi-moraka-on-the-occasion-of-the-panel-discussion-governing-the-digital-commons-africas-agency-in-shaping-ai-for-peace-security-and-development/>), to be delivered by a team led by the Presidency. Establishing would need a strategy adopted and running.

### Data localisation policies · 3 Establishing

South Africa's rule covers government data on national security. [The communications department told Parliament on 24 February 2026](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>) that, under the National Data and Cloud Policy, such data shall be stored only in infrastructure inside the country. [A draft commented on in 2021](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/digital-sovereignty-in-the-brics-countries/south-african-digital-sovereignty-at-the-crossroad-of-securitization-and-development/08C1119DE09286B84626FA49F6AD0586>) went further and required a copy of data to be kept in the country. No change to the rule has been found in the last twelve months. No body has been found that checks compliance or grants exemptions. Operating would need a body named to enforce the rule.

### Data governance policy · 4 Operating

South Africa's [National Policy on Data and Cloud, gazetted on 31 May 2024](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202406/50741gen2533.pdf>), sets government policy on collecting, storing, using and sharing data. [On 2 April 2025 the Minister for Public Service and Administration signed a directive on data governance](<https://www.dpsa.gov.za/dpsa2g/documents/km/2025/Determination%20and%20Directive%20on%20the%20Implementation%20of%20Data%20Governance%20in%20the%20Public%20Service.pdf>), in force on signature. It requires each head of a national or provincial department to set up a data governance committee and to appoint a chief data officer. No change has been found in the last twelve months, nor any report that departments have done so. Mature would need a rule or standard issued under the policy.

### Open data policy · 2 Preparing

[Research ICT Africa says it developed an Open Data Policy Framework for South Africa](<https://researchictafrica.net/project/datacipation-shaping-harmonised-and-inclusive-digital-and-data-policies-in-africa/>) in 2026, in a project with GIZ and AUDA-NEPAD. Its project page, from September 2026, does not say who commissioned the framework or whether the government has approved it. No source found says the government has adopted it. No national open data portal has been found, and no data published under an open data policy. Establishing would need an open data policy adopted and still running.

## Legislation and regulation

### Data protection legislation · 5 Mature

South Africa's [Protection of Personal Information Act of 2013](<https://www.businessday.co.za/news/law/2026-08-17-information-regulator-goes-to-the-sca-in-new-bid-to-stop-publication-of-matric-results/>) has been [in force since 1 July 2021](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/popia-grace-period-limits-regulators-fines/P3gQ2qGAlAk7nRD1>). In 2024/25 the Information Regulator [received 1,355 complaints under it and resolved 745](<https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Information-Regulator-Annual-Report-2025-210mm-x-260mm-1.pdf>). In the last twelve months, [regulations on health information were published on 6 March 2026](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202603/54268gon7198.pdf>). [On 26 October 2026 the Regulator fined Central Johannesburg TVET College R500,000 and First National Bank R1 million](<https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/MEDIA-STATEMENT-REGULATOR-FINES-CJC-AND-FNB-FOR-POPIA-CONTRAVENTION.pdf>). [On 18 August 2026 a commissioner said work is under way to amend the Act](<https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-08-18-sahrc-sounds-alarm-over-misuse-of-personal-information-in-digital-age/>). No bill or draft of the amendment has been found.

### Cybersecurity legislation · 5 Mature

South Africa's [Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 was signed on 26 May 2021](<https://www.michalsons.com/focus-areas/cybersecurity-law/cybersecurity-bill-in-south-africa-overview>) and [came partly into operation on 1 December 2021](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/calls-for-finalisation-of-cyber-security-bill/G98YdMLGkRW7X2PD>). [A parliamentary reply of 29 November 2024](<https://www.protectionweb.co.za/cyber-security/specialist-police-investigate-cyber-fraud-prosecutions-remain-low/>) put 37 cases with 88 charges under the Act on court rolls. In the last twelve months, [the Acting Minister of Police told Parliament in October 2026](<https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/41149/>) that the Hawks had registered eight cyber fraud, extortion and ransomware cases over three years. They had made one arrest. No separate cybersecurity act exists, and [in October 2025 a Hawks cybercrime head said a Cybersecurity Bill was still to come](<https://www.gcis.gov.za/newsroom/media-releases/government-urges-vigilance-against-rising-cyber-threats>).

### Legislation covering digital id · 3 Establishing

South Africa's Identification Act of 1997 is in force. It [makes Home Affairs the sole authority keeping the National Population Register](<https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/government-amend-regulations-prevent-traffic-documents-being-used-identification>), and [its regulations were last amended on 23 June 2025](<https://www.dha.gov.za/images/gazettes/gazette-52893-230625-dha.pdf>). In May 2026 Home Affairs [gazetted draft regulations](<https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/smart-ids-go-digital-home-affairs-publishes-draft-rules/>) that [would regulate access to identity information](<https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/south-africa-gazettes-digital-id-draft-regulation-seeks-comments>); they were still drafts at the end of September 2026. A replacement bill [is due in Parliament only in 2027 or 2028](<https://techcentral.co.za/south-africa-behind-neighbours-digital-id/286057/>). No source found says the 1997 Act provides for a biometric register, a unique identity number or electronic checks. Operating would need a law in force that provides for digital identity.

### Digital payments legislation · 4 Operating

South Africa's [National Payment System Act of 1998](<https://facephi.com/observatory/en/national-payment-system-south-africa-psp-licences/>) gives the Reserve Bank responsibility for clearing and settlement. [The Reserve Bank has designated non-banks as clearing system participants under it](<https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/payments-and-settlements/regulation>), among them Yoco Technologies in 2021 and Payfast in 2024. In the last twelve months it [published a draft framework in May 2026](<https://techcabal.com/2026/10/26/south-africa-fintech-payment-network/>) that would let approved non-banks issue e-money. [On 1 September 2026 the governor said a National Payment System Bill](<https://techcentral.co.za/national-payment-system-bill-sarb-fintech/285597/>) would soon go out for comment. No rule has been found that requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a switch. Mature would need such a rule.

### Legislation enabling data interoperability · 3 Establishing

South Africa's [Public Service Regulations, 2016](<https://www.dpsa.gov.za/dpsa2g/documents/acts&regulations/regulations2016/PUBLIC%20service%20regulations%2016%20April%202019.pdf>) took effect on 1 August 2016. The Minister for the Public Service and Administration made them under the Public Service Act. Chapter 6 carries a regulation 97 headed *Minimum interoperability standards*. No change has been found in the last twelve months. No source found says the law in force requires public bodies to share data or to connect to the government's exchange platform. None says it bars them from asking a person for what another public body already holds. Operating would need a law, decree or regulation that requires public bodies to share.

### AI legislation / regulations · 1 Absent

South Africa has no law on AI. [In August 2026 a technology company's chief executive wrote](<https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/sci-tech/2026-08-28-bruce-von-maltitz-sas-ai-rules-wont-land-until-2027-waiting-is-a-choice/>) that it has no law governing AI and that national rules are expected in 2027. [In March 2026 the law firm Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr wrote](<https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Technology-Communications/corporate-and-commercial-and-technology-and-communications-alert-18-march-Social-media-and-AI-Legal-risks-and-South-Africas-response>) that it has no single law on AI or deepfakes. [A research centre wrote in August 2026](<https://www.globalcenter.ai/research/hypothetical-no-more-south-africa-s-legal-litmus-test-for-automating-welfare>) that it has no national AI policy or regulations. No change has been found in the last twelve months. Preparing would need a bill, draft decree or draft regulation on AI.

### E-commerce legislation · 3 Establishing

South Africa's law is the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act of 2002. [Home Affairs listed it in May 2025](<https://www.id4africa.com/2025/files/PS5A-S1-1-SouthAfrica_FINAL.pdf>) as the law that regulates electronic transactions, and no bill to replace it is named. [TechCentral reported on 11 September 2026](<https://techcentral.co.za/south-africa-behind-neighbours-digital-id/286057/>) that the South African Accreditation Authority accredits providers of advanced electronic signatures under it. Altron Security, TrustFactory and Impression Signatures hold accreditation, and the report gives no date for any of them. No change has been found in the last twelve months. Operating would need a regulation made under the Act.

### Statistics legislation · 3 Establishing

South Africa's statistics law is the [Statistics Act of 1999](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a6-99.pdf>). [An amendment act was assented to on 10 December 2024](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202501/51805-statisticsamendmentact29of2024.pdf>), and [the amendment was proclaimed for implementation on 1 October 2025](<https://www.gov.za/news/speeches/deputy-minister-nonceba-mhauli-stats-sa-202526-q3-report-portfolio-committee-planning>). In the last twelve months, [a parliamentary committee report of 23 June 2026](<https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Docs/atc/01ls62wgfubry77aimpfb3gfqgvq2vzzwq.pdf>) lists rolling out the amendments as work in hand for Statistics South Africa. No decree, regulation or order made under the Act has been found. Operating would need rules made under it.

### Access to information legislation · 5 Mature

South Africa's law is [the Promotion of Access to Information Act of 2000](<https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/contracts-and-commercial-law/1797238/landmark-ruling-for-private-bodies-under-paia-information-regulators-enforcement-notice-against-mining-house>). [The Information Regulator, established on 1 December 2016](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-unpacks-regulatory-complexities-posed-by-ai/KA3WwMdzPryvrydZ>), enforces it. In the last twelve months, [complaints under the Act could be lodged online from January 2026](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-strengthens-cyber-defences-as-breaches-mount/DZQ58vV8WyPMzXy2>). [On 22 May 2026 the Regulator ordered the mining company Sibanye-Stillwater](<https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/contracts-and-commercial-law/1797238/landmark-ruling-for-private-bodies-under-paia-information-regulators-enforcement-notice-against-mining-house>) to give a complainant access to compliance reports. [On 16 November 2026 it set aside a public service department's refusal of a request](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-calls-out-dpsa-in-cellphone-allowance-dispute/rW1xL75nkZPMRk6m>). No source found reports how many requests are made under the Act.

## Data protection

### Data protection authority · 5 Mature

South Africa's [Information Regulator was established on 1 December 2016](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-unpacks-regulatory-complexities-posed-by-ai/KA3WwMdzPryvrydZ) to enforce the data protection and access to information laws. [National Treasury appropriated R135.7m for it for 2025/26](https://www.treasury.gov.za/documents/National%20Budget/2025May/ene/FullENE.pdf). Its [first fine, R5 million on the justice department, came in July 2023](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/popia-grace-period-limits-regulators-fines/P3gQ2qGAlAk7nRD1). In the last twelve months, [the Polokwane High Court reduced its fine on Blouberg Local Municipality to R250,000 in April 2026](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/INFORMATION-REGULATOR-TURNS-TO-THE-COURTS-TO-FINE-BLOUBERG-LOCAL-MUNICIPALITY-FOR-CONTRAVENTION-OF-POPIA_.pdf). [In October 2026 it fined Central Johannesburg TVET College R500,000 and First National Bank R1 million](<https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/MEDIA-STATEMENT-REGULATOR-FINES-CJC-AND-FNB-FOR-POPIA-CONTRAVENTION.pdf>). [On 11 September 2026 Parliament invited comment on candidates for its vacant positions](https://www.parliament.gov.za/press-releases/media-statement-justice-committee-calls-public-input-candidate-cvs-information-regulator).

### National data protection readiness · 5 Mature

[South Africa's data protection law came into force on 1 July 2021](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-unpacks-regulatory-complexities-posed-by-ai/KA3WwMdzPryvrydZ>). [The African Union's list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records that South Africa signed the Malabo Convention on 16 February 2023, with no ratification. The Information Regulator [has received over 8,000 reports of security compromises](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-concerned-by-alarming-rate-of-data-breaches/rxP3jqBE2ZLMA2ye>), 1,220 of them since April 2026. In August 2026 it [gave the South African Bureau of Standards 90 days](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-concerned-by-alarming-rate-of-data-breaches/rxP3jqBE2ZLMA2ye>) to revise its policies and put security measures in place. [A commissioner said in August 2026 that the law is to be amended](<https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-08-18-sahrc-sounds-alarm-over-misuse-of-personal-information-in-digital-age/>); no bill or draft has been found.

## Regional collaboration

[In June 2025 South Africa's ICT minister signed a declaration of intent on digital cooperation with Eswatini](<https://techafricanews.com/2025/06/30/eswatini-and-south-africa-sign-digital-cooperation-pact-to-accelerate-regional-transformation/>), covering policy harmonisation, broadband, cybersecurity and data protection. ### Regional legal harmonisation · 2 Preparing

[The African Union's register of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records that South Africa signed the Malabo Convention on cyber security and personal data protection on 16 February 2023. It records no ratification. The convention has been in force since 8 June 2023, with 20 instruments deposited among the Union's 55 members. No change has been found in the last twelve months. No national law giving effect to a shared African legal text has been found for South Africa. Establishing would need South Africa to ratify the convention.

### Shared regional infrastructure · 4 Operating

Mozambique's research network MoRENet [has a border point in Cape Town](<https://africarxiv.ubuntunet.net/bitstreams/9427cb02-feef-4b3f-b03b-84cb4efea3cb/download>), connected to South Africa's research networks TENET and SANReN. It carries Mozambique's international capacity of 60 Gbps, and was described as operational in October 2025. [That month WACREN announced a 10 Gbps link from Lagos to the ZAOXI exchange point in Cape Town](<https://wacren.net/en/newsletter/africas-research-and-education-networks-achieve-historic-lagos-cape-town-interconnection/>), through the same networks. No change has been found in the last twelve months. Mature would need a second kind of shared infrastructure in service, such as an exchange point or data centre shared under an agreement between national bodies.

### Cross-border data transfers · 3 Establishing

[South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act](<https://infotrust.com/articles/south-africas-protection-of-personal-information-act-popia/>) bars sending personal information abroad unless the recipient is under adequate legal protection. Transfers may also go on the person's consent or where a contract requires it, with no authorisation step. [The same source, of January 2024](<https://infotrust.com/articles/south-africas-protection-of-personal-information-act-popia/>), says the Act names no countries with adequate protection and no way to recognise them. The sender judges adequacy itself. No change has been found in the last twelve months. Operating would need a published route that needs no case-by-case judgment: a list of countries, model contract terms or a general authorisation.

## Standards

### National interoperability standards · 3 Establishing

The Department of Public Service and Administration [told Parliament on 28 August 2025](<https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/32258/>) that it had published the Minimum Interoperability Standards. In the last twelve months, [Operation Vulindlela's report of 31 July 2026](<https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/Operation%20Vulindlela%20Progress%20Report%20Q1%20-%202026.pdf>) says the standards were being updated between April and June 2026. The update was due to be tested in priority digital services by September 2026, and no source found says it has been issued. The 2023 Public Service Regulations [require each department's IT officer to ensure its systems interoperate](<https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/32258/>), without naming these standards. Operating would need public bodies to be required to use them.

### National quality standards · 3 Establishing

South Africa's standards body is the South African Bureau of Standards, whose board acts under the Standards Act of 2008. In the last twelve months, [Notice 3890, gazetted on 17 April 2026](<https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202604/54520gen3890.pdf>), listed a second edition of SANS 19784-1, the national standard on the programming interface for biometric applications. It is listed among revised information technology standards. No source found says the bureau or an accredited certifier offers certification against a national standard on information technology or data. Operating would need such certification on offer in the country.

### Adoption of international standards · 2 Preparing

South Africa's standards body, SABS, [takes part in ISO/IEC JTC 1](<https://www.iso.org/member/1485.html?t=PT&view=participation>), the joint committee on information technology. It also takes part in the subcommittees on information security, biometrics and artificial intelligence. South Africa was listed among the participating members at [the committee's plenary in Chengdu in November 2025](<https://jtc1info.org/sd-2-history/jtc-1-plenaries/jtc-1-plenary-50/>). No source found names an international standard on information technology or data adopted as a national standard. Nor has a rule been found that requires public bodies to meet one. Establishing would need an international standard adopted as a national one.
