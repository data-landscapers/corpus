# Angola

## National identity law

**Law in force.** Angola's identity card rests on Lei n.Âº 4/09, the law on civil identification and the citizen's identity card. The National Assembly amended it [in August 2017](<https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2017/lei-n-o-20-17-de-31-de-agosto/>) and again [in January 2021](<https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2021/lei-n-o-3-21-de-25-de-janeiro/>), each in force on publication. On 29 April 2026 the Council of Ministers [reviewed a civil and criminal identification bill](<https://angop.ao/noticias/economia/estado-angolano-encaixa-receitas-de-9-36-bilioes-de-kwanzas-para-oge-2026/>). It would gather the rules on the identity card and criminal records into one law. [Committees discussed it on 26 October 2026](<https://www.rna.ao/pages/new.php?uid=236573>), and on 30 October [the Assembly approved two of its chapters in detail](<https://parlamento.ao/Noticia_72422>).

**Digital identity covered and rules made.** Under the law as amended, each card's [number acts in law as the unique citizen number](<https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2021/lei-n-o-3-21-de-25-de-janeiro/>). The card carries an encrypted QR code. Since 2017 it has also [carried a microprocessor](<https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2017/lei-n-o-20-17-de-31-de-agosto/>). The chip holds the holder's tax, social security, birth-record and voter numbers, so that public and private bodies can read them.
