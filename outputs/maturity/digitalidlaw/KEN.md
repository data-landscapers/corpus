# Kenya

## National identity law

**Law in force.** Kenya has [no stand-alone digital identity law](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/data-and-policy/article/an-assessment-of-the-legal-and-regulatory-framework-supporting-the-implementation-of-the-national-integrated-identity-management-system-niims-in-kenya/617BC651E2C09DE2CDD175C73A1C90E2>). Its digital identity system, the National Integrated Identity Management System, began under executive order no. 1 of 2018, in which the president directed a central master population database to be built. The government then wrote the system into the 1947 Registration of Persons Act by an omnibus amendment, and no Act of its own was passed for it. The High Court ruled in January 2020 that an appropriate and comprehensive regulatory framework should be enacted before the system was rolled out.

**Digital identity covered and rules made.** With no identity law of its own in force, none provides for a digital identity. The system is [anchored on the Huduma Namba regulations of 2020](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/data-and-policy/article/an-assessment-of-the-legal-and-regulatory-framework-supporting-the-implementation-of-the-national-integrated-identity-management-system-niims-in-kenya/617BC651E2C09DE2CDD175C73A1C90E2>), and civil society organisations objected that regulations cannot create a system of that weight. The inserted section 9A has the system keep a national population register and assign each person a unique number. Kenya therefore answers the first question only.
