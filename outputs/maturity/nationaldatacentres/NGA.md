# Nigeria

## Nationally owned data centres for hire

**In service, Tier III, and two of them.** [Galaxy Backbone, a government-owned technology services company, operates an Uptime Institute Certified Tier III national shared services centre and data centre in Abuja, per its communications head, who says it would host financial institutions' data](<https://techcabal.com/2026/07/07/cbns-local-data-order-puts-nigerias-data-centres-to-test/>). [21st Century Technologies' Lagos data centre is Tier IV certified and holds a Capitoline Certificate of Conformance for Constructed Facilities; it names no Uptime Institute certificate](<https://techeconomy.ng/shaping-the-future-of-data-infrastructure-21st-century-technologies-tier-iv-data-centre>). Together these place two nationally owned data centres for hire at Tier III or above in service, the first three of the study's questions.

**Owned in the country.** [Rest of World describes Galaxy Backbone as a privately held company established by the Nigerian government, which provides cloud services to government agencies and private companies and runs two data centres, one in Abuja and one in Kano; the article gives no further ownership detail](<https://restofworld.org/2025/aws-google-cloud-nigeria-alternatives/>). Owned in the country means by the state, a state enterprise or a company owned mostly in the country; a facility a foreign group controls is not counted.

**The state's data hosted nationally.** [The Galaxy Backbone data centre in the National Shared Services Centre, Abuja, commissioned on 20 December 2022, serves government as well as corporate customers](<https://www.thecable.ng/a-data-center-to-drive-the-digital-economy/>).
