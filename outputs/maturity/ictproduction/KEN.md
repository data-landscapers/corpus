# Kenya

## ICT exports and production

**ICT service exports, as a share of GDP.** In 2024, [ICT service exports from Kenya were 0.7088 per cent of GDP](<https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/BX.GSR.CCIS.CD>), by the World Bank's series of computer and telecommunications services sold abroad. The same series [put the share at 0.8872 per cent in 2021, 0.9097 per cent in 2022 and 0.7063 per cent in 2023](<https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/BX.GSR.CCIS.CD>). Since 2021 the share fell by 0.18 points. At 0.5 per cent or more, the share is past the study's highest line for exports. The figure counts computer and telecommunications services sold abroad, not equipment made or assembled at home.

**ICT equipment made in the country.** [East Africa Device Assembly Kenya, backed by Safaricom, made 700,000 devices in the year to March 2026](<https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/safaricom-backed-firm-assembles-700-000-devices-in-a-year-5504228>). Its plant at Athi River makes smartphones and tablets. [M-KOPA's Nairobi factory, opened with HMD in January 2023, had made over 1.5 million phones by November 2024](<https://www.m-kopa.com/newsroom/m-kopa-spurs-local-smartphone-assembly-and-job-creation-in-kenya>). It employed over 325 people. [ITWeb reported in November 2026 that the Athi River plant can build 1.2 million to 3 million low-cost 4G smartphones a year](<https://itweb.africa/article/afdb-backs-local-tech-assembly/j5alrvQAXgxvpYQk>). [In August 2026 the Universal Service Advisory Council noted progress at the JKUAT Device Assembly Plant](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/07/kenya-moves-to-boost-digital-inclusion-through-locally-assembled-devices/>). [In October 2026 an industry analyst called the Athi River firm a natural first buyer for African-made chips](<https://www.digitaleconomy.ke/post/the-fifth-layer-what-will-it-take-for-africa-to-make-its-chips>).
