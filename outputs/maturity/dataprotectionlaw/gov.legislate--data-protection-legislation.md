# Data protection legislation: general law

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The norm is the African Union's Malabo Convention: Article 8(1) commits each party to a legal framework for personal data, Article 13 sets the principles and Articles 16 to 19 the rights. **It names the subject and measures nothing**: it sets no test of whether a law is applied, and matching its principles is a reading of two texts, not a dated fact. Ratification is the flag.

## The ladder

| Stage | Standing | Regulations | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that the country has no general data protection law, with none on record as enacted or in preparation | | |
| 2 Preparing | `announced`, `drafting`, `tabled` or `passed` | Any | Any |
| 3 Establishing | `enacted`, `transition` or `in-force` | Any | Any |
| 4 Operating | `in-force` | Any | `applied` or `reported`, dated within three years |
| 5 Leading | `in-force` | `issued` | `reported`, for a year ending within two years |

*Advancing.*

- La Nation, 5 September 2026, treats the Code du numérique, Article 379, as the framework that currently governs personal data processing in Benin, including employer access to work messaging ([source, 2026-09-05](https://www.lanation.bj/societe/societe-votre-patron-peut-il-lire-vos-messages-whatsapp-professionnels)).

- Seventeen officers of Benin's data protection authority (APDP) were sworn in before the Court of First Instance of Cotonou on 14 September 2026 under Article 468(2) of the Digital Code, on the instruction of the APDP president ([source, 2026-09-14](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/7230/17-apdp-benin-officers-sworn-in-to-strengthen-commitment-to-personal-data-protection)).
