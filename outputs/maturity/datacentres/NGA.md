# Nigeria

## Data centres for hire in service

**In service, Tier III, and two of them.** [Nigeria has at least two data centres for hire in service rated Tier III or IV: Galaxy Backbone's Uptime Institute Certified Tier III Abuja centre and Certified Tier IV Kano centre, plus OADC's Tier-III certified Lagos colocation hall (as claimed by the operator)](<https://techcabal.com/2026/07/07/cbns-local-data-order-puts-nigerias-data-centres-to-test/>). [ThisDay reports Open Access Data Centres CEO Ayotunde Coker saying banks and financial services companies are OADC's clients and that it can host more data in its facilities](<https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/10/15/as-cbn-begins-data-migration-local-hosting/>). [Open Access Data Centres, a WIOCC subsidiary, has said it invested more than $500 million over eight years in Nigerian digital infrastructure including data-centre and cloud facilities](<https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/the-next-bottleneck-for-african-digital-identity-is-infrastructure-not-software>). Together these place two data centres for hire at Tier III or above in service, which answers the first three of the study's four questions. Capacity is given where a source states it; it sets no stage.

**A global cloud region.** [Digital Realty Nigeria's CEO Ike Nnamani, speaking at TSSF 7.0 (Lagos, 16 September 2026), said no single hyperscaler, among them Amazon, Oracle, Meta and Microsoft, operates a data region in Nigeria](<https://nigeriacommunicationsweek.com.ng/2026/09/20/business-remarks-charges-stakeholders-on-rethinking-nigerias-digital-infrastructure-strategy-tssf-70>). Only a live region of a public cloud provider answers this question; an announced region, a content cache or an edge site does not.
