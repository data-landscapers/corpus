# Non-governmental contribution to national policy: consultation on digital law and policy

*As at 2026-10-18.*

## The norm

The African Charter on Democracy, Elections and Governance, Articles 12, 27 and 28, commits its parties to create conditions for civil society to take part and to build partnership and dialogue between government, civil society and the private sector. The Malabo Convention, Article 24, asks each party to draw up its cybersecurity policy with stakeholders, and the African Commission's Declaration of Principles on Freedom of Expression, Principle 17, asks for regulation made with them. None says how. This study asks whether a country asks, publishes what it hears, is required to ask and seats outsiders on a standing body; it does not measure a country against any of the three texts.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says a named digital law or policy was made without consulting people outside government, or that they were refused a part in it |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: people outside government were asked |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: what they said was published |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a consultation was held because a rule required it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: a standing body with outside members gave its view |
