# Sudan

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** No. [The Sudanese Center for Policy Studies reported on 19 July 2026](<https://sudancps.com/en/2026/07/19/sudans-privacy-gap-balancing-digital-transformation-and-data-laws-intro/>) that no statute governs cross-border transfer of data. Mobile banking data passes through infrastructure Sudan neither owns nor regulates.

**Open without permission each time.** Not answered, because no rule on sending data abroad was found. [Global Voices wrote on 21 October 2023](<https://advox.globalvoices.org/2023/10/21/the-ongoing-conflicts-impact-on-ict-and-digital-transformation-efforts-in-sudan/>) that Sudan has no law imposing data localisation.

**Published way to send data without asking.** None. With no transfer rule there is no list of countries, model terms or general authorisation. [The 2026 report](<https://sudancps.com/en/2026/07/19/sudans-privacy-gap-balancing-digital-transformation-and-data-laws-intro/>) only proposes localisation as a first step for new legislation.

**Open to African partners.** Not yet. [The African Union status list](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records that Sudan signed the Malabo Convention on 15 March 2023. Signature is not ratification.

***Also on file***. [Global Voices](<https://advox.globalvoices.org/2023/10/21/the-ongoing-conflicts-impact-on-ict-and-digital-transformation-efforts-in-sudan/>) reports a former senior Central Bank official saying government bodies work under an undeclared localisation policy.

***Not found***. No source found of a law or regulation on sending personal data abroad. No ratification of the Malabo Convention is on record.
