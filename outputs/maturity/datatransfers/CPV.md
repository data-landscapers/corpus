# Cape Verde

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** [Law 41/VIII/2013](<https://www.cnpd.cv/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lei-n-41_VIII_2013-regime-juridico-geral-de-proteccao-de-dados-pessoais-das-pessoas-singulares.pdf>), published on 17 September 2013 and in force thirty days later, allows transfer abroad only to a country with adequate protection. The authority, the CNPD, decides which states are adequate. Article 20 sets other grounds, such as consent.

**Open without permission each time.** [DLA Piper states](<https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=CV&t=law>) that data may go to a country with adequate protection, or otherwise on the person's consent or in listed situations such as contract necessity or vital interests. Some transfers go without permission each time. No source found says a rule keeps personal data in general at home.

**Published way to send data without asking.** No dated source says the CNPD has published its list of adequate countries, model contract terms or a general authorisation. The nearest fact is [article 19](<https://www.cnpd.cv/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lei-n-41_VIII_2013-regime-juridico-geral-de-proteccao-de-dados-pessoais-das-pessoas-singulares.pdf>), which gives the CNPD the decision on adequacy.

**Open to African partners.** Cape Verde has ratified the Malabo Convention. [The African Union list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records ratification on 13 November 2020 and deposit on 5 February 2022.
