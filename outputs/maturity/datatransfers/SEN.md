# Senegal

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** Yes. Article 9 of [Law 2008-12](<https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/693598/data-security-and-cybercrime-in-senegal>) bars a data owner from sending data to a country that does not give sufficient protection. [A law-firm note of 11 May 2018](<https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/693598/data-security-and-cybercrime-in-senegal>) reports the rule.

**Open without permission each time.** Not answered. The same [note](<https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/693598/data-security-and-cybercrime-in-senegal>) says the data protection authority must be informed and its authorisation sought. It names no route that avoids authorisation.

**Published way to send data without asking.** None found. The nearest held fact is the [2018 note](<https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/693598/data-security-and-cybercrime-in-senegal>) on the authorisation step. No list of countries or model terms is on file.

**Open to African partners.** Yes. [The African Union status list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records that Senegal ratified the Malabo Convention on 3 August 2016.

***Also on file***. [Georgetown University wrote in 2021](<https://africachinainitiative.georgetown.edu/research-working-group/blog-posts/huawei-helping-or-hindering-african-countries-secure-their-data-sovereignty-2/>) that the president ordered state data repatriated to the national data centre in June 2021. It cites no legal instrument.

***Not found***. No source found saying some transfers go without the authority's authorisation. No list of countries, model terms or general authorisation is on record.
