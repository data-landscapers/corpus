# Morocco

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** Morocco's [law 09-08](<https://rm.coe.int/16806fdcb3>) protects transfers of personal data to foreign countries, in particular where the country does not provide an adequate level of protection. Articles 43 and 44 set the rule. The Council of Europe's committee described them in October 2012.

**Open without permission each time.** Yes. The law tests the receiving country's protection, so data may go to countries judged adequate without asking each time. The rules on [hosting sensitive data in Morocco](<https://www.anrt.ma/sites/default/files/2022-02/loi_05-20_cybersecurite_fr.pdf>) cover vital entities and infrastructures and not personal data in general.

**Published way to send data without asking.** Yes. [Morocco's 2015 adequacy list](<https://dial.global/africas-digital-future-seamless-data-flows/>) names the countries personal data may be sent to. A comment by the DIAL research group records it.

**Open to African partners.** No. The list of 2015 did not recognise a single African country. The [African Union status list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) shows no signature or ratification of the Malabo Convention.

***Also on file***. A [2021 law on sensitive data](<https://en.hespress.com/112805-morocco-overtakes-south-africa-as-africas-top-data-center-hub.html>) is reported to require local storage. The report names no law.

***Not found***. No source found says Morocco has ratified the Malabo Convention or treats an African partner's protection as adequate.
