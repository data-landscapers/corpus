# Cote d'Ivoire

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** [Decree 2015-79 of 4 February 2015](<https://www.artci.ci/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/decret_2015_79.pdf>), issued under law 2013-450, requires prior authorisation from the telecoms regulator, ARTCI, to transfer personal data to a third country. The destination must give equal or higher protection. Articles 6 and 8 set sanctions for non-compliance.

**Open without permission each time.** Every transfer waits on ARTCI. The decree requires prior authorisation even for a destination with equal protection, and names no route without it. The [decree](<https://www.artci.ci/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/decret_2015_79.pdf>) is the only source found on the point.

**Published way to send data without asking.** No list of countries, model contract terms or general authorisation was found. The nearest fact is the [same decree](<https://www.artci.ci/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/decret_2015_79.pdf>), which sets authorisation as the route for each transfer.

**Open to African partners.** Côte d'Ivoire has ratified the Malabo Convention. [The African Union list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records ratification on 8 March 2023 and deposit on 3 April 2023.

***Also on file***. [The national data governance strategy SNGD 2030](<https://www.telecom.gouv.ci/new/uploads/publications/174196707541.pdf>) was published 1 January 2024. It sets as a principle that data classed as critical information infrastructure be processed and stored in the country.

***Not found***. No source found says any transfer goes without ARTCI's prior authorisation. No source found says ARTCI has published a list of countries, model contract terms or a general authorisation.
