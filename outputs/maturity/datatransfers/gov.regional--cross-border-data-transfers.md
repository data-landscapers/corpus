# Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

*As at 2026-10-31.*

## The norm

The African Union's Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (the Malabo Convention), article 14, bars sending personal data to a country outside the Union unless that country protects it adequately. The Union's Data Policy Framework, of 2022, and the digital trade protocol of the African Continental Free Trade Area, of 2024, ask states to let data move across borders under stated safeguards. This study asks whether the rule protects personal data sent abroad while letting it go: no rule, a rule keeping data at home and a rule that makes every transfer wait on permission all fall short, and recognising African partners is the top of the ladder.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | Closed or unprotected: a rule in force keeps personal data in general in the country, with no transfer rule on record; or a dated source says nothing protects personal data sent abroad |
| 2 Preparing | Protected but closed: a transfer rule is being written; or one is in force but every transfer needs the authority's permission, or a rule keeping personal data in general at home stands beside it |
| 3 Establishing | Protected and open: yes to 1 and 2 |
| 4 Operating | Workable: yes to 1, 2 and 3 |
| 5 Leading | Open to Africa: yes to all four |

## Countries

### Mauritania: 5 Leading

Mauritania bars transfers abroad without sufficient protection, publishes a list of adequate countries since July 2025, and ratified the Malabo Convention in 2023.

- Articles 20 to 25 of Loi n° 2017-020 (Journal Officiel no. 1400, dated 22 July 2017) bar a data controller from transferring personal data to a third country unless it ensures a sufficient level of protection, with exceptions for one-off non-massive transfers on express consent or on stated necessity, and with authorisation by the data protection authority (APD) on guarantees such as contract clauses; the document is the enacted text and gives no repeal ([source, 2017-07-22](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/112890/MRT-112890.pdf)).
- Deliberation 005/2025 of the data protection authority (APD), adopted 30 July 2025, requires public- and private-sector controllers using cloud services abroad to obtain prior APD authorisation, with a separate foreign-transfer authorisation request where the host country is not on the country list published in deliberation 004/2025. The text is the amended version published 2026-04-30 ([source, 2025-07-30](https://www.apd.mr/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%85%d8%af%d8%a7%d9%88%d9%84%d8%a9-%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85-005-2025-%d8%aa%d8%aa%d8%b9%d9%84%d9%91%d9%82-%d8%a8%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b4%d8%b1%d9%88%d8%b7-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%af%d9%86%d9%8a%d8%a7-%d8%a7/)).
- Deliberation 004/2025 of the APD, adopted 14 July 2025 under article 73 of law 2017-020, sets the conditions, rules and procedures for transferring personal data abroad, with article 21 of the law having the APD publish and update a list of countries it judges adequate ([source, 2025-07-14](https://www.apd.mr/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%85%d8%af%d8%a7%d9%88%d9%84%d8%a9-%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85-004-2025-%d8%aa%d8%ad%d8%af%d8%af-%d8%b4%d8%b1%d9%88%d8%b7-%d9%88%d9%82%d9%88%d8%a7%d8%b9%d8%af-%d8%a5%d8%ac%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%a1%d8%a7/)).
- NotreAfrik says the Malabo Convention reached its 15-ratification threshold and entered into force on 8 June 2023, thirty days after Mauritania's ratification, so Mauritania had ratified by about May 2023; the article gives no exact date for it ([source, 2023-05](https://notreafrik.com/convention-de-malabo-ratifications/)).

### Morocco: 4 Operating

Morocco's law 09-08 protects transfers abroad and its 2015 adequacy list names countries data may go to; none are African.

- The Council of Europe Consultative Committee states that Moroccan law 09-08 (articles 43 and 44) lays down protection of transfers of personal data to foreign countries, in particular where the country does not provide an adequate level of protection; dated 18 October 2012, the document does not give the law's date ([source, 2012-10-18](https://rm.coe.int/16806fdcb3)).
- The DIAL expert comment states that Morocco's 2015 adequacy list, a list of countries personal data may be sent to, did not recognise a single African country; the year is the only date given ([source, 2015](https://dial.global/africas-digital-future-seamless-data-flows/)).

### Angola: 3 Established

Since 2011 a transfer needs notice to the agency if the destination is adequate, authorisation if not; no published list is on file.

- Lei n.º 22/11, arts 33-34, makes transfer of personal data abroad to a country with adequate protection subject to notification to the APD, and to a country without it subject to APD authorisation on stated grounds such as the data subject's written consent; art. 67 puts the law in force on publication, 17 June 2011 ([source, 2011-06-17](https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2011/lei-n-o-22-11-de-17-de-junho/)).

### Cape Verde: 3 Established

Transfers go to adequate countries or on consent and listed grounds since 2013; no published list is on file. Cape Verde ratified Malabo in 2020.

- DLA Piper states that Cape Verde's Data Protection Law (Law 133/V/2001 as amended, including by Law 41/VIII/2013 and Law 121/IX/2021) permits international transfer of personal data only to a country with an adequate level of protection, or otherwise with the data subject's consent or in listed situations such as contract necessity or vital interests; the document gives no date for the rule, so its published date of 2025-01-16 is used ([source, 2025-01-16](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=CV&t=law)).
- Lei n.º 41/VIII/2013, published 17 September 2013 and in force thirty days later, amends the general data protection law (Lei 133/V/2001) so that article 19 allows transfer abroad only to a country assuring an adequate level of protection, the CNPD deciding which foreign states do, and article 20 lets the CNPD permit transfer elsewhere on consent, contract necessity, public interest, vital interests or a public register, or authorise it where the controller gives sufficient guarantees such as adequate contractual clauses ([source, 2013-10](https://www.cnpd.cv/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lei-n-41_VIII_2013-regime-juridico-geral-de-proteccao-de-dados-pessoais-das-pessoas-singulares.pdf)).

### Comoros: 3 Established

### Djibouti: 3 Established

The Code Numérique, published September 2025, allows transfers abroad with equivalent protection, on consent or by contract; no published list is on file.

### Somalia: 3 Established

- Organic Law 2004-63 of 27 July 2004, arts. 50-52, bars transfer of personal data abroad where it harms public security or Tunisia's vital interests, allows it only to a country with an adequate level of protection, and requires prior authorisation of the data protection authority (INPDP) in every case, decided within one month; the document notes no replacement law had been adopted at its 2026 retrieval ([source, 2004-07-27](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/)).

### Zambia: 2 Nascent

Zambia enacted a data protection law in 2021 with transfer conditions, but no start date is on file; it ratified the Malabo Convention in 2020.

- Zambia's Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021, enacted 24 March 2021, sets conditions in Part X (sections 70-71) for sending personal data outside the Republic: data subject consent plus Commissioner-approved standard contracts or intragroup schemes, or a Ministerial prescription of permissible transfers on an adequate-level-of-protection test, or Commissioner approval of a transfer in necessity; sensitive personal data must stay in the Republic; the Act itself says it commences on a date the Minister appoints by statutory instrument, and the document gives no commencement date ([source, 2021-03-24](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf)).

### Zimbabwe: 2 Nascent

Zimbabwe's 2024 guideline bars transfers abroad unless the authority is notified and conditions are met; whether any transfer needs no permission is not shown.

- POTRAZ, the designated data protection authority, issued guideline CDPG 5 of 2024 under sections 6(1)(a) and 28(3) of the Cyber and Data Protection Act, effective from publication, barring data controllers from sending personal data abroad unless they have notified the Authority and met stated conditions (impact assessment, data subject consent, adequate protection in the destination country, a data-sharing agreement) ([source, 2024-11-13](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/05%20Guideline%20on%20cross%20border%20transfer.pdf)).
