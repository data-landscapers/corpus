# Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The African Union's Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (the Malabo Convention), article 14, bars sending personal data to a country outside the Union unless that country protects it adequately. The Union's Data Policy Framework, of 2022, and the digital trade protocol of the African Continental Free Trade Area, of 2024, ask states to let data move across borders under stated safeguards. This study asks whether a rule exists, is in force, has published means of complying and has been applied; it does not judge the rule.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no rule on sending personal data abroad and none is being written |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a rule being written |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a rule in force |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: the authority has published the means of complying |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: the rule has been applied in the last three years |

### Angola: 5 Leading

Angola's 2011 law requires notice or the regulator's authorisation to send data abroad; a firm was fined in 2026; no source found reports guidance.

- Valor Económico reported on 26 June 2026 that the APD fined Crescer Tech about USD 505,000 (c. 549 million kwanzas) for no notification or prior authorisation and an unauthorised international transfer to foreign cloud servers, and Fast Digital Center USD 112,000 (c. 110 million kwanzas) for inadequate security measures and no notification; deliberation dates are not given, so the publication date is used ([source, 2026-06-26](https://valoreconomico.co.ao/artigo/apd-aplica-multas-milionarias-a-empresas-por-armazenarem-informacoes-pessoais-sem-autorizacao)).
- Presidential Decree 60/21 of 10 March 2021, the fee regulation of Angola's data protection agency (APD), lists an act of authorisation for international data transfer at a fee of Kz 350,450, so the APD's permission was a condition for sending personal data abroad; the document gives no date of effect other than its publication date, 2021-03-10 ([source, 2021-03-10](https://lex.ao/docs/presidente-da-republica/2021/decreto-presidencial-n-o-60-21-de-10-de-marco/)).

- Regulation 22(3) of the Telecommunications (Subscribers Identification and Registration) Regulations 2020 (Statutory Instrument No. 35, Gazette of 6 November 2020) says subscribers' registration information shall not be transferred outside Sierra Leone without prior approval by the National Telecommunications Commission, and 22(4) requires any request for approval to justify the purpose; the document is dated 6 November 2020 and gives no commencement date or later repeal ([source, 2020-11-06](https://www.parliament.gov.sl/uploads/statutory_instruments/S.%20I.%20Subscribers%20Identification%20and%20Registration%20Management%20Regulations,%202020.pdf)).

### Somalia: 3 Established

Somalia's 2023 data protection law bars sending personal data abroad without adequate protection or a stated exception; no source found reports published guidance and enforcement.

- Articles 30 and 31 of Law No. 005 of 2023 bar a data controller from transferring personal data outside Somalia unless the recipient country, organisation or sector gives adequate protection, the recipient is bound by a law, binding corporate rules or contractual clauses giving it, or a stated exception such as the person's consent or a contract applies; the law takes effect on adoption by Parliament, promulgation by the President and publication in the Official Bulletin, and the document's date is the presidential signature date, taken as a proxy ([source, 2023-03-22](https://moct.gov.so/en/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Somalia-Data-Protection-Act-2.pdf)).

### South Africa: 3 Established

Since July 2020 the law bars sending personal data abroad without adequate protection or consent, but no approved country list or guidance is known.

- The document states that POPIA prohibits transfers of personal information outside South Africa unless the recipient is under adequate legal protection, or the data subject consents, or a contract or the data subject's interest requires it; POPIA took effect in July 2020 with a grace period to 30 June 2021 (event date taken from the commencement it states) ([source, 2020-07](https://infotrust.com/articles/south-africas-protection-of-personal-information-act-popia/)).

### Tanzania: 3 Established

Tanzania's 2023 data protection law sets rules for sending personal data abroad; no source found says whether the authority has published guidance or country lists.

### Mozambique: 2 Nascent

### Zambia: 2 Nascent

Zambia's Data Protection Act, passed in 2021, sets conditions for sending personal data abroad; no source found says whether it has come into effect.

- Zambia's Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021, enacted 24 March 2021, sets conditions in Part X (sections 70-71) for sending personal data outside the Republic: data subject consent plus Commissioner-approved standard contracts or intragroup schemes, or a Ministerial prescription of permissible transfers on an adequate-level-of-protection test, or Commissioner approval of a transfer in necessity; sensitive personal data must stay in the Republic; the Act itself says it commences on a date the Minister appoints by statutory instrument, and the document gives no commencement date ([source, 2021-03-24](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%203%20The%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202021_0.pdf)).
