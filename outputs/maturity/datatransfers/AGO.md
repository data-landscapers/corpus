# Angola

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Rule in force.** Angola's [Law 22/11 on personal data protection](<https://lex.ao/docs/assembleia-nacional/2011/lei-n-o-22-11-de-17-de-junho/>) came into force on publication, 17 June 2011. Articles 33 and 34 make sending personal data abroad to a country with adequate protection subject to notification to the data protection agency (APD), and to a country without it subject to APD authorisation on stated grounds such as the data subject's written consent. [Hunton reported in 2011](<https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/angola-passes-personal-data-protection-law>) that the APD must approve transfers to countries without adequate protection. The APD's fee regulation, [Presidential Decree 60/21 of 10 March 2021](<https://lex.ao/docs/presidente-da-republica/2021/decreto-presidencial-n-o-60-21-de-10-de-marco/>), lists an act of authorisation for international data transfer at Kz 350,450.

**Means of complying published and rule applied.** The APD [fined Crescer Tech US$505,000 in 2026](<https://apd.ao/ao/gca/index.php?id=218&preview=1>) for failing to notify it and obtain authorisation for the international transfer of personal data to a cloud located abroad. [Valor Económico reported on 26 June 2026](<https://valoreconomico.co.ao/artigo/apd-aplica-multas-milionarias-a-empresas-por-armazenarem-informacoes-pessoais-sem-autorizacao>) a fine of about USD 505,000 (c. 549 million kwanzas) on the company for no notification or prior authorisation and an unauthorised international transfer to foreign cloud servers.
