# Botswana

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Rule in force.** The Data Protection Act has been in effect since January 2025. [Mmegi reported on 25 November 2025](<https://www.mmegi.bw/business/govt-moves-to-in-house-local-data/news>) that it allows data transfers abroad only to approved countries. The Act was published in the [Extraordinary Gazette of 29 October 2024](<https://www.datalaw.africa/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Extraordinary-Gazette-29-10-2024.pdf>) as the Data Protection Act, 2024.

**Means of complying published and rule applied.** Botswana keeps a list of countries that data may go to. [A DIAL expert comment published on 15 June 2026](<https://dial.global/africas-digital-future-seamless-data-flows/>) says the list held only two African countries. Under the rule [Mmegi reported in November 2025](<https://www.mmegi.bw/business/govt-moves-to-in-house-local-data/news>), data may go to the countries on that list and to no others. A country that is not on the list cannot receive data sent from Botswana under the Act.

***Also on file***. The [Agricultural Information Management System (AIMS)](<https://www.mmegi.bw/news/botswana-moves-to-strengthen-agricultural-data-integration/news>), which the country calls AIMS, run by the SADC Secretariat with FAO and the EU, and by the Ministry of Lands and Agriculture for Botswana. A requirement that a copy of the data remain in Botswana, [reported by Mmegi on 25 November 2025](<https://www.mmegi.bw/business/govt-moves-to-in-house-local-data/news>).

***Not found***. No source from the last three years names a country approved to receive data. None names an organisation given or refused permission, ordered to comply or penalised for sending data abroad.
