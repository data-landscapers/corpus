# Tunisia

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Rule in force.** Tunisia's [Organic Law 2004-63 of 27 July 2004](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>), in articles 50 to 52, bars sending personal data abroad where it would harm public security or Tunisia's vital interests. It allows a transfer only to a country with an adequate level of protection. In every case it requires the prior authorisation of the data protection authority, the INPDP, which must decide within one month. The [source](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>) notes that no replacement law had been adopted when it was retrieved in 2026. [A Tunisian lawyer wrote in October 2026 that the 2004 regime for transfers abroad is outdated](<https://www.elair-avocat.com/en/news/gdpr-tunisian-law-ai-obligations-2026/>), and that [organic bill No. 095/2025, before the Assembly's Rights and Freedoms Committee, contains a revised framework for them](<https://www.elair-avocat.com/en/news/gdpr-tunisian-law-ai-obligations-2026/>).

**Means of complying published and rule applied.** The published means of complying is a list of countries judged adequate. [A DIAL article of 15 June 2026](<https://dial.global/africas-digital-future-seamless-data-flows/>) says Tunisia's 2018 adequacy list of countries for data transfers identified only five African countries. The [2004 law](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>) leaves the authorisation of each transfer to the INPDP, the authority it names as owner of the rule.

***Not found***. No source from the last three years names a country approved to receive data, an organisation given or refused permission, or an order or penalty for sending data abroad.
