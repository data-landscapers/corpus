# Tunisia

## Rule on sending data abroad

**Protected when sent abroad.** Yes. Articles 50 to 52 of [Organic Law 2004-63 of 27 July 2004](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>) allow transfer of personal data abroad only to a country with an adequate level of protection. They bar it where it harms public security.

**Open without permission each time.** No. The [same law](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2004-63-du-27-juillet-2004-portant-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>) requires prior authorisation of the authority, INPDP, in every case, decided within one month. No replacement law had been adopted at its 2026 retrieval.

**Published way to send data without asking.** No. [DIAL wrote on 15 June 2026](<https://dial.global/africas-digital-future-seamless-data-flows/>) that Tunisia's 2018 adequacy list names only five African countries. Authorisation is still required for each transfer.

**Open to African partners.** Not yet. [The African Union status list of 2 February 2026](<https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf>) records that Tunisia signed the Malabo Convention on 23 April 2019 and has not ratified.

***Also on file***. [Lawyer Zied El Air wrote in 2026](<https://www.elair-avocat.com/en/news/gdpr-tunisian-law-ai-obligations-2026/>) that organic bill 095/2025 contains a revised framework for international transfers. [Decree-law 2022-2](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/decret-loi-n-2022-2-du-4-janvier-2022-portant-organisation-de-lactivite-du-renseignement-de-credit/>) bars credit information companies from moving their databases abroad.

***Not found***. No source found of transfers going without authorisation under the 2004 law. No ratification of the Malabo Convention is on record.
