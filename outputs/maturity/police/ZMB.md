# Zambia

## Station records

**Governance, planning, finance.** The Zambia Police Strategic Plan 2023-2026 [gives guidelines for an e-record management system for the service's electronic records](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>), by a University of Zambia study. The service's Technical Services Directorate, the Ministry of Home Affairs and Internal Security and the SMART Zambia Institute [build and run the police's electronic systems](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>).

**Records made digitally.** In 2024 [the occurrence books, crime register books and docket movement books were kept on paper at headquarters and across the service](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>). Headquarters had [a Police Information Management System, a database of police records including crime reports and case files](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>), officials told the study between November 2024 and January 2025. UNDP said in November 2021 that [an Electronic Occurrence Book had been rolled out to the Victim Support Unit](<https://www.sadc.int/latest-news/eu-committed-fighting-sgbv-sadc-region>).

**How the record is made.** Statements [are recorded and dockets opened by hand, so complainants and witnesses attend a station in person](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>), informants told the study between November 2024 and January 2025.

**Last twelve months.** The SMART Zambia Institute [received routers, switches, access points and firewalls for 40 police stations, to connect them to a centralised digital system](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/02/03/smart-zambia-drives-police-digitalisation-with-new-tech-rollout-to-40-stations/>), as reported on 3 February 2026.

**Stations doing the digital entry.** In 2024 [the occurrence book, crime register and docket were digitised nowhere in the service, and rollout from headquarters to divisions and stations was planned](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>).

***Noted, not assessed***. All at headquarters unless stated, by [the University of Zambia study](<https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.7.1.89>): for the public, police e-services on ZamPortal and online payment of fines; identification, Interpol motor vehicle clearance, police clearance by fingerprint and a Biometric Identification System; single-function, firearm certificates and cross-border permits; surveillance, CCTV cameras and the Safe City cameras and National Command Centre in Lusaka; personnel, the police cooperative's e-thrift management system, introduced in 2023; statistics, a Crime Intelligence Analysis Centre, planned; enabling, computers and Digital Communication Systems. Also identification: [cyber-investigation and forensics tools for the Forensics and Cybercrime Department](<https://zambia24.com/2026/04/22/new-cyber-tools-for-police/>), [the headquarters forensic department](<https://www.boz.zm/sites/default/files/2026-06/Zambanker_March_2026.pdf>) and [a DNA laboratory](<https://www.sadc.int/latest-news/eu-committed-fighting-sgbv-sadc-region>). Also enabling: [the Zambia Police Service LAN and Last Mile Connectivity Project, for 40 stations](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/02/03/smart-zambia-drives-police-digitalisation-with-new-tech-rollout-to-40-stations/>), and [the Government Wide Area Network, which had connected 27 of a target of 442 police stations and posts in 2022](<http://www.szi.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Final-National_e-Government_Plan_-2023-Final-17.08.2023.pdf>).

***Not held***. No fact shows a station entering a report or a case digitally, and who enters the records held in the headquarters database is not stated. The centralised system the 40 stations are to join is not yet in place, with no pilot, station count or source of finance given for it.
