# Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

Africa CDC's Digital Transformation Strategy (2023) and PHC Digitalisation Framework (2025–26): 100,000 facilities connected by 2030 and 90 per cent digitally enabled primary care by 2035.

**It does not measure what either sub-indicator measures.** It counts connection and *digitally enabled*, which a clinic with a tablet and paper registers meets. **The review bears this out**: most of the 15 coverage shares it found count facilities where a system is deployed, not clinics entering their own data. Countries publish the norm's kind of figure. The ladders borrow only the 90 per cent line, for stage 5, and the pages say so.

## The ladder

| Stage | Tiers | Where digitised | Primary clinics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that routine reporting has no digital entry at any level | | |
| 2 Nascent | Hospitals only, or some districts; or a national rollout contracted | Anywhere, or not stated | None, or pilot sites |
| 3 Established | Any: a system keyed at district takes the clinics' reports | At the district, from clinics' paper forms | Facility entry in a minority, or not published |
| 4 Operating | The primary tier, T3 or T4 | At the facility | More than half enter their own reports, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | The primary tier | At the facility | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years, with reporting completeness published |

## Countries

### Togo: 4 Operating

96.5% of all health facilities entered complete reports into the national system in 2021, the ministry says; there is no separate figure for primary clinics.

*Advancing; Community integrated.*

- The health ministry's monitoring and evaluation plan for the PNDS 2023-2027, validated on 6 October 2023, requires each health facility to key its validated monthly or quarterly activity report (RMA/RTA) into DHIS2 before the 5th of the following month, the facility's SNIS respondent doing the entry, and to send the paper copy to the prefectoral health directorate (DPS) by the same date; it also sets the 20th as the deadline at DPS level for the reports to be entered in DHIS2 ([source, 2023-10-06](https://extranet.who.int/uhcpartnershiplivemonitoring/system/files/doc_attachments/Final_Plan%20de%20suivi%20%26%20%C3%A9valuation%20PNDS%202023-2027.pdf)).
- The plan (validated 6 October 2023) names the facilities producing and keying SNIS reports as the peripheral care units (USP), where the facility head is the SNIS respondent, and the CHP, CHR and CHU hospitals, where a data or statistics officer is; it says the CHR and CHU data are taken into account by the health district in whose area they sit ([source, 2023-10-06](https://extranet.who.int/uhcpartnershiplivemonitoring/system/files/doc_attachments/Final_Plan%20de%20suivi%20%26%20%C3%A9valuation%20PNDS%202023-2027.pdf)).
- The health ministry's plan gives the SNIS report completeness rate as 96.5% in 2021, defined as the number of health facilities that keyed a complete SNIS report into DHIS2 over the total number of health facilities due to key SNIS reports, sourced to the health statistics yearbook; it prints the rate only, without the counts, covers all facilities and not primary clinics alone, notes duplicate entries in hospital data as a known bias, and gives timeliness of 77.3% in 2021 ([source, 2021](https://extranet.who.int/uhcpartnershiplivemonitoring/system/files/doc_attachments/Final_Plan%20de%20suivi%20%26%20%C3%A9valuation%20PNDS%202023-2027.pdf)).

### Angola: 3 Established

Clinics report on paper, and municipal or provincial offices enter the figures into the national reporting system (2025).

*Advancing.*

- The article states that on the DHIS2 platform data are collected and reported on paper at health facilities, then aggregated and entered into the system at municipal or provincial level; no date is given, so the article's publication date is used ([source, 2025-06-25](https://doi.org/10.5937/smclk6-58193)).
- The article states that health facilities collect and report their data in paper format, with entry into DHIS2 made at municipal or provincial level; it gives no count or share of facilities entering their own reports, and no date, so the article's publication date is used ([source, 2025-06-25](https://doi.org/10.5937/smclk6-58193)).

### Benin: 3 Established

Health zone offices entered clinics' paper reports into the national system in 2022; how many clinics enter their own is not published.

*Community integrated.*

- The Ministry of Health's 2019 statistical yearbook states that health facilities send standard paper reports to the health zone before the 5th of each month and the zone keys them before the 20th, entry being done in every health zone and, since September 2014, on the single DHIS2 platform for aggregated facility statistics ([source, 2019](https://www.instad.bj/images/docs/insae-publications/annuelles/AS-INSAE/Archive/Annee_2019/Annuaire_Statistique_Sant%C3%A9_2019.pdf)).
- The Ministry of Health's 2019 statistical yearbook states that the SNIGS collects data monthly, quarterly and annually from the country's health facilities on standard paper forms, naming a monthly return for health centres (B5a) and one for hospitals (B5b) and reporting indicators for the national teaching hospital (CNHU); it names no tier below the health centre ([source, 2019](https://www.instad.bj/images/docs/insae-publications/annuelles/AS-INSAE/Archive/Annee_2019/Annuaire_Statistique_Sant%C3%A9_2019.pdf)).
- The health ministry's 2022 statistical yearbook says SNIGS collects facilities' data on standardised paper forms, that a facility sends its paper report to the zone sanitaire before the 5th of each month and the zone keys it before the 20th, and that entry is done in all zones sanitaires, since September 2014 on the single DHIS2 platform, described as the tool for entering, validating and analysing facilities' aggregate statistics ([source, 2022](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/front-office-resources/production/uploads/publication/attachment/46e5f76a-5f8f-4780-9d32-d51df5c239c2/02be6fdb-3d72-4984-adba-f696163051fd.pdf)).
- The health ministry's 2022 statistical yearbook says that certain health facilities and community relays with adequate equipment key their completed monthly forms directly into the data warehouse by the 20th of each month, as an exception to entry at the zone sanitaire; it gives no number of such facilities ([source, 2022](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/front-office-resources/production/uploads/publication/attachment/46e5f76a-5f8f-4780-9d32-d51df5c239c2/02be6fdb-3d72-4984-adba-f696163051fd.pdf)).
- Abt Global names one private facility, the Minonkpo Clinic in Cotonou, as recording its data directly into DHIS2 since 2020, against 752 private health facilities in the country; it gives no count of facilities entering their own data, and the publication date is used ([source, 2023-11-22](https://www.abtglobal.com/insights/impact-briefs/pshpa-trains-private-health-facilities-to-submit-rmnch-data-for-dhis2)).

### Botswana: 3 Established

All public health facilities reported into the national system in 2016-18; they report on paper (2025), and it is unclear where the figures are entered.

- WHO's 2024-2025 report lists heavy reliance on paper-based reporting by facilities as a challenge and recommends revising the DHIS2 dashboards and trackers 'to enable onsite data capturing', so entry is not at the facility; the report does not name the level at which the paper reports are keyed (no figures given) ([source, 2025](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2026-05/WHO%20Botswana%20Biennial%20Report%202024-2025_web.pdf)).
- MEASURE Evaluation's country profile, as of August 2019 and on an in-country contact, records a national deployment of DHIS2 used for aggregating health data from all facilities within the country ([source, 2019-08](https://www.measureevaluation.org/his-strengthening-resource-center/country-profiles-1/botswana.html)).
- MEASURE Evaluation's country profile gives 100% of public health facilities as reporting through DHIS2, for 2016 to 2018, on the 2016-2018 Master Facility List and an in-country contact; it gives no numerator or denominator, and does not say where the reports are keyed ([source, 2018](https://www.measureevaluation.org/his-strengthening-resource-center/country-profiles-1/botswana.html)).

### Burkina Faso: 3 Established

Clinics and hospitals reported into the national system in 2024, entered at district or facility; the share of clinics entering their own is not published.

*Advancing.*

- The Ministry of Health's strategy lists, among innovations in the health system, the scaling-up of decentralised entry of routine data in health facilities; it gives no count of facilities and no date, so the document's date is used, and it does not name the system in that sentence ([source, 2025-01](https://data.inafran.ru/files/Doc/Burkina%20Faso/Strategiceskij%20plan%20razvitia%20cifrovogo%20zdravoohranenia%20na%202025-2029%20gg.pdf)).
- The Countdown to 2030 country team states that health centres ('centres de santé', also 'établissements de santé') report monthly through DHIS2 on antenatal care, deliveries, vaccination, outpatient consultations and hospitalisations, and that 83% of expected monthly facility reports were received nationally in 2024; it does not define the tier of the facilities counted ([source, 2024](https://www.countdown2030.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Burkina-Faso_Full-Report_CAM2025_LAST-FINAL.pdf)).
- The Ministry of Health's 2024 statistical yearbook states that health facilities' activity reports go to the district health information and epidemiological surveillance centres (CISSE), and hospital units' reports to the hospital information services (SPIH/SIM), by the 5th of the following month, and that district and hospital data managers have until the 20th to key them into the online platform Endos-BF and check their quality ([source, 2024](https://www.wakatsera.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/annuaire-statistique-2024-du-ministere-burkinabe-de-la-Sante.pdf)).
- The Ministry of Health's 2024 statistical yearbook states that Endos-BF is also keyed by head nurses of health posts (infirmiers chefs de poste), and that in the health districts implementing decentralised entry the heads of health facilities enter their monthly activity reports themselves by the 5th of the following month; it does not say how many districts do so ([source, 2024](https://www.wakatsera.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/annuaire-statistique-2024-du-ministere-burkinabe-de-la-Sante.pdf)).
- The 2024 yearbook states that Endos-BF takes the monthly activity reports of health facilities, public and private, headed by infirmiers chefs de poste, and of the units of the regional and university hospitals (CHR/CHU), validated by each regional health directorate by the 25th of the following month ([source, 2024](https://www.wakatsera.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/annuaire-statistique-2024-du-ministere-burkinabe-de-la-Sante.pdf)).
- The 2024 yearbook's methodological note says facility heads key their own monthly reports in the districts implementing decentralised entry, and gives no number or share of facilities or districts doing so in the passages read ([source, 2024](https://www.wakatsera.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/annuaire-statistique-2024-du-ministere-burkinabe-de-la-Sante.pdf)).

### Burundi: 3 Established

Health centres enter their own reports into the national reporting system (2023), but no figure shows how many of them do.

*Advancing; Community integrated.*

- The ministry's yearbook states its 2023 data were collected from public, faith-based, associative and private health facilities through the national DHIS2 platform, with report forms set by level of care (community, health centre, hospital), and tabulates 2023 DHIS2 data for health centres, district hospitals and national hospitals such as CHU Kamenge and HPRC ([source, 2023](https://extranet.who.int/uhcpartnershiplivemonitoring/system/files/doc_attachments/Annuaire%20des%20Statistiques%20Sanitaires%202023.pdf)).
- The yearbook's method note says the community health workers' monthly report goes to the health centre for entry, that entry of reports in DHIS2 must be finished by the 10th of the following month with the facility head validating, and that facility and district SIS officers make corrections in DHIS2; for 2023 it gives community reports entered in DHIS2 at 98.9% completeness and 89.8% timeliness nationally. A second passage has facility monthly reports reaching the district office within 10 days without saying in what form ([source, 2023](https://extranet.who.int/uhcpartnershiplivemonitoring/system/files/doc_attachments/Annuaire%20des%20Statistiques%20Sanitaires%202023.pdf)).
- The World Bank assessment states that facilities still record data by hand in standard paper registers, and that entry into DHIS2 is then done at the facility: by the deputy head of the health centre, who alone is allowed to key it, and by the hospital information officer directly in DHIS2; no date is given, so the report's date is used ([source, 2023-03](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099050923073520082/pdf/P17512808c1cbf01009a830d30eb4cb8732.pdf)).
- The World Bank assessment gives no count or share of health centres keying their own reports; it gives report completeness in DHIS2 of more than 95% for health centres and 68% for hospitals, and timeliness of 40 to 60%, citing the UGADS progress report at 30 June 2022 ([source, 2022-06-30](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099050923073520082/pdf/P17512808c1cbf01009a830d30eb4cb8732.pdf)).

### Cameroon: 3 Established

The national system is in use countrywide with entry at the facility (2022 ministry plan), but no figure shows how many primary clinics do so.

*Advancing.*

- The national statistics institute states that DHIS2 carries aggregate patient data from the Monthly Activity Reports of all health facilities across the national territory, and uses those reports for 2018 to 2022; it does not break the facilities down by level ([source, 2022](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Rapport-sur-les-statistiques-de-letat-civil-au-Cameroun_version-finale-13-03-2024-1.pdf)).
- The ministry's plan describes DHIS2 as the national health data repository and a nationwide generic HMIS fed by the monthly activity report of health facilities, in a country it counts as having 197 health districts and 6,200 facilities, and refers to a separate hospital form; it does not break use down by facility level ([source, 2022-04](https://biblio-minsante.cm/server/api/core/bitstreams/7f4eeb8b-67fe-4692-b639-3c43e8a3580c/content)).
- The Ministry of Public Health's DHIS2 plan states that a nationwide generic HMIS is in place for aggregate data with data entry at the facility level, scoring aggregate data 70% in a maturity self-assessment of 117 indicators, while facilities still fill paper registers and monthly reporting booklets that the plan budgets to reprint for 6,200 facilities; dated to the plan, April 2022 ([source, 2022-04](https://biblio-minsante.cm/server/api/core/bitstreams/7f4eeb8b-67fe-4692-b639-3c43e8a3580c/content)).
- The Ministry of Public Health's family health directorate gives completeness of monthly activity reports in DHIS2 as 96% nationally and timeliness as 89% for October to December 2025 (lowest in the East, 83% and 70%; North 100% and 98%), read from DHIS2 on 23 February 2026; it gives no count or share of facilities entering their own reports ([source, 2025-12](https://biblio-minsante.cm/server/api/core/bitstreams/2bc60008-7482-4a7c-842d-15f112a2efd7/content)).

### Cape Verde: 3 Established

The national reporting system operates in every public health facility (February 2024), but no figure shows how many primary clinics enter their own reports.

- The Ministry of Health's terms of reference state that DHIS2, which manages aggregated health information mainly for epidemiological surveillance and has been implemented for routine epidemiological management since November 2018, is in operation in all structures of the national health service, which it lists as two central hospitals, four regional hospitals, 32 health centres, 34 health posts and 114 basic health units among others; no date is given for the position, so the document's published date is used ([source, 2024-02](https://www.mf.gov.cv/documents/20126/0/Terms%2Bof%2BReference%2B-ugpe.pdf/b0d39856-c19f-5ca9-2081-a9cdeb683c0a?t=1706880078938)).
- The Ministry of Health's terms of reference say DHIS2 for aggregated data is in production in all SNS structures, which places the application in the structures themselves; it does not say in so many words who keys the reports, and the document is undated so the published month is used ([source, 2023-05](https://backend-ugpe.gov.cv/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/ToR-TA-FOR-THE-UPGRADE-OF-THE-HEALTH-INFORMATION.pdf)).

### Central African Republic: 3 Established

The national reporting system runs in all 35 districts (2025), but only 21.4% of 14 facilities checked in 2024 entered data electronically.

- The poster states that DHIS2 has been deployed in all 35 health districts, with data managers recruited and offline and VSAT solutions used for connectivity; it does not say that facilities enter their own reports, and describes reporting systems before the reform as fragmented and mostly paper ([source, 2025](https://community.dhis2.org/t/dhis2-pour-la-reconstruction-du-snis-cas-de-la-rca/72669)).
- The IMPULSE study (Journal of Global Health 2025) found that 21.4% of the 14 health facilities it assessed in the Central African Republic used a functional electronic system for routine data entry and analysis, against over 75% in Ethiopia, Tanzania and Uganda; the 14 were hospitals and health centres giving emergency obstetric care in Bangui and health regions 1, 2 and 7, chosen for access and security, with data collected between November 2022 and July 2024 ([source, 2024-07](https://jogh.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/jogh-15-04295n.pdf)).
- The IMPULSE study found a functional electronic system for data entry and analysis in 83.3% of the 6 district health offices it assessed in the Central African Republic, against 21.4% of the 14 facilities, and states that all data offices used DHIS2 for key data analysis; data collected between November 2022 and July 2024 ([source, 2024-07](https://jogh.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/jogh-15-04295n.pdf)).
- The plan states that data managers are in post in the 35 health districts, the 7 health regions and 5 central hospitals for keying SNIS data, and refers to facilities' SNIS reports arriving at the district; undated, so the document's published date is used ([source, 2020-11-12](http://sante.gouv.cf/sites/default/files/2020-12/15.Plan%20de%20relance%20de%20la%20vaccination%20de%20routine%20en%20RCA_Bangui12112020.pdf)).

### Chad: 3 Established

District offices enter health centres' monthly paper reports into the national system (Gavi audit, 2024); how many centres enter their own is not known.

*Advancing.*

- Gavi's programme audit, on site in August 2024, records that in one district visited the district immunisation officer receives the monthly activity reports of 16 health centres and keys them into DHIS2 himself, and that in N'Djamena Centre district (14 health centres, 7 of them private) entry into DHIS2 is likewise done at the district from the monthly vaccination reports the health centres send ([source, 2024-08](https://www.gavi.org/sites/default/files/document/audit/Programme-audit-report_Chad-2024-FRENCH.pdf)).
- The DHIS2 programme (University of Oslo) reports that, a year after the June 2021 pilot in Moyen-Chari and Batha and following the official national launch of DHIS2 in November 2022, 96.4% of Chad's districts were entering their data directly into DHIS2; no numerator or denominator is given ([source, 2022-11](https://dhis2.org/fr/chad-hmis-transition/)).
- PNDS 4 states that data are collected daily in health facilities on primary tools (registers and forms) and compiled into monthly activity reports (RMA), refers to the entry of the RMA being decentralised to the health districts, and lists extending DHIS2 to the level of the health centres as an action still to be carried out ([source, 2022-11](https://extranet.who.int/cpcd/sites/default/files/public_file_repository/TCD_Chad_Plan-national-de-d%C3%A9veloppement-sanitaire-%28PNDS4%29_2022-2030.pdf)).
- The Countdown to 2030 country team reports facility reporting rates above 90% since 2020 and the share of districts whose monthly reports are at least 90% complete over a year rising from 78% in 2019 to 83% in 2023, with 2023 completeness held down by hospitals and by some hard-to-reach districts without internet coverage; it gives no share or count of health centres entering their own reports ([source, 2023](https://www.countdown2030.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/CHARTBOOK-TCHAD-2024.pdf)).

### Comoros: 3 Established

The national reporting system reached all 91 public health facilities (WHO, 2025), but it is not known where their reports are entered.

- DHIS2 states that almost 99 percent of public health facilities already used DHIS2 for health service reporting before the eLMIS project launched in 2024; the share is of all public facilities (the document counts 74 sites), not of primary clinics alone, with no numerator given and no statement of where the reports are keyed ([source, 2024](https://dhis2.org/supply-chain-management-dhis2-open-msupply-comoros/)).
- WHO Comoros reports DHIS2 extended to 91 public-level health structures in 2025, which it gives as 100% of public-level structures; the figure is of all public structures the system reaches, not of primary clinics alone, and the report does not say whether each structure keys its own reports ([source, 2025](https://afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2026-05/OMS%20Comores%20_%20Rapport%20annuel%20des%20r%C3%A9sultats%202025%20%20PUBLICATION.pdf)).
- WHO Comoros states DHIS2 reached all 91 public-level health structures (100%) in 2025 and calls this a national extension; the report names no tier, so every tier is read from 'all public structures' only ([source, 2025](https://afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2026-05/OMS%20Comores%20_%20Rapport%20annuel%20des%20r%C3%A9sultats%202025%20%20PUBLICATION.pdf)).
- The World Bank's results framework for project P175840 counts 69 public health facilities reporting data in DHIS2 at 31 August 2024, from a baseline of 0 in May 2021 and against a target of 80 by December 2025; it gives no denominator and does not split primary clinics from hospitals ([source, 2024-08-31](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099111924122078581/pdf/P175840-1a3a0438-2d5c-487f-8960-7e60d83dfb5b.pdf)).
- The Ministry of Health's strategy of July 2021 says the national health information system (SNIS) is semi-electronic, computerised at the level of the regions on GESIS, with data passing from districts to regions by hand or by e-mail; the keying level it names is the region, above the district ([source, 2021-07](https://extranet.who.int/cpcd/sites/default/files/public_file_repository/com_comoros_strategie-nationale-de-sant-numerique_2022-2026.pdf)).

### Congo: 3 Established

All 52 health districts enter clinics' paper reports into the national system (2024); no figure shows how many clinics enter their own.

*Advancing.*

- WHO states that DHIS2 has been used since 2020 as the national warehouse 'at the peripheral level' for routine health data, enabling electronic aggregation, with data completeness of 87% and timeliness of 47.9% in 2024; the document does not say whether entry is at the facility or the district office ([source, 2024](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2025-04/9789290314417-fre.pdf)).
- The PNDS states that data are produced on paper as compiled reports in nearly all public, private and faith-based facilities and sent to the health districts for entry, all 12 departments and 52 districts being trained on DHIS2 and given a modem and three years of internet; facility completeness is above 87% and timeliness 48.94%; undated, so the published date is used ([source, 2024-09-01](https://sante.gouv.cg/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/PLAN-NATIONAL-DE-DEVELOPPEMENT-SANITAIRE-DU-CONGO-2023-2026_VERSION-OFFICIELLE.pdf)).
- The PNDS states that standard collection tools exist in health centres (CSI), district referral hospitals (HRD) and private facilities but not general hospitals, whose forms are configured in DHIS2 with monthly reporting from hospitals still to begin; health posts are not mentioned; undated, so the published date is used ([source, 2024-09-01](https://sante.gouv.cg/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/PLAN-NATIONAL-DE-DEVELOPPEMENT-SANITAIRE-DU-CONGO-2023-2026_VERSION-OFFICIELLE.pdf)).
- The PNDS states that facilities produce their reports on paper and the health districts key them into DHIS2, and that paper collection tools remain much used while the computer tools provided are not often used; no count of facilities entering their own data is given; undated, so the published date is used ([source, 2024-09-01](https://sante.gouv.cg/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/PLAN-NATIONAL-DE-DEVELOPPEMENT-SANITAIRE-DU-CONGO-2023-2026_VERSION-OFFICIELLE.pdf)).

### Cote d'Ivoire: 3 Established

In 2020 departmental health offices entered clinics' paper reports into the national system; nothing more recent shows where reports are entered.

*Community integrated.*

- PMI Measure Malaria states that health centres which collect and report health data on paper management tools still have no access to the information in DHIS2, for want of computers, internet and reliable power, and that health structures send their monthly reports to the districts: in its 20 supported districts 85% of structures sent them on time and, in the third quarter of 2022, 75% of districts reached 100% completeness of monthly reports and all reached 99% ([source, 2022](https://measuremalaria.cpc.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CI-Qualite-des-Donnees-et-Util_FS-23-619-PMI_FR_508.pdf)).
- The Plan National de Développement Sanitaire 2021-2025 says the ministry keeps both paper and electronic tools for regular reporting, that DHIS 2 is deployed in every departmental health directorate and only a few first-contact establishments, and that district reports reached 100% completeness and 98.23% timeliness in 2020; the deployment statement is undated, so the date is the plan's published date ([source, 2021-10](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/CIV_Cote-d-Ivoire_Plan-National-de-D%C3%A9veloppement-Sanitaire_2021-2025.pdf)).
- The health ministry's RASS 2020 describes the reporting chain: first-contact establishments (ESPC), general hospitals and regional hospital centres each draw up a monthly activity report from the registers care providers fill in, the reports go to the departmental health directorate, and there the district's epidemiological surveillance officer keys all the data of the reports received from the ESPC into the DHIS2 database ([source, 2020](https://www.snisdiis.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Rapport-Annuel-sur-la-Situation-Sanitaire-RASS-2020-VF.pdf)).
- By the RASS 2020's account of data collection, the first-contact establishments (ESPC) do not enter their own reports: all the data of the monthly reports received from the ESPC are keyed into DHIS2 at the departmental health directorate by the district's epidemiological surveillance officer ([source, 2020](https://www.snisdiis.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Rapport-Annuel-sur-la-Situation-Sanitaire-RASS-2020-VF.pdf)).
- The RASS 2020 states that the data of the referral hospitals, the general hospitals and regional hospital centres, are keyed into DHIS2 by the hospitals' own data managers, while the reports of first-contact establishments are keyed at the departmental health directorate ([source, 2020](https://www.snisdiis.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Rapport-Annuel-sur-la-Situation-Sanitaire-RASS-2020-VF.pdf)).

### DR Congo: 3 Established

Health zone offices enter facilities' paper reports into the national system (2021), which covers 75.3% of all health facilities (2022).

*Advancing; Community integrated.*

- The Ministry of Public Health's Countdown to 2030 analysis report, signed 14 August 2022, states that the DHIS2 database lists 16,010 health facilities of which 12,050 report in DHIS2 (7,046 public, 2,596 private, 1,699 faith-based, 709 unclassified), the remaining 25% being private facilities that do not; this is a share of all health facilities whose monthly reports are in DHIS2, not of primary clinics, and the report does not say who keys them; it gives completeness of expected monthly facility reports as 84% in 2017 rising to 96% in 2020 and 2021 ([source, 2022](https://www.countdown2030.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Rapport-technique-actualise-RDC_Atelier-danalyse-donnees-SRMNEA-Nut_14.08.2022.pdf)).
- A HISP evaluation that visited 5 health zones in Kinshasa and Haut-Katanga in the last quarter of 2021 states that facilities send their reports on paper to the health zone office, where they are entered into the SNIS, the national DHIS2 platform; decentralised entry exists in some large facilities with reliable power and internet, but most entry is done centrally in the health zones and most facilities have no access to the platform ([source, 2021](https://dhis2.org/drc-data-use/)).

- The Ministry of Health's methodology for its 2024 annual statistics states that patient data is recorded at the point of care in HMIS registers and tally sheets, aggregated by the facility each month, and then entered into the DHIS2 platform at council level, for data covering January to December 2024 from 11,805 public and private health facilities ([source, 2024](https://www.moh.go.tz/storage/app/uploads/public/698/4e8/b7b/6984e8b7b067c606076637.pdf)).
- The May 2019 guidelines describe MTUHA as collecting routine data from dispensaries, health centres and council hospitals through the councils, with regional referral hospitals supervised by the ministry, while national, zonal and specialised hospitals and private hospitals use different data collection platforms ([source, 2019-05](https://www.moh.go.tz/storage/app/uploads/public/661/fe3/71d/661fe371de96f490413146.pdf)).

### Uganda: 3 Established

Every level reported into the national system in 2023/24, but in 2026 a mid-level health centre carried its report to the district office.

*Advancing; Community integrated.*

- At the 11 August 2026 launch, the Uganda Muslim Medical Bureau said many Health Centre IIIs lack computers and connectivity to submit reports, with Ziiru Health Centre III carrying its report physically to Buvuma district headquarters, and the in-charge of St Francis Kijjaguzo Health Centre III spoke of a transition still to be made from the manual Health Management Information System to the ministry's digital tools ([source, 2026-08-11](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/health/ucc-launches-digital-skilling-for-health-cent-NV_239098_082026)).
- The study describes paper HMIS registers (maternity, outpatient, inpatient) capturing patient data at user points in the 265 surveyed HC IVs and hospitals, later aggregated and entered into DHIS2, which it calls the system used to capture aggregated data at health facility level; undated in the passages, so the publication date is used ([source, 2025-10-10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12512544/)).
- The Ministry of Health reports that 6,546 health facilities were coded in DHIS2 in FY 2023/24 (52.4% government, 14.8% private not-for-profit, 32.8% private) and publishes DHIS2 figures for that year by level from national and regional referral hospitals through general hospitals and HC IVs to HC IIIs and HC IIs and clinics, for example 672,747 deliveries at HC IIIs and 127,371 at HC IIs and clinics; it notes many private facilities do not report through DHIS2 ([source, 2024-06](https://library.health.go.ug/sites/default/files/resources/Annual%20Health%20Sector%20Performance%20Report%20%20FY%202023_24.pdf)).
- The Ministry of Health states that in FY 2023/24 decentralising data entry to facilities continued, with daily checks of data entered in DHIS2 alerting district biostatisticians to follow up on reporting; it gives no count of facilities entering their own data, and reports a monthly stock-status (HMIS 105-6) reporting rate of 87% in June 2024 ([source, 2024-06](https://library.health.go.ug/sites/default/files/resources/Annual%20Health%20Sector%20Performance%20Report%20%20FY%202023_24.pdf)).

### Zambia: 3 Established

District offices enter paper reports into the national system each month, with a few facilities entering directly (April 2024).

- Gavi's programme audit states that the Ministry of Health's DHIS2 is the national system for recording and reporting health information, including aggregate immunisation data from routine service delivery points, that the programme relies heavily on paper, and that data entry into DHIS2 is done monthly at the district level with a few health facilities reporting directly; the statement is undated, so the report's date of April 2024 is used (audit fieldwork was in August and September 2023) ([source, 2024-04](https://www.gavi.org/sites/default/files/about/governance/audit/Programme-audit-report-Zambia-April-2024.pdf)).
- Gavi's programme audit says only that 'a few' health facilities report directly into DHIS2, the rest being keyed at the district; it gives no count or share, though it cites 3,320 health facilities countrywide from a 2022 cold chain inventory; undated statement, the report's date of April 2024 used ([source, 2024-04](https://www.gavi.org/sites/default/files/about/governance/audit/Programme-audit-report-Zambia-April-2024.pdf)).
- The Countdown to 2030 country team (University of Zambia and Ministry of Health) states that 99% of expected monthly facility reports (mean of antenatal care, delivery, immunisation and outpatient forms) were in DHIS2 in 2024, against 93% in 2019, with 99% of districts at 90% completeness or more; it gives no count or share of facilities entering their own reports and does not say where the data are keyed ([source, 2024](https://data.gffportal.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/Zambia-COUNTDOWN.pdf)).
- The paper describes the HMIS as collecting monthly reports from all facilities under the districts: rural health posts, rural and urban health centres, and district and provincial hospitals, with up to 21 paper registers in smaller facilities and 25 in some district hospitals and the provincial hospital at the time of the 2019 interviews; entry into DHIS2 is at the district office, not at these facilities ([source, 2019-06](https://www.ovid.com/journals/hpop/fulltext/10.1093/heapol/czad077~identifying-barriers-to-the-production-and-use-of-routine)).
- The authors, from 37 interviews in May and June 2019, state that facilities aggregate patient data each month onto paper reports and submit them to district health offices, where information officers key them into DHIS2; some larger health centres and hospitals aggregate electronically before submitting to the district office ([source, 2019-06](https://www.ovid.com/journals/hpop/fulltext/10.1093/heapol/czad077~identifying-barriers-to-the-production-and-use-of-routine)).

### Zimbabwe: 3 Established

All levels of the health system report on paper, and district offices enter the figures into the national system (2023).

- The U.S. President's Malaria Initiative profile states that monthly HMIS data collection is paper-based at village, health facility and hospital levels, and that the paper records go to the district, where the district health information officer enters them into the Zimbabwe DHIS2; no date is given, so the document's published year is used ([source, 2023](https://mesamalaria.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ZIMZABWE-Malaria-Profile-PMI-FY-2024.pdf)).
- PMI states that monthly HMIS collection at health facility level is on paper, keyed at the district; its routine table gives 1,776 health facilities reporting into the HMIS/DHIS2 in 2022 (1,758 in 2018) with 96% of expected monthly reports received (97.2% in 2018), which counts reports received and not facilities entering them ([source, 2022](https://mesamalaria.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ZIMZABWE-Malaria-Profile-PMI-FY-2024.pdf)).
- PMI states that the HMIS collects routine aggregate health information from four levels of the health system: primary (over 1,700 health centres, clinics and rural hospitals), secondary (about 140 district-level hospitals), tertiary (eight provincial hospitals) and quaternary (six hospitals); all report on paper. The document's published year is used ([source, 2023](https://mesamalaria.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ZIMZABWE-Malaria-Profile-PMI-FY-2024.pdf)).

### Egypt: 2 Nascent

Births and deaths are entered at district level into the national health information system (2025); routine clinic reporting is not shown.

- Egypt's answers to the WHO EMRO civil registration questionnaire state that notifications made at health offices go as copies to the health department (district) level, where they are processed electronically into the Ministry of Health's national health information system, then reviewed at directorate and central level; work is electronic from the directorates and automation of the health offices is under implementation, with a pilot of electronic registration at health-office level in some governorates. The document describes this flow for birth and death data only and carries no date of its own beyond the published year; the latest year it cites is 2011 ([source, 2025](https://www.emro.who.int/images/stories/crvs/documents/egypt.pdf)).

### Equatorial Guinea: 2 Nascent

The national reporting system was piloted in two districts, Baney and Añisok, in 2025; a planned national expansion in 2026 is not confirmed.

*Advancing.*

- The health ministry (MINSABSIS) states that the DHIS2 pilot was run during June 2025 in two health districts, Baney (Región Insular) and Añisok (Región Continental), with 104 health professionals trained; no count of facilities is given ([source, 2025-06](https://guineasalud.com/finaliza-con-exito-la-prueba-piloto-de-la-plataforma-dhis2-en-baney-y-anisok/)).
- EHAS states the first pilot was deployed between February and May 2025 so that health facilities in two districts, insular Baney and continental Añisok, could enter real data; no count of facilities is given ([source, 2025-05](https://ehas.org/que-hacemos/sis/sis-guinea-ecuatorial/)).
- EHAS states that in the Baney and Añisok pilot (February to May 2025) data can be entered from the health facilities themselves with the DHIS2 Android capture app or web app, where digital means are available; otherwise entry is at the District Office ([source, 2025-05](https://ehas.org/que-hacemos/sis/sis-guinea-ecuatorial/)).
- EHAS states that in the Baney and Añisok pilot (February to May 2025) data is entered at the District Offices where the health facility lacks digital means; otherwise the facility enters it ([source, 2025-05](https://ehas.org/que-hacemos/sis/sis-guinea-ecuatorial/)).

### Eswatini: 2 Nascent

Hospitals and maternity units record births and deaths digitally (2022), and the national reporting system was introduced in 2024; clinic reporting is not yet shown.

*Advancing.*

- The Central Statistical Office's 2022 vital statistics report shows, in its registration flow charts, an HMIS officer at the hospital or maternity recording birth and death outcomes in the Ministry of Health's inpatient system, producing the Ministry's hospital birth and death statistics; HMIS supplied the report with 28,541 health-institution births and 1,354 health-institution deaths for 2022 ([source, 2022](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2023-10/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202022%20%281%29.pdf)).
- WHO Eswatini reports that DHIS2 was introduced nationally in 2024 as a single platform bringing together health data that had sat in separate programme databases, Eswatini being among the last seven countries in the African region to introduce it; in 2024 test and production servers were set up, a national Health Sector Indicator Compendium finalised, dashboards built and 25 Strategic Information Officers trained in configuration ([source, 2024](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2025-07/Eswatini%202024%20Annual%20Report%20.pdf)).

### Mauritius: 2 Nascent

### Sao Tome and Principe: 2 Nascent
