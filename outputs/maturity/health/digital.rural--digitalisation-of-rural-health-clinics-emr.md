# Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

Africa CDC's Digital Transformation Strategy (2023) and PHC Digitalisation Framework (2025–26): 100,000 facilities connected by 2030 and 90 per cent digitally enabled primary care by 2035.

**It does not measure what either sub-indicator measures.** It counts connection and *digitally enabled*, which a clinic with a tablet and paper registers meets. **The review bears this out**: most of the 15 coverage shares it found count facilities where a system is deployed, not clinics entering their own data. Countries publish the norm's kind of figure. The ladders borrow only the 90 per cent line, for stage 5, and the pages say so.

## The ladder

| Stage | Tiers | Where digitised | Primary clinics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that patient records are on paper at every tier | | |
| 2 Nascent | Some hospitals or pilot facilities; or a national system procured | Anywhere | None, or pilot sites |
| 3 Established | Most T1 and T2 hospitals, or primary clinics in some districts | Either; the record stays in the facility | A minority, or not published |
| 4 Operating | The primary tier | `shared`: at the point of care, on a unique patient identifier, retrievable at another facility | More than half |
| 5 Leading | The primary tier | As 4, across the public network | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years |

## Countries

### Botswana: 3 Established

All public hospitals held an electronic record (2019-20) and a centralised EMR reached 22 facilities in one district (March 2026); no shared record is established.

*Advancing.*

- The Assistant Minister of Health told Parliament on 5 March 2026 that Phase 1 of the centralised Electronic Medical Record had been implemented in 22 health facilities in Ngami, to be extended to 21 facilities in Okavango by end of March 2026; the facility types and a national denominator are not given ([source, 2026-03-05](https://www.botswanaspeaks.gov.bw/media/COMMITTEE%20OF%20SUPPPLY%20SPEECHES/HEALTH-Committee%20of%20Supply%20Speech%20-%20FINAL%2004-03-2026%20Vol%20I.pdf)).
- The study names 13 public hospitals as facilities using the system at its survey of November 2019 to May 2020: referral, psychiatric, general and district hospitals and three primary hospitals (Gumare, Kasane, Letlhakeng); of 420 staff surveyed, 152 of 222 in referral hospitals (69.1%) and 135 of 198 in general hospitals (68.5%) used it more than once a day ([source, 2020-05](https://noyam.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/EHASS20234416.pdf)).
- The authors quote the National eHealth Strategy, adopted in March 2020, as stating that all public hospitals have an electronic medical record that is real time; no count of hospitals is given ([source, 2020-03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379582/)).
- The authors quote the March 2020 National eHealth Strategy as saying the public hospitals' electronic medical record is real time, and report from their 2019-20 survey that most eRecord systems are not interoperable within or between facilities, that there is no national unique patient identifier, and that patients hold duplicate electronic and paper records ([source, 2020-03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379582/)).
- MEASURE Evaluation's country profile, as of August 2019 and on an in-country contact, states that all hospitals are on a centralised Integrated Patient Management System, used in hospitals only to collect patient diagnostic information ([source, 2019-08](https://www.measureevaluation.org/his-strengthening-resource-center/country-profiles-1/botswana.html)).

### Burundi: 3 Established

41 of 71 hospitals held a patient record and six health centres piloted one (2023); no shared record or later coverage is established.

*Advancing.*

- The World Bank assessment, citing PROGISSA, states that 41 of 71 public and faith-based hospitals are computerised (patient health records, facility management and automatic reporting), 39 of the 41 on OpenClinic, with nine more waiting; they are not interconnected and each has its own patient identifier; no date is given, so the report's date is used ([source, 2023-03](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099050923073520082/pdf/P17512808c1cbf01009a830d30eb4cb8732.pdf)).
- The World Bank assessment states that six health centres (CDS) are computerised under a pilot financed by Enabel, with 29 more financed by Enabel and four by the government planned during 2023; no denominator of health centres is given, and the report's date is used ([source, 2023-03](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099050923073520082/pdf/P17512808c1cbf01009a830d30eb4cb8732.pdf)).
- The PNDIS II states that in the computerised hospitals OpenClinic GA runs on a local server in each hospital for clinical users, with data exchanged between clinical services, laboratory, imaging and pharmacy in real time without further manual entry; it does not use the words point of care, and the record stays on the hospital's server ([source, 2019](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/BDI_Burundi_National-plan-development-digital-health_2020-2024.pdf)).

### Eswatini: 3 Established

CMIS in 73% of all health facilities, 233 sites (2026), record shared between clinics; no current plan, domestic budget line or primary-clinic denominator held.

*Externally run; Community integrated; Advancing.*

- The Data.FI project says CMIS is used in 73% of the country's health facilities, with 233 live sites; the share is of all health facilities, not of primary clinics alone, and no denominator is given (no date stated, published date used) ([source, 2026-04-07](https://datafiproject.substack.com/p/even-on-the-rainiest-afternoons-in)).
- Data.FI says clinicians open a client's full record in CMIS on a unique ID number and are led through guided modules at their point of service, and that the record is available to clinicians at another clinic if the patient moves district (no date stated, published date used) ([source, 2026-04-07](https://datafiproject.substack.com/p/even-on-the-rainiest-afternoons-in)).
- Data.FI describes CMIS in use in the outpatient department of Piggs Peak Government Hospital and at local health clinics, within 233 live sites; it names no other tier (no date stated, published date used) ([source, 2026-04-07](https://datafiproject.substack.com/p/even-on-the-rainiest-afternoons-in)).
- Data.FI describes CMIS as an open-source, government-owned electronic medical record whose records are backed up in government-owned cloud systems, with the Ministry of Health working on its connectivity (no date stated, published date used) ([source, 2026-04-07](https://datafiproject.substack.com/p/even-on-the-rainiest-afternoons-in)).
- Data.FI, a PEPFAR-funded USAID cooperative agreement ending 14 December 2025, reports that it funds and carries out CMIS system development, security assessment and facility network support; the document states no domestic budget line ([source, 2024-03](https://datafi.thepalladiumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Data.FI-2024-SAPR_Final-for-dissemination_508.pdf)).

### Kenya: 3 Established

Public Level 2 to 4 facilities reported migrated to the government's HMIS (September 2026); a shared record and primary clinics' own entry are not established.

*Advancing.*

- The government set 30 September 2026 for facilities to migrate from the SHA Provider Portal to HMIS and then extended the deadline to 30 October 2026, with all public Level 2 to 4 facilities reported migrated and 28 private HMIS platforms certified by the Digital Health Agency ([source, 2026-09-30](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/health/health-science/article/2001559265/why-sha-is-shifting-hospitals-from-portal-to-hmis-duale-explains)).
- The signed US-Kenya Cooperation Framework states that the Government of Kenya has already deployed TaifaCare HMIS, a hospital management system with clinical management, laboratory, pharmacy and billing modules, in 1,500 public health facilities; it gives no denominator and does not say how many of the 1,500 are primary clinics ([source, 2025-12-04](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-0007QN-Kenya-Health-12.4.2025.pdf)).
- The trial authors state that consultation data at Penda Health's 16 primary care facilities were recorded directly in the cloud-based EMR at the point of care by the clinical officers, who used it for documentation and order entry, during enrolment from 22 April to 16 July 2025 ([source, 2025-07](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04503-6)).

### Morocco: 3 Established

Hospital system stated as deployed in all public hospitals (2024), with keyed patient files in one province's primary clinics; no shared record, no clinic count.

*Advancing.*

- The government states that the integrated hospital information system has been developed and deployed in all public hospitals of the twelve regions; the same passage calls the new system generalised nationally to health centres, dispensaries and private clinics while listing the primary care system as still being deployed. The document gives no date, so its published date is used ([source, 2024-12-01](https://www.maroc.ma/sites/default/files/2024-12/sante-fr.pdf)).
- The government states that a specific information system for primary health care establishments is being put in place and is in the course of deployment, with no count or share of establishments; the document gives no date, so its published date is used ([source, 2024-12-01](https://www.maroc.ma/sites/default/files/2024-12/sante-fr.pdf)).
- A Marrakech medical thesis surveying residents and interns of the CHU Mohammed VI who use HOSIX in daily practice reports that 94.2% of respondents had used it for more than two years and 37.1% for more than five; it gives the sample variously as 80 and 85 responses and as 100 participants, and dates the survey inconsistently, so the defence date is used ([source, 2024-05-29](https://wd.fmpm.uca.ma/biblio/theses/annee-htm/FT/2024/these258-24.pdf)).
- The thesis describes doctors at the CHU Mohammed VI writing and amending the medical observation, diagnoses and history in HOSIX and ordering and reading laboratory tests there, while the paper record is kept alongside: 31.4% of respondents cite time lost filling both the physical file and the HOSIX report, and 71.4% cite system freezes. The defence date is used ([source, 2024-05-29](https://wd.fmpm.uca.ma/biblio/theses/annee-htm/FT/2024/these258-24.pdf)).
- A purchase notice put online on 23 April 2024 by the health ministry's delegate for Taounate province buys 19,200 working hours of technical assistance to key the HOSIX database of patient files for users of the province's primary health care establishments, local emergency units, reproductive health reference centre and birthing houses, by contracted staff holding a secretarial, IT or management diploma rather than by clinicians ([source, 2024-04-23](https://www.marchespublics.gov.ma/bdc/entreprise/consultation/show/52009)).
- The Taounate purchase notice of 23 April 2024 places the HOSIX patient-file database in the province's primary health care establishments, local emergency units, reproductive health reference centre and birthing houses; it gives no count of establishments and covers one province ([source, 2024-04-23](https://www.marchespublics.gov.ma/bdc/entreprise/consultation/show/52009)).

### Nigeria: 3 Established

EMR at the point of care in 100 of Lagos State's 326 primary clinics (May 2025); no national share, and no shared record, is established.

*Advancing.*

- The Minister of State for Health told the National Council on Health on 20 November 2025 that over 200 hospitals are integrated with Electronic Medical Records systems; he gives no denominator and does not say which hospital tiers, and names no primary facilities ([source, 2025-11-20](https://health.gov.ng/opening-remarks-by-the-honourable-minister-of-state-for-health-and-social-welfare-dr-iziaq-adekunle-salako-delivered-at-the-national-council-on-health-nch-2025/)).
- The Lagos State Ministry of Health reports the EMR platform deployed in 100 of the state's 326 primary health care facilities (Lagos State only, not a national figure), per the Lagos State Primary Health Care Board at a review workshop on 22 May 2025 ([source, 2025-05-22](https://lagosministryofhealth.org/lagos-drives-digital-revolution-in-primary-healthcare/)).
- The EMR is in use in primary health care centres in Lagos State (100 of 326 PHCs), with Coker Aguda PHC described as fully digitalised end to end; the document does not state that hospitals use it ([source, 2025-05-22](https://lagosministryofhealth.org/lagos-drives-digital-revolution-in-primary-healthcare/)).
- The Permanent Secretary of the Lagos State Primary Health Care Board says the EMR in 100 PHCs is giving improved coordination and data integrity 'at the point of care', and Coker Aguda PHC reports patients served from reception to treatment on the digital system ([source, 2025-05-22](https://lagosministryofhealth.org/lagos-drives-digital-revolution-in-primary-healthcare/)).

### Rwanda: 3 Established

All 42 district hospitals used an EMR in 2023 and eBuzima reached most public facilities by 2026; a shared record is not established.

*Advancing.*

- A Rwanda Biomedical Centre officer's conference slides state that eBuzima had been rolled out to the majority of public health facilities as at 8 April 2026, for services other than HIV; no numerator, denominator or tier breakdown is given, and the slides describe it as centralised, web-based and synchronising records across public facilities ([source, 2026-04-08](https://cquin.icap.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rwandas-eBuzima-EMR_KP-and-PrEP-Modules_Zephanie_Final.pdf)).

- Tom Frieden writes that the eBuzima electronic health record is now in more than two thirds of health centres, giving no numerator, denominator or source; the piece is undated, so its publication date (a proxy) is used ([source, 2026-09-28](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/make-every-life-count-dr-tom-frieden-72x6c/)).

### Somalia: 3 Established

C+ DDS in over 40 facilities (April 2026) and RAAD in 111 primary facilities (2025). No denominator or unique patient identifier held.

*Advancing.*

- RAAD was deployed in 111 public primary healthcare facilities across six states between 2023 and 2025, per the ministry's own case study; no denominator of primary facilities is given ([source, 2025](https://community.dhis2.org/t/a-standards-based-approach-to-dhis2-integration/72690)).
- The ministry's case study places RAAD in public primary healthcare facilities only (111 of them, 2023 to 2025), without splitting health centres from lower-level clinics and naming no hospital ([source, 2025](https://community.dhis2.org/t/a-standards-based-approach-to-dhis2-integration/72690)).
- RAAD captures standardised clinical data as routine care documentation, aligned with the national HMIS registers, in place of an average of 15 paper forms, per the ministry's case study of 2023 to 2025 ([source, 2025](https://community.dhis2.org/t/a-standards-based-approach-to-dhis2-integration/72690)).
- The consortium reports the system operational in over 40 facilities across 9 regions and more than 25 districts of South-Central Somalia, capturing over 3,000 consultations a day; it gives no denominator and does not say what tier the facilities are, though it names a maternal health clinic and health posts (April 2026) ([source, 2026-04-14](https://caafimaad-plus.org/partnering-for-a-data-driven-somali-health-system/)).
- The consortium says the system replaces manual reports with real-time entry, lets a nurse call up a patient's history instantly and makes a family's record available at any facility on the system (April 2026) ([source, 2026-04-14](https://caafimaad-plus.org/partnering-for-a-data-driven-somali-health-system/)).

- The Minister of Health's reply NW4495 states the Free State Department of Health has implemented HMS2 in phases in 16 hospitals since March 2023 and is implementing Meditech, a legacy system, in 3 hospitals; no total of hospitals is given ([source, 2025-08-01](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/31734/)).
- The Minister of Health's reply NW4495 states KwaZulu-Natal has developed the eHealth system, implemented in phases in 42 public hospitals since March 2020; no total of hospitals is given ([source, 2025-08-01](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/31734/)).
- The Minister of Health's reply NW4495 states the Western Cape Department of Health has implemented Clinicom in 53 hospitals, the Primary Health Care Information System in 308 PHC facilities and PREHMIS in City of Cape Town PHC facilities, and recently began implementing the Electronic Continuity of Care Record to collate this patient data ([source, 2025-08-01](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/31734/)).
- The Minister of Health's reply NW4495 states the Western Cape's Primary Health Care Information System is implemented in 308 PHC facilities, with PREHMIS in City of Cape Town PHC facilities (number not given); the same reply counts 244 Western Cape and 3,333 national PHC facilities reporting to the 2024/25 Ideal Clinic Assessment ([source, 2025-08-01](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/31734/)).
- The National Department of Health describes its EMR solution as capturing clinical information on health encounters at the point of care by health service providers, verified against the patient's unique identifier in the Health Patient Registration System and intended to feed a longitudinal electronic health record; published date used ([source, 2026-03-03](https://www.health.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NDoH04-20252026-Final-BSC-Invitation-to-Bid-EMR-implementation-03-03-2026.pdf)).

### Uganda: 3 Established

The ministry's EMR ran in referral and general hospitals, 5 Health Centre IVs and 2 IIIs in 2025; no shared record or clinic share.

*Advancing.*

- In Kiryandongo district the EMR system is in use at Kiryandongo General Hospital, Nyakadoti Health Centre III and Mpumwe and Apodorwa Health Centre IIs, as reported from a public baraza on 19 September 2026 ([source, 2026-09-19](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/health/kiryandongo-health-facilities-struggle-with-e-NV_241031_092026)).
- The in-charge of Nyakadoti Health Centre III said patient data must be captured in the EMR at every stage of the visit, which with too few computers lengthens queues; the hospital superintendent said everything stops when power fails ([source, 2026-09-19](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/health/kiryandongo-health-facilities-struggle-with-e-NV_241031_092026)).
- The article names three primary clinics in Kiryandongo district using the EMR (Nyakadoti Health Centre III, Mpumwe Health Centre II, Apodorwa Health Centre II), with no denominator and no national figure ([source, 2026-09-19](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/health/kiryandongo-health-facilities-struggle-with-e-NV_241031_092026)).
- Among all 265 government HC IVs and hospitals, eAFYA was found in 16.6% (44/265) and the study calls the prevalence of typical EMR systems (eAFYA, Clinic Master, IHMIS) minimal, a ministry informant placing eAFYA and Clinic Master mostly in HC IVs and above; 94.3% (250/265) of facilities kept both paper and digital patient records and 1.9% (5/265) digital only. Survey date not given in the passages, so the publication date is used ([source, 2025-10-10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12512544/)).
- The Ministry of Health says that as of 2025 the EMR covers 92 per cent of targeted hospitals: four national referral hospitals, all regional referral hospitals, 47 general hospitals, 5 Health Centre IVs and 2 Health Centre IIIs ([source, 2025](https://health.go.ug/electronic-medical-records-rollout-transforms-patient-care-and-data-management/)).
- The Ministry of Health counts 5 Health Centre IVs and 2 Health Centre IIIs using the EMR as of 2025, with no denominator given; Health Centre IVs and some Health Centre IIIs were first brought on in 2024 ([source, 2025](https://health.go.ug/electronic-medical-records-rollout-transforms-patient-care-and-data-management/)).

### Zambia: 3 Established

SmartCare Pro was in use at 877 of 3,540 health facilities of all kinds (April 2025); primary clinics' share and shared-record use are not established.

*Advancing.*

- The Global Fund's April 2025 case study states that SmartCare Pro is in use at 877 health facilities, against 3,540 health facilities in Zambia, with further scale-up under way; the count is of all health facilities and is not split for primary clinics ([source, 2025-04](https://www.theglobalfund.org/media/w4gdfjob/publication_zambia-digital-health_casestudy_en.pdf)).
- The SmartCare Pro programme managers' presentation states that SmartCare Legacy and SmartCare Plus are together used in approximately 1,800 healthcare facilities in 10 provinces; no denominator and no split by facility type; the slides are undated, so the published date (a proxy, March 2025) is used ([source, 2025-03](https://smartcarezambia.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Smart-CarePro-Program-Managers-Presentation-final_kpp_mkm_2.pdf)).
- The paper states that within some larger health centres and hospitals data are collected digitally through the electronic health record system 'when possible', and that the record system was implemented in select districts; elsewhere patient history is written in paper 'passports' carried by the patient; interviews of May and June 2019 ([source, 2019-06](https://www.ovid.com/journals/hpop/fulltext/10.1093/heapol/czad077~identifying-barriers-to-the-production-and-use-of-routine)).
- The presentation describes SmartCare Pro as capturing the patient encounter at facility level, following clinical workflows with embedded guidelines and decision prompts, on a central server that identifies each client uniquely and lets patients be tracked between facilities in real time; undated slides, so the published date (a proxy) is used ([source, 2025-03](https://smartcarezambia.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Smart-CarePro-Program-Managers-Presentation-final_kpp_mkm_2.pdf)).
- The implementers (IHM Southern Africa, Co-Develop) describe SmartCare Pro as a centralised electronic health record that uses the National Digital ID service to create a unique patient record, with a nurse at a rural clinic in Kafulafuta identifying a patient by fingerprint to reach his record, said to be retrievable at any public hospital; no figures are given and the publication date is used ([source, 2026-01-15](https://dpi.africa.com/how-digital-public-infrastructure-is-transforming-health-and-identity-in-rural-zambia)).
