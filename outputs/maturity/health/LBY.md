# Libya

## HMIS

**Governance, planning, finance.** The Ministry of Health's Health Information Centre [gives technical support to the DHIS2 platform](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), as WHO described it in 2024. WHO states that [all health information system activities in 2024 followed the 2024-2025 HIS operational plan](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). The 2023 rollout [was enabled by WHO with UNICEF, IOM, GIZ, the International Rescue Committee, the International Medical Corps and UNFPA](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>).

**Tiers in use.** WHO states that in 2023 it and the ministry [rolled out DHIS2 in 40 municipalities](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>). The rollout [covered 465 primary health care facilities and 51 hospitals](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>).

**Where data is digitised.** WHO states that [655 health staff were trained on DHIS2 in 2023](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>). They came from public primary health care facilities, hospitals and municipal health administration. They were given laptops, desktops and tablets to enable data entry. In December 2024 WHO and the ministry [visited five health facilities in Suq Aljuma municipality, Tripoli](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). They assessed DHIS2 data entry compliance. They also [assessed the timeliness and completeness of reporting](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>).

**Last twelve months.** Libya Review reported on 7 November 2025 on a meeting of the ministry and WHO. It [explored the feasibility of extending DHIS2 in public facilities](<https://libyareview.com/60731/libya-who-review-expansion-of-national-digital-health-system/>). The extension would cover [pharmacy management, supply forecasting and service monitoring](<https://libyareview.com/60731/libya-who-review-expansion-of-national-digital-health-system/>).

**Primary clinics doing the digital input.** WHO counts [465 primary health care facilities among the 516 facilities where DHIS2 was rolled out in 2023](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>). WHO also states that [DHIS2 reached 100% national geographic coverage by March 2023](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). It says [consistent reporting from health facilities remains a challenge](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>).

***Also on file***. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its [unified electronic system](<https://ar.libyaobserver.ly/article/37094>), [DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>) and the [national cancer registry](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). Single-function: the ministry's [electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines](<https://ar.libyaobserver.ly/article/34348>) and the [National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11167861/>). Also single-function: [HeRAMS](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), the [integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>) and [event-based surveillance](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). Also single-function: [EMFLU 2.0](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), the [LIVE Oxygen Platform](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), [EWARN](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>), [RASED](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>) and the [EPI data management systems](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>).

The sources name hospitals and primary health care facilities, not their levels. Nothing found shows the operational plan running beyond 2025 or financing dated after 2023.

## EMR

**Governance, planning, finance.** Libya Herald described the Unified Electronic Health File System in May 2022. It called it [a Ministry of Health project under implementation for more than five years](<https://libyaherald.com/2022/05/trail-run-of-the-unified-electronic-health-file-system-inaugurated/>). The National Centre for Health System Reform works under the ministry's supervision. In January 2024 it [launched the National Comprehensive Health Care Programme and the Health Number System in Tripoli](<https://libyaherald.com/2024/01/national-comprehensive-health-care-programme-and-health-number-system-launched/>). They are the ministry's strategic option for implementing the electronic health file. WHO states that in 2024 the ministry and WHO [developed a digital transformation roadmap for 2025-2030](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). It [promotes electronic health records and hospital management systems on open-source platforms](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>).

**Tiers in use.** A Sabratha University field study was published in December 2025. It says that [Sabratha Teaching Hospital has moved to apply an electronic medical records system](<https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/download/1132/922>). It says [difficulties prevent its full use](<https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/download/1132/922>).

**Where data is digitised.** The Unified Electronic Health File System was trialled in May 2022. Its technical committee [showed information being entered directly from patients attending the clinic](<https://libyaherald.com/2022/05/trail-run-of-the-unified-electronic-health-file-system-inaugurated/>). The Sabratha study's author states that [most public hospitals in Libya use traditional paper records](<https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/download/1132/922>).

**Last twelve months.** The Sabratha study was published on 24 December 2025. It states that [the move towards electronic medical records is slow or stalled in many government hospitals](<https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/download/1132/922>).

**Primary clinics doing the digital input.** Libya Herald reports that [the trial operation of the system was inaugurated in May 2022](<https://libyaherald.com/2022/05/trail-run-of-the-unified-electronic-health-file-system-inaugurated/>). The first phase covered two sites. They were [the Ghout al-Shaal clinic in Tripoli and the al-Kish clinic in Benghazi](<https://libyaherald.com/2022/05/trail-run-of-the-unified-electronic-health-file-system-inaugurated/>).

***Also on file***. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its [unified electronic system](<https://ar.libyaobserver.ly/article/37094>), [DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>) and the [national cancer registry](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). Single-function: the ministry's [electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines](<https://ar.libyaobserver.ly/article/34348>) and the [National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11167861/>). Also single-function: [HeRAMS](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), the [integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>) and [event-based surveillance](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>). Also single-function: [EMFLU 2.0](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), the [LIVE Oxygen Platform](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292741716-eng.pdf>), [EWARN](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>), [RASED](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>) and the [EPI data management systems](<https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/9789292742584-eng.pdf?ua=1>).
