# Cameroon

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Cameroon has a draft and no law. [A draft law on the right of access to and use of public information and on the digital transformation of the administration was published on 22 May 2023](<https://www.minpostel.gov.cm/images/Documentation/patnuc/textes_preparation_pour_consultation_publique/e-Government/230519_Loi%20EadmOpendata%20FIN_CLEAN.pdf>). [A national workshop at Ebolowa on 3 to 14 November 2025 gave the draft its technical validation](<https://www.droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1182-cameroun-la-legislation-sur-e-government-facilitant-lacces-a-linformation-publique-au-stade-de-la-validation-officielle>), and [the project behind it lists the text among those examined there](<https://patnuc.cm/2025/11/20/services-publics-et-transparence-les-evolutions-cles-de-la-loi-e-gov/>).

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the draft, [the draft validated at Ebolowa in November 2025](<https://www.droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1182-cameroun-la-legislation-sur-e-government-facilitant-lacces-a-linformation-publique-au-stade-de-la-validation-officielle>). A bill makes no rules and sets up no oversight body until it is passed, promulgated and in force. Once it is, what would put it to work is a decree or regulation made under it, or an information commission or commissioner appointed, and what would show it applied is a decision or penalty on a request for information, or published figures of requests.
