# Guinea-Bissau

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Guinea-Bissau has a draft and no law. [A drafting commission met on 17 to 23 April 2024 and all those taking part validated the draft law on access to information](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/guinee-bissau-validation-du-projet-de-loi-sur-lacces-linformation>), by UNESCO's account. The next step it gives is submission to the Ministry of Justice for adoption. No law is in force.

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the draft, [the draft validated in April 2024](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/guinee-bissau-validation-du-projet-de-loi-sur-lacces-linformation>). A bill makes no rules and sets up no oversight body until it is passed, promulgated and in force. Once it is, what would put it to work is a decree or regulation made under it, or an information commission or commissioner appointed, and what would show it applied is a decision or penalty on a request for information, or published figures of requests.

***Also on file***. [Constitution of Guinea-Bissau, article 51 (freedom of information, right to inform and be informed)](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/guinee-bissau-validation-du-projet-de-loi-sur-lacces-linformation>).

***Not found***. No source found says an access to information law is in force.
