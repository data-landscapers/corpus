# Congo

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** The Republic of the Congo has no access to information law and no draft of one. [UNESCO's assessment of the country's internet development, dated 2025, recommends that the government draft and adopt a freedom of information law](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/evaluation-du-developpement-de-linternet-en-republique-du-congo>), and [lists a commission on access to public information among the bodies still to be set up](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/evaluation-du-developpement-de-linternet-en-republique-du-congo>). The assessment names no bill in preparation.

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the statement that there is no law, [UNESCO's 2025 assessment](<https://www.unesco.org/fr/articles/evaluation-du-developpement-de-linternet-en-republique-du-congo>). With no law and no draft there are no rules, no information commission or commissioner and no decisions on requests to record. A bill published, put out for consultation or sent to cabinet or parliament would be the first step, and a law in force the second.
