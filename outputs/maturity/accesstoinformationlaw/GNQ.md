# Equatorial Guinea

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Equatorial Guinea has a bill passed by parliament and not shown as promulgated. [The Prime Minister presented the bill for a Code of Transparency, Access to Public Information and Good Governance in Public Finance Management to the Chamber of Deputies on 5 February 2026](<https://www.guineaecuatorialpress.com/noticias/comparecencia_de_osa_nsue_ante_el_pleno_de_la_camara_de_los_diputados>). [The Senate approved it unanimously on 8 May 2026](<https://www.guineaecuatorialpress.com/noticias/pleno_de_aprobacion_de_los_proyectos_de_ley_de_transparencia_y_zonas_economicas>), which leaves promulgation by the Head of State.

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the draft, [the bill the Senate approved on 8 May 2026](<https://www.guineaecuatorialpress.com/noticias/pleno_de_aprobacion_de_los_proyectos_de_ley_de_transparencia_y_zonas_economicas>). A bill makes no rules and sets up no oversight body until it is passed, promulgated and in force. Once it is, what would put it to work is a decree or regulation made under it, or an information commission or commissioner appointed, and what would show it applied is a decision or penalty on a request for information, or published figures of requests.
