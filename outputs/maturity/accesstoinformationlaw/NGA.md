# Nigeria

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Nigeria's law is [the Freedom of Information Act, 2011, assented to on 28 May 2011](<https://nimasa.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/freedom-of-information-act.pdf>), which gives any person a right to request information held by public institutions. [The Federal High Court treats it as taking precedence over the Petroleum Industry Act where the two conflict](<http://saharareporters.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/12.%20Judgment%20%28HEDA%20v.%20NNPCL%29-min.pdf>).

**Law put to work and applied.** The courts enforce the Act. [On 13 October 2025 the Federal High Court in Abuja held the national oil company a public institution under the Act and ordered it to supply information requested on a $3 billion loan](<http://saharareporters.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/12.%20Judgment%20%28HEDA%20v.%20NNPCL%29-min.pdf>). [On 24 February 2026 the Federal High Court in Lagos ruled that the Nigeria Shippers Council had breached the Act over a journalist's request of 2020, and ordered disclosure or reasons](<https://mediarightsagenda.org/federal-high-court-rules-that-foi-act-overrides-other-laws-in-journalists-suit-against-nigeria-shippers-council/>). The Act has no information commission; rules made under it are not recorded.
