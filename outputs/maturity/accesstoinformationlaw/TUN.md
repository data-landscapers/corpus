# Tunisia

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Tunisia's law is [Organic Law no. 22 of 2016 on the right of access to information](<https://inai.tn/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/loi-22-inai.pdf>), promulgated on 24 March 2016. It gives every person a right to ask public bodies for information. It took effect one year after publication.

**Law put to work and applied.** The law's oversight body is set up and has handled appeals, but has since stalled. [The government's 2024 return to UNESCO says a national information commission oversees the law](<https://www.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2024/11/Tunisia_SDG16102_2024.pdf>). It reports 7,347 appeals received in 2023 and 6,375 requests received in 2021. [The government told a deputy, as reported on 30 November 2025, that the access to information body has been working without its president and deputy](<https://www.lapresse.tn/2025/12/01/donnees-personnelles-et-acces-a-linformation-lexecutif-engage-une-mise-a-jour-urgente-du-cadre-juridique/>). The body has nine members elected by Parliament for six years. The government said this has blocked legally valid meetings.
