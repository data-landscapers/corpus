# Madagascar

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Madagascar has a bill and no law. [A decree of the Prime Minister presented bill no. 017/2025 on access to information of a public nature, in 88 articles, after the Council of Ministers deliberated it on 3 December 2025](<https://pub-7e0d3a35c1b949698a7ced344fa56252.r2.dev/textes/017-2025/PROJET_LAICP___PR_SENT_E_AU.PDF>). [Deputies debated it in committee from 8 May 2026](<https://www.rfi.fr/fr/afrique/20260511-madagascar-l%C3%A9gislative-projet-loi-information-%C3%A0-caract%C3%A8re-public>), and [it was left off the agenda of the extraordinary session opened on 25 August 2026](<https://www.rfi.fr/fr/afrique/20260827-madagascar-une-session-extraordinaire-de-l-assembl%C3%A9e-nationale-sans-le-projet-de-loi-sur-l-information-publique>).

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the draft, [bill no. 017/2025](<https://www.rfi.fr/fr/afrique/20260827-madagascar-une-session-extraordinaire-de-l-assembl%C3%A9e-nationale-sans-le-projet-de-loi-sur-l-information-publique>). A bill makes no rules and sets up no oversight body until it is passed, promulgated and in force. Once it is, what would put it to work is a decree or regulation made under it, or an information commission or commissioner appointed, and what would show it applied is a decision or penalty on a request for information, or published figures of requests.
