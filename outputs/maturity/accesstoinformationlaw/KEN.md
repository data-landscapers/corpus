# Kenya

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Kenya's law is the Access to Information Act, 2016. [It gives effect to Article 35 of the Constitution](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-27-state-vows-proactive-disclosure-as-kenya-marks-access-to-information-day>), and [the press describes it as the guarantee of the right to information](<https://nation.africa/kenya/news/new-bill-sharing-state-secrets-on-whatsapp-emails-could-land-you-in-jail-5516338>). It has stood since 2016 with no repeal recorded.

**Law put to work and applied.** [The Access to Information Regulations of 2023, made under the Act, define proactive disclosure and set what public bodies must publish](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-27-state-vows-proactive-disclosure-as-kenya-marks-access-to-information-day>). Regulations made in the last three years are counted as the law applied. [A forum in September 2026 assessed how public institutions carry the Act out](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-27-state-vows-proactive-disclosure-as-kenya-marks-access-to-information-day>). A decision or penalty on a request, and published figures of requests, are not recorded. A separate bill of 2026 on classified information is noted and does not change the Act's standing.
