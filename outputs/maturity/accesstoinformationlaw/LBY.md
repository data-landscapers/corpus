# Libya

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Libya has a draft and no law. [A Council of Ministers draft bill on the national information system, issued on 8 October 2022 over the Prime Minister's signature, would give every citizen a right to request information from the information units of public bodies](<https://lawsociety.ly/legislation/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84/>). Its passage is not recorded.

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the draft, [the draft bill of October 2022](<https://lawsociety.ly/legislation/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84/>). A bill makes no rules and sets up no oversight body until it is passed, promulgated and in force. Once it is, what would put it to work is a decree or regulation made under it, or an information commission or commissioner appointed, and what would show it applied is a decision or penalty on a request for information, or published figures of requests.
