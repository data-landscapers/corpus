# Niger

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Niger's law is [Ordinance no. 2011-22 of 23 February 2011 on the charter of access to public information and administrative documents](<https://cyrilla.org/api/files/1566209433658g62junbhzjn.pdf>), an ordinance with the force of law that gives any person a right to ask public bodies for information. [The Minister of Communication cited it on 22 October 2025 as standing](<https://anp.ne/le-niger-veut-se-doter-dune-politique-de-communication-et-dune-strategie-nationale-de-lutte-contre-la-desinformation/>).

**Law put to work and applied.** The nearest held fact is the law itself, [Ordinance no. 2011-22](<https://cyrilla.org/api/files/1566209433658g62junbhzjn.pdf>). What would put it to work is a decree, regulation or order made under it, an information commission or commissioner appointed, or an existing body such as an ombudsman taking up its oversight. What would show it applied is a decision, order or penalty on a request for information, or figures of requests published by the body that oversees it. Neither is recorded for Niger.

***Not found***. No source found names rules made under the law or says its oversight body is set up.
