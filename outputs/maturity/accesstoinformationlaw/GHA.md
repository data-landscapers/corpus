# Ghana

## Access to information law

**Law in force.** Ghana's law is the Right to Information Act, 2019 (Act 989). [It puts the constitutional right to information to work and sets the procedure for asking public institutions for what they hold](<https://gna.org.gh/2026/09/rti-act-public-institutions-urged-to-provide-accurate-timely-information-to-citizens/>).

**Law put to work and applied.** [The Right to Information Commission, with a board and chairman, oversees and enforces the Act](<https://gna.org.gh/2026/09/rti-act-public-institutions-urged-to-provide-accurate-timely-information-to-citizens/>), and [the 2026 budget votes it GHS 20,413,271](<https://www.mofep.gov.gh/sites/default/files/budget-statements/Appropriation%20%28%20No.%202%29%20Act%2C%202025%20Act%201163_0.pdf>). [On 5 August 2026 the Commission imposed penalties of GHS 20,000 each on 254 public institutions that had not filed their 2025 annual reports under the Act](<https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/upsa-37-military-hospital-among-254-public-institutions-fined-by-rti-commission-over-report-delays.html>). A newspaper count puts the practice lower: [21 institutions failed to release information in 24 requests made between January 2025 and July 2026](<https://thefourthestategh.com/2026/09/transparency-in-words-secrecy-in-practice-how-mdas-are-betraying-govts-transparency-pledge>). The penalties are for missed reports; a published decision on a single request is not recorded.
