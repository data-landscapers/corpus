# Rwanda

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [Paratus Rwanda, which began operating in Rwanda about a year before this 18 August 2026 announcement, sells Starlink-powered internet to businesses (Starlink for Business, Essential Access plans, primary, backup and remote connectivity, Layer 2)](<https://paratus.africa/blog/paratus-rwanda-rings-in-afpif-2026/>). [A country availability table lists Starlink as available in Rwanda](<https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html>). [Starlink is live in Rwanda, listed among early adopters; local-currency plans priced from RWF 40,000 to 549,000 and measured at 267 Mbps down, 37 Mbps up, 27 ms](<https://technomag.co.zw/starlinks-african-expansion-availability-costs-and-performance/>). [Starlink users in Rwanda record median download speeds of around 75 Mbps, so the service is in use](<https://itweb.africa/article/starlink-drives-african-broadband-speeds-higher/GxwQD71DnGyvlPVo>). [Rwanda is said to have moved to approve Starlink operations as part of digital inclusion strategy](<https://www.independent.co.ug/uganda-licenses-starlink-as-africa-satellite-internet-rollout-expands/>). These place satellite broadband licensed and on sale, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Services on a second satellite network are not stated. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.

**Subscribers counted by the regulator.** [Subscribers: 4,489 at 2025-06, per RURA (Rwanda Utilities Regulatory Authority), Starlink subscribers for Q2 2025 as reported by TechCabal](<https://techcabal.com/2026/06/26/how-starlinks-growth-is-changing-africa-telcos-playbook/>). Only a count the regulator publishes, from the past three years, answers this question; counts given by an operator or an analyst do not.
