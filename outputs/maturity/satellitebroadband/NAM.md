# Namibia

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [Eutelsat OneWeb LEO satellite services, branded Twoobii-OneWeb by Q-KON, launched commercially in Namibia on 19 June 2026 through licensed partners Echo Namibia and Oblixx Communication Networks, aimed at businesses, with up to 100 Mbps and 2TB per site](<https://www.qkon.com/2026/07/22/q-kon-and-partners-launch-fully-compliant-eutelsat-oneweb/>). [Paratus Namibia launched a Eutelsat OneWeb LEO satellite internet service for residential and commercial customers countrywide, with capped and uncapped packages (article of 2026-10-08 gives no other date)](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/08/paratus-namibia-eutelsat-oneweb-leo-satellite-service/>). [Paratus Namibia launched Eutelsat's OneWeb LEO satellite internet service for residential and commercial customers across Namibia, with capped and uncapped packages](<https://www.itweb.africa/article/paratus-namibia-launches-eutelsats-oneweb-leo-connectivity/dgp45qaBnYVvX9l8>). [Starlink's official availability map lists Namibia among 16 further African markets marked 'Starting in 2026', which the map legend says means service coverage or regulatory authorisation still pending](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/3108-141168-internet-par-satellite-starlink-cible-16-nouveaux-marches-africains-d-ici-fin-2026>). These place satellite broadband licensed and on sale, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Services on a second satellite network are not stated.
