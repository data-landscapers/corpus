# Namibia

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [Q-KON and its licensed partners launched Eutelsat OneWeb's low-orbit service on 19 June 2026](<https://www.qkon.com/2026/07/22/q-kon-and-partners-launch-fully-compliant-eutelsat-oneweb/>), for businesses. It offers up to 100 Mbps and 2 TB a site. [Paratus Namibia launched OneWeb service for homes and firms across the country in October 2026](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/08/paratus-namibia-eutelsat-oneweb-leo-satellite-service/>), with capped and uncapped packages. [In November 2025 the regulator, CRAN, approved Paratus's Yursat Adapt satellite packages](<https://thebrief.com.na/2025/12/cran-approves-revised-tariff-structures-for-telecom-operators/>). They offer 10 to 20 Mbps for N$1,550 to N$3,350 a month. [Starlink's own map listed Namibia as starting in 2026, with authorisation still pending](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/3108-141168-internet-par-satellite-starlink-cible-16-nouveaux-marches-africains-d-ici-fin-2026>).

**Subscribers counted by the regulator.** No source found since November 2023 gives a regulator's count of satellite broadband subscribers. [CRAN's approval of the Yursat packages in November 2025](<https://thebrief.com.na/2025/12/cran-approves-revised-tariff-structures-for-telecom-operators/>) set their prices and gave no count.
