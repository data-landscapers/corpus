# Sudan

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [Canar Telecommunication procured YahClick satellite capacity under a USD 15 million six-year agreement to deliver broadband to consumers and businesses in Sudan](<https://static.mubasher.info/File.Mix_Announcement_File/EA30B31E-ED03-4213-B71E-D07FE7C64718.pdf>). [The Rapid Support Forces ordered Starlink satellite internet networks disabled indefinitely in En Nahud locality, West Kordofan, from 26 August 2026, per the Dar Hamar emergency room; this is a ban by an armed group, not by the regulator](<https://sudantribune.net/article/317878>). [MyBroadband's table of Starlink availability in Africa lists Sudan as having no planned launch date, so Starlink is not on sale in Sudan](<https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html>). [Technomag lists Sudan among seven countries where Starlink's launch date is unspecified and not among the 26 active markets, so Starlink is not on sale in Sudan](<https://technomag.co.zw/starlinks-african-expansion-availability-costs-and-performance/>). These place satellite broadband licensed and on sale, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Services on a second satellite network are not stated. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.
