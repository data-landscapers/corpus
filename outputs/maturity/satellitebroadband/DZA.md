# Algeria

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [State-owned Algerie Telecom Satellite (VSAT and GMPCS licences) and Djezzy (VSAT licence, to connect isolated sites with connectivity services mainly for businesses) are the only companies authorised to provide satellite services in Algeria](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/1004-137437-services-telecoms-par-satellite-l-algerie-ouvre-la-porte-a-de-nouveaux-acteurs>). [Algeria is listed among about 34 countries where Starlink is in regulatory limbo, neither approved nor explicitly banned, as of June 2026](<https://cioafrica.co/namibia-shuts-the-door-on-starlink-twice/>). [ARPCE launched a call for competition on 9 April 2026 for two licences for public satellite networks in non-geostationary orbit (NGSO), so no NGSO/LEO provider such as Starlink was yet licensed in Algeria; Starlink's service date for Algeria is stated as unknown](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/1004-137437-services-telecoms-par-satellite-l-algerie-ouvre-la-porte-a-de-nouveaux-acteurs>). These place a satellite broadband provider licensed, which answers the first of the study's four questions. Sale to households or businesses is not stated, or was suspended. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.

**Subscribers counted by the regulator.** Nothing found gives a regulator's count of the subscribers to [the service](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/1004-137437-services-telecoms-par-satellite-l-algerie-ouvre-la-porte-a-de-nouveaux-acteurs>). Only a count the regulator publishes, from the past three years, answers this question; counts given by an operator or an analyst do not.
