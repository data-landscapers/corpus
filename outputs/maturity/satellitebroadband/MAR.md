# Morocco

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [The minister said any customer in areas without terrestrial broadband can get financial support of up to 2,500 dirhams per subscription to satellite internet from an approved provider under the VSAT initiative](<https://www.lavieeco.com/au-royaume/plan-national-du-haut-debit-un-taux-de-realisation-de-99-pour-la-premiere-phase/>). [Morocco is listed among about 34 countries where Starlink sits in regulatory limbo, neither approved nor explicitly banned, as of June 2026 per CIO Africa](<https://cioafrica.co/namibia-shuts-the-door-on-starlink-twice/>). [Challenge.ma reports that no approval for Starlink has been announced by the ANRT in Morocco; only a legal entity has been created and administrative preparations begun since 2024, so Starlink is not yet licensed](<https://www.challenge.ma/starlink-pourquoi-le-maroc-joue-t-il-la-carte-de-lobservation-320501/>). These place satellite broadband licensed and on sale, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Services on a second satellite network are not stated. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.
