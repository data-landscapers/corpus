# Morocco

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [Morocco is listed among about 34 countries where Starlink sits in regulatory limbo, neither approved nor explicitly banned, as of June 2026 per CIO Africa; no licence is stated](<https://cioafrica.co/namibia-shuts-the-door-on-starlink-twice/>). [Challenge.ma reports that no approval for Starlink has been announced by the ANRT in Morocco; only a legal entity has been created and administrative preparations begun since 2024, so Starlink is not yet licensed](<https://www.challenge.ma/starlink-pourquoi-le-maroc-joue-t-il-la-carte-de-lobservation-320501/>). [Maroc Hebdo reports Starlink Internet Services Morocco was created in Casablanca, but final approvals from the ANRT and the DGSSI are still to be obtained before commercial launch, so no licence is held](<https://www.maroc-hebdo.com/article/starlink-lance-sa-filiale-marocaine>). A dated statement that no satellite broadband provider is licensed, or that its sale or use is banned, sets the lowest stage, and nothing dated later names one licensed. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.

**Subscribers counted by the regulator.** With [no provider licensed](<https://cioafrica.co/namibia-shuts-the-door-on-starlink-twice/>), there is nothing to count. Only a count the regulator publishes, from the past three years, answers this question; counts given by an operator or an analyst do not.
