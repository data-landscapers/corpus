# Liberia

## Satellite broadband licensed and on sale

**Licensed, on sale, and from more than one network.** [ITWeb reports Ookla Q1 2026 data measuring Starlink users in Liberia (median latency 222ms, no local gateway), among 23 of 27 African countries where Starlink is described as licensed (as_of is the measurement quarter)](<https://itweb.africa/article/starlink-drives-african-broadband-speeds-higher/GxwQD71DnGyvlPVo>). [MyBroadband's table of Starlink status in African countries, dated 17 July 2026, lists Liberia as 'Available' for Starlink](<https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html>). [LTA Board Chairman Clarence Massaquoi said in an interview that Starlink has entered Liberia ('Starlink is here today') while the LTA drafts its first satellite regulations; the article does not describe a licence or subscriber count](<https://fpa.news/liberia-lta-unveils-sweeping-telecom-reforms-revokes-star-cell-license-after-six-years-of-inactivity/>). [Le360 lists Liberia among 29 African countries where Starlink has been granted a licence as of May 2026](<https://afrique.le360.ma/economie/internet-haut-debit-en-seulement-4-ans-starlink-operationnel-dans-un-pays-africain-sur-deux_UYXWC5RPWNGGFIYLU5VHL6L7ZU/>). These place satellite broadband licensed and on sale, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Services on a second satellite network are not stated. Prices and speeds are given where a source states them; they set no stage.

**Subscribers counted by the regulator.** Nothing found gives a regulator's count of the subscribers to [the service](<https://itweb.africa/article/starlink-drives-african-broadband-speeds-higher/GxwQD71DnGyvlPVo>). Only a count the regulator publishes, from the past three years, answers this question; counts given by an operator or an analyst do not.
