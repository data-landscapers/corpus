# DT-related training in secondary education: internet in secondary schools

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The AU Digital Education Strategy asks for half of institutions connected by 2027 and a digital literacy and coding framework for all students. The lines are the frame's; this study does not measure a country against the norm.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | The latest figure, for 2023 or later, is below 17 |
| 2 Nascent | Yes to 1 |
| 3 Established | Yes to 2 |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 3 |
| 5 Leading | Yes to 3 and 4 |

## Countries

### Namibia: 5 Leading

In 2024, 81.2 per cent of secondary schools had internet for teaching, and digital literacy or computing is compulsory in the secondary curriculum.

- 81.2 per cent of secondary schools in Namibia had internet for teaching in 2024, by UNESCO's SDG 4.a.1 series ([source, 2024](https://databrowser.uis.unesco.org/)).
- The NIED Information and Communication Syllabus (Grade 8 & 9) states the subject is "compulsory, non-promotional (but assessable)" for Grades 4-12, covering digital, media and information literacy, implemented in Grade 8 in 2025 and Grade 9 in 2026 ([source, 2024-12](https://www.nied.edu.na/assets/documents/02Syllabuses/04JuniorSecondary/InformationandCommunication/JSInformationandCommunicationSyllabusDecember2025.pdf)).
