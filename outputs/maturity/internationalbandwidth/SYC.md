# Seychelles

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [The World Bank project information document says Seychelles has strong international connectivity through three submarine cables](<http://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099100726053521931>). [SCS states PEACE is Seychelles' second submarine fibre cable landing, in service from 16 November 2022 alongside the SEAS cable commissioned over ten years earlier, giving two submarine routes; the SEAS landing station is not named](<https://www.nation.sc/articles/16417/seychelles-achieves-security-in-international-connectivity-peace-operational-in-seychelles>). [Seychelles Cable Systems (SCS), via a press release in Seychelles Nation, states the PEACE submarine cable (Pakistan, Seychelles, Kenya, Djibouti, France) landed at Ile Perseverance with its cable landing station at SCS House, and that operators in Seychelles could use it for traffic from 16 November 2022](<https://www.nation.sc/articles/16417/seychelles-achieves-security-in-international-connectivity-peace-operational-in-seychelles>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [SCS states operators in Seychelles could, from 16 November 2022, use the PEACE system to carry their traffic, the system being owned by SCS, a public-private vehicle of the government, Cable & Wireless Seychelles and Airtel Seychelles](<https://www.nation.sc/articles/16417/seychelles-achieves-security-in-international-connectivity-peace-operational-in-seychelles>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
