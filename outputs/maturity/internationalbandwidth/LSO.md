# Lesotho

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [The LCA/ITU sector review (data collected August 2016) states that Lesotho's operators draw international bandwidth from the WACS, EASSy and SEACOM cables, which Lesotho, being landlocked, reaches via South Africa: Vodacom Lesotho via its group's ownership of WACS and EASSy, Econet Telecom Lesotho via SEACOM and EASSy capacity through Liquid Telecom's terrestrial fibre](<https://lca.org.ls/wp-content/uploads/filr/2334/Final%20Integrated%20Report%2013022018.pdf>). [The LCA 2010/11 annual report states that the EASSy submarine cable was completed ahead of schedule, Lesotho being a participant, and that the country is now connected through terrestrial backhaul (dated by the 2010/11 reporting year; the document gives no month)](<https://lca.org.ls/wp-content/uploads/filr/2326/lca_annual_report_2010-11.pdf>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://lca.org.ls/wp-content/uploads/filr/2334/Final%20Integrated%20Report%2013022018.pdf>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
