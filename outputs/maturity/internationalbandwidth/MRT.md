# Mauritania

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Agence Ecofin says Mauritania has been connected to the ACE cable since 2011 and still depends heavily on it, with no fully operational alternative, so ACE outages disrupt internet nationwide; ACE is the single route in service](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/0505-138141-internet-la-mauritanie-amorce-le-deploiement-de-son-deuxieme-cable-sous-marin>). [DatacenterDynamics reports that only one subsea cable currently lands in Mauritania, the ACE system at Nouakchott, in which International Mauritania Telecom is the Mauritanian participant](<https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ellalink-to-expand-subsea-cable-to-mauritania/>). [The ACE consortium says the ACE cable was put into service on 15 December 2012 and lists Nouakchott as one of its landing stations, the first international submarine cable to land in Mauritania](<https://ace-submarinecable.com/en/submarine-cable/>). These place one international fibre route in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A second route in service is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [EllaLink says it has built a new neutral cable landing station in Nouadhibou that lets Mauritania's national operators access the infrastructure via two diverse routes (landing marked 4 May 2026; the document does not say the branch is yet in service)](<https://ella.link/press-releases/ellalink-subsea-cable-lands-mauritania/>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
