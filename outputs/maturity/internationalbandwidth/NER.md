# Niger

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Maroc Telecom's 5,700 km transafrican terrestrial cable from Morocco to Niger via Mauritania, Mali and Burkina Faso, recorded as completed in 2022, lets Moov Africa Niger connect to international bandwidth via the Maroc Telecom network (deal record for the e& financing; the record's own 'Connects to submarine cable at CIV and TGO' note is not a Niger landing)](<https://leseco.ma/business/maroc-telecom-plus-de-454-mmdh-investis-en-afrique.html>). [Niger's ANP reports that Niger's and Algeria's ministers presided on 6 October 2026 at Assamaka over the official launch of operation of the trans-Saharan fibre backbone linking Niger to Algeria; it gives no capacity and does not say that Niger's international traffic runs over it](<https://anp.ne/niger-algerie-lancement-a-assamaka-de-lexploitation-de-la-dorsale-transsaharienne-a-fibre-optique/>). These place one international fibre route in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A second route in service is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://leseco.ma/business/maroc-telecom-plus-de-454-mmdh-investis-en-afrique.html>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
