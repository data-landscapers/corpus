# Benin

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Mobile Europe (15 Jul 2026) reports that CSquared's existing West Africa architecture includes the Equiano cable landing in Nigeria/Togo and reaching Benin (with Ghana and Burkina Faso) via terrestrial backbone](<https://www.mobileeurope.co.uk/csquared-boosts-internet-resilence-integrating-with-2africa-west/>). [The deal record states Maroc Telecom's West Africa submarine cable lands at Cotonou, Benin, among its landing points, with the south segment (Casablanca to Cote d'Ivoire, Togo, Benin, Gabon, Mauritania) operational in July 2021](<https://medias24.com/2023/06/01/150-millions-deuros-pour-le-west-africa-cable-sous-marin-a-fibre-optique-de-maroc-telecom/>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://www.mobileeurope.co.uk/csquared-boosts-internet-resilence-integrating-with-2africa-west/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
