# Egypt

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Telecom Egypt's brochure says it owns and operates 10 in-service subsea landing stations](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [They are Abu Talat 1, Abu Talat 2, Alexandria, Hod el Dars, Port Said 1, Ras Ghareb, Sidi Kerir, Suez, Zafarana 1 and Zafarana 2](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [Its network connects 12 subsea systems from the East and 14 from the West](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [Routes therefore land at different stations on the Red Sea and Mediterranean coasts](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [The brochure is dated 2025-01-10 (PDF creation date)](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [Capacity reports Telecom Egypt completed SEA-ME-WE-6 landings at Port Said and Ras Ghareb in July 2025](<https://capacityglobal.com/news/telecom-egypt-5pbs-red-sea-bypass/>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station. That is the third of the study's questions, and the stage rests on that and on the sale of capacity below. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Telecom Egypt describes WeConnect as an open, neutral, transparent ecosystem for cross-connecting subsea cable systems landing at different Egyptian stations](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [It lists international operators, local ISPs and cloud/OTT players as clients, and describes its Smart Village data hub as open access](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [The brochure is dated 2025-01-10](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [WeConnect lets partners click-to-order cross-connectivity between the 14 subsea cable systems at its 10 Egyptian cable stations, on an open and neutral model](<https://ir.te.eg/en/CorporateNews/PressRelease/184/Telecom-Egypt-Announces-the-Launch-of-WeConnect>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable. It also counts where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
