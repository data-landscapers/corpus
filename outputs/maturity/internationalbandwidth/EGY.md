# Egypt

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Telecom Egypt's brochure says it owns and operates 10 in-service subsea landing stations (Abu Talat 1, Abu Talat 2, Alexandria, Hod el Dars, Port Said 1, Ras Ghareb, Sidi Kerir, Suez, Zafarana 1, Zafarana 2) and its network connects 12 subsea systems from the East and 14 from the West, so routes land at different stations on the Red Sea and Mediterranean coasts; brochure dated 2025-01-10 (PDF creation date)](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [Capacity reports Telecom Egypt completed SEA-ME-WE-6 landings at Port Said and Ras Ghareb in July 2025](<https://capacityglobal.com/news/telecom-egypt-5pbs-red-sea-bypass/>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, the third of the study's questions, and the stage rests on that and on the sale of capacity below. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Telecom Egypt describes WeConnect as an open, neutral, transparent ecosystem for cross-connecting subsea cable systems landing at different Egyptian stations, with international operators, local ISPs and cloud/OTT players listed as clients, and describes its Smart Village data hub as open access; brochure dated 2025-01-10](<https://te.eg/interactivemap/assets/downloads/Telecom_Egypt_The_Digital_Hub.pdf>). [Telecom Egypt's WeConnect platform lets partners click-to-order cross-connectivity between the 14 subsea cable systems at its 10 Egyptian cable stations on an open and neutral model](<https://ir.te.eg/en/CorporateNews/PressRelease/184/Telecom-Egypt-Announces-the-Launch-of-WeConnect>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
