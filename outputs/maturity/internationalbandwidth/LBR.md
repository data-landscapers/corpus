# Liberia

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [TechAfrica News reports Liberian officials saying the country depends on a single international route, the ACE submarine cable, landing at one point in Monrovia, a single point of failure](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/06/16/liberia-advances-regional-fiber-optic-expansion-strategy-through-transco-clsg-partnership/>). [LTA Chairman Clarence Massaquoi said Liberia has one submarine cable already landed in Monrovia (cable not named), with a second landing at Buchanan sought but not yet built](<https://www.liberianobserver.com/news/liberia-on-the-verge-of-5g-revolution/article_992b027e-4c8a-4578-bf70-baf2e2eabeeb.html>). [The Conversation Africa (Jess Auerbach Jahajeeah, 2024-03-15) states that in Liberia and Sierra Leone only one fibre optic cable comes into the country, so internet traffic stops when it breaks; Liberia was among the countries worst hit by the 14 March 2024 cable failures](<https://theconversation.com/undersea-cables-for-africas-internet-retrace-history-225912>). [The ACE Consortium says the ACE cable was put into service on 15 December 2012, with a landing station at Monrovia, and that it is the first international submarine cable to land in Liberia](<https://ace-submarinecable.com/en/submarine-cable/>). These place one international fibre route in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A second route in service is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing held says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/06/16/liberia-advances-regional-fiber-optic-expansion-strategy-through-transco-clsg-partnership/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
