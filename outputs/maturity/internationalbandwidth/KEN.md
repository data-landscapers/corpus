# Kenya

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** Kenya's international traffic leaves over several submarine cables, all landing at Mombasa. [The Communications Authority's June 2026 figures put international bandwidth at 28,950 Gbps over seven cable systems](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/19/kenya-fixed-internet-fibre-wireless-satellite-growth/>). They are SEACOM, TEAMS, EASSy, Lion2, DARE1, PEACE and 2Africa. [Business Daily, citing TeleGeography, counts seven subsea cable landings](<https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/kenya-to-get-two-new-undersea-fibre-optic-cables-in-ai-race-5547996>). [In November 2026 Kenya Engineer also named seven submarine cable connections serving Mombasa](<https://www.kenyaengineer.co.ke/amaco-and-arise-iip-advance-us700-million-power-and-ai-infrastructure-project-in-mombasa/>). [Seacom says traffic moves inland from the Mombasa landing stations](<https://seacom.co.ke/news/seacom-deploys-a-new-high-capacity-nairobikampala-route-to-strengthen-east-africas-digital-backbone>), over a Nairobi-Kisumu-Kampala route it opened in June 2026. No source names separate landing stations for any two cables.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Telkom Kenya calls its Milimani Exchange open access](<https://telkom.co.ke/press-release/telkom-launches-ksh-150m-data-centre-to-boost-colocation-and-data-back-up/>). There, in 2018, other clients could buy international capacity on TEAMS, EASSy and LION2. No newer source on wholesale terms is on file.
