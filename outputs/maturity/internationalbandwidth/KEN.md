# Kenya

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Existing submarine cables at Mombasa connect Kenya to global internet networks (plural; no names or count given)](<https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/business/article/2001557487/new-submarine-cable-puts-kenyas-coast-on-digital-growth-path>). [Existing submarine cables are already landed at Mombasa, which the LuLu system is to connect to](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/09/lulu-cable-system-kenya-coast-launch/>). [TechTrends Kenya says systems (plural, unnamed, count not given) land on Kenya's coast at Mombasa and give routes into global internet infrastructure, with terrestrial fibre carrying traffic to Nairobi](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/15/kenya-ai-data-centre-investment-race/>). [Business Daily, citing TeleGeography, says Kenya currently has seven subsea cable landings, fifth in Africa](<https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/kenya-to-get-two-new-undersea-fibre-optic-cables-in-ai-race-5547996>). [Reporting the Communications Authority of Kenya's June 2026 statistics, Kenya's international bandwidth of 28,950 Gbps runs over seven cable systems: SEACOM, TEAMS, EASSy, Lion2, DARE1, PEACE and 2Africa; landing stations not given](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/19/kenya-fixed-internet-fibre-wireless-satellite-growth/>). [Seacom states that traffic moves inland from subsea cable landing stations in Mombasa, Kenya, over its Nairobi-Kisumu-Kampala route launched 17 June 2026](<https://seacom.co.ke/news/seacom-deploys-a-new-high-capacity-nairobikampala-route-to-strengthen-east-africas-digital-backbone>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Telkom Kenya describes its Milimani Exchange facility as open access, giving local and international clients access to international capacity on TEAMS, EASSy and LION2](<https://telkom.co.ke/press-release/telkom-launches-ksh-150m-data-centre-to-boost-colocation-and-data-back-up/>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
