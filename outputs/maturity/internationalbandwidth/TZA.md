# Tanzania

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [The Horohoro redundancy route launched with Kenya gives Tanzania a land path to eight undersea cables via Kenya, alongside its three marine cable connections in Dar es Salaam, per Minister Silaa](<https://dailynews.co.tz/new-fibre-link-to-boost-east-africas-digital-resilience/>). [African Business says international traffic reaches Dar es Salaam via several subsea cables, and that Wingu's Dar es Salaam data centre has direct access to the SEACOM, EASSy and 2Africa cables](<https://african.business/2026/08/free-article/tanzanias-digital-dividend>). [Minister Kairuki said in a 18 July 2026 speech that Tanzania has four submarine cable stations (vituo vinne vya mikongo ya bahari), without naming the cables or stations](<https://www.mawasiliano.go.tz/news/tanzania-yaonesha-mafanikio-ya-utekelezaji-wa-akili-unde-ai-katika-mkutano-wa-kimataifa-wa-waico-2026>). [The Citizen reports Tanzania is linked to the SEACOM, EASSy and expanding 2Africa submarine cable systems, and that submarine cables land in Dar es Salaam (all at one city, so two routes, not independent](<https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/wingu-africa-now-targets-tanzania-as-region-s-digital-hub-5441614>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://dailynews.co.tz/new-fibre-link-to-boost-east-africas-digital-resilience/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
