# Mali

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Afribone Mali says its international links run over optical fibre through Dakar and Abidjan to the ACE and MainOne submarine cables](<https://afribone.com/a-propos/>). [SMTD-SA (Malian government information portal) states the Mali and Senegal national fibre backbones were interconnected and inaugurated on 5 October 2026 at Kidira-Diboli under ROA-BN, giving onward access to the submarine cable from Portugal and Cape Verde](<https://gouvernement.ml/cooperation-numerique-et-technologique-les-backbones-du-mali-et-du-senegal-sinterconnectent/>). [Agence Ecofin reports SMTD-SA and Senegal Numerique commissioned on 5 October 2026 a fibre link at Kidira-Diboli joining the Malian and Senegalese backbones, giving landlocked Mali a route to submarine cables (2Africa, ACE, SAT-3 landing in Senegal) and described as an additional corridor to a pre-existing terrestrial axis it does not name](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/0810-142240-le-mali-ouvre-une-nouvelle-route-vers-l-internet-mondial-par-le-senegal>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://afribone.com/a-propos/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
