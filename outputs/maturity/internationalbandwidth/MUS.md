# Mauritius

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Journal du Dimanche states that the existing LION and LION2 cables carry much of Mauritius's international communications today, with landing stations not named](<https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/>). [Mauritius Telecom's CEO states in a LinkedIn post that Mauritius has five submarine cables (cables not named); 2026-02-22](<https://lexpress.mu/node/554669>). [Telecom Review Africa states Mauritius is linked to four submarine cable systems, SAFE, LION, LION2 and METISS, with landing stations not named](<https://www.telecomreviewafrica.com/articles/features/12397-sublime-seas-to-storage-sphere-mauritius-expands-hyperscale-data-hub/>). [Xalam Analytics states Mauritius has four high-capacity submarine cables in service (cables not named), with a fifth on the way; chart shows 4 cables in 2024E against 2 in 2020](<https://cms.d4dhub.eu/assets/Initiatives/Data-Governance-in-Africa/Digital-Investment-Facility/2507_Country-Market-Briefs/Data-Center-Market-Brief-Mauritius.pdf>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
