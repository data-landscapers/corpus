# Mozambique

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Submarine Networks (31 July 2025) states 2Africa has two Mozambique landing points, Maputo and Nacala, and that EASSy and SEACOM both land in Maputo, so a cable in service at Nacala is separate from those at Maputo](<https://www.submarinenetworks.com/en/systems/asia-europe-africa/2africa/wiocc-and-tmcel-partner-to-boost-2africa-connectivity-in-mozambique>). [MoRENet's deck (30 October 2025) states its international capacity is 60 Gbps with active redundancy over two continental routes (Atlantic via a Cape Town PoP onto AmLight-SACS, and East African routes with IP transit via WIOCC); before this it had 5 Gbps over a single route through South Africa](<https://africarxiv.ubuntunet.net/bitstreams/9427cb02-feef-4b3f-b03b-84cb4efea3cb/download>). [Submarine Networks states that before 2Africa arrived only two international subsea cables connected Mozambique, EASSy and SEACOM, both landing in Maputo](<https://www.submarinenetworks.com/en/systems/asia-europe-africa/2africa/wiocc-and-tmcel-partner-to-boost-2africa-connectivity-in-mozambique>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, the third of the study's questions, and the stage rests on that and on the sale of capacity below. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [WIOCC and Tmcel signed a framework agreement on 23 July 2025 in Maputo under which WIOCC delivers capacity on the 2Africa East subsea cable and terrestrial routes into South Africa to Tmcel, as reported by Submarine Networks](<https://www.submarinenetworks.com/en/systems/asia-europe-africa/2africa/wiocc-and-tmcel-partner-to-boost-2africa-connectivity-in-mozambique>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
