# DR Congo

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Paratus Group (press release republished by ITWeb Africa) says it switched on on 2026-02-09 the 2,000km protected terrestrial G2M fibre route from Goma in eastern DRC via Kigali (Rwanda), Kampala (Uganda) and Nairobi (Kenya) to Mombasa on the coast, now live and carrying traffic, linking DRC to coastal subsea cable capacity](<https://itweb.africa/article/paratus-lights-up-new-east-africa-fibre-highway-linking-goma-to-mombasa/wbrpOqg2aJQMDLZn>). [Paratus says its 2,000 km Goma-to-Mombasa (G2M) terrestrial fibre route from Goma in eastern DRC, through Kigali (Rwanda), Kampala and Nairobi, is live and carrying traffic, giving access to subsea capacity landing at Mombasa](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/09/paratus-expands-east-africa-connectivity-itw-africa/>). [Airtel Congo RDC SA says it became the first operator in DRC to put the first phase of the 2Africa submarine cable into service, activating an initial 200 Gbps on 2025-12-18; the document names no landing station](<https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/airteldrc_airtel-congo-rdc-sa-1er-r%C3%A9seau-%C3%A0-mettre-activity-7407828782549655552-0p5z>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, the third of the study's questions, and the stage rests on that and on the sale of capacity below. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Paratus says the G2M Goma-Mombasa route is already carrying traffic for its first wholesale customers and is designed for carriers, ISPs and enterprises, i.e. operators other than the owner buy capacity on it](<https://itweb.africa/article/paratus-lights-up-new-east-africa-fibre-highway-linking-goma-to-mombasa/wbrpOqg2aJQMDLZn>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
