# Botswana

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Botswana's National Broadband Strategy (June 2018) states Botswana is connected to two submarine cables, EASSy and WACS, with the submarine cable landing stations reached through Namibia and South Africa, two different neighbouring countries](<https://www.bocra.org.bw/sites/default/files/documents/National-Broadband-Strategy-FINAL%28June2018%29.pdf>). [BoFiNet (Botswana Fibre Networks) announced on 2026-09-23 a long-term international capacity lease with an unnamed South African partner, raising its available international capacity through the Ramatlabama and Pioneer Gate border crossings (both into South Africa) from 300 Gbit/s to 1 Tbit/s; two crossings, one neighbour, so not independent](<https://www.connectingafrica.com/fiber-networking/bofinet-upgrades-its-available-international-capacity-to-1tbit-s>). [BoFiNet's Integrated Report 2025 (financial year to 31 March 2025) states Botswana has 3 international submarine cable systems, EASSy, WACS and Equiano, and that BoFiNet manages EASSy and WACS international cable access](<https://www.bofinet.co.bw/uploads/571/01M07F2TQ2HE6M06GD31SSDFZG.pdf>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://www.bocra.org.bw/sites/default/files/documents/National-Broadband-Strategy-FINAL%28June2018%29.pdf>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
