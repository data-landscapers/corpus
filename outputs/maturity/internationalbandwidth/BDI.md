# Burundi

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [ARCT's 2020 ICT indicators report (July 2021) states Burundi has access to submarine cables (EASSy, SEACOM, TEAMS) via partners and cable operators in neighbouring countries, and lists cross-border fibre connection points with Rwanda (Gasenyi, Kanyaru Haut), the DRC (Ruhwa, Gatumba) and Tanzania (Kobero, Mugina/Manyovu)](<https://arct.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/RAPPORT-SUR-LES-INDICATEURS-TIC-2020-VERSION-PUBLIE.pdf>). [TTCL, through the National ICT Broadband Backbone, and Burundi Backbone System agreed to raise connectivity capacity to Burundi from 4Gbps to 10Gbps, so a terrestrial Tanzania-Burundi link was already carrying 4Gbps; reported by Tech Africa News on 9 September 2026](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/09/09/ttcl-bbs-increase-tanzania-burundi-connectivity-capacity-10gbps/>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://arct.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/RAPPORT-SUR-LES-INDICATEURS-TIC-2020-VERSION-PUBLIE.pdf>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
