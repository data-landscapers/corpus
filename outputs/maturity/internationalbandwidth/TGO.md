# Togo

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Lomé Data Centre states its own direct access to four submarine cables, ACE, 2Africa, WACS and Equiano, with no landing stations named; operator self-description, page dated 2025-09-24](<https://lomedatacentre.tg/en/n-home-page-en/>). [Mobile Europe (15 Jul 2026) reports CSquared's existing architecture includes the Equiano system landing in Nigeria and Togo, reaching Benin, Ghana and Burkina Faso via terrestrial backbone](<https://www.mobileeurope.co.uk/csquared-boosts-internet-resilence-integrating-with-2africa-west/>). [Data Landscapers' table of African-owned infrastructure companies lists CSquared, operating in Togo among other countries, with one cable landing station, for the Equiano cable at Lomé](<https://data-landscapers.com/2026/06/11/sovereign-infrastructure/>). [ISOC-Togo, reported by republicoftogo.com, says Togo stayed connected through Google's Equiano cable during the March 2024 submarine cable cuts](<https://www.republicoftogo.com/toutes-les-rubriques/high-tech/gouvernance-numerique-connecter-les-zones-oubliees>). [Data Landscapers' audit of Google says the Equiano cable has been operational since 2022 with landings in Togo, Nigeria, Namibia, St Helena and South Africa](<https://data-landscapers.com/2026/05/20/google-in-africa/>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Ecofin reports Liquid Intelligent Technologies acquired a fibre pair (up to 12 Tbps) on Google's Equiano cable, which landed in Togo days earlier](<https://www.ecofinagency.com/telecom/3003-43499->). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
