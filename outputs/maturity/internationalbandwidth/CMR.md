# Cameroon

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Africa Business Insight (2026-05-25) states that Cameroon is connected to five submarine cable systems, without naming them or their landing stations](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>). [Xalam Analytics states that Cameroon is connected to four submarine cables offering direct routes to Europe, South Africa and the Americas; the cables and landing stations are not named in the passages](<https://cms.d4dhub.eu/assets/Initiatives/Data-Governance-in-Africa/Digital-Investment-Facility/Data-Center-Market-Briefs-Cameroon-2026.pdf>). [L'Economie reports that the October 2025 internet disruption in Cameroon was attributed to a problem on the WACS submarine cable, described as the cable that links the country to the global network](<https://leconomie.info/extension-du-reseau-de-fibre-optique-le-cameroun-sendette-a-hauteur-de-108-milliards-de-fcfa-aupres-de-la-chine/>). [MINPOSTEL's Secretary General reported that in 2025 Camtel raised the capacity of the NCSCS submarine cable from 100 Gbps to 300 Gbps, with a study towards 2.4 Tbps](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/sanction-des-operateurs-telecoms-fibre-5g-cybersecurite-startups-au-cameroun/>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
