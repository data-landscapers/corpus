# Cameroon

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Africa Business Insight said in May 2026 that Cameroon is connected to five submarine cable systems](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>). [Xalam Analytics counts four, with direct routes to Europe, South Africa and the Americas](<https://cms.d4dhub.eu/assets/Initiatives/Data-Governance-in-Africa/Digital-Investment-Facility/Data-Center-Market-Briefs-Cameroon-2026.pdf>). [In July 2025 Camtel staff said its network is meshed and redundant, with four submarine cables giving several exits](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-camtel-projette-de-creer-9-datacenters-decentralises-pour-les-prochaines-annees/>). [In June 2025 ST Digital's chief executive also spoke of several cables](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/datacenters-certifies-ecosysteme-delaisse-le-paradoxe-de-la-gouvernance-numerique-au-cameroun/>). [The internet disruption of October 2025 was put down to a fault on the WACS cable](<https://leconomie.info/extension-du-reseau-de-fibre-optique-le-cameroun-sendette-a-hauteur-de-108-milliards-de-fcfa-aupres-de-la-chine/>). [In 2025 Camtel raised the NCSCS cable's capacity from 100 Gbps to 300 Gbps, MINPOSTEL's secretary general said](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/sanction-des-operateurs-telecoms-fibre-5g-cybersecurite-startups-au-cameroun/>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable. Open-access or regulated wholesale terms also count.
