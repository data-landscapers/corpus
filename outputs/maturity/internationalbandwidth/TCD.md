# Chad

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** In February 2026 [Connecting Africa reported that the N'Djamena-Mberé fibre route to the Cameroonian border is Chad's only entry point for capacity from submarine cables](<https://www.connectingafrica.com/fiber-networking/chad-tells-operators-to-fast-track-fiber-link-to-cameroon>). Those cables all land in Cameroon. [Chad's telecoms minister said in October 2026 that the country has only one active international internet access, through Cameroon](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/tchad-le-plan-de-connexion-au-reseau-internet-mondial-presente-au-gaids-a-seoul/>). [In March 2026 Chad told ITU-D that a 35 km fibre link to Cameroon is its only operational link to the internet](<https://www.itu.int/md/D26-SG01-C-0040/en>). [The link is joined to Camtel's network and the SAT3 cable](<https://www.itu.int/md/D26-SG01-C-0040/en>). In May 2025 [the director general of the regulator ARCEP had said Chad has gateways with Cameroon and Sudan](<https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/connectivite-au-tchad-entre-vulnerabilite-et-ambitions-de-transformation-numerique/>). He said one cut can stop service for days. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://www.connectingafrica.com/fiber-networking/chad-tells-operators-to-fast-track-fiber-link-to-cameroon>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
