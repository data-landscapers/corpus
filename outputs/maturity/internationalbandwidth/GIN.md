# Guinea

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [On 27 October 2026 the ministry's secretary general told MPs that Guinea depends on a single submarine cable, at the end of its life and close to saturation](<https://mediaguinee.com/2026/10/loi-de-finances-rectificative-medias-publics-en-difficulte-et-risques-de-coupure-dinternet-au-coeur-des-echanges-avec-les-deputes/>). [The ACE consortium says ACE landed at Conakry as Guinea's first international cable and entered service on 15 December 2012](<https://ace-submarinecable.com/en/submarine-cable/>). [In May 2026 GUILAB's director general said ACE is still the only cable and nearing critical load](<https://mediaguinee.com/2026/05/2e-cable-sous-marin-la-guinee-propulse-sa-souverainete-numerique-avec-la-signature-de-laccord-medusa-africa/>). [In July 2025 he said it carried about 54% of the country's connectivity](<https://mediaguinee.com/2025/07/atterrissement-dun-second-cable-sous-marin-en-guinee-la-guilab-et-ses-partenaires-se-concertent-a-conakry/>). [The Medusa cable, signed in May 2026, is to be Guinea's second](<https://medusascs.com/news/guinea-seals-its-alliance-with-medusa-submarine-cable-system-for-a-second-submarine-cable/>). These place one international fibre route in service. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [GUILAB runs the ACE landing station and manages Guinea's capacity on the cable](<https://www.ecofinagency.com/telecom/1105-36842-guilab-1st-iso-9001-2015-certified-operator-in-guinea>). Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable. Open-access or regulated wholesale terms also count.
