# Comoros

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Digital Watch Observatory states Comoros is linked to regional and global fibre systems including EASSy, FLY-LION3 and 2Africa, with Moroni a multi-cable landing point (three named systems, one landing point)](<http://web.archive.org/web/20260824175002/https://dig.watch/countries/comoros>). [The Comorian minister states that 2Africa will be a fourth international submarine cable joining those already operational, i.e. three in service; the cables and landing stations are not named](<https://pp.itu.int/2022/en/itu_policy_statements/kamalidini-souef-comoros/>). [Comores Câbles states on its own undated corporate page that the 2Africa submarine cable has been put into service; the page lists EASSy and FLY-LION3 as regional systems without saying they land in Comoros](<https://www.comorescables.km/Technologies>). [Huawei Marine announced it delivered the 260km Avassa submarine cable for Comoros Telecom and Mayotte carrier STOI, linking Grande Comore, Anjouan and Mayotte, described as the second international submarine cable between Comoros and Mayotte and as securing Comoros' international gateway](<https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2016/11/avassa-submarine-cable-project>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<http://web.archive.org/web/20260824175002/https://dig.watch/countries/comoros>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
