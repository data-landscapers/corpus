# Gambia

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [GAMTEL states two international routes in service: the ACE cable, and a 28 Gbps backup link with Free (Senegal) on Cross Gambia (CG2) to which traffic is automatically rerouted if ACE fails](<https://gamtel.gm/about-us/>). [GAMTEL says it is connected to the ACE (Africa Coast to Europe) cable through 4 separate international gateways, with 25,000 Megabit capacity from Portugal and France Telecoms](<https://gamtel.gm/about-us/>). [TechGriot reports The Gambia remains structurally dependent on a single undersea cable, the ACE submarine cable, with terrestrial redundancy through Senegal still thin](<https://techgriot.co/english/africa/2026/09/senegal-and-gambias-digital-deal-is-really-about-cables-and-spectrum/>). [The Point reports, citing an unnamed source, that the ACE submarine cable station in Sukuta is the facility connecting The Gambia to international internet networks and lost power on 4 June 2026, causing a nationwide outage; a temporary outage, not a route loss](<https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/nation-hit-by-internet-outage-amid-ongoing-power-cuts>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://gamtel.gm/about-us/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
