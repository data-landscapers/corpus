# Eswatini

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [UASF states EPTC's connectivity relied on an upstream provider in South Africa through its North, West and South routes (three routes, all via South Africa)](<https://uasf.esccom.org.sz/programmes/connectivity-programmes/>). [ESCCOM's investigation confirmed that Jenny Internet Eswatini had established and was using a direct cross-border fibre link into Eswatini at the Malutha Border Post, bypassing EPTC's backbone (the neighbouring country is not named); the Commission ordered Jenny to use EPTC backbone connectivity from 1 July 2026](<https://esccom.org.sz/publications/notices/docs/EPTC%20AND%20JENNY%20FINAL%20%20DECISION%20Notice%205.pdf>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://uasf.esccom.org.sz/programmes/connectivity-programmes/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
