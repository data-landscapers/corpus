# Gabon

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [TM Africa states Gabon already lands four subsea cable systems (2Africa, ACE, Maroc Telecom's West Africa cable and SAT-3/WASC); landing stations are not named for them](<https://tmafrica.co.ug/news/gabon-builds-digital-state-shrinking-oil-base>). [SPIN's ACE Gabon page states ACE is the second submarine cable operated in Gabon, an alternative to SAT-3 operated by Gabon Telecom; ACE Gabon holds and operates the Libreville landing station (page date 2019-01-24, no event date given)](<https://spin.ga/nos-activites/ace-gabon/>). [Axione Gabon states the ACE submarine cable system links Gabon to the international network and carried a significant share of national traffic, with full service restored on 4 June 2026 after a fault on its terrestrial section on 27-28 May 2026 (a repaired outage, so the route is in service)](<https://axionegabon.com/details-d-actualite/communique-incident-sur-cable-sous-marin-ace>). These place two or more international fibre routes in service, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That two of them share no landing station is not stated. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://tmafrica.co.ug/news/gabon-builds-digital-state-shrinking-oil-base>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
