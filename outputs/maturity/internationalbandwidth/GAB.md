# Gabon

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [TM Africa says Gabon lands four subsea cable systems: 2Africa, ACE, Maroc Telecom's West Africa cable and SAT-3/WASC](<https://tmafrica.co.ug/news/gabon-builds-digital-state-shrinking-oil-base>). [SPIN's ACE Gabon page says ACE was the second submarine cable operated in Gabon, after Gabon Telecom's SAT-3](<https://spin.ga/nos-activites/ace-gabon/>). [It says ACE Gabon operates the Libreville landing station](<https://spin.ga/nos-activites/ace-gabon/>). [Axione Gabon says ACE carried a significant share of national traffic](<https://axionegabon.com/details-d-actualite/communique-incident-sur-cable-sous-marin-ace>). [It says full service was restored on 4 June 2026 after a fault on 27-28 May](<https://axionegabon.com/details-d-actualite/communique-incident-sur-cable-sous-marin-ace>). [In October 2026 SPIN's director-general said the national backbone links Gabon physically with Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Congo, though cross-border traffic stays low](<https://agpgabon.ga/gabon-numerique-interview-bertrand-mahoukou-bitchinda-expose-la-strategie-de-la-spin-pour-etendre-la-fibre-optique/>). No source found says 2Africa, or another cable of more than 100 Tbps design capacity, is in service carrying Gabon's traffic. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://tmafrica.co.ug/news/gabon-builds-digital-state-shrinking-oil-base>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
