# Central African Republic

## Fibre routes to the rest of the world

**One route, two, and two independent.** [Africa Business Insight (25 May 2026) states that under the Central African Backbone the Central African Republic established fibre links with Cameroon (five submarine cables) and the Republic of the Congo (two), two different neighbours](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>). [Agence Ecofin (12 Oct 2026) states the Central African Republic has two terrestrial fibre links to neighbours today, through Cameroon and through Congo-Brazzaville, carried on the Central African Backbone (about 935 km of fibre) and reaching the submarine cables](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/1210-142321-internet-qu-est-ce-que-la-liaison-par-fibre-avec-la-rdc-peut-apporter-a-la-centrafrique>). [Camerounlink/Agence Ecofin (1 Sep 2026) states Cameroon is the Central African Republic's main gateway to submarine cables via the Central Africa Backbone, and that the planned Congo-Brazzaville interconnection (WACS, 2Africa) remains to be realised per a ministry official on 30 Aug 2026; a Cameroon fibre cut disrupted CAR internet the week before](<https://www.camerounlink.com/news/2026/9/1/acces-aux-cables-sous-marins-quelles-options-pour-la-centrafrique-au-dela-du-cameroun-156491/>). Together these place two international fibre routes in service that share no landing station, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: one route, two, and two independent of each other. Bandwidth is given where a source states it; bandwidth sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [these routes](<https://africabusinessinsight.com/central-african-republic-seeks-new/>), or that they run on open-access or wholesale terms. Capacity counts as sold where operators other than the owner buy it at the landing station or on the cable, or where either runs on open-access or regulated wholesale terms.
