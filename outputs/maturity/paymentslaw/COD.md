# DR Congo

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** [Loi n° 18-019 of 9 July 2018](<https://anapi.cd/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/221.07.18-Loi-du-9-juillet-2018_Systemes-de-paiement-1.pdf>) on payment systems and securities settlement systems is the Democratic Republic of the Congo's payments law. It was published in the Journal Officiel on 23 July 2018. Orange Money RDC [says it is governed by the central bank's e-money regulation and its instructions for e-money institutions](<https://www.orange.cd/particuliers/resources/other/RAPPORT%20PILIER%20III%20OMRDC_Exercice%20S1%202024%20-%20VF.pdf>), Instruction 55 among them. No amendment to the law is reported.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [The central bank's map of the payment system lists the providers it has approved](<https://www.bcc.cd/intermediaires-financiers/systemes-de-paiement/infrastructures>). At end-2023 they included five non-bank financial companies, e-money issuers among them, and 81 money-transfer firms. Orange Money RDC, a company owned by Orange RDC and CECOM, [has held an e-money institution licence since 29 September 2016](<https://www.orange.cd/particuliers/resources/other/RAPPORT%20PILIER%20III%20OMRDC_Exercice%20S1%202024%20-%20VF.pdf>). The central bank [licensed Wave as an e-money institution in November 2026](<https://leconomie.info/article/rdc-wave-decroche-le-feu-vert-reglementaire-de-la-banque-centrale-pour-lancer-ses-operations>). [Instruction No. 58 of September 2024](<https://regalert.today/document/instruction-no-58-on-the-interoperability-of-electronic-payment-systems-and-part-34b2c78f-c351-4d8d-a6ee-2eba0ba23e98>) obliges every regulated entity to join the National E-Payment Switch, [called Mosolo](<https://decrypt-eco.net/2026/09/29/rdc-le-cegft-propose-six-mesures-pour-accompagner-la-bcc-sur-linteroperabilite/>), within six months. That covers banks, financial companies, postal services and aggregators, under sanction. Settlement runs through the central bank's SAREC system.
