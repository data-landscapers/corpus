# Morocco

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** Morocco's [Law 103-12 on credit institutions](<https://www.bkam.ma/content/download/757666/8545663/loi%20bancaire%20fr%2022.pdf>), enacted on 24 December 2014, defines means of payment, electronic money among them, and payment services, and regulates payment institutions. Bank Al-Maghrib supervises them; in July 2026 [it cut the cap on domestic card interchange fees](<https://conseil-concurrence.ma/communique-conjoint-du-conseil-de-la-concurrence-et-de-bank-al-maghrib/>) from 0.65% to 0.50%, effective 1 October 2026.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [The law defines payment institutions](<https://www.bkam.ma/content/download/757666/8545663/loi%20bancaire%20fr%2022.pdf>) as entities offering payment services, separate from credit institutions, and requires them to obtain Bank Al-Maghrib's approval before operating. Bank Al-Maghrib and the Competition Council said in July 2026 that [newly licensed payment institutions](<https://conseil-concurrence.ma/communique-conjoint-du-conseil-de-la-concurrence-et-de-bank-al-maghrib/>) and older ones were taking up card acquiring. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other. Payment institutions also [open accounts remotely alongside banks](<https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP_Deliberation-n-D-108-2020_26-03-2020.pdf>), under a 2020 ruling of the data protection commission.
