# Ethiopia

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** The National Bank of Ethiopia introduced [two sets of rules in 2020](<https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/52044/>): the Payment Instrument Issuers directives and the Payment Systems Operators directive. Firms are licensed and operating under both, The Reporter said in July 2026. [Directive ONPS/10/2025, the revised rules for payment instrument issuers, entered into force on 12 May 2025](<https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ONPS102025-Oversight-of-the-Natioanl-Payment-System-Licensing-and-Authorization-of-Payment-Instrument-Issuer-Amendment-Directive.pdf>) under the amended National Payment System Proclamation. It [raised daily e-money limits](<https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/05/27/nbe-mandates-interoperability-among-mobile-wallets/>) and set the capital for an issuer at 100 million birr. No act of parliament on payments is named.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [The operators directive created four licence categories](<https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/52044/>), for switch, point-of-sale, cash machine and payment gateway operators. Companies that are not banks, Chapa, Arifpay, Santimpay and Kacha among them, were licensed under it. The issuers directives let telecom operators launch mobile wallets. The [May 2025 directive requires issuers to make their wallets interoperate through the national switch or another licensed switch](<https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ONPS102025-Oversight-of-the-Natioanl-Payment-System-Licensing-and-Authorization-of-Payment-Instrument-Issuer-Amendment-Directive.pdf>). It also requires financial institutions that offer digital financial services to take part in the Ethiopian Instant Payment System.
