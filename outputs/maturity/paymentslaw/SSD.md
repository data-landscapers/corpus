# South Sudan

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** South Sudan has a bill and no law. The Bank of South Sudan [opened a five-day workshop on 10 August 2026](<https://www.eyeradio.org/boss-parliament-back-stronger-legal-framework-for-digital-payments/>) to validate the National Payment System Bill, 2025, which covers mobile money, e-money, payment switches and settlement, before its next stage of consideration. At its close on 14 August the governor [called for faster progress](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/17/south-sudan-pushes-digital-payments-as-central-bank-calls-for-faster-payment-bill/>); the bill had not been passed.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** The [bill would set licensing frameworks](<https://itweb.africa/article/south-sudan-pushes-cashless-transition/nWJad7bNDyL7bjO1>) as the regulatory foundation for mobile money and electronic banking, the governor said in urging its swift passage. With no law or rules in force, no company that is not a bank is licensed under one and no rule requires providers to connect. South Sudan therefore answers the first question only.

***Noted, not assessed***. None held.

***Not held***. No source held says a payments law or e-money rules are in force.
