# Ghana

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** Ghana's [Payment Systems and Services Act, 2019 (Act 987)](<https://digitalpolicyalert.org/digest/dpa-digital-digest-ghana>) has applied since February 2020. The Bank of Ghana acts under it: on 14 July 2026 it [revoked a licence under section 13](<https://www.myjoyonline.com/bog-officials-police-restrict-access-to-zeepay-headquarters-after-licence-revocation/>). The Act sets licensing requirements for payment service providers and electronic money issuers.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [The Act requires payment service providers and electronic money issuers to hold a Bank of Ghana licence](<https://digitalpolicyalert.org/digest/dpa-digital-digest-ghana>). Zeepay Ghana, a fintech company, [held a dedicated electronic money issuer licence](<https://www.myjoyonline.com/bog-officials-police-restrict-access-to-zeepay-headquarters-after-licence-revocation/>) until the Bank revoked it in July 2026 over inadequate cash backing of its e-money. Ghana [mandated mobile money interoperability in 2018](<https://iafrikan.com/tomorrow-west-africas-central-bank-finds-out-who-really-runs-its-payments-market>); the rule that did so is not named. The Bank of Ghana assesses licence applicants on financial stability, technology, compliance with anti-money-laundering rules and consumer protection.
