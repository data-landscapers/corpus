# Nigeria

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** The Central Bank of Nigeria [already licenses mobile money operators, payment service providers and digital banks](<https://businesstech.ng/fintech-regulatory-commission-bill-nigeria/>), operators told a public hearing on 2 March 2026. The hearing was on the [Nigerian Fintech Regulatory Commission Bill 2025](<https://businesstech.ng/fintech-regulatory-commission-bill-nigeria/>), which would create a single regulator to license and supervise all fintech services and passed second reading in the House of Representatives on 28 October 2025.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [The central bank's licences](<https://businesstech.ng/fintech-regulatory-commission-bill-nigeria/>) go to mobile money operators and payment service providers, OPay among the operators that said so at the hearing. The law or rules under which it grants them are not named, and no rule is named that requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national switch. Nigeria therefore reaches the third question on the licences alone.

***Also on file***. [NIBSS Instant Payment](<https://iafrikan.com/tomorrow-west-africas-central-bank-finds-out-who-really-runs-its-payments-market>).

***Not found***. No rule held requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch.
