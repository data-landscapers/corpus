# Niger

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** Niger is bound by the rules of the BCEAO, the central bank of the eight-state West African Economic and Monetary Union, which licenses e-money issuers and supervises them with the union's banking commission. The commission's 2025 report [counts one licensed e-money issuer in Niger](<https://www.horonyafinance.com/umoa-la-monnaie-electronique-gagne-du-terrain-les-encours-bondissent-de-352-en-2025/>) at 31 December 2025, among 18 across the union.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** That [issuer](<https://www.horonyafinance.com/umoa-la-monnaie-electronique-gagne-du-terrain-les-encours-bondissent-de-352-en-2025/>) is not a bank. In April 2026 the BCEAO [told every bank, e-money issuer and payment institution in the eight states](<https://iafrikan.com/tomorrow-west-africas-central-bank-finds-out-who-really-runs-its-payments-market>), Niger included, to be connected to its instant payment platform, PI-SPI, by 30 June 2026. By a communiqué of 25 June it [set 30 September 2026 as the new deadline](<https://financesao.com/uemoa-pi-spi-atteint-104-etablissements-autorises/>) for them, and 30 June 2027 for microfinance institutions. L'Oeil du Sahel reports [a BCEAO communique of 2 October 2026 requiring interoperable e-money transfers in the union to pass through PI-SPI from 2 November 2026](<https://oeildusahel.com/pi-spi-mobile-money-impact-economique-aes/>), and the banking commission's figure of [4.6 billion FCFA of e-money outstanding at Airtel Money Niger at the end of 2024](<https://oeildusahel.com/pi-spi-mobile-money-impact-economique-aes/>).
