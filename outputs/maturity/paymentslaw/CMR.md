# Cameroon

## Payments law

**Law or rules in force.** [Cameroon's regulatory framework](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-finance/1709-141667-cameroun-konoom-obtient-son-agrement-et-se-lance-sur-le-marche-du-paiement-mobile>) lets payment institutions, and also banks and microfinance institutions, offer payment services under the conditions the regulation sets. A licence is granted by order of the finance minister on a decision of COBAC, the banking commission of the six-state Central African Economic and Monetary Community.

**Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect.** [Konoom Cameroun was licensed as a payment institution](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-finance/1709-141667-cameroun-konoom-obtient-son-agrement-et-se-lance-sur-le-marche-du-paiement-mobile>) by arrêté n°681/MINFI of 21 July 2026, following COBAC decision n°D-2026/092; Orange Money Cameroun and Mobile Money Corporation, the MTN subsidiary, were already licensed. COBAC's 2025 annual report [counts seven licensed payment institutions in the region](<https://ecomatin.net/cemac-le-mobile-money-brasse-44-000-milliards-fcfa-en-2025-et-genere-47-milliards-de-benefices-aux-operateurs>), those two among them. No rule is named that requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch, so Cameroon answers the first three questions and not the fourth.
