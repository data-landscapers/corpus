# ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The frame's anchor (`lookups/maturity-norms.csv`) is the African Union's decentralisation charter of 2014, Article 16(2): local governments "shall be provided with the required human, financial and technological resources", and ICT "shall be made accessible and effectively used". Its reference measure is the United Nations' Local Online Services Index.

**Neither measures what the sub-indicator measures.** Article 16 sets a duty and no measure. The Index scores one city's portal, the `web` class. The ladder takes the Charter's two words: *accessible* is the connection and *effectively used* is `systems`. *(Read 2026-10-10.)*

## The ladder

| Stage | What the office does | How connected | Local governments |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that local government offices have no connection of their own | `personal` or `none` | |
| 2 Preparing | A programme to connect them is being prepared or piloted; or only the capital city or the upper tier is connected | Any, or not stated | Pilot local governments, or none |
| 3 Establishing | `systems` or `internet`: past its pilot and live in some local governments outside the capital | `institutional` | A minority, a count with no total, or not published |
| 4 Operating | `systems` or `internet` | `institutional` | More than half, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | `systems` | `institutional` | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years that gives the rural share |

### Uganda: 4 Operating

In 2022, 60.6 per cent of districts, municipalities and town councils answering a survey had office internet; the share of all is not known.

*On the government network; Advancing.*

- NITA-U's National IT Survey, carried out January to April 2022, found 60.6% of local government administrations had institutional internet access; 95 of 128 sampled districts gave full data, with 17 municipalities and 10 town councils inside them also responding ([source, 2022](https://demo.nita.go.ug/sites/default/files/2024-09/National%20IT%20Survey%20Report%202022%20-%20Final.pdf)).
- NITA-U's 2022 survey reports 60.6% of local governments with institutional internet access, 80.5% giving staff internet over institution wireless networks and 26% over institution-owned USB modems; among those connected NITA-U was the provider for 70.1%, Airtel 26% and MTN 14.3% ([source, 2022](https://demo.nita.go.ug/sites/default/files/2024-09/National%20IT%20Survey%20Report%202022%20-%20Final.pdf)).
- NITA-U's 2022 survey found local governments used the internet mostly for email (90.9%) and for providing services to the public (74%), least for purchasing (18.2%); 2.5% of local government staff routinely used the internet at work ([source, 2022](https://demo.nita.go.ug/sites/default/files/2024-09/National%20IT%20Survey%20Report%202022%20-%20Final.pdf)).
- The gender ministry's feasibility study states that local governments transfer project funds to community group accounts using the Integrated Financial Management System (IFMS), that their accounting officers prepare and submit quarterly warrants on it, and that as of July 2022 all local governments had been migrated to the IFMS; it gives no count of local governments and does not describe the link each uses ([source, 2022-07](https://socialprotection.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/mdocs/Report%20on%20possible%20deployment%20of%20Government%20Payment%20Mechanisms%20in%20Social%20Protection%20Programmes.pdf)).
