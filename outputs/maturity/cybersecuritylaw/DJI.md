# Djibouti

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** [Law n°66/AN/14/7ème L of 20 July 2014 on cybersecurity and the fight against cybercrime is part of Djibouti's legal framework](<https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/loi-n-18-an-23-9eme-ll-portant-ratification-de-la-convention-de-lunion-africaine-sur-la-cybersecurite-et-la-protection-de-donnees-a-caractere-personnel/>). The 2023 law ratifying the African Union's Malabo Convention cites it. Djibouti's National Assembly [passed a Digital Code on 30 June 2025](<https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/national-assembly-of-the-republic-of-djibouti-approves-digital-code>), covering cybersecurity, cybercrime and punishable online offences. Its legislative section was still to be promulgated by the President and published in the Journal Officiel. [The Code has eight books and nearly 800 articles, and Book VI deals with cybersecurity and cybercrime](<https://techafricanews.com/2025/07/02/a-first-for-east-africa-djibouti-enacts-comprehensive-digital-code/>).

**Use of the law.** The nearest fact is in the text itself: [Jones Day reports, in a note of 1 July 2025,](<https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/national-assembly-of-the-republic-of-djibouti-approves-digital-code>) that the Digital Code makes online offences punishable. The Assembly adopted it on 30 June 2025, and the same report says its legislative section awaited promulgation by the President, then publication in the Journal Officiel. That adoption was reported within days: [TechAfrica News](<https://techafricanews.com/2025/07/02/a-first-for-east-africa-djibouti-enacts-comprehensive-digital-code/>) carried it on 2 July 2025 under a headline saying Djibouti enacts the Code.
