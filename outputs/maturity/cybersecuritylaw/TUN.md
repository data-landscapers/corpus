# Tunisia

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** [Decree-law no. 2023-17 of 11 March 2023 on cybersecurity](<https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/decret-loi-n-2023-17-du-11-mars-2023-relatif-a-la-cybersecurite/>), published in JORT no. 26, is in force. It sets audit, labelling, classification, incident-response and critical-infrastructure duties for public and private bodies, with fines of 50,000 to 100,000 dinars. Decree-law 54 of September 2022 makes spreading falsehoods by electronic means an offence. An amendment [replacing jail with fines of 10,000 to 20,000 dinars](<https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/tunisia-softens-its-false-news-penalty-what-really-changed-and-why-now>) for a first offence took effect on 30 September 2026, Al Jazeera reports.

**Use of the law.** The journalists' union SNJT [recorded six prosecutions of journalists under Decree 54](<https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/tunisia-softens-its-false-news-penalty-what-really-changed-and-why-now>) between April 2025 and April 2026. In January 2025 a National Guard spokesperson said [2,000 cybercrime cases had been recorded](<https://www.lapresse.tn/2025/01/15/2000-affaires-de-cybercriminalite-sont-recensees-depuis-le-debut-de-2024/>) since the start of 2024. Most were referred to the courts and 800 had passed through all legal stages. Detention warrants were issued against several defendants.
