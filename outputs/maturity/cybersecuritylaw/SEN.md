# Senegal

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** Senegal's Cybercrime Law, [Law 2008-10 of 25 January 2008, completes the Penal Code and the Penal Procedure Code and lists offences with penalties of six months to 10 years](<https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/693598/data-security-and-cybercrime-in-senegal>). The same 2018 practitioner source says no separate cybersecurity act had yet been adopted. In August 2026 [the Ministry of Telecommunications and Digital Economy submitted a bill on the protection of critical information infrastructure and digital security to the National Assembly's Intercommission](<https://news.broadcastmediaafrica.com/2026/08/17/senegal-advances-new-cybersecurity-bill-to-protect-critical-infrastructure-and-strengthen-digital-sovereignty/>), which would set up a National Cybersecurity Authority and CERTs.

**Use of the law.** Senegal's national police [recorded 3,902 cybercrime complaints in 2024](<https://infosdujour.sn/lutte-contre-la-cybercriminalite-la-police-nationale-enregistre-3-902-plaintes-en-2024-194-personnes-defer/>)), cleared 223 and referred 194 people to prosecutors, it said on 16 April 2025. Le Soleil, citing the same police report, [puts these at 3,902 of 18,951 offences](<https://lesoleil.sn/actualites/societe-fait-divers/division-speciale-de-cybersecurite-police-des-reseaux-sociaux-gardien-des-moeurs/>)) recorded that year. It also reports that Moustapha Diakhate was sentenced on 28 November 2024 to two months in prison for an insult made through a computer system.
