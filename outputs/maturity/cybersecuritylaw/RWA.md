# Rwanda

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** Rwanda has a cybersecurity law in force. The East African Policy Observer drew on the Carnegie Endowment's Africa Technology Policy Tracker in a commentary of 30 July 2026. It says that [Rwanda's binding legal instruments include cybersecurity law](<https://eastafricanpolicyobserver.org/commentary/new-africa-tech-policy-insights-on-nigeria-rwanda-and-zambia-ictworks/>). The law is the 2018 Cybercrime Law. On 5 June 2025 Rwanda's Supreme Court [upheld the constitutionality of its Article 39](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/opposition-leader-victoire-ingabire-re-arrested-court-ruling-deals-blow-to-freedom-of-expression/>), according to CIVICUS Monitor. The article criminalises publishing rumours through digital platforms.

**Use of the law.** The nearest fact found is the court's ruling of 5 June 2025. In it the Supreme Court [upheld Article 39 of the 2018 Cybercrime Law](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/opposition-leader-victoire-ingabire-re-arrested-court-ruling-deals-blow-to-freedom-of-expression/>), as reported by CIVICUS Monitor. It is a ruling on the article's constitutionality, and it leaves the article in force. The ruling does not record an arrest, charge or conviction under the law. It is the only fact found about the law in the courts.
