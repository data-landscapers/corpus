# Rwanda

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** Rwanda has a cybersecurity law in force. The East African Policy Observer, drawing on the Carnegie Endowment's Africa Technology Policy Tracker, says in a commentary of 30 July 2026 that [Rwanda's binding legal instruments include cybersecurity law](<https://eastafricanpolicyobserver.org/commentary/new-africa-tech-policy-insights-on-nigeria-rwanda-and-zambia-ictworks/>). The law is the 2018 Cybercrime Law, and on 5 June 2025 Rwanda's Supreme Court [upheld the constitutionality of its Article 39](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/opposition-leader-victoire-ingabire-re-arrested-court-ruling-deals-blow-to-freedom-of-expression/>), which criminalises publishing rumours through digital platforms, according to CIVICUS Monitor.

**Use of the law.** The nearest held fact is the court's ruling of 5 June 2025, in which the Supreme Court [upheld Article 39 of the 2018 Cybercrime Law](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/opposition-leader-victoire-ingabire-re-arrested-court-ruling-deals-blow-to-freedom-of-expression/>), as reported by CIVICUS Monitor. It is a ruling on the article's constitutionality, and it leaves the article in force. The ruling does not record an arrest, charge or conviction under the law, and it is the only held fact about the law in the courts.

***Also on file***. [Africa Technology Policy Tracker](<https://eastafricanpolicyobserver.org/commentary/new-africa-tech-policy-insights-on-nigeria-rwanda-and-zambia-ictworks/>), run by the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace with the African Telecommunications Union.

***Not found***. Any arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years.
