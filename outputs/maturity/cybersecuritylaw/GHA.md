# Ghana

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** [Digital Policy Alert](<https://digitalpolicyalert.org/digest/dpa-digital-digest-ghana>) says Ghana's Cybersecurity Act of 2020 sets measures for critical infrastructure, cybercrime prevention and incident response. It names the Cyber Security Authority as the body the Act created. In August 2026 the [Cyber Security Authority sanctioned bodies](<https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/office-of-registrar-of-companies-and-service-provider-fined-ghc240-000-for-breaches-cybersecurity-directive-breaches.html>) under Section 92 of the Act, Act 1038. An [October 2026 op-ed](<https://www.myjoyonline.com/bold-ambitions-fragile-foundations-is-ghana-ready-for-the-ai-and-data-centre-risks-it-is-inviting/>) calls the Act the framework for protecting critical infrastructure and prosecuting cybercrime. [From 1 October to 14 November 2025 the Authority consulted on a draft amendment bill](<https://www.csa.gov.gh/public_document.php>). [In December 2025 the communications minister said over 272 comments had come in](<https://techfocus24.com/communication-ministry-seeks-private-sector-input-on-cybersecurity-act/>), to be taken in before the bill goes to Cabinet.

**Use of the law.** On 13 August 2026 the Graphic reported that the [Cyber Security Authority fined the Office of the Registrar of Companies GH¢240,000](<https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/office-of-registrar-of-companies-and-service-provider-fined-ghc240-000-for-breaches-cybersecurity-directive-breaches.html>). The fine was 10,000 penalty units for each of two breaches of Section 92(2) of Act 1038. The Authority also fined Purpleline Solutions Limited GH¢120,000 for providing cybersecurity services without a licence. One is a state office and the other a private company.
