# Niger

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** Niger has a cybercrime law dating from 2019. The military authorities amended it in 2024 to restore prison terms of up to five years and fines for disseminating data likely to disturb public order, and [Human Rights Watch reports it was the law under which charges were brought in October 2025](<https://www.hrw.org/fr/news/2025/11/06/des-journalistes-nigeriens-arretes-en-vertu-de-la-loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite>). The law is in force and the authorities apply it, including its public-order offence of disseminating data, with the charges dated to 30 October 2025.

**Use of the law.** The law was used against journalists. [Per Human Rights Watch, police in Niamey arrested six journalists on 30 October 2025](<https://www.hrw.org/fr/news/2025/11/06/des-journalistes-nigeriens-arretes-en-vertu-de-la-loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite>) and the authorities charged them with complicity in disseminating a document likely to disturb public order under the 2019 cybercrime law, for sharing an invitation to a state press conference. Three of the six were detained at Kollo prison, and the arrests took place in Niamey, the capital, on a single day.

***Noted, not assessed***. None held.

***Not held***. No count of cases investigated, prosecuted or decided under the law, for a period ending in the last two years, is held.
