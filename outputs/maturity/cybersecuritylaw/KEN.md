# Kenya

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** The Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act of 2018 stands. In March 2026 the Court of Appeal [struck down sections 22 and 23](<https://nation.africa/kenya/news/major-victory-as-court-declares-illegal-sections-of-cybercrimes-law-used-to-hunt-down-bloggers-activists-5382142>), its false-publication offences, and upheld the rest, including the investigative powers. The [Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act, 2025](<https://parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-10/Computer%20Misuse%20and%20Cybercrimes%20%28Amendment%29%20Act%2C%202025.pdf>) amended it from 4 November 2025. On 3 September 2026 Kenya [launched an official guide](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-03-kenya-rolls-out-new-cybercrime-playbook-as-digital-threats-grow>) to investigating and prosecuting 76 listed cyber offences under the Act.

**Use of the law.** CPJ reports that journalist Peter Mwita was [charged in a Mombasa court](<https://cpj.org/2026/02/kenya-court-due-to-rule-on-cybercrimes-law-as-another-journalist-faces-jail/>) under the Act in February 2026, over a WhatsApp message, and pleaded not guilty. CPJ lists other bloggers and journalists arrested under it. The [Daily Nation says](<https://nation.africa/kenya/news/major-victory-as-court-declares-illegal-sections-of-cybercrimes-law-used-to-hunt-down-bloggers-activists-5382142>) dozens of young social media users have faced arrest and prosecution under the Act, more of them after the June 2024 protests. They include the teacher Albert Ojwang, arrested over a post, who died in custody in June 2025.
