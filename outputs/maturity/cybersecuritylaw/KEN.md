# Kenya

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** The Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act stands. On 3 September 2026 Kenya [launched an official guide](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-03-kenya-rolls-out-new-cybercrime-playbook-as-digital-threats-grow>) to investigating and prosecuting 76 listed cyber offences under it, presented as the common operational framework under the Act. The [Nation reports](<https://nation.africa/kenya/news/puzzle-over-ruto-cybersecurity-unit--5505608>) that it has been amended, widening investigators' and courts' powers. Two false-publication sections were [struck down by the Court of Appeal](<https://www.kictanet.or.ke/kensafespace-project-impact-digital-rights-kenya/>) in March 2026, [the Southern Africa Litigation Centre says](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>), and the Act otherwise stands.

**Use of the law.** The nearest held fact is the machinery built on the Act: Kenya's cybersecurity framework [rests on the National Computer and Cybercrimes Coordination Committee](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-09-11-kenya-steps-up-push-to-get-new-cyber-agency-running>) under the Act, to which the new National Cybersecurity Agency (Legal Notice 89 of 2026) adds capacity. The Act was also [put in place to address risks](<https://citizen.digital/article/stakeholders-urge-ratification-of-malabo-convention-for-effective-digital-governance-in-kenya-n389789>) on digital platforms. Both items date from September 2026, the same month as the prosecution guide, and the Committee and the Agency sit side by side in that framework.
