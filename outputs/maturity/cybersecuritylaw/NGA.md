# Nigeria

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** The Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act is [an enacted law of 2015](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>) and was [amended in 2024](<https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/08/how-misuse-of-cybercrime-act-cost-nigeria-n1-1trn-in-7-yrs/>). [The amended Act contains interception provisions](<https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/09/when-privacy-becomes-content-the-dangerous-normalisation-of-leaked-private-conversations/>), and SERAP says [the 2024 amendment retains ambiguous cyberstalking wording in section 24](<https://blueprint.ng/cybercrimes-serap-nge-seek-reform-media-freedom-cites-ecowas-court-verdicts/>). [Section 24 prescribes up to three years' imprisonment or a fine for sending a message known to be false](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>). The Southern Africa Litigation Centre said on 15 October 2026 that [section 24 is repeatedly used to arrest journalists for their reporting](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>).

**Use of the law.** [In December 2024 activist Olamide Thomas was arrested and charged for allegedly insulting the President's son on Facebook](<https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/08/how-misuse-of-cybercrime-act-cost-nigeria-n1-1trn-in-7-yrs/>). [The Department of State Services is prosecuting activist and presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore over his description of the President as a criminal](<https://punchng.com/sowore-serap-lawyer-demand-dss-withdraw-charges/>), and [its Director-General announced on 30 August 2026 that the prosecution would be suspended until after the 2027 election](<https://punchng.com/sowore-serap-lawyer-demand-dss-withdraw-charges/>). SERAP says [authorities continue to use section 24 to arrest and charge journalists, bloggers, activists and ordinary citizens](<https://blueprint.ng/cybercrimes-serap-nge-seek-reform-media-freedom-cites-ecowas-court-verdicts/>). [Reporters Without Borders reported in 2024 that at least eight journalists had been arrested, prosecuted or detained under the Act](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>).
