# South Africa

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** The Cybercrimes Act (2020) is South Africa's cybercrime law. IOL, in an article dated 16 September 2026 on whether South Africa will ban under-16s from social media, [reports the Act](<https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-09-16-south-africa-has-no-plan-to-ban-under-16s-from-social-media2/>) as an existing law that already provides protections. On 12 October 2026 the authors Fritz and Limpitlaw [state that the Act criminalises a range of online wrongdoing](<https://www.businessday.co.za/lifestyle/2026-10-12-we-regulate-cybercriminals-now-its-time-to-regulate-the-platforms-they-use/>).

**Use of the law.** The nearest held fact is what the Act covers and provides. IOL says it [deals with hacking, online fraud and sharing intimate images without consent](<https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-09-16-south-africa-has-no-plan-to-ban-under-16s-from-social-media2/>), offered as protections South Africa already has. Fritz and Limpitlaw say it [provides investigative powers, evidence preservation and international co-operation](<https://www.businessday.co.za/lifestyle/2026-10-12-we-regulate-cybercriminals-now-its-time-to-regulate-the-platforms-they-use/>). The two accounts, dated about four weeks apart, describe the same Act, and neither names a case brought under it.
