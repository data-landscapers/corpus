# South Africa

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** South Africa's Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 [was signed into law on 26 May 2021](<https://www.michalsons.com/focus-areas/cybersecurity-law/cybersecurity-bill-in-south-africa-overview>), and ITWeb says it [came into partial operation on 1 December 2021](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/calls-for-finalisation-of-cyber-security-bill/G98YdMLGkRW7X2PD>). On 12 October 2026 Fritz and Limpitlaw [state that the Act criminalises a range of online wrongdoing](<https://www.businessday.co.za/lifestyle/2026-10-12-we-regulate-cybercriminals-now-its-time-to-regulate-the-platforms-they-use/>) and provides investigative powers and evidence preservation. Michalsons says no separate cybersecurity act exists. In October 2025 a Hawks cybercrime head [said a Cybersecurity Bill was still forthcoming](<https://www.gcis.gov.za/newsroom/media-releases/government-urges-vigilance-against-rising-cyber-threats>).

**Use of the law.** On 3 June 2025 the Bellville Specialised Commercial Crimes Court [convicted Lucky Erasmus on 17 Cybercrimes Act charges](<https://connectedworld.clydeco.com/post/102kdeu/cybercrime-crackdown-south-african-court-issues-landmark-sentence>). It sentenced him to eight years, three of them suspended. A parliamentary reply of 29 November 2024 put [37 cases with 88 charges under the Act](<https://www.protectionweb.co.za/cyber-security/specialist-police-investigate-cyber-fraud-prosecutions-remain-low/>) on the prosecutors' court rolls. In October 2026 the Acting Minister of Police [told Parliament](<https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/41149/>) that the Hawks had made one arrest. They had registered eight cyber fraud, extortion and ransomware cases over three years.
