# Mali

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** IPI states that Mali [amended its Penal Code in December 2024](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>), incorporating provisions previously contained in the 2019 cybercrime law, and that journalists have been arrested and imprisoned under the new penal code.

**Use of the law.** Journalist Chahana Takiou, founder-director of the newspaper 22 septembre, was [sentenced on 3 August 2026 to 12 months in prison, 6 suspended](<https://lessor.ml/posts/mali-le-journaliste-chahana-takiou-ecope-dune-peine-de-12-mois-dont-6-avec-sursis-6a70f92a500ac>), for undermining the credit of the State, after the cybercrime pole summoned him. IPI reports that journalist [Abdrahamane Keita of Le Témoin was detained on 9 June 2026](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>) and charged with undermining national unity and the credibility of the state and spreading false information, with his trial postponed from 17 to 31 August, and that the [trial of commentator Mohamed Youssouf Bathily began on 10 August 2026](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>). The criminal chamber of the cybercrime pole [had 4 cases involving 8 accused](<https://www.lessor.ml/posts/pole-de-lutte-contre-la-cybercriminalite-le-ministre-kassogue-salue-loperationnalisation-de-la-chambre-criminelle-6a0efcf332029>) on its roll on 21 May 2026.
