# Mali

## Cybercrime and cybersecurity law

**Law in force.** IPI states that Mali [amended its Penal Code in December 2024](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>), incorporating provisions previously contained in the 2019 cybercrime law, and that journalists have been arrested and imprisoned under the new penal code. Human Rights Watch says articles 20 and 21 of the [2019 cybercrime law criminalise online "threats" and "insults" with up to 10 years in prison](<https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/09/press-freedom-under-fire-in-mali>), and RSF says the law was applied to [journalist Youssouf Sissoko in March 2026](<https://rsf.org/en/broadcast-suspension-and-arrests-journalists-mali-nearly-six-years-transition-marked-repression>).

**Use of the law.** Journalist Chahana Takiou, founder-director of the newspaper 22 septembre, was [sentenced on 3 August 2026 to 12 months in prison, 6 suspended](<https://lessor.ml/posts/mali-le-journaliste-chahana-takiou-ecope-dune-peine-de-12-mois-dont-6-avec-sursis-6a70f92a500ac>), for undermining the credit of the State, after the cybercrime pole summoned him. IPI reports that journalist [Abdrahamane Keita of Le Témoin was detained on 9 June 2026](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>) and charged with undermining national unity and the credibility of the state and spreading false information, with his trial postponed from 17 to 31 August, and that the [trial of commentator Mohamed Youssouf Bathily began on 10 August 2026](<https://ipi.media/mali-military-junta-uses-cybercrime-provisions-to-target-journalists/>). The criminal chamber of the cybercrime pole [had 4 cases involving 8 accused](<https://www.lessor.ml/posts/pole-de-lutte-contre-la-cybercriminalite-le-ministre-kassogue-salue-loperationnalisation-de-la-chambre-criminelle-6a0efcf332029>) on its roll on 21 May 2026. Human Rights Watch reports that editor [Youssouf Sissoko was arrested on 5 February 2026](<https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/09/press-freedom-under-fire-in-mali>) under the 2019 law, charged with spreading false information and insulting a foreign head of state, and RSF says he was [sentenced on 23 March 2026 to two years in prison](<https://rsf.org/en/broadcast-suspension-and-arrests-journalists-mali-nearly-six-years-transition-marked-repression>) and a 1 million CFA franc fine.
