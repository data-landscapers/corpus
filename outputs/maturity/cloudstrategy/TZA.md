# Tanzania

## Government hosting and cloud policy

**Policy adopted.** The e-Government Authority's [Data Center Standards and Guidelines for Public Institutions, version 2.0, was approved by its board chairperson on 18 February 2026](<https://www.ega.go.tz/uploads/standarddocuments/sw-1773257204-FINAL%20Reviewed_Data%20Center%20Standards%20and%20Guidelines%20for%20Public%20Institutions-18.2.2026.pdf>). It is issued under section 49 of the e-Government Act No. 10 of 2019, which requires the authority to issue technical standards on storing and processing electronic data.

**Named hosting and moves.** The [standard rests on section 25 of the Act, which requires a public institution to host its systems in a government-approved hosting environment](<https://www.ega.go.tz/uploads/standarddocuments/sw-1773257204-FINAL%20Reviewed_Data%20Center%20Standards%20and%20Guidelines%20for%20Public%20Institutions-18.2.2026.pdf>). The authority says it wrote the standard to guide public institutions in deciding where to host government ICT applications, and to stop them setting up new data centres. It will also serve as the minimum requirements for judging whether an existing data centre can be an approved hosting environment.

***Also on file***. [Cloud Computing Guidelines for Financial Service Providers, 2025](<https://www.bot.go.tz/webdocs/Feedback/en/2025082512452979.pdf>); [Wingu Cloud Exchange (WCX)](<https://dailynews.co.tz/wingu-africa-launches-wcx-in-tanzania/>).

***Not found***. No source found says the government has named where public bodies must host: a government cloud or data centre the policy tells them to use, or a list of approved providers.
