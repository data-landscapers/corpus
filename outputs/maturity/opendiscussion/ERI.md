# Eritrea

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** UNESCO states, in a document dated January 2026, that the Ministry of Information [runs all television, radio and newspapers in Eritrea](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>). It states that [privately owned outlets effectively ceased to exist after a government ban in 2001](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>). It presents that position as continuing. No outlet independent of the state has been found carrying public criticism.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Nothing has been found on a cut to internet access or a block on a platform or outlet in Eritrea. Nothing has been found on any bar against such a cut either. The nearest fact found is the [2001 government ban on privately owned media](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>). UNESCO states the ban ended private outlets.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** Nothing has been found on a person arrested or an outlet punished for what was said. Nothing has been found on a law repealed. The nearest fact found is again the [2001 ban, after which UNESCO says the Ministry of Information runs all television, radio and newspapers](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>).

***Also on file***. [The RSF World Press Freedom Index 2026, which ranked Eritrea 180th of 180](<https://rsf.org/en/2026-rsf-index-press-freedom-25-year-low>). [The 1996 Press Proclamation, owned by the Government of Eritrea](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>). [Penal Code Article 154 on false information discrediting state bodies](<https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/country-document/2026-01/UNESCO_org_UPR46_ERI_E_Main.pdf>).

***Not found***. No source says whether anyone in Eritrea has been cut off, arrested or charged for speech in the last twelve months. No source says whether any law has been repealed.
