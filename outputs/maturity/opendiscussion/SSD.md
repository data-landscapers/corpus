# South Sudan

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** [AMDISS, a South Sudanese media body, publicly criticised the blocking of Facebook and TikTok on 28 January 2025](<https://www.africafex.org/digital-rights/amdiss-welcomes-lifting-of-the-ban-on-facebook-and-tiktok-and-calls-on-the-authorities-to-keep-the-platforms-accessible>) as restraining press freedom and public discourse.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** [On 22 January 2025 the National Communication Authority directed all internet service providers to block all social media accounts for 30 to 90 days](<https://cpj.org/2025/01/south-sudan-blocks-social-media-access-amid-unrest/>). Zain and MTN told users Facebook and TikTok were no longer accessible, and CPJ's sources said both were unreachable without a VPN on 23 January. [AMDISS urged the regulator in January 2025 to expedite lifting the ban](<https://www.africafex.org/digital-rights/amdiss-welcomes-lifting-of-the-ban-on-facebook-and-tiktok-and-calls-on-the-authorities-to-keep-the-platforms-accessible>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** Nothing on punishment for speech is held. The nearest held fact is that [the Cybercrime and Computer Misuse Act 2026 was signed by President Kiir on 18 February 2026](<https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/explainer-cybercrime-and-computer-misuse-act-2026-offensives-and-penalties>).

***Also on file***. [On 22 January 2025 the National Communication Authority directed internet providers to block all social media accounts for 30 to 90 days, and Facebook and TikTok were unreachable without a VPN on 23 January](<https://cpj.org/2025/01/south-sudan-blocks-social-media-access-amid-unrest/>); [the Cybercrime and Computer Misuse Act 2026, signed on 18 February 2026](<https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/explainer-cybercrime-and-computer-misuse-act-2026-offensives-and-penalties>); [the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Provisional Order 2021](<https://cipesa.org/wp-content/files/briefs/Analysis-of-the-South-Sudan-Cyber-Crimes-and-Computer-Misuse-Provisional-Order-2021.pdf>).

***Not found***. Nothing found says whether anyone in South Sudan was punished for what they said or published in the last twelve months, or that no one was. Nothing found says when the social media block ended.
