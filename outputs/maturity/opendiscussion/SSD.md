# South Sudan

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

***Noted, not assessed***. [On 22 January 2025 the National Communication Authority directed internet providers to block all social media accounts for 30 to 90 days, and Facebook and TikTok were unreachable without a VPN on 23 January](<https://cpj.org/2025/01/south-sudan-blocks-social-media-access-amid-unrest/>); [the Cybercrime and Computer Misuse Act 2026, signed on 18 February 2026](<https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/explainer-cybercrime-and-computer-misuse-act-2026-offensives-and-penalties>); [the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Provisional Order 2021](<https://cipesa.org/wp-content/files/briefs/Analysis-of-the-South-Sudan-Cyber-Crimes-and-Computer-Misuse-Provisional-Order-2021.pdf>).

***Not held***. No source dated in the last three years says a newspaper, broadcaster, party or group inside the country criticised a government policy in public, and none says the internet was cut in the last twelve months.
