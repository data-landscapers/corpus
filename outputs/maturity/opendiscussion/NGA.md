# Nigeria

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** In August 2026 SERAP and the Nigerian Guild of Editors [called for Section 24 of the Cybercrimes Act to be suspended](<https://blueprint.ng/cybercrimes-serap-nge-seek-reform-media-freedom-cites-ecowas-court-verdicts/>). On 18 July 2026 the advocacy centre RULAAC [urged the National Assembly to revise the Data Protection (Amendment) Bill](<https://saharareporters.com/2026/07/19/rulaac-warns-data-protection-bill-could-give-nigerian-govt-sweeping-powers-over-social>). It warned the bill could let regulators shut platforms.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** The Nigeria Union of Journalists says [internet connectivity in Zamfara North was disconnected for about three months from late May 2025](<https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-authorities-in-nigeria-must-reconnect-people-in-zamfara-north-now/>). Neither the government nor the providers explained it. In June 2021 [the federal government suspended Twitter indefinitely and telecom providers were directed to block it](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 10 November 2026 a court [remanded journalist Daniel Ojukwu on cybercrime and criminal defamation charges](<https://cpj.org/2026/11/nigerian-court-detains-journalist-daniel-ojukwu-on-cybercrime-charges/>) over published investigations. The founder of his outlet, Fisayo Soyombo, faces the same charges. The Department of State Services is [prosecuting Omoyele Sowore under the Cybercrimes Act for calling President Tinubu a criminal](<https://punchng.com/sowore-serap-lawyer-demand-dss-withdraw-charges/>). On 30 August 2026 its Director-General said the prosecution would be suspended until after the 2027 election. Media Rights Agenda [recorded 38 arrests and detentions of journalists and citizens in 2025](<https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/metro/mra-records-86-attacks-on-journalists-citizens-in-2025-report/>).

***Also on file***. [TikTok access restricted in Kaduna, Kano, Kebbi and Sokoto](<https://punchng.com/foundation-sues-as-tiktok-restricts-access-to-kano-kebbi-others/>), with a [joint civil society statement](<https://paradigmhq.org/joint-statement-on-the-unexplained-tiktok-disruption-in-northern-nigeria-by-civil-society-organisations/>) and a [report of a lawsuit](<https://itweb.africa/article/tiktok-sued-over-nigeria-blackout/Gb3Bw7WaOZ1q2k6V>). [The NITDA Code of Practice for Interactive Computer Service Platforms](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>). [The Data Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2026](<https://saharareporters.com/2026/07/19/rulaac-warns-data-protection-bill-could-give-nigerian-govt-sweeping-powers-over-social>). The Southern Africa Litigation Centre says [section 24 of the Cybercrimes Act is used to arrest journalists](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>). In September 2021 the government [ordered phone and internet services shut in four northern states](<https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-authorities-in-nigeria-must-reconnect-people-in-zamfara-north-now/>).

***Not found***. No source names who ordered the Zamfara North disconnection. No law repealing a speech offence, and no bar on cutting the internet, has been found.
