# Nigeria

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project and the Nigerian Guild of Editors [called in August 2026 for enforcement of Section 24 of the Cybercrimes Act to be suspended](<https://blueprint.ng/cybercrimes-serap-nge-seek-reform-media-freedom-cites-ecowas-court-verdicts/>). On 18 July 2026 the Rule of Law and Accountability Advocacy Centre [urged the National Assembly to withdraw or revise parts of the Data Protection (Amendment) Bill](<https://saharareporters.com/2026/07/19/rulaac-warns-data-protection-bill-could-give-nigerian-govt-sweeping-powers-over-social>) that could let regulators shut digital platforms.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** No cut in the last twelve months is counted. The nearest held fact is that [the federal government announced an indefinite suspension of Twitter on 5 June 2021 and telecom providers were directed to block it](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** The Department of State Services is [prosecuting Omoyele Sowore under the Cybercrimes Act for describing President Tinubu as a criminal](<https://punchng.com/sowore-serap-lawyer-demand-dss-withdraw-charges/>); its Director-General announced on 30 August 2026 that the prosecution would be suspended until after the 2027 election. On 31 August 2026 [SERAP and Sowore's lawyer demanded that the DSS withdraw the charges](<https://punchng.com/sowore-serap-lawyer-demand-dss-withdraw-charges/>). In an oral statement dated 15 October 2026 the Southern Africa Litigation Centre said [section 24 of the Cybercrimes Act is repeatedly used to arrest journalists for their reporting](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>).

***Also on file***. [TikTok access restricted in Kaduna, Kano, Kebbi and Sokoto](<https://punchng.com/foundation-sues-as-tiktok-restricts-access-to-kano-kebbi-others/>), with a [joint civil society statement](<https://paradigmhq.org/joint-statement-on-the-unexplained-tiktok-disruption-in-northern-nigeria-by-civil-society-organisations/>) and a [report of a lawsuit](<https://itweb.africa/article/tiktok-sued-over-nigeria-blackout/Gb3Bw7WaOZ1q2k6V>); [the NITDA Code of Practice for Interactive Computer Service Platforms](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>); [the Data Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2026](<https://saharareporters.com/2026/07/19/rulaac-warns-data-protection-bill-could-give-nigerian-govt-sweeping-powers-over-social>). Older: [Ambrose Nwaogwugwu, arrested in May 2020](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>); [Abdulganiu Olushipo, detained in March 2024](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>); [Segun Olatunji, held 14 days from 15 March 2024](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>); [Olamide Thomas, arrested in December 2024](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>); [Reporters Without Borders' 2024 count of at least eight journalists arrested, prosecuted or detained under the Cybercrimes Act](<https://www.dataphyte.com/topic/technology/who-protects-nigerias-digital-citizens/>).

***Not found***. Nothing found dates an arrest, charge or jail term, or an outlet suspended or closed, for what was said or published, to the last twelve months, other than the Sowore prosecution.
