# Madagascar

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** On 3 September 2026 Madagascar-Tribune [questioned the government's cybercrime bill n°042/2026](<https://www.madagascar-tribune.com/Loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite-l-Etat-renforce-les-controles-alors-que-l-acces-a-l.html>), saying the permanent access powers of its new digital police raise issues of public freedoms and of balance with freedom of expression, and contrasting it with the stalled access-to-information bill.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** No held source reports a cut of internet access or a bar on one. The nearest held fact is the [Council of Ministers decision of 8 July 2026 to block pornographic websites](<https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/madagascar-annonce-le-blocage-des-sites-diffusant-des-contenus-obscenes>), which is a block of named sites and not of access.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** No held source reports a person punished for speech, or a punishing law removed. The nearest held fact is the same bill n°042/2026, whose [digital police unit would hold permanent access powers](<https://www.madagascar-tribune.com/Loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite-l-Etat-renforce-les-controles-alors-que-l-acces-a-l.html>).

***Also on file***. [The Upin, the digital protection and investigation unit in bill n°042/2026](<https://www.madagascar-tribune.com/Loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite-l-Etat-renforce-les-controles-alors-que-l-acces-a-l.html>); [the blocking of websites carrying obscene content](<https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/madagascar-annonce-le-blocage-des-sites-diffusant-des-contenus-obscenes>).

***Not found***. No source says the country went the last twelve months with no one punished for what they said or published.
