# Algeria

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

***Also on file***. Amnesty International says [seven journalists and media workers were detained, prosecuted or convicted from November 2025](<https://www.aialgerie.org/2026/05/13/algerie-les-autorites-doivent-liberer-les-journalistes-detenus-arbitrairement-et-respecter-la-liberte-de-la-presse/>) over their work or opinions. Journalist Hassan Bouras has been held in pre-trial detention since 12 April 2026. On 7 July 2026 the Algiers appeal court [upheld journalist Saad Bouakba's conviction over statements on historical events](<https://www.cfjustice.org/fr/algerie-committee-for-justice-la-condamnation-en-appel-du-journaliste-saad-bouakba-et-la-fermeture-du-media-rassd-aggravent-la-censure-du-debat-public/>). On 15 June 2025, the first day of the baccalaureate, [Facebook and TikTok were blocked in Algeria](<https://www.tsa-algerie.com/bac-2025-en-algerie-pas-de-blocage-total-dinternet-une-premiere/>). From 7 June 2026 [Algeria confined its exam shutdown measures to the inside of exam centres](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>), leaving the rest of the country connected. A [national fact-checking system for information was drafted for the Council of Government](<https://www.echoroukonline.com/الحكومة-تقف-دقيقة-صمت-على-ضحايا-الحرائ>). The presidency [put forward a national authority for regulating the digital space](<https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/algerie-vers-une-regulation-stricte-de-tiktok-facebook-et-youtube>).
