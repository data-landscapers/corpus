# Algeria

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** No paper, party or group inside Algeria has been found criticising a government policy in public. The nearest fact is [Access Now's review of the 2026 exam season](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>). It reports how the Education Minister explained the government's own exam measures.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** When the Baccalaureate began on 7 June 2026, [Algeria confined its exam shutdown measures to the inside of exam centres](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>). The rest of the country stayed connected, Access Now says. Education Minister Mohamed Seghir Saadaoui [explained that the cuts would apply only inside the centres from then on](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>). No law or court ruling barring cuts has been found.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** The nearest fact is the same [Access Now review of exam shutdowns](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>), dated October 2026. It covers connectivity during the exams, not arrests or charges for what people said.

***Also on file***. A [national fact-checking system for information was drafted for the Council of Government](<https://www.echoroukonline.com/الحكومة-تقف-دقيقة-صمت-على-ضحايا-الحرائ>). The presidency [put forward a national authority for regulating the digital space](<https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/algerie-vers-une-regulation-stricte-de-tiktok-facebook-et-youtube>).

***Not found***. No source found says government policy is criticised in public inside the country. None says anyone was punished for what they said.
