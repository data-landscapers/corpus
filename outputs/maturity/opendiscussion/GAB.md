# Gabon

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** Nine civil-society organisations [met in Libreville on 11 July 2026 and called the suspension unconstitutional and disproportionate](<https://www.gabonreview.com/gabon-cinq-mois-de-silence-numerique-quand-le-remede-devient-le-poison/>). They included ROLBG, SOS Prisonniers, Tournons la Page Gabon and the Ligue nationale des droits humains. They demanded its repeal. On 25 July 2026 the group APSAD [presented a report finding self-censorship among public-debate actors](<https://gabonactu.com/blog/2026/07/26/reseaux-sociaux-reguler-dans-le-respect-des-libertes-apsad/>). It urged an end to blanket suspensions.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** The communications regulator, the HAC, suspended social media in Gabon by [decision no. 0002/HAC/2026, until further notice](<https://www.gabonreview.com/gabon-cinq-mois-de-silence-numerique-quand-le-remede-devient-le-poison/>). The Presidency's spokesman [defended the suspension as temporary and conservatory on 17 July 2026](<https://www.gabonreview.com/suspension-des-reseaux-sociaux-la-presidence-defend-une-mesure-temporaire/>), with no lifting date given. [The HAC lifted the suspension, ordered on 17 February 2026, on 24 October 2026](<https://agpgabon.ga/gabon-la-hac-leve-la-suspension-des-reseaux-sociaux/>), citing cooperation with TikTok, Meta and YouTube. Global Voices counts [248 days without Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube](<https://globalvoices.org/2026/11/11/estoring-social-media-access-comes-at-a-cost-in-gabon/>). [Maroc Telecom lists temporary restrictions on certain internet uses in Gabon for February 2026](<https://www.iam.ma/documents/66341/0/Maroc+Telecom+-+Rapport+financier+S1+2026+%282%29.pdf/261de29d-cee8-407f-1e28-dbb3a53f985f>). No bar on such cuts has been found. [The communication authority suspended Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp across Gabon in February 2026 without express legal basis, Village de la Justice says](<https://www.village-justice.com/articles/gabon-dote-droit-des-reseaux-sociaux-analyse-des-ordonnances-fevrier-2026,57009.html>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 10 August 2026 the HAC [suspended the news site Info241 until further notice](<https://cpj.org/2026/08/gabons-online-news-outlet-info241-suspended-for-publishing-allegations-about-president/>). Its article of 9 August had relayed an activist's allegations about President Oligui Nguema. Activist Bob Mengome was [arrested on 19 January 2026 and charged with defamation](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/restrictive-regulation-on-social-media-use-adopted-amid-ongoing-suspension-of-social-media-platforms/>) over a voice message criticising the president. APSAD [found self-censorship among public-debate actors](<https://gabonactu.com/blog/2026/07/26/reseaux-sociaux-reguler-dans-le-respect-des-libertes-apsad/>) in July 2026.

***Not found***. No law repealing a speech offence has been found. No source says a court or other body has barred such cuts.
