# Djibouti

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** The US State Department's 2024 human rights report says [the government owned the only radio and television stations and the newspaper La Nation held a monopoly on authorized print media](<https://dj.usembassy.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/165/2025/08/624521_DJIBOUTI-2024-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT-1.pdf>). It adds that official media generally did not criticise government policy.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Exiled journalist Zineb Haddaji told CIVICUS Lens in July 2026 that [opposition and exiled media websites are blocked in Djibouti and social media access is restricted at times of political tension](<https://lens.civicus.org/interview/djibouti-silence-is-the-mechanism-that-makes-authoritarianism-possible/>). No law or ruling barring such cuts has been found.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** The opposition party RADDE says [police arrested a journalist and a cameraman of its citizens' rights programme at their homes on 20 November 2025](<https://en.kaabtv.com/djibouti-opposition-condemns-arbitrary-arrest-of-journalist-and-cameraman/>). The US State Department says authorities arrested Farhya Houssein on 25 June 2024 for online criticism of the government, and [a court sentenced her on 17 July 2024 to one month in prison and two years' probation](<https://dj.usembassy.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/165/2025/08/624521_DJIBOUTI-2024-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT-1.pdf>).
