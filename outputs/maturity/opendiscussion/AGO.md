# Angola

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** On 22 August 2026 three journalists' bodies [called the penalties of the new Law Against False Information on the Internet excessive and inhibiting](<https://www.novojornal.co.ao/politica/detalhe/jornalistas-advertem-que-lei-vai-inibir-jornalismo-de-cidadania-erro-jornalistico-dependera-da-interpretacao-dos-tribunais-e-do-lesado-74134.html>). They were the Sindicato dos Jornalistas Angolanos, the Comissao de Carteira e Etica and the Forum das Mulheres Jornalistas. UNITA, the largest opposition party, [voted against the bill in the National Assembly on 21 May 2026](<https://www.novojornal.co.ao/politica/detalhe/lei-contra-fake-news-aprovada-definitivamente-com-votos-do-mpla-prs-e-pha-unita-votou-contra-diploma-que-abre-espaco-para-criminalizar-jornalistas-e-utilizadores-71959.html>). On 27 January 2026 the bar association told the Assembly president that the bill [threatens free expression and needs reformulation](<http://oaa.ao/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/OAA-PARECER-SOBRE-AS-PROPOSTAS-DE-LEI-R.pdf>). A jurist of the civic movement Mudei [said the law's definition is unclear](<https://www.rfi.fr/fr/afrique/20260810-angola-une-loi-sur-les-fausses-informations-suscite-des-craintes-pour-la-libert%C3%A9-d-expression>).

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Access Now says Angola [shut down the internet for at least three hours in July 2025](<https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/resilience-and-resistance-internet-shutdowns-in-africa-in-2025/>), its first shutdown, ahead of protests over fuel prices. On 23 January 2026 the National Assembly approved the cybersecurity bill in general. UNITA abstained, [saying it feared internet cuts on election and protest days](<https://www.novojornal.co.ao/politica/detalhe/proposta-de-lei-de-ciberseguranca-aprovada-mas-com-a-abstencao-da-unita-que-receia-que-governo-recorra-ao-corte-de-internet-nos-dias-das-eleicoes-e-das-manifestacoes-69381.html>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 2 October 2026 a Luanda court [sentenced Reporter Angola editor Daniel Frederico in absentia to 14 months in prison](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/law-on-false-information-on-the-internet-adopted-activists-and-journalists-detained-prosecuted/>) for slander and defamation. The case concerned his corruption allegations against a prosecutor. It dates from 2022, and at the closing arguments in November 2024 [the prosecutor's side asked for three years in prison](<https://www.ecosefactos.com/jornalista-angolano-condena-tres-anos-de-prisao/>). The Law Against False Information on the Internet was [published on 4 August 2026 with prison terms of up to 10 years](<https://www.novojornal.co.ao/politica/detalhe/publicada-em-diario-da-republica-a-lei-que-pune-a-disseminacao-de-informacoes-falsas-na-internet-com-ate-10-anos-de-prisao-73765.html>).
