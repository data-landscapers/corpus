# Senegal

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** The Association of Online Press Publishers and Professionals (APPEL), a Senegalese professional body, with Paradigm Initiative, [backed a constitutional challenge lodged by 23 MPs on 10 March 2026](<https://paradigmhq.org/paradigm-initiative-welcomes-landmark-ruling-against-repressive-media-legislation-in-senegal/>) against the media regulator law over risks of administrative censorship, in Paradigm Initiative's account. The Conseil constitutionnel [gave its Decision n° 2/C/2026 on that law on 7 April 2026](<https://conseilconstitutionnel.sn/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Decision-N%C2%B0-2C2026.pdf>).

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** [RSF says that in early 2024 restrictions such as internet shutdowns undermined the right to inform in Senegal](<https://rsf.org/en/country/senegal>), without saying who ordered them or for how long. [CIPESA says shutdowns were imposed during the 2023 unrest](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>) and that a court found they violated freedom of expression and access to information.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** Nothing found names a person punished for speech or publication. The nearest held fact is the law itself: the deputies [adopted the bill creating the Conseil national de régulation des médias on 3 March 2026](<https://aps.sn/conseil-national-de-regulation-des-medias-le-texte-adopte-par-les-deputes/>), and it carries the number [Loi n° 2026-09](<https://www.vie-publique.sn/docs/be7f281f-9d15-46c2-aedf-1b5213032b17/loi-2026-09-creation-conseil-national-regulation-medias-cnrm-senegal.pdf>).

***Also on file***. [The Conseil national de Régulation des Médias law](<https://www.vie-publique.sn/docs/be7f281f-9d15-46c2-aedf-1b5213032b17/loi-2026-09-creation-conseil-national-regulation-medias-cnrm-senegal.pdf>); [the Commission nationale d'Accès à l'Information (CONAI)](<https://primature.sn/publications/conseil-des-ministres/conseil-des-ministres-du-29-octobre-2025>); [Loi n° 2025-15 on access to information](<https://www.vie-publique.sn/docs/e37fee2b-7841-437a-ae96-b7f969b6a9ac/loi-2025-15-accees-a-l-information.pdf>); the [shutdowns of early 2024](<https://rsf.org/en/country/senegal>) and [of 2023](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>).

***Not found***. No source says the country went the last twelve months with no one punished for what they said or published.
