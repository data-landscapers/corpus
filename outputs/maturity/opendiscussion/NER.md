# Niger

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** [The Maison de la Presse, the umbrella body of journalists' associations, spoke out against post-coup press violations and was forced by the Ministry of the Interior to shut down in January 2024](<https://www.modernghana.com/news/1527749/nigers-media-adjustment-regulation-control.html>), as ModernGhana reports. No criticism of a government policy inside the country in the last three years has been found beyond that.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** [Niger's National Communication Observatory suspended nine outlets from reaching audiences in Niger on 8 May 2026, and a France 24 channel screen showed "program blocked"](<https://cpj.org/2026/05/niger-suspends-9-french-media-outlets-for-alleged-public-order-threat/>), as the Committee to Protect Journalists reports. [The signals of RFI and France 24 were cut within a week of the July 2023 coup](<https://www.modernghana.com/news/1527749/nigers-media-adjustment-regulation-control.html>). No law or court ruling barring such cuts has been found.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** [On 21 September 2026 the lawyer Abrourahamane Lirwana was charged and detained over comments about the army, and prosecutors sought five years on 6 October](<https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/10/08/nigers-junta-tightens-its-grip-on-free-expression>), Human Rights Watch says. [Niamey police arrested six journalists on 30 October 2025 and charged them under the 2019 cybercrime law](<https://www.hrw.org/fr/news/2025/11/06/des-journalistes-nigeriens-arretes-en-vertu-de-la-loi-sur-la-cybercriminalite>). [The editor Ali Soumana was jailed in September 2025 after a complaint from the Prime Minister](<https://www.modernghana.com/news/1527749/nigers-media-adjustment-regulation-control.html>). [The regulator suspended nine France-based outlets on 8 May 2026](<https://cpj.org/2026/05/niger-suspends-9-french-media-outlets-for-alleged-public-order-threat/>), citing a threat to public order.

***Also on file***. [The 2019 cybercrime law, Loi 2019-33](<https://mcnti.gouv.ne/ova_doc/loi_2019-33_portant-repression-de-la-cybercriminalite-au-niger_-vf/>); [the Council of Ministers' draft ordinance on the electronic press and draft decree on foreign correspondents, 10 September 2026](<https://www.gouv.ne/index.php/les-communiques-du-gouvernement/1303-conclusions-du-conseil-des-ministres-du-jeudi-10-septembre-2026>); [the government's electronic press text, welcomed by publishers in September 2026](<https://actuniger.com/societe/22346-presse-en-ligne-au-niger-les-editeurs-saluent-le-projet-de-loi-et-plaident-pour-une-mise-en-oeuvre-concertee.html>); [the national strategy against disinformation](<https://anp.ne/le-niger-veut-se-doter-dune-politique-de-communication-et-dune-strategie-nationale-de-lutte-contre-la-desinformation/>); [Freedom House's 2026 score of 27 out of 100](<https://freedomhouse.org/country/niger/freedom-world/2026>); [MFWA's August 2026 account of Sahel cybercrime laws and journalists](<https://thenationonlineng.net/mfwa-sahel-juntas-weaponising-cybercrime-laws-to-silence-journalists/>).

***Not found***. No source dated in the last three years says a newspaper, broadcaster, party or group inside the country criticised a government policy in public, and none says the internet was cut in the last twelve months.
