# Gambia

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** On 14 May 2026 the Gambia Press Union spoke to the African Commission's 87th session in Banjul. It [called the Cybercrime Bill 2023, the Communications Bill 2025 and the Criminal Offences Act 2025 threats to media freedom](<https://gambiaj.com/society/human-rights/gpu-warns-press-freedom-gains-mask-growing-threats-against-journalists-in-the-gambia/>). In March 2025 the Union and the Edward Francis Small Center for Rights and Justice [criticised the Criminal Offence and Procedure Bill 2025](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/new-criminal-law-and-draft-cybercrime-bill-in-the-gambia-threatens-press-freedom-freedom-of-expression-and-digital-rights/>). They objected to its power to ban publications and its false-publication offence.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** No cut, block or bar on either is on record for the Gambia. The nearest fact on file is a regulator's draft. The Public Utilities Regulatory Authority put its [draft Broadcasting and Online Content Regulations, 2026](<https://pura.gm/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Broadcasting-and-Online-Content-Regulations-2026-.pdf>) out for consultation in April 2026.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 24 March 2025 the National Assembly [passed the Criminal Offence and Procedure Bill 2025](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/new-criminal-law-and-draft-cybercrime-bill-in-the-gambia-threatens-press-freedom-freedom-of-expression-and-digital-rights/>). It [dropped the offences of criminal defamation and sedition](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/new-criminal-law-and-draft-cybercrime-bill-in-the-gambia-threatens-press-freedom-freedom-of-expression-and-digital-rights/>). The Press Union said in May 2026 that [the Supreme Court had struck down criminal defamation and false publication online](<https://gambiaj.com/society/human-rights/gpu-warns-press-freedom-gains-mask-growing-threats-against-journalists-in-the-gambia/>), with no date for the ruling. In October 2024 the High Court [charged The Voice's editor and a reporter with false publication](<https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/new-criminal-law-and-draft-cybercrime-bill-in-the-gambia-threatens-press-freedom-freedom-of-expression-and-digital-rights/>). The charge followed an article on the president's successor.

***Also on file***. [The Access to Information Act](<https://www.voicegambia.com/gpu-says-access-to-information-law-yet-to-be-fully-implemented/>). [The draft Broadcasting and Online Content Regulations, 2026](<https://pura.gm/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Broadcasting-and-Online-Content-Regulations-2026-.pdf>) and the draft Journalists Registration Guidelines, 2026, both of the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority. [Its public consultation on seven draft instruments, 1 to 10 April 2026](<https://pura.gm/public-consultation-guidelines-and-notice/>). [A draft framework on broadcasting and online content with child-protection provisions](<https://www.voicegambia.com/le-ministre-de-linformation-exhorte-les-medias-a-proteger-les-enfants-vulnerables-dans-leurs-reportages/>), from the Ministry of Information, Media and Broadcasting Services.

***Not found***. Nothing found says the internet was cut in the last twelve months. Nothing found says anyone was punished for speech in that time. No law, court ruling or undertaking from the last three years bars the government from cutting the internet.
