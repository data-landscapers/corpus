# Tunisia

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** The National Union of Tunisian Journalists, with Human Rights Watch, Amnesty and RSF, [denounced Decree-Law 2022-54 as a tool of repression](<https://nawaat.org/2026/07/16/liberte-dexpression-en-tunisie-recul-alarmant-sous-le-joug-du-decret-54-et-de-larticle-86/>). Their report is dated 16 July 2026. The union [called for the El Mekki judgment to be annulled](<https://nawaat.org/2026/07/16/liberte-dexpression-en-tunisie-recul-alarmant-sous-le-joug-du-decret-54-et-de-larticle-86/>). In October 2026 it and civil society groups [demanded that Decree 54 be scrapped](<https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/tunisia-softens-its-false-news-penalty-what-really-changed-and-why-now>).

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Access Now, citing OONI data, says [mobile internet disruptions hit messaging apps across Tunisia](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>) during the Baccalaureate from 3 June 2026. They [came back during the resit session of 29 June to 2 July](<https://www.accessnow.org/noexamshutdown-one-step-forward-another-step-back/>). Access Now counts Tunisia among exam-shutdown offenders for the first time. No law or ruling barring cuts has been found.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 15 July 2026 the Sfax Court of Appeal [sentenced journalist Haythem El Mekki to one year in prison](<https://nawaat.org/2026/07/16/liberte-dexpression-en-tunisie-recul-alarmant-sous-le-joug-du-decret-54-et-de-larticle-86/>). His offence was a post about a Sfax hospital. Journalists Borhen Bsaies and Mourad Zeghidi [got one year, cut to eight months, under Decree-Law 54](<https://nawaat.org/2026/07/16/liberte-dexpression-en-tunisie-recul-alarmant-sous-le-joug-du-decret-54-et-de-larticle-86/>). On 25 May 2026 Sonia Dahmani [was convicted again under Decree-Law 54 and sentenced to two years](<https://cpj.org/2026/05/cpj-condemns-tunisias-judicial-harassment-of-sonia-dahmani-after-fresh-conviction/>). Journalist Ghassen Ben Khelifa [got two years in March 2026](<https://cpj.org/2026/05/cpj-condemns-tunisias-judicial-harassment-of-sonia-dahmani-after-fresh-conviction/>).

***Also on file***. [Lawyer and commentator Sonia Dahmani, arrested in May 2024 and later convicted several times](<https://nawaat.org/2026/07/16/liberte-dexpression-en-tunisie-recul-alarmant-sous-le-joug-du-decret-54-et-de-larticle-86/>). [RSF's 2024 count of over ten journalists facing legal proceedings](<https://rsf.org/en/country/tunisia>). [Diwan FM, which RSF says keeps a balanced, critical approach](<https://rsf.org/en/country/tunisia>). Decree-Law 54 of 2022 [before the Council of Ministers](<https://www.alchourouk.com/article/%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-54-%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%8A%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%B1-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%AD-3-%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%8A%D9%85>) and [in parliament](<https://www.arp.tn/loi/project/4139>).

***Not found***. Nothing found shows twelve months in which no one was punished for speech. No law or court ruling bars the government from cutting the internet.
