# Libya

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** In 2025 a representative of Internet Society Libya [said Article 39 on defamation in the draft cybercrime law could be used to suppress activists and journalists](<https://informatics.ly/en/session/cybercrime-law-session/>), and proposed civil rather than criminal defamation.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** No held source reports a cut or block of internet access, or a law or ruling that bars one. The nearest held fact is [a 2025 session on the draft law on cybercrime and digital evidence](<https://informatics.ly/en/session/cybercrime-law-session/>).

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** On 14 August 2026 the Committee for Justice documented that [Internal Security Agency personnel arrested Benghazi activist Aziz Al-Orfi over Facebook and TikTok posts on public affairs](<https://www.cfjustice.org/libya-committee-for-justice-documents-the-arrest-of-activist-aziz-al-orfi-in-benghazi-and-calls-for-the-protection-of-freedom-of-expression/>). He says he was held five days on accusations of incitement.

***Also on file***. [Article 14 of the Constitutional Declaration of 2011](<https://security-legislation.ly/ar/latest-laws/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2011-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87/>), which guarantees freedom of opinion, expression and press in accordance with the law.

***Not found***. Nothing found gives a cut or block of internet access, a bar on one, or a person arrested, charged or jailed for what was said in the last twelve months beyond the one arrest above.
