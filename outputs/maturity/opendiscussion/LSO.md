# Lesotho

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** Nothing on public criticism of government policy is held. The nearest held fact is the [Southern Africa Litigation Centre's oral statement on the human rights situation in Africa, published on 15 October 2026](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>), which says that courts in Lesotho struck down criminal defamation.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Nothing on a cut or block is held, and no bar on cutting the internet has been found either. The nearest held fact is the [same oral statement from the Southern Africa Litigation Centre](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>), which concerns criminal defamation and not access to the internet.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** [The Southern Africa Litigation Centre said in October 2026](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>) that courts in Lesotho struck down criminal defamation. It gave no date for the ruling, so the removal of the offence has been found without a year.

***Also on file***. [The Mo Ibrahim Foundation 2024 IIAG scores](<https://assets.iiag.online/2024/profiles/2024-IIAG-profile-ls.pdf>) for Freedom of Expression and Belief, Media Freedom and Digital Freedom.

***Not found***. No law, ruling of the country's own court or undertaking given in court in the last three years is held that bars the government from cutting the internet. Nothing found says government policy is criticised in public inside the country, or that the internet was cut.
