# Lesotho

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** Nothing on public criticism of government policy has been found. The nearest fact found is the [Southern Africa Litigation Centre's oral statement on the human rights situation in Africa, published on 15 October 2026](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>). It says that courts in Lesotho struck down criminal defamation.

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** Nothing on a cut or block has been found, and no bar on cutting the internet has been found either. The nearest fact found is the [same oral statement from the Southern Africa Litigation Centre](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>). It concerns criminal defamation and not access to the internet.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** [The Southern Africa Litigation Centre said in October 2026](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>) that courts in Lesotho struck down criminal defamation. It gave no date for the ruling, so the removal of the offence has been found without a year.

***Also on file***. [The Mo Ibrahim Foundation 2024 IIAG scores](<https://assets.iiag.online/2024/profiles/2024-IIAG-profile-ls.pdf>) for Freedom of Expression and Belief, Media Freedom and Digital Freedom.

***Not found***. No source from the last three years says anyone in the country criticised a government policy in public. None says the internet was cut in the last twelve months.
