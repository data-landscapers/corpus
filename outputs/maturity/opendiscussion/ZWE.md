# Zimbabwe

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** On 23 June 2026 MISA Zimbabwe, a media-freedom body inside the country, [published an analysis criticising the Zimbabwe National AI Strategy 2026-2030](<https://zimbabwe.misa.org/2026/06/23/implications-of-zimbabwes-national-ai-strategy-on-freedom-of-expression-privacy-and-access-to-information/>), including its proposed National Reality Check Platform for truth-verification, as lacking legal safeguards for freedom of expression. On 15 October 2026 the Media Alliance of Zimbabwe, with International Media Support, held a dialogue [criticising the Cyber and Data Protection Act as being used to silence journalists](<https://hanyani.com/is-the-data-protection-act-meant-to-protect-citizens-or-censor-freedom-of-expression/>).

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** CIPESA says the state security minister [ordered an internet shutdown in January 2019](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>) amid nationwide fuel-price protests, through an intercept directive to mobile network operators, and that the High Court [ruled that month that the minister had no authority under the Interception of Communications Act](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>) to order it.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** The Media Alliance of Zimbabwe's coordinator said [four journalists were reportedly arrested in 2026 on cyberbullying charges](<https://hanyani.com/is-the-data-protection-act-meant-to-protect-citizens-or-censor-freedom-of-expression/>) over stories they wrote. Amnesty International said a Bulawayo24 journalist has been [in custody since 18 February 2026, charged with cyberbullying and broadcasting without a licence](<https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/05/east-and-southern-africa-media-freedom-under-attack-amid-prevailing-impunity/>), after publishing a story alleging corruption. The Southern Africa Litigation Centre said Zimbabwe's courts [struck down criminal defamation](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>), giving no date. CIPESA's note on the fourth-cycle review [lists offences of false information, cyberbullying, incitement, insulting the President, sovereignty and national interest](<https://cipesa.org/2026/08/uganda-and-zimbabwes-fourth-cycle-uprs-must-turn-digital-progress-into-stronger-rights-protections/>).

***Not found***. Nothing found says whether any outlet was suspended or closed for what it published in the last twelve months, or whether access was cut in that time.
