# Zimbabwe

## Freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

**Public criticism of government policy.** On 23 June 2026 MISA Zimbabwe, a media-freedom body inside the country, [published an analysis criticising the National AI Strategy 2026-2030](<https://zimbabwe.misa.org/2026/06/23/implications-of-zimbabwes-national-ai-strategy-on-freedom-of-expression-privacy-and-access-to-information/>). It found the strategy's truth-checking platform short of legal safeguards for free expression. On 15 October 2026 the Media Alliance of Zimbabwe held a dialogue [criticising the Cyber and Data Protection Act as a tool to silence journalists](<https://hanyani.com/is-the-data-protection-act-meant-to-protect-citizens-or-censor-freedom-of-expression/>).

**Cuts and blocks, and a bar on them.** CIPESA says the state security minister [ordered an internet shutdown in January 2019](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>) amid fuel-price protests. The order went to mobile operators as an intercept directive. That month the High Court [ruled that the minister had no authority to order it](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/mozambiques-internet-shutdown-case-african-courts-draw-a-line-on-executive-power/>) under the Interception of Communications Act.

**Punishment for speech, and its removal.** The Media Alliance of Zimbabwe's coordinator said [four journalists were reportedly arrested in 2026 on cyberbullying charges](<https://hanyani.com/is-the-data-protection-act-meant-to-protect-citizens-or-censor-freedom-of-expression/>) over their stories. Amnesty International says a Bulawayo24 journalist has been [in custody since 18 February 2026, charged with cyberbullying](<https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/05/east-and-southern-africa-media-freedom-under-attack-amid-prevailing-impunity/>) after a story alleging corruption. Paradigm Initiative's Londa 2025 report says [arrests of journalists and rights defenders continued in 2025](<https://paradigmhq.org/press-release-zimbabwe-ranks-14th-out-of-29-in-the-latest-digital-rights-and-inclusion-report/>), under sedition and false-news laws. The Southern Africa Litigation Centre says Zimbabwe's courts [struck down criminal defamation](<https://www.southernafricalitigationcentre.org/oral-statement-under-item-3-the-human-rights-situation-in-africa/>), giving no date.

***Also on file***. CIPESA's note on the fourth-cycle UN review [lists offences of false information, cyberbullying and insulting the President](<https://cipesa.org/2026/08/uganda-and-zimbabwes-fourth-cycle-uprs-must-turn-digital-progress-into-stronger-rights-protections/>). The [network degraded during the 2023 elections](<https://cipesa.org/2026/08/uganda-and-zimbabwes-fourth-cycle-uprs-must-turn-digital-progress-into-stronger-rights-protections/>). [Section 164C of the Cyber and Data Protection Act covers false data messages](<https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf>). The [Freedom of Information Act dates from 2020](<https://zimlii.org/akn/zw/act/2020/1/eng@2020-07-01>).

***Not found***. Nothing found says whether an outlet was suspended or closed in the last twelve months. Nor does anything say whether access was cut in that time.
