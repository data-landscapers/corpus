# Open discussion of government policy: freedom to discuss policy online and in the press

*As at 2026-10-17.*

## The norm

The African Commission's Declaration of Principles on Freedom of Expression and Access to Information in Africa (2019) asks states not to disrupt access to the internet or to digital services for the whole or part of a population (Principle 38), and to repeal laws that make sedition, insult and false news crimes and to replace criminal defamation with civil remedies (Principle 22). The African Charter on Democracy, Elections and Governance, Article 2, commits its parties to freedom of the press. This study does not measure a country against either text.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the government cut the internet or blocked a service or news site in the last twelve months; or says no independent newspaper, broadcaster or news site is allowed to work |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: policy is criticised in public |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: and no one was punished for it in twelve months |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: and a law that punished speech has gone |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: and the government is barred from cutting the internet |
