# Ethiopia

## Control of state data

**Rule on state data.** [Digital Policy Alert records](<https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/12100-data-localisation-requirements-in-personal-data-protection-proclamation-proclamation-no-13212024>) that Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024 entered into force on 24 July 2024. It requires data controllers and processors to store locally collected personal data on servers in Ethiopia. It covers personal data in general, not state data. In December 2025 the innovation ministry [said the Digital Ethiopia 2030 strategy rests on a sovereign national cloud](<https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_8021907>). In June 2026 the government was [building a central national data cloud at the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute](<https://birrmetrics.com/ethiopia-plans-local-laptop-tablet-assembly-in-digital-sovereignty-push/>), larger than all public institutions' data centres combined, the Prime Minister said. In November 2026 the innovation minister [ordered faster work on the National Cloud Data Center](<https://techreviewafrica.com/news/8212/x>) at the Addis Ababa ICT Park, which is to host government services. He told public institutions to leave standalone servers.

**Hosted nationally.** A 2024 status deck on the national ID says [Fayda runs on the local government cloud, gCloud](<https://id4africa.com/2024/ps1/PS1-1-Ethiopia-final.pdf>). Its software is built apart from the cloud provider, on an on-premises cloud.

**Contract terms.** No source found since October 2023 says a government contract carries terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit. The nearest held fact is that [Fayda's software is kept apart from its cloud provider](<https://id4africa.com/2024/ps1/PS1-1-Ethiopia-final.pdf>).
