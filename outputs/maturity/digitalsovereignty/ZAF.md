# South Africa

## Control of state data

**Rule on state data.** [A DCDT presentation to Parliament's communications committee](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>), dated 24 February 2026, cites the National Data and Cloud Policy. Under it, some government data shall be stored only in digital infrastructure within South Africa's borders. That covers data with content on national security and the sovereignty of the Republic. [A 2025 book chapter reports](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/digital-sovereignty-in-the-brics-countries/south-african-digital-sovereignty-at-the-crossroad-of-securitization-and-development/08C1119DE09286B84626FA49F6AD0586>) that the 2021 Draft National Policy on Data and Cloud required a copy of data to be kept within South Africa. It also required copies for law enforcement, while allowing citizen data to be held abroad.

**Hosted nationally.** [The DCDT states](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>) that SITA, the State IT Agency, operates the Centurion data centre, the primary of two national facilities. It hosts mission-critical systems and sensitive government data. Both facilities are National Key Points.

**Contract terms.** No source found since October 2023 says a government contract carries terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit. The nearest held fact: [the one given above](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>).
