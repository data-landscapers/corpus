# South Africa

## Control of state data

**Rule on state data.** [A DCDT presentation to Parliament's communications committee, dated 24 February 2026, states that the National Data and Cloud Policy provides that government data carrying content on national security and the sovereignty of the Republic shall be stored only in digital infrastructure located within South Africa's borders](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>). [A 2025 book chapter reports that the Draft National Policy on Data and Cloud, commented on in 2021, required a copy of data to be kept within South African borders as well as copies for law enforcement, while allowing citizen data to be held abroad (2021, before the three-year window)](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/digital-sovereignty-in-the-brics-countries/south-african-digital-sovereignty-at-the-crossroad-of-securitization-and-development/08C1119DE09286B84626FA49F6AD0586>).

**Hosted nationally.** [The DCDT states SITA, the State IT Agency, operates the Centurion data centre (primary of two national facilities) hosting mission-critical systems and sensitive government data; both are National Key Points](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>).

**Contract terms.** No source found since October 2023 says a government contract carries terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit. The nearest held fact: [the one given above](<http://www.ellipsis.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/260224_PCCDT_presentation_-_Data_Centres.pdf>).
