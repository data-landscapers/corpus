# Djibouti

## Satellite data in weather services

**Satellite data stream.** A 2025 GeoSphere Austria peer review says Djibouti's meteorological agency [has no nowcasting observations other than web satellite imagery](<https://alliancehydromet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Djibouti-CHD.pdf>). Its PUMA satellite workstation was not working, so imagery came through the web. A 2022 World Bank roadmap had found [the Meteosat receiving station out of service](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099061323094013108/pdf/P1705480165e750370a40903df6490528c1.pdf>). In May 2026 the African Union Commission listed Djibouti among 41 member states with [a PUMA station installed](<https://www-cdn.eumetsat.int/files/2026-05/7-%20Infrastructure%20and%20Capacity%20Building_AUC_PDF.pdf>).

**Warnings issued.** No source found since October 2023 says the weather service issues warnings drawing on satellite data. The nearest held fact is the [African Union Commission's May 2026 list](<https://www-cdn.eumetsat.int/files/2026-05/7-%20Infrastructure%20and%20Capacity%20Building_AUC_PDF.pdf>), which records a PUMA station and not how it is used.

**ISO 9001 certified.** No source found since October 2023 says the weather service holds ISO 9001 certification. The nearest held fact is the [2025 peer review, which describes the agency's observing network](<https://alliancehydromet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Djibouti-CHD.pdf>), which does not mention certification.
