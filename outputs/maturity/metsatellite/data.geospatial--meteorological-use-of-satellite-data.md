# Meteorological use of satellite data: satellite data in weather services

*As at 2026-10-18.*

## The norm

The Integrated African Strategy on Meteorology asks for sustained access to geostationary and polar-orbiting satellites, WMO's observing standards and ISO 9001 certification for every national weather service. This study asks the four questions above; it does not measure a country against the norm.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A source from the past three years says the national weather service receives no satellite data. |
| 2 Receiving | Yes to 1 |
| 3 Used in forecasts | Yes to 2 |
| 4 Warnings issued | Yes to 3 |
| 5 Certified | Yes to 4 |

## Countries

### Seychelles: 5 Leading

The national weather service was ISO 9001-certified in 2025, with satellite-based warnings.

- The South African Weather Service peer review says the Seychelles Meteorological Authority has implemented a QMS with ISO 9001/2015 certification for international air navigation in full, also covering climate services ([source, 2025-06-12](https://alliancehydromet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CHD-Report-Seychelles-final-draft_12June2025.pdf)).

### Tanzania: 5 Leading

The national weather service was ISO 9001-certified in 2024, with satellite-based warnings.

- The DMI peer review says the Tanzania Meteorological Authority holds ISO 9001:2015 certification for its aeronautical meteorological services only, successfully re-audited in January 2024 ([source, 2024-01](http://alliancehydromet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CHD-Tanzania-official-version.pdf)).

### Burkina Faso: 4 Operating

The national weather service issued warnings drawing on satellite data in 2025; ISO 9001 certification is not on record.

- ANAM states that the 10-minute satellite data reception is decisive for tracking fast weather events and for issuing more responsive early alerts, and that the images help early detection of violent storms ([source, 2025-08](https://meteoburkina.bf/news/lanam-poursuit-la-modernisation-de-ses-infrastructures-des-stations-de-derni%C3%A8re-g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ration-pour-mieux-servir-le-burkina-faso/)).

### Ethiopia: 4 Operating

The national weather service issued warnings drawing on satellite data in 2026; ISO 9001 certification is not on record.

- The Ethiopian Meteorological Institute's page carries a Rapidly Developing Thunderstorms convection warning product for nowcasting, produced with NWC-SAF software ([source, 2026-08-28](https://www.ethiomet.gov.et/satellite-imagery/)).

### Libya: 4 Operating

The national weather service issued warnings drawing on satellite data in 2024; ISO 9001 certification is not on record.

- The National Meteorological Centre says it issued and disseminated early warnings to public sectors and the media 48 hours and 72 hours ahead of the two August 2024 episodes, whose assessments drew on satellite images, and updated them more than once ([source, 2024-08-20](https://wmo.int/media/news-from-members/parts-of-libya-affected-extreme-weather-lnmc)).

### Mozambique: 4 Operating

The national weather service issued warnings drawing on satellite data in 2023; ISO 9001 certification is not on record.

- WMO says meteorological warnings based on satellite-aided forecasts were disseminated through community radio and volunteers, and ahead of Cyclone Freddy in 2023 ([source, 2023](https://wmo.int/site/science-action/weather-forecasts-and-early-warnings/mozambiques-life-saving-early-warning-systems)).

### Tunisia: 4 Operating

The national weather service issued warnings drawing on satellite data in 2024; ISO 9001 certification is not on record.

### Chad: 3 Established

The national weather service used satellite data in its forecasts in 2026; warnings drawing on it are not on record.

- WMO reports that a collaboration with EUMETSAT and AEMET enabled Chad to deploy satellite-based nowcasting (NWCSAF, RDT) to track convective events a few hours before they hit ([source, 2026-02-03](https://wmo.int/media/news/strengthening-early-warning-services-chad)).

### DR Congo: 3 Established

The national weather service used satellite data in its forecasts in 2024; warnings drawing on it are not on record.

- The MeteoSwiss peer review says METTELSAT has a functional PUMA satellite reception system at its headquarters giving access to EUMETCast ([source, 2024-07](https://meteordcongo.cd/documents/14/DRC-CHD-Report-NGA-Annex_2.pdf)).
- The WMO-hosted METTELSAT document says the agency has PUMA 2015 and MESSIR NEO equipment connected to the EUMETCast Africa data stream to receive satellite images and regional and global model data ([source, 2024-02](https://wmo.int/sites/default/files/2024-02/4.%20Syst%C3%A8me%20d%E2%80%99Observation%20de%20la%20METTELSAT.pdf)).

### Djibouti: 3 Established

The national weather service used satellite data in its forecasts in 2025; warnings drawing on it are not on record.

### Guinea-Bissau: 3 Established

The national weather service used satellite data in its forecasts in 2026; warnings drawing on it are not on record.

### Morocco: 3 Established

### Rwanda: 3 Established

The national weather service used satellite data in its forecasts in 2026; warnings drawing on it are not on record.

- The UNDP/Neuralio feasibility study says METEO RWANDA receives satellite data from EUMETSAT's Meteosat Second and Third Generation via EUMETCast, with imagery as often as every 15 minutes, used mainly as static images (JPEG cloud maps) by forecasters ([source, 2026-07-13](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-09/sh-tracked-d4_final_report_ai_and_ml_integration.pdf)).
- The FMI peer review says Rwanda accesses EUMETSAT satellite products through a ground-based satellite receiver, and that Meteo Rwanda's forecasting draws on EUMETSAT satellite imagery alongside a C-band radar ([source, 2023-10](https://alliancehydromet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Rwanda_CHD-Report_20231021_signed.pdf)).
