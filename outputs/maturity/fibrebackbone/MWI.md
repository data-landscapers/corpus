# Malawi

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [ESCOM states its national fibre backbone covers all districts of Malawi including Likoma Island (coverage extended through investment in 2017 and 2021)](<https://africabbmaps.itu.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ESCOM-Presentation-v.02-ITU-2025-Africa-BoradBand-Mapping.pdf>). [Airtel Malawi says its proprietary fibre backbone has been reinforced with new routes from Lilongwe to Salima and Blantyre to Mangochi](<https://www.maraviexpress.com/airtel-malawi-plc-brings-new-era-of-digital-connectivity-with-volte-and-5g-services/>). [ESCOM's optic fibre business states its national fibre backbone network is 7000 km, built on its electricity poles](<https://africabbmaps.itu.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ESCOM-Presentation-v.02-ITU-2025-Africa-BoradBand-Mapping.pdf>). [Transport and MPLS backbone run in two rings centred on Lilongwe and Blantyre, linking Mzuzu, Kasungu, Salima, Zomba and Mangochi](<https://africabbmaps.itu.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ESCOM-Presentation-v.02-ITU-2025-Africa-BoradBand-Mapping.pdf>). Together these say the fibre reaches every province or region. The fibre was built, it is in service between the capital and other regions, and it reaches them all. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [this fibre](<https://africabbmaps.itu.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ESCOM-Presentation-v.02-ITU-2025-Africa-BoradBand-Mapping.pdf>), or that it runs on open-access or wholesale terms. A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it. It also counts as open where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
