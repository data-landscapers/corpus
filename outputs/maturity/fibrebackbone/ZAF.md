# South Africa

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [BusinessDay reports Telkom's Openserve has completed a third redundant long-haul fibre route between Cape Town and Pretoria via Springbok and Mahikeng, alongside two existing routes between the two cities](<https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-09-07-openserve-completes-third-fibre-route-to-reduce-network-outage-risk/>). [TechCentral reports that state-owned Broadband Infraco operates a national fibre network (no provinces or km given)](<https://techcentral.co.za/state-broadband-merger-limps-into-a-second-decade/280723/>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [BusinessDay reports Openserve, Telkom's wholesale infrastructure unit, runs South Africa's largest wholesale fibre network (about 180,000km), delivering high-capacity connectivity to licensed operators and enterprises; As of is the article's published date](<https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-09-07-openserve-completes-third-fibre-route-to-reduce-network-outage-risk/>). [TechCentral describes Broadband Infraco as a fibre wholesaler, a 'carrier of carriers' for cheaper long-distance bandwidth, whose biggest public-sector customers were SITA and Eskom (both since lost)](<https://techcentral.co.za/state-broadband-merger-limps-into-a-second-decade/280723/>). A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
