# Zambia

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [The World Bank appraisal PID refers to Zambia's current 10,000 km of fiber optic cable, which the project aims to extend by at least 2,000 km; it does not say which regions or towns the fibre links to the capital, so this is a national fibre total only](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012025023033676/txt/P505094-630a5ef9-d23a-4221-b7c8-397c94a90b4d.txt>). [A 2022 ZICTA gap analysis, reported to Parliament's ICT committee, found 83 of 116 districts have national fibre optic backbone points of presence and 11 more have transit backbone without a POP; 22 districts have neither, and 18 links totalling 1,685 km would close the gap](<https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/committee_reports/REPORT%20ON%20THE%20COMMMITTEE%20ON%20MEDIA%2C%20INFORMATION%20AND%20COMMUNICATION%20TECHNOLOGIES.pdf>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [this fibre](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012025023033676/txt/P505094-630a5ef9-d23a-4221-b7c8-397c94a90b4d.txt>), or that it runs on open-access or wholesale terms. A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
