# Mauritania

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [The director general of the state company SDIN (Societe nationale de developpement des infrastructures numeriques) said at its HQ inauguration that it operates a national optical fibre network of more than 1,760 km on three main axes linking the country's regions; reported by AMI. The statement is dated 2025-12-16, the document's date](<https://ami.mr/fr/archives/285647>). [The World Bank completion report says construction of five fibre-optic links in Mauritania was under way when the project closing date was extended on 2019-10-20 to allow completion of the fibre backbone links, after delays in signing the construction contract](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099915008262277976/pdf/BOSIB0ef45e1210300bb6f0e08fc7c02a45.pdf>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [The World Bank says the backbone was built under a PPP and open-access model giving operators fair access to the infrastructure, with state vehicle SDIN established and an affermage contract with IMT signed; no named buyers of capacity are given; No event date is given, so the report's published date is used](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099915008262277976/pdf/BOSIB0ef45e1210300bb6f0e08fc7c02a45.pdf>). A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
