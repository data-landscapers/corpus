# Kenya

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [Ministry of ICT page says the remaining 18 counties and 3 towns were connected in phase 2, completed 2017, giving all 47 counties a NOFBI connection](<https://ict.go.ke/last-mile-county-connectivity-project-lmccp>). [ICT PS John Tanui said the public sector added 17,000 km of fibre in Kenya since 2022 (private operators 24,000 km); no places or regions named, and the article does not say the 17,000 km is all backbone](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/13/kenya-ksh-13-billion-expand-fibre-network-27000-kilometres/>). [Tech Africa News says public-sector fibre coverage in Kenya rose from 22,486 km in 2022 to more than 30,400 km](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/09/16/kenya-icta-tender-national-fibre-cross-border-connectivity/>). [Per Treasury figures cited by TechTrends Kenya, Kenya's public sector fibre network stood at 30,454 km at the most recent count, up from 22,486 km in 2022 (about 8,000 km laid under the current administration)](<https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/16/kenya-floats-tender-for-national-fibre-backbone-cross-border-links/>). Together these say the fibre reaches every province or region, which answers the first three of the study's four questions: it was built, it is in service between the capital and other regions, and it reaches them all. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** Nothing found says other operators buy capacity on [this fibre](<https://ict.go.ke/last-mile-county-connectivity-project-lmccp>), or that it runs on open-access or wholesale terms. A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
