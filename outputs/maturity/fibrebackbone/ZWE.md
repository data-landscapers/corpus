# Zimbabwe

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [In an article of 12 September 2026, consultant Willard Shoko says Liquid Intelligent Technologies operates over 26,000 km of fibre in Zimbabwe](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [Shoko calls it the core long-haul terrestrial routing layer](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [Information minister Soda Zhemu said at a post-Cabinet briefing that the total fibre backbone grew 33.83 percent to surpass 19,000 km in Q1 2026](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200058335/internet-traffic-surges-57>). [Fibre coverage rose from 4,046 km to 8,500 km](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200058335/internet-traffic-surges-57>). [Shoko says Powertel and Paratus Zimbabwe formed an equal-investment PPP to build a DWDM fibre backhaul network across Zimbabwe](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [It starts with an active 800 Gbps link between Plumtree and Bulawayo, on Powertel's powerline-based national fibre](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Shoko says the DFA Zimbabwe 1,500 km backbone is open-access and gives local ISPs and mobile operators wholesale backhaul](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [Powertel, the licensed national carrier and ZESA subsidiary, built and operates the Plumtree-Bulawayo fibre route, per its managing director](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200057978/powertel-join-paratus-zimbabwe-to-launch-new-digital-highway>). [It granted Paratus, an investment partner, an Indefeasible Right of Use (IRU) on the route](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200057978/powertel-join-paratus-zimbabwe-to-launch-new-digital-highway>). A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it. It also counts as open where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
