# Zimbabwe

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [Consultant Willard Shoko says Liquid Intelligent Technologies operates over 26,000 km of fibre in Zimbabwe as the core long-haul terrestrial routing layer; Dated by the article (2026-09-12)](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [Information minister Soda Zhemu said at a post-Cabinet briefing that the total fibre backbone grew 33.83 percent to surpass 19,000 km in Q1 2026, and fibre coverage rose from 4,046 km to 8,500 km](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200058335/internet-traffic-surges-57>). [Shoko says Powertel and Paratus Zimbabwe formed an equal-investment PPP to build a DWDM fibre backhaul network across Zimbabwe, starting with an active 800 Gbps link between Plumtree and Bulawayo, on Powertel's powerline-based national fibre](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [Shoko says the DFA Zimbabwe 1,500 km backbone is open-access and gives local ISPs and mobile operators wholesale backhaul](<https://cleantechnica.com/2026/09/12/integrating-starlink-leo-infrastructure-with-zimbabwes-terrestrial-fibre-networks/>). [Powertel, the licensed national carrier and ZESA subsidiary, built and operates the Plumtree-Bulawayo fibre route and granted Paratus an Indefeasible Right of Use (IRU) on it, per Powertel's managing director; Paratus is an investment partner](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200057978/powertel-join-paratus-zimbabwe-to-launch-new-digital-highway>). A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
