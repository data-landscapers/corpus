# Gambia

## Long-distance fibre across the country

**Built, in service and reaching every region.** [WBBA states GAMTEL's National Broadband Network (420 km of fibre-optic links, around 31 central office sites) was deployed in 2019, extending the 2015 ECOWAN backbone and 947 km of legacy fibre in four rings, as a national backbone serving all key areas of the country](<https://worldbroadbandassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/WBBA-GAMTEL_Final.pdf>). [The record says the World Broadband Association's case study states the National Broadband Network was deployed in 2019: 420 km of fibre-optic links and access networks around 31 central office sites, extending the 2015 ECOWAN backbone, owned and operated by GAMTEL; in July 2020 the minister said the network is earning](<https://www.dropbox.com/s/9yvhtd9i4i0vrs0/cr18197.pdf?dl=0>). These place backbone fibre in service between the capital and at least one other region, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. That it reaches every province or region is not stated. A length in kilometres is given where a source states one; length sets no stage.

**Capacity sold to other operators.** [WBBA states the national backbone (ECOWAN, NBN, legacy fibre) is accessible to private service providers, who use lit or dark fibre or buy capacity from GAMTEL, and that all MNOs and ISPs use GAMTEL's network](<https://worldbroadbandassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/WBBA-GAMTEL_Final.pdf>). A backbone counts as open where other operators buy or lease capacity on it, or where it runs on open-access or wholesale terms, whoever owns it.
