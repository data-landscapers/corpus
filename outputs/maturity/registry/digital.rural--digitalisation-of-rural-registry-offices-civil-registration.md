# Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The frame's anchor: the AU Digital Transformation Strategy's 99.9 per cent legal identity by 2030, and the 100 per cent of births and 80 per cent of deaths of SDG 17.19.2(b) that the APAI-CRVS papers restate.

**Neither measures what the sub-indicator measures**: both count people and events registered, which a paper office meets. **One programme text does**: the APAI-CRVS costed plan for 2017 to 2021 counts *countries with electronic data capture on the local level*, 5 in 2016 and 40 by 2021. It sets no share of offices, so stage 3 meets it. The ladder borrows nothing; the 90 per cent line is the health study's. *(Read 2026-10-10.)*

## The ladder

| Stage | Events | How the record is made | Local offices |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that local offices register on paper only | | |
| 2 Preparing | A system for local offices is being prepared or piloted; or one runs at the national office or in the capital only | Any, or not stated | Pilot offices, or none |
| 3 Establishing | B: past its pilot and live in some local offices | `connected`, `local` or `keyed-elsewhere` | A minority, a count with no total, or not published |
| 4 Operating | B | `connected` | More than half, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | B and D | `connected` | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years that gives the rural share |

## Countries

### Rwanda: 4 Operating

Births and deaths are entered digitally at all categories of registration office in 2025, the statistics office says; the rural share is not known.

*Identity integrated; Backlog capture; Advancing.*

- NISR states that nine events are registered through NCI-CRVS in 2025, births and deaths among them, each module being the electronic register of that event: 356,838 births and 39,355 deaths that occurred in 2025 were registered in it ([source, 2025](https://statistics.gov.rw/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/Rwanda%20Civil%20Registration%20Vital%20Statistics%202025_0.pdf)).
- NISR states that the national centralized NCI-CRVS captures vital events in real time at the site of the event where official registration takes place, as a single data entry point, and that the paper-based system has been entirely phased out (2025); its Figure 5 milestone diagram still words registration as in paper books and the electronic system ([source, 2025](https://statistics.gov.rw/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/Rwanda%20Civil%20Registration%20Vital%20Statistics%202025_0.pdf)).
- NISR states that NCI-CRVS is fully operational at all five categories of registration office (health facilities public and private, cells, sectors, districts and embassies) in 2025; it gives no count of offices using it, only the country's 416 sectors and 2,148 cells ([source, 2025](https://statistics.gov.rw/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/Rwanda%20Civil%20Registration%20Vital%20Statistics%202025_0.pdf)).

### Algeria: 3 Established

Births are registered in a national digital register across 1,541 communes, a 2019 count; the share of local offices entering digitally is not known.

*Identity integrated.*

- The interior ministry's presentation to the EGMRG states that since 2018 the national identification number is generated automatically from the RNAEC, so that each newborn has a birth act and a NIN at the end of its registration; deaths are not mentioned ([source, 2018](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-decentralisation-algeria.pdf)).
- The interior ministry's presentation states that since 2018 the NIN is generated automatically from the national automated register (RNAEC) and the newborn holds its birth act and NIN at the end of its registration; it does not describe the entry at the commune office in more detail ([source, 2018](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-decentralisation-algeria.pdf)).
- The article states that the register holds birth, marriage and death acts with their amendments, corrections and registrations, citing article 25 bis 1, and that data-entry errors are corrected centrally; no date is given, so the published date is used ([source, 2025-10-10](https://imcra-az.org/uploads/public_files/2025-10/81246.pdf)).
- The Interior ministry's ID4Africa deck shows the automated national civil-status register holding three databases, births, deaths and marriages, with entry, control and update (saisie, contrôle et MAJ) at commune level; it gives no date for the state described, so the presentation date is used ([source, 2019-06-19](https://www.id4africa.com/2019_event/presentations/InF3/4-Abderrazak-Henni-MOI-Algeria.pdf)).
- The deck's system architecture shows 1,541 commune databases (C1 to C1541) where entry, control and update are done, linked over the ministry's network to 48 wilaya databases for error correction and to a national database at the ministry; elsewhere the deck speaks of 'the 1541 communes'. No date is given, so the presentation date is used ([source, 2019-06-19](https://www.id4africa.com/2019_event/presentations/InF3/4-Abderrazak-Henni-MOI-Algeria.pdf)).

### Angola: 3 Established

Since 2023 births are entered digitally at offices in Luanda and three other cities; most offices use paper, and the digital count is not known.

*Rural gap; Identity integrated; Regressing.*

- Angola's presentation of 18 July 2026 states that biographic and biometric data are collected and validated at the act of issuing the birth entry (assento de nascimento), under phase I of the digital identity architecture, covering all national citizens; it gives no count of offices and does not mention deaths ([source, 2026-07-18](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-digitisation-angola.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that births are registered in the RCI system from 2023 at civil registration posts, which issue a birth registration bulletin carrying the Unified Citizen Number; it describes no death registration in RCI ([source, 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062824094544617/pdf/P179485-40c865ab-d980-432a-acf1-940f22b02d4c.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that RCI, first piloted in four Luanda registries, is available in all CR posts in Luanda and in one CR post each in Huambo, Benguela and Lubango, with plans to extend it gradually to the whole country; it gives no count of RCI offices, and elsewhere puts Luanda province at 39 CR units and the country at at least 255 CR units in 2023 (DNIRN data). No date is given for the coverage; dated to the diagnostic's 2024 revision ([source, 2024](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062824094544617/pdf/P179485-40c865ab-d980-432a-acf1-940f22b02d4c.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that RCI (Registo Civil Integrado), introduced in 2023, is a web-based system with a centralised database in which the birth is entered at the CR post, the Unique Citizen Number being assigned as registration begins, and which is interconnected with other CR posts and the civil and criminal identification database ([source, 2023](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/d1c06154-9015-4e69-9112-ccf5b7092b4a/download)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that from 2023 births are registered in RCI at the CR posts equipped with it, which issue a birth registration receipt carrying the Unique Citizen Number; it describes no death registration in RCI ([source, 2023](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/d1c06154-9015-4e69-9112-ccf5b7092b4a/download)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that RCI, piloted in four Luanda registries, is in all registry offices in Luanda except Cacuaco's and in one CR post each in Huambo, Benguela and Lubango, with plans to extend it country-wide; it gives no count of RCI offices, and puts Luanda province at 39 CR units and the country at at least 255 CR units in 2023 (DNIRN data). The coverage is undated; dated to the diagnostic's 2024 revision ([source, 2024](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/d1c06154-9015-4e69-9112-ccf5b7092b4a/download)).

### Botswana: 3 Established

A national system registered births and deaths in 2024; how many of the 35 district offices enter records in it is not known.

*Identity integrated; Regressing.*

- Statistics Botswana describes the Births and Deaths Registration System as the national system registering births, stillbirths and deaths, the primary data source for the 49,180 births and 14,070 deaths registered in 2024 ([source, 2024](https://statsbots.org.bw/sites/default/files/publications/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202024.pdf)).
- Statistics Botswana describes the BDRS as a 'partially automated and decentralized' system designed to give real-time vital statistics and integrated with the National Identification System, which assigns a birth registration number at birth, an arrangement introduced in 2003; it does not say in terms where the record is keyed (no date given for the present state; the report's August 2026 date is used) ([source, 2026-08](https://statsbots.org.bw/sites/default/files/publications/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202024.pdf)).
- Statistics Botswana states that the Department of Civil and National Registration currently operates 35 district registration offices and 17 on-site registration points at named hospitals and clinics, without saying how many of them enter records in the BDRS (the report's August 2026 date is used) ([source, 2026-08](https://statsbots.org.bw/sites/default/files/publications/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202024.pdf)).

### Burkina Faso: 3 Established

Birth-entry software was deployed in 170 civil registration centres in 2025; the total number of centres, and how many register in it, is not known.

*Advancing; Backlog capture.*

- The Secretary General of the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Mobility reported, at the SNEC steering committee of 25 June 2026, that in 2025 the software for systematic declaration of civil-status events was deployed in 30 main centres and 140 secondary centres; no total number of centres is given ([source, 2025](https://www.wakatsera.com/strategie-nationale-de-letat-civil-2023-2027-plus-de-81-dexecution-financiere-enregistres-en-2025/)).
- Burkina Faso's delegate Ousmane Diallo Nacanabo told the Abidjan symposium of 10-12 December 2024 that an integrated civil-status management system, set up after a 2023 diagnostic, includes a national application for the systematic declaration of civil-status events through which births are declared directly from health centres or surveillance sites; deaths are not named, and the article does not say where the registration record itself is made ([source, 2024-12](https://digitalmag.ci/digitalisation-de-letat-civil-lexperience-du-burkina-faso-de-la-centrafrique-et-du-congo/)).
- AIB reports that the training covered configuring smartphones and entering civil-status events in real time on the VENEEM platform, at central offices and at secondary centres alike; the document does not say where the record is held ([source, 2026-08-31](https://www.aib.media/sanguie-pouni-des-agents-de-letat-civil-outilles-a-lutilisation-de-la-plateforme-veneem/)).

### Cameroon: 3 Established

In September 2026, 60 of 432 principal civil status centres were digitised, 13.9 per cent; the share of all local offices is not known.

*Advancing; Backlog capture.*

- EcoMatin, on the BUNEC Director General's figures given on national television on 14 September 2026, says the pilot phase has digitised only 60 principal civil status centres of the country's 432 (a count it says includes diaspora centres and 149 special centres), a progress rate of 13.9% ([source, 2026-09-14](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-52-milliards-fcfa-recherches-pour-numeriser-letat-civil)).
- UNDP reports that in the East Region two centres had passed into production by August 2026, Bertoua 1er (about 10 acts a day) and the Bertoua City Council (10 to 15 acts), while Bertoua II, Batouri, Abong-Mbang, Betare-Oya and Garoua-Boulai were still being equipped and trained; it gives no regional or national total of centres ([source, 2026-08](https://www.undp.org/cameroon/stories/dans-lest-du-cameroun-la-digitalisation-de-letat-civil-transforme-progressivement-lacces-lidentite-legale-bertoua-cameroun-aout-2026)).
- UNDP describes each commune receiving its own server, workstations and network with SIGEC installed on them; at Bertoua 1er backups run automatically on the commune's server with a copy on an external hard disk, and disaggregated statistics can be produced and transmitted to BUNEC ([source, 2026-08](https://www.undp.org/cameroon/stories/dans-lest-du-cameroun-la-digitalisation-de-letat-civil-transforme-progressivement-lacces-lidentite-legale-bertoua-cameroun-aout-2026)).
- UNDP says the East Region's civil status centres are changing how birth, marriage and death acts are recorded, kept and issued; agents at Bertoua II were trained on recording and producing birth and marriage acts in SIGEC, and the region's first digitalised birth act was produced a few weeks before August 2026 ([source, 2026-08](https://www.undp.org/cameroon/stories/dans-lest-du-cameroun-la-digitalisation-de-letat-civil-transforme-progressivement-lacces-lidentite-legale-bertoua-cameroun-aout-2026)).
- At Maroua 3 council 343 certificates were issued for births, marriages and deaths from January to March, the year not stated, with birth certificates produced on the system while the parent waits, per UNDP Cameroon; date is the publication date ([source, 2025-09-22](https://www.undp.org/cameroon/stories/digitalizing-birth-certificates-where-technology-giving-every-child-name-and-future-0)).

### Cape Verde: 3 Established

In 2017 offices in all 22 municipalities entered births and deaths digitally, in urban centres only; whether records reach the national register is not known.

*Rural gap; Identity integrated.*

- UNICEF's country profile, compiled December 2016 to November 2017, states under both birth and death registration that processing is electronic and that all vital events are recorded directly in the computer using specific applications; marriages are processed the same way ([source, 2017-11](https://data.unicef.org/crvs/cabo-verde/)).
- UNICEF's profile, compiled to November 2017, states that all vital events are recorded directly in the computer, using applications located in the civil registry conservatórias, with a certificate issued immediately; it does not say where the record is held or that it is held in a national register ([source, 2017-11](https://data.unicef.org/crvs/cabo-verde/)).
- UNICEF's profile, compiled to November 2017, states that the applications in which vital events are recorded are available in all municipalities of the country, in the civil registry conservatórias, with civil registration services in all 22 municipalities (22 of 22, a share of municipalities); it adds that registration stations exist only in the urban centres of the 22 municipalities, rural communities having been reached by mobile teams during the birth registration project and by registration in health structures then being introduced ([source, 2017-11](https://data.unicef.org/crvs/cabo-verde/)).

### Chad: 3 Established

Births were registered digitally at 97 hospital registry points in 2025; the total of local offices is not known.

*Identity integrated; Advancing.*

- ANATS's coordinator of cooperation and civil registration told the ID4Africa 2025 meeting that more than 120,000 children have been registered through hospital-based registry offices since the civil registration system was integrated with health facilities in 2023; no date is given for the count, so the document's publication date is used ([source, 2025-06-03](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202506/chad-mali-senegal-pursue-birth-registration-reforms-to-close-gaps)).
- The account gives 97 digitised hospital-based civil registration points at present, which the government plans to raise to 600 in the next few years; no total of local offices is given and no date for the count, so the document's publication date is used ([source, 2025-06-03](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202506/chad-mali-senegal-pursue-birth-registration-reforms-to-close-gaps)).
- ANATS's coordinator says the system connects civil registry offices, hospitals, vaccination centres, municipalities, mobile units, refugee camps and schools, with all data sent by SMS to the national agency, which validates it and sends it back for issuance; undated, so the document's publication date is used ([source, 2025-06-03](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202506/chad-mali-senegal-pursue-birth-registration-reforms-to-close-gaps)).
- The World Bank states that civil registration officers in six hospitals enter the declaration in the TASDJIL mobile application, the ANATS office receives it in its system, verifies the information, assigns a national identification number (NNI) and issues a birth certificate electronically within 5 to 15 minutes; undated, so the 2023 publication year is used ([source, 2023](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062923114552178/txt/P17712007468b1040987f0e2e23aeb2064.txt)).

### Egypt: 3 Established

Births and deaths are entered digitally at 4,571 health offices, as stated in 2026; the share of all offices is not known.

*Identity integrated; Advancing.*

- Egypt's presentation gives its 2026 position as full nationwide automation of 4,571 health offices across the 27 governorates; it gives no separate total of offices ([source, 2026](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-egypt_crvs_transformation_journey.pdf)).
- Egypt's presentation says that in 2026 a central database for births and deaths runs on a secure platform and directly updates the statistics office's population clock and its monthly and annual reports of births and deaths ([source, 2026](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-egypt_crvs_transformation_journey.pdf)).
- Egypt's presentation describes the 2026 system as automated health offices feeding a central births and deaths database, using secured mobile GPRS/3G tunnels where landlines are lacking, in place of the 2017 paper registers ([source, 2026](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-egypt_crvs_transformation_journey.pdf)).
- The paper states that data entry is done at the health office, which prints the birth or death registration certificate once entry is complete, that the offices are linked online to a central database over a secured GPRS/3G mobile network, and that registration is completed within 15 minutes against one month on paper; the system dates from 2017 ([source, 2017](http://www.au.int/sites/default/files/newsevents/workingdocuments/37474-wd-crvs-v-the_electronic_birth_and_death_registration_system_in_egypt.pdf)).
- The paper states that EBDRS was developed and implemented in 2017 to automate 4,571 health offices, 300 health departments and 27 health directorates and link them to a central database, by the account of the Ministry of Planning, Monitoring and Administrative Reform; it gives no total number of health offices against the 4,571 ([source, 2017](http://www.au.int/sites/default/files/newsevents/workingdocuments/37474-wd-crvs-v-the_electronic_birth_and_death_registration_system_in_egypt.pdf)).

### Eritrea: 3 Established

Births and deaths were handwritten at local offices and entered in regional electronic registers one level up in 2017; current practice is not known.

- The Danish National ID Centre note of 2 November 2023, citing the Dutch foreign ministry's report of 6 February 2017, states that a birth is first entered in the handwritten family register at the local kebabi office, which issues a transfer letter that the parents take to the sub-zoba office, where the data is entered into the database and the hard copies archived; a death goes the same way, by a 'death letter' from the kebabi to the sub-zoba ([source, 2017-02-06](https://fln.dk/media/ow3jujfq/erit402.pdf)).
- Births and deaths are both registered in the sub-zoba's digital database after entry in the kebabi's handwritten register, according to the Dutch foreign ministry's report of 6 February 2017 as cited by the Danish National ID Centre; the note adds that there are no fixed or automatic routines for death registration ([source, 2017-02-06](https://fln.dk/media/ow3jujfq/erit402.pdf)).
- Citing EASO (May 2015), the note states that every one of the six zobas keeps an electronic population register, first introduced in zoba Maekel in 2003, updated by the sub-zoba administrations (54 sub-zobas), while each of the approximately 2,000 kebabi administrations keeps a handwritten family register; it gives no count of offices doing the digital entry ([source, 2015-05](https://fln.dk/media/ow3jujfq/erit402.pdf)).

### Eswatini: 3 Established

Births and deaths were entered in the national register at 14 service centres in 2022; the total number of offices is not known.

*Identity integrated; Backlog capture; Advancing.*

- The Central Statistical Office's 2022 report states that the civil registration officer fills the birth or death form by hand and a typist then enters it into the National Population Register, the registration being complete only once it is in the system; the register sits on a mainframe at the national data centre, reached from Home Affairs headquarters and the civil registration service centres over a wide-area network ([source, 2022](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2023-10/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202022%20%281%29.pdf)).
- The report states that births and deaths (and marriages and divorces) are captured into the National Population Register on registration, and counts 47,524 birth and 5,968 death registrations made in 2022, on Ministry of Home Affairs data ([source, 2022](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2023-10/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202022%20%281%29.pdf)).
- The report's Table 3.1, sourced to the Ministry of Home Affairs, lists 14 civil registration service centres with births and deaths registered in the National Population Register in 2022, and says the register's application software is installed in all civil registration centres; it gives no separate total of offices ([source, 2022](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2023-10/Vital%20Statistics%20Report%202022%20%281%29.pdf)).
- The AfDB and COMESA assessment (answers collected October 2022 to April 2023) lists Eswatini among the countries where all registration records are transferred electronically from local offices to regional offices and on to a central office, with entry of vital events done electronically in every office ([source, 2023](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourcefiles/Digitalization%20of%20Civil%20Registration%20and%20Vital%20Statistics%20in%20Africa.pdf)).
- The AfDB and COMESA assessment's statement that Eswatini's records move electronically from local offices to the centre is made of birth and death records, and it says all entry of vital events there is electronic (answers collected October 2022 to April 2023) ([source, 2023](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourcefiles/Digitalization%20of%20Civil%20Registration%20and%20Vital%20Statistics%20in%20Africa.pdf)).

### Gambia: 3 Established

Births are registered on a central digital system, with 79 local offices holding digital registers in 2025; the total number of offices is not known.

*Identity integrated; Backlog capture; Advancing.*

- The Registrar General states that as of June 2026 over 1.4 million people were registered in the eCRVS and issued a birth certificate and health insurance card, 54.64% of births registered being female and 45.36% male; no count of deaths is given ([source, 2026-06](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourceuploads/day-2-interoperability-gambia.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic states that the eCRVS birth module is in operation and a marriage module developed; it reports no death module in operation (no date given; document's published year used) ([source, 2025](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099102025103014657/pdf/P502279-e8627dee-4568-45e7-bb51-9a1aea1ff040.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic describes civil registration as a centralised system with a centralised register, carried out locally by Ministry of Health staff in each district at 258 health facilities and 79 centres, the civil registration offices holding digitalised registers; it does not say in terms that entry is online at the moment of registration (document's published year used) ([source, 2025](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099102025103014657/pdf/P502279-e8627dee-4568-45e7-bb51-9a1aea1ff040.pdf)).
- The World Bank diagnostic counts 79 Civil Registration Offices as registration points with digitalised registers issuing birth certificates, with no total of offices against which to set it (document's published year used) ([source, 2025](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099102025103014657/pdf/P502279-e8627dee-4568-45e7-bb51-9a1aea1ff040.pdf)).

### Ghana: 3 Established

Births were registered digitally at two Accra registry centres in 2024; how many offices do so, and how the record is made, is not known.

*Identity integrated.*

- The Eastern Regional Registrar of the Births and Deaths Registry says officers are expected to complete birth registrations digitally in communities and that poor internet connectivity is a major operational obstacle to doing so; the assembly member for Bepowase (Ayensuano District, about 3,000 people, no telecommunication network) says birth registration 'has been digitalised' and is very difficult there for want of a network. The document gives no date for this; dated from its published date, itself a proxy ([source, 2026-09](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1530759/unicef-bdr-and-kuami-eugene-push-for-improved.html)).
- A Ghana Statistical Service and UNECA paper using Births and Deaths Registry administrative data says births in Ablekuma West Municipal Assembly are registered on a digital registration platform that existed before the March 2024 integration with the Ghana Card: 2,310 births registered March to October 2023 and 1,722 March to October 2024 ([source, 2024-10](https://getinthepicture.org/system/files/sites/default/files/C1_Session%204.2_Owusu_Paper_0.pdf)).
- The paper covers the two existing Births and Deaths Registry offices of Ablekuma West Municipal Assembly, the Dansoman and Gbegbeyise registry centres, whose birth registrations on the digital platform it analyses (1,045 and 677 births, March to October 2024); this is one municipal district of 29 in Greater Accra and the paper gives no national count of offices ([source, 2024-10](https://getinthepicture.org/system/files/sites/default/files/C1_Session%204.2_Owusu_Paper_0.pdf)).
- The Births and Deaths Registry's report on 2022 says a district registration officer enters a death in the register and completes the paper Form 6, that the death register is kept by the district officer and sent to the national office only when full, and that completed forms are scanned into an electronic database managed by the IT unit at the national office; data come from 470 registries across 261 registration districts ([source, 2022](https://bdr.gov.gh/storage/formidable/7/STATISTICAL-REPORT-ON-DEATH-REGISTRATION-2022.pdf)).

### Guinea: 3 Established

Commune offices made digital birth certificates in September 2026, when a paper shortage halted printing; how many offices do so is not known.

*Backlog capture; Regressing.*
