# Sudan

## Statistics law

**Law in force.** Sudan's statistics law is the Statistics Act of 2003. An assessment by UN Women and the Central Bureau of Statistics, published in December 2021, [treats the Act as the current statistics law, calls it outdated and notes it is silent on gender statistics](<https://data.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/documents/Publications/Assessment%20of%20Sudan%20National%20Gender%20Statistics%20System.pdf>). The statistics strategy of 2012 had already listed [an inadequate Statistics Act, and some of its provisions not being implemented, among the system's weaknesses](<https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/arabstates/NSDS-Abridged-Version_Final_English_Nov-2012.pdf>).

**Rules made under the law and the law applied.** The nearest held fact is the 2021 assessment. [The assessment rests on fieldwork from March to July 2021 and finds the 2003 Act still the governing text](<https://data.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/documents/Publications/Assessment%20of%20Sudan%20National%20Gender%20Statistics%20System.pdf>). It is the latest source found for Sudan on the subject and is nearly five years old. Neither source names a regulation made under the Act, and the 2012 strategy says parts of the Act were not being carried out.

***Also on file***. [National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (NSDS)](<https://data.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/documents/Publications/Assessment%20of%20Sudan%20National%20Gender%20Statistics%20System.pdf>).

***Not found***. No source found names a decree, regulation or order made under the law. The Act's own text is not held, and nothing on it after 2021.
