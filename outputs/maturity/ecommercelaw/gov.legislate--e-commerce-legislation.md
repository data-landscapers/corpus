# E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

*As at 2026-10-17.*

## The norm

Chapter I of the Malabo Convention sets rules for electronic commerce, contracts and advertising, and Article 16 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol commits each party to a legal framework for electronic transactions, Article 27 to protecting those who buy online. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, has rules made under it and has been applied; it does not measure the law against either.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no law on electronic transactions or e-commerce and no draft of one |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a bill or draft, and no law in force |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a law in force |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: rules made under it |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: the law applied in the last three years |

### Ghana: 5 Leading

Ghana's 2008 Electronic Transactions Act is in force; no source found reports a November 2023 notice required e-commerce platforms to register, and later enforcement.

- Digital Policy Alert reports that in November 2023 Ghana's Ministry of Communications and Digitalisation issued an enforcement notice under the Electronic Transactions Act requiring all e-commerce platforms and digital delivery companies to register by January 2024 ([source, 2023-11](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/digest/dpa-digital-digest-ghana)).
- The op-ed names the Electronic Transactions Act as part of Ghana's existing legal foundation ([source, 2026-10-16](https://www.myjoyonline.com/bold-ambitions-fragile-foundations-is-ghana-ready-for-the-ai-and-data-centre-risks-it-is-inviting/)).

### Morocco: 5 Leading

Morocco's 2020 trust services law is in force, with a 2023 decree and three approved qualified providers listed in September 2026.

- Under Article 53 of Law 43-20 the DGSSI publishes its list of approved trust service providers, which names three: EURAFRIC INFORMATION (AfricTRUST, CertiSign+ and CertiSeal+), Barid Al Maghrib (Classe 3 signature and seal, qualified timestamp) and DamaneSign (qualified signature and seal), all at the qualified level; the page has no date, so its published date of 2026-09-03 is used ([source, 2026-09-03](https://www.dgssi.gov.ma/fr/prestations-et-produits-reglementes/)).
- The DGSSI lists the conformity certificates it issued under Articles 8 and 17 of Law 43-20 for qualified signature and seal creation devices: six current ones from CC-1/2024 (25 April 2024) to CC-1/2026 (18 March 2026), the latest being an Entrust nShield module ([source, 2026-03-18](https://www.dgssi.gov.ma/fr/prestations-et-produits-reglementes/)).
- The page cites Decree n° 2-22-687 made to apply Law 43-20, which sets transitional periods running from 13 July 2023 (Article 33: six months for non-qualified trust service providers) and lists exempt cryptology means in its annex 6; the same date starts the one-year period under Article 80 of the law for providers approved under Law 53-05 ([source, 2023-07-13](https://www.dgssi.gov.ma/fr/prestations-et-produits-reglementes/)).
- The DGSSI register of regulated services, applying Articles 8, 17, 46, 52 and 53 of Loi 43-20 on trust services for electronic transactions, treats the law as operating; the page has no date, so its published date of 2026-09-03 is used ([source, 2026-09-03](https://www.dgssi.gov.ma/fr/prestations-et-produits-reglementes/)).
