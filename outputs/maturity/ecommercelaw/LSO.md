# Lesotho

## E-commerce law

**Law in force.** The ministry responsible says Lesotho [lacks a legal and regulatory framework for electronic transactions](<https://www.itweb.africa/article/lesotho-revises-electronic-transactions-bill/KA3WwMdzPLDvrydZ>), which leaves government, businesses and consumers uncertain over electronic contracts, signatures, records, online services and digital commerce. A [bill is in draft](<https://www.itweb.africa/article/lesotho-revises-electronic-transactions-bill/KA3WwMdzPLDvrydZ>): the Electronic Transactions and Communications Bill, 2026 revises a 2022 bill of the same name. The government opened a [two-day validation workshop in Maseru on 28 September 2026](<https://www.gov.ls/development/micsti-on-electronic-transactions-communications-bill/>) to gather stakeholder views.

**Rules made under the law and the law applied.** No rules exist yet, because there is no law to make them under. The nearest held fact is the bill's progress: after the workshop, which was held with the United Nations Development Programme, the [draft goes to the Office of the Parliamentary Counsel for refinement](<https://www.itweb.africa/article/lesotho-revises-electronic-transactions-bill/KA3WwMdzPLDvrydZ>). The [FinMark Trust said in June 2026](<https://finmark.org.za/knowledge-hub/blog/building-a-cross-border-dpi-for-financial-inclusion-in-sadc>) that Lesotho, with the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Zimbabwe, lacks comprehensive legislation on electronic transactions.
