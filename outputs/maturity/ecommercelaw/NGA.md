# Nigeria

## E-commerce law

**Law in force.** Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act gives electronic signatures legal effect. [PLAC Legist says section 17 of the Act, as amended in 2024, sets which transactions are valid by electronic signature](<https://placng.org/Legist/cybercrimes-amendment-act-exposes-lapses-in-legislative-process/>). A wider law is still a bill. [TechCabal reports](<https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/>) that the National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill would give qualifying electronic signatures the same validity as handwritten ones. By May 2026 the House of Representatives had approved it, and it was waiting for the Senate. [In July 2026 the Senate ICT committee chair said the bill was at third reading](<https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/09/national-digital-economy-and-e-governance-act-2026-ready-in-weeks/>), with assent expected within weeks.

**Rules made under the law and the law applied.** No source found names rules made under the Cybercrimes Act on electronic signatures. [The bill would empower NITDA to accredit certification authorities](<https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/>), TechCabal says. Those bodies vouch for electronic signatures. That power sits in a bill still before the Senate in May 2026.
