# Nigeria

## E-commerce law

**Law in force.** Nigeria has a bill, not yet a law. [TechCabal reports](<https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/>) that the National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill, introduced in July 2024, gives qualifying electronic signatures the same validity as handwritten ones. By May 2026 the House of Representatives had approved the bill, and it was waiting for the Senate.

**Rules made under the law and the law applied.** Because the bill has not passed, no rules are yet made under it and nothing is applied. The nearest held fact is its design: [as TechCabal describes it](<https://techcabal.com/2026/08/13/nigerias-identity-law-raises-questions-over-digital-signatures/>), the bill empowers the National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA) to accredit certification authorities, the bodies that vouch for electronic signatures. That power sits in a bill that was still before the Senate in May 2026.

***Noted, not assessed***. [National Public Key Infrastructure (NIMC Root Certification Authority)](<https://techeconomy.ng/gitex-nigeria-2026-african-digital-trust-framework-nimc>).

***Not held***. No source held says a law on electronic transactions or e-commerce is in force.
