# Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy asks for a framework, with implementing acts, for interoperability and levels of assurance, and Article 19 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol for mutual recognition of electronic authentication and digital identities. No African Union instrument requires a country to pass a data sharing law. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, requires sharing and has been applied; it does not measure the law against either.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no law or decree on public bodies sharing data and no draft of one |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a bill or draft, and nothing in force |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a law, decree or regulation in force |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: it requires public bodies to share |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: it has been applied in the last three years |

## Countries

### Algeria: 4 Operating

A December 2025 presidential decree makes a national system the only channel for data exchange between public bodies; its application is not yet recorded.

- Presidential decree no. 25-320 of 30 December 2025 makes the national interoperability system the exclusive framework for exchanging digital data between public institutions and administrations over the secure national interconnection network (Art. 10), and requires each body to classify and catalogue its data to gain access (Art. 12) ([source, 2025-12-30](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/JO-FRANCAIS/2025/F2025087.pdf)).
- Presidential decree no. 25-320 of 30 December 2025, published in the Journal Officiel no. 87, sets up a national data governance system whose national interoperability system secures data exchange between public institutions and administrations (Art. 1, 2, 5, 9) ([source, 2025-12-30](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/JO-FRANCAIS/2025/F2025087.pdf)).

### Botswana: 4 Operating

A 2025 act tells public bodies not to collect what another body already holds; no rules made under it are recorded.

- Digital Services Act, 2025 (Act No. 22 of 2025, an act) states that a public or private body shall avoid collecting information already held by another public or private body (s.16(4)) and that citizens and businesses are to give the same information only once, shared and reused as necessary (s.5(m)), with administrative penalties of up to P500,000 for non-compliance with Authority directions (s.34); the text says nothing of any order or regulation yet made under it ([source, 2025-10-17](http://www.bocra.org.bw/sites/default/files/sites/default/files/documents/digital_services_ACT.pdf)).
- Digital Services Act, 2025 (Act No. 22 of 2025, an act of parliament) was passed by the National Assembly on 14 August 2025 and assented on 17 October 2025; it provides for public bodies exchanging data through an Information Gateway (s.22), a Framework for Secure Exchange of Information (s.12) and data retrieval between bodies (s.16), and its own text gives commencement as by Ministerial Order in the Gazette, with no commencement date stated in this document ([source, 2025-10-17](http://www.bocra.org.bw/sites/default/files/sites/default/files/documents/digital_services_ACT.pdf)).

### Cote d'Ivoire: 4 Operating

A 2017 ordinance binds every administrative authority to connect to the state's exchange platform; whether it has been applied since 2023 is not known.

- Ordonnance n° 2017-500 (ordinance, 2017) states in chapter V that the State sets up an e-services interoperability platform to which all administrative authorities are bound to connect, and that they must follow the general interoperability framework ([source, 2017-08-02](https://anssi.gouv.ci/documents/22/Ordonnance_2017-500_02_ao%C3%BBt_2017_sur_les_eservices.pdf)).
- Ordonnance n° 2017-500 of 2 August 2017 (an ordinance of the President, made in the Council of Ministers) on electronic exchanges between users and administrative authorities and between administrative authorities, with a chapter on interoperability of e-services, was issued on that date; the document gives no later repeal ([source, 2017-08-02](https://anssi.gouv.ci/documents/22/Ordonnance_2017-500_02_ao%C3%BBt_2017_sur_les_eservices.pdf)).

### Gabon: 4 Operating

### Mozambique: 4 Operating

A 2017 decree obliges public bodies to share data and use one exchange platform; a revised regulation was still in consultation in June 2026.

- Decree no. 67/2017 (decree) Article 11 obliges public administration entities to share the data they hold and reuse data held by other state entities, Article 12 obliges them to reuse citizen data already supplied via the interoperability platform, and Article 17 obliges them to use the single interoperability platform ([source, 2017-12-01](https://www.intic.gov.mz/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/BR_188_I_SERIE_2017.pdf)).
- INTIC's chairman said Mozambique has made progress including a thorough review of the Electronic Government Systems Interoperability Regulation, which sets standards for integrating and managing public information systems; the report names no decree number or year, and does not say the regulation covers data exchange or obliges bodies to share (a regulation, under review; as_of is the report's published date) ([source, 2025-12-05](https://techafricanews.com/2025/12/05/mozambique-prioritizes-data-governance-as-cornerstone-of-digital-sovereignty/)).

### Tunisia: 4 Operating

A 2020 decree-law and its decree require public structures to exchange data electronically; no rule made under them since 2023 is recorded.

- Government decree no. 2020-777 of 5 October 2020 says public structures must use electronic exchange among themselves and with users (art. 4), must make the list of their available data flows and licences available to the interoperability operator (art. 20), and may not demand more than one electronic copy of a document (art. 11); the text is binding from 1 January 2021 ([source, 2020-10-05](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/decret-gouvernemental-n-2020-777-du-5-octobre-2020-fixant-les-conditions-les-modalites-et-les-procedures-dapplication-du-decret-loi-du-chef-du-gouvernement-n-2020-31-du-10-juin/)).
- Circular of the Head of Government no. 04 of 2026 lists among its references decree-law no. 31 of 10 June 2020 on electronic data exchange between structures and their users and between structures, as approved by law no. 14 of 7 April 2021; the date of the fact is the approving law, and the circular is of 28 August 2026 ([source, 2021-04-07](https://www.pm.gov.tn/sites/default/files/2026-09/%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%20%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%AF04%20%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9%202026_0_0.pdf)).

### Angola: 3 Established

A 2018 presidential decree makes a national interoperability plan binding on public services; whether it requires them to share data is not known.

- Presidential Decree (Decreto Presidencial) n.º 46/18 of 14 February 2018 approves the national interoperability plan PNAGIA for central and local state administration, enters into force on publication, and states that adherence of public administration services to it is mandatory; the decree is a binding executive instrument, though the plan itself says its legal-level measures are still to be legislated ([source, 2018-02-14](https://lex.ao/docs/presidente-da-republica/2018/decreto-presidencial-n-o-46-18-de-14-de-fevereiro/)).

### Benin: 3 Established

A 2020 decree sets up the national data exchange platform; whether public bodies are required to use it is not known.

- Decree (décret) no. 2020-209 of 18 March 2020, issued by the Presidency, sets up the XRoad BJ national interoperability platform; the document describes it as the founding instrument of the platform and states nothing on whether it obliges public bodies to use it ([source, 2020-03-18](https://api.xroad.bj/media/617ac98a74baa_D%C3%A9cret%20n%C2%B02020-209%20du%2018%20mars%202020%20portant%20mise%20en%20place%20de%20la%20Plateforme%20d%27Interop%C3%A9rabilit%C3%A9%20XRoad%20BJ%20(1).pdf)).

### Burkina Faso: 3 Established

A 2018 order of the Prime Minister sets interoperability rules for public administrations; whether it requires them to share data is not known.

- Burkina 24 reports that the Referentiel general d'interoperabilite (RGI), a normative document fixing the rules that make the information systems of the different public administrations compatible, was adopted by an order (arrete) of the Prime Minister dated 23 May 2018, and was the subject of a training seminar for IT directorates on 15-17 October 2019; it is an arrete, not an act or decree, and some of its communication protocols are described as mandatory and others as recommended ([source, 2018-05-23](https://burkina24.com/2019/10/15/burkina-un-referentiel-pour-moderniser-ladministration-publique/)).

### Mauritania: 3 Established

- Decree (Premier Ministre) n° 2022-009 on electronic exchanges between users and administrative authorities and between administrative authorities, published in Journal Officiel n° 1505 after the Council of Ministers heard it on 3 November 2021, applies to any information exchanged electronically between administrative authorities and sets a security reference (RGS) their information systems must meet; the document gives only the year 2022 from the decree number, and its text is an excerpt that stops inside Article 4 ([source, 2022](https://mtnima.gov.mr/sites/default/files/2024-02/D%C3%A9cret%20n%C2%B02022-009%20Relatif%20aux%20%C3%A9changes%20%C3%A9lectroniques%20entre%20les%20usagers%20et%20les%20autorit%C3%A9s%20administratives%20et%20entre%20les%20autorit%C3%A9s%20administratives_%5BJ.O%20n%C2%B01505%5D.pdf)).

### Sao Tome and Principe: 3 Established

### Tanzania: 3 Established

The 2019 e-government act has a section on data sharing and exchange; whether it requires public institutions to share is not known.

- The e-Government Act (Cap. 273, Act No. 10 of 2019, an act of parliament) commenced on 19 September 2019 (GN No. 964 of 2019) and applies to all public institutions; its Part VIII, Electronic Government Data Management, has a section 48 headed Data sharing and exchange and it defines interoperability as systems exchanging data, though the section text is not held and the date is the Act's commencement ([source, 2019-09-19](https://oagmis.oag.go.tz/portal/acts/revised/559/download)).

### Zambia: 3 Established

The 2021 electronic government act lets public bodies share data and does not require it; an amendment bill was presented in January 2026.

- The Electronic Government Act, 2021 (Act No. 41 of 2021, an act of Parliament, assented 19 May 2021) is an enacted e-government law whose own text provides for public bodies sharing data: s.12(3) lets a public body retrieve documents from the issuing public body with the data subject's consent, and s.17 provides for joint automated procedures between public bodies ([source, 2021-05-19](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2041%20OF%202021%2C%20THE%20ELECTRONIC%20GOVERNMENT%20ACT%2C%202021_0.pdf)).

### Cameroon: 2 Nascent

A draft e-government law described in 2025 would have administrations share data; it has not been passed.

- PATNuC's explainer of 20 November 2025 describes the e-Gov reform on access to public information and digital transformation of the administration as one of the texts examined at the Ebolowa workshop, and says it provides that administrations must make their information systems compatible to share data securely, with GovTech Cameroun steering interoperability; it reports no promulgation, so this is a bill (the document itself gives no date for the bill, so as_of is the workshop and the document's published date of 2025-11-20) ([source, 2025-11](https://patnuc.cm/2025/11/20/services-publics-et-transparence-les-evolutions-cles-de-la-loi-e-gov/)).

### Ghana: 2 Nascent

- A representative of the Communications Minister said at the Ghana AI Summit that government was preparing a Data Harmonisation Act (data governance and classification standards across public institutions) and that a proposed Data Exchange Act (a bill, not an act in force) would improve interoperability and facilitate secure data sharing among government agencies ([source, 2026-07-29](https://thechronicle.com.gh/government-rolls-out-10-year-ai-implementation-roadmap-to-drive-jobs-economic-growth/)).
- NITA's draft NITA Data Harmonisation Bill 2025 (a bill, not yet law) would give a legal basis for data sharing, standardisation and system integration across public and private institutions; public consultation was set for 19 to 27 November 2025, then Cabinet and Parliament ([source, 2025-10-23](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/ghanas-data-harmonisation-bill-2025-to-be-opened-for-public-consultation-from-nov-19-to-27-2025.html)).

### Liberia: 2 Nascent
