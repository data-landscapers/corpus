# Guinea

## Data sharing law

**Law or decree in force.** [UNICEF Guinea](<https://www.unicef.org/guinea/rapports/larr%C3%AAt%C3%A9-dinterop%C3%A9rabilit%C3%A9-entre-le-minist%C3%A8re-de-la-sant%C3%A9-et-le-matd>) lists a government interoperability arrêté between the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralisation (MATD), published in May 2019, for promoting vaccination and obtaining birth certificates. [Its scope is health and civil registration only](<https://www.unicef.org/guinea/rapports/larr%C3%AAt%C3%A9-dinterop%C3%A9rabilit%C3%A9-entre-le-minist%C3%A8re-de-la-sant%C3%A9-et-le-matd>). The arrêté is authored by the government, and UNICEF Guinea gives only the month of publication, May 2019, for a text covering the Ministry of Health and MATD.

**Sharing required and the law applied.** The nearest held fact is the same [May 2019 arrêté](<https://www.unicef.org/guinea/rapports/larr%C3%AAt%C3%A9-dinterop%C3%A9rabilit%C3%A9-entre-le-minist%C3%A8re-de-la-sant%C3%A9-et-le-matd>), which UNICEF Guinea describes as running between the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralisation. It is listed for two purposes, promoting vaccination and obtaining birth certificates, and UNICEF Guinea dates it to May 2019.

***Not found***. No source found says a law requires public bodies to share data, to connect to the government's exchange platform, or not to ask a person for what another public body already holds.
