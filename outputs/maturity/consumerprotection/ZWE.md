# Zimbabwe

## Consumer protection

**Rules in force.** [The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe directed banks and deposit-taking microfinance institutions to cap cash withdrawal fees at 2% and POS charges at 1.5%, and to remove balance inquiry fees on banking and mobile banking platforms, by 31 March 2026](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200052094/rbz-cuts-limits-bank-charges-expands-mobile-and-zipit-limits>). [From 1 April 2026 POS charges on local and international cards were capped at 1.5% of the transaction, up to US$20](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/opinion-analysis/article/200055031/zig-is-legal-tender-so-why-cant-you-use-it-online>). [The Consumer Protection Commission said in October 2024 that the Consumer Protection Act gives consumers the right to choose how they pay, and that the law requires businesses to offer electronic payment](<https://zimbabwenow.co.zw/articles/12707/electronic-transactions-a-consumer-right-consumer-protection-commission>).

**Complaints handled.** No source found since October 2023 names a financial ombudsman or a regulator's unit that takes complaints about digital financial services. The nearest held fact: [in February 2026 the central bank's governor said it continued to receive complaints from the public about the cost of banking](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200052094/rbz-cuts-limits-bank-charges-expands-mobile-and-zipit-limits>).

**Enforcement reported.** No source found since October 2023 reports a fine, sanction or complaint figures. The nearest held fact: [the fee caps above](<https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200052094/rbz-cuts-limits-bank-charges-expands-mobile-and-zipit-limits>).
