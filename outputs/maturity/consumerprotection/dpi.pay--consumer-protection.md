# Consumer protection: consumer protection

*As at 2026-11-11.*

## The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy asks for consumer protection in digital financial services across disclosure, responsible lending, data privacy and dispute resolution. This study asks the four questions above; it does not measure a country against the norm.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 No rules | A source from the past three years says no consumer-protection rules apply to digital financial services. |
| 2 Drafting | Yes to 1 |
| 3 Rules in force | Yes to 2 |
| 4 Complaints handled | Yes to 3 |
| 5 Enforced | Yes to 4 |

## Countries

### Cape Verde: 5 Leading

Cabo Verde's payment-services law is applied to digital banking, and the central bank handles and counts internet-banking complaints.

- The Banco de Cabo Verde's 2024 conduct supervision report says banks refunded clients for unauthorised card and internet-banking transactions under Decree-Law 8/2018, the legal regime for payment services and electronic money ([source, 2024](https://www.bcv.cv/SiteCollectionDocuments/2025/RSC%202024.pdf)).
- A Banco de Cabo Verde official says the BCV handles complaints about digital payment systems, though often cannot resolve them where the user caused the failure ([source, 2025-03-19](https://www.asemana.cv/index.php/atualidade/14109-maior-parte-das-fraudes-acontece-por-uso-indevido-dos-sistemas-de-pagamentos-digitais-bcv)).
- The Banco de Cabo Verde recorded 10 complaints about internet banking among 169 banking complaints in 2024, and banks refunded complainants 3.25 million escudos, mainly for unauthorised card and internet-banking transactions ([source, 2024](https://www.bcv.cv/SiteCollectionDocuments/2025/RSC%202024.pdf)).

### Ghana: 5 Leading

The central bank shut down over 400 illegal online lenders in 2025 and reports complaints against payment service providers.

- Bank of Ghana, with EOCO and the Cyber Security Authority, said it had uncovered and shut down over 400 illegal online lending operators ([source, 2025-10-15](https://www.graphic.com.gh/business/business-news/ghana-news-bog-eoco-crack-down-on-400-illegal-online-lenders.html)).
- The Bank of Ghana reports 23 complaints against payment systems and service providers in 2023, digital-fraud complaints rising 48% on 2022, and monetary awards and refunds of GHS 3,803,073.61 across all complaints ([source, 2023](https://www.bog.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/2023-COMPLAINTS-MANAGEMENT-REPORT_Publication.pdf)).

### Kenya: 5 Leading

The competition authority reported 355 complaints about digital lenders in the year to June 2025, five times the year before.

- Business Daily reports Competition Authority of Kenya data showing borrower complaints about digital lenders rose to 355 cases in the year to June 2025 from 67 the year before ([source, 2025-06](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/complaints-about-digital-lenders-jump-five-times--5528224)).

### Malawi: 5 Leading

The central bank counted nearly 47,000 complaints about failed digital financial services in 2024.

- RBM's 2024 report counts 46,969 banking-sector complaints linked to failed digital financial services, where accounts were debited without the service delivered ([source, 2024](https://times.mw/financial-consumer-complaints-increase/)).
- Malawi recorded 36,481 complaints of digital payment challenges out of 396.6 million digital transactions from April 2023 to March 2024, as stated by RBM's Deputy Governor ([source, 2024-03](https://times.mw/reserve-bank-of-malawi-demands-seamless-digital-payment-systems/)).

### Mozambique: 5 Leading

The central bank reported 44 complaints against mobile money providers in the first half of 2026.

- Banco de Mocambique reports 44 mobile money complaints in the first half of 2026 (Vodafone M-Pesa 26, M-Mola 18) and says it issued directives for correction, including reimbursement of MZN 73,243,072 in improperly charged fees, across all complaints ([source, 2026-06](https://bancomoc.mz/media/ofiltvbv/dsc_comunicado_estati-sticas-das-reclamac-o-es-recebidas-no-banco-de-moc-ambique-contra-instituic-o-es-financeiras-no-i-semestre-de-2026_v01_en.pdf)).

### Nigeria: 5 Leading

Enforcement of consumer protection in digital finance was reported in 2026.

- TechCabal reports MTN suspended its airtime lending in April 2026 under a regulatory suspension linked to the FCCPC's new requirements, cutting the lending base to about a quarter of its first-quarter run rate and costing about 50 billion naira in first-half revenue ([source, 2026-04](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/24/mtn-sees-nigeria-rebound-as-airtime-lending-returns/)).

### Seychelles: 5 Leading

The central bank published complaint figures for payment service providers for 2025.

- The Central Bank of Seychelles reports that payment service providers received 25 complaints in 2025 (14 in the first half, 11 in the second) ([source, 2025](https://www.cbs.sc/Downloads/ConsumerProtection/ComplaintsData/Consumer%20Complaints%20Data%20-H1%20and%20H2%202025.pdf)).

### Tanzania: 5 Leading

The central bank barred 69 digital lending apps in 2024 for breaching its consumer protection guidance.

- The Bank of Tanzania named 69 digital lending apps on 21 November 2024 as unlicensed and failing the August 2024 guidance note, barred them from lending, and said it was working with the TCRA to block them ([source, 2024-11-21](https://www.bot.go.tz/Adverts/PressRelease/en/2024112512091545.pdf)).
- Nukta Habari reports the Bank of Tanzania on 21 November 2024 cancelled the permits of 69 online lending apps for breaching licence conditions, including failing to protect customer privacy ([source, 2024-11-21](https://nukta.co.tz/bot-yafuta-vibali-vya-apps-69-zinazotoa-mikopo-mtandaoni)).

### Burkina Faso: 4 Operating

A financial mediator took complaints, e-money included, in 2024; no source found reports enforcement figures since 2023.

- The report says decree 2022-1066 of 9 December 2022 created the Observatoire de la qualité des services financiers (OQSF-BF) with financial mediation between providers, including electronic money issuers, and users, and that the UEMOA Banking Commission can also be seized when a complaint is unsatisfied ([source, 2024-12](https://www.finances.gov.bf/fileadmin/user_upload/storage/Etude_diagnostique_mecanismes_de_recours_interne_des_PSF_Rapport_Final.pdf)).

### Cote d'Ivoire: 4 Operating

A body took complaints about digital financial services in 2025; enforcement is not reported.

- The OQSF-CI executive secretary says consumers can bring complaints on financial services to the Observatoire through a green line, an email address and a website, and that financial mediation is part of its consumer-protection mission ([source, 2025-07-03](https://infocentrale.net/2025/07/05/entretien-zoom-sur-les-plateformes-digitales-pour-saisir-loqsf-en-cote-divoire/)).

### Eswatini: 4 Operating

The central bank's ombudsman took complaints, mobile money included, in 2026; enforcement on digital financial services is not on record.

- The World Bank FSAP note says a CBE Ombudsman and an FSRA Ombudsman have been in place for several years taking complaints about credit, transaction and savings products, the CBE one without finalized procedural rules ([source, 2024-10](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099082525170036534/pdf/P505756-da939ce0-36a3-4072-9fbb-8f90280267ab.pdf)).
- The Central Bank of Eswatini says a consumer unsatisfied with a financial institution's response to a complaint can escalate it to the CBE's Ombudsman, and that the Market Conduct and Consumer Protection Unit supervises institutions including mobile money service providers ([source, 2026](https://www.centralbank.org.sz/market-conduct-consumer-protection-unit/)).

### Ethiopia: 4 Operating

A financial consumer protection directorate took complaints in 2026; enforcement on digital financial services is not on record.

- The NFIS II assessment, reported by The Reporter, says NFIS II established a dedicated financial consumer protection directorate that achieved a 90 percent complaint resolution rate, while auditors cautioned that consumer safety mechanisms remain ill-equipped for digital fraud, scams and social-engineering risks, given weaknesses in the law on electronic transaction liability ([source, 2026-10-03](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/53174/)).
- The National Bank of Ethiopia states that financial institutions are required to inform customers about fraud risks and their responsibilities in safeguarding financial instruments under Articles 5.1.7 and 5.2.8 of Financial Consumer Protection Directive No. FCP/01/2020, and instructs banks, payment instrument issuers and microfinance institutions to run a fraud awareness campaign ([source, 2026-03-25](https://nbe.gov.et/announcements/)).

### Lesotho: 4 Operating

The central bank's consumer protection division handled financial complaints in 2026; no source found reports enforcement on digital financial services.

- The Central Bank of Lesotho's Financial Consumer Protection Division handles and resolves financial complaints, and the bank plans to train its staff in complaints data analysis ([source, 2026-02](https://newsdayonline.co.ls/cbl-tightens-financial-customer-protection-efforts/)).

### Rwanda: 4 Operating

### Senegal: 4 Operating

### South Africa: 4 Operating

Credit rules bound digital lenders and the financial ombud took digital banking complaints in 2026; no fine or sanction under them is on record.

- The article (Magolego, Financial Regulation Journal, 2026-02-18) states that the National Credit Act affordability assessment, sections 81 and 82 read with Regulation 23A, is a conduct and consumer protection requirement applying to credit providers including digital lenders, with debt review and reckless lending findings as remedies ([source, 2026-02-18](https://financialregulationjournal.co.za/2026/02/18/credit-risk-and-affordability-in-south-african-credit-regulation-distinguishing-two-assessments-in-a-digital-credit-environment/)).
- The National Financial Ombud South Africa Banking Division resolved disputes in 2025 and reports that mobile banking and internet banking fraud were the highest complaint generators, 39% of all cases received ([source, 2025-12-15](https://nfosa.co.za/nfo-banking-division-returns-r60-million-to-consumers-in-2025/)).
- The National Financial Ombud Scheme takes consumer complaints about banking fraud, most of it mobile and digital banking, and closed 3,651 fraud cases in 2025, per the ombud ([source, 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/business/fraud-complaints-at-the-banking-ombud-nearly-double-in-a-year-as-digital-scams-surge/)).

### Uganda: 4 Operating

Regulators took complaints about mobile money and digital lenders in 2024; no source found reports enforcement on digital financial services.

- Umra officials said the authority had received multiple complaints from clients about licensed digital lenders still harassing them ([source, 2024-06](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/online-lenders-harassing-borrowers-risk-closure-4645060)).
- The Bank of Uganda runs a Financial Consumer Empowerment Mechanism that takes customer complaints about supervised financial institutions after 21 days without a satisfactory answer from the institution, guided by the Mobile Money Guidelines 2013 among others ([source, 2024-04-29](https://govinfohub.go.ug/index.php/2024/04/29/bou-details-redress-procedure-for-aggrieved-bank-customers/)).

### Zambia: 4 Operating

The consumer protection commission handled mobile money complaints in 2024; no source found reports enforcement of the e-money fee rules.

### Algeria: 3 Established

Central bank rules of August 2025 make payment providers ring-fence customer funds and disclose fees; no complaints body has been found.

- Launchbase Africa reports Bank of Algeria Instruction 06-2025 of 17 August 2025 requires payment service providers to hold customer funds in segregated escrow accounts, carry a bank guarantee or liability insurance, and give users contracts setting out fees and dispute resolution procedures ([source, 2025-08-17](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/08/19/algeria-opens-for-fintech-new-psp-rules-create-a-playbook-for-payments-startups/)).
- The Bank of Algeria's director general of credit and banking regulation says the bank is drafting a regulation to protect consumers of financial services, notably digital ones, to be promulgated before the end of 2024 ([source, 2024-10-09](https://algerie-eco.com/2024/10/09/protection-des-consommateurs-des-services-financiers-un-reglement-sera-promulgue-avant-fin-2024/)).

### Botswana: 3 Established

Consumer-protection rules for digital finance were in force in 2026; no source found reports a complaints body.

### Gabon: 3 Established

### Guinea: 3 Established
