# Togo

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [ISOC Pulse, from self-reported PeeringDB data, lists the Togo Internet Exchange Point (TGIX) in Lomé with 9 member ASNs and 18 Gbps cumulative port capacity, 7 of 9 peering at the route server, one ASN joined and none left in the last 12 months](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/677/>). [Lomé Data Centre's own home page lists a local Internet Exchange Point among its connectivity features and peering among its interconnection services; it does not name the exchange or give members](<https://lomedatacentre.tg/en/n-home-page-en/>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** [TGIX members registered outside Togo include PCH (two ASNs, United States), and GVA (Côte d'Ivoire), per ISOC Pulse/PeeringDB; 6 of 9 members are registered in Togo](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/677/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.

***Also on file***. [Lomé Data Centre](<https://lomedatacentre.tg/en/n-home-page-en/>); [IXP (WARCIP Togo)](<https://numerique.gouv.tg/actualites/discours-de-madame-le-ministre-a-loccasion-de-latelier-sur-la-politique-sectorielle-2018-2022>).
