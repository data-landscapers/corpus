# Niger

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [ANP, citing the Smart Villages project (PVI), reports that the project achieved the creation of Niger's first Internet Exchange Point with the operator Niger Telecoms, improving the resilience of data exchange and the quality of internet traffic](<https://anp.ne/niger-grace-aux-infrastructures-adaptees-fournies-par-le-pvi-plus-de-900-localites-accedent-a-une-connectivite-numerique-haut-debit/>). [Agence Ecofin reports that Niger inaugurated a new Internet Exchange Point in Niamey on 19 August 2025, opened by the Minister of Communication and New Information Technologies, as a place where ISPs interconnect directly to exchange traffic locally](<https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-numerique/2108-130856-le-niger-se-dote-d-un-nouveau-point-d-echange-internet>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://anp.ne/niger-grace-aux-infrastructures-adaptees-fournies-par-le-pvi-plus-de-900-localites-accedent-a-une-connectivite-numerique-haut-debit/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
