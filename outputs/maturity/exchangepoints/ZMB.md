# Zambia

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [The operator's own page describes LSK IX in Lusaka as Zambia's neutral, non-profit exchange where ISPs and network operators interconnect, with 14+ networks connected and public peering and route server services running](<https://lusakaixp.co.zm/>). [Traffic: 2.8 Tbps (peak) at LSK IX on 2026-08-29, as the operator's page states ("2.8 Tbps Peak Traffic Handled")](<https://lusakaixp.co.zm/>). [AFRINIC states its DNS deployment at the Lusaka IXP in Lusaka went live on 2025-12-08, cutting DNS query response time from 23.188 ms to 1.524 ms (about 93.4%), which shows the exchange is operating](<https://www.linkedin.com/posts/afrinic_great-news-from-lusaka-our-deployment-at-activity-7403752672203509763-lW7D>). [PeeringDB's record of LSK-IX in Lusaka, last updated 2024-08-02, lists 12 peers and 12 connections (10 open peers) with 76G total capacity, all IPv6-ready](<https://www.peeringdb.com/ix/615>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://lusakaixp.co.zm/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
