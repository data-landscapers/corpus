# Sudan

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Internet Society Pulse, from PeeringDB data of August 2026, shows SIXP Sudan (Sudan Internet Exchange Point) with four members at 1 Gbps each](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/660/>). Three use RPKI, and no ASN joined or left in the previous 12 months. No city is given. [PeeringDB's record for SIXP Sudan (Sudan Internet Exchange Point), operated by the National Information Center of Sudan in Khartoum, shows 4 peers, 4G total capacity and 50% IPv6, last updated 2020-01-22](<https://www.peeringdb.com/ix/2320>). [The National Information Center's WSIS Forum 2016 deck lists a National Internet Exchange Point among its services](<https://www.itu.int/net4/wsis/forum/2016/Content/AgendaFiles/document/14d93639-cc47-4322-b464-5d97518a6653/NIC_SUDAN.pdf>). The deck says it keeps traffic within Sudan, keeps service available nationally during an internet blackout and lowers internet costs. These place an exchange point in service. A global content network serving from it is not stated.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/660/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city. It also does so where networks from other countries peer there. The anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.

***Also on file***. None found.

***Not found***. No source found says a global content network has a cache at the exchange or peers there.
