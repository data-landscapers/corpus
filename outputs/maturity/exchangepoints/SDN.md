# Sudan

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Internet Society Pulse, from PeeringDB data of August 2026, shows SIXP Sudan (Sudan Internet Exchange Point) with four members at 1 Gbps each, three using RPKI, and no ASN joined or left in the previous 12 months; city not given](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/660/>). [PeeringDB's record for SIXP Sudan (Sudan Internet Exchange Point), operated by the National Information Center of Sudan in Khartoum, shows 4 peers, 4G total capacity and 50% IPv6, last updated 2020-01-22](<https://www.peeringdb.com/ix/2320>). [The National Information Center's WSIS Forum 2016 deck lists a National Internet Exchange Point among its services, saying it keeps traffic within Sudan, keeps service available nationally during an internet blackout and lowers internet costs](<https://www.itu.int/net4/wsis/forum/2016/Content/AgendaFiles/document/14d93639-cc47-4322-b464-5d97518a6653/NIC_SUDAN.pdf>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/ixp/660/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
