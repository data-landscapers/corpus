# DR Congo

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [KINIX (Kinshasa Internet Exchange), founded in 2012 and run by ISPA-DRC as one of three community IXPs of the RDC-IX project, was operating with more than 20 participating networks when it relocated its infrastructure into the Raxio DRC data centre in Kinshasa, per Raxio Group's 20 August 2026 statement](<https://www.raxiogroup.com/raxio-drc-to-host-kinshasa-internet-exchange-kinix-strengthening-national-connectivity/>). [ACIX (Africa Congo Internet Exchange), established in 2023 and operated by DE-CIX for the NGO Internet pour Tous, was in service in Kinshasa and expanded to a second data-centre site in OADC Texaf's Kinshasa FIH1 facility, per ITWeb Africa on 19 May 2026](<https://itweb.africa/article/distributed-exchange-boosts-drc-connectivity/4r1ly7R9GkLvpmda>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://www.raxiogroup.com/raxio-drc-to-host-kinshasa-internet-exchange-kinix-strengthening-national-connectivity/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
