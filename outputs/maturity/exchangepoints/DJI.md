# Djibouti

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Wingu Africa presented to Djibouti's communications minister an internet exchange point (IXP) that is already in service within its data centre in Djibouti, per the Agence Djiboutienne d'Information report of the 28 September 2026 meeting](<https://www.adi.dj/article/138942>). [We Are Tech Africa states that Djibouti operates three data centres and an Internet Exchange Point (IXP); the exchange is not named and no members or city are given](<https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/djibouti-seeks-itu-smart-africa-support-for-telecoms-and-ai-development>). [Wingu Africa says it has launched the AMS-IX Djibouti Internet exchange in partnership with AMS-IX, expanding on the existing DjIX infrastructure](<https://www.connectingafrica.com/data-centers/wingu-africa-secures-60m-to-fuel-east-african-expansion>). [The French Treasury note says the data centre operator also runs the Djibouti Internet Exchange (DjIX), a neutral and independent exchange point](<https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/PagesInternationales/Pages/0f39f73e-5050-4335-abba-112c8823fe7d/files/7eec865a-efa2-431e-af9e-961511113d91>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://www.adi.dj/article/138942>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
