# Rwanda

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [The Internet Society says RINEX, the Rwanda Internet Exchange, has become a central part of Rwanda's digital ecosystem and, through its Sustainable Peering Infrastructure Grant Program, expanded its operations, including new equipment at its disaster recovery site](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/07/how-local-peering-is-strengthening-africas-internet/>). [Gewape Cloud Group says on its own LinkedIn account that it is now connected and live on RINEX, the Rwanda Internet Exchange Point run by RICTA, and can exchange traffic directly with the other local networks connected there; the post gives no city or member count for RINEX](<https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gewape-cloud-group_gewapecloud-rinex-rwandacloud-activity-7468194906369593344-ao9G>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/07/how-local-peering-is-strengthening-africas-internet/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
