# Kenya

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [ITWeb Africa, citing LINX, lists TikTok among the platforms active on the LINX Nairobi exchange (alongside Starlink)](<https://itweb.africa/article/linx-expands-kenya-peering-network/5yONP7ErXpRMXWrb>). [The companies say combined traffic across the two Kenyan LINX exchange points rose twentyfold over the past year, reaching a peak above 330 Gbps (no starting figure given)](<https://itweb.africa/article/linx-expands-kenya-peering-network/5yONP7ErXpRMXWrb>). Together these place an exchange point in service, a global content network serving from it and its traffic shown growing, the first three of the study's questions, and the stage rests on that and on its reach below. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** [LINX operates two interconnected exchange points in Kenya, Nairobi and Mombasa; a cloud provider connected to Mombasa in 2025 and to Nairobi in 2026, and both are described as providing routing options](<https://itweb.africa/article/linx-expands-kenya-peering-network/5yONP7ErXpRMXWrb>). [LINX states LINX Mombasa in Mombasa is a second exchange point in service in Kenya alongside LINX Nairobi in Nairobi, the first IXP with a physical presence in both cities](<https://www.linx.net/news/linx-mombasa-ready-for-business/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
