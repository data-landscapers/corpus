# South Africa

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Content delivery network CDN77 has joined JINX Johannesburg as a new member since March 2026, per the article](<https://tech.africa/cinx-oadc-cape-town-expansion/>). [Microsoft, Cloudflare, Akamai, Meta and Fastly hold 100 Gbps ports at CINX Cape Town (Teraco Rondebosch), and Amazon holds 100 Gbps at Africa Data Centres Diep Rivier, per INX-ZA's member export of 10 August 2026](<https://tech.africa/cinx-oadc-cape-town-expansion/>). These place an exchange point in service with a global content network serving from it, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Traffic shown growing over a year is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** [South Africa has two exchanges in different cities run by INX-ZA: CINX in Cape Town (122 networks) and JINX in Johannesburg (208 networks), per INX-ZA's member export of 10 August 2026](<https://tech.africa/cinx-oadc-cape-town-expansion/>). [Africa Data Centres describes two exchanges operated by INX-ZA in different cities, JINX in Johannesburg and CINX in Cape Town, both in service](<https://www.africadatacentres.com/africa-data-centres-opens-newly-expanded-carrier-grade-facilities-in-johannesburg-and-cape-town/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
