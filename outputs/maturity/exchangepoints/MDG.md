# Madagascar

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Internet Society says Madagascar has revived its main IXP, MGIX, and is now focused on attracting new IXP members](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/07/how-local-peering-is-strengthening-africas-internet/>). [Internet Society Pulse (from PeeringDB) lists one active IXP in Madagascar, MGIX in Antananarivo, with 4 members as of February 2026; under 1% of the 7 active domestic networks reach IXPs](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/country/MG/>). [Internet Society Foundation grant record says MGIX, Madagascar's first IXP, has been suspended since 2019 and the 2024 grant of USD 49,958 to iRENALA is to restart it](<https://www.isocfoundation.org/2025/01/meet-the-new-grantees-improving-peering-and-interconnection-around-the-globe/>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** [Internet Society Pulse states Madagascar has 1 active IXP, MGIX, at one physical location in Antananarivo, present in 1 of 2 population centres over 300,000, as of February 2026; the origin of members is not stated](<https://pulse.internetsociety.org/en/ixp-tracker/country/MG/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
