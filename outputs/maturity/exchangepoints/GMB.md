# Gambia

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [The SIXP administrator told AfPIF 2015 that the Serekunda Internet Exchange Point (AS37698), established in July 2014, is the first and only IXP in The Gambia and has seven ASNs peering](<https://www.afpif.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Gambia-IXP-Experience-.pdf>). [Traffic: 50.541 Mbit/s (maximum reached) at SIXP, Serekunda, as stated by the SIXP administrator](<https://www.afpif.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Gambia-IXP-Experience-.pdf>). [The Internet Society reports that the first Internet Exchange Point in The Gambia, the Serrekunda Internet Exchange Point (SIXP), was launched on 12 July 2014 in Banjul with African Union AXIS support](<https://www.internetsociety.org/news/press-releases/2014/first-internet-exchange-point-in-the-gambia-launched-in-banjul/>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://www.afpif.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Gambia-IXP-Experience-.pdf>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
