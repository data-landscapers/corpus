# Tunisia

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [ATI (Tunisie Internet) states on its institutional page that it operates and manages Tunisia's national internet exchange point, TUNIXP](<https://www.ati.tn/a-propos/>). [Inflect, a colocation marketplace, states that TunIXP is Tunisia's primary national exchange point, keeping local traffic in the country and reducing reliance on international transit, as of September 2025](<https://inflect.com/datacenters/africa/tunisia>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://www.ati.tn/a-propos/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
