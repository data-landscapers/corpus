# Ghana

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Google and Akamai connect at GIXA in Accra, and nowhere else in Accra, per bgp.tools and PeeringDB data of 10 August 2026; Meta is also present there](<https://tech.africa/accra-three-internet-exchanges/>). [The op-ed says the Ghana Internet Exchange (GIX) in Accra enables local traffic routing](<https://www.myjoyonline.com/bold-ambitions-fragile-foundations-is-ghana-ready-for-the-ai-and-data-centre-risks-it-is-inviting/>). [Traffic: 200 Gbps (aggregate, operator's own figure announced 10 August 2026, against 525 Gbps of connected ports) at Accra-IX on 2026-08-10; the article notes no public traffic graph exists and that connected ports are not traffic](<https://tech.africa/accra-three-internet-exchanges/>). These place an exchange point in service with a global content network serving from it, which answers the first two of the study's four questions. Traffic shown growing over a year is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** [GVA of Cote d'Ivoire holds one of four 100 Gbps ports at Accra-IX, as seen in public routing data in August 2026, so a network from another country is a member there](<https://tech.africa/accra-three-internet-exchanges/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.

***Also on file***. [MDXi Appolonia City data centre](<https://www.appolonia.com.gh/news/mainone-data-centre-opens/>).

***Not found***. No source found gives the exchange's traffic at two dates a year apart showing growth.
