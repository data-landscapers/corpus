## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [Traffic: 6 Gbps (peak) at BFIX, reached after Internet Society training from 2019, up from 0.5 Gbps](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/05/local-infrastructure-lower-costs-how-peering-is-moving-the-needle-on-internet-affordability/>). [Traffic: 6 Gbps (peak) at BFIX, up from 0.5 Gbps after 2019 Internet Society support](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2025/04/local-businesses-benefit-from-burkina-fasos-internet-development>). [Burkina Faso has IXP peering in operation: per Internet Society Pulse, 80% of networks are IXP members or customers of IXP members and traffic exchange capacity has grown consistently over five years; the exchange is not named in this document](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/07/how-local-peering-is-strengthening-africas-internet/>). [BFIX (Burkina Faso Internet Exchange) is in service: the Internet Society says 80% of networks in Burkina Faso are IXP members or customers of IXP members, citing Internet Society Pulse data](<https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2026/05/local-infrastructure-lower-costs-how-peering-is-moving-the-needle-on-internet-affordability/>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.
