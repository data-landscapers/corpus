# Mozambique

## Exchange points keeping traffic local

**In service, with a content network, and growing.** [MOZIX, the Mozambique Internet Exchange in Maputo run by CIUEM at Eduardo Mondlane University, says it was created in May 2002 and has grown from 5 to 18 peers that exchange traffic between themselves](<https://mozix.org.mz/>). [Diario Economico quotes the communications minister calling MozIX (Mozambique Internet Exchange Point) the national internet traffic exchange point, which the new UEM data centre will help keep running](<https://www.diarioeconomico.co.mz/2026/08/03/trends/inovacao/uem-inaugura-centro-de-dados-para-reforcar-transformacao-digital-e-investigacao-cientifica/>). [O.Economico reports the university's rector saying UEM runs the national traffic exchange point MOZIX, which lets part of the traffic between national networks be exchanged locally without crossing international infrastructure](<https://www.oeconomico.com/uem-inaugura-centro-de-dados-para-acelerar-economia-do-conhecimento-e-reforcar-soberania-digital/>). [Traffic: over 150 Gbps (peak) at MOZIX on 2025-05-15, with 19 peering members, as stated by CIUEM's director](<https://capacityglobal.com/news/mozambique-internet-exchange-launches-new-pop-at-digital-realtys-maputo-data-centre/>). These place an exchange point in service, which answers the first of the study's four questions. A global content network serving from it is not stated. Member counts and traffic are given where a source states them; only traffic shown growing sets a stage.

**A second city or networks from abroad.** Nothing found names a second city or networks from other countries at [the exchange](<https://mozix.org.mz/>). An exchange reaches beyond one city where a second exchange or point of presence is in service in another city, or where networks from other countries peer there; the anycast DNS operators found at almost every exchange do not count.
