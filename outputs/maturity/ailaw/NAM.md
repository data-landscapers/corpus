# Namibia

## AI law or binding rule

**Binding rule on AI in force.** Namibia has no law on AI. The minister for information and communication technology said at a meeting on 3 August 2026 that [the government has put a national AI strategy before legislation, so no AI legislation has yet been introduced](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/05/namibia-and-deloitte-discuss-ai-strategy-and-digital-infrastructure-development/>); [a second report of the meeting says the same](<https://itweb.africa/article/namibia-taps-deloitte-for-ai/KzQenMjyxWY7Zd2r>). A year earlier, at the launch of the country's AI readiness assessment, [the absence of a formal regulatory framework for AI](<https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/namibia-launches-artificial-intelligence-readiness-assessment-report>) was named as a standing problem. In October 2026 a commentator wrote of [the absence of explicit, standalone national AI laws](<https://technews.com.na/2026/10/23/the-ai-policy-gap-are-parastatals-being-left-behind/>), leaving parastatals bound only by governance and privacy law.

**General AI law and the law applied.** With [no AI legislation introduced by August 2026](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/05/namibia-and-deloitte-discuss-ai-strategy-and-digital-infrastructure-development/>), there is no general AI law to apply and no draft of one is named. The readiness assessment recommended [a national framework for governing AI](<https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/namibia-launches-artificial-intelligence-readiness-assessment-report>), and the strategy the minister described is the step the government has chosen to take first.
