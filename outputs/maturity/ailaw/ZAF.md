# South Africa

## AI law or binding rule

**Binding rule on AI in force.** South Africa has no law on AI. The chief executive of a technology company wrote in August 2026 that [South Africa currently has no law governing artificial intelligence and that national rules are expected in 2027](<https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/sci-tech/2026-08-28-bruce-von-maltitz-sas-ai-rules-wont-land-until-2027-waiting-is-a-choice/>); [the same piece ran a week earlier in a second outlet](<https://it-online.co.za/2026/08/21/sas-ai-rules-wont-land-until-2027-waiting-for-them-is-a-choice/>). A research centre wrote the same month that [the country has no national AI policy or regulations in place](<https://www.globalcenter.ai/research/hypothetical-no-more-south-africa-s-legal-litmus-test-for-automating-welfare>).

**General AI law and the law applied.** With [no law governing AI in August 2026](<https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/sci-tech/2026-08-28-bruce-von-maltitz-sas-ai-rules-wont-land-until-2027-waiting-is-a-choice/>), there is no general AI law to apply. Rules expected in 2027 are an intention; no bill or draft regulation on AI is named in either piece. The [research centre's paper](<https://www.globalcenter.ai/research/hypothetical-no-more-south-africa-s-legal-litmus-test-for-automating-welfare>) says so in a study of automated decisions in social grants, where existing law is the only test.

***Also on file***. [Copyright Amendment Bill](<https://www.itweb.co.za/article/paperback-writer-meet-the-shredder/kYbe97XbZ35qAWpG>).

***Not found***. No bill, draft decree or draft regulation on AI has been found.
