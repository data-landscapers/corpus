# Kenya

## AI law or binding rule

**Binding rule on AI in force.** Kenya has a bill before parliament and no rule in force. The [Artificial Intelligence Bill was introduced in the Senate on 19 February 2026](<https://techcabal.com/2026/03/19/why-data-protection-has-become-africas-default-ai-policy-tool/>). A memorandum sent to the Senate in May 2026 describes [the bill as creating an AI Commissioner's office, an advisory committee and a classification of high-risk systems](<https://www.kictanet.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AI-BILL-2026-Memorandum.docx.pdf>), with transparency duties and standards for the public sector. It was [still a bill in September 2026](<https://cipesa.org/2026/09/cipesa-weighs-in-on-kenyas-draft-guidance-notes-on-ai-and-emerging-technologies/>), and in October 2026 [the Senate ICT Committee recommended that the Senate not proceed with it](<https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-10-15-senate-committee-shoots-down-nyamus-bill-on-ai>).

**General AI law and the law applied.** No law on AI is in force: a university centre wrote in September 2026 that [Kenya has no AI laws and that most regulation touching AI falls under the Data Protection Act](<https://cipit.strathmore.edu/artificial-intelligence-in-east-africas-public-sector-ai-adoption-and-regulatory-preparedness-within-east-africa/>). The Senate bill would apply across sectors. An earlier [Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Society Bill of 2023](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17191>), which proposed licensing, was not passed.

***Not found***. No source found says a law, decree or regulation that sets a rule for AI is in force, and no Senate vote on the bill has been found.
