# AI legislation / regulations: AI law or binding rule

*As at 2026-10-17.*

## The norm

The African Union's Continental AI Strategy, adopted in 2024, asks member states to review and amend existing laws, analyse the gaps and put an enabling framework in place, with independent institutions to oversee it. It sets no date and requires no AI act. This study asks whether a draft exists, a binding rule is in force, a law on AI itself is in force and that law has been applied; it does not measure a law against the strategy.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the country has no law or regulation on AI and no draft of one |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: a bill or draft, and no binding rule in force |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: a binding rule on AI in force, for one sector or one use |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: a law on AI itself in force |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: that law has been applied in the last three years |

## Countries

### Comoros: 3 Established

A media code in force since February 2026 requires AI-generated content to be labelled; there is no law on AI itself.

- Al-watwan reports the UNESCO RAM 2.0 finding that Comoros has legislation with several provisions on artificial intelligence in the media ([source, 2026-10-02](https://alwatwan.net/societe/intelligence-artificielle-i-le-diagnostic-ram-2.0-r%C3%A9v%C3%A8le-les-d%C3%A9fis-des-comores.html)).
- The CNPA states that the new Code de l'information et de la communication, adopted by Loi n°25-021/AU of 26 December 2025 and promulgated by Décret n°26-023/PR of 2 February 2026, requires explicit labelling of any content generated or heavily modified by AI (article 231), with CNPA sanctions up to 1,000,000 KMF ([source, 2026-02-02](https://www.cnpa-comores.org/un-nouveau-souffle-pour-le-cnpa/)).

### Egypt: 3 Established

A 2024 financial regulator decree on robo-advisors is in force; a general AI law is still a draft in 2026.

- The guide says FRA Board Decree No. 57 of 2024, regulating robo-advisors for investment, is AI-specific and requires client suitability assessments and a right to discontinue automated transactions ([source, 2024](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/egypt-artificial-intelligence/?export-pdf=)).

### Gabon: 3 Established

An ordinance of April 2026 governs AI-generated content and bans deepfakes; there is no law on AI itself.

- The article cites an ordinance of April 2026 governing AI-generated content, notably prohibiting deepfakes, as evidence of Gabon's will to regulate; it gives no article numbers or text ([source, 2026-04](https://www.echosdeleco.com/articles/ia-le-gabon-dans-le-top-13-africain-mais-le-fosse-entre-les-engagements-officiels-et-la-realite-reste-beant)).
- Village de la Justice says ordinance n°0011/PR/2026, published in Journal Officiel n°110 of 8-15 April 2026, prohibits sexual deepfakes without consent and deepfakes attributing false statements to public figures ([source, 2026-04](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/gabon-dote-droit-des-reseaux-sociaux-analyse-des-ordonnances-fevrier-2026,57009.html)).

### Mauritius: 3 Established

Regulations in force since March 2026 govern AI in higher education; there is no law on AI itself.

- The Higher Education (Use of Artificial Intelligence) Regulations 2026 (GN No. 39 of 2026), made by the Minister on 3 April 2026 under the Higher Education Act 2017, apply to AI use in the higher education sector and oblige higher education institutions to adopt AI policies and require disclosure of AI tool use, coming into operation on 31 March 2026 ([source, 2026-03-31](https://supremecourt.govmu.org/downloadPDF/legislation/18106230)).

### Morocco: 3 Established

The electoral law revised for the 2026 elections makes AI-made falsehoods a crime; a general AI bill of 2025 has not passed.

- A Hespress interview with academic Adel Ben Hamza (published 2026-07-30) says Morocco's legislator wrote into the revised electoral framework for the 2026 legislative elections provisions that criminalise use of AI and digital means to alter the truth or harm the private lives of individuals and candidates, and excluded electoral offences from alternative penalties; it names no law or article, and the date is the publication date ([source, 2026-07-30](https://www.hespress.com/%d8%a8%d9%86-%d8%ad%d9%85%d8%b2%d8%a9-%d8%aa%d8%ac%d8%b1%d9%8a%d9%85-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%aa%d8%b6%d9%84%d9%8a%d9%84-%d8%a8%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b0%d9%83%d8%a7%d8%a1-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%a7%d8%b5%d8%b7%d9%86%d8%a7-1784231.html)).
- EuroMedAI says Organic Law No. 53.25, promulgated on 16 January 2026, names artificial intelligence tools among the means by which electoral offences can be committed ([source, 2026-01-16](https://euromedai.org/en/ai-morocco-2026-elections-legal-framework/)).

### Nigeria: 3 Established

A 2025 data protection directive sets duties for anyone deploying AI; several AI bills are before parliament and none has passed.

- The Liberalist states that the Nigeria Data Protection Commission issued the General Application and Implementation Directive in 2025, whose Article 43 names artificial intelligence and requires controllers deploying it to document parameters, assess disparate outcomes and test the system, and whose Article 44 tells them to cease using AI systems that pose undue risks to human rights; it calls these current regulatory text ([source, 2025](https://theliberalist.org/nigeria-needs-no-ai-law-it-already-has-one/)).

### Tanzania: 3 Established

Regulations of January 2025 prohibit fabricated AI-generated content online; there is no law on AI itself.
