# Justice: justice

*As at 2026-11-12.*

## The norm

The African Commission's Fair Trial Principles ask for systems recording proceedings and storing court information, with public access to decisions. This study asks the four questions above; it does not measure a country against the norm.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |

| 2 Under way | Yes to 1 |
| 3 In service | Yes to 2 |

### Algeria: 5 Leading

Algerian lawyers filed civil briefs online from 2026, and administrative court parties could track cases and read judgments online.

- The Minister of Justice said on 22 September 2026 that the national electronic window lets litigants and their lawyers follow the progress of their cases from any administrative court or administrative court of appeal in the country ([source, 2026-09-22](https://www.lejourdalgerie.com/extension-des-services-du-guichet-electronique-national-aux-cours-administratives-dappel-et-au-conseil-detat/)).
- Fildalgerie reports that the Ministry of Justice is making electronic exchange of civil requests and reply briefs exclusive for lawyers at the Cours de justice from 18 January 2026, after wide uptake by lawyers ([source, 2025-12-18](https://fildalgerie.dz/justice-numerique-lechange-electronique-des-documents-et-le-paiement-en-ligne-obligatoires-des-janvier-2026/)).
- Algeria's Minister of Justice announced on 22 September 2026 that the national electronic window for administrative jurisdictions now covers the administrative courts of appeal and the Council of State, letting litigants and their lawyers consult and track their cases and read the operative parts of judgments and decisions online from any administrative jurisdiction ([source, 2026-09-22](https://www.lejourdalgerie.com/extension-des-services-du-guichet-electronique-national-aux-cours-administratives-dappel-et-au-conseil-detat/)).

### Kenya: 5 Leading

- UNDP, with Dutch support, handed Mali's Ministry of Justice laptops, tablets and modems worth USD 493,193 on 5 November 2026 to equip the operationalisation of its e-justice platform ([source, 2026-11-05](https://www.maliweb.net/economie/developpement/modernisation-de-la-justice-renforcement-de-letat-de-droit-le-pnud-3121305.html)).
- Justice Minister Mamoudou Kassogue said on 5 November 2026 that the ministry is operationalising a national e-justice platform whose first services are digital criminal-record extracts and nationality certificates, with later features to cover case processing ([source, 2026-11-05](https://www.maliweb.net/economie/developpement/modernisation-de-la-justice-renforcement-de-letat-de-droit-le-pnud-3121305.html)).

### Mauritania: 2 Nascent

Ministries were digitising criminal and commercial case handling in 2025; no court is known to run cases on it yet.

- AMI reports that the justice ministry, the digital transformation ministry and ANRPTS are jointly working on digitising the criminal justice chain and the commercial justice chain from case registration to judgment, appeal and cassation ([source, 2025-02-04](https://www.ami.mr/archives/219925)).

### Namibia: 2 Nascent

The Supreme Court was still on paper in 2025, with e-justice being extended; none in service there is on record.

- Chief Justice Shivute said the Supreme Court remains entirely paper-based and efforts to extend e-justice to it are under way, with a task force defining an integrated electronic case management system ([source, 2025-07-16](https://www.namibian.com.na/namibia-to-implement-e-justice-system-in-supreme-court/)).
- The Uganda Judiciary reports that Namibia is preparing to roll out its own Integrated Electronic Case Management System (IECMS), and a 15-member Namibian delegation benchmarked Uganda's ECCMIS in May 2025 ([source, 2025-05-16](https://judiciary.go.ug/data/news/1683/Namibian%20Judiciary%20Team%20Concludes%20ECCMIS%20Benchmarking%20Visit%20to%20Uganda.html)).

### Zambia: 2 Nascent

A case system for all court levels was being built in 2025; none in service is on record.

- Synergy International Systems says the Judiciary of Zambia is developing, with Treasury funding, an Electronic Case Management System to connect the Supreme, Constitutional, Appeal, High and Subordinate Courts, and that it currently relies on manual processes ([source, 2025-01-20](https://www.synisys.com/news/zambia-electronic-case-management-system/)).
