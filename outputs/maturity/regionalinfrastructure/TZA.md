# Tanzania

## Digital links and facilities shared with other African countries

**Shared infrastructure agreed and under construction.** Tanzania's and Kenya's ministers [launched a cross-border project at the Horohoro and Lunga Lunga border on 20 July 2025](<https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/kenya-and-tanzania-launch-terrestrial-fiber-link-connection/>). It joins Tanzania's backbone with Kenya's backbone and sea cables. TTCL and Kenya's ICT Authority carry it out. TTCL and Congo's SOCOF [made the Kalemie to Kigoma lake cable official on 20 October 2025](<https://afriqueitnews.com/tech-media/rdc-tanzanie-interconnexion-fibre-optique-pourrait-transformer-region/>), with works due in early 2026. Tanzania [reaffirmed that cable in September 2026](<https://dailynews.co.tz/tanzania-drc-agree-to-fast-track-sgr-link-and-fibre-optic-cable-across-lake-tanganyika/>). TTCL and Uganda's NITA-U [signed a memorandum on 29 September 2023](<https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/questions-swirl-over-internet-deal-between-uganda-tanzania-5654914>) to join Uganda's backbone to Tanzania's fibre loop.

**Shared infrastructure in service, by kind.** TTCL and Kenya's ICT Authority [launched a fibre link between Dar es Salaam and Mombasa in July 2025](<https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/east-african-countries-to-launch-regional-satellite>). The two governments [officially opened the route at Horohoro](<https://dailynews.co.tz/new-fibre-link-to-boost-east-africas-digital-resilience/>). TTCL says it runs at 1.6 terabits a second, and several Kenyan companies have started using it. No exchange point or data facility run with another country is on record.
