# Guinea

## Digital links and facilities shared with other African countries

**Shared link or facility agreed and under construction.** Guinea and Sierra Leone [signed an agreement in November 2024](<https://natca.gov.sl/2025/11/17/the-republic-of-sierra-leone-signs-free-roaming-agreement-with-republic-of-guinea-to-ease-cross-border-connectivity/>) to join their national internet backbone networks, as Sierra Leone's regulator records. Guinea's backbone company and its Sierra Leonean counterpart then [signed an interconnection agreement in Conakry](<https://www.telecomreviewafrica.com/articles/general-news/12188-guinea-and-sierra-leone-link-national-fibre-networks-for-regional-digital-integration/>), reported on 23 June 2025, to link the two fibre networks at Pamelap on the border. In June 2024 Guinea's backbone company and Mali's SMTD [agreed, with ministry facilitation, to interconnect the two countries' fibre networks](<https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/optical-fixed-networks/17258-guinea-adds-cote-d-ivoire-to-regional-fibre-interconnectivity-plan.html>).

**In service and carrying another country's traffic.** The [interconnection agreement](<https://www.telecomreviewafrica.com/articles/general-news/12188-guinea-and-sierra-leone-link-national-fibre-networks-for-regional-digital-integration/>) sets a first capacity of 42.5 gigabits a second at Pamelap and plans 100 gigabits a second by 2026. Those are the terms of the agreement between the two backbone companies. Sierra Leone's regulator, [writing in November 2025](<https://natca.gov.sl/2025/11/17/the-republic-of-sierra-leone-signs-free-roaming-agreement-with-republic-of-guinea-to-ease-cross-border-connectivity/>), described the agreement of the year before and did not describe a link carrying traffic.
