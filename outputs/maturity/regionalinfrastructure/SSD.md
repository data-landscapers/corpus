# South Sudan

## Digital links and facilities shared with other African countries

**Shared link or facility agreed and under construction.** On 22 October 2026 Bayobab, an MTN company, said it had [laid fibre on the Kenya and South Sudan segments of its East to West route](<https://tech.africa/bayobab-east-west-fibre-south-sudan/>). Civil works were finished on that 2,038 km northern route. South Sudan [invited bids for about 397 km of fibre from Juba to Nadapal](<https://southsudanpress.com/analysis-south-sudan-fibre-juba-nadapal-procurement.html>), towards the Kenyan border, in September 2026. A World Bank grant pays for it. The bank's June 2026 report [counts a first cross-border connectivity memorandum between Kenya and South Sudan as signed](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063026190082191/txt/P176181-84d9770b-853c-4bb8-8e5a-996eac681382.txt>). In October 2025 South Sudan [licensed Bayobab for fifteen years](<https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/27/why-connecting-landlocked-africa-requires-more-than-building-national-fibre-networks/>) to build national and cross-border fibre. [A World Bank mission in August 2025](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099120225134529047/txt/P176181-10a2aeb5-d3fb-4c67-bc53-da109b62a9de.txt>) set out fibre tenders from Juba to the Kenyan and Sudanese borders under the US$67 million regional project.

**In service and carrying another country's traffic.** The [Bayobab route through Kenya and South Sudan](<https://tech.africa/bayobab-east-west-fibre-south-sudan/>) was built but not yet lit in October 2026. The [Juba to Nadapal route](<https://southsudanpress.com/analysis-south-sudan-fibre-juba-nadapal-procurement.html>) was still in procurement, with bids closing on 26 October 2026.
