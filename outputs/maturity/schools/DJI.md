# Djibouti

## EMIS

**Governance, planning, finance.** The education ministry [developed the pupil management system it calls GOSE](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020824112541874/pdf/P16605912c1be00df18eb6117cf4d0e79cd.pdf>) and, as at 28 November 2023, planned its extension. The minister's [circular of 1 September 2025](<https://www.lanation.dj/une-rentree-scolaire-2025-2026-placee-sous-le-signe-de-la-performance-et-de-la-reforme/>) makes consolidating it a priority for the 2025-2026 school year. The World Bank's September 2019 appraisal [costed the data systems work at US$2.55 million](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/605651570154537746/pdf/Djibouti-Expanding-Opportunities-for-Learning-Project.pdf>). Of this, US$0.8 million came from an IDA grant and US$1.75 million from a Global Partnership for Education grant.

**Levels in use.** As at 28 November 2023 [each pupil had a unique identifier in GOSE from pre-school to senior school](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020824112541874/pdf/P16605912c1be00df18eb6117cf4d0e79cd.pdf>). The World Bank reported this.

**How the record is kept.** At that date [the school principal registered and transferred pupils in the application](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020824112541874/pdf/P16605912c1be00df18eb6117cf4d0e79cd.pdf>), and pupil and teacher absences were transmitted through it to central management. Pupils' grades and history were additions the ministry planned.

**Last twelve months.** The education minister gave an interview on 20 November 2025. The minister said [the ministry's platforms give it reliable data to detect pupils in difficulty before they drop out](<https://www.lanation.dj/interview-exclusive-avec-sem-moustapha-mohamed-mahamoud-ministre-de-leducation-nationale-et-de-la-formation-professionnelle/>). More than 3,000 pupils had been supported through the system, the minister said.

**Primary schools doing the digital input.** [All public schools transmitted their data through GOSE](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020824112541874/pdf/P16605912c1be00df18eb6117cf4d0e79cd.pdf>) as at 28 November 2023, the World Bank reported. The rollout was in phases, with refugee and private schools to follow. The project measured primary schools transmitting on time, with a target of 70 per cent of public and 50 per cent of private schools. That target was still unmet at that date.

***Also on file***. [OPAD and ED-ASSIST](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/605651570154537746/pdf/Djibouti-Expanding-Opportunities-for-Learning-Project.pdf>) were the tools behind the yearly school statistics in 2019. The [quality assurance system (SAQ)](<https://www.lanation.dj/interview-exclusive-avec-sem-moustapha-mohamed-mahamoud-ministre-de-leducation-nationale-et-de-la-formation-professionnelle/>) runs in more than 60 pilot schools. There is a [national human resources platform](<https://www.lanation.dj/une-rentree-scolaire-2025-2026-placee-sous-le-signe-de-la-performance-et-de-la-reforme/>). Inspectors and pedagogical advisors use [classroom observation tools](<https://www.globalpartnership.org/blog/djibouti-transforming-education-build-its-human-capital>). Schools are mapped on [Giga Maps](<https://giga.global/impact/ai-as-a-tool-for-connectivity/>). Also listed: [Moodle for distance learning and teacher training, and 8,600 tablets distributed to schools](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020824112541874/pdf/P16605912c1be00df18eb6117cf4d0e79cd.pdf>). The 2019 appraisal lists [One Child, One Tablet, smart classrooms, ADSL and Wimax school connections, and planned portals for staff records and teacher training resources](<https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/605651570154537746/pdf/Djibouti-Expanding-Opportunities-for-Learning-Project.pdf>).

***Not found***. No share of primary schools entering absences, nor a count with its total, and no figure for rural schools. Nothing dated after 2023 says how many schools use the system. The 2025-2026 priority rests on a circular, not a costed plan.
