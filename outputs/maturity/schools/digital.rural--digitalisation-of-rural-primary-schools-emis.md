# Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The African Union's Digital Education Strategy (2022), fourth objective: a move from *EMIS 1.0*, "aggregate statistical data collection for generating annual reports", to *EMIS 2.0*, individual-level data on unique identifiers, in at least half of member states by 2027. Its device and connection targets (20 per cent of students and half of teachers by 2027; half of institutions connected) count `enabling`.

**It draws this study's line, and keeps one name for both sides of it.** Its *EMIS 1.0* is the school census, in its own words. Its *EMIS 2.0* is met by a learner register, this ladder's stage 3: it asks for a record per learner, not for a school that uses it during the year. The ladder borrows nothing from it; the 90 per cent line is the health study's. *(Read 2026-10-09: pages 10, 55 to 58.)*

## The ladder

| Stage | Levels | How the record is kept | Primary schools |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated statement that primary schools keep learner records on paper only | | |
| 2 Preparing | An EMIS for primary schools is being prepared or piloted; or one runs in secondary schools only | Any, or not stated | Pilot schools, or none |
| 3 Establishing | P: past its pilot and live in some primary schools, for routine entry or as a learner register | `routine`, `enrolment` or `keyed-elsewhere` | A minority, a count with no total, or not published |
| 4 Operating | P | `routine` | More than half enter attendance or results during the year, on a share or a count with a denominator |
| 5 Leading | P | `routine`, with attendance entered daily | 90 per cent or more, on a figure published within two years that gives the rural share |

### Cape Verde: 3 Established

Teachers must record pupils' absences and marks in a system put in all 395 basic schools by 2019; the share entering them is not known.

- Despacho 120/GME/2025 orders at 2.11 that teachers must record the information from pupils' assessment electronically as well, on the result-entry sheets of the SIGE platform, where each teacher gets the final marks calculated by the legal formulas; it states an obligation for basic and secondary education in 2025/2026, not a measure of practice ([source, 2025-09-01](https://boe.incv.cv/Bulletins/DownloadAct?id=86791)).
- The Ministry of Education states that implementation of SIGE was concluded in February 2019 in all basic education schools of the country, 395 in total (395 of 395); the figure is of schools where it was implemented, not of schools entering data ([source, 2019-02](https://minedu.gov.cv/recursos_educativossigemenu)).
- The government states that SIGE is 'already used in all' basic and secondary schools (25 June 2025); it gives no count of schools and no total, and says used, not how many enter data ([source, 2025-06-25](https://www.governo.cv/damos-mais-um-passo-significativo-no-caminho-da-modernizacao-e-inclusao-digital-no-setor-da-educacao-dne/)).
- The eSkola app, fully integrated with SIGE and updated from it in real time, shows parents each pupil's timetable, test calendar, marks, absences and school history, so SIGE holds marks and absences per pupil during the year (25 June 2025) ([source, 2025-06-25](https://www.governo.cv/damos-mais-um-passo-significativo-no-caminho-da-modernizacao-e-inclusao-digital-no-setor-da-educacao-dne/)).
- The ministry's guidance for the 2023/2024 school year (July 2023) requires teachers to write the lesson summary and record absences daily in SIGE's digital class register during the lesson, to enter each assessment within 15 days, and to have all marks in SIGE at least 24 hours before the marks meetings; class directors complete each pupil's data and hold assessment meetings in SIGE ([source, 2023-07](https://minedu.gov.cv/media/orientacao/2024/09/04/CADERNO_DE_ORIENTA%C3%87%C3%95ES_GERAIS_ANO_LETIVO_2023-2024_-_vers%C3%A3o_final_2_1.pdf)).

### Morocco: 3 Established

Every school has registered its pupils in a national per-pupil record since 2014; how many primary schools enter attendance or marks is not known.

*Advancing.*

- The diagnostic states that MASSAR's student register covers every child enrolled in any of the more than 10,000 national educational establishments, ages 6 to 18: 6,512,192 unique students as of May 2014, about 94 per cent of the 6.9 million children of that age, and by the World Bank's reckoning 98 per cent of children of primary age (6 to 10) ([source, 2014-05](https://id4d.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2018-04/Morocco_ID4D_Diagnostic_Web404018.pdf)).

- The 2019 atlas describes MASSAR, adopted in 2013, as in service and its pupil cohorts as exhaustive, but gives no count or share of primary schools entering data; 'all schools' is stated as what the platform was designed to cover ([source, 2019](https://www.csefrs.ma/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/ATLAS-TERRITORIAL-DE-LABANDON-SCOLAIRE-18-12-web.pdf)).
