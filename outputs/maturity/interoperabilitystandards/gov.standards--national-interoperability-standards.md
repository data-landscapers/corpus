# National interoperability standards: technical standards for government systems

*As at 2026-09-30.*

## The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy asks for technical standards for checking identity in dealings between government, business and citizens, built on open standards; its interoperability framework names international ones. This study asks whether standards exist, are published, are compulsory and are checked; it does not measure them against either.

## The ladder

| Stage | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1 Absent | A dated source says the government has no technical standards for its systems to exchange data and none are being written |
| 2 Preparing | Yes to 1: standards being written |
| 3 Establishing | Yes to 1 and 2: standards published |
| 4 Operating | Yes to 1, 2 and 3: public bodies are required to use them |
| 5 Leading | Yes to all four: a system has been checked against them in the last three years |

## Countries

### Burkina Faso: 4 Operating

Burkina Faso adopted an interoperability referential in 2018, and a 2024 decree requires public platforms to respect it; no checked system is known.

- The decree on the public administration's digital platforms, held in a capture dated 2 August 2024, makes a favourable opinion a condition of putting a platform into service, and the file for it includes a check that the platform respects the interoperability referential, which the decree says is adopted by order of the Prime Minister; the capture date is used, the passages giving no signing date ([source, 2024-08-02](https://lefaso.net/IMG/pdf/decret_portant_modalite_de_mise_en_exploitation_des_plateformes_numeriques_de_l_administration_publique.pdf)).
- The Referentiel general d'interoperabilite (RGI), adopted by Prime Minister's arrete of 23 May 2018, is described as a normative collection of protocols (communication rules, voice and file exchange protocols) ensuring compatibility between public administrations' information systems; a training seminar on it was held 15-17 October 2019 ([source, 2018-05-23](https://burkina24.com/2019/10/15/burkina-un-referentiel-pour-moderniser-ladministration-publique/)).

### Cote d'Ivoire: 4 Operating

Côte d'Ivoire's 2021 decree adopts a list of technical standards and binds public bodies to them; no system checked against them is known.

- Article 3 of Decree 2021-913 states that public bodies are bound to conform to the RGI, making its technical, syntactic and semantic interoperability standards compulsory for their information systems ([source, 2021-12-22](https://anssi.gouv.ci/documents/28/décret_2021-913_du_22_décembre_2021_adoption_référentiel_général_dinteropérabilité_systèmes_dinformation.pdf)).
- Decree 2021-913 of 22 December 2021 adopts the Referentiel General d'Interoperabilite (RGI), annexed to it, a list of reference standards for e-administration systems covering technical protocols (IP, TCP, HTTP, SMTP, SOAP/REST web services), data formats and encodings (XML, JSON, XSD, PDF/A, UTF-8, geospatial formats), pivot data formats, and semantic key terms ([source, 2021-12-22](https://anssi.gouv.ci/documents/28/décret_2021-913_du_22_décembre_2021_adoption_référentiel_général_dinteropérabilité_systèmes_dinformation.pdf)).

### Sao Tome and Principe: 4 Operating

A 2024 law publishes interoperability standards for government systems and makes the mandatory ones compulsory; no system is known to have been checked.

- Lei 1/2024 grades the QNI's technical specifications as mandatory or recommended, and requires the mandatory ones to be adopted in all new IT products and projects of the public institutions it covers ([source, 2024-01-29](https://inic.gov.st/viewdoc.php?id=26)).
- Lei 1/2024, gazetted 29 January 2024, institutes the National Interoperability Framework (QNI) with technical specifications regulating ICT use for interoperability of e-government services, applicable to all public institutions ([source, 2024-01-29](https://inic.gov.st/viewdoc.php?id=26)).

### Benin: 3 Established

Benin published technical standards in its interoperability framework of 2020; whether a decree makes them compulsory for public bodies is not known.

- ASIN's July 2026 legal note treats Benin's interoperability framework as in place, setting registration, technical and contractual conditions for connecting to the Xroad-BJ exchange platform, with the Comité national d'interopérabilité supervising; the note's date is the published date, 2026-07-28, no event date being given ([source, 2026-07-28](https://asin.bj/doc/142/download)).
- Benin's Cadre d'interopérabilité, a 32-page framework published by ASIN and instituted by décret n° 2020-209 of 18 March 2020, sets recommendations and referenced technical standards for interoperable government information systems (W3C SOAP/WSDL/XML/XSL/SVG/WCAG 2.0/CSS2/PNG, ISO 10918 JPEG, ISO/IEC 26300 ODF, MPEG4, RFC 1952, RFC 2306) and a catalogue of interoperability solutions; the PDF carries no publication date, so the decree date is a floor ([source, 2020-03-18](https://api.xroad.bj/documents/10/download)).

### Guinea-Bissau: 3 Established

Guinea-Bissau's 2025 decree on interoperability sets out to establish technical standards; whether public bodies must use them is not known.

- WARDIP reports that Decreto n.º 27/2025, the National Regulation on Interoperability of Digital Systems and Platforms of public and private services, was published in the Boletim Oficial on 31 October 2025, and lists among its objectives to establish technical standards for developing interoperable digital solutions and to harmonise communication between systems; the page gives no list or content of the standards ([source, 2025-10-31](https://wardip.gw/governo-aprova-regulamento-nacional-de-interoperabilidade-para-acelerar-a-transformacao-digital-na-guine-bissau/)).

### Mauritius: 3 Established

Mauritius published technical standards in its e-government interoperability framework in 2002; whether public bodies must use them is not known.

- Version 1.0 of the Mauritius e-Government Interoperability Framework, dated November 2002, sets technical standards and specifications for interoperability across government information systems and for data exchange between public agencies ([source, 2002-11](https://cib.govmu.org/Documents/Maintenance%20Agreement/egiframework.pdf)).

### Mozambique: 3 Established

Mozambique's interoperability regulation sets standards for public information systems and was under revision in 2026; whether public bodies must use them is not known.

- INTIC chairman Lourino Chemane said Mozambique has completed a thorough review of the Electronic Government Systems Interoperability Regulation, which sets standards for integrating and managing public information systems; the report is dated 2025-12-05 and gives no other date ([source, 2025-12-05](https://techafricanews.com/2025/12/05/mozambique-prioritizes-data-governance-as-cornerstone-of-digital-sovereignty/)).

### South Africa: 3 Established

The public service department had published minimum interoperability standards by 2025 and was updating them in 2026; whether their use is compulsory is not known.

- The DPSA reply says it published the Minimum Interoperability Standards (MIOS) so departments can achieve system interoperability and data exchange ([source, 2025-08-28](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/32258/)).
- The Operation Vulindlela Q1 2026/27 progress report says work progressed on updating the Minimum Interoperability Standards, described as the common standards needed to connect public-sector systems (the report does not list data formats or interfaces), with testing in priority digital service initiatives due September 2026; period is the April-June 2026 quarter, report published 2026-07-31 ([source, 2026-06](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/Operation%20Vulindlela%20Progress%20Report%20Q1%20-%202026.pdf)).

### Tanzania: 3 Established

Tanzania's e-Government Agency published technical standards for government systems in 2016; whether public bodies must use them is not known.

- The e-Government Agency issued version 1.0 of the e-Government Interoperability Framework - Standards and Technical Guidelines (eGA/EXT/GIF/001) in February 2016, setting nine technical standards areas for government systems to exchange data: interconnection, data integration (character encoding, data exchange formats, data modelling language), access, collaboration, application design and development, application integration, system standards, metadata and security ([source, 2016-02](https://www.ega.go.tz/uploads/standards/sw-1574945623-inter.pdf)).

### Uganda: 3 Established

Uganda's 2026 interoperability framework publishes data-sharing standards; no law or decree requiring public bodies to use them is known.

- NITA-U's e-Government Interoperability Framework e-GIF-2026, dated 15 April 2026, sets standards for government systems sharing data, among them DCAT for metadata and machine-readable ODRL usage policies, for all ministries, agencies and local governments; it describes itself as mandatory ([source, 2026-04-15](https://www.nita.go.ug/sites/default/files/2026-07/Uganda%20e-Government%20Interoperability%20Framework%20%28e-GIF%29-2026.pdf)).

### Zambia: 3 Established

Zambia issued an interoperability standard for the public service in 2019; whether public bodies must use it is not known.

- The e-Government Division (Smart Zambia Institute) issued the Public Service ICT Electronic Government Interoperability Standard, 1st Edition, version 1.0 (F), in February 2019, to enable exchange of data between government information systems of different public bodies; the captured text sets principles and names data and technical interoperability guidelines in scope, and shows no formats or protocols (excerpt ends at section 5.10) ([source, 2019-02](https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/ZMB588.pdf)).

### Angola: 2 Nascent

Angola produced a draft interoperability framework with technical guidelines in 2026 and has no common standards; whether the draft has been adopted is not known.

- IMA's terms of reference say a seven-month study with 25 public institutions produced the Framework and Roadmap of Interoperability, with organisational, semantic, technical and infrastructure guidelines and a phased roadmap; the document gives no adoption instrument. Dated by the document's publication date, 2026-02-09 ([source, 2026-02-09](https://sys.portais.gov.ao/uploads/Td_R_Consultoria_para_Implementacao_da_Interoperabilidade_da_Administracao_Publica_1_13f0252ef0.pdf)).

### Botswana: 2 Nascent

Botswana's interoperability framework and standards were being aligned under a European Union project in 2026; whether any are published is not known.

- Expertise France's project page, published 2026-02-17, says its EU-funded project (running August 2024 to August 2027) works with ESTDEV to improve and align Botswana's National Interoperability framework, standards, guidelines and tools, and lists a 'harmonised and operational national interoperability framework for e-government services' as an expected result; the page does not say the framework has been adopted or issued, and its date is the page's published date ([source, 2026-02-17](https://www.expertisefrance.fr/en/projects/digital-transformation-botswana)).

### DR Congo: 2 Nascent

The Democratic Republic of the Congo adopted draft digital standards for the public sector in 2022; an order giving them legal force is not known.

- The government adopted the draft digital norms and standards for the public sector on 15 April 2022, in five books including a common data-reference architecture framework and the general interoperability framework, with legal force still to be given by ministerial order ([source, 2022-04-15](https://kt.cd/2022/04/16/adoption-du-projet-des-normes-et-standards-du-numerique-applicable-au-secteur-public/)).
- A ministry workshop on 2-3 February 2022 validated five books of public-sector digital standards, including a data-reference architecture framework and a general interoperability framework meant to give real-time interoperability of public information systems, still to be tidied and promulgated ([source, 2022-02-03](https://medd.gouv.cd/atelier-sur-lelaboration-et-adoption-des-referentiels-numeriques-du-secteur-public-en-rdc/)).

### Ghana: 2 Nascent

Ghana's technology agency issued a draft second version of its interoperability framework, with technical standards, in 2022; a final version is not known.

- NITA's eGovernment Interoperability Framework version 2.0, labelled a draft for stakeholder review, sets eGIF standards for government systems across technical annexes on data, network, service-oriented architecture, channels, business process, security and applications interoperability; the captured text carries no printed date, so 2022 is the published-date proxy ([source, 2022](https://nita.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Ghana-eGovernment-Interoperability-Framework-eGIF-Version-2.0_v.Draft-for-Stakeholder-Review-1.pdf)).

### Kenya: 2 Nascent

Kenya's ICT Authority said in 2025 it would finalise its interoperability framework and standards; whether the 2023 edition was approved is not known.

- The ICT Authority, under KDEAP, held a two-day workshop with ministries, departments and agencies on 6-7 November 2025 on Government Enterprise Architecture and the national e-Government Interoperability Framework, and says it will develop and finalize the national GEA standards and interoperability framework, so none is yet final ([source, 2025-11-07](https://kdeap.icta.go.ke/kenya-advances-digital-government-gea-interoperability-workshop-held-at-kicc/)).
- The ICT Authority's 2nd Edition Government Enterprise Architecture and Government Interoperability Framework sets out a framework for standardised data exchange between government agencies (interoperability charter, data, technical and metadata standards), but its approval date and document history are blank and its scope section says it is to be adopted by the entire government, ministries, counties and agencies; the document gives no date for the events and is dated by its 2023 publication year ([source, 2023](https://cms.icta.go.ke/sites/default/files/2024-07/GEA%20-%20GIF%202nd%20Edition.pdf)).

### Mauritania: 2 Nascent

Mauritania's ministry held a workshop in 2026 to develop a framework of standards for public digital services; whether it has been adopted is not known.

- The digital transformation ministry (MTNIMA) held a three-day workshop to develop a unified framework of public digital-service standards to ensure greater interoperability, and presented a technical study and implementation mechanism for stakeholder feedback before full adoption; the document gives no workshop date, so the published date is used ([source, 2026-03-27](https://techafricanews.com/2026/03/27/mauritania-moves-to-standardize-public-digital-services-with-national-framework-initiative/)).

### Senegal: 2 Nascent

Senegal's ministry presented a draft interoperability framework with technical standards in May 2026; whether it has been adopted is not known.

- The ministry announced that on 5 May 2026 it would present the draft decree governing the interoperability platform and introduce the national interoperability framework, data-exchange architectures, technical standards and security requirements; both were still at presentation stage, not adopted ([source, 2026-05-04](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/mctnsn_dpi-newdealtechnologique-activity-7457180609757528065-UqRT)).

### Libya: 1 Absent

A 2026 paper says Libya's government institutions lack unified interoperability standards; whether any are being written is not known.

- The paper's abstract says Libyan government institutions lack unified interoperability standards across their fragmented information systems; no date is given for the statement, so the paper's published date of 2026-06-30 is used ([source, 2026-06-30](https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/en/article/view/2167)).
