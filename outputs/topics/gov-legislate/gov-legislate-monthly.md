## Algeria

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Draft 2027 finance law, e-payment and digital-platform provisions</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Projet de loi de finances 2027: Des avantages fiscaux pour booster le E-paiement](https://lechodalgerie.dz/projet-de-loi-de-finances-2027-des-avantages-fiscaux-pour-booster-le-e-paiement/)** — L'Echo d'Algérie, 11 October 2026

Algeria's draft 2027 finance law proposes a 10% tax reduction until the end of 2028 for flat-rate and professional taxpayers who take at least half their receipts electronically. It would also require resident and non-resident e-commerce platforms to register with the tax authority and declare transactions yearly, on pain of a DA 1 million fine. The bill is not yet adopted.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[L’Algérie prévoit de soumettre les plateformes numériques étrangères à de nouvelles obligations de TVA en 2027](https://algerian-business.com/tva-plateformes-numeriques-algerie-plf-2027/)** — Algerian Business, 12 October 2026

Algeria's draft 2027 finance law proposes that foreign digital platforms selling services to customers in Algeria register with the tax authority, keep records for ten years and declare and pay VAT quarterly. Non-compliance would carry a fine of 5 million dinars after 30 days' notice and possible suspension of access to the platform. The provision is a draft, not yet adopted.

</div>

</div>

<p class="monthly-story__name">General authorisation regime for cloud services</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Communiqué — retrait définitif de l’autorisation de cloud computing de DATAGIX](https://www.arpce.dz/fr/pub/h9v7b0)** — Autorité de régulation de la poste et des communications électroniques (ARPCE), 22 October 2026

Algeria's telecoms regulator ARPCE definitively withdrew, from 22 October 2026, the general authorisation it issued to DATAGIX in February 2024 to provide cloud hosting and data storage. The regulator cited breaches of the rules in force and told the company to contact it without delay.

## Congo

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

The Republic of the Congo's money transfer regulator, ARTF, said on 5 October 2026 it will enforce article 13 of the 2026 finance law, which requires every money transfer operator to register on its digital platform and declare all transfers. Congo's money-transfer regulator ARTF will require every money-transfer operator to use electronic payment terminals from January 2027, as the finance law provides, Les Dépêches de Brazzaville reported on 8 October 2026. The terminals record each transaction in real time for tax purposes; 200 of 1,000 ordered were shown to about a hundred transfer and exchange operators in Brazzaville.

Uganda's communications regulator said it is reviewing its enforcement against unauthorised Wi-Fi and hotspot resellers after complaints from operators. A public notice was promised within the week.

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Press Release on Illegal Wi-Fi Hotspots](https://www.ucc.co.ug/press-release-on-illegal-wi-fi-hotspots/)** — Uganda Communications Commission, 13 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission said on 13 October 2026 that about 4,000 unlicensed operators resell public Wi-Fi at UGX 1,000 per GB against UGX 2,000 from licensed providers, costing about UGX 148 billion a year in tax. The Uganda Revenue Authority will block equipment imports by unlicensed operators, and a nationwide exercise to take down installations and seize routers is imminent.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[UCC Tightens Licensing Rules for Wi-Fi Hotspot Operators](https://nilepost.co.ug/news/380128/ucc-tightens-licensing-rules-for-wi-fi-hotspot-operators)** — Nile Post, 20 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission said on 20 October 2026 that commercial Wi-Fi hotspot resellers must be licensed and must stop reselling home internet packages. Its seven-day deadline for unlicensed operators ended on 19 October 2026. It will open a registration system by service category and set up a joint committee with government, security agencies and seven hotspot operators.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[UCC Moves to Tighten Monitoring of Internet Data Resellers](https://nilepost.co.ug/business/380753/ucc-moves-to-tighten-monitoring-of-internet-data-resellers)** — Nile Post, 22 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission agreed with mobile operators and licensed internet providers in ISPAU to tighten tracking of data consumption and billing, aimed at unlicensed resellers of bulk connectivity over public Wi-Fi. Eligible resellers are to be brought into licensing through onboarding guidelines, and illegal access points and routers removed; no date or design for the monitoring was given.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[KACITA, UCC Agree to Form Committee on Unlicensed Internet Resellers](https://www.nilepost.co.ug/news/381731/kacita-ucc-agree-to-form-committee-on-unlicensed-internet-resellers)** — Nile Post, 26 October 2026

Kampala traders and the Uganda Communications Commission agreed to form a joint committee, led by the commission, to recommend how unlicensed internet resellers are regulated. Resellers will first register for an inventory. The commission is pursuing about 4,000 unlicensed Wi-Fi resellers and puts potential tax losses at Shs148 billion a year.

</div>

Uganda's ICT minister told Parliament the government needs two weeks to settle a licensing dispute over local Wi-Fi hotspot operators, it was reported on 28 October 2026.
