## Burkina Faso

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Digital ministry annual work plan 2026</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Burkina Faso confirms in 2026 its resolve to make digital technology a pillar of sovereignty](https://africalibertynews.info/burkina-faso-confirms-in-2026-its-resolve-to-make-digital-technology-a-pillar-of-sovereignty/)** — Africa Liberty News, 17 September 2026

Burkina Faso's digital transition ministry approved a 2026 work plan of CFA 61 billion, about double the 2025 budget of CFA 30.4 billion, reported on 17 September 2026. It aims to connect 750 of some 1,700 localities without mobile coverage, co-financed by the state and operators Onatel, Orange and Telecel, and to digitise 100 administrative procedures. The board session's date is not given.

## Gabon

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Digital economy ministry 2027 budget request</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Gabon/Loi de finances 2027 : les budgets de la Communication et de l’Économie numérique examinés à l’Assemblée nationale](https://agpgabon.ga/gabon-loi-de-finances-2027-les-budgets-de-la-communication-et-de-leconomie-numerique-examines-a-lassemblee-nationale/)** — Agence Gabonaise de Presse, 25 October 2026

Gabon's National Assembly examined the 2027 budget requests of the communication and digital economy ministries on 25 October. The digital economy ministry asks for FCFA 37.2 billion in 2027, down about FCFA 16.5 billion from FCFA 53.7 billion in 2026, for connectivity, digital sovereignty and modernising the administration, while the communication ministry's request rises to FCFA 21.5 billion from FCFA 17.5 billion.

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[بيان مصرف ليبيا المركزي عن الإيراد والإنفاق من 01/01/2026 حتى 31/08/2026](https://cbl.gov.ly/micifaf/2026/05/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%86%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%82-%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1-%D8%A3%D8%BA%D8%B3%D8%B7%D8%B3-2026-4.pdf)** — مصرف ليبيا المركزي (Central Bank of Libya), 10 September 2026

The Central Bank of Libya's statement of revenue and expenditure for January to August 2026, released on 10 September, records spending of about 68.7 billion dinars and oil revenue of 80.8 billion. Telecommunications revenue was 31.1 million dinars, and the General Authority for Communications and its affiliates spent 25.2 million dinars, almost all on salaries and running costs.

The Central Bank of Libya's statement of state revenue and spending for January to September 2026, issued on 8 October, puts revenue at LYD 114.5bn, mostly from oil, and spending at LYD 88.6bn, of which LYD 1.6bn was development spending. Telecommunications brought in LYD 31.1m, and the General Authority for Communications spent about LYD 31.7m. It also lists electronic payment indicators.

## Seychelles

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Domestic-state digital-transformation budget line</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Seychelles targets 2027 start for digital system to modernise public service](https://www.seychellesnewsagency.com/public/articles/22565/seychelles-targets-2027-start-for-digital-system-to-modernise-public-service)** — Seychelles News Agency, 19 October 2026

Seychelles plans to begin implementing a digital human resource management system in 2027, Vice-President Sebastien Pillay announced after meeting Canadian vendor FreeBalance at State House on 14 October 2026. The system would cover employee records, payroll, performance management and workforce analytics. Pillay said technology alone would not improve efficiency unless administrative procedures are also reviewed.

## South Africa

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Universal Service and Access Fund collections and unspent balance</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Minister of Communications and Digital Technologies - reply to National Assembly oral question 763 (Hlazo-Webster): USAF collections and unspent funds](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/41023/)** — Department of Communications and Digital Technologies (via Parliamentary Monitoring Group), 30 September 2026

South Africa's communications minister told the National Assembly, in an oral reply approved on 30 September 2026, that ICASA collected R1,287,583,749 for the Universal Service and Access Fund over the five years to 2025/26, with about R652 million left unspent at the end of 2025/26. He cited delays in set-top-box installation and broadband projects; 2025/26 spending included a R567 million write-off of digital-migration stock.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Here's how much government collected towards digital migration in the last five years](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/how-much-collected-digital-migration-last-five-years/)** — The Citizen, 5 October 2026

South Africa's communications minister told Parliament that about R1.29 billion was collected for the Universal Service and Access Fund over five years and about R652 million was unspent at the end of 2025/26. Spending that year included a R567 million write-off of set-top-box inventory, and there is still no analogue switch-off date.

</div>

## Tanzania

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Development allocation to the registration authority</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Unclaimed IDs law in offing](https://dailynews.co.tz/unclaimed-ids-law-in-offing/)** — Daily News (Tanzania), 17 September 2026

Tanzania's Prime Minister told the registration authority to put a legally backed reminder-and-penalty mechanism into law for processed identity cards left uncollected, saying the state cannot keep absorbing the cost of printing cards that are then abandoned. He also directed deeper integration with employment, social-service and economic institutions, and put more than 132bn/- to the authority's development projects for 2026/27, including 31 registration offices.

</div>
