## Algeria

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Digital Algeria 2030 (National Digital Transformation Strategy)</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[À l'ère du tout numérique](https://www.elmoudjahid.dz/fr/editorials/a-l-ere-du-tout-numerique-4195)** — El Moudjahid, 8 September 2026

An El Moudjahid editorial on Algeria's Algérie Numérique 2030 strategy cites the opening of the national digital services centre at Mohammadia, with Tier III data centres, and ministries joining the Dzair Digital Services portal. It lists a product-traceability system in commerce, electronic medical records in hospitals, a national agricultural information system and the Takwin vocational training platform.

</div>

</div>

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Digitalisation oversight and the national portal</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Portail national des services numériques : Le HCN va assurer des formations](https://www.elmoudjahid.dz/fr/info-en-continu/portail-national-des-services-numeriques-le-hcn-va-assurer-des-formations-33381)** — El Moudjahid, 19 September 2026

Algeria's High Commission for Digitisation trained information system managers from several sectors to use a mechanism it built for verifying electronic documents issued through the Dzair Digital Services portal. Administrative bodies can then accept these documents without asking citizens to obtain them again from the issuing office. The training follows presidential instructions of 12 July 2026 to widen the portal's use.

## Burundi

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Burundi adopts Right-to-Information Law, with broad exceptions](https://www.burunditimes.com/burundi-adopts-right-to-information-law-with-broad-exceptions/)** — Burundi Times, 30 September 2026

Burundi's Senate unanimously adopted a bill on 29 September 2026 creating a legal right of access to public information. It sets up a National Commission on Access to Public Information to hear appeals and requires covered bodies to publish some information proactively, with exceptions for national security, private life and ongoing judicial investigations. ## Cote d'Ivoire

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

Côte d'Ivoire's digital transition ministry and the state IT company SNDI held a national workshop on 3 and 4 September 2026 in Abidjan to choose which public services to digitise first. Expected outputs include a first catalogue of about 100 services built on the national interoperability platform, which SNDI's director general said is already exchanging data between administrations.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[En quête d'efficacité, la Côte d'Ivoire vise 700 services publics en ligne d'ici 2029](https://www.wearetech.africa/accueil/fils/actualites/gestion-publique/en-quete-d-efficacite-la-cote-d-ivoire-vise-700-services-publics-en-ligne-d-ici-2029)** — wearetech.africa, 4 September 2026

Cote d'Ivoire set a target of 700 high-impact digital public services by 2029 and a zero-paper administration by 2030, at a national prioritisation workshop in Abidjan. A first portfolio of about 100 services is to be built or rebuilt on the national interoperability platform so one administration can draw on data another already holds; the ministry acknowledged that data exchange between existing platforms remains limited and that some functions were built twice.

</div>

</div>

Zimbabwe's ICT minister Tatenda Mavetera set out a three-phase plan for the postal and courier sector at its annual conference: ring-fencing the Universal Service Fund, then Smart Post Offices by 2030 with AI, drones and e-commerce, then strategic equity partnerships.
