## Nigeria

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Device Management System and Type Approval enforcement</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Device Mgt System won't expose users' data - NCC](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/09/device-mgt-system-wont-expose-users-data-ncc/)** — Vanguard, 9 September 2026

The Nigerian Communications Commission said on 9 September 2026 that its new Device Management System will not give it access to users' personal data or communications. The system records IMEI numbers to check that SIM-enabled devices have type approval, and unregistered devices will be barred from networks. The first phase, with the customs service and importers, covers devices already in stock and new imports.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Stakeholders Push for Extension of DMS Implementation Timeline, Seek Clarification on Grey Areas](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/09/09/stakeholders-push-for-extension-of-dms-implementation-timeline-seek-clarification-on-grey-areas/)** — ThisDay, 9 September 2026

Mobile device manufacturers asked the Nigerian Communications Commission to extend the timetable for its Device Management System, which checks each device's IMEI against registers of stolen or counterfeit handsets before activation. The commission plans to charge about N670 per IMEI and over N3,700 for high-end devices, with free onboarding of existing stock closing on 7 September. Manufacturers said the window was too short and the appeal procedure unclear.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[NCC Moves to Strengthen Type Approval Compliance for SIM-enabled Devices](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/09/10/ncc-moves-to-strengthen-type-approval-compliance-for-sim-enabled-devices/)** — ThisDay, 10 September 2026

Nigeria's communications regulator is strengthening enforcement of type approval for SIM-enabled devices through a technology-enabled framework, supported by a device management system that determines compliance electronically. The underlying obligation is in the communications statute, which requires approval before a device may be connected.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[TYPE APPROVAL ENFORCEMENT: Why your phone may stop working soon](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/09/type-approval-enforcement-why-your-phone-may-stop-working-soon/)** — Vanguard, 11 September 2026

The Nigerian Communications Commission has begun implementing a Device Management System that checks whether SIM-enabled devices meet its type-approval rules, using a central register of IMEI numbers, Vanguard reported on 11 September 2026. The first phase registers devices in stock and new imports with the Nigeria Customs Service; non-compliant and stolen devices can be blocked from networks. The commission says the system gives it no access to device contents.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[NCC Deploys 8,526 Telecom Sites, Vows to End Call Masking](https://von.gov.ng/ncc-deploys-8526-telecom-sites-vows-to-end-call-masking/)** — Voice of Nigeria, 12 September 2026

Nigeria's mobile operators have built 8,526 of 12,179 committed coverage and capacity sites, about 70%, up from around 5,000 at the previous meeting, the Nigerian Communications Commission board said after its 110th meeting. The board also said its Device Management System is live, allowing stolen devices to be blocked across networks, and restated zero tolerance for call masking.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Nigeria’s Phone Crackdown: Protecting Networks or Pricing Out Everyday Users?](https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/16/nigerias-phone-crackdown-protecting-networks-or-pricing-out-everyday-users/)** — Tech Africa News, 16 October 2026

From 10 October 2026, Nigeria's communications regulator began texting owners of phones not verified under type approval that network access could be blocked after 30 days of active use. The checks run on the NCC's Device Management System against each phone's IMEI, and grey-market and overseas-bought phones are among those flagged. A registration route and official fees had not been clearly published by 16 October.

</div>

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Zambia launches national digital trust infrastructure](https://www.itweb.africa/article/zambia-launches-national-digital-trust-infrastructure/VgZeyqJlpzp7djX9)** — ITWeb Africa, 9 September 2026

Zambia and Altron Security have launched a National Public Key Infrastructure, intended as a sovereign root of trust for digital identity, electronic signatures and secure online government, with certificates carrying legal validity under the country's electronic communications and transactions law. The master cryptographic keys were generated under independently audited conditions in a purpose-built facility and are held offline, split among several custodians so that no single body controls them. The Smart Zambia Institute's national coordinator said the launch is only the beginning and its value depends on whether government can sustain the infrastructure, fund it and get public institutions to adopt it.
