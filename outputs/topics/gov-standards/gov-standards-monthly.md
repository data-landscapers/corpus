## Ghana

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Cyber and Information Security Directive 2026</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[BoG orders banks to give fraud units direct access to CEOs](https://www.myjoyonline.com/bog-orders-banks-to-give-fraud-units-direct-access-to-ceos/)** — MyJoyOnline, 6 October 2026

The Bank of Ghana directed banks to give their fraud units direct and unrestricted access to the managing director or chief executive, Governor Johnson Asiama told heads of banks. He said fraud staff must hold the right skills and certifications, and that the central bank will tighten supervision of technology, customer-fund safeguarding and third-party risk. The meeting date is not given.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[BoG develops AI rules for financial sector](https://www.myjoyonline.com/bog-develops-ai-rules-for-financial-sector/)** — MyJoyOnline, 11 October 2026

The Bank of Ghana is drafting a directive on the use of artificial intelligence by financial institutions, its governor told the banks' annual meeting. The Bank will also run thematic reviews of how banks have implemented its revised cyber and information security directive.

</div>

</div>

The Ghana Health Service, supported by WHO, convened a national workshop to revise its Medical Records Strategy and Operational Guidelines, written nearly a decade ago. Ghana's communications minister Samuel Nartey George announced a national data exchange hub to connect 48 public registries, so that developers can query several institutions in one request while each keeps ownership of its database. NITA will set common data-classification standards, and private firms wanting government data will have to share their own.

## Nigeria

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Device Management System and Type Approval enforcement</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Nigeria’s Phone Crackdown: Protecting Networks or Pricing Out Everyday Users?](https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/16/nigerias-phone-crackdown-protecting-networks-or-pricing-out-everyday-users/)** — Tech Africa News, 16 October 2026

From 10 October 2026, Nigeria's communications regulator began texting owners of phones not verified under type approval that network access could be blocked after 30 days of active use. The checks run on the NCC's Device Management System against each phone's IMEI, and grey-market and overseas-bought phones are among those flagged. A registration route and official fees had not been clearly published by 16 October.

</div>

</div>

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Device Checker for type-approved handsets</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[NCC device verification questions Nigeria's phone imports](https://techcabal.com/2026/10/20/device-verification-phones-nigeria/)** — TechCabal, 20 October 2026

Nigeria's Communications Commission launched a Device Checker on 7 October 2026 that lets buyers dial *315# to see whether a phone is type approved, using its IMEI. An article on 20 October 2026 notes that 99% of smartphones are imported and about 63 million devices are sold each year, and questions how well the NCC can enforce compliance. Buyers of flagged phones have no standard refund route.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Device Checker: Nigeria introduces app to simplify phone verification](https://techcabal.com/2026/10/29/nigeria-device-checker-verification-app/)** — TechCabal, 29 October 2026

Nigeria's communications regulator, the NCC, announced a Device Checker app on 29 October 2026. Authorised manufacturers, importers, distributors and dealers use it to scan handset IMEIs and upload device data. The NCC is also licensing phone dealers in Kano State.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Why unverified phones could affect consumers, networks](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/11/why-unverified-phones-could-affect-consumers-networks/)** — Vanguard, 5 November 2026

Nigeria's Communications Commission urged buyers to check a phone on its Device Checker platform before paying, Vanguard reported on 5 November 2026. The NCC says about 231 million devices already on Nigerian networks were verified automatically before the framework took effect in October 2026. Zonal Controller Tunji Jimoh said the platform reads no personal data. The technical presentation, however, described linking a device's IMEI to subscriber identification.

</div>

</div>

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Public-sector IPv6 migration</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[NITDA Moves to Boost Digital Public Services as MDAs Begin IPv6 Migration Training](https://techeconomy.ng/nitda-moves-to-boost-digital-public-services-as-mdas-begin-ipv6-migration-training)** — Techeconomy, 20 October 2026

Nigeria's NITDA began its first IPv6 migration readiness training for federal ministries, departments and agencies, with AFRINIC and the Nigeria IPv6 Council, as reported on 20 October 2026. It runs in two streams, for management and for technical staff, and NITDA is funding certification for the technical staff. The move is tied to online public services and the National Sovereign Cloud Initiative.
