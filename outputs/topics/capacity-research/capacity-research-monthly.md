## Egypt

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Peer-reviewed assessment of readiness for AI-driven transformation in public healthcare</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Governing AI-driven digital transformation in public healthcare: assessing administrative readiness and institutional capacity in Egypt's health system](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1883794/full)** — Frontiers in Digital Health, 3 September 2026

A peer-reviewed study in Frontiers in Digital Health, published on 3 September 2026, assesses whether Egypt's public health institutions are ready for AI-enabled digital transformation. The authors surveyed 387 health administrators and policymakers across 24 governorates and interviewed 18 senior officials and experts; their readiness model explains 68 per cent of the variance in transformation success.

## Ghana

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Gov't seeks additional funding for Damongo Science and Technology University](https://gna.org.gh/2026/09/govt-seeks-additional-funding-for-damongo-science-and-technology-university/)** — Ghana News Agency, 2 September 2026

Ghana's Education Minister Haruna Iddrisu said on 2 September 2026 that a US$30 million Chinese government grant will not be enough to complete and equip the University of Science and Technology at Damongo, which he put at more than US$100 million. The Savannah Region university is planned as a centre for science, robotics, digital sciences and artificial intelligence.

Ghana's education minister Haruna Iddrisu disclosed a US$30 million grant from China's government for a University of Science and Technology at Damongo focused on artificial intelligence and emerging technologies. ## Morocco

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[تجربة مغربية للتصويت الرقمي تجمع التعرف على الوجه و"البلوك تشين"](https://www.hespress.com/%d8%aa%d8%ac%d8%b1%d8%a8%d8%a9-%d9%85%d8%ba%d8%b1%d8%a8%d9%8a%d8%a9-%d9%84%d9%84%d8%aa%d8%b5%d9%88%d9%8a%d8%aa-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85%d9%8a-%d8%aa%d8%ac%d9%85%d8%b9-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%aa%d8%b9-1802990.html)** — Hespress, 7 September 2026

Researchers at Mohammed I University in Oujda and Abdelmalek Essaâdi University in Tétouan tested AdaptVote, an e-voting model combining facial recognition and blockchain, with 300 people in Nador over 12 hours. The network was available for only 35% of the trial; 95.7% passed identity checks first time and voting took 2.1 minutes against 4.5 on paper. It is a research platform, not an official system.
