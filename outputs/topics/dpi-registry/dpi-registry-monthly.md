## Angola

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Beneficial ownership register</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Legal Regime of the Beneficial Owner](https://www.primeadvogados.co.ao/en/publications/newsletters-and-flashes/legal-regime-of-the-beneficial-owner/537/)** — Prime Advogados, 1 September 2026

A Prime Advogados note explains Angola's Law No. 7/26 of 19 August 2026 on beneficial ownership, which covers almost every private entity holding a tax number, from companies and NGOs to trusts and political parties. Filing goes to an electronic central register, the CRBE, interoperable with the commercial registries and notaries, with fines up to Kz 50 million, records kept for ten years and existing entities given until 15 February 2027 to comply.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Governo cria equipa para implementar Central de Registo do Beneficiário Efectivo](https://www.giranoticias.com/economia/2026/10/32053-governo-cria-equipa-para-implementar-central-de-registo-do-beneficiario-efectivo.html)** — Gira Notícias, 17 October 2026

Angola's Minister of State for Economic Coordination set up a technical group from ten state bodies, given 180 days, to build the central beneficial-ownership register (CRBE) required by the new beneficial-ownership law. The Justice secretary of state coordinates, with the Financial Intelligence Unit as deputy, as Angola remains on the FATF grey list it rejoined in October 2024.

</div>

Angola's territorial administration ministry put mobile brigades into service from late August 2026 to speed up updating of the electoral register, and more than 1.6 million citizens have updated their data, reported on 8 September 2026. Citizens over 18 in the municipality of Caianda, in Moxico Leste province, began updating their electoral data after a one-stop public service desk opened there. The municipal administrator was the first to update his own, and urged traditional authorities, religious leaders and youth organisations to bring communities in ahead of the 2027 general elections.

## Cameroon

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Digitalisation of civil registration</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Cameroun : 52 milliards FCFA recherchés pour numériser l'état civil](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-52-milliards-fcfa-recherches-pour-numeriser-letat-civil)** — EcoMatin, 16 September 2026

The director-general of Cameroon's national civil registration office, BUNEC, said on 14 September 2026 that completing the digitisation of civil-status records will cost 52 billion FCFA, against 27 billion estimated in 2018. <div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

Cameroon's civil-registration office, BUNEC, sent a study mission to Côte d'Ivoire's ONECI on 26–30 October 2026 on digitisation and interoperability. Cameroon has computerised 60 of its 432 principal civil-registration centres. It aims to computerise all of them by 2029.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[État civil : le Cameroun s’inspire de la Côte d’Ivoire pour interconnecter ses données](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/etat-civil-le-cameroun-s-inspire-de-la-cote-d-ivoire-pour-interconnecter-ses-donnees)** — We Are Tech Africa, 29 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/CIV/CIV-monthly.html" title="CIV">Côte d'Ivoire</a>

Cameroon's civil registration bureau BUNEC visited Côte d'Ivoire's ONECI in Abidjan from 26 to 30 October 2026. Cameroon plans to computerise and interconnect 432 main registry centres by 2029. ONECI reported its CityWeb software in every centre, online e-certified birth extracts, and weak connectivity in some centres.

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[État civil au Congo-Brazzaville : fini le temps des actes « bricolés » !](https://lesechos-congobrazza.com/societe/12368-etat-civil-au-congo-brazzaville-fini-le-temps-des-actes-bricoles)** — Les Echos du Congo-Brazzaville, 6 September 2026

Congo-Brazzaville's territorial administration issued a circular on 1 September 2026 tightening control of civil registration.
