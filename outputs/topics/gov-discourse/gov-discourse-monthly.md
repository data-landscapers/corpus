<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Criminal defamation prosecutions of journalists</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Criminal defamation threat hangs over dozens of Angolan journalists ahead of 2027 vote](https://cpj.org/2026/10/criminal-defamation-threat-hangs-over-dozens-of-angolan-journalists-ahead-of-2027-vote/)** — Committee to Protect Journalists, 8 October 2026

CPJ reported on 8 October 2026 that 38 Angolan journalists have faced criminal defamation investigations since 2015, over 200 in all. One, Reporter Angola editor Daniel Frederico, was sentenced in absentia to 14 months on 2 October 2026 over another outlet's article. Defamation in Angola carries prison terms of up to 18 months.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Law on False Information on the Internet adopted; use of criminal defamation to intimidate journalists](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/law-on-false-information-on-the-internet-adopted-activists-and-journalists-detained-prosecuted/)** — CIVICUS Monitor, 31 October 2026

Angola's National Assembly adopted Law 6/26 against false information online on 21 May 2026, CIVICUS Monitor reported. The law took effect on 4 August and sets fines and criminal penalties; media report prison terms of up to ten years. The update also records a 14-month defamation sentence passed on editor Daniel Frederico on 2 October 2026.

</div>

## Gabon

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

Gabon's presidential spokesman said on radio that he uses a VPN, outside the law, to get round the social-media suspension the communications authority imposed in February 2026. The outlet reports the suspension still in force and the authority silent on any sanction.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Gabon : la HAC lève la suspension des réseaux sociaux](https://agpgabon.ga/gabon-la-hac-leve-la-suspension-des-reseaux-sociaux/)** — Agence Gabonaise de Presse, 24 October 2026

Gabon's media regulator, the Haute autorité de la communication, lifted on 24 October 2026 the nationwide suspension of social media it had ordered on 17 February. It cited three presidential ordinances of 26 February regulating social-media use and reporting protocols now operating with TikTok, Meta and YouTube that give it access to their tools for flagging illicit content.

## Zambia

## Zimbabwe

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Londa digital rights and inclusion score</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Press Release: Zimbabwe ranks 14th out of 29 in the latest digital rights and inclusion report](https://paradigmhq.org/press-release-zimbabwe-ranks-14th-out-of-29-in-the-latest-digital-rights-and-inclusion-report/)** — Paradigm Initiative, 26 October 2026

Paradigm Initiative's Londa 2025 report ranks Zimbabwe 14th of 29 African countries on digital rights and inclusion. That is four places lower than before. It records no internet shutdown in 2025, but continued arrests of journalists and use of sedition and false-news laws. It urges laws on online gender-based violence and rights assessments before surveillance projects.
