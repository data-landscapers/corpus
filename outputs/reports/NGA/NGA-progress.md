# Nigeria: progress report, August 2025 – August 2026

*Of 270 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 158 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-26, 54 did not, 48 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 696 sources for this place in the window — 93 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 603 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Nearly four times as many sources sit in the later half of this window as in the earlier, so arrival counts measure coverage as much as construction.

No system climbed a maturity band, and every system that changed band went backwards. Satellite broadband regressed, its largest provider pausing new orders over congestion and then brought inside a biometric verification regime; the retail central bank digital currency is under review and being redesigned toward a wholesale model; and enforcement of the new internet-platform rules was paused pending a unified framework. One programme stalled — 260 state schools whose January 2026 completion date passed with many incomplete.

Everything else arrived or held. Nigeria's digital state was substantially built before the window opened.

The same year produced a data-localisation directive, a virtual-assets executive order, a crypto tax circular, an identity statute, an open-banking launch and a three-year payments vision.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
One position worsened and the rest of the section arrived.

Internet-platform regulation regressed: enforcement of new rules was paused in July pending a unified national digital-economy policy framework. The same month's competition directive put the same class of company under investigation for abuse of dominance.

Data-protection enforcement held its band and changed character within it: already active at the window's start, having lifted compliance from about 4% [to 55%+](https://techafricanews.com/2025/08/25/nigeria-probes-1369-organizations-in-major-data-privacy-crackdown/) by March 2025, what the year added was judicial confirmation. The Federal High Court upheld the Commission's registration power in July, and days later a private claimant won the first damages award under the Act without needing the Commission at all. Neither judgment is held, and one has no established delivery date.

What arrived: an executive order that creates a coordination council rather than a regulator, and a tax framework that collects duty in the token. The two rows that held position throughout are the statute itself and the 2021 draft data and cloud policy — five years a draft, while the localisation question it was written to answer was settled instead by a central bank circular.
