# Eritrea: status report

*Compiled 2026-09-01 by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository, from `outputs/reports/ERI/ledger.csv` (40 systems and instruments, 15 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Eritrea's digitally-tracked state consists of a small number of discrete projects rather than a connected system: a customs platform delivered with UNDP support in January 2026 ([ASYCUDA World](https://shabait.com/2026/01/08/eritrea-launches-landmark-digital-customs-system)), a completed national household survey using digital data collection ([EPHS 2025](https://www.undp.org/eritrea/news/driving-data-digital-technology-eritrea-strengthens-data-systems-landmark-survey)), and a domestically built offline AI application ([Eri-Chat](https://shabait.com/2025/11/01/eri-chat-offline-ai-app/)). No foundational digital-ID system, mobile-money service or data-protection law is held on record. Connectivity is among the lowest recorded on the continent — [23.7% of the population held a mobile connection in late 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-eritrea) — and the state telecom monopoly EriTel reports no submarine cable landing and, as of July 2026, [no announced Starlink launch date](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html). A July 2026 legal notice ordering all cash deposited into banks by 31 July or "face legal action" is the period's most consequential governance development, issued against a cash-based, under-banked economy. The evidence base itself is unusually constrained: the outlets producing dated material are limited to Eritrea's Ministry of Information (Shabait) and the diaspora/opposition outlet Awate.com, neither neutral, with harder figures coming from UNDP/UNECA and reference indices.

## ICT Infrastructure

### Connectivity

| System or instrument | Status | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| EriTel national network expansion programme | [Implemented, ongoing expansion](https://shabait.com/2026/05/19/substantial-investment-in-expansion-of-communication-service/) | Minister reports 184 towers installed, ~90% geographic mobile coverage, service 'reaching 4G level' (government claim) |
| Starlink satellite internet service | ***Not held*** | Starlink's own coverage-availability map lists Eritrea 'No planned launch date' |
| Submarine fibre-optic cable landing | ***Not held*** | Cable industry calls Eritrea a 'black hole' that does not answer landing requests; still cable-less as of Apr 2026 |

<!-- narrative: ict-infrastructure -->
Eritrea's international connectivity runs entirely on satellite. The country holds no submarine fibre-optic cable landing, and cable firms describe the government as unresponsive even to a Red Sea route sought after Houthi attacks made Yemeni waters unusable — a position unchanged as of April 2026 and one that leaves Eritrea, on the industry's own undated count, as the sole non-landing state among Africa's 38 coastal countries. [Starlink](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/broadband/658586-starlink-launches-in-27th-african-country-before-south-africa.html), available in half of Africa's sovereign states by July 2026, carries no announced launch date for Eritrea, one of only seven African states — all in the north or the Horn — in that position; no licence application or refusal has been found. Domestically, the state telecom monopoly EriTel [reports 184 towers installed and roughly 90% geographic mobile coverage as of May 2026](https://shabait.com/2026/05/19/substantial-investment-in-expansion-of-communication-service/), alongside international bandwidth risen to about 1,536 Mbps over the preceding decade — figures from the responsible minister that cannot be independently verified.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
The clearest concrete step in the period is the [January 2026 launch of ASYCUDA World](https://shabait.com/2026/01/08/eritrea-launches-landmark-digital-customs-system), UNCTAD's automated customs platform, replacing software in use since 2007; the deployment came with a full transfer of source code to the Eritrean government, a national-ownership condition rather than a licensed or hosted arrangement. Statistical infrastructure has also advanced: [the National Statistics Office completed the Fourth Round Population and Health Survey in 2025](https://www.undp.org/eritrea/news/driving-data-digital-technology-eritrea-strengthens-data-systems-landmark-survey), covering 9,794 households across all six regions using tablet-based interviewing rather than paper forms, with the full report due in 2026 under a UNDP/UNECA-backed programme that has funded Eritrean statistical-capacity building since at least 2019. No foundational digital-identity system is held on record — [an analytical profile finds the country lacks one](https://globaldigitalidentityindex.com/eritrea-population-digital-identity) and sets out what a future system would have to overcome — and no mobile-money or digital-payments service is held either, consistent with the cash-based, under-banked economy discussed under Governance below.
