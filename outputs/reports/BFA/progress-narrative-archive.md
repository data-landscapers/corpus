# BFA — archived progress narrative

*Archived by CC on 2026-08-26, before the indicator-framed rewrite of the progress
report removed the per-Level-1 narrative blocks (`documentation/progress-report-redesign.md`
§1, and its review item 4). This is drafted, register-checked, cited prose about the same
evidence the ledger holds; it is the source material for BFA's indicator Developments
drafting pass, not a published document. Block keys are the Level-1 chapter markers the
renderer used.*

## summary

Twelve months ago Burkina Faso's governance of data rested on a 2021 statute whose enforcement decrees had never been made, supervised by an authority that had publicly admitted it had no repressive powers and was in its third year of budget contraction. Twelve months on the legislature has voted a substantive data-protection law with turnover-indexed fines and a merged regulator to enforce it, the 2024 security statutes have acquired their machinery, and the state has built a service layer it did not have. What has not moved is the accountability side, and the domestic money fell across the whole period.

## governance

The band change here is the two votes of 3 August 2026, and it is incomplete by construction: a substantive data-protection law and a merged regulator were voted, and neither text is held, neither is promulgated, and the merged authority has no stated commencement, seat or leadership. The merger also removes the separation that let broadcasting regulation and data-protection supervision act as checks on each other.

The 2024 cybersecurity and unique-identification statutes both moved from asserted duty to operating machinery in a single sitting on 30 July 2026, giving the state critical-infrastructure classification, auditor accreditation and a mass biometric-enrolment apparatus it did not have a year ago. The policy layer hardened in the same period: the twelve programmes to 2030 were presented in August 2025, an artificial-intelligence roadmap was validated in June 2026 where a year earlier there was no national strategy at all, and the data-repatriation programme acquired its first published number in July 2026.

What did not move is the accountability side. The enforcement decrees under the 2021 law were never issued in the five years it ran. The authority's own 2024 report, validated in plenary in June 2025, was still unpublished as at 25 July 2026, against a statutory duty. The access-to-information law and the whistleblower bill are where they were eighteen months ago. And automated traffic enforcement went live with no data-protection authorisation, retention rule or oversight body named anywhere in the record.

## finance

Domestic money moved the other way across the whole period. [The six tracked appropriation lines fell from FCFA 13.56bn for 2025 to FCFA 9.61bn for 2026, below even the 2024 level](https://budgetouvert.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/loi-de-finances-pour-lexecution-du-budget-de-letat-exercice-2026.pdf); [the digital transformation programme fell 67%](https://budgetouvert.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/loi-de-finances-pour-lexecution-du-budget-de-letat-exercice-2026.pdf) as a single chapter named after a foreign software vendor disappeared from the volume; and the data-protection authority's vote fell for a second consecutive year while its remit expanded.

## ict-infrastructure

Storage and the legal machinery around it are where the band genuinely changed. At the window's opening the two mini data centres were building sites with a cost quoted at over FCFA 15bn, the 750-locality tender dossier had just been finalised with nothing launched, and the twelve programmes to 2030 were four days from being presented. Since then the data centres were commissioned in January 2026 at FCFA 16bn and ten times the prior capacity, the localisation doctrine acquired an inventory and a number, a domestic commercial host appeared to answer it, and the two decrees of 30 July 2026 gave the 2024 security law the critical-infrastructure mapping and auditor-accreditation machinery it had asserted without.

Connectivity accreted detail. The white-zone programme moved from tender to service orders without any 2026 locality count being published, and the government network spent the year in architecture and procurement. Three things did not move at all: the satellite operator is still unlicensed, there is still no national computer emergency response team, and there is still no named instrument for powering rural sites.

## dpi

The service layer was built inside the window, and that is this section's band change. At its opening there were two working assets — a payments platform from January 2025 and a land platform from March 2025 — and procedures sat on separate ministry platforms with no authoritative index. Everything that now constitutes the service layer was launched since: a central portal in May 2026, seventeen regional portals in June, and six further services between March and July.

Identity moved band twice in opposite directions at once. The regional biometric card went from validated specifications to a card in mass production and adopted by a second member state. The foundational electronic identifier went from a failed tender to a signed enrolment decree and is still not enrolling anyone.

Payments changed from a test rail to a live one with nine authorised domestic institutions, then immediately slipped: the connection deadline moved to September 2026, and the mobile-money client figures are all still the mid-2025 numbers, so what looks like a moving market has only its regulatory perimeter moving.

The genuinely new kind of object is the one the state built last: an automated enforcement chain that reads plates, matches them to a vehicle register and collects the fine through the national payments rail, with immobilisation as the sanction. It went live three days into the sitting that voted its would-be supervisor out of existence.

## technology

At the window's opening there was no national artificial-intelligence strategy, a privately founded institute nine days old, a research centre of excellence on a foreign grant, and one exploratory conversation with a foreign ambassador described as preliminary with no agreement signed.

Twelve months later the country holds a validated roadmap for 2026 to 2030 with a sovereign-language flagship, sits inside a six-country governance framework, hosted the region's digital-economy salon, and — the genuine maturity change — has two live deployments: automated traffic enforcement running end to end from camera to payment since 1 August 2026, and a hosting-side sovereignty market. That is a move from policy absent to policy validated and systems in operation.

What merely accreted detail is the capacity story: training lawmakers, training the Prime Minister's own directors, announced youth sessions. All of it is the same position — the state is training competent users, and none of it creates an appeal route, a retention rule or an accountable authority. The partnership picture also did not move: a Gulf memorandum has produced no reported project in eleven months, a United States initiative no reported cohort in seven, and a Chinese project passed its stated completion date with nothing on file.

## inclusion

Three things changed band, all in the window's second half. Connectivity moved from plan to programme: a year ago the record shows an 800-site rollout with the tender still open and no named programme; today the white-zone drive is launched, financed at a stated FCFA 75bn, with the 2025 operator commitments delivered in full. Payments moved from nothing to a live regional rail with nine domestic institutions authorised. And the unique electronic identifier moved from awareness campaign to legal instrument.

Two substantial operations closed inside the window and neither has a successor: a seven-year financial-inclusion project ending in June 2026 [with 81% of indicators met or exceeded](https://www.sidwaya.info/transformation-numerique-inclusion-financiere-et-financement-des-pme-le-bilan-dun-projet-structurant-au-burkina-faso/), and a gender-and-digital programme ending in 2025 with no evaluation, which was the only such instrument the base holds. Consumer protection improved through the courts rather than the regulator, with 2023 rules becoming enforceable in December 2025 and [cutting standard mobile-internet prices by 28 to 45%](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/telecom/burkina-faso-tightens-telecom-regulations-but-affordability-concerns-persist).

## gaps

The absences below did not close during the period and several deepened. The two laws voted on 3 August have no held text; the universal-access fund published no account of its levy in either direction across the whole window; and the 2025 execution annexe dropped the exclusion that made programme-grain outturn comparable, so the one year of visible delivery data cannot be set against the next. They are the first briefs for the country-deep sweep.
