# Gambia: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-27 by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository, from `outputs/reports/GMB/ledger.csv` (68 systems and instruments, 13 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The Gambia's identity and payment spines both entered service inside twelve months. The [National Identity Management System launched on 1 July 2026](https://standard.gm/barrow-inaugurates-national-data-centre-digital-identity-system/), issuing instant GAM ID cards from a state data centre opened the same day, with nationwide enrolment from 4 August 2026; no enrolment figure has been published since. Payments clear through [BANTABA 2.0, launched by the Central Bank on 15 December 2025](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/gambia-launches-bantaba-2-0-first-real-time-interoperable-payment-system).

The country has a data-protection statute and no visible regulator. The [Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act was enacted in 2025](https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/review-of-gambias-personal-data-protection-and-privacy-act-2025) with extraterritorial scope and an independent Commission provided for; nothing held states whether that Commission has been constituted.

The most specific claim the state makes for its own digitalisation is fiscal. The finance minister reported tax collected rising [from D11 billion in 2022 to D25 billion in 2025](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/digital-reform-doubles-gambias-tax-revenue-to-d25b-finance-minister) and attributed it to digitalisation of collection rather than to rate rises — an attribution by the collecting side rather than an evaluation of it.

Two questions about foreign operators are open: [Starlink is still unlicensed](https://orbitaltoday.com/2026/05/24/starlink-licence-gets-delayed-by-the-gambia/) after a refusal stated in May 2026, and 80% of the state mobile operator [passed to YCELL on 9 July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/gamtel-ycell-sign-landmark-share-investment-and-network-modernization-agreement).

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The law is in force and its regulator is not on record. The [Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act 2025](https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/review-of-gambias-personal-data-protection-and-privacy-act-2025) ends a legislative process stalled for several years: extraterritorial jurisdiction, seven lawful bases, a prohibition on processing genetic, biometric, health, racial and political data absent a lawful basis and a specific condition, and rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection and protection from solely automated decisions. It provides for an independent Commission. Nothing held states whether that Commission has been constituted, staffed or funded.

Two further bills would complete the frame, and both are unfinished. The [Communications Bill 2025 passed second reading on 2 March 2026](https://standard.gm/lawmakers-back-bill-to-protect-personal-data-in-digital-age/) and went to committee; it covers electronic communications, broadcasting, electronic commerce, electronic signatures, computer misuse and cybercrime in one instrument, and the Press Union [set out the media laws it wants repealed alongside it on 18 July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/national-news/lawmakers-engaged-over-communication-bill). The [State Intelligence Service Bill was validated at a workshop reported 31 July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/sis-validates-bill-to-reposition-as-robust-futuristic-intelligence-service) and is reported to require judicial warrants and oversight for intrusive measures. Its text is not held and does not appear in the National Assembly's bills library, checked 2026-08-08, so those provisions are held as reported rather than as drafted.

Policy sits ahead of instruments. The [National Data Policy 2025 is still a draft](https://mocde.gov.gm/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/National-Data-Policy-2025-The-Gambia-draft.docx) with no adoption on record, and the [Master Plan 2024-2034](https://mocde.gov.gm/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/National-Digital-Economy-Masterplan.pdf) carries no mid-term review or costed plan. The [National Cybersecurity Strategy and Action Plan 2022-2026](https://mocde.gov.gm/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/National-Cybersecurity-Strategy-2022-2026-Final-.pdf) runs out at end-2026; the bodies it designs, GM-CSIRT among them, are not evidenced as standing. Regional cooperation runs through Senegal: the two regulators [signed a cooperation and free-roaming agreement in June 2026](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6078/senegal-and-the-gambia-enhance-digital-integration-through-regulatory-cooperation-agreement) with no commencement date given.

<!-- narrative: ict-infrastructure -->
Hosting arrived in 2026, in two facilities ten weeks apart. [QGroup House, the first data centre, was inaugurated on 30 April 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/barrow-inaugurates-qgroup-house-unveils-gambias-first-data-centre), built by QGroup and QCell with Huawei as vendor, and the state's [National Data Centre at Abuko opened on 1 July 2026](https://standard.gm/barrow-inaugurates-national-data-centre-digital-identity-system/) carrying the national identity system. Neither facility's capacity, tier rating nor operating cost is published, and nothing states who operates them.

The backbone is under a modernisation contract and not yet modernised. GAMTEL signed a [US$50m partnership with SYSROAD, announced 5 January 2026, to take national capacity from 40 Gbps to 800 Gbps](https://techafricanews.com/2026/01/05/gamtel-and-sysroad-sign-50-million-ppp-to-modernize-the-gambias-broadband-network/); no completion date or milestone schedule accompanies it. The network it upgrades was built on a [US$25.9m China Eximbank concessional loan whose servicing fell into arrears](https://www.dropbox.com/s/9yvhtd9i4i0vrs0/cr18197.pdf?dl=0), repayable by GAMTEL and collateralised against an escrow balance the lender can reach; nothing published since 2020 states the network's condition, utilisation or debt position.

Satellite access is unresolved. The Minister of Digital Economy said on [24 May 2026 that he had refused to approve Starlink's permit](https://orbitaltoday.com/2026/05/24/starlink-licence-gets-delayed-by-the-gambia/), on the ground that the operator lacked the competencies and equipment for a VSAT permit and required a general satellite licence instead. An analyst [argued in February 2026 for data-localisation and lawful-interception conditions](https://standard.gm/expert-urges-govt-to-put-conditions-for-data-sovereignty-before-approving-starlink-licence/) before any approval; no such conditions appear in an adopted instrument.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Identity is the spine, and it changed hands in January 2026. Card production moved from a foreign concession to an [eight-year build-operate-transfer contract with Margins ID](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/govt-signs-id-card-deal-with-margins-id-systems-applications-ltd) carrying a 70/30 revenue share and a phase-2 data centre, with [ten-year card validity](https://standard.gm/new-id-card-to-get-10-years-validity/). No contract value is published. Underneath sits the electronic civil registration system, which [registered about 1.17m people with a unique identification number](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2024/03/28/afw-transformative-journey-of-mass-birth-registration-and-health-insurance-in-the-gambia) and remains the interoperability backbone; the [June 2025 commitment to integrate it with cards and the passport](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/govt-to-integrate-id-cards-with-electronic-civil-registration) has produced no published milestone. The [social registry stands at 203,022 households across 40 of 43 districts](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/nasikiliza/the-gambia-social-registry-a-success-story-in-systems-building), unchanged since April 2024.

Payments are interoperable at the switch and plural at the front. [BANTABA 2.0 clears in real time across banks, mobile money, microfinance institutions and fintechs](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/gambia-launches-bantaba-2-0-first-real-time-interoperable-payment-system). Government collection runs through three separate arrangements: [Yonna Wallet, authorised on 2 March 2026](https://standard.gm/govt-payments-now-digitalised-live-on-yonna-wallet-app), the [SUTURA platform wired in by memorandum in July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/accountant-general-seals-pact-with-sutura-to-strengthen-digital-revenue-collection), and the revenue authority's [tax invoicing, live since 9 July 2025](https://standard.gm/govt-launches-digital-tax-invoicing-system). None publishes a transaction volume or a fee structure. Separately, UNDP is [piloting blockchain-based payment distribution with FreedomPay Wallet](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/undp-pushes-blockchain-from-dpi-pilots-to-deployment), grouped with pilots in Haiti, Syria, Kenya and Guatemala said to cut distribution costs from about 10% to as little as 2%; no Gambia-specific volume or beneficiary count is published.

Tax administration is where the state's digitalisation claim is loudest and least verifiable. The [digital excise tax stamp is studied by five African revenue administrations](https://standard.gm/south-africa-rwanda-customs-to-study-gambias-digital-tax-stamp-success/), and the authority attaches to it a claimed 95% reduction in smuggling of excisable goods, for which it gives no method and which no independent party has verified.

Interoperability has a blueprint and no adoption: the [National Enterprise Architecture was validated under GovStack on 22 May 2026](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/5564/the-gambia-validates-national-enterprise-architecture-framework-under-govstack-initiative) and is not adopted, funded or implemented. Where exchange does run, it runs through customs — the [three-month roadmap agreed with Senegal on 16-17 July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/gra-senegalese-customs-agree-to-eliminate-transit-impediments-through-ict) is the most operationally specific data-sharing commitment the base holds.

Statistics are the most complete system in the stack. The [fifth labour force survey was disseminated in July 2026](https://www.gbosdata.org/downloads-file/627-the-gambia-labour-force-survey-findings-report) with [the first comparison across three rounds](https://www.gbosdata.org/downloads-file/628-the-gambia-labour-market-progress-and-trends-2). Both volumes are donor-financed and no domestic budget line for the series is established.

Nothing in the base establishes an artificial-intelligence position for The Gambia — no policy, no strategy, no readiness assessment and no ministerial statement.

The technology-sector position is the disposal of the state mobile operator. Government [told lawmakers in December 2025 that YCELL had bid D6.7 billion for 80% of GAMCEL](https://standard.gm/govt-to-sell-80-percent-shares-at-gamcel-for-d6-billion/), and the [agreement signed on 9 July 2026 carries a GMD 6.1 billion modernisation commitment](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/headlines/gamtel-ycell-sign-landmark-share-investment-and-network-modernization-agreement) covering 4G and 5G radio access, core, operations and billing systems, hybrid tower power and revenue assurance. The base does not reconcile the two figures, and no post-transaction financial position for either operator is published.

One announced platform has produced nothing. A [memorandum with the Kalp Foundation for a blockchain-based Gambia One platform was signed in January 2025](https://itweb.africa/article/the-gambia-signs-blockchain-pact-to-boost-digital-economy/8OKdWqDXbnYqbznQ); nineteen months later nothing held records a build, a pilot, funding or a withdrawal. The other innovation activity on record is a [youth showcase on 9 July 2026](https://thepoint.gm/africa/gambia/national-news/young-gambians-unveil-digital-innovations-to-transform-childrens-lives), with no follow-on funding.
