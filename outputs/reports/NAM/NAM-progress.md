# Namibia: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-21

*Compiled 2026-08-21 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 129 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 63 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-21, 35 did not, 19 carry no stated baseline, and 12 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 123 sources for this place in the window — 27 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-02), 96 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Namibia's twelve months to 6 August 2026 delivered networks and deferred statutes. Commercial fifth-generation service, a privately owned mobile network and 25 new towers all entered service inside the window ([launch account](https://www.connectingafrica.com/investment/paratus-launches-namibia-s-first-private-mobile-network)); the Data Protection Bill missed a September 2025 tabling and the electronic signature regime missed a February 2026 deployment, and both are marked *Stalled* ([tabling announcement](https://www.namibian.com.na/govt-to-introduce-data-protection-bill-in-september/)).

The regulatory decision of the period was a refusal. A satellite operator's licence application went from pending, with a ministerial ownership exemption under review, to refused on 23 March 2026 and upheld on 22 June after 624 reconsideration requests were dismissed ([regulator account](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/06/22/namibias-communications-regulator-confirms-dismissal-of-starlinks-licence-appeals/)).

Namibia is the one country in this batch with no coverage cliff: its base runs continuously across the window, so the ten rows carrying no baseline are genuinely unestablished positions rather than an artefact of when the wiki started reading.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
The refusal is the movement. At 30 June 2025 the satellite operator's application was pending with a ministerial exemption from the ownership rule under review; by 22 June 2026 the licence and spectrum had been declined and every reconsideration dismissed, the operator's own late request among them ([regulator account](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/06/22/namibias-communications-regulator-confirms-dismissal-of-starlinks-licence-appeals/)). The ownership requirement itself did not change.

Three network positions advanced. Commercial fifth-generation service went from none in June 2025 to live in three centres by the end of August ([cabinet account](https://itweb.africa/article/namibia-expedites-data-centre-smart-city-development/KWEBb7yLVARvmRjO)); a privately owned mobile network went from announced to launched on 2 September 2025; and 25 towers were inaugurated across nine regions ([tower account](https://www.namibiansun.com/technology/namibia-bolsters-digital-access-with-25-new-network-towers2025-08-25167910)).

The measured position that worsened is cyber. Detected vulnerabilities and events rose 39.8% and 56.7% quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, on top of a 37% year-on-year rise across calendar 2025 ([regulator account](https://www.namibian.com.na/cyber-threats-surge-as-namibia-records-half-a-million-vulnerabilities/)). The national data centre directed by cabinet in September 2025 has no site, capacity, cost or date at either end of the window.
