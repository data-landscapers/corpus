# Africa: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-25

*Compiled 2026-08-25 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections run from the region's institutions outwards to what funds them. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 105 bodies, instruments and systems on this place's ledger, 50 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-25, 10 did not, 42 carry no stated baseline, and 3 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 628 sources for this place in the window — 71 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 557 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The continental layer signed more than it built. The Commission [signed a cooperation agreement with the ITU](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6310/african-union-commission-itu-sign-cooperation-agreement) in July 2026 and [an AI memorandum with Google](https://cioafrica.co/auc-signs-partnership-with-google-to-advance-africas-ai-digital-capacity/) in February.

Two payment developments were substantive. The Bank of Central African States [acceded to PAPSS on 2026-07-11](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/09/beac-joins-papss-to-strengthen-cross-border-payments-in-central-africa/), extending the rail into a third region; and the continent's central banks [ruled that a Payment Systems Directive is not to be led from outside them](https://aacb.org/sites/default/files/past_event_documents/2025_List%20of%20Decisions_Yaounde_Nov_2025.pdf). At the window's close a third rail entered the record from the private side: a pan-African payments operator [argues that stablecoins are moving from speculation into the plumbing of remittances and business settlement](https://itweb.africa/article/moving-stablecoins-from-hype-to-utility-in-africas-next-payments-evolution/PmxVEMKEOwkvQY85), citing Mastercard's agreement to acquire BVNK at up to US$1.8bn and Visa's stablecoin-card expansion through Stripe's Bridge into more than 100 countries. It is an operator's account of its own market, and no African regulator's treatment of stablecoin settlement is held against it.

The instruments the layer rests on did not move. A majority of the Union's 55 members [remain outside the Malabo Convention](https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf); no implementation report against the Continental AI Strategy of July 2024 is held; and the fund announced at Kigali in April 2025 [is still cited at US$60bn with nothing capitalised](https://restofworld.org/2026/africa-ai-sovereignty-big-tech/).

The Commission's year was one of external agreements. It [signed a memorandum with Google](https://cioafrica.co/auc-signs-partnership-with-google-to-advance-africas-ai-digital-capacity/) at Addis Ababa on 2026-02-17 on AI and cloud infrastructure, skills and policy, and [a cooperation agreement with the ITU](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6310/african-union-commission-itu-sign-cooperation-agreement) at Geneva in July across AI, digital public infrastructure, connectivity and cybersecurity, which names the Commission the continental coordinating partner for its 55 member states. Alice Munyua reads the Google instrument as exposing African data to US jurisdiction under the CLOUD Act, [against a 2025 programme budget 77.5 per cent externally funded](https://alicemunyua.substack.com/p/the-gatekeepers-part-xii).

The African Telecommunications Union completed a leadership cycle at its [seventh Conference of Plenipotentiaries, Abuja, 23-24 July 2026](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/27/atu-conference-adopts-abuja-declaration-to-advance-meaningful-connectivity-across-africa/): Kezias Kazuba Mwale of Zambia elected secretary general, a 25-state administrative council seated and the chairmanship passed from Algeria to Nigeria.

The Association of African Central Banks took the period's most specific decision. <!-- narrative: instruments -->
The binding continental instrument did not change. The Malabo Convention has been in force since 2023-06-08, and on [the depositary's register of 2026-02-02](https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/29560-sl-AFRICAN_UNION_CONVENTION_ON_CYBER_SECURITY_AND_PERSONAL_DATA_PROTECTION_0.pdf), 20 of 55 states had deposited an instrument of ratification. The one movement is that Equatorial Guinea, where the Convention was adopted and after whose then-capital it is named, [signed on 2026-07-28](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/au-infrastructure-and-energy_equatorial-guinea-signs-the-malabo-convention-activity-7488275982521565184-ETtI) and has still not deposited.

The Continental AI Strategy of July 2024 is likewise unchanged. Ministers at the Economic Commission for Africa [called for its translation into national frameworks](https://thenationonlineng.net/stakeholders-urge-shift-in-digital-public-infrastructure-trajectory/) with dedicated data-governance funding; no implementation report against it is held.

What advanced were declarations. Ministers [adopted the Algiers Declaration](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/4639/african-ministers-adopt-algiers-declaration-on-telecommunications-sovereignty-and-connectivity) on telecommunications sovereignty for 2026-2030 in March, and the ATU ministerial meeting [adopted the Abuja Declaration on Meaningful Connectivity](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/27/atu-conference-adopts-abuja-declaration-to-advance-meaningful-connectivity-across-africa/) on 2026-07-24, committing member states to technology-neutral regulation, fair access to spectrum and fibre, shared infrastructure and data governance. The text of neither is published, and the Abuja text was sought and not obtained.

Under the AfCFTA, state parties [adopted eight calls to action](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/african-nations-recommit-to-operationalize-afcfta-digital-single-market-framework) at Lagos on 2026-07-07 to expedite ratification of the Protocol on Digital Trade. No ratification count is published with them, and an August account of implementation reports that [the protocol's eight annexes, approved in February 2025, and its intellectual-property annexes, adopted in February 2026, all still await national ratification and incorporation](https://srnnews.com/the-media-line-africa-has-a-free-trade-deal-now-comes-the-hard-part/). Accession to the agreement itself runs well ahead of accession to its digital provisions: the same account puts [49 of 54 signatories as having deposited instruments of ratification by July 2026](https://srnnews.com/the-media-line-africa-has-a-free-trade-deal-now-comes-the-hard-part/), and the Secretariat closed a visit to Mogadishu in August having agreed that [Somalia will become the 50th state party on depositing in September 2026](https://au-afcfta.org/2026/08/from-ratification-to-the-first-shipment-the-afcfta-secretary-general-in-mogadishu/), on a sequence running from ratification through domesticating the electronic tariff book and configuring preferential tariffs in customs to a first shipment.

The Union's non-binding data instrument acquired a rough measure of its reach. An August analysis [puts about a third of the 54 member states aligned to the AU Data Policy Framework, and records 25 national regulatory sandboxes across 15 countries by late 2024](https://dial.global/good-ai-needs-good-data-across-africa-that-foundation-is-already-being-built/). The alignment figure is that analyst's estimate rather than a Union count, and the framework carries no compliance mechanism, reporting cycle or enforcement route.

PAPSS moved most. The Bank of Central African States [acceded on 2026-07-11](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/09/beac-joins-papss-to-strengthen-cross-border-payments-in-central-africa/), extending a rail whose cores were West and East Africa into a third region; Kenya's Pesalink [joined as a connectivity provider](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/), the first national switch wired in for transaction termination; and a wallet-based Nigeria-to-Ghana rail [settling fully in naira](https://itweb.africa/article/papss-onafriq-launch-new-trade-rail-for-west-africa/G98YdqLGPX5MX2PD) opened with the central bank's approval. PAPSS is cited at more than 160 banks and 400 million accounts across 19 countries. What the rail does not fix is the currency layer beneath it: AfricaNenda's deputy chief executive [puts more than 40 African currencies, many not directly convertible, as the constraint that payment rails alone cannot lift, with connected regional systems able to reach more than 60% of African countries only if regulators harmonise licensing and settlement](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/17/africa-is-building-a-single-payments-market-but-its-currencies-remain-divided/), and sets the cost of sending money within Africa at 8.78% against an SDG target of 3% — a figure given in interview, with no corridor set or methodology behind it. The commercial answer to the same problem arrived a fortnight earlier and routes around the continental rail entirely: a pan-African payments operator [tied up with a US infrastructure provider to give African businesses dedicated dollar accounts on ACH, Fedwire, RTP and SWIFT with stablecoin conversion](https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/13/flutterwave-and-caliza-partner-to-expand-global-payment-access-for-african-businesses/), aimed at transfers above US$1m and stated as cutting reliance on correspondent banking.

Cross-border data exchange advanced twice over. Smart Africa [took SADX into a technical mission at Kigali](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/5165/) with Rwanda, Benin and Ghana in April 2026, on trust chains, interface contracts and incident response. The AfCFTA Secretariat's ADAPT, a system of the same purpose, [was launched five days after SADX was announced](https://data-landscapers.com/2026/05/28/building-same-road-twice/) in November 2025, on Data Landscapers' account. Neither has a live exchange on the record.

The one continental system that entered service is Africa CDC's [Central Data Repository](https://africacdc.org/news-item/africa-cdc-establishes-central-data-repository-to-strengthen-public-health-surveillance/), launched on 2026-01-28, a federated store of national surveillance, laboratory and programme data in which member states retain ownership of their data. A second, smaller one arrived in August: a university research network [launched an open-source analyser comparing identity and data-protection laws across more than 50 African countries](https://www.africa.engineering.cmu.edu/news/2026/08/11-demystifying-african-id.html), built as a retrieval-augmented chatbot and stated as intended to help states harmonise their frameworks. No usage figure, governance arrangement or maintenance commitment accompanies it. A third was announced rather than launched: an enterprise AI vendor and an edge data-centre operator [said they would sell AI processing units hosted inside the operator's African sites as a sovereign AI and cloud offering](https://techeconomy.ng/ai-analytics-intelligence-partners-with-oadc-to-advance-cloud-solutions), with financial institutions named as the first market. No site list, capacity, price or launch date is published, so the sovereignty claim rests on hosting location alone.

What those systems run against was measured twice this window by parties with an interest in the answer. Alongside the reported-loss figures collected from member countries, a security vendor [put African organisations at 3,237 attacks a week in July against a global average of 2,336, with ransomware victims up 87% year on year and the continent's phishing rate the world's highest at 1 in 106 emails](https://techtrends.africa/ransomware-attacks-double-year-over-year/) — Angola highest of the markets it broke out at 5,714, then Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa. It is one vendor's telemetry from its own installed base, on an undisclosed sample and denominator, and it is a different series from the loss figures, not a check on them.

The layer's own use figures were restated rather than measured afresh: an industry association's data, cited on 2026-08-12, [puts 416 million Africans on mobile internet in 2025 generating US$240bn of economic value, or 7.8% of continental GDP](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/tech/empreinte-numerique-ce-que-nos-usages-en-ligne-revelent-de-nous). The value is a modelled figure of the association's own, reaching the base through a secondary account.

The continental layer's financing did not move. The Africa AI Fund announced at Kigali in April 2025 [is still a US$60bn figure](https://restofworld.org/2026/africa-ai-sovereignty-big-tech/) with no capitalisation, governance or disbursement on the record.

Consolidation, not fundraising, is where the money went. A trade researcher [counted 84 mergers and acquisitions in Africa's digital economy worth an estimated US$11.4bn in disclosed value between 1 January and 17 August 2026, past the 68 recorded across the whole of 2025](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/24/84-ma-deals-worth-11-4-billion-in-disclosed-value-recorded-so-far-in-2026/), led by financial services on 27 deals and by targets in South Africa, Nigeria and Egypt, with [a mobile group's proposed US$6.2bn purchase of 75 per cent of a tower company the largest single disclosed value](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/24/84-ma-deals-worth-11-4-billion-in-disclosed-value-recorded-so-far-in-2026/). It is a research count rather than a regulatory or exchange series, disclosed value only, and it stands against [US$1.44bn raised by startups in the first half of the year on the same publisher's figures](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/24/84-ma-deals-worth-11-4-billion-in-disclosed-value-recorded-so-far-in-2026/) — the exit route now larger than the entry one.

What raised money in August raised it on markets rather than from pledges. A continental infrastructure financier [issued a five-year digital bond of CHF 350m, listed, traded and settled on a regulated digital exchange and stated as the first by an African institution, at a 1.4925% coupon and arranged by two European banks](https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/13/africa-finance-corporation-breaks-new-ground-with-chf350m-digital-bond/). Alongside it, a British development finance institution and a continental infrastructure fund [announced a strengthened partnership to scale infrastructure financing, connectivity among the named sectors](https://techafricanews.com/2026/08/13/bii-and-africa50-strengthen-partnership-to-scale-infrastructure-financing-across-africa/) — with no commitment amount, fund vehicle, country allocation or timetable, so no finance record was built for it.

Private money moved on narrower objects. A private-capital fund [put US$12m into a provider of application-to-person messaging operating across 35 African countries through 15 operator partnerships](https://www.africaprivateequitynews.com/p/bluepeak-private-capital-fund-ii) — one-time passwords, transaction alerts and delivery updates, which is to say the rails a great deal of the continent's payment estate actually runs on. Philanthropic and corporate money went to language: [26 projects covering more than 50 African languages, dialects and sign languages across 47 countries were selected on 14 August, each awardee eligible for up to US$250,000 in cash and US$400,000 in compute credits](https://iafrica.com/lingua-africa-funds-26-projects-covering-50-african-languages-tied-to-health-farming-and-justice-use-cases/), against health, agriculture and justice use cases. No single total is published for it, so the programme can only be bounded from its per-award ceiling.

The accelerator layer published a claim rather than a commitment: a technology company said startups from its African programmes [had raised more than US$1bn cumulatively since 2017, at a survival rate it puts above 90%](https://techeconomy.ng/google-backed-startups-hit-1bn-funding-milestone-with-90-survival-rate). It is the company's own figure, with no cohort list, no definition of survival and no source for the failure rate it is set against.

The development financiers took equity rather than lending in one case: the World Bank Group's private-sector arm [invested US$25m of equity in a listed pan-African e-commerce platform operating in eight countries](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/world-bank-group-invests-in-africa-s-digital-commerce-infrastructure-to-support-jobs-and-small-businesses), inside a wider US$50m raise. The stated development case — [about 60,000 active sellers, around 1,800 direct jobs and income for more than 100,000 independent sales agents](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/world-bank-group-invests-in-africa-s-digital-commerce-infrastructure-to-support-jobs-and-small-businesses) — is the investor's own projection, and carries no date on which it is to be tested.

One call opened at the end of the window: a private foundation [offered up to US$500,000 a project for artificial intelligence widening access to digital contraception information across sub-Saharan Africa, closing on 25 August](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/breves/breves-une/la-fondation-gates-offre-jusqu-a-500-000-pour-l-utilisation-de-l-ia-dans-le-planning-familial). No envelope total, grant count or decision date accompanies it, so the ceiling is the only figure the call can be bounded by.

The Union's own finances bound what it can carry: members fund [about 22 to 25 per cent of its budget](https://www.theafricareport.com/419860/afdb-says-africa-must-fund-its-own-growth-as-aid-model-frays/) a decade after the 2016 Kigali Decision, and its institutional systems are built with World Bank money — a tender of 2026-07-27 for wireless infrastructure at its headquarters [runs under the BIASHARA 2063 project](https://au.int/en/bid/supply-and-installation-wireless-infrastructure-solution-african-union-commission-headquarters), whose envelope and results framework are not held. Against that, the AfCFTA Secretariat [puts the requirement for digital trade at more than 700 data centres](https://isds.bilaterals.org/africa-s-digital-trade-push) against about 220 on the continent, on its own estimate.
