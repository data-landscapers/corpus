# Mauritius: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-19

*Compiled 2026-08-19 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 70 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 39 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-19, 14 did not, 8 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 67 sources for this place in the window — 13 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-03), 54 in the later (2026-04 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The comparison below rests mostly on its later half: of 52 sources for Mauritius in this window, 12 fall before March 2026 and 40 after, so this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label.

What it does establish is a year in which policy and law moved a great deal and delivery figures did not move at all. A [national data strategy](https://lexpress.mu/node/553938), a [national AI strategy](https://lexpress.mu/node/556638), a [fintech strategy](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/mauritius-unveils-national-fintech-strategy-to-modernize-payments), a [critical-infrastructure cyber regime](https://lexpress.mu/node/558304), [regulations on data protection officers](https://lexpress.mu/s/protection-des-donnees-encadrement-renforce-du-role-des-data-protection-officers-559111) and a [119-page omnibus amending 58 Acts](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) all arrived inside it. Over the same twelve months the identity wallet published no user figure, the payment switch published no volume, and the interoperability framework remained the one written in 2002.

The one measured movement is somebody else's: a bank's [retail payment figures](https://lexpress.mu/node/553532). The one position that did not move and should have is the [state's own interception conduct](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/in-the-shadows-of-mass-surveillance/), unreformed at both ends of the window.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Hosting gained an anchor rather than capacity. The estate was ten facilities at the start of the window and is ten at the end; what changed is that the [Rose-Belle facility was certified Tier IV in December 2025](https://lexpress.mu/node/552966) and took on e-health, the government portal, the identity wallet and government artificial-intelligence workloads, giving the ageing central government centre a disaster-recovery pair it did not have.

Connectivity moved in commitments, not in cable. [LION and LION2 carried most international traffic at both ends of the period](https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/) and SAFE still retires around 2027. Against that the year produced a [prime-ministerial commitment to the America-India Connect system](https://mitci.govmu.org/mitci/mauritius-to-join-america-india-connect-subsea-cable-programme-announces-pm-ramgoolam/) with a non-disclosure agreement signed and terms unfinished, and [two operator-named landings](https://lexpress.mu/node/553579) with no contract. Nothing new is in service.

Cybersecurity is where the year's clearest advance sits. At the start of the period the country could punish cybercrime and had no framework to designate and regulate operators of critical systems; the [designation regulations came into force on 1 June 2026](https://lexpress.mu/node/558304) with [penalties up to 25 years and Rs 2m](https://itweb.africa/article/mauritius-sets-cyber-laws-framework/JN1gP7OAEZYqjL6m), and the [July omnibus would add a compulsory central-bank threat-sharing platform](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf). Both are twelve-month-old positions that did not exist a year ago.

Satellite is the row that stayed still: the [authorisation regime was in force at both ends](https://www.icta.mu/documents/2025/05/decision_authorising_internet_through_non_geostationary_satellite_orbit.pdf) and no service has been authorised under it.

The foundational layers did not move, and the absence of movement is an absence of published figures rather than of activity. The [identity wallet](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202409/mauritius-launches-digital-id-wallet-with-thales-tech-for-govt-service-access) carried no user figure at the start of the period and carries none at the end; the [payment switch](https://www.africanenda.org/uploads/files/siips_2024_MauCas_CaseStudy_en.pdf) published no national volume at either end; the [interoperability framework](https://cib.govmu.org/Documents/Maintenance%20Agreement/egiframework.pdf) is still the 2002 one and the [health strategy still the 2015 one](https://health.govmu.org/health/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/eHealth-Strategy-Seamless-Continuity-of-Care-2015.pdf).

What did move is governance around them. The fintech committee went from not existing to [statutory form from 1 September 2026](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) in a single month, on the back of a [strategy published in June](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/mauritius-unveils-national-fintech-strategy-to-modernize-payments). A digital travel authorisation, a statutory electronic licensing channel for clinical trials and [wider statutory data-sharing by the statistics office](https://lexpress.mu/node/560855) all appeared inside the window and none is enacted.

The welfare system began its digitisation after thirteen years: [10.5m documents are being scanned](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/public-management/mauritius-bets-on-digital-transformation-to-improve-welfare-system-efficiency) into a platform serving about 800,000 payments a month, with integration stated at about eighteen months from May 2026.

The only measured movement in this section belongs to a commercial bank: [cash fell from 30% to 27% of consumer spend and QR payments rose 67%](https://lexpress.mu/node/553532) across 2025. Cross-border, the period added two announcements and no established corridor — a [Seychelles link announced without corroboration](https://platformafrica.com/2026/07/16/from-connectivity-to-intelligence-how-mauritius-telecom-is-building-the-intelligent-layer-of-everyday-mauritius/), and [regional settlement participation reported by another country's central bank](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/currency/7976492/southern-african-countries-consider-joining-sadc-rtgs).

<!-- narrative: governance -->
This is the section that moved most, and the movement is asymmetric. The data-protection regime advanced on three fronts inside the window: [enforcement volume became public](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/world-bank-african-dpas-outline-formula) at about 3,000 actions a year, [European adequacy became a stated pursuit](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/theres-no-digital-id-without-data-protection-mauritius-privacy-chief), and [regulations on data protection officers were promulgated](https://lexpress.mu/s/protection-des-donnees-encadrement-renforce-du-role-des-data-protection-officers-559111) for entry into force in January 2027. A [national data strategy](https://lexpress.mu/node/553938) that did not exist a year ago was launched in January 2026.

The Act those instruments hang off did not move. The [2017 statute is in force at both ends](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf), amendments to it were committed in July with no text and no date, and it is not among the 58 Acts the year's omnibus amends — so the period's expanded inter-agency data sharing arrives against an unchanged safeguards Act.

Neither did the state's own conduct. The [interception apparatus described in Parliament as running since 2017 without judicial oversight](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/in-the-shadows-of-mass-surveillance/) is at the same position at both ends of the window: no reform instrument, no inquiry outcome, no judicial-authorisation requirement, and [leaked recordings still testing the framework](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/can-mauritians-still-trust-the-privacy-of-their-communications/).

Two commitments were made and not delivered inside the window: a [Freedom of Information Bill announced in January](https://lexpress.mu/node/553938) and [Electronic Transactions Act amendments referred to in July](https://lexpress.mu/s/face-aux-cyberattaques-le-secteur-postal-prepare-sa-riposte-560993). And one debate opened that had no earlier position at all: [consultations on verifying social-media identity](https://lexpress.mu/node/558887), on 2,300 online-harm cases in the first half of 2026.

A year ago Mauritius had no artificial-intelligence position on the record. It now has a [strategy with guidelines that govern by impact rather than geography](https://lexpress.mu/node/556638), a [legal frame in the form of an incentive scheme and an immigration category](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf), a [live model registry](https://journaldudimanche.com/intelligence-artificielle-mauritius-telecom-devoile-airegistry-mu/), an [enterprise platform whose school pilot has already been narrowed](https://platformafrica.com/2026/07/16/from-connectivity-to-intelligence-how-mauritius-telecom-is-building-the-intelligent-layer-of-everyday-mauritius/), and a [certification work-stream it co-chairs](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/ai/mauritius-telecom-and-tm-forum-pioneer-trusted-model-as-a-service-for-the-ai-native-telco-55907/). That is a complete policy stack built in twelve months.

What it does not have, at either end of the period, is a measure. The compute the stack assumes is an operator claim, and the [study that would establish how many Mauritian firms use artificial intelligence was commissioned in July 2026](https://www.lemauricien.com/le-mauricien/mcci-une-etude-de-marche-sur-ladoption-de-lia-par-les-entreprises/713704/) — by a chamber of commerce, after the strategy and after the budget. A [domestic critique published in March](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/why-mauritius-must-build-an-ai-ecosystem-before-an-ai-infrastructure/) argued the sequencing was the wrong way round.

The hyperscaler relationship deepened without becoming capacity: an [Amazon memorandum with no disclosed value](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6321/mauritius-telecoms-allmyt-summit-2026-brings-together-digital-leaders-to-drive-innovation), and the establishment that [Google's ground station at Baie-du-Jacotet already existed](https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/).
