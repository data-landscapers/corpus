# Mauritius: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/MUS/ledger.csv` (65 systems and instruments, 9 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Mauritius has an unusually complete digital policy stack and a thin published record of what it delivers. A [national AI strategy](https://lexpress.mu/node/556638), a [national data strategy](https://lexpress.mu/node/553938), a [fintech strategy](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/mauritius-unveils-national-fintech-strategy-to-modernize-payments), a [critical-infrastructure cyber regime in force](https://itweb.africa/article/mauritius-sets-cyber-laws-framework/JN1gP7OAEZYqjL6m) and a [119-page omnibus amending 58 Acts](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) all sit on the record; no user figure has been published for the Mobile ID wallet since it launched in 2024, and none for the national payment switch at all.

The data-protection regime is genuinely strong — [about 3,000 enforcement actions a year](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/world-bank-african-dpas-outline-formula) and an [EU adequacy bid](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/theres-no-digital-id-without-data-protection-mauritius-privacy-chief) — and sits alongside a [state interception apparatus described in Parliament as running since 2017 without judicial oversight](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/in-the-shadows-of-mass-surveillance/), for which no reform instrument has been published. The year's legislative volume arrived as a budget instrument, and the Data Protection Act 2017 is not among the Acts it amends.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Hosting is the country's strongest asset. [Ten data centres operate across five locations](https://www.telecomreviewafrica.com/articles/features/12397-sublime-seas-to-storage-sphere-mauritius-expands-hyperscale-data-hub/), and the [Rose-Belle Tier IV facility](https://lexpress.mu/node/552966) is the sovereign-hosting anchor: it carries e-health, the government portal, the Mobile ID wallet, the penalty-points system and government artificial-intelligence workloads, and gives the ageing Government Online Centre the disaster recovery it lacks.

International capacity is ageing and its replacement is not contracted. [LION and LION2 still carry most international traffic](https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/), and SAFE retires around 2027. Against that sits a [prime-ministerial commitment to join the America-India Connect system](https://mitci.govmu.org/mitci/mauritius-to-join-america-india-connect-subsea-cable-programme-announces-pm-ramgoolam/), behind which is a signed non-disclosure agreement and unfinished negotiation, and [two operator-planned landings](https://lexpress.mu/node/553579) with no contract.

Satellite is regulated but not authorised. The [2025 decision on non-geostationary broadband](https://www.icta.mu/documents/2025/05/decision_authorising_internet_through_non_geostationary_satellite_orbit.pdf) requires service through a locally licensed provider, a local point of presence with Mauritian-geolocated addresses, real-time terminal dashboards and provider-retained terminal ownership, and no service has been authorised under it. Domestic opinion is [divided on whether it matters](https://newsmoris.com/starlink-expands-to-30-african-markets-sparking-debate-over-mauritius-digital-future/) given existing coverage.

Cybersecurity is where 2026's law took effect. The [critical-infrastructure designation regime came into force on 1 June 2026](https://lexpress.mu/node/558304) with a twelve-month compliance window and [penalties up to 25 years' imprisonment and Rs 2m](https://itweb.africa/article/mauritius-sets-cyber-laws-framework/JN1gP7OAEZYqjL6m); before it, no framework existed to designate and regulate operators of critical systems. Beside it, the July omnibus would let the [central bank compel financial institutions, and others it specifies, into a threat-sharing platform](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) — with the perimeter set by the central bank rather than the cyber authority.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Identity is delivered and unmeasured. The [Mobile ID wallet launched on 4 September 2024](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202409/mauritius-launches-digital-id-wallet-with-thales-tech-for-govt-service-access), billed as the first African wallet built to the international standard, on the [legal basis of regulations made the same year](https://mnis.govmu.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/28_The-NIC_Mobile-ID_Reg-2024-.pdf); no user figure has been published in the two years since. It sits on a [complete civil-registration base](https://csd.govmu.org/DocumentsList/FINALISED%20ANNUAL%20REPORT%20CSD.pdf), which is what makes it unusual: most states here are building register and credential at once.

Payments are the same pattern. The [MauCAS switch is a mature interoperable rail](https://www.africanenda.org/uploads/files/siips_2024_MauCas_CaseStudy_en.pdf) publishing no national transaction volume; the adoption evidence comes from one bank, which [reported QR payments up 67% in 2025 and cash falling from 30% to 27% of consumer spend](https://lexpress.mu/node/553532). Around that rail the state is building governance: a [fintech strategy of June 2026](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/mauritius-unveils-national-fintech-strategy-to-modernize-payments) and a [governance committee given statutory form from 1 September 2026](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf), minister-chaired and expressly not a body corporate.

The cross-border ambition runs ahead of the corroboration. The [India payment link of February 2024](https://lexpress.mu/node/554033) is the one established corridor; a [Seychelles corridor was announced as launched](https://platformafrica.com/2026/07/16/from-connectivity-to-intelligence-how-mauritius-telecom-is-building-the-intelligent-layer-of-everyday-mauritius/) with no corroboration from the partner or either central bank, and Mauritian participation in the [Southern African settlement system reaches the record through a South African spokesperson](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/currency/7976492/southern-african-countries-consider-joining-sadc-rtgs) rather than through the Bank of Mauritius.

The welfare state is being digitised in bulk: [10.5m benefit documents scanned](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/public-management/mauritius-bets-on-digital-transformation-to-improve-welfare-system-efficiency) into a future platform serving about 800,000 payments a month.

The plumbing under all of it is old: the standing interoperability instrument is [a framework published in 2002](https://cib.govmu.org/Documents/Maintenance%20Agreement/egiframework.pdf) and the standing health strategy [one from 2015](https://health.govmu.org/health/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/eHealth-Strategy-Seamless-Continuity-of-Care-2015.pdf), neither with a published successor.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The data-protection regime is among the most active on the continent: [about 3,000 enforcement actions a year, 99% resolved without proceedings](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/world-bank-african-dpas-outline-formula), mandated chief data officers and administrative fines being added; [regulations promulgated on 12 June 2026](https://lexpress.mu/s/protection-des-donnees-encadrement-renforce-du-role-des-data-protection-officers-559111) formalise data protection officers from 1 January 2027; and the commissioner has confirmed the country is [pursuing European adequacy](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/theres-no-digital-id-without-data-protection-mauritius-privacy-chief).

Against that sits the conduct of the state itself. [Parliamentary revelations describe large-scale interception since 2017](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/in-the-shadows-of-mass-surveillance/) — calls, internet traffic and messaging platforms — wired into major operators, accessed by counter-terrorism and national security units without judicial oversight, with the Prime Minister confirming illegal wiretapping. No reform instrument or judicial-authorisation requirement has been published since, and [leaked recordings continue to test the framework](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/can-mauritians-still-trust-the-privacy-of-their-communications/).

The year's legislation arrived as a budget instrument. The [omnibus introduced on 24 July 2026](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) amends 58 Acts and repeals one, on a staged commencement from 1 September 2026, carrying the fintech committee, the AI City Scheme, the threat-sharing platform, a digital travel authorisation and a virtual-asset solicitation rule that presumes any publicly accessible communication targets Mauritius. Its companion [Finance Bill widens what the statistics office may share with the finance ministry](https://lexpress.mu/node/560855). The Data Protection Act is not among the Acts amended.

Two commitments stand unfulfilled, neither with a text or a date: a [Freedom of Information Bill announced in January 2026](https://lexpress.mu/node/553938), and a [National Data Governance Strategy with Data Protection Act amendments committed in July](https://mitci.govmu.org/mitci/mauritius-to-join-america-india-connect-subsea-cable-programme-announces-pm-ramgoolam/).

The sharpest domestic objection is to the priority rather than the substance: a [named analyst argues that contract enforcement, not technology, is what limits growth](https://lexpress.mu/s/mauritius-needs-faster-justice-not-just-ai-560867), prescribing digitalised case management alongside a small-claims process.

<!-- narrative: ai-tech -->
Mauritius put governance before deployment. The [national strategy and its guidelines launched on 9 April 2026](https://lexpress.mu/node/556638) govern by impact rather than geography: vendor-neutral, border-agnostic, with mandatory bias audits in financial services and gaming and duties on foreign providers to keep a local representative. They are [non-binding and read as a third model](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/23/mauritius-new-ai-strategy-sets-a-new-bar-ethics-before-execution/) beside the scale-first and penalty-first approaches elsewhere.

What the state operator has built is a platform layer rather than a compute one. The [AI Registry went live on 9 July 2026](https://journaldudimanche.com/intelligence-artificielle-mauritius-telecom-devoile-airegistry-mu/) as a directory of Mauritius-relevant models, agents and skills that does not host or sell what it lists, its stack open-sourced. The [enterprise platform's school pilot was deliberately narrowed](https://platformafrica.com/2026/07/16/from-connectivity-to-intelligence-how-mauritius-telecom-is-building-the-intelligent-layer-of-everyday-mauritius/) from a student tutor to a teacher assistant after teacher and parent resistance — a rare documented retreat on pedagogical rather than technical grounds. The [certification work the operator co-chairs](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/ai/mauritius-telecom-and-tm-forum-pioneer-trusted-model-as-a-service-for-the-ai-native-telco-55907/) is defining a framework that does not yet exist.

The compute underneath is an assertion: installed accelerators are the operator's own claim, and a [domestic critique argues the infrastructure-first sequencing risks obsolescence on delivery](https://www.mauritiustimes.com/mt/why-mauritius-must-build-an-ai-ecosystem-before-an-ai-infrastructure/).

The legal frame is an incentive scheme, not a governance one: the [AI City Scheme and an AI Founder permit at US$1m invested](https://maurice-info.mu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Economic-and-Financial-Measures-Miscellaneous-Provisions-Bill.pdf) certify a hub and expedite immigration. And the country has [no measured baseline of enterprise adoption](https://www.lemauricien.com/le-mauricien/mcci-une-etude-de-marche-sur-ladoption-de-lia-par-les-entreprises/713704/) — commissioned by a chamber of commerce in July 2026, after the strategy and after the budget. Information technology contributes [about 5.4% of gross domestic product against an ambition of 10-12%](https://itweb.africa/article/mauritius-launches-ai-readiness-survey/GxwQDM1DPZj7lPVo).

What is contracted with the hyperscalers is skills and confidentiality: the [Amazon memorandum carries no disclosed value and no binding deliverable](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6321/mauritius-telecoms-allmyt-summit-2026-brings-together-digital-leaders-to-drive-innovation), its training running initially in rented premises, and [Google already operates a ground station at Baie-du-Jacotet](https://journaldudimanche.com/google-amazon-et-maurice-les-coulisses-dun-tournant-numerique-majeur/).

The 2026-2027 budget sets the year's clearest training commitment: a [50,000-trainee target](https://lexpress.mu/node/559426) for artificial intelligence skills — 25,000 professionals, 12,000 pupils, 8,000 teachers and 5,000 civil servants in one year — with no published enrolment figure and no measure of the base it starts from.

Inclusion has one named sub-national commitment: a [ministerial mission to Rodrigues in May 2026](https://lexpress.mu/node/558566) set a cybersecurity working framework, a youth awareness project, and integration of the national sign-on through post office agents.

<!-- narrative: finance -->
The 2026-2027 budget is the year's clearest financial commitment to the digital programme: it names [artificial intelligence 35 times](https://lexpress.mu/node/559426), funding a Rs 25m national AI learning platform, an investment tax credit of 15% a year over three years, a special economic zone of 83 arpents with preferential data-centre power, e-gates, e-visa and e-judiciary, and a Rs 13m cyber forensic laboratory.

External financing is old: the largest digital-sector commitment on record is a [US$73.7m Chinese preferential buyer's credit from 2018](http://mof.govmu.org/English/PublishingImages/2019/MOFED%20Annual%20Report%20-%20FY%202017-18.pdf), with no repayment or completion statement published since. The commercial money movement runs the other way: one payments gateway [reports its Mauritian volume up over 300% year on year](https://itweb.africa/article/mauritius-emerges-as-an-african-payments-hub/), crediting the country's exit from the financial action task force grey list.

<!-- narrative: gaps -->
Six are subject areas with nothing on record: technical capacity to run the estate, satellite data, rural data capture, local-government digitalisation, Chinese cooperation, and any measure of digital literacy.

The other three sit under things the country announces confidently: what compute the state operator holds, how many Mauritian firms use artificial intelligence, and what the budget's tax and revenue measures say — the Finance Bill being a browser-only download that three automated fetch paths could not retrieve.
