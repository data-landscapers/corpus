# Rwanda: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/RWA/ledger.csv` (89 systems and instruments, 9 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Rwanda runs one national instant payment rail carrying every domestic interoperable retail payment at a maximum of RWF 20 between institutions, a biometric identity programme [4.4 million enrolments into a roughly 14 million target](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/rwanda-accelerates-biometric-enrollment-ahead-of-digital-id-switch), and a data-protection law in force since 2021 that has never produced a published enforcement action or register of controllers. Third-generation networks are dated for [nationwide switch-off on 30 June 2027](https://www.minict.gov.rw/news-detail/rwanda-to-switch-off-3g-networks-on-30-june-2027). Not one new Rwandan statute reached the books in the twelve months the base can evidence, and the domestically financed share of the ministry's development programme fell for a third consecutive year while its externally financed share rose to [RWF 65.8bn](https://www.minecofin.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minecofin/Publications/REPORTS/National_Budget/Annual_State_Finance_Laws/2026_to_2027_Original_Annual_Finance_Law/2026-2027_Original_Annual_Finance_Law.pdf).

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
The ministry [fixed 30 June 2027 for the third-generation shutdown](https://www.minict.gov.rw/news-detail/rwanda-to-switch-off-3g-networks-on-30-june-2027) on 28 July 2026, with operator pilot shutdowns during 2026, and named December 2028 as a working target for the older network — expressly conditional on coverage, voice reliability, mobile money continuity, dependent-system migration and affordable handsets. A government-commissioned study found more than two million people could still be on that network in 2030 without a planned transition; the study is unpublished, so its method is unestablished. Voice over fourth-generation networks, the technical precondition, switched on days later, with no coverage or handset-base figure published.

Fifth-generation service launched in June 2025 with no site or subscriber figure since, and the most recent internet-penetration figure is a projection for end-2025 rather than a measurement. Regulator statistics for the first quarter of 2026 give 14,001,080 mobile-cellular subscriptions at 97.1 per 100 inhabitants and 10,738,906 internet subscriptions. The largest connectivity instrument, a US$200m acceleration project, was last reported 55% complete in July 2025 and has published no figure since against a December 2026 closing date.

A hyperscaler hybrid-cloud deployment went live in April 2026, with deployment count and host facilities undisclosed — the only onshore compute named. The ministry's sector plan records a [50% hosting-price cut at the national data centre and 14.9% of 4,660 government websites and web applications hosted in the country](https://www.minict.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/minict_user_upload/Documents/Strategies/ICT__SSP_2024-2029_.pdf), and dates the data centre and cloud service directives to 2023. The national incident response team's [profile of 30 June 2022](https://cyber.gov.rw/index.php?eID=dumpFile&f=328&t=f&token=9b6ee1f7301ba76a4b9ee014ddb16fa8607782cf) gives its constituency and its statutory basis in the 2017 authority law, and publishes no caseload, resolution time or service-level target.

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Effective 14 July 2026 the central bank [migrated all domestic interoperable retail payments onto one state-owned open-source rail](https://itweb.africa/article/rwanda-migrates-retail-payments-to-ekash/rxP3jqBEnOoMA2ye) by directive, connecting 22 institutions; a bank-to-wallet transfer that could previously cost as much as RWF 5,000 now costs [at most RWF 20](https://taarifa.rw/2026/08/02/mtn-mobile-money-faces-scrutiny-over-fees-as-rwandas-digital-economy-expands/), about one US cent — a maximum that applies between institutions, with each operator's own tariff on transfers inside its network unchanged. Reconcile settled the point on 2026-08-09: the banking industry's own public notice of 14 July 2026 puts it as a maximum fee of RWF 20 with institutions free to charge less or nothing, on every transfer between banks and electronic money issuers, so it is a ceiling on interoperability rather than a flat retail price. **The instrument behind it is not held.** BNR Directive No. 45/2026 is absent from the central bank's document library as enumerated on 2026-08-09 (493 documents), its issue date is given variously as 9 January and 9 June 2026, and nothing on file states what governs on-net tariffs if the ceiling does not. A bank and a mobile-money operator launched [instant loans of up to RWF 2,000,000 and wallet saving over a short code](https://taarifa.rw/2026/08/02/bpr-bank-rwanda-and-mtn-momo-rwanda-launch-mofaya-digital-loan-and-savings-solution) on 2 August 2026, with no pricing, interest rate or eligibility rule published. The physical layer moved the other way: the second operator's market share fell from 16.9% to 14.3% and its active agents from 53,316 to 48,847 over the same year, in the cash-in and cash-out network the new rail does not touch.

Identity is enrolling against a hard deadline. Nationwide registration opened on 7 August 2025 and reached 4.4 million by June 2026; no figure has been published since, in the run-up to a June 2027 sunset after which legacy cards become invalid and banks, hospitals, telecoms operators and government institutions are expected to use the new authentication. The consent and access-log layer on which the privacy design rests has no go-live date, no live status and no user figure.

The exchange layer is the gap the country's own analysts name most often. A national data-sharing policy was approved in May 2025 and its governance unit is now staffed, but the platform itself has no go-live announcement with its 2026 target year eight months gone. The stack is being sold abroad: [two neighbours have adopted the electronic billing machine](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/rwanda-turns-digital-public-infrastructure-into-a-continental-growth-strategy), a third is mid-adoption, and one state has taken the electronic procurement platform and is co-developing it — with no revenue, contract value or licensing terms disclosed for any adoption.

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The data-protection law has been in its enforcement phase since October 2023, carries extraterritorial reach and fines to RWF 5m or 1% of global turnover, and requires the supervisory authority to authorise every transfer of personal data out of the country. In five years it has produced [no published enforcement action, fine total, breach register or count of registered controllers](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/32861/opinions/why-2026-is-the-year-of-accountability-for-data-controllers-and-processors). Its appropriation is [RWF 130m for 2026/27](https://www.minecofin.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minecofin/Publications/REPORTS/National_Budget/Annual_State_Finance_Laws/2026_to_2027_Original_Annual_Finance_Law/2026-2027_Original_Annual_Finance_Law.pdf), flat and down from RWF 150m before that, of which the 2024/25 vote was spent at 35.1%, all in one quarter; the national identification programme executed at 140.7% of its appropriation.

The sector's plan for 2024/25 to 2028/29 replaced the 2018 to 2024 plan, with no progress report against its nine outcomes held. A national artificial-intelligence agency was [approved by Cabinet on 8 June 2026](https://www.ktpress.rw/2026/06/rwandas-ai-ambition-takes-shape-as-cabinet-approves-new-agency/) with its structure, operations and timeline not made public and no establishing law held. A virtual-assets bill had its rationale approved on 1 April 2026, with nothing on record since. Rwanda's bilateral health agreements with the United States, and the data-sharing agreements attached to them, are [withheld and in litigation](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/2026.04.02-Complaint.pdf) since 2 April 2026.

A development agency reported in July 2026, from engagements run to March 2026, that Rwandan participants said [no dedicated national organisation conducts risk assessments, evaluations or active monitoring of artificial-intelligence errors and harms](https://www.undp.org/publications/small-states-big-signals-what-adoption-practice-reveals-about-trust-safety-and-ai-performance-globally). No review framework is triggered by a commercial partnership either.

The commercial stack arrived before the institutions meant to govern it. A three-year non-binding memorandum with a frontier artificial-intelligence firm was [signed on 17 February 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-rwanda-mou) covering the health ministry, public-sector developer teams and education; the vendor's own announcement is the only primary held and no value is published. The measurable side is employment and it is genuinely moving off a small base. The information and communication sector [employed 19,000 workers in the second quarter of 2026 against about 12,000 a year earlier](https://www.ktpress.rw/2026/07/why-tech-is-rwandas-fastest-growing-source-of-new-jobs/) — up 62.6% against 2.7% economy-wide, still about 0.4% of total employment, against a target of 50,000 digital jobs by 2029. The fisc moved the other way: the science, technology and innovation appropriation fell 7.3% to RWF 856.6m for 2026/27, all recurrent with no domestically financed development.

The continental instruments Rwanda hosts have not converted into delivery. A declaration adopted in the capital in April 2025 endorsed a council and a US$60bn fund; sixteen months on, neither has a constitution, capitalisation, manager or first disbursement.
