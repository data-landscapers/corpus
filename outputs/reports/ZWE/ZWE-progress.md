# Zimbabwe: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-14

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 170 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 86 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-14, 40 did not, 35 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 128 sources for this place in the window — 26 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 102 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). **The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label**, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The twelve months to 6 August 2026 produced a national artificial-intelligence strategy, a whole-of-government resource planning system and an enforcement date for data protection, and produced no national digital identity. The strategy was launched on 13 March 2026 after a January target slipped ([launch account](https://itweb.africa/article/zimbabwe-unveils-five-year-strategy-for-ai-innovation/PmxVEMKELYkvQY85)); the identity system announced in May 2025 for a July 2025 start has no launch, enrolment or coverage figure on record at either end of the window ([ministerial account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/southerneye/local/article/200055327/minister-denies-id-backlog-amid-growing-public-outcry)).

The clearest measured movement is satellite: terminal subscriptions rose from 40,146 at the second quarter of 2025 to 67,057 at the fourth ([regulator report](https://technomag.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Q4-2025-Abridged-Sector-performance-report-HMed-final-1.pdf)), while members of parliament reported package prices rising from about US$65 to more than US$100 ([National Assembly account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200058417/starlink-kits-gather-dust-at-government-rural-hospitals-as-parent-ministry-dithers-on-subscription)).

Thirty rows carry no stated baseline. Zimbabwe's base is markedly thicker after February 2026 than before it, and the shape line printed above records how far that bears on the comparison.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
The private group restructured rather than the country rebuilding. Passive infrastructure was transferred as a going concern into a separately listed company in December 2025, which began trading by introduction on 31 March 2026, while the parent left the domestic exchange after 27 years ([pre-listing statement](https://ewzinvestor.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/zw-eco-2026-cr-00.pdf)). Ownership and control were unchanged by the transfer.

Measured capacity moved modestly. Equipped international bandwidth rose 6.46% to 1,688,770 Mbps over the fourth quarter of 2025, and fifth-generation base stations from 319 to 379 by the first quarter of 2026 ([regulator report](https://technomag.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Q4-2025-Abridged-Sector-performance-report-HMed-final-1.pdf)). Rural fifth-generation coverage stood at 0.0% throughout.

The one position that regressed is the state fixed operator's balance sheet: government arrears roughly doubled to close to US$42m ([annual general meeting account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/business/article/200057743/govt-debt-to-telone-doubles-to-us42m)). The national fibre backbone figure cannot be stated as a movement: the regulator gives 14,357 km at the fourth quarter of 2025 and two ministers gave more than 14,000 km and more than 17,000 km against a briefing figure of more than 19,000 km, and the accounts do not reconcile ([briefing account](https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200058335/internet-traffic-surges-57)).
