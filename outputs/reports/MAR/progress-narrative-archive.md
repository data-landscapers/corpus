# MAR — archived progress narrative

*Archived by CC on 2026-08-26, before the indicator-framed rewrite of the progress
report removed the per-Level-1 narrative blocks (`documentation/progress-report-redesign.md`
§1, and its review item 4). This is drafted, register-checked, cited prose about the same
evidence the ledger holds; it is the source material for MAR's indicator Developments
drafting pass, not a published document. Block keys are the Level-1 chapter markers the
renderer used.*

## summary

The single largest movement in the twelve months to 6 August 2026 is that fifth-generation mobile service went from a licence tender to 3.2m subscribers. The tender opened on 16 July 2025 and three operators launched commercially on 7 November ([launch account](https://en.hespress.com/125066-what-5g-could-mean-for-moroccos-economy-and-its-people.html)); the [regulator reported 3.2m subscribers, 8.4% of mobile-internet users, by March 2026](https://www.hespress.com/%D8%A5%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82-%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%B3-5g-%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%AE%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%BA-1652175.html).

The second is that Morocco began deploying live facial recognition without a data-protection instrument being made. Patrol vehicles moved from a May 2026 demonstration to field service in Rabat in July ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)), while law 09-08 of 2009 remained the operative statute at both ends of the window and the framework bill drafted to modernise it sat with the general secretariat throughout.

Twenty-five rows did not move at all, and nine carry no stated baseline. Morocco's base is thicker in the later half of the window than the earlier, so the second figure understates continuity rather than overstating change.

## governance

The legislative pipeline filled and did not empty. The electoral organic bill was tabled in October 2025 ([bill text](https://www.chambredesrepresentants.ma/sites/default/files/loi/Projet_loi_53.25_0.pdf)), the framework bill on artificial intelligence and data governance went to the general secretariat in November 2025 ([drafting account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-drafts-landmark-law-to-govern-ai-data-and-digital-identity)), and the digital health bill was adopted in the government council in July 2026 ([bill account](https://medias24.com/2026/07/23/sante-numerique-ce-que-le-projet-de-loi-changer-dans-votre-parcours-de-soins-1728651/)). None is recorded as promulgated, and the private member's bill on regulating artificial intelligence has sat with the chamber's bureau since January 2025 unchanged.

The one governance instrument that both began and completed inside the window is the facial-recognition deployment, which required no instrument: the vehicles went from a demonstration in May 2026 to field service in July ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)).

Two host-country agreements were approved by the council of ministers on 9 April 2026, one for a regional cyber-incident coordination centre and one for a continental development agency office ([programme account](https://assabah.ma/928742.html)). Neither facility is on record as established.

## finance

The financing position advanced by two development-bank pipeline entries and one large programme: the merchant payment acquisition fund entered the pipeline in February 2026 and the insurance inclusion programme in April, each at about US$0.5m ([insurance activity](https://d-portal.iatistandard.org/ctrack.html#view=act&aid=46002-P-MA-H00-029)); the US$250m acceleration programme was approved and signed in June 2026 and had not entered force by 6 August ([ministry account](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/07/330823/morocco-to-launch-250-million-world-bank-backed-digital-transformation-program/)).

Two other financing instruments did not move at all across the window: the memorandum signed in April 2025 between Vodafone Business and Maroc Telecom on smart-city and energy-management solutions has produced no delivery, expansion or sum since ([memorandum account](https://itweb.africa/article/vodafone-maroc-telecom-sign-digital-services-deal/P3gQ2qGAn5W7nRD1)), and the 2020 First Financial and Digital Inclusion Development Policy Financing has no completion or results report on file.

## ict-infrastructure

At the start of the window a competitive tender for fifth-generation licences was open and no network was in service; at the end three were, with 3.2m subscribers reported for March 2026 against 2.63m and 38% outdoor population coverage at the fourth quarter of 2025 ([measurement account](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026)). The same measurement firm records [median speeds falling over the period: the fifth-generation median down 38% from 261.7 Mbps at launch to about 160-165 Mbps at month six, and the fourth-generation median down 28%](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026).

The compute programme was revised downwards inside the window. The consortium announced in June 2025 that phase 1 would launch in the first quarter of 2026 at over 40 MW, with up to 500 MW contracted from a national generator; the ministry's memorandum of April 2026 gives phase 1 as 16 MW at Nouaceur and 36 MW cumulative by end-2027 ([ministry communique](https://techafricanews.com/2026/03/11/ministry-of-digital-transition-and-administration-reform-highlights-ais-role-in-digital-transformation/)). The Q1 2026 launch date passed with no delivery reported.

Two hosting positions did not move: cloud regions announced in May 2024 for Casablanca and Settat are recorded nowhere as having entered service, and the cloud-first policy stands with its 2027 observatory not established.

## dpi

Identity moved by process rather than by scale. The newest issuance figure the base holds is still 4.6m cards for calendar 2024; what changed is that renewal moved online in May 2026, leaving only biometric capture in person ([renewal account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-digitizes-national-id-renewal-process)), and that a national digital wallet was launched into construction in February 2026 on eight partnership conventions ([launch account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-strengthens-e-government-architecture-with-idarati-x-0)).

The passport regime advanced from the 2008 decree to a draft decree approved on 9 July 2026, adding a fourth language, removing the holder's address from the biodata page and abolishing temporary passports ([approval account](https://en.hespress.com/141646-morocco-to-introduce-new-biometric-passport-featuring-amazigh-english-starting-august.html)).

Against these, the registry's own extension target lapsed. The December 2024 operation set at least five programmes served by end-2024 and eight by 2028; the end-2024 date has passed with no delivery or coverage figure held ([approval account](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/12/04/world-bank-greenlights-70m-boost-for-morocco-s-innovative-social-protection-delivery-systems)). The single-window trade platform's last recorded position is 2016.

## technology

Everything in this section postdates 1 August 2025 except an emerging cloud-first posture. The roadmap was first named in March 2026, the two institutes were constituted by founding agreement in November 2025 and March 2026, and the start-up programme with a technology park was announced in April 2026 ([programme account](https://techafricanews.com/2026/04/09/orange-maroc-partners-technopark-to-accelerate-ai-and-digital-transformation/)).

Three of those four positions have not moved since they were announced. The Dakhla institute has no statute, campus, budget or intake on record ([Dakhla agreement](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/3156/morocco-signs-agreements-to-launch-green-data-center-and-ai-institute-in-dakhla)); the start-up programme has no cohort, intake or budget; the roadmap has no published text.

The one measured position in the section is a year stale. Offshoring employment and export revenue stand at 148,500 and MAD 26.2bn for end-2024 at both ends of the window ([agreements account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-signs-three-deals-to-strengthen-digital-offshoring)), against a 2030 target of 270,000 jobs set in agreements signed in January 2026. A regional research and development office was inaugurated at Agadir on 29 June 2026 with no headcount or investment figure disclosed ([inauguration account](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/6122/oracle-inaugurates-regional-rd-office-in-agadir)).

## inclusion

The measured position added inside the window is an operator's own: 6,000 internet boxes refurbished in Morocco in 2025 at a stated EUR 1.5m in savings, with no Morocco figure held for 2024 to compare it against ([operator report](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/telecom/orange-a-reconditionne-164-000-box-internet-en-2025-en-afrique-et-au-moyen-orient)).

## gaps

Thirty rows are ***Not held*** at both ends of the window, and all 30 were searched on 6 August. Twenty-six name published documents, three are searched absences, and one — installed data-centre capacity — returned three incompatible national figures and became a contradiction rather than a gap.

Two of them bear directly on what this report could otherwise have said. The regulator's market observatory is absent from the base for every period, so the subscriber and coverage series this report cites comes from a private measurement firm rather than from the body that licensed the networks. The probe of 6 August found the regulator publishes quarterly observatories to the first quarter of 2026, so this is an intake gap and not a publication one. And no digital-ministry appropriation is held in any finance law, so a [US$250m external programme](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/06/12/two-new-programs-to-advance-morocco-s-digital-transformation-and-climate-resilience) is the largest priced commitment in a country whose own digital spending is nowhere in the base.

The third notable absence is oversight of deployed systems: no data-protection authorisation for the facial-recognition patrols, and no annual report of the data-protection commission at any year. Both were absent before the patrols entered service and remain absent after.
