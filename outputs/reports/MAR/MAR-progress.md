# Morocco: progress report, August 2025 – August 2026

*Of 132 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 69 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-25, 35 did not, 11 carry no stated baseline, and 17 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 140 sources for this place in the window — 36 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-02), 104 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The single largest movement in the twelve months to 6 August 2026 is that fifth-generation mobile service went from a licence tender to 3.2m subscribers. The tender opened on 16 July 2025 and three operators launched commercially on 7 November ([launch account](https://en.hespress.com/125066-what-5g-could-mean-for-moroccos-economy-and-its-people.html)); the [regulator reported 3.2m subscribers, 8.4% of mobile-internet users, by March 2026](https://www.hespress.com/%D8%A5%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82-%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%B3-5g-%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%AE%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%BA-1652175.html).

The second is that Morocco began deploying live facial recognition without a data-protection instrument being made. Patrol vehicles moved from a May 2026 demonstration to field service in Rabat in July ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)), while law 09-08 of 2009 remained the operative statute at both ends of the window and the framework bill drafted to modernise it sat with the general secretariat throughout.

Twenty-five rows did not move at all, and nine carry no stated baseline. Morocco's base is thicker in the later half of the window than the earlier, so the second figure understates continuity rather than overstating change.

The legislative pipeline filled and did not empty. The electoral organic bill was tabled in October 2025 ([bill text](https://www.chambredesrepresentants.ma/sites/default/files/loi/Projet_loi_53.25_0.pdf)), the framework bill on artificial intelligence and data governance went to the general secretariat in November 2025 ([drafting account](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-drafts-landmark-law-to-govern-ai-data-and-digital-identity)), and the digital health bill was adopted in the government council in July 2026 ([bill account](https://medias24.com/2026/07/23/sante-numerique-ce-que-le-projet-de-loi-changer-dans-votre-parcours-de-soins-1728651/)). None is recorded as promulgated, and the private member's bill on regulating artificial intelligence has sat with the chamber's bureau since January 2025 unchanged.

The one governance instrument that both began and completed inside the window is the facial-recognition deployment, which required no instrument: the vehicles went from a demonstration in May 2026 to field service in July ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)).

Two host-country agreements were approved by the council of ministers on 9 April 2026, one for a regional cyber-incident coordination centre and one for a continental development agency office ([programme account](https://assabah.ma/928742.html)). Neither facility is on record as established.

Thirty rows are ***Not held*** at both ends of the window, and all 30 were searched on 6 August. Twenty-six name published documents, three are searched absences, and one — installed data-centre capacity — returned three incompatible national figures and became a contradiction rather than a gap.

Two of them bear directly on what this report could otherwise have said. The regulator's market observatory is absent from the base for every period, so the subscriber and coverage series this report cites comes from a private measurement firm rather than from the body that licensed the networks. The probe of 6 August found the regulator publishes quarterly observatories to the first quarter of 2026, so this is an intake gap and not a publication one. The third notable absence is oversight of deployed systems: no data-protection authorisation for the facial-recognition patrols, and no annual report of the data-protection commission at any year. Both were absent before the patrols entered service and remain absent after.
