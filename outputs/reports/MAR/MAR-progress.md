# Morocco: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-06

*Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 119 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 46 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-06, 37 did not, 8 carry no stated baseline, and 27 are ***Not held*** at both ends. A further 1 took a position dated after 2026-08-06 and are carried in the status report rather than compared here.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 122 sources for this place in the window — 37 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-02), 85 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
The single largest movement in the twelve months to 6 August 2026 is that fifth-generation mobile service went from a licence tender to 3.2m subscribers. The tender opened on 16 July 2025 and three operators launched commercially on 7 November ([launch account](https://en.hespress.com/125066-what-5g-could-mean-for-moroccos-economy-and-its-people.html)); the regulator reported 3.2m subscribers, 8.4% of mobile-internet users, by March 2026.

The second is that Morocco began deploying live facial recognition without a data-protection instrument being made. Patrol vehicles moved from a May 2026 demonstration to field service in Rabat in July ([deployment account](https://assabah.ma/938960.html)), while law 09-08 of 2009 remained the operative statute at both ends of the window and the framework bill drafted to modernise it sat with the general secretariat throughout.

Twenty-five rows did not move at all, and nine carry no stated baseline. Morocco's base is thicker in the later half of the window than the earlier, so the second figure understates continuity rather than overstating change.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
At the start of the window a competitive tender for fifth-generation licences was open and no network was in service; at the end three were, with 3.2m subscribers reported for March 2026 against 2.63m and 38% outdoor population coverage at the fourth quarter of 2025 ([measurement account](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026)). The same measurement firm records median speeds falling over the period: the fifth-generation median down 38% from 261.7 Mbps at launch to about 160-165 Mbps at month six, and the fourth-generation median down 28%.

The compute programme was revised downwards inside the window. The consortium announced in June 2025 that phase 1 would launch in the first quarter of 2026 at over 40 MW, with up to 500 MW contracted from a national generator; the ministry's memorandum of April 2026 gives phase 1 as 16 MW at Nouaceur and 36 MW cumulative by end-2027 ([ministry communique](https://techafricanews.com/2026/03/11/ministry-of-digital-transition-and-administration-reform-highlights-ais-role-in-digital-transformation/)). The Q1 2026 launch date passed with no delivery reported.

Two hosting positions did not move: cloud regions announced in May 2024 for Casablanca and Settat are recorded nowhere as having entered service, and the cloud-first policy stands with its 2027 observatory not established.
