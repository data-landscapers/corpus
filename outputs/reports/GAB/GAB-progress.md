# Gabon: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-14

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.*

*Of 111 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 46 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-14, 23 did not, 15 carry no stated baseline, and 27 are ***Not held*** at both ends.*

*Shape check, run before the comparison: 83 sources for this place in the window — 24 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 59 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.*

**Movement values.** *Advanced* — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. *Stalled* — a stated target passed without delivery. *Regressed* — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. *Closed* — the programme ended. *No change* — the position at both ends is the same. ***Baseline not held*** — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in *Advanced, slipped*.

## Summary of the period

<!-- narrative: summary -->
Over the period Gabon acquired hosting capacity it did not have, and did not close the governance questions that capacity raises. Two Tier III data centres went from nothing to one in service and one due by December 2026; the national internet exchange point went from two dormant members to reconnection by both large operators; and a domestic payment scheme went from not existing to being the pilot for a standard now binding across six states.

Against that, the electronic identity programme published its first coverage figures and they showed 78,500 disputed files; the social-media suspension took effect and has not been lifted; and the World Bank operation passed its closing date having disbursed under a fifth of its envelope.

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Hosting moved from nothing to a working estate. At the period's opening the Nkok facility was under construction from a groundbreaking of 8 August 2025, with the operator saying five months to service; delivery then slipped twice, and the facility entered service on 3 July 2026 at 92 racks and 1 MW ([inauguration](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/gabon-launches-14-million-tier-iii-data-center-in-digital-sovereignty-push)). The second facility was not on the record at all at the opening and is now under construction at 160 racks and over 1 MW for delivery by December 2026 ([inspection](https://www.gabonreview.com/datacenter-tier-iii-doumba-satisfait-de-lavancement-du-chantier-une-livraison-attendue-entre-novembre-et-decembre/)). Utilisation is published at neither site, so what the period added is capacity rather than use.

The exchange point moved from dormant to active on the same evidence at both ends: two members and about 80 Mbps of local traffic unchanged since 2019, against reconnection by the two large operators and traffic above 250 Mbps from 13 May 2026 ([relaunch](https://www.gabonreview.com/souverainete-numerique-le-gabon-relance-gabix-son-point-dechange-internet-local/)). The member count after the relaunch is still not published.

Two positions did not move, and the fact is the finding. The fixed-network authorisation granted in January 2025 has produced no build obligation figure, coverage target or deployment in nineteen months, and the fibre-to-the-home programme announced nine localities for 2025 and has published no completion. The cybersecurity apparatus is unchanged: the asset-inventory method has run fourteen months with no asset count, coverage or review output ([the method](https://www.gabonreview.com/laninf-renforce-la-cybersecurite-nationale-avec-secbom/)), and the United States grant to the digital agency stands where it did, at US$49,313 disbursed of US$1,249,997 committed, with its planned end date passed.

The identity programme's movement is a publication rather than a build. At the opening it had launched in June 2024 with no enrolment figure on the record and the results indicator at a zero baseline; it now stands at 335,384 enrolled, 164,944 cards produced, 147,224 collected and 78,500 disputed ([the minister's answer of 2 June 2026](https://africacoeurnews.com/2026/06/03/gabon-17720-cartes-didentite-electroniques-toujours-non-retirees/)). The systems behind the card did not move: the digital identity system and the biometric register have been in workshops since June 2025 with nothing operating ([the three projects](https://www.gabonreview.com/identification-legale-et-digitale-le-gabon-enclenche-la-mise-en-oeuvre-de-trois-projets-strategiques/)).

Payments moved most. The domestic scheme did not exist at the opening and launched in December 2025 with more than 1,000 codes ([launch](https://gimac-afr.com/2025/12/09/cemac-madigipaie-revolutionne-leconomie-numerique/)), extended to a transport network in February 2026 and gained municipal facilitation in July. The regional standard did not exist and is now in force across six states. The regional switch reached 20.3m transactions worth FCFA 863bn across 153 participants in 2025 ([2025 figures](https://leconomie.info/article/paiements-numeriques-gimacpay-enregistre-203-millions-de-transactions-pour-863-milliards-de-fcfa-en-2025)), against a 2024 comparator the outlet reconstructed from stated growth rates rather than a published number.

Government systems moved from absent to launched without moving to measured. The public finance system did not exist at the opening and entered service in January 2026 ([launch](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/1801-52022-gabon-launches-integrated-digital-platform-for-public-finance-management)); no transaction volume or coverage figure has followed. The single digital window was presented in February 2026 and has no build stage, launch date, budget or procurement record. The interoperability indicator has not moved off zero ([report](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099121924052032986/pdf/P175987-0914ff29-7941-4230-8202-16cca6789388.pdf)).
