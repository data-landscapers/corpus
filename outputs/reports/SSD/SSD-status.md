# South Sudan: status report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base, from `outputs/reports/SSD/ledger.csv` (63 systems and instruments, 12 of them ***Not held***). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.*

**Status values.** *Implemented* — in operation or in force. *Piloting* — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. *In development* — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. *Planned* — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. *Enacted* — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. *Under review* — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. *Discontinued* — closed or superseded. ***Not held*** — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

## Summary of position

<!-- narrative: summary -->
South Sudan is placed [192nd of 193 on the UN E-Government Development Index for 2024](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/1912-51559-south-sudan-orders-full-digitization-of-public-institutions), and what it has built since collects revenue rather than delivers services. Interbank settlement went live in [October 2025](https://boss.gov.ss/boss-launches-first-ever-electronic-interbank-payment-system/), and cash was removed as a channel for [passport payments in January 2026](https://www.sudanspost.com/south-sudan-introduces-digital-payment-system-for-passports-after-supply-crisis/) and [tax at revenue offices in July 2026](https://www.eyeradio.org/revenue-authority-changes-tax-payment-process/).

Identity is the structural gap: the World Bank's ID4D diagnostic puts [close to 90% of the population without any government-recognised identification and about 90% of births unrecorded](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/07/22/in-the-worlds-youngest-nation-how-do-you-prove-you-exist). A digital national identity card and a centralised civil register were [announced on 28 July 2026 with no law, budget, timetable, vendor or platform named](https://www.eyeradio.org/civil-registry-to-introduce-digital-system-for-birth-death-and-marriage-registration/).

Governance follows the systems. A [cybercrime statute is in force from February 2026](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/minister-ateng-defends-cybercrime-law-amid-concerns) while no data-protection statute exists, and the e-Government platform most of this revenue flows through has run since 2019 under a [single-sourced concession a UN commission has examined at length](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/cohrsouthsudan/a-hrc-60-crp-5.pdf).

<!-- narrative: infrastructure -->
Two backbone programmes are under construction, neither with a completion date: a state one that moved from a [US$9m design budget in June 2025](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2025/06/30/govt-approves-9-million-for-fiber-optic-design/) to a [2,400 km route through Kenya announced in October 2025](https://techafricanews.com/2025/10/09/south-sudan-announces-2400-km-fiber-optic-network-to-strengthen-digital-infrastructure/), and a private one under [Bayobab's 15-year licence, effective 16 October 2025](https://techafricanews.com/2025/11/25/mtn-digital-infrastructure-secures-15-year-license-to-expand-fibre-network-in-south-sudan/), [in execution with NCT South Sudan from early 2026](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/4204/nct-south-sudan-and-bayobab-collaborate-to-roll-out-national-backbone-fiber-network-project). Fibre tendering for the state programme is [stated to start in the second half of 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063026190082191/txt/P176181-84d9770b-853c-4bb8-8e5a-996eac681382.txt).

The cost of connection has risen twice and the new prices are unpublished. The regulator approved a phased tariff exchange-rate adjustment [to avert what it called network collapse in June 2026](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/nca-adjusts-telecom-tariff-exchange-rate-to-avert-network-collapse), and [phase two took effect at midnight on 26 July 2026](https://www.eyeradio.org/you-will-pay-more-for-calls-data-from-tonight-as-second-tariff-adjustment-takes-effect/). Neither the regulator nor the operators have [published the revised rates or said how many further phases are planned](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/07/27/network-telecom-companies-declare-another-increment/).

Rural coverage is where the gap is concrete: in Kauto Administrative Area, [one site at Kuron carries the only signal, four more have been requested for years, and an extension expected from February 2026 has not been delivered](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/kauto-leaders-call-for-urgent-expansion-of-mobile-network-coverage).

The power behind it is private: Finnfund has put [US$10m into CREI for solar-hybrid energy at MTN's telecom sites](https://itweb.africa/article/south-sudan-green-network-gets-5m-boost/), 490 of 499 renovated, with coverage reported up from 69% to 80% over three years.

[MGI Communications, a Swiss operator, holds both the international gateway and the national data centre](https://www.eyeradio.org/nca-establishes-gateway-and-data-center-oversight-committee), under an oversight committee the regulator constituted in January 2026. The data centre was [described as halfway complete in April 2026](https://www.eyeradio.org/ateny-outlines-s-sudans-digital-transformation-plans-at-nairobi-summit/) having been [reported as nearing completion in September 2025](https://itweb.africa/article/huawei-expresses-interest-in-south-sudans-digital-future/nWJadMbNYGgMbjO1); the two have not been reconciled.

**No construction record for the facility is published anywhere the wiki can reach, as at 9 August 2026.** Searched that day: the regulator's own publications page carries five documents, none of them about the data centre and none later than a 2022 conference report; the regulator's news feed mentions neither the facility's progress nor the oversight committee's output; the ICT ministry's website publishes nothing after 18 December 2025; and MGI Communications has issued no statement on the build. The absence is the finding — a facility the state calls a strategic national asset, contracted to a foreign operator and placed under a dedicated oversight committee, has no published procurement record, contract, commissioning schedule or progress report. Both dated accounts are summit remarks, and neither is a construction report.

**The donor and corporate routes are now closed too, as at 10 August 2026, and their closure is itself informative.** The [Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project (P176181)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063026190082191/txt/P176181-84d9770b-853c-4bb8-8e5a-996eac681382.txt) is the only World Bank digital operation covering South Sudan, and its Implementation Status and Results Report of 30 June 2026 carries **no data-centre workstream at all** — the South Sudan component is fibre backbone, a national CSIRT and a research network, and the CSIRT is recorded as not established, unchanged from its November 2022 baseline against an April 2028 target — while a full-text search of the Bank's document archive returns **nothing** on a South Sudan national data centre. The facility is not donor-financed, which is why the archive that reliably publishes procurement records holds none for it. MGI Communications is a privately held Swiss company of about nine people in Zug that files no public accounts, and its own site carries no statement on the facility.

The same report does establish one thing dated: the project's results framework records **"guidelines on government data hosting, including adoption of cloud computing" for South Sudan as *not published*, measured 16 June 2026** — unchanged from its November 2022 baseline, against a target of April 2028. So the state is building a national data centre with no published policy governing what may be hosted in it.

It is not established that there is only one facility. The regulator's January 2026 statement describes MGI in the present tense as managing "critical data centre infrastructure" alongside the gateway; the minister in April 2026 describes a national data centre being built and halfway complete; the September 2025 account describes a Juba data hub nearing completion. Whether these are one facility reported inconsistently or two cannot be chosen on anything the wiki holds, and no source distinguishes them.

Spectrum governance is at inception: the [5G and spectrum strategy has a work plan and no band plan](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/20/south-sudan-launches-national-spectrum-management-and-5g-strategy-development/). The regulator has meanwhile exercised a power for which no instrument is published, [blocking social media for 30 to 90 days in January 2025](https://cpj.org/2025/01/south-sudan-blocks-social-media-access-amid-unrest/).

<!-- narrative: dpi -->
Identity is the defining gap and every credential is scarce. About [10% of the population holds a Nationality Certificate](https://statelessnessalliance.org/online-marketplace/strengthening-civil-registration-and-ending-statelessness-in-south-sudan), the prerequisite for the National Identity Card, and the directorate [held 380,000 personal identity cards in January 2026](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/juba-passport-id-card-issuance-resumes), required for banking, land and court. Stock held and cards issued are reported on different bases and no reconciled coverage figure exists. National Identity Card distribution, running in Greater Yei since July 2026, [extended to a school-based registration campaign for examination candidates at Juba Day Secondary School on 4 August 2026](https://catholicradionetwork.org/2026/08/04/immigration-directorate-launches-school-based-national-id-registration-for-examination-candidates/), with the directorate stating plans to reach more schools; no national coverage figure is published.

What recovered in 2026 was supply, not systems: [five production machines in May 2026](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/05/08/government-procures-5-advanced-machine-systems-for-national-id-and-passport-production/) and [37,500 passport booklets in July, with stock stated to December 2026](https://www.eyeradio.org/over-37000-new-passport-booklets-arrive-stocks-to-last-until-december-officical/) and processing at about three days.

Everything above it is announced rather than built: a [digital national identity card for every adult and a centralised register of births, deaths, marriages and divorces](https://www.eyeradio.org/civil-registry-to-introduce-digital-system-for-birth-death-and-marriage-registration/) were set out on 28 July 2026 as intent only. The one biometric register that exists is a payroll: a [US$15m World Bank-backed registration of public servants](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/01/16/15m-biometric-drive-south-sudan-lays-foundations-for-digital-public-infrastructure-to-purge-ghost-workers/), extended to the [organised forces in June 2026](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/06/24/government-launches-biometric-registration-exercise-for-organised-forces/), with data on a Ministry of Finance server and no data-protection authority.

Payments have wholesale plumbing and no retail rail. [SSIPS went live on 10 October 2025 with 13 banks](https://boss.gov.ss/boss-launches-first-ever-electronic-interbank-payment-system/), combining real-time gross settlement and automated clearing on ISO 20022; the retail instant system [announced with AfricaNenda in February 2025 has no documented go-live](https://africanenda.org/bank-of-south-sudan-launches-the-countrys-first-national-instant-payment-system/) and its December 2025 target passed. Retail payments run on telecom mobile money, [legal tender since July 2025](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2025/07/19/central-bank-affirms-mobile-money-as-legal-tender/) with no conduct supervision on record.

Service delivery is revenue-first: the regulator's [e-Services Platform](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/02/13/south-sudan-digitizes-regulation-to-strengthen-revenue-collection/), an [e-visa portal charging US$50 for citizens of Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and DR Congo](https://techcabal.com/2026/07/20/south-sudan-slaps-visa-kenya-rwanda-uganda/), and [electronic cargo tracking](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/south-sudan/350431/south-sudan-defends-crawford-capital-deal-as-digital-reforms-boost-revenue), all from 2026. Beyond revenue, the one operating information system of scale is the education ministry's, [launched on 5 August 2026 on the DHIS2 platform the health ministry has run since 2018](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/south-sudan-launches-new-education-emergencies-data-system-strengthen-crisis-education-response).

<!-- narrative: governance -->
The state acquired enforcement powers before rights. The [Cyber Crimes and Computer Abuse Act was signed on 18 February 2026](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/minister-ateng-defends-cybercrime-law-amid-concerns), criminalising hacking and cyberterrorism alongside false information, online impersonation and hate speech, and its [full text was withheld from journalists at signing](https://www.onecitizendaily.com/index.php/2026/02/20/ict-ministry-vows-to-ensure-effective-operation-of-cyber-crime-act/). There is no data-protection statute, only the Transitional Constitution's article 22 privacy right: a [policy bill was drafted in October 2025](https://paradigmhq.org/press-release-paradigm-initiative-partners-with-koneta-hub-to-drive-dialogue-on-data-protection-law-in-south-sudan) and the ICT minister [said a bill would come in 2026](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/south-sudan-to-get-data-protection-bill-in-2026-says-minister), but none is before the Assembly.

The institution meant to regulate the sector has an unsettled legal basis, and the two accounts of it come from the same man. On 21 May 2026 the ICT minister Ateny Wek Ateny said in terms that the statute existed — ["the law is now out already and it has been signed by the president"](https://www.eyeradio.org/govt-to-establish-ict-authority-to-boost-digital-services-and-data-protection/), the remark [reported the same day as a signed legal framework](https://szn.ss/south-sudan-to-establish-ict-authority-as-part-of-media-tech-reforms/); on 5 August 2026 the same minister's briefing to the President was reported as putting a regulator in place [while the government awaits the passage of legislation](https://www.eyeradio.org/govt-set-to-establish-ict-authority-to-regulate-internet-service-providers-ateny/). This is one minister's account changing over eleven weeks, not two outlets disagreeing. Neither states how the Authority's competence divides from the existing regulator's.

**No instrument creating the Authority can be found in any published register, as at 9 August 2026.** The Ministry of Justice's national law collection lists 77 Acts, the most recent the Labour Act 2017, and does not carry the Cybercrime and Computer Misuse Act 2026 either, so an absence there is no evidence of non-existence. The Office of the President's *Decrees and Orders* page publishes no documents. The Assembly's bills register stops in August 2023 and names no ICT Authority Bill, and the ICT ministry's news feed ends on 18 December 2025. The statutory basis of a regulator can be asserted on a podium and neither confirmed nor refuted from any published source.

**The reason no instrument can be produced is that South Sudan has no gazette to produce it from, as at 10 August 2026.** On 12 January 2026 the President ordered an eight-member committee to [operationalise the South Sudan Official Gazette within fourteen days](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/kiir-forms-technical-committee-to-launch-south-sudan-official-gazette) and to clear a publication backlog covering *every* critical law, republican decree, order and resolution enacted **since 2011** — an official admission that the state has published no authoritative record of its own legislation in the fourteen years since independence. The committee was to submit Volume One for the President to launch; no maiden edition, physical or electronic, is on file seven months later. The same week the Office of the President classified all documents bearing the President's signature as "privileged executive communication" that **cannot be photographed, scanned or shared online**, with decrees delivered confidentially to the officeholder concerned — so the one class of document that would settle the Authority's status is under an explicit bar on circulation. The Assembly offers no second route: its Hansard and document pages return 404 and its bills page is a procedural explainer rather than a register. Whether the ICT Authority has a statute is therefore unknowable from any public record South Sudan currently maintains, and will stay so until the gazette runs.

The World Bank's [seventh implementation report on the Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project records the first cross-border digital connectivity memorandum between Kenya and South Sudan as delivered, offered as the template for the remaining bilaterals](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063026190082191/txt/P176181-84d9770b-853c-4bb8-8e5a-996eac681382.txt); no text, capacity or tariff terms are published, and the same report records that no national e-commerce strategy exists, unchanged from its November 2022 baseline against an April 2028 target.

The largest governance question concerns a contract, not a statute. Under a [single-sourced contract of 16 November 2019](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/cohrsouthsudan/a-hrc-60-crp-5.pdf), Crawford Capital is the exclusive provider of e-Government services covering trade permits, visas, tax, customs and work permits. The UN Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan records that the Ministry of Finance approved single-sourcing noting a national security concern, following written advice from the then head of the Internal Security Bureau that the company should work with its ICT department; it concludes that the services are likely to be used without regard for data protection to facilitate unlawful state surveillance. That conclusion is the Commission's. The Commission [puts the eServices profit share at 75% to the contractor](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/cohrsouthsudan/a-hrc-60-crp-5.pdf); the company [says it takes a 2% platform service fee](https://www.sudanspost.com/crawford-denies-75-govt-revenue-retention-amid-mounting-public-scrutiny/), describing a separate tax contract, and the [government calls the engagement lawful and cabinet-endorsed](https://citizen.digital/article/south-sudan-confirms-digital-services-partnership-with-crawford-capital-limited-n382816). The accounts are unreconciled.

The digitisation mandate itself is a decree: in December 2025 the President [granted the Electronic Services Council full authority to digitise all government institutions, with a January 2026 deadline](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/1912-51559-south-sudan-orders-full-digitization-of-public-institutions), and no compliance report has been published since.
