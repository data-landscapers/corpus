Madagascar's government filed bill 040/2026 on electronic communications and digital infrastructure on 28 August 2026. Its vote was adjourned in the previous session, and it was not yet on the National Assembly's agenda for the new session. Article 111 would guarantee operators stable legal, tax, customs and financial regimes for the term of their licence.

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Cybercrime bill overhauling the 2014 law</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

Madagascar's 96-article cybercrime bill n° 042/2026, which would replace the 2014 law and was postponed at September's extraordinary session, returns to committee in the National Assembly on 2 November. Contested provisions include article 75, giving a new digital investigation unit (UPIN) permanent direct access to operators' systems with fines of 100 to 500 million ariary for refusal, and article 52 on criminal liability for administrators of pages and groups; the access-to-public-information bill n° 017/2025 is also on the session's agenda.

### Connectivity

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Unique mobile internet penetration</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[GSMA: Digital Reforms Could Connect 1.12 million More People in Madagascar and Add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-digital-reforms-could-connect-1-12-million-more-people-in-madagascar-and-add-mga-4-1-trillion-to-gdp-by-2031/)** — GSMA, 16 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/XAF/XAF-monthly.html" title="XAF">Africa</a>

A GSMA report launched in Antananarivo says four reforms could bring about 1.13 million more people in Madagascar onto mobile internet and add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031: cheaper smartphones through lower levies and duties, a costed rural connectivity plan, lower sector taxes, and voice services in Malagasy. Unique mobile internet penetration was 14.3% in 2025. A companion GSMA report finds spectrum pricing and licensing reform across Africa could add more than US$120 billion to GDP between 2026 and 2035.

</div>

</div>

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Taxes on entry smartphones and mobile services</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[Obstacles à l'accès à internet - 47% du coût d'un smartphone sont constitués de taxes](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202610170043.html)** — Midi Madagasikara (via allAfrica), 17 October 2026

At the launch of a GSMA report on Madagascar's digital economy, operators said taxes make up 47% of the cost of an entry-level smartphone, including a 6% copyright duty. Mobile services also carry an 8% excise duty.

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[TECHNOLOGIE - Deux Malgaches sur dix connectés à Internet](https://www.lexpress.mg/2026/10/technologie-deux-malgaches-sur-dix.html)** — L'Express de Madagascar, 17 October 2026

Only about two in ten people in Madagascar use the internet, although 3G and 4G networks cover more than 80% of the population. Operators blame the cost of handsets, and the 2026 finance law keeps the taxes on them.

</div>

</div>

<div class="monthly-story" markdown="1">

<p class="monthly-story__name">Mobile broadband population coverage</p>

<div class="bulletin-item" markdown="1">

**[ACCÈS À INTERNET - Madagascar veut réduire la fracture numérique](https://www.lexpress.mg/2026/11/acces-internet-madagascar-veut-reduire.html)** — L'Express de Madagascar, 12 November 2026

A COMESA consultation on financing digital infrastructure met in Antananarivo from 10 to 13 November 2026. GSMA estimates cited there put 3G and 4G coverage at about 81% of Madagascar's population in 2026. Yet 66% of people live in covered areas without using mobile internet. 4G reaches an estimated 67% of rural people against 96% in towns.

</div>
