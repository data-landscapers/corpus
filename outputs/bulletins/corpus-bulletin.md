## Governance

### Data protection

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="XGL" markdown="1">

**[UN expert highlights global challenges to international transfer of personal data](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/10/un-expert-highlights-global-challenges-international-transfer-personal-data)** — Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, 23 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/catalogue/#places=XGL" title="XGL">Global</a>

In a report to the UN General Assembly, the Special Rapporteur on the right to privacy, Ana Brian Nougrères, found that none of the cross-border transfer instruments she reviewed, from adequacy decisions to standard contractual clauses and binding corporate rules, protects privacy by itself. She urged States to negotiate a legally binding international instrument on data transfers, and asked for low-cost tools so developing countries and small firms can use transfer mechanisms and for data protection to be written into digital trade agreements. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Standards](#standards).*</span>

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="TGO" markdown="1">

**[JNPDP 2026 : l’IPDCP mobilise les citoyens pour une meilleure protection des données personnelles](https://levisionnaire.tg/jnpdp-2026-lipdcp-mobilise-les-citoyens-pour-une-meilleure-protection-des-donnees-personnelles/)** — Levisionnaire, 23 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/TGO/TGO-monthly.html" title="TGO">Togo</a>

Togo's data protection authority, the IPDCP, will hold the country's first National Personal Data Protection Day in Lomé on 29 October 2026, on personal data and digital sovereignty, with a panel of counterpart authorities from Benin, Burkina Faso, Mali and Senegal. Ahead of it, the authority planned an awareness session for over 250 young people in Dapaong on 24 October and announced a network of more than 500 volunteers to carry its messages locally. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Literacy](#literacy).*</span>

</div>

### Regional collaboration

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="CMR XAF" markdown="1">

**[Yaoundé Declaration Targets 100% Local Hosting of Critical Government Data by 2030](https://www.businessincameroon.com/public-management/2310-17029-yaounde-declaration-targets-100-local-hosting-of-critical-government-data-by-2030)** — Business in Cameroon, 23 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/CMR/CMR-monthly.html" title="CMR">Cameroon</a><a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/XAF/XAF-monthly.html" title="XAF">Africa</a>

The Yaoundé Declaration on E-Governance and Artificial Intelligence in Africa, adopted when the E-Gov'A conference closed on 17 October 2026, asks African governments to host all classified critical or sovereign public data inside their own borders by 2030, and where domestic capacity is lacking to prefer another African country over providers off the continent. The organisers, Smart Click Africa and Digital Business Africa, are to run an African Observatory for E-Government and AI; the hosting target is a recommendation and binds no government. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Digital sovereignty](#digital-sovereignty) and [Data Storage](#data-storage).*</span>

</div>
