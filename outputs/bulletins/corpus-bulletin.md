<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="NGA" markdown="1">

**[Nigeria SEC toughens crypto rules](https://www.itweb.africa/article/nigeria-sec-toughens-crypto-rules/dgp45vaBlmw7X9l8)** — ITWeb Africa, 24 August 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/countries/NGA/" title="NGA">Nigeria</a>

Nigeria's Securities and Exchange Commission has issued draft rules setting minimum capital at N500 million for digital asset platform operators and real-world asset tokenisation platforms and N200 million for virtual asset service providers, on a N30 million registration fee and a fidelity insurance bond covering at least a quarter of paid-up capital. Operators serving Nigerian residents would have to be locally incorporated with a resident top executive, and no digital asset business could target the market without SEC approval. The article's headline figure of $1.41m does not reconcile with the naira amounts it goes on to give, and is not repeated here. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Digital Payments and Fintech](#digital-payments-and-fintech).*</span>
