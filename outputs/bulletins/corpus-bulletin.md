## Governance

### Strategies, plans and policies

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="XAF" markdown="1">

**[Spectrum Policy in Africa: Enabling investment and empowering consumers through effective spectrum pricing](https://www.gsma.com/connectivity-for-good/spectrum/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Spectrum-Policy-in-Africa.pdf)** — GSMA Intelligence, 16 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/XAF/XAF-monthly.html" title="XAF">Africa</a>

A GSMA Intelligence study finds African mobile operators held on average 570 MHz of spectrum below 7 GHz at the end of 2025, against 750 MHz elsewhere, and paid 24% more per unit. Only 9 of 54 countries have licences of at least 20 years or a published spectrum roadmap. It estimates pricing and licensing reforms could add more than $120 billion to Africa's GDP between 2026 and 2035. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Connectivity](#connectivity).*</span>

</div>

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="NGA" markdown="1">

**[Nigeria’s Phone Crackdown: Protecting Networks or Pricing Out Everyday Users?](https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/16/nigerias-phone-crackdown-protecting-networks-or-pricing-out-everyday-users/)** — Tech Africa News, 16 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/NGA/NGA-monthly.html" title="NGA">Nigeria</a>

From 10 October 2026, Nigeria's communications regulator began texting owners of phones not verified under type approval that network access could be blocked after 30 days of active use. The checks run on the NCC's Device Management System against each phone's IMEI, and grey-market and overseas-bought phones are among those flagged. A registration route and official fees had not been clearly published by 16 October. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Access to services](#access-to-services).*</span>

<div class="bulletin-item" data-places="MDG XAF" markdown="1">

**[GSMA: Digital Reforms Could Connect 1.12 million More People in Madagascar and Add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-digital-reforms-could-connect-1-12-million-more-people-in-madagascar-and-add-mga-4-1-trillion-to-gdp-by-2031/)** — GSMA, 16 October 2026 <a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/MDG/MDG-monthly.html" title="MDG">Madagascar</a><a class="wip-item-card__status wip-item-card__status--active country-box" href="https://corpus.data-landscapers.io/reports/XAF/XAF-monthly.html" title="XAF">Africa</a>

A GSMA report launched in Antananarivo says four reforms could bring about 1.13 million more people in Madagascar onto mobile internet and add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031: cheaper smartphones through lower levies and duties, a costed rural connectivity plan, lower sector taxes, and voice services in Malagasy. Unique mobile internet penetration was 14.3% in 2025. A companion GSMA report finds spectrum pricing and licensing reform across Africa could add more than US$120 billion to GDP between 2026 and 2035. <span class="bulletin-item__also">*Also under [Connectivity](#connectivity) and [Access to services](#access-to-services).*</span>
