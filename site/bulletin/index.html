Uganda’s Digital Economy: Rights Trends, Regulatory Gaps and Policy Responses — CIPESA, 8 September 2026 Uganda

A rights organisation maps Uganda’s digital-economy growth against the gaps it finds in biometric-data, artificial-intelligence and platform-work safeguards. Also under Data protection.

Travel Notes from the 10th Central Bank Payments Conference in Istanbul: Rethinking Regulation — AfricaNenda, 8 September 2026 Global

Notes from a central bank payments conference in Istanbul argue for rethinking how instant payment systems are regulated. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Power Brunch: Nigeria's data sovereignty push goes beyond local servers — TechCabal, 8 September 2026 Nigeria

With Nigerian financial firms facing a January 2027 data-localisation deadline, the sovereignty argument is moving past where servers sit to backups, access rights and where data is analysed. Also under Data Storage and AI.

NRS Reform: Inside the drive to build a smarter, simpler tax system — Vanguard, 8 September 2026 Nigeria

An account of Nigeria’s tax reform argues the authority is entering a phase where the quality of the information it holds matters as much as the revenue it collects. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and National statistics.

MultiChoice seeks stronger piracy regulations — ITWeb South Africa, 8 September 2026 South Africa

A South African broadcaster called for stronger rules against pirate streaming, its chief executive telling a briefing that technology alone cannot stop unauthorised distribution. Also under Cybersecurity.

Le Burkina Faso repense l’aménagement numérique de son territoire à l’horizon 2030 — wearetech.africa, 8 September 2026 Burkina Faso

Burkina Faso is revising its digital territorial development plan to a 2030 horizon, against an international union figure putting about 70 per cent of the population off the internet in 2024. Also under Connectivity.

Implementation of the AU Data Policy Framework: South Sudan Data Governance Capacity Building and Engagement Workshop — draft concept note and agenda — KICTANet, 8 September 2026 South Sudan

The African Union Commission, AUDA-NEPAD, GIZ and KICTANet convened a three-day workshop in Juba from 8 to 10 September on domesticating the African Union Data Policy Framework in South Sudan. This is a forward notice of the convening; no outcome or workplan is on record. Also under Regional collaboration, Data protection, Technical Capacity and Data Exchange.

Egypt Targets $8bn Outsourcing Exports as ICT Sector Records 24.3% Growth — Tech Africa News, 8 September 2026 Egypt

Egypt's communications minister put outsourcing exports at US$5.1 billion at the end of 2025 and set a US$8 billion target for 2028, opening the sixth Tech Invest Egypt conference in Cairo, and gave ICT sector growth of 24.3 per cent for the fourth quarter of the 2025/26 financial year as the highest on record. It is the third ministerial figure for the same 2025 outturn — US$4.8 billion and US$5.2 billion are also on the record — and no statistical release stands behind any of them. Also under ICT Industry and AI.

Digital Public Infrastructure Adoption is a Governance Problem — Caribou, 8 September 2026 EthiopiaSouth AfricaGlobal

Two development organisations argue that digital public infrastructure fails on governance rather than technology, drawing on Ethiopian and South African examples. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Digital Identity and CRVS.

Legislation and regulation

South Africa Pushes Subsea Cable, AI and Digital Skills Agenda to Expand Connectivity — Tech Africa News, 8 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's operator association has asked the Competition Commission for a five-year exemption letting Vodacom, MTN, Telkom, Cell C, Rain and Liquid share limited infrastructure planning information through an independent third party, to find connectivity gaps and avoid duplicated build. The communications minister, who called the proposal encouraging for rural areas and townships, also argued that disruption to Middle East routes gives the country an opening as an alternative submarine cable hub. Also under Connectivity, Training and skills and AI.

Kenya Proposes Standalone Licence Framework for Data Centres — Tech Africa News, 8 September 2026 Kenya

The Communications Authority of Kenya has proposed a standalone Data Centre licence for co-location operators, taking them out of the Network Facilities Provider-Tier 2 category they currently sit under, and opened comment for thirty days from its public notice. The Authority gives visibility over data-centre operations, support for investment and proportionality with comparable jurisdictions as its reasons; the notice itself is not held, so the closing date is unestablished. Also under Data Storage.

Congo extends state control over mining with bid to lock down geological data — The Citizen, 8 September 2026 DRC

The Democratic Republic of Congo is vesting its geological data in the state and charging for access, extending state control over mining; a resource-governance institute questions whether release will be even-handed. Also under Open data.

Regional collaboration

Africa Accelerator for Digital Public Infrastructure (AA4DPI) — United Nations Development Programme, 8 September 2026 Africa

UNDP has published the standing description of its Africa Accelerator for Digital Public Infrastructure, led jointly by the Regional Bureau for Africa and the Digital and AI Hub, which supports countries to assess digital readiness, strengthen governance, build implementation capacity and develop investment-ready programmes across digital identity, digital payments and data exchange. Its first published cohort session ran on 2 and 3 September with delegations from Ethiopia, Malawi, Rwanda, Sierra Leone and Zambia; no budget, duration or deliverable set is published for the accelerator itself. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov, Digital Identity and CRVS, Digital Payments and Fintech and Data Exchange.

Kenya's Flowt raises $550,000 as it targets a $1 million loan book — TechCabal, 8 September 2026 Kenya

A Kenyan fintech raised US$550,000 to lend to small climate-focused businesses, turning bank and mobile-money records into lender-ready data against a target loan book of US$1 million. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Egypt Partners With Vodafone, Elsewedy and Cassava on $1bn Data Centre Investment — Tech Africa News, 8 September 2026 Egypt

Two data centre partnerships announced in Cairo project about US$1 billion of investment between them. The first establishes Africa Data Centres Egypt, combining Vodafone Business, Elsewedy Electric and Cassava Technologies, starting at 20MW over three years on a targeted path to 200MW; the second, between Vodafone Business and Cassava, is described as Egypt's first sovereign AI data centre, offering GPU-as-a-Service locally so that AI workloads run without moving data out of the country. The sums are projections stated at announcement rather than committed capital. Also under Data Storage and AI.

The cross-border routes Kenyans use to be paid narrowed one by one over the past year: an international payments provider restricted accounts, a dollar-banking service closed, a remittance wallet paused and a streaming platform ended creator monetisation.

M-PESA Ethiopia Expands Cardless ATM Withdrawals Through Awash Bank Partnership — Tech Africa News, 8 September 2026 Ethiopia

M-PESA Ethiopia has extended cardless ATM withdrawals to Awash Bank's machines, letting wallet holders draw cash without a bank card by authorising the withdrawal in the app. The service has been live with Bank of Abyssinia since August; the date it started with Awash Bank is not stated.

Libya's North Africa Bank to launch direct payment service to China using Alipay — Libya Herald, 8 September 2026 Libya

Avec WorkKE, le Kényan Kennedy Mokaya démocratise le travail en ligne en Afrique — wearetech.africa, 8 September 2026 Kenya

WorkKE, a Kenyan platform launched in 2024, matches businesses to freelancers and uses local cybercafes as access relays, releasing escrow-held payments as project stages are validated and settling over mobile money. It reports more than 1,650 registered users, over 500 of them freelancers, and 200 partner cybercafes, and is extending into Uganda, Tanzania, Nigeria, South Africa and Ghana; the counts are the company's own. Paludisme : le Bénin intègre l'IA dans son dispositif national de diagnostic — wearetech.africa, 8 September 2026 Benin

Benin's health ministry has contracted the South Korean firm Noul to put twenty AI-assisted malaria diagnostic devices into the main public referral hospitals, moving the miLab MAL system from individual facilities into the national malaria control programme, which has classified it as a point-of-service diagnostic. The device automates blood-smear preparation, digitisation and image analysis; the supporting evidence is the vendor's account of a 2025 evaluation in 211 children, and the award date is not established.
