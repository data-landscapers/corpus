Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Uganda targets $500bn through digitalisation — ITWeb Africa, 4 September 2026 Uganda

Uganda's ICT and national guidance minister set digital infrastructure, data and artificial intelligence as instruments of a tenfold growth strategy toward a US$500 billion economy, opening the third annual ICT National Summit in Kampala. She called for investment in digital skills, stronger local innovation and scaled homegrown solutions, and named digital fraud, misinformation and cyber threats as the counterweight. Also under AI.

Tanzania tells WTPF session how its citizens enjoy access to inclusive, secure communication services — Daily News Tanzania, 4 September 2026 Tanzania

Tanzania set out three ICT priorities at the ITU's seventh World Telecommunication/ICT Policy Forum in Nassau: expanding affordable access, strengthening digital skills and AI use across social groups, and building secure and inclusive infrastructure that protects personal data. The communications minister said the country had completed 758 4G towers reaching more than 1,400 villages and about 8.5 million people, established 800 digital clubs, provided Wi-Fi at 100 public locations and computer laboratories in 250 public schools. Also under Data protection and Access to services.

South African online shoppers prefer local to Temu, Shein — Connecting Africa, 4 September 2026 South Africa

South African cross-border e-commerce growth fell to 7% in 2025 from an annual range of 30 to 50% before 2024, after the November 2024 removal of the small-parcel import concession put parcels under R500 on the standard 45% duty plus 15% VAT. Shein's growth slowed to 11% and Temu recorded an average monthly decline of 42%, while roughly 19 million cross-border platform orders in 2025 still made up 18.6% of South African e-commerce transactions. Also under ICT Industry.

Liberia: Massaquoi Drives LTA Push for Wider, Safer Internet Access — allAfrica.com (The Liberian Investigator), 4 September 2026 Liberia

Liberia's telecommunications regulator is being run to its 2025-2029 strategic plan, which puts universal connectivity, digital inclusion, consumer protection, regulatory oversight, innovation and investment at its centre. Its acting chairperson's programme is described as extending internet access to schools and underserved communities, strengthening consumer protection, opening the country to satellite connectivity and returning it to regional telecommunications affairs. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

Kenya opens digital economy to more private investment as growth opportunities expand — TechReviewAfrica, 4 September 2026 Kenya

Kenya's Cabinet Secretary for Information, Communications and the Digital Economy opened the digital economy to greater private investment at an East Africa private capital conference, naming fibre and wireless networks, data centres, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, digital services and business process outsourcing as the opportunities and citing growth of 4.6% in 2025 and 5.3% in the first quarter of 2026. No instrument, amount or counterparty is named. Also under New investments.

Home Affairs officials 'paid to ignore' fraudulent visa documents — IOL / The Star, 4 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Special Investigating Unit found Home Affairs adjudicators took kickbacks to approve visas on fraudulent supporting documents without conducting any verification. It has flagged 205 visas for revocation and deportation with seven disciplinary referrals, under a 2024 proclamation covering almost two decades of visa administration, and recommends reducing human intervention through technology and integrating departmental systems so verifications run automatically. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Former ministers detail early border system options before Travizory — Seychelles Nation, 4 September 2026 Seychelles

Seychelles' Commission of Inquiry into the border management agreement with Travizory Border Security heard testimony from two former ministers on how the country initially explored established international providers before Travizory entered the picture. The report is paywalled beyond that point. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

En quête d'efficacité, la Côte d'Ivoire vise 700 services publics en ligne d'ici 2029 — wearetech.africa, 4 September 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Cote d'Ivoire set a target of 700 high-impact digital public services by 2029 and a zero-paper administration by 2030, at a national prioritisation workshop in Abidjan. A first portfolio of about 100 services is to be built or rebuilt on the national interoperability platform so one administration can draw on data another already holds; the ministry acknowledged that data exchange between existing platforms remains limited and that some functions were built twice. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Data Exchange.

DMAP gains momentum as Government, World Bank reaffirm commitment to digital transformation — Public Private Partnership Commission (PPPC), Malawi, 4 September 2026 Malawi

A World Bank mission team on the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project called on the ICT minister in Lilongwe, its task team leader saying the project anticipates funding for around 100 additional cell towers and connectivity for 500 government institutions, with biometric digital ID among key procurements. The minister said existing capacity of 12.5 Gbps is insufficient and called for at least 50 Gbps, a future-ready national data centre and integration of all ministries under the Bomalathu eService Portal. Also under New investments.

Djibouti se dote d'une stratégie d'IA sur fond de préparation encore limitée — wearetech.africa, 4 September 2026 Djibouti

Djibouti presented the main lines of its first national artificial-intelligence strategy, covering 2026 to 2030 around six priorities: data and AI governance, cybersecurity, training, research, innovation and inclusion. A UNESCO-supported readiness assessment presented alongside it classes the country as emerging, with an ICT development index of 61.6 against AI readiness of about 24.5 out of 100 and no legislation dedicated to artificial intelligence beyond the data-protection provisions of the digital code. Also under AI.

AI and new digital technologies must not leave SA's workforce behind: Ramokgopa — SAnews, 4 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's government told NEDLAC's annual summit that the transition to an artificial-intelligence-enabled economy must not leave workers behind, warning that technological progress is not automatically inclusive and can deepen inequality, displace workers and widen the digital divide if poorly managed. The answer was put in education and re-skilling, worker protection as workplaces automate, and social dialogue; no programme, budget or instrument was announced. Also under AI and Training and skills.

AfricaNenda calls for balanced regulation as AI and digital assets reshape payments — TechReviewAfrica, 4 September 2026 Africa

AfricaNenda's deputy chief executive called for a balanced regulatory approach as artificial intelligence, digital currencies, stablecoins and tokenised assets reshape payments, telling a central bank payments conference in Istanbul that financial stability, security, competition and inclusion remain central priorities while policymakers must reconcile innovation with stability and changing geopolitical realities. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

ICASA Approves Transfer of Cybertek Telecom Licences to Optipulse — TechAfrica News, 4 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's communications regulator approved the transfer of Cybertek's individual electronic communications network service and electronic communications service licences to Optipulse on 18 August, in a notice under section 9(5) of the Electronic Communications Act. The approval is a change of ownership of the licensed operations. Also under ICT Industry.

Sovereignty just acquired a pyramid-shaped target — theCUBE Research, 4 September 2026 Egypt

A commentary sets out Egypt as the first country to receive competing artificial-intelligence data-centre bids from both superpowers: a 102-page Huawei proposal covering military, surveillance and public-sector use obtained on 26 August, against a United States counter-offer that has not been published. China's president arrived in Cairo on 1 September and agreements on artificial intelligence and technology transfer were reported being signed that week; no award is on file. Also under AI, Data Storage, China activities and US / hyperscaler activities.

Op-Ed: Nigeria’s digital future needs trust — theinsightnewsonline.com, 4 September 2026 Nigeria

An opinion piece argues that Nigeria's digital future depends on pairing innovation with accountable institutions and a fairer distribution of opportunity, taking a 1973 newspaper report of a N44,000 fraud at the electricity authority as its starting point for a question about turning resources and ingenuity into lasting public benefit. Also under Public debate and participation in policymaking and Access to services.

La CEEAC veut sécuriser sa frontière numérique — Infos Gabon, 4 September 2026 Africa

ECCAS member states met in Douala from 31 August to 4 September to draft a regional framework governing cross-border data flows in Central Africa, assessing national readiness and consolidating country contributions into a consensus on the pillars of a future instrument. Technical and financial support came from GIZ, AUDA-NEPAD, the Economic Commission for Africa and the African Union Commission. Also under Regional collaboration and Data Exchange.

[Blog] The Age of Quantum Computing will soon be Upon Us: Is Africa Ready? — UNECA, 4 September 2026 Africa

An Economic Commission for Africa blog argues that quantum computing is advancing fast enough to reshape economies, digital security, payment systems and scientific discovery, and that the question for Africa is not whether it will transform the world but whether the continent will be ready when it does. Also under Cybersecurity.

Regional collaboration

Lagos urges Nigeria to lead Africa in digital finance — BizWatch Nigeria, 4 September 2026 Nigeria

A technology adviser to the Lagos State governor called for Nigeria to lead Africa in digital finance, tokenised assets and cross-border settlement, at the close of a three-day digital finance summit in Lagos. He said regulation, infrastructure and trust are what would attract global capital, and urged Lagos to position itself as a hub for cross-border settlements. Also under Public debate and participation in policymaking and Digital Payments and Fintech.

IGAD Moves to Forge Regional AI Framework to Drive Integration, Development — Fana Media Corporation S.C., 4 September 2026 East Africa

IGAD opened a two-day consultation in Addis Ababa to develop a common Horn of Africa framework and strategy for artificial intelligence. Its executive secretary put data sovereignty and accountability at the centre, set three priorities - directing investment where socioeconomic impact is greatest, closing gaps in infrastructure, policy and skills, and building regional synergies through harmonisation - and insisted the framework be owned by member states rather than treated as a secretariat project. Also under AI.

Africa CDC advances public health intelligence framework — The Star (Kenya), 4 September 2026 KenyaAfrica

Africa CDC and member states validated components of a continental behavioural intelligence and infodemic management framework at a five-day meeting in Mombasa, with participants from six countries alongside the ECOWAS regional surveillance centre and the WHO Africa Infodemic Response Alliance. The framework is to turn community feedback, social listening, service-delivery and programme data and rapid research into decision-ready intelligence; Africa CDC states it is not another database or reporting platform. Also under Data Exchange and Standards.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Tanzania fintechs, banks told to rethink partnerships — The Citizen, 4 September 2026 Tanzania

The UN Capital Development Fund counted 171 active fintech companies in Tanzania in 2026, up from 33 in 2021, against more than 70% of adults holding a mobile money account and 6.4 billion digital payment transactions in 2024. Founders and the fund told a Dar es Salaam session that partnerships with banks and mobile operators remain the main barrier to scale, citing banks assessing early-stage firms through a static lens, limited patient capital, talent shortages, regulatory and tax uncertainty and customer acquisition costs. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Ruto@Four: Digital government: How eCitizen has transformed public services — The Star (Kenya), 4 September 2026 Kenya

A four-year retrospective on Kenya's eCitizen platform records the count of services rising from about 350 in 2022 to 20,855 by 2024, with the president saying in November that the platform hosted 22,515 services and more than 13 million users. The piece sets that expansion against the platform's contested governance and reliability. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

NITDA DG Says Digital Skills, Innovation Key to Nigeria's Economic Transformation — TechAfrica News, 4 September 2026 Nigeria

NITDA's director-general told GITEX Nigeria that digital skills, innovation and human capital rather than physical resources will decide the country's economic future, pointing to the integration of digital skills into formal education and to the 3 Million Technical Talent programme as the pipeline, and saying the agency's remit extends past producing skilled Nigerians to making those skills into businesses and products. Also under Training and skills and Innovation ecosystem.

MTN Ghana trains media, CSOs on responsible AI use — B2B Ghana, 4 September 2026 Ghana

MTN Ghana trained journalists and civil society representatives in Accra on the capabilities, limitations and responsible use of artificial intelligence in their work, covering prompting and practical applications of tools including ChatGPT for research, content development, data analysis and everyday workflow. Also under AI and Training and skills.

Malawian farmers help shape digital plant-health services — NIBIO (Norwegian Institute of Bioeconomy Research), 4 September 2026 Malawi

Farmers, extension workers, agro-dealers and policymakers tested Malawi's digital plant-health service and proposed changes before wider rollout. Its voice platform reaches basic-phone users in Chichewa and other local languages and takes around 30,000 calls a month despite never having been publicly promoted since its June 2025 launch; participants asked for coverage of soil health, fertilisers, irrigation and weather and for changes to visualisations, language and accessibility. Also under Access to services.

Digital Profiteers (Part 3): How combating fraud in SA's digitalised grants system hurts the poor — Daily Maverick (Open Secrets), 4 September 2026 South Africa

Open Secrets published the final part of an investigation into South Africa's digitalised grants system, arguing anti-fraud effort falls on beneficiaries rather than on the private actors deducting from their grants. SASSA flagged more than 420,000 beneficiaries for review in 2025/26 claiming R1 billion saved and targets up to 350,000 in 2026/27 for R1.5 billion; the piece documents a pensioner losing R205 a month to a funeral policy he never consented to. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech and Access to services.

African mobile money shifts beyond adoption — TechTrendsKE, 4 September 2026 ZambiaGhana

MTN mobile money executives told the group's fintech summit in Johannesburg that high adoption numbers mask weak use of savings and investment products. The Ghanaian chief executive said the platform processes more than 26 million transactions a day but that fewer than 0.3% of users actively invest; the Zambian chief executive said agent numbers were not on their own solving access and that the company had deployed artificial intelligence to monitor agent activity and liquidity to find where customers cannot get cash. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech and Access to services.

Ghana's Office of the Head of the Local Government Service signed a memorandum with Celltel Networks to turn all 261 metropolitan, municipal and district assemblies into what both parties call smart assemblies, under a Local Government Service IT Infrastructure and Systems Improvement Programme spanning waste management, public safety, education, healthcare, environmental monitoring and citizen engagement. JICA, Makerere University Explore New Frontiers in AI and Research Collaboration — Makerere University News, 4 September 2026 Uganda

Zimbabwe's internet traffic jumps 57% — ITWeb Africa, 4 September 2026 Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe's internet and data traffic rose 57.28% in the first quarter of 2026, the regulator reported, attributing it to faster LTE and 5G deployment, rising smartphone adoption and data-hungry applications. Operators added 13 5G base stations in the quarter to reach 379, covering 18.94% of the population, and deployed 161 further LTE sites; mobile internet and data was the largest revenue contributor at 46.34% of quarterly operator revenue.

Telecom Egypt to extend Red2Med system to Taba with petabit link — Developing Telecoms, 4 September 2026 Egypt

Telecom Egypt signed an agreement with the Greek contractor Power Sub Link to build a 200 km repeaterless subsea cable from Sharm El Sheikh to Taba up the Gulf of Aqaba coast, designed for 5 Pbps. It will interconnect with the Red Sea festoon system that forms the southern half of the Red2Med route to Port Said. No cost or in-service date is stated.

Safaricom Ethiopia Surpasses 15 Million Subscribers After Four Years of Operations — TechAfrica News, 4 September 2026 Ethiopia

Safaricom Ethiopia passed 15 million 90-day active subscribers in under four years of commercial operation, marking five years since its unified licence of 9 July 2021. It has deployed more than 3,500 network sites, all 4G-enabled and 5G-ready, reaching about 60% of the population, and says the growing base is moving the business towards breakeven; it puts community investment at ETB 684 million over four years. Also under ICT Industry and Digital Payments and Fintech.

Orange Côte d'Ivoire Warns of Legal Action After Telecom Cables Damaged in Cocody — TechAfrica News, 4 September 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Orange Cote d'Ivoire warned it may take legal action after construction works severed several of its telecommunications cables in an Abidjan district on 2 September, disrupting fixed, mobile and internet services for more than 1,121 customers. The operator named the construction company responsible, cited repeated damage to its fibre and called on construction firms to coordinate better when working near telecommunications infrastructure.

L'air du temps : Le reseau mobile : mauvaise qualite et desarroi des usagers — Le Sahel (ONEP), 4 September 2026 Niger

Niger's electronic communications and post regulator launched a campaign on 27 July to verify mobile network coverage and the voice and data service map. A press account reports users facing poor quality for several months - interrupted calls, correspondents shown as unavailable or engaged - with the regulator's expert findings still awaited. Also under Digital divides.

Katsina Urges Robust Investment in Fibre Optic Infrastructure — The Punch, 4 September 2026 Nigeria

Katsina State called for public-private collaboration to expand fibre optic infrastructure and make connectivity affordable, at a sensitisation programme on fibre deployment. The state investment promotion agency's director-general told regulators, operators and businesses that fibre is investment infrastructure rather than a telecommunications issue, and that the state's fibre drive should be measured by the businesses, households and institutions actually connected rather than by kilometres deployed. Also under Digital divides.

Internet : l'Egypte renforce ses capacites regionales avec un nouveau cable sous-marin — Agence Ecofin, 4 September 2026 Egypt

Egypt plans a roughly 200 km subsea cable from Sharm El Sheikh on the Red Sea to Taba on the Gulf of Aqaba, carried by Telecom Egypt with the Greek company Power Sub Link, adding high-capacity domestic routing options. The account notes the country already has the continent's largest concentration of submarine cables, about twenty connected with five more under construction.

Glo's decline, T2's missing data widen MTN, Airtel's Internet lead — TechCabal, 4 September 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's telecoms regulator published three consecutive months of unchanged subscriber figures for T2 Mobile because the operator filed no reports, putting its base at 802,534 for April, May and June 2026 while its own porting data showed a net loss of 1,824 subscribers. Glo grew 7.8% between April and May then lost 7.3% in June, and the regulator recorded more than 5,000 fibre cuts nationally in the first half of 2026. Also under ICT Industry.

Ethio Telecom Targets 96.2 Million Customers, ETB 295 Billion Revenue in 2026/27 — TechAfrica News, 4 September 2026 Ethiopia

Ethio Telecom set 2026/27 targets of 96.2 million customers and ETB 295 billion revenue in its annual business plan, the second year of its Next Horizon strategy. The plan raises 4G capacity from 22.79 million to 35.31 million subscribers and 4G population coverage from 82.23% to 95%, 5G sites from 340 to 648 and 5G towns from 33 to 73, backbone fibre from 23,442 km to 25,642 km and gateway capacity from 4.3 Tbps to 5.2 Tbps, with telebirr targeted at 67.69 million customers. Also under ICT Industry, Digital Payments and Fintech and Data Storage.

Hackers breach Rand Water's defences — ITWeb South Africa, 4 September 2026 South Africa

Rand Water, Africa's largest bulk water utility and supplier to more than 16 million people, disclosed a cyber security incident on 3 September and is operating from its Centurion disaster-recovery site while it establishes the extent of the damage. Its debt officer said intruders reached some servers at night before IT staff could block them and that some servers were damaged; water treatment, quality control and bulk supply are stated to remain fully operational.

The Western Cape's Social Work Integrated Management System now accepts secure external reports of child abuse and neglect, identified or anonymous, from the professionals the Children's Act obliges to report - health professionals, legal practitioners, teachers, traditional leaders and religious leaders - routing them into the provincial case-management system rather than a separate channel. Digital health records system in pilot phase — Nation Online, 4 September 2026 Malawi

Malawi's health ministry has begun piloting a digital health records system intended to replace hard-copy health passport books, at Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital in Blantyre and selected health centres nationwide. Each patient is given a unique identifier stored on a server linked to facilities through an interoperability layer, and the ministry expects most facilities digitised within about a year. Also under Sectoral management information systems.

ATDI lança Portal do Cidadao na FACIM 2026 para digitalizar mais de 140 servicos publicos em Mocambique — MZNews, 4 September 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's digital transformation agency unveiled the Portal do Cidadao at the Maputo international fair, a single virtual counter for about 140 public services where citizens check requirements, submit applications, track case status, receive notifications and obtain official documents remotely. The agency named two supporting pillars - interoperability between public bodies so citizens are not asked twice for the same data, and a digital identity for secure authentication. Sun King joins Kenya's growing smartphone financing race — TechCabal, 4 September 2026 Kenya

Sun King began selling financed smartphones in Kenya, entering the market M-KOPA and Watu built. Its handset is sold only on a pay-as-you-go contract - a KES 2,299 deposit and KES 55 a day for 365 days, KES 22,374 in all - repaid through the phone's own lock screen, app, SIM toolkit or USSD, with the device locked if payments stop. The company opened a Tatu City assembly plant with capacity for 700,000 units a year in October 2025. Also under Access to services.

Malawi police digitises traffic fines — ITWeb Africa, 4 September 2026 Malawi

The Malawi Police Service introduced electronic payment of traffic fines, going live at headquarters in Lilongwe and across the country's six police regions from the week of 4 September, with payment through named commercial banks and both mobile money services. The ICT industry association welcomed it as fraud-reducing but urged the service to secure the system and to train staff and inform the public. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Le pari de la Banque centrale pour moderniser le syst\u00e8me de paiements — Ouragan, 4 September 2026 DRC

The Central Bank of Congo instructed its technical services to draw up a roadmap for modernising the national payments system, its governor said after a central bank payments conference in Istanbul. The stated aims are the security, fluidity and continuity of transactions and assessing the uses and risks of artificial intelligence, tokenisation, stablecoins and central bank digital currencies; the delegation gave sustained attention to offline payment, given electricity and internet constraints in several parts of the country.
