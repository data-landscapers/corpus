Strategies, plans and policies

Spectrum Policy in Africa: Enabling investment and empowering consumers through effective spectrum pricing — GSMA Intelligence, 16 October 2026 Africa

A GSMA Intelligence study finds African mobile operators held on average 570 MHz of spectrum below 7 GHz at the end of 2025, against 750 MHz elsewhere, and paid 24% more per unit. Only 9 of 54 countries have licences of at least 20 years or a published spectrum roadmap. It estimates pricing and licensing reforms could add more than $120 billion to Africa's GDP between 2026 and 2035. Also under Connectivity.

Nigeria’s Phone Crackdown: Protecting Networks or Pricing Out Everyday Users? — Tech Africa News, 16 October 2026 Nigeria

From 10 October 2026, Nigeria's communications regulator began texting owners of phones not verified under type approval that network access could be blocked after 30 days of active use. The checks run on the NCC's Device Management System against each phone's IMEI, and grey-market and overseas-bought phones are among those flagged. A registration route and official fees had not been clearly published by 16 October. Also under Access to services.

Morocco appoints Ahmed Rahhou as Minister of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Transformation and Administration Reform — Tech Review Africa, 16 October 2026 Morocco

King Mohammed VI swore in Ahmed Rahhou on 16 October 2026 as Minister of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Transformation and Administration Reform in Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri's new government. He replaces Amal El Fallah Seghrouchni, and the new ministry brings AI, digital transformation and administrative reform under one portfolio. Also under AI and Other GovTech and e-Gov.

GSMA: Digital Reforms Could Connect 1.12 million More People in Madagascar and Add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031 — GSMA, 16 October 2026 MadagascarAfrica

A GSMA report launched in Antananarivo says four reforms could bring about 1.13 million more people in Madagascar onto mobile internet and add MGA 4.1 trillion to GDP by 2031: cheaper smartphones through lower levies and duties, a costed rural connectivity plan, lower sector taxes, and voice services in Malagasy. Unique mobile internet penetration was 14.3% in 2025. A companion GSMA report finds spectrum pricing and licensing reform across Africa could add more than US$120 billion to GDP between 2026 and 2035. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

EAC Partner States Advance Measure to Strengthen E-Commerce and Digital Trade — East African Community, 16 October 2026 East Africa

At a regional e-commerce meeting in Dar es Salaam on 13-15 October 2026, EAC Partner States updated the baseline data in the monitoring tool for the EAC E-Commerce Strategy. They also named priority regional actions on consumer protection, online dispute resolution and readiness for the AfCFTA Protocol on Digital Trade. Also under Regional collaboration.

Burundi : une stratégie nationale pour renforcer les capacités de cybersécurité — We are Tech Africa, 16 October 2026 Burundi

Burundi's Council of Ministers adopted a National Cybersecurity Strategy on 14 October 2026, after work begun by a government commission in 2020 and a World Bank-funded PAFEN consultancy from 2023. The strategy provides for a legal and institutional framework, protection of critical infrastructure and public data, and a national incident-response team. Also under Cybersecurity.

ATU secures endorsements for 49 African proposals ahead of ITU Plenipotentiary Conference — TechReviewAfrica, 16 October 2026 Africa

The African Telecommunications Union says all 49 African Common Proposals for the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha (9-27 November 2026) now have the more than 15 country endorsements each needs, with Ghana making 23 endorsing states. The proposals cover affordable connectivity, cybersecurity, fair access to satellite orbits, AI for development and protection of submarine cables. Also under Regional collaboration.

Algeria reaches preliminary partnership agreement with Roblox to train video game developers — Tech Review Africa, 16 October 2026 Algeria

Algeria has reached a preliminary agreement with Roblox on a programme to train Algerian video game developers, announced by Sid Ali Zerrouki. It comes with child-protection conditions: wider parental controls, fast-track reporting channels, and the platform's chat feature kept switched off in Algeria until safeguards are judged sufficient. Also under Training and skills.

Legislation and regulation

Uganda Champions AU single digital market plan to boost cross-border trade — Daily Monitor, 16 October 2026 Uganda

Ahead of a technology and intellectual property symposium on 22-23 October, Uganda's trade minister set out plans to back the AU's single digital market, including a trust mark for vetted online sellers and rules giving online buyers redress, to be rolled out in the EAC first. A Jumia-led e-commerce industry association and a call for a Consumer Protection Act were also presented. Also under Regional collaboration.

House Scrutinizes US$52M National ID Deal — The New Dawn, 16 October 2026 Liberia

Liberia's House of Representatives has sent a proposed US$52 million concession with OeSD International for a national ID and digital civil registry to a joint committee, which has two weeks to report. This account dates the President's communication to plenary on 15 October 2026 and says the build-operate-transfer system would hold demographic and biometric data on up to 10 million people. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS and Registries.

House Begins Review of Boakai’s Proposed National Digital ID Overhaul — Liberian Observer, 16 October 2026 Liberia

Liberia's House of Representatives referred President Boakai's proposed concession with OeSD International for a combined national ID and civil registration system to a joint committee, with 14 days to report; the referral is not ratification. Under the build-operate-transfer deal, OeSD would build and run a system issuing biometric ID numbers and cards and recording births, deaths and marriages, then hand it to the government. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS, Registries and Data protection.

Ghana to host UN convention signing on digital cargo documents on October 26 — MyJoyOnline, 16 October 2026 Ghana

Ghana will host the signing ceremony for the UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra on 26 October 2026. The Ghana Shippers' Authority says the convention gives electronic bills of lading and similar documents the legal standing of paper ones, but that the benefits depend on ratification and readiness. Also under Data Exchange.

Murang’a County sues State over patient, hospital system shutdown — Daily Nation, 16 October 2026 Kenya

Murang'a County has petitioned Kenya's High Court against the Ministry of Health, the Digital Health Agency, the Health Cabinet Secretary, SHA and the Attorney-General after losing access to its Afya.KE health information system on 14 October 2026. It seeks orders restoring access and protecting clinical records across its more than 176 health facilities while the petition is heard. Liberia: Naymote Urges Legislature to Tighten US$52M National ID Deal, Cites Cost, Electoral and Data Protection Concerns — FrontPage Africa, 16 October 2026 Liberia

Liberian civil society group Naymote, in a statement of 15 October 2026, urged the Legislature to tighten the proposed national ID agreement, which it values at US$52 million. It asks for full disclosure of financing, fees and contract terms, an independent value-for-money review, government ownership of identity data, free first-time enrolment and public hearings, and says the electoral commission must keep control of the voters' roll. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS and Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Crédits et gestion du risque : l’engagement et la responsabilité des acteurs pour des données fiables au centre de la 8e Rencontre annuelle du BIC-UEMOA — C'Finance, 16 October 2026 Burkina FasoWest Africa

At the 8th annual meeting of the UEMOA credit information bureau, speakers said most microfinance institutions fall outside the duty to report to the bureau: in Burkina Faso, 20 of 120 licensed institutions met the FCFA 2bn threshold at the end of 2025. Panellists called for reporting by all microfinance institutions and wider use of client consent, which a banker said is now required for bureau access across the eight UEMOA states. Also under Data Exchange.

City Power’s electricity theft crackdown faces data problem — ITWeb South Africa, 16 October 2026 South Africa

Johannesburg utility City Power has put out a tender for near-real-time monitoring of prepaid electricity purchases, aimed at non-technical losses of R3.96bn in 2024/25, or 20.85% of electricity bought. Bidders may propose AI for theft prediction and customer risk scoring, but outputs must be explainable, auditable and compliant with POPIA, with humans making decisions and the data staying City Power's. Experts quoted say prepaid meters show token sales, not consumption, which limits what monitoring can find. Also under AI, Energy and Digitalisation of sub-national government.

Bold ambitions, fragile foundations: Is Ghana ready for the AI and Data Centre risks it is inviting? — MyJoyOnline, 16 October 2026 Ghana

In an opinion piece, cybersecurity and AI governance specialist Victor Awaga asks whether Ghana is ready for the risks of its push into AI and data centres, including the US$250 million AI Computing Centre announced in April 2026. He points to strain on the power grid, thin cybersecurity capacity, and data protection and cyber laws written before generative AI and large data centres. Also under AI, Data Storage, Energy and Cybersecurity.

World Bank Plans $265M Program To Expand Digital Coverage in Central Africa — Africa Monitoring, 16 October 2026 Central Africa

The World Bank is preparing CARDIP, a regional digital integration programme for the six CEMAC countries, with an estimated cost of $265.5 million and financing not yet approved. Appraisal is set for 3 November 2026 and board consideration for 12 April 2027, with CEMAC as the implementing body. The first phase would fund broadband and cross-border links in the Central African Republic, Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon, alongside work on ID, payments and data exchange systems. Also under New investments, Connectivity and Data Exchange.

UBA launches pan-African payment system to ease cross-border transactions — The Citizen (Mwananchi Communications), 16 October 2026 Tanzania

UBA Tanzania has launched cross-border payments over the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System and says it is the first bank in Tanzania to sign up. Its customers can pay into other participating countries from shilling accounts at branches or through WhatsApp and Facebook, for a 2.5% fee capped at US$20, up to US$5,000 a transfer and US$10,000 a day. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

SADC STRENGTHENS REGIONAL CYBERSECURITY AND CYBERCRIME COOPERATION — SADC, 16 October 2026 AngolaSouthern Africa

The Secretariat reports that the number of operational national incident-response teams in the region has risen from 4 in 2018 to 13. Also under Cybersecurity.

Liberia Deepens Cybersecurity Ties With Morocco, Secures Membership In African Digital Security Networks — FrontPage Africa, 16 October 2026 LiberiaMorocco

Liberia's telecoms minister and the head of Morocco's DGSSI also agreed to pursue a memorandum of understanding on cybersecurity capacity building and technical help. Also under Cybersecurity.

Ethiopia modernises cross-border payments via PAPSS — ITWeb Africa, 16 October 2026 Ethiopia

Ethiopia has joined the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, letting its financial institutions make instant cross-border payments in local currencies with counterparts elsewhere in Africa. The National Bank of Ethiopia reports that digital transaction volumes grew by 146% a year from 2020 to 2025, and the country received $8.2 billion in remittances in 2025/26. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.
