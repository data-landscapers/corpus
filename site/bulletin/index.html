Strategies, plans and policies

Will 90,000km fibre move Nigeria from buffering to better browsing? — The Sun Nigeria, 26 August 2026 Nigeria

Stakeholders question whether Project BRIDGE, the 90,000 km fibre deployment starting in October, has addressed what has frustrated Nigerian fibre for years: right-of-way charges, multiple taxation, approval delays, vandalism and fibre cuts running at an average of about 1,100 a week on the NCC's earlier count. The programme takes the national backbone from about 35,000 km to 125,000 km across all 36 states and more than 770 local government areas, financed by $500 million from the World Bank, $100 million from the EBRD and a €22 million EU grant. ALTON and ATCON both name state right-of-way pricing and damage to existing infrastructure, rather than the laying itself, as the risk to the investment. Also under Connectivity and Digital divides.

UK supports South Sudan fintech — ITWeb Africa, 26 August 2026 South Sudan

Bank of South Sudan governor Addis Ababa Othow met British ambassador David Ashley in Juba to discuss monetary policy, digital banking regulation and public sector financial management, with no agreement, figure or timeline announced beyond the meeting itself. The central bank and the finance ministry are engaging telecommunications companies and mobile money operators to integrate the National Payment System, which is intended to connect commercial banks directly with mobile network operators and move a cash-dominated economy onto interoperable electronic infrastructure. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech and Access to services.

Study finds South Korea DPI aid boosts exports and investment — Biometric Update, 26 August 2026

A study traces how South Korea has used concessional lending to export its own digital government platforms - customs and public procurement systems - and finds recipients adopt the administrative procedures and certification standards embedded in them, not only the software. It puts a number on the commercial return: a one percent rise in Korean digital public infrastructure aid is associated with a 0.44 percent rise in exports to the recipient within one to two years, strongest where internet penetration and logistics capacity are already good. The benefits are asymmetric, and the study says so. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Soberania digital e aposta na formação de jovens reforçadas com a entrada em funcionamento de novo centro tecnológico — OPaís, 26 August 2026 Angola

Angola's telecommunications ministry put a requalified government technology centre into service, presenting it as the place where state and citizen data will be stored and protected inside national territory. Minister Mário Oliveira said after visiting the facility on 25 August that protecting citizens' data and reinforcing digital sovereignty remain executive priorities, and placed the centre within the country's digital-transformation policies. Also under Data Storage and Training and skills.

Kenya Artificial Intelligence Accelerator Programme — Open Call for High-Impact AI Use Cases — Kenya Artificial Intelligence Programme (MICDE / Technopolis Development Authority / UNDP Kenya), 26 August 2026 Kenya

Kenya's information and digital economy ministry, its technopolis authority and the United Nations development programme opened a call for high-impact artificial intelligence use cases in public service delivery and the green transition, running 24 August to 6 September 2026 and open to public institutions, universities, research entities, startups and individual innovators. Successful applicants are offered stage-appropriate access to computing, data preparation and technical assistance rather than cash. Also under AI and Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Digital migration progress: What the numbers say — ITWeb South Africa, 26 August 2026 South Africa

South Africa's broadcasting digital migration programme told Parliament's communications committee on 25 August that 52,869 households registered for subsidised set-top boxes still cannot be located, down from about 80,000 at the previous meeting, and that no analogue switch-off date has been set pending a steering committee of broadcasters and stakeholders. A SITA-facilitated cleaning of registration data dating to September 2015 cut the installation target from 447,815 to 401,803 households, against which 290,506 installations are complete and verified. The department said the state has spent close to R2 billion on dual illumination, and MPs described the programme as failed and wasteful. Also under Connectivity and Digital divides.

Legislation and regulation

Tshwane shuts online food stores over non-compliance — ITWeb South Africa, 26 August 2026 South Africa

The City of Tshwane shut the Hatfield fulfilment premises of Zulzi, Yassir and Uber Eats Market on Friday 21 August in a health-department enforcement operation — Yassir and Uber Eats Market for allegedly trading without a Certificate of Acceptability or business licences, Zulzi over what the city describes as fraudulent compliance documents including a fraudulent liquor licence. The city says it will also investigate the sale and dispatch of medicines through the Zulzi platform, and that a digital storefront does not displace the municipal health, licensing and food-safety obligations attached to the physical premises behind it. Uber Eats says the merchant is unavailable on its app while the matter is addressed; Yassir says the store stays closed until the requirements are satisfied. Also under Digitalisation of sub-national government and ICT Industry.

Nigeria's NCC grants MTN conditional approval for IHS deal — TechCabal, 26 August 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's NCC granted MTN Nigeria a conditional Approval-in-Principle in mid-July for the acquisition of IHS Towers' Nigerian business, disclosed by the regulator on 25 August and separate from the competition clearance the FCCPC had already given. The conditions are compliance with the NCC's corporate governance guidelines, a bar on amending existing contracts as a consequence of the transaction, a prohibition on the deal conferring exclusivity on MTN Nigeria, and an investment plan with measurable milestones; final approval is withheld until the regulator confirms compliance. Nigeria carries 15,848 of IHS's roughly 28,000 African tower sites, more than half the portfolio, in a transaction valuing IHS at about $6.2 billion enterprise value and the stake purchase at about $2.2 billion. Also under ICT Industry and Connectivity.

Mozambique: Calls to strengthen data protection, digital space legislation — Club of Mozambique, 26 August 2026 Mozambique

The president of Mozambique's information and communication technologies institute told a Maputo debate that passing the recent data protection and digital space laws is the easier half, and called for institutional capacity, technical skills and working oversight and accountability mechanisms to enforce them - and for citizens to know what rights they now hold and how to use them. Also under Data protection.

Health data is Kenya's new land: We must own it before it's gone — The Standard, 26 August 2026 Kenya

A health policy analyst argues in The Standard that Kenyan health data is being collected to train AI medical devices sold globally while the communities it comes from receive nothing, citing a European inventor who sought a proof-of-concept study in a Nairobi settlement because European regulatory requirements were too demanding. He credits three convergent policy developments — the Pharmacy and Poisons Board's 2025 guidelines on AI-powered medical tools, the National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025–2030 naming data sovereignty a priority, and the Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026 tabled in the Senate — but says each stops short of settling who owns the training data or who profits when the tools go global. Also under Data protection and AI.

Data protection

Your NIN is your identity, stop giving it away — The Observer (Uganda), 26 August 2026 Uganda

Uganda's Personal Data Protection Office says about 14,000 organisations are registered to handle personal information while more than 500,000 entities — businesses, schools, hospitals and informal vendors — collect or process it outside the legal framework. The Observer argues that the gap turns routine demands for a National Identification Number into an open licence to harvest identity, and that individual vigilance cannot substitute for registration backed by audits and consequences. PDPO national director Baker Birikujja says collecting personal data without registration is a criminal offence. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS and Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Second Regular Session of the UNDP Executive Board 2026 — United Nations Development Programme, 26 August 2026

PM welcomes AI, warns of dangers of deepfakes in elections — Club of Mozambique, 26 August 2026 Mozambique

Opening a meeting of the regional forum of electoral commissions in Maputo, Mozambique's prime minister set digital disinformation and manipulated video against electoral integrity, and argued in the same speech for using artificial intelligence to administer elections - managing voter registers, communicating with voters and strengthening data analysis. What is asked for is stronger technical and operational capacity in the region's electoral bodies; no instrument or rule follows from the speech. Also under AI.

Cloud dependence exposes African AI startups to hidden cost risks - Expert — Nairametrics, 26 August 2026 Nigeria

An artificial intelligence and cloud infrastructure practitioner argues that renting computing capacity is not itself the risk for African startups - almost all of them rent rather than own - but that concentrating the workload with a single provider exposes growth and margins to that provider's terms. The argument is about dependence rather than cost, and is one practitioner's assessment with no survey behind it. Also under Data Storage and AI.

Ventures Platform's second fund closes at $84 million, almost double its first — TechCabal, 26 August 2026 Nigeria

Ventures Platform closed VP Pan-African Fund II at $84 million, 1.8 times its first institutional fund, adding the EBRD, Norfund, the Dutch family office Alphatron and the Ashesi University Foundation to the limited partners from the $64 million first close in November 2025. The firm will back roughly the same number of companies with larger cheques — up to $3 million, averaging about $1.5 million — targeting entry stakes of 10% to 12%, on founding partner Kola Aina's reading that secondary sales are its most reliable route to liquidity and that a small stake leaves nothing to sell. Nigeria's iDICE programme is an anchor limited partner and wrote one of the fund's largest individual cheques. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

USTDA Advances U.S. Digital Banking Technology in Nigeria — U.S. Trade and Development Agency, 26 August 2026 Nigeria

A United States trade agency awarded a training grant to a Nigerian commercial bank as it moves onto a hyperscale cloud platform sold by a Washington-based vendor, covering governance and financial management of cloud services for senior managers and non-technical adaptation training for staff. The agency's director framed it as strengthening American competitiveness in the market for sensitive digital infrastructure, which is the second thing the grant is for. No grant value is stated. Also under Data Storage, Digital Payments and Fintech, US / hyperscaler activities and Training and skills.

Safaricom Ethiopia Wins Praise for Rapid Subscriber and Network Growth — Tech Africa News, 26 August 2026 Ethiopia

The consortium board behind Ethiopia's second mobile operator reviewed five years of operation and put the pace in comparative terms: the Vodacom group chief executive said the operator reached its current subscriber scale in five years where the industry typically takes fifteen to twenty, and the Safaricom chief executive said the Ethiopian team built in four years half the number of towers Safaricom has deployed in Kenya over twenty-six. No subscriber or tower figure is given with either comparison. Also under ICT Industry.

Liquid targets Kenya connectivity boost — ITWeb Africa, 26 August 2026 Kenya

Kenya's ICT principal secretary John Tanui met Liquid Intelligent Technologies Kenya chief executive Neeraj Pradhan in Nairobi to discuss accelerating broadband and extending public Wi-Fi into public spaces and underserved communities, with no agreement, cost or timeline announced. The talks are framed against the Digital Superhighway target of 100,000 km of fibre and 25,000 public Wi-Fi hotspots across all 1,450 wards, backed by a $390 million World Bank programme and state budget allocations. Also under Access to services.

Cote d'Ivoire : 68 millions $ pour connecter 575 localites rurales — wearetech.africa, 26 August 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Côte d'Ivoire is continuing its FCFA 38 billion, about $68 million, Programme national de connectivité rurale to bring mobile and internet coverage to 575 localities, and set out its phase figures in a government communiqué of 24 August. Phase one, at FCFA 10 billion, was completed in 2024 with 160 radio sites serving 175 localities and about 221,267 inhabitants; phase two commits FCFA 18 billion for 240 further localities and phase three FCFA 10 billion for 160 more. The national broadband backbone stood at 5,207 km with 4G covering 65% of the territory in 2024. Also under Rural digital data capture and Digital divides.

Blu Label Unlimited smacked by R4.9bn Cell C write down — ITWeb South Africa, 26 August 2026 South Africa

Blu Label Unlimited booked a R4.9 billion full-year loss writing its Cell C stake down to the R9 billion the operator's November listing priced it at, a 297% year-on-year swing in net income. Stripping out the write-down, the year to May produced net profit after tax of R677 million on revenue of R9.4 billion; the company declared a 10c dividend, adopted a policy of paying out 30% to 50% of core headline earnings excluding Cell C, and has started buying back its own shares. It retains 49.53% of Cell C, whose market value of R8.7 billion now exceeds Blu Label's own R7.3 billion. Also under ICT Industry.

After the Cell C divorce, Blu Label is making a play for electricity — TechCabal, 26 August 2026 South Africa

A South African digital distribution group built on airtime, vouchers and payments is moving into electricity, holding a multi-year trading licence granted in February 2026, having identified around 400MW of potential capacity and planning to deploy up to 180MW in the near term. SMART Zambia's e-Government Division has equipped Sinazongwe Town Council in Southern Province with high-speed internet, telepresence systems, desktops and laptops, and solar power to keep the offices running through load-shedding. Digital Payments and Fintech

How a diverted tuition fee birthed a $450 million remittance platform — TechCabal, 26 August 2026 Ghana

PayAngel, founded in 2013 after a diverted tuition payment cost a relative their university place, routes diaspora remittances to verified schools, hospitals, insurers and retailers directly rather than to a family member trusted to forward them, identifying the payer through each institution's own reference system. The company says it has processed more than $450 million, has over 100,000 active remitters across 22 countries and an annualised revenue run rate of about $4 million, and holds regulatory approval in the United Kingdom, United States, Canada, Australia and Ghana. A sender transferring at least £100 a month qualifies their beneficiary for rolling life and hospitalisation cover, and a separate product lets African businesses collect from diaspora customers directly. Also under Access to services and ICT Industry.

Structure and History — Centre for Artificial Intelligence Research (CAIR), 26 August 2026 South Africa

South Africa's national artificial intelligence research centre is a distributed network rather than a building: nine established and two emerging research groups across eight universities, funded mainly by the science department and coordinated by the state research council, with its directorate in a Cape Town computer science department. It was established in 2011, and the research groups run from computational logic and knowledge representation to the ethics of artificial intelligence and artificial intelligence for development.

Intron's new voice model can follow African speakers who switch languages mid-sentence — TechCabal, 26 August 2026 NigeriaRwanda

Intron released Sahara v2.5, adding code-switching speech recognition in 12 African languages and what it says is the first African trilingual model, built for Rwanda's Kinyarwanda-English-French mix. On its own benchmarks and evaluation set the Lagos-founded company reports an average word error rate of 34.3% on code-switched African speech against 53.8% for Google's Gemini 3.6, and coverage of 31 languages after adding Nupe, Kanuri, Nigerian Fulfulde, Tigrinya, Kikuyu, Dholuo and Somali. It says the models are deployed at more than 40 organisations across six countries, with US patents filed on the trilingual algorithms. Also under ICT Industry and Access to services.

Egypt's Offshoring Sector Generates $5.2bn as AI and Digital Services Drive Growth — Tech Africa News, 26 August 2026 Egypt

Egypt generated $5.2 billion in offshoring services exports in 2025, across 252 companies operating 282 delivery centres, 177 of them multinational, employing more than 195,000 specialists. A prime-ministerial tour of Teleperformance, Concentrix, Vodafone Intelligent Solutions and Sutherland in Alexandria — which together employ nearly 15,000 there — was used to press the case for growing the industry outside Cairo and moving it from back-office work into generative-AI-enabled services. Also under ICT Industry and National statistics.

AI helps SASRIA forecast national risks — ITWeb South Africa, 26 August 2026 South Africa

South Africa's state-owned special risks insurer is combining artificial intelligence with human intelligence and social science, in collaboration with the state research council, to read social and behavioural signals for the conditions that precede unrest and destructive events. An executive told a summit panel that triangulating social media with other data can identify potential unrest weeks ahead; no accuracy figure or evaluation is offered for the claim. Also under National statistics.
