NITDA Inaugurates Taskforce to Drive Nigeria’s National Sovereign Cloud Initiative — Tech Africa News, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

NITDA's own account of the same inauguration sets out the sequence and the mandate: the taskforce follows the launch of the initiative's regulatory, technical, assurance and investment instruments, and is the principal multi-stakeholder mechanism for putting them into effect, with responsibilities spanning regulatory concurrence, institutional coordination, implementation monitoring, indigenous capacity and investment mobilisation. The agency states that it complements rather than replaces the statutory responsibilities of the regulators and agencies taking part. Also under Data Storage and Standards.

Nigeria launches national cloud taskforce — ITWeb Africa, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria has inaugurated the National Sovereign Cloud Initiative Implementation Taskforce, moving its sovereign-cloud programme from framework to implementation. The taskforce is to align regulators, government institutions and private operators behind technical standards, cloud governance, infrastructure assurance and investment, monitor progress and clear the bottlenecks that slow adoption. Two figures frame it: Nigerian organisations are estimated to spend about US$850 million a year on foreign cloud infrastructure, and the National Digital Cloud Policy targets US$750 million of private cloud and data-centre investment within 24 months. Also under Data Storage and Standards.

Niger Advances Construction of Local Electronics Plant for Smartphones, Computers and Tablets — Tech Africa News, 25 August 2026 Niger

Niger's communications and vocational-training ministers inspected the DHY Technologie electronics assembly plant under construction at Hamdallaye in Tillabery region on 20 August, with the authorities reporting the smartphone, computer and tablet facility more than 60% built after six months. The government frames locally assembled devices for citizens and public institutions as a way to build domestic technical capacity and cut dependence on imported equipment. Also under ICT Industry.

NAC Okays Framework To Build Nigeria's Digitised Modern Addressing System — ThisDay, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's National Addressing Council, chaired by the Vice-President, ratified the preliminary framework for a national digitised addressing system and named the science and technology ministry as its secretariat for technical implementation, systems integration, interoperability and standards. NIMC takes individual verification, the Corporate Affairs Commission business address verification, NIPOST postcode administration, and states and local governments implementation. The framework replaces the 2017 addressing policy, described at the meeting as obsolete, focused on mail delivery and silent on business address verification. Also under Registries and Standards.

Mercado & Finanças: 5G em Todas as Capitais Provinciais Até Junho de 2027 — Diário Económico (Moçambique), 25 August 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's INCM issued spectrum licences to Tmcel, Vodacom and Movitel carrying a June 2027 deadline for 5G service in every provincial capital, with the same package covering urban centres, industrial zones, economic poles and tourism destinations, and 4G obligations reaching border and rural areas in a later phase. Also provided for are 700,000 subsidised smartphones under a government-led, World Bank-supported programme starting at $5 million and able to grow to $25 million. Tmcel and Movitel representatives put on record that many target localities have no grid electricity, roads or backhaul. Also under Connectivity and Digital divides.

Licences, taxes, services... : le Liberia definit les regles du marche des telecoms par satellite — wearetech.africa, 25 August 2026 Liberia

The Liberia Telecommunications Authority has drawn up guidelines covering orbital and ground satellite services, with particular attention to direct-to-device, cybersecurity, data protection and national security. They span geostationary and non-geostationary constellations and cover gateway, VSAT, satellite broadband, backhaul, broadcasting, managed data and direct-to-device services, each requiring authorisation from the authority. The text still has to be signed and published in the National Gazette before it takes effect. Also under Connectivity and Cybersecurity.

INTIC engages Mozambican AI Association to promote national artificial intelligence strategy — TechReviewAfrica, 25 August 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's INTIC presented its draft National Artificial Intelligence Strategy to the Mozambican Association of Artificial Intelligence at a meeting chaired by board chairman Lourino Chemane, describing it as still under development through an open multi-sector consultation covering public service delivery, public-administration modernisation, innovation and productivity. No adoption date, budget or governance instrument for the strategy is stated. Also under AI.

China offers Botswana AI support — ITWeb Africa, 25 August 2026 Botswana

China's ambassador to Botswana has publicly offered artificial-intelligence cooperation in smart mining, digital government and talent training, pitching Chinese technology as a lever for the economic diversification Botswana is pursuing away from minerals. The offer is placed against Xi Jinping's July pledge at the Shanghai World AI Conference of 5,000 training places for developing countries and AI application cooperation centres for bodies including the African Union. It is a media note from an embassy, not an agreement. Also under China activities and AI.

Beyond digital adoption: The critical role of strategy implementation in Uganda's education operational efficiency — African Journal of Science, Technology, Innovation and Development, 25 August 2026 Uganda

A study of Uganda's education sector finds that digital adoption has no statistically significant direct effect on operational efficiency, and works only through strategy implementation, which fully mediates the relationship. Data came from structured questionnaires put to 55 administrators and teachers between May and June 2025, analysed by partial least squares structural equation modelling. The authors recommend that institutions and policymakers prioritise strategic execution, leadership support, digital infrastructure and continuous capacity building. Also under Training and skills and Sectoral management information systems.

Legislation and regulation

Who will control elections tech and servers? Bill languishes as 2027 nears — Daily Nation, 25 August 2026 Kenya

The Elections (Amendment) Bill 2024, whose clause 23 would vest ownership of the intellectual property in Kenya's election technology and access to its election servers in the IEBC, remains stalled in the National Assembly. It passed the Senate in December 2024 and was read a first time in February 2025, since when the Justice and Legal Affairs Committee has tabled no report. The provision is set against the 2017 refusal to open IEBC servers hosted in France and a vendor's 2022 refusal of server access on intellectual-property grounds. Also under Data protection, Cybersecurity and Data Storage.

Data protection

The Please Call Me millions: The data that didn’t exist — ITWeb South Africa, 25 August 2026 South Africa

A reconstruction of the twenty-year Please Call Me claim against Vodacom turns on the operator's position that the data needed to value the idea did not exist: it told the Constitutional Court it retains only six months of operational data and holds no record linking a Please Call Me message to any call it induced, so both sides' figures were modelled rather than measured. Court documents record that Vodacom decided in 2013 not to track the revenue, having been advised to, and the settlement reached at the end of 2025 is confidential, so the amount is not on the record. The case is a data-retention and record-keeping story as much as a compensation one. Also under ICT Industry.

No agreement, instrument, funding or timeline is announced; it is an engagement reported as discussion. Also under Cybersecurity and Regional collaboration.

ITU launches Sub-Saharan broadband push — ITWeb Africa, 25 August 2026 Malawi

The ITU has opened a five-day intermediate GIS and data-management course in Lilongwe for broadband-mapping practitioners from Malawi, Zimbabwe, Botswana and Zambia, hosted by the Malawi regulator MACRA, funded by the European Union and delivered by Esri South Africa College. It sits under the Africa-BB-Maps project, which runs to 2028 and is meant to establish or strengthen harmonised national broadband mapping in eleven sub-Saharan regulators, from Benin and Botswana to Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe. Also under Connectivity, Digital divides and National statistics.

Development of Data Centre Infrastructures Underpinning the Digital Economy (PIDA project prospectus) — African Union / AUDA-NEPAD (PIDA), 25 August 2026 CAR

It is costed at US$9m across the eleven countries, is a quarter of the way through, and is still at project-definition stage. Also under Data Storage and Cybersecurity.

Standards

New system rewrites producer price data — The Namibian, 25 August 2026 Namibia

The Namibia Statistics Agency has moved producer price index compilation off a spreadsheet system onto an automated processing engine, disclosed in the second-quarter bulletin released on 21 August and carrying retrospective revisions back to 2015. Missing prices are now imputed as a turnover-weighted average of observed price relatives within the product group rather than by unweighted class mean, imputation is capped at three consecutive periods before an item must be replaced, and automated validation flags extreme swings and entry errors across establishment files. Also under National statistics.

Mozambique picks Tablu Tech for digital trust infrastructure — Biometric Update, 25 August 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's INTIC has opened discussions with local partner Tablu Tech on a platform called MoDi Sign to modernise the country's advanced digital signature system, extending digital identity, signatures and public key infrastructure into a national digital trust layer for public and private transactions. It follows a May tender by the digital transformation agency ATDI for a national mobile digital identity and electronic signature infrastructure, and a February memorandum with Brazil's ITI covering credentialing, auditing, training and technical assistance for running a root certification authority. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Kagame Urges African Countries to Take Active Role in Shaping AI Future — Bantu Gazette, 25 August 2026 Rwanda

President Paul Kagame urged African countries to take an active role in shaping the future of artificial intelligence, telling a news conference in Kigali that the continent has the knowledge and talent to compete globally and should not accept being left behind. YC-backed startup ThriveAgric raises $3.93 million in commercial paper sale — TechCabal, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

ThriveAgric raised ₦5.3 billion, about $3.93 million, in the first series of a ₦50 billion commercial paper programme approved by Nigeria's securities regulator, oversubscribed against a ₦5 billion target. The proceeds fund working capital for aggregating smallholder produce for off-takers rather than production. The company says its operating system is accumulating the farmer transaction and farm-activity data intended to let financiers underwrite those farmers directly. Also under ICT Industry.

South Korea Approves $170 Million Loan for AI and Digital Training Institute in Tanzania — Tech Africa News, 25 August 2026 Tanzania

South Korea has approved a US$170 million concessional loan from its Economic Development Cooperation Fund to build an artificial-intelligence and digital training institute in Tanzania, covering campus and laboratory construction, programmes in AI, robotics, data analytics and the internet of things, equipment, curriculum and operations support, tied to procurement from Korean firms. Further training institutes and a large-scale data centre are said to be planned. The source gives no approval or signature date, only its own publication date, so the loan is not recorded as a dated commitment in the finance layer. Also under Training and skills and AI.

South African AI Startup Verascient Raises $1.2m to Scale Enterprise Platform — Tech Africa News, 25 August 2026 South Africa

South African enterprise AI startup Verascient raised $1.2 million, about R19.5 million, in an oversubscribed round backed by Founder Collective, Andrena Ventures, Cambridge Enterprise and Summit Ventures. Its platform builds workflows and agents over institutional knowledge scattered across email, spreadsheets, meeting records and legacy software, on a temporal knowledge graph that tracks how information changes while preserving provenance, access permissions and historical context. It is aimed first at financial services, insurance and logistics. Also under AI.

MTN has 5 million fintech users in Nigeria. It wants 30 million. — TechCabal, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

MTN has set a medium-term target of 30 million fintech customers in Nigeria against five million today, group chief executive Ralph Mupita telling TechCabal that converting 30% or even 50% of the telecom base is the measure of success. The operator is bringing in technology from Alibaba, Alipay and Ant International to compete with OPay, PalmPay and Moniepoint, and will increasingly lend off its own balance sheet rather than only through the partner banks that currently score its customer data for credit. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Lisk shuts down blockchain, cutting off a backer of African startups — TechCabal, 25 August 2026

Swiss blockchain company Lisk said it will discontinue its chain on 31 October and wind down its DAO, ending a funding and accelerator pipeline that had backed African early-stage Web3 companies including Azza and ClapMi in Nigeria, LovCash in South Africa and Afrikabal in Rwanda. It cited chain revenue insufficient to support the LSK token, proposes burning 100 million tokens to cut supply from 400 million to 300 million, and has arranged a migration path onto Celo. African blockchain startups raised $90.1 million in 2025, down 26.6% on the year. Also under ICT Industry.

Collective X adopts ‘always on’ approach to skills fund — ITWeb South Africa, 25 August 2026 South Africa

The South African non-profit Collective X has removed the fixed application windows on its R200 million Digital Skills Impact Fund, so employers can apply year-round for co-funding of work-integrated digital skills programmes. It reports more than 1,800 young people through the fund across all nine provinces, 97% of them absorbed into employment and over 75% still employed after six months, against targets of 40,000 digitally skilled workers a year by 2030 and 500,000 young people into digital roles by 2034. The R200 million is a standing application envelope rather than a commitment to a named recipient. Also under Training and skills.

2025 Africa Tech Venture Capital — Partech Partners, 25 August 2026

Partech's annual account of African tech venture capital reports that 2025 diverged from the global market's concentration on artificial intelligence, with growth instead in enterprise software, e-commerce, cleantech and mobility, and Series A and B activity picking up in both deal count and capital. Its headline finding is debt: debt financing passed its 2021 peak and made up almost half of all capital raised, which the authors read as a sign that more companies now have the cash-flow visibility and governance to borrow rather than dilute. The page carries no date of its own and is captured here as a reference study. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

The Rapid Support Forces ordered every public market in En Nahud, West Kordofan closed and Starlink services cut off indefinitely from 26 August. Tmcel begins ANGOSAT-2 satellite trials — ITWeb Africa, 25 August 2026 MozambiqueAngola

Mozambique's state operator Tmcel has begun proof-of-concept testing of Angola's ANGOSAT-2 satellite, a step towards a commercial agreement to carry communication and broadcasting signals across Mozambique over Angolan space infrastructure. It follows the public broadcaster's own trial transmitting television by the same satellite and implements a bilateral memorandum signed in June. Angola is running a parallel pilot with Telecom Namibia for satellite backhaul, both framed as work under the SADC regional infrastructure master plan. Also under Use of satellite data.

Fibre is changing Nigeria’s Internet. The hard part is getting everyone connected — TechCabal, 25 August 2026 Nigeria

Fibre has overtaken fixed wireless as Nigeria's largest fixed-broadband technology for the first time, with fibre-to-the-premises connections rising from 84,141 in late 2025 to about 265,000 by June, and the median fixed download speed up 155% since 2023 to 34.47 Mbps on Ookla's data. The gain is concentrated at the bottom of the distribution — the slowest tenth of connections improved almost threefold while the fastest tenth barely moved — so the country is raising its broadband floor rather than its ceiling. What follows is harder: affordability, the cost of rural build, and long-distance fibre concentrated among the few operators able to fund backbone, with the Wholesale Fixed Broadband Market Assessment the instrument meant to force sharing and open access. Also under Digital divides and Rural digital data capture.

Côte d'Ivoire – Raxio Group — Raxio Group, 25 August 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Raxio's own page for CIV1, its Côte d'Ivoire facility, describes the country's first carrier-neutral Tier III data centre, built at Grand Bassam inside the VITIB technology village 30 km from Abidjan, with 2,000 square metres of white space on major fibre routes and serving the wider UEMOA region. It is an undated operator product page captured today, so it records the facility's specification rather than any event. Also under Data Storage.

An Egyptian banking commentator argues that digital identity and remote account opening would cut banks' operating costs on branches, staff and paperwork, offset by investment in the technology and the cyber-security it requires, with the net effect positive and the larger gain accruing to customers through flexibility and financial inclusion. Somalia pushes for stronger adoption of eAqoonsi digital ID app — Biometric Update, 25 August 2026 Somalia

Somalia's identification authority renewed its public call for citizens to adopt the eAqoonsi digital ID app, restating that the app-held credential is legally equivalent to the physical card. The app has passed 50,000 downloads, against more than one million digital IDs issued as of November 2025 and a target of 15 million by 2029. An estimated $125 million is still needed to implement the World Bank-funded programme in full, and inclusion complaints over weak connectivity and low digital literacy were reported in April.

Police Nationale du Burkina Faso - CNIB — Police Nationale du Burkina Faso, 25 August 2026 Burkina Faso

The national police's standing page for Burkina Faso's identity card sets out what the outgoing credential is and how it is checked: compulsory from the age of fifteen, valid for ten years, and matched at the identification office against demographic similarity and fingerprints, with facial biometrics added to that stack in 2009. The page carries no date of its own, and the card it describes is the one the regional biometric card has been replacing since December 2025. Also under Access to services.
