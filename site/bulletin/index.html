رئيسة الحكومة تُصدر منشورا يتعلق بتطوير وحوكمة المؤسسات والمنشآت العمومية — Assabah News, 2 September 2026 Tunisia

Tunisia's head of government issued a circular directing ministers, secretaries of state, heads of public enterprises and state controllers to adopt strategic action plans on the governance and performance of public establishments. Among its legal bases is the 2020 decree-law on electronic data exchange between public bodies. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Why Kenyan Creators Are Pushing Back on the YouTube Tax — KICTANet, 2 September 2026 Kenya

Kenyan creators are contesting the withholding tax on video-platform earnings, which sits alongside the digital services tax, value-added tax on digital marketplace supply and the digital asset tax. The civil-society network carrying the campaign published its own brief on digital-tax reform in 2024. Also under Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Transformation Numerique : La Cote d'Ivoire et Mistral Renforcent leur Cooperation pour une Intelligence Artificielle au Service des Ivoiriens — Portail Officiel du Gouvernement de Côte d'Ivoire (distribué par APO Group / Africa Newsroom), 2 September 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Côte d'Ivoire's Ministry of Digital Transition published its own communiqué on the strategic partnership it signed with the French AI company Mistral on 17 August in Abidjan. The work is a study and strategic audit of the national AI ecosystem, mapping the actors and resources available, and sits inside the Feuille de Route Numérique 2026-2028 and its forty structuring projects. No sum is stated and no financier of the audit is named. Also under AI and MoUs and other agreements.

Premier Forum national sur l'intelligence artificielle : Djibouti prend le virage de la révolution technologique — La Nation (Djibouti), 2 September 2026 Djibouti

Djibouti opened its first national forum on artificial intelligence at the Palais du Peuple on 2 and 3 September, organised by the ministry for the digital economy and innovation under the president's patronage. The framing is explicitly sovereignty: AI as a lever of independence, innovation and development rather than a technology to be received. The forum sits against Vision Djibouti 2035 and UNESCO's readiness assessment. Also under AI and Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Le Bénin recherche un expert international pour renforcer son système d'identité numérique — wearetech.africa, 2 September 2026 Benin

Benin's ANIP, through the World Bank-financed WURI programme, called for expressions of interest in an international expert on digital public infrastructure strategy, to define a sustainable economic model and institutional framework for the identification agency. The 60-day mission spread over five months covers a strategic diagnosis, a comparison of international DPI and national identity models, and a roadmap for financing, operation, maintenance and modernisation. Applications closed 8 September 2026. The question being asked is how a national ID agency pays for itself once the donor project ends. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Kenya’s new phone number rules will make recycling costlier for telcos — TechCabal, 2 September 2026 Kenya

Kenya's Communications Authority published draft procedures for deactivating and recycling mobile numbers: deactivation only after three consecutive months without revenue-generating activity, then three further months of contact attempts by message and the details given at registration, with at-risk numbers published in national media. The document is out for consultation, with memoranda due 11 September 2026. Also under Data protection and Digital Identity and CRVS.

Legislation and regulation

US pushes looser approach to AI regulation, while EU pushes new law — Al Jazeera, 2 September 2026

At a G20 innovation ministerial in Chapel Hill on 1 September, the United States urged other governments to adopt the Carolina Principles, a framework arguing that regulation should not single out particular technologies. The European Commission moved the other way in the same week, sending information requests to more than 30 AI companies worldwide under the AI Act. The two positions are the choice most African governments drafting AI rules will be asked to pick between. Also under US / hyperscaler activities, EU activities and AI.

Starlink goes live in Uganda; price comparisons against MTN, Airtel — Pulse Uganda, 2 September 2026 Uganda

Prices published with the Ugandan launch put a residential 100 Mbps package at Shs203,704 a month against an estimated Shs2.3 million to start, plus a Shs437,036 regulatory charge — against fibre offers from the two mobile incumbents advertised at Shs110,000 and Shs99,000 for the same speed. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

POPIA grace period limits regulator's fines — ITWeb South Africa, 2 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Information Regulator called the compliance grace period the data protection Act's key weakness: a public body that remedies a violation within the stated timeframe cannot then be fined. Its chair said the high-value fines issued since enforcement powers commenced in July 2021 have largely not materialised, with bodies either complying in time or challenging in court. Also under Data protection.

Osinbajo, African Lawyers Seek Accountability From Tech Firms Over Risks to Children — Premium Times, 2 September 2026 Egypt

African lawyers meeting in Cairo reviewed and prioritised six pillars of a proposed declaration on children's rights and design accountability, covering safety-by-design requirements, restrictions on exploitative targeting and manipulative practices, and age-appropriate design standards. The session chair argued companies cannot engineer risk into products and then leave children and parents to manage it. Also under Data protection and Regional collaboration.

ICASA turns to ConCourt to clarify its powers — ITWeb South Africa, 2 September 2026 South Africa

ICASA has asked the Constitutional Court to settle whether it may condone a late licence-renewal notice from a class licensee, having lost the question in both the Pretoria High Court and the Supreme Court of Appeal to Open Heaven, a Hammanskraal community radio station that filed two weeks late. The regulator reads section 11(9) of the Electronic Communications Act as granting that power only for individual licences, and section 19(2) as deliberately withholding it for class licences; Open Heaven argues the Act leaves latitude where good cause is shown. The court heard the matter on 27 August and has not ruled. Also under Connectivity.

Govt welcomes Starlink internet service provider — New Vision, 2 September 2026 Uganda

Starlink began commercial service in Uganda, ending the regulatory process that followed a memorandum and a five-year operating licence signed with the communications regulator in May 2026. The licence requires a national gateway and physical point of presence, an operational office, and registration of every device activated in the country. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

Ghana's NCA Receives Four Applications for 5G Spectrum Licences — Tech Africa News, 2 September 2026 Ghana

Ghana's National Communications Authority closed applications for 700MHz, 2.3GHz and 3GHz mobile-broadband spectrum on 2 September with four applications received, and opened Envelope A under the published Selection and Award Procedure. Envelope B, holding the Best Price Offer, stays sealed. The authority was careful to say that opening Envelope A determines nothing about the eligibility, compliance or completeness of any application. Also under Connectivity.

Data protection

Police Launch Vigilant App for Public Reporting of Financial Fraud — Nigeria Info FM, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

The Nigeria Police Force launched the NPF Vigilant App on 1 September at its conference centre in Jabi, Abuja, a public channel for reporting suspected financial fraud, uploading evidence and tracking a case through to feedback. It was built by the Force ICT Department with financial-sector stakeholders and is meant to coordinate police handling with banks; the Central Bank of Nigeria, NIBSS, banks and fintechs attended. Inspector-General Olatunji Disu said the technology must be deployed lawfully and ethically, with respect for privacy and data protection. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Cybersecurity.

Africa cannot afford to scale AI faster than it can govern it — BusinessDay NG, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

Roseline Ilori of Bridge57 Solutions argues that Africa cannot scale AI faster than it can govern it, and that the question worth asking is not how quickly the continent adopts the technology but whether the systems being deployed are safe, fair, transparent and accountable. The gap she names is institutional rather than technical: regulators unable to supervise automated systems, businesses that do not understand the risks, and citizens with no way to challenge an algorithmic decision. Also under Public debate and participation in policymaking and AI.

Kenya and Lesotho Deepen ICT Regulatory Cooperation on National Addressing System — Tech Africa News, 2 September 2026 KenyaLesotho

Kenya's Communications Authority is hosting a Lesotho Communications Authority delegation to benchmark Kenya's National Addressing System and the legal framework behind it. Kenya's system is designed to give every street, building and parcel of land a verifiable address, supporting last-mile e-commerce delivery and emergency response, and the authority has driven the draft National Addressing System Bill, 2025 with standards developed jointly with the Kenya Bureau of Standards. No visit dates are stated. Also under Registries.

New investments

South Africa's startup funding is recovering, but founders still struggle to find fit — TechCabal, 2 September 2026 South Africa

South African startups raised $335.9 million in 2025, more than three times the $100.4 million of 2024, across 42 rounds rather than 25, with average deal size nearly doubling to $7.99 million. The recovery is uneven: only three of the 42 rounds carried a debt component, and continent-wide the first half of 2026 held roughly flat at $1.44 billion while deal count fell from 252 to 174 and early-stage startups took just $9 million against $25 million a year earlier. The argument is that the money is not absent but badly matched to stage. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

Instarc Secures EUR1.25 Million to Scale Cloud-Native Compliance Platform in South Africa — Tech Africa News, 2 September 2026 South Africa

Instarc, a Tallinn-based regulatory-technology firm, raised a €1.25 million strategic investment from HFO Investments to commercialise its compliance platform for South African accountable institutions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act. The platform covers client onboarding, identity and ownership verification, configurable workflows, document management, retrievable audit records and APIs into existing systems, and the company says it is built to adapt to other regulatory regimes. The date the investment was agreed is not stated in the reporting held. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

Alan acquires Senegal's Tanel in rare Francophone healthtech exit — TechCabal, 2 September 2026 SenegalCôte d'Ivoire

French health insurer Alan acquired Dakar-based Tanel in a deal that closed in June 2026 for an undisclosed sum, its first move into Africa and a full exit for Ventures Platform, AAIC Investment and a group of angels. Tanel is a licensed insurer carrying its own risk rather than administering another underwriter's cover, selling employer health plans in Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire across a contracted network of more than 1,200 pharmacies and providers, covering about 70,000 lives and profitable in 2025. Alan had backed Tanel's $2.1 million seed round in 2024, giving it two years of visibility before it approached the founders, who stay on as general managers. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

Orange and Free to back La Reunion-South Africa subsea cable — CNBC Africa (Reuters), 2 September 2026 South Africa

Two French operators will fund the €93 million cable between Réunion and South Africa, one acting as landing party and its marine subsidiary laying infrastructure designed by a French manufacturer. The cable is scheduled to enter service in 2028, replacing a system commissioned in 2002; the partners did not disclose their respective shares. Also under EU activities.

MTN Nigeria targets fibre gaps with FlyX — ITWeb Africa, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

MTN Nigeria launched FlyX, a 5G fixed-wireless home broadband service for locations where indoor reception is weak or its FibreX fibre product is unavailable, at ₦30,000 for 30 days at 50Mbps and ₦45,000 at 100Mbps, both unlimited. The operator holds a target of eight million Nigerian homes on fibre within three years and names lengthy approvals, the physical demands of cable installation and slow cost recovery as what stands in the way. Fixed wireless is the faster-to-deploy substitute rather than the plan.

MTN Nigeria is making more money but earning less from each customer — TechCabal, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

MTN Nigeria's average revenue per user fell 8.82% to $3.41 in the second quarter of 2026, its first decline since Q2 2025, even as first-half service revenue rose 25.9% to ₦2.99 trillion and subscribers grew 8.9% to 92.2 million. Naira ARPU also fell, 1.67% to ₦5,101, so the drop is not an exchange-rate artefact: 7.5 million subscribers were added in the half, against inflation of 15.91%, diluting the average. Nigeria supplied 30.64% of MTN Group's half-year revenue but ranks eighth of thirteen group markets on ARPU, against Ghana's $6.66. Also under ICT Industry.

Ministro Mário Oliveira inaugura ZAP Fibra em Benguela — Angop - Agência Angola Press, 2 September 2026 Angola

Angola's telecommunications ministry inaugurated an operator's fibre network in Benguela province, the operator having installed a data centre at Lobito as the platform behind its internet and television services along the coastline. The minister named nationwide fibre coverage as the objective and said a submarine cable landing at Benguela is being prepared. Also under Data Storage.

Free investit massivement dans un câble sous-marin pour sécuriser la connexion de La Réunion — Univers Freebox, 2 September 2026 South Africa

A French operator set out its investment in the Réunion to South Africa cable, one of four consortium members behind a system carrying 16 fibre pairs at 20 Tbps each. The consortium is wholly French and about 79 per cent of the cost rests on public bodies. Also under EU activities.

FG Set to Boost Telecoms Connectivity in Abia Rural Communities as Deputy Speaker Kalu Secures NUCAP Project for Bende — ThisDay, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's communications ministry named a federal constituency in Abia State a priority area under its universal communications access programme. A tower on the Umuahia–Ohafia road is nearing completion, and a preliminary assessment indicates five to eight further sites may be needed to cover the local government area. Also under Digital divides.

\"RéuNION\" : bientôt un nouveau câble sous-marin entre La Réunion et l'Afrique du Sud — Outre-mer La 1ère (France Télévisions), 2 September 2026 South Africa

The Réunion regional authority launched RéuNION, a submarine cable from Saint-Paul to Durban costing €93.1 million and carrying 16 fibre pairs at 20 Tbps each. It is funded by €30m from the EU's regional development fund, €20m from the Connecting Europe Facility and €43.1m from the consortium, and must be built within three years under the European funding terms. Also under EU activities.

Cybersecurity

IEC shoots down ANC's glitch claims ahead of election — TimesLIVE, 2 September 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Electoral Commission rejected claims that a system failure blocked a party's candidate submissions for the November local elections, its chief electoral officer saying the commission held no reports of a non-functional online nomination system on deadline day. More than 140,000 candidates have been nominated for about 10,500 council seats, to be scrutinised between 4 and 16 September. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Ghana sharpens digital finance security — ITWeb Africa, 2 September 2026 Ghana

A second account of the same National Cyber Security Awareness Month launch, adding the Financial Industry Command Security Operations Centre as standing infrastructure for threat-intelligence sharing among Ghana's regulated financial institutions. See the Cyber Security Authority's launch entry for the campaign itself. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Ghana Cyber Security Authority Launches 2026 Awareness Month to Strengthen Digital Finance Security — Tech Africa News, 2 September 2026 Ghana

Ghana's Cyber Security Authority launched the 2026 National Cyber Security Awareness Month on 2 September, themed on securing the country's digital finance ecosystem and observed each October. Director-general Divine Selase Agbeti pressed banks, fintechs, payment service providers, insurers and merchants to treat cyber resilience as a core governance responsibility rather than a regulatory box, warning that lost trust in digital payments would undermine the wider digital economy. CSA figures put reported incidents at 2,008 in the first half of 2025 against 1,317 a year earlier, a 52% rise, with reported losses of GH¢14.94 million, more than 90% of it online fraud and impersonation. Also under Literacy and Digital Payments and Fintech.

Cameroun : Abe Marcellinus prémunit les PME contre les cybermenaces — wearetech.africa, 2 September 2026 Cameroon

A profile of Cameroonian engineer Abe Kelvin Bemarc Marcellinus, whose company WorqSphere, founded 2025, gives small and medium businesses device monitoring, threat detection and remote isolation of a compromised or stolen machine that keeps working offline. It holds critical data locally through power and network outages and counts electricity cuts in attendance tracking, and an AI assistant translates security alerts into plain language for firms with no cybersecurity staff. Also under Innovation ecosystem.

AI now enables 55% of cybercrime in Africa as losses hit $484m – CID — GBC Ghana Online (Ghana Broadcasting Corporation), 2 September 2026 Ghana

Ghana's police CID put Interpol's 2026 African Cyber Threat Assessment on the record at the awareness month launch: AI now enables 55% of reported cybercrime in Africa, with related losses of $484 million. CID director-general COP Lydia Yaako Donkor described generative AI supplying convincing phishing messages, cloned voices and synthetic images and video, with cryptocurrency, mule accounts and fraudulent investment schemes moving the proceeds — an industrialised, borderless ecosystem rather than isolated offending. She tied Ghana's exposure directly to the spread of mobile money, internet banking and instant transfers. Also under AI and Digital Payments and Fintech.

Rural digital data capture

Why an Imperial PhD candidate trusts herds of cattle more than satellites — TechCabal, 2 September 2026 Nigeria

A career profile of Nigerian geospatial engineer Kayode Adeniyi that carries unusually direct field evidence about data systems designed away from the places they run in: a 2021 Ministry of Education library survey lost weeks of collected data because the app assumed the connectivity of Abuja's Garki and Wuse, and was rebuilt offline-first around SQLite. His flood modelling in Lere LGA, Kaduna treats herder observation as a higher-revisit sensor than satellite imagery, and a flood-warning system he built issues alerts in Hausa, Yoruba, Efik and Ijebu. Also under Use of satellite data and AI.

Côte d'Ivoire : Koffi Nusiko Dabla crée un marché virtuel pour les agrotransformateurs — wearetech.africa, 2 September 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

A profile of Koffi Nusiko Dabla, Togolese and based in Côte d'Ivoire, who co-founded Afoomarket in 2021 as an online marketplace for African agro-processors. Sellers run their own storefront, and the platform carries ordering, card and mobile money payment, a local delivery partner network, seller vetting and WhatsApp support, so a small producer gets a commercial presence online without building the infrastructure for one. Also under Innovation ecosystem.
