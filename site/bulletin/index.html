Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

World Bank raises Africa’s 2026 growth forecast, urges AI investments — Reuters via CNBC Africa, 6 October 2026 Sub-Saharan Africa

The World Bank's October Africa Economic Update urges Sub-Saharan governments to invest in AI for productivity and jobs, pointing to low-cost applications on affordable devices, shared data centres and stronger data-protection laws. It puts the region's 2026 growth at 4.3%, 0.3 of a point above its April forecast. Also under AI.

WOAN is dead but measurable interventions still imperative — ITWeb South Africa, 6 October 2026 South Africa

South Africa's withdrawal of the requirement to reserve high-demand spectrum for a wireless open access network ends a seven-year impasse and frees that spectrum for licensing by the regulator. Commentators in this analysis ask how the competition and transformation aims the network was meant to serve will now be delivered, and press for measurable obligations in its place. Also under Connectivity and Public debate and participation in policymaking.

ICT Minister Mavetera directs Potraz to establish unit to police operator deals — The Herald (Zimbabwe), 6 October 2026 Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe's ICT minister asked the telecommunications regulator to consider a dedicated unit to oversee operators' commercial arrangements, mediate disputes and enforce active infrastructure sharing. She also wants the Universal Service Fund to reach every marginalised ward and the regulator's sharing and tower-relocation directives evaluated in 2027. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

FCDO Answered 78 Questions. ICT4D Came Up Zero Times — ICTworks, 6 October 2026 Africa

A commentary notes that across 78 questions at the UK development minister's first parliamentary scrutiny session on 15 September, nobody raised digital development, connectivity or digital public infrastructure. The author reads that as the sector being moved out of the development brief rather than merely short of budget. Also under Public debate and participation in policymaking.

Legislation and regulation

Egypt: Personal Data Protection Law Update — Baker McKenzie, 7 October 2026 Egypt

Egypt's Personal Data Protection Center opened its electronic portal for accreditation, licence and permit applications in early October, weeks before the data-protection law's compliance grace period ends on 31 October 2026, on a law firm's account. Applying needs a Digital Egypt login tied to an Egyptian national ID, so foreign controllers and processors must act through an authorised local representative. Also under Data protection.

Problem with plan for digital IDs in South Africa — MyBroadband, 6 October 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Home Affairs missed its first-quarter target to sign a work order starting procurement of digital ID hosting infrastructure, its quarterly report to Parliament's committee says. It also missed revising the National Identification Registration Bill for public comment, and now aims to finalise the procurement in the second quarter. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

El Gobierno pone en marcha el Boletín Oficial del Estado digital — Guinea Ecuatorial Press (Oficina de Información y Prensa), 6 October 2026 Eq. Guinea

Equatorial Guinea's government announced a digital edition of its official gazette at boe.gob.gq, giving free online access to laws, decrees, ministerial orders and circulars with search of past publications. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Data protection

Zambia, US finalise $3.6 billion health deal after public outcry over contentious clauses — Zambia Monitor, 6 October 2026 Zambia

A second account of the Zambian health minister's briefing puts the money to be mobilised for the sector at US$3.6 billion and gives Thursday as the signing day. The minister said the contested clauses on data protection, intellectual property, benefit sharing and specimens have all been reviewed, and that the specimen-sharing obligation is gone entirely. Also under US / hyperscaler activities.

Zambia Ready To Sign US$1.5 Billion US Health Deal After Specimen-Sharing Clause Is Dropped — Lusaka Times, 6 October 2026 Zambia

Zambia's health minister said the five-year health memorandum with the United States is finalised and due for signature this week, after negotiators removed a clause requiring Zambia to share specimens from its disease-surveillance system. The revised text also addresses health data, intellectual property and benefit sharing; this account puts US funding at US$1.5 billion over five years, tapering annually, and an opposition party wants the final text published before signing. Also under Digital sovereignty and US / hyperscaler activities.

Nigeria’s NDPC and NOA Sign MoU to Expand Data Protection Awareness — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's data protection commission and the National Orientation Agency signed a memorandum on joint public awareness work. It covers training for the agency's staff, coordinated campaigns across traditional and digital media, and guidance on applying the 2023 data-protection Act to the agency's own mandate. Also under Literacy.

GID tells court it cannot access Semlex ID data — Daily Observer (Gambia), 6 October 2026 Gambia

A Gambia Immigration Department officer told a court on 6 October that the department can no longer reach data generated under the former Semlex biometric ID system, its access having ended with the contract. That leaves open who holds the historical identity records, although Semlex says it handed the system to the government in March 2024. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Regional collaboration

Niger-Algérie : lancement à Assamaka de l'exploitation de la dorsale transsaharienne à fibre optique — Agence Nigérienne de Presse, 6 October 2026 NigerAlgeria

Niger and Algeria launched operation of the fibre link between In Guezzam and the Arlit-Agadez region at a ceremony at Assamaka, on the border, on 6 October, chaired by the two communications ministers. Algeria's account gives a starting capacity of 100 Gbps. Also under Connectivity.

L'Algérie et le Niger lancent l'exploitation de la dorsale transsaharienne à fibre optique — Algérie Eco (reporting Algérie Presse Service), 6 October 2026 AlgeriaNiger

The Algerian account of the Assamaka ceremony, summarised above, says the link was built by Algerie Telecom and Niger Telecom, starts at 100 Gbps and can be expanded as traffic grows. Also under Connectivity.

Coopération Niger-Algérie : arrivée du ministre Hamani Kargné à Agadez pour le lancement des travaux d'interconnexion par fibre optique — Ministère de la Communication et des Nouvelles Technologies de l'Information (Niger), 6 October 2026 NigerAlgeria

Niger's communications ministry reported its minister's arrival at Agadez on 5 October with an Algerian delegation, describing the mission as the launch of fibre interconnection works between the two countries; the ceremony itself is summarised above. Also under Connectivity.

Bakel : inauguration de l’interconnexion des “backbones” du Sénégal et du Mali — APS, 6 October 2026 SenegalMali

Senegal's and Mali's state fibre operators inaugurated the interconnection of their national backbones at the Kidira-Diboli border crossing on 5 October, to secure and diversify routes between the two countries. No capacity or cost is given. Also under Connectivity.

TikTok sued over Nigeria blackout — ITWeb Africa, 6 October 2026 Nigeria

A second report of the suit over TikTok access in four northern Nigerian states, summarised above, frames it as a test of platform accountability and digital rights. Also under Access to services.

Sudanese Banks and the Post-War Opportunity: Can We Turn a Cash-Based Economy into a Digital Economy? — Sudan Horizon, 6 October 2026 Sudan

An opinion piece argues that Sudan's banks are investing too slowly in electronic services at a moment when war and high inflation have made cash costly and risky and disrupted branch networks. The author presents the post-war period as a chance to build an economy less dependent on cash. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech and Access to services.

Réseaux sociaux : après 9 mois, l’aveu du porte-parole de la Présidence relance la polémique — Gabon Mail Infos, 6 October 2026 Gabon

Gabon's presidential spokesman said on radio that he uses a VPN, outside the law, to get round the social-media suspension the communications authority imposed in February 2026. The outlet reports the suspension still in force and the authority silent on any sanction. Also under Access to services.

Foundation sues as TikTok restricts access to Kano, Kebbi, others — Punch Newspapers, 6 October 2026 Nigeria

The Foundation for Digital Justice filed a fundamental-rights suit against TikTok at the Federal High Court in Abuja on 5 October. It alleges that users in Kaduna, Kano, Kebbi and Sokoto states have been restricted since mid-September while the rest of Nigeria keeps full access, and asks for an order restoring it. Also under Access to services.

MoUs and other agreements

Un partenariat de près de 88 milliards FCFA pour développer la production locale de solutions technologiques — APS, 6 October 2026 Senegal

Senegal's telecommunications ministry signed a US$150 million partnership convention, nearly CFA 88 billion, with an Africa investment council and the Indian firm Blue Cloud Softech Solutions under the Senegal Digital Factory project. It covers local assembly of electronic devices, quality control, certification and intellectual property; the ministry puts computer hardware imports at CFA 419 billion a year. Also under ICT Industry and Innovation ecosystem.

Connectivity

Who was held accountable for EPTC pension crisis?- Sen. Tony — The Independent News Eswatini, 6 October 2026 Eswatini

Eswatini senators asked on 5 October whether anyone had been held to account for the pension crisis at the state posts and telecommunications corporation, after Parliament approved the government guarantee for its US$26 million World Bank loan under the Digital Eswatini Project. The ICT minister said E26 million of the loan goes to the post office pension fund, affecting about 500 employees, and that the bank will manage and audit the loan. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Vodacom Western Cape Invests R500 Million to Expand 5G and Digital Connectivity — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026 South Africa

Vodacom says it is investing more than R500 million in its Western Cape network this financial year. About R380 million goes to modernising the radio network at more than 50 sites, with 4G capacity upgrades, new 5G sites and backup power in townships including Khayelitsha, Delft and Mitchells Plain. Also under Access to services.

Burkina Faso/Transformation digitale : 222 procédures de dématérialisation engagées au premier trimestre 2026 — C'Finance, 6 October 2026 Burkina Faso

Burkina Faso's digital ministry reports 46.70% average physical execution of its minister's 20-action objectives contract at 30 June 2026. In the half year it described 222 administrative procedures for moving online, connected 10 more sites to the government network, counted 120 official platforms hosted abroad for repatriation and drafted nine implementing texts for the information-systems security law. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov, Digital sovereignty and Cybersecurity.

BoFiNet launches Digital Delta IXP — ITWeb Africa, 6 October 2026 Botswana

Botswana's state fibre operator BoFiNet announced the launch of the Digital Delta Internet Exchange Point, where service providers, mobile operators and content and cloud providers can exchange local traffic inside the country. It builds on the operator's Tier III-certified data centre.

Tunisia’s Orange and Focus Partner to Expand Local Cloud Services — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026 Tunisia

Orange Tunisie and Focus Tunisie announced a partnership to offer companies cloud and infrastructure resources hosted and operated in Tunisia, pitched on control over where data sits. No value, capacity or start date is given. Also under ICT Industry.

Can Saudi Arabia's AI projects benefit Africa amid ongoing conflict? — Connecting Africa, 6 October 2026 Africa

An analysis asks whether Saudi Arabia's spending on data centres, AI and cloud can reach Africa while the kingdom is under attack from the Houthis. It cites the US$300 million committed to the infrastructure company WIOCC by a Saudi investor with the Africa Finance Corporation, and an investor's view that the conflict does not reduce Saudi capacity to do business. Also under Gulf/UAE activities, AI and Digital sovereignty.

Cybersecurity

The biggest AI security risk may already be inside your company — TechCabal, 6 October 2026 South Africa

Half of 903 South Africans surveyed had got round their employer's restrictions on AI tools in the past year, an Oliver Wyman partner told a cybersecurity roundtable in Johannesburg, citing a report due in November. He said 35% had seen AI-generated work passed off as human, and that consumer trust in AI rose from 11% in 2023 to 44% in 2026. Also under AI.

CERT-SC takes the lead in third Seychelles National Cyberdrill — Seychelles Nation, 6 October 2026 Seychelles

Seychelles' national computer emergency response team designed and delivered the country's third national cyberdrill itself for the first time. The two-day exercise tests how organisations detect, respond to and recover from cyberattacks.

Mozambique launches its first Design Lab to integrate digital public services — Club of Mozambique, 6 October 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's digital transformation agency held the country's first Digital Service Delivery Design Lab, bringing public bodies and partners together to pick priority services, map how citizens use them now and redesign them. The process runs in five stages, ending in assigned responsibilities and action plans. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Digital Identity and CRVS.

FinTech Players Call for Interoperable Systems to Drive Uganda’s Digital Economy — Nile Post, 6 October 2026 Uganda

Fintech firms, regulators and development partners at Uganda's eighth annual fintech conference called for interoperable systems, shared digital infrastructure and stronger data standards, to move from separate products towards connected services. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

B3 : le ministère de l’Intérieur dévoile sa nouvelle stratégie — Tuniscope, 6 October 2026 Tunisia

Tunisia's interior ministry, answering a deputy's written question, said its plan for the criminal-record extract rests on digitising the record through interconnection with the justice ministry, to update judgments, and the finance ministry, to enter fines automatically. The aim is faster issue of the extract. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Digital Identity and CRVS

State to Expand Digital Birth Registration to SHA Hospitals Within 3 Months — Kenyans.co.ke, 6 October 2026 Kenya

Kenya will extend electronic birth notification to every hospital contracted by the Social Health Authority within three months, by December 2026, the immigration principal secretary said. Newborns notified this way receive a Unique Personal Identifier, and talks with the health ministry on linking civil registration to hospital systems are complete. Also under Registries.

Somalia Moves to Link National ID with Public Services — Goobjoog News, 6 October 2026 Somalia

Somalia's identity authority is moving to link its registration services to HALMEEL, the national unified public service delivery centre, where an area has been set aside for it. The aim is one national identity across several government services; the integration is planned and not yet in place. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Caravane nationale de sensibilisation du projet WURI : l'étape de Niamey officiellement lancée — Agence Nigérienne de Presse, 6 October 2026 Niger

The Niamey stage of Niger's national awareness caravan on civil registration and identification opened on 6 October under the World Bank-supported WURI project. The two-day session is to take stock of the caravan's regional stages; the project has been restructured to speed up civil registration and biometric identification. Also under Registries and Access to services.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Zimbabwe eyes India’s UPI tech for national payments network; NPCI talks may wrap by October 31 — Moneycontrol, 6 October 2026 Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe's central bank is negotiating with NPCI International to build a real-time payments system on India's UPI technology, linking banks, mobile-money operators and fintechs. The governor told Bloomberg an agreement is possible by 31 October 2026; cross-border payments and remittances are not part of the first deployment. Also under India activities.

Why Airtel Money’s $7bn IPO doesn’t include its Nigerian fintech business — TechCabal, 6 October 2026 Nigeria

Airtel Money's London listing leaves out its Nigerian business. Its prospectus, as reported, says a Central Bank of Nigeria directive required it to transfer its 25% stake in SmartCash back to Airtel's Nigerian network company, for US$3 million; Nigeria made up 1.24% of the unit's quarterly revenue and 6.02% of its customers at June 2026. Also under ICT Industry.

The strategy behind Equity Bank’s new digital wallet — TechCabal, 6 October 2026 Kenya

Equity Bank's fintech arm, Finserve Africa, has launched a wallet called EMMA, reached by Android application, short code or the Equitel SIM toolkit and opened with an identity document and a facial check. Users can send money to M-PESA merchants, Airtel Money and bank accounts; the analysis reads it as the bank's bid to keep everyday payments, and the data behind them, on its own platform.

KCB Kenya Partners With Stima Sacco to Expand Digital Financial Access — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026 Kenya

KCB Bank Kenya and Stima Sacco launched a link that lets the Sacco's members deposit straight into their accounts through the bank's mobile application. KCB says its digital Sacco products have grown from 345 to 407, without dating either count. Also under Access to services.

Registries

KZN launches the Public Works and Infrastructure Property Finding Portal — SAnews, 6 October 2026 South Africa

KwaZulu-Natal is launching a portal listing its public works properties, after an audit of all 10,067 assets and the release of a first batch of 81 unused buildings and land parcels. Organisations and developers can identify assets to lease or reuse, and residents can log complaints about them. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Attorney General declares 2021 digital number plates contract null, illegal — Daily Monitor, 6 October 2026 Uganda

Uganda's Attorney General told a parliamentary committee that the supplier of the digital number plate system has consistently failed the core obligations of its 2021 contract, which the newspaper reports him declaring null and illegal. Defaults cited include no training of Ugandan staff and no transfer of the system's technology to the state enterprise. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

Sectoral management information systems

CS Duale Calls for Timely Transition to HMIS and SHA Contracting — Kenya Ministry of Health, 6 October 2026 Kenya

Kenya's health cabinet secretary urged providers to meet the timelines for moving from the insurer's provider portal to the health management information system and for completing the 2026-2029 contracting cycle. The ministry puts migration at 6,427 public facilities, 38 certified private facilities and 38 of 518 faith-based ones, and says correctly submitted claims will still be paid during the change.

Antimicrobial resistance in Africa: The data gap behind the crisis — TechCabal, 6 October 2026 Africa

A contributed analysis argues that Africa's response to antimicrobial resistance is held back by missing laboratory data. It cites a WHO estimate that one in five laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections in its African Region in 2023 was resistant to antibiotics, a figure that cannot count infections never tested. Also under National statistics.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

TANESCO modernizes its customer service systems in line with Vision 2050 goals — Daily News (Tanzania), 6 October 2026 Tanzania

Tanzania's energy minister launched new customer service systems for the state power utility on 6 October: an AI-powered assistant available round the clock, a portal and a dedicated unit for large customers, and a 185 hotline, with a revised service charter. Also under AI and Access to services.

Senegal: AI enters judicial archives — APA News, 6 October 2026 Senegal

A high court in the Dakar suburbs has begun piloting a locally deployed AI application to digitise, manage and analyse judicial archives, with UNDP and Korean funding. Computing and digitisation equipment was handed to the justice ministry's dematerialisation directorate, and a phased national rollout is planned with no cost or timetable stated. Also under AI.

Paperless courts spread to Midlands, Mashonaland West — The Herald (Zimbabwe), 6 October 2026 Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe's Judicial Service Commission took its electronic case management system live in Midlands and Mashonaland West provinces on 1 October, after installing recording and transcription machines and issuing computers to court staff. Litigants and lawyers can file and follow cases online.

NRA Prepares to Launch Digital Customs System at Jendema Border — Sierraloaded, 6 October 2026 Sierra Leone

Sierra Leone's revenue authority has begun consultations with security agencies and local stakeholders at the Jendema border crossing as it prepares to launch the ASYCUDA World customs system there. The system will automate import, export and transit procedures; no launch date is given.

Government institutions urged to improve customer service quality — Kenya Broadcasting Corporation, 6 October 2026 Kenya

Opening Kenya's National Customer Service Week, the public service principal secretary told government institutions that good service is a standard citizens are entitled to and that the week's exhibitions should be used to listen to them.

Gauteng transport department launches digital system to tackle operating-licence backlog — Eyewitness News, 6 October 2026 South Africa

Gauteng's transport department launched a digital system to clear its backlog of public-transport operating licences, after six to eight months of testing with e-hailing, taxi and bus operators, municipalities and law enforcement. E-hailing operators are first in line to regularise.

Le Togo apprend ses langues grâce à l'IA — Republic of Togo, 6 October 2026 Togo
