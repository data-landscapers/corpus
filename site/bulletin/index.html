Tunisie | Les plateformes publiques à l’épreuve de la résilience numérique - Kapitalis — Kapitalis, 16 November 2026 Tunisia

An opinion piece argues that digital sovereignty means controlling infrastructure, protecting data and keeping services running in incidents. It cites two PHP vulnerabilities flagged in October 2026. It also cites the Microsoft 365 outage of 12 and 13 November. Also under Cybersecurity and Digital sovereignty.

South Africa must seize the opportunities of the AI revolution — South African Government, 16 November 2026 South Africa

President Ramaphosa is leading a delegation to Silicon Valley to inform South Africa's approach to AI. He cites a Microsoft ranking of South Africa 46th of 147 economies for AI adoption. He wants AI used in education, health and criminal justice. Also under AI, Digital sovereignty and Data Storage.

Kenya and EU Advance Digital Partnership Talks to Unlock Investment Opportunities — TechAfrica News, 16 November 2026 Kenya

Kenya's ICT Principal Secretary expects technical talks on an EU data-adequacy decision to end positively. He spoke on 13 November 2026 before the EU-Kenya Digital Dialogue. The meeting also covered digital public infrastructure, AI, digital identity and cybersecurity. Also under Data protection and EU activities.

InfoReg must not be AI regulator, says ex-member — ITWeb, 16 November 2026 South Africa

A founding member of the Information Regulator says it should not also regulate AI. He proposed an independent AI institute, created by presidential proclamation. It would work with the Regulator on data matters. Also under Data protection and AI.

ديوان الضرائب يعلن انتهاء التعامل بالفاتورة الورقية وبداية التعامل بالفاتورة الإلكترونية — Sudan News Agency (SUNA), 15 November 2026 Sudan

Sudan's Taxation Chamber ended paper invoices on 15 November 2026, after a three-month grace period, and made the electronic invoice mandatory in private-sector dealings with government at federal and state level. Registration runs through the Baladna platform. An offline mode covers areas without network coverage. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov.

UBOS Unveils New Data Strategy to Support Uganda's Tenfold Economic Growth Ambition — Nile Post, 15 November 2026 Uganda

The Uganda Bureau of Statistics launched its Fourth Plan for National Statistical Development, Strategic Plan IV and new Service Delivery Standards during Africa Statistics Week 2026. The standards set response times for users' data requests. State Minister Lugoloobi said data use in decision-making remains inadequate. Also under National statistics.

Senegal MPs reject 2027 digital ministry budget — Socialnetlink, 15 November 2026 Senegal

Committees of Senegal's National Assembly declined on 13 November 2026 to approve the 2027 draft budget of the Ministry of Telecommunications and Digital Affairs. MPs questioned the consistency of the forecasts behind FCFA 64.97 billion in payment appropriations. A committee rejection is not final, and the bill continues through parliamentary procedure. Also under Domestic budget appropriations and expenditure.

Over 4500 WiFi providers rush to register for licensing as deadline nears — Pulse Uganda, 15 November 2026 Uganda

Over 4,500 public Wi-Fi operators have sought community internet licences or agent status with licensed providers, the Uganda Communications Commission said. It extended the deadline for unlicensed operators after they petitioned Parliament. It promised licences within 14 days of a complete application. Also under Connectivity.

NCC plans spectrum incentives to push telecom operators into more network investment — National Daily Newspaper, 15 November 2026 Nigeria

The Nigerian Communications Commission is considering an Incentive-Based Spectrum Investment Framework. Operators would receive spectrum incentives for measurable commitments on coverage, capacity and quality. Monthly data use rose from about 518,000 terabytes in January 2023 to 1.73 million in September 2026. Also under Connectivity.

Court puts State on notice over use of AI in public services — Daily Nation, 15 November 2026 Kenya

Kenya's High Court gave the government six months to complete a national policy and legal framework for the use of artificial intelligence. Justice Edward Muriithi declined to order Parliament to enact legislation within 90 days. Also under AI and Legislation and regulation.

Kenya enforces mandatory crypto licensing — ITWeb Africa, 16 November 2026 Kenya

The Central Bank of Kenya and the Capital Markets Authority require every virtual-asset business to apply for a licence. Their joint notice is dated 13 November 2026. It confirms the VASP Regulations 2026 were gazetted on 22 July 2026. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.

Data protection

Cyberattack alert: What Nigerian banks must tell customers — TechCabal, 16 November 2026 Nigeria

The article sets out that in Nigeria reporting a cyberattack to the Central Bank, notifying affected customers and making a public announcement are separate duties. Banks must tell those affected where a personal-data breach is likely to create a high risk to individuals. CBN figures show N134.48 billion lost to fraud from 2020 to 2025. NIBSS figures put payment fraud losses at N25.85 billion in 2025, down from N52.26 billion. Also under Cybersecurity.

High Court questions ODPC's authority to award compensation for data privacy violations — TechTrendsKE, 15 November 2026 Kenya

Kenya's High Court set aside a KSh1.25 million compensation award made by the Data Protection Commissioner. Justice Francis Andayi found that the Data Protection Act does not expressly empower the Commissioner to award damages. The complaint, about CCTV in a clinic examination room, was returned to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, and Regulation 14(3)(e) stands.

Nineteen African Countries Race for Seats on UN Body That Shapes Global Telecom Rules — Ecofin Agency, 15 November 2026 Africa

Nineteen African countries are contesting Africa's 13 ITU Council seats for 2027-2030. All 13 incumbents are standing: Algeria, Egypt, Ghana, Kenya, Mauritius, Morocco, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa, Tanzania, Tunisia and Uganda. Six challengers are Cabo Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, DR Congo, Gabon, Libya and Mali. Also under Connectivity.

Après la panne de Microsoft 365 : la dépendance numérique, un risque pour Maurice — Le Defi Media Group, 15 November 2026 Mauritius

Mauritius's CERT-MU classed the 12-14 November 2026 Microsoft 365 outage as a high-level alert on 13 November. It said public services were not affected, because government data is hosted on its own cloud in Mauritius. The Stock Exchange of Mauritius set up alternative disclosure arrangements, and work on the national sovereign cloud was still in progress. Also under Cybersecurity and Data Storage.

Data Storage

Africa's AI Infrastructure Gap: Investment Opportunities and Risks for Data-Centre Expansion — Bloomsbury Intelligence and Security Institute (BISI) — Bloomsbury Intelligence and Security Institute, 16 November 2026 Africa

A Bloomsbury Intelligence and Security Institute briefing dated 16 November 2026 says Africa holds about 0.6% of global data-centre capacity. The Africa Data Centres Association counts 360 MW active, 238 MW under construction and 656 MW planned, which would take the continent to about 1.2 GW by 2030. It expects weak power supply, project costs and limited payment guarantees to delay large projects. A single AI data centre of 1 GW or more is unlikely before 2030. Also under AI and Energy.

Microsoft blamed server settings applied after an update and began a rollback. Also under Digital sovereignty and Cybersecurity.

Signature électronique et PKI Racine : L’ANSSI pose les bases de la confiance numérique en Côte d’Ivoire — Digital Mag Côte d'Ivoire, 15 November 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

Côte d'Ivoire's cybersecurity agency ANSSI said on 11 November 2026 that the national root PKI is used only by public bodies and is in its first phase. That phase is the systematic use of electronic signature, time-stamping and archiving across ministries. Linkage to digital identity and then regional and international interoperability are to follow. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Namibians lose N$32.45 million to payment fraud — The Namibian, 15 November 2026 Namibia

Payment fraud cost Namibians N$32.45 million in the first half of 2026, up 18.9% from N$27.29 million a year earlier, according to the Bank of Namibia's Financial Stability Report. Electronic fund transfer fraud accounted for N$21.87 million, or 67.4%, and was up 47.3% year on year. The report links it to phishing and vishing. Also under Digital Payments and Fintech.
