Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Senegal and Gambia Advance Digital Cooperation on Connectivity, Cybersecurity — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 SenegalGambia

Senegal's Minister of Telecommunications and Digital Affairs, Samba Diouf, visited Banjul to review the implementation roadmap for the telecommunications and digital-development memorandum of understanding signed between Senegal and The Gambia. Connectivity, cybersecurity, capacity building and digital transformation were named as the areas discussed, with initiatives to be advanced according to readiness; no first projects, funding or timetable are stated. Also under Connectivity.

Namibia advances national digital trust framework — ITWeb Africa, 31 August 2026 Namibia

Namibia's Communications Regulatory Authority and the German identity supplier Veridos signed an implementation agreement with the Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety and Security for a national public key infrastructure. CRAN acts as national Root Certification Authority and the ministry becomes the first Certification Service Provider, issuing electronic IDs — the base for digital identity, electronic signatures and identity verification in government services. No timetable or cost is stated.

RDC : le gouvernement renforce son dispositif de protection de l'espace informationnel face aux contenus abusifs sur les réseaux sociaux et les plateformes numériques — Actualite.cd, 30 August 2026 DRC

The Council of Ministers adopted a government-wide strategy against abusive content on social media and digital platforms at its 97th session on 28 August. It sets up an interministerial task force chaired by the communication ministry, a citizen alert mechanism and the integration of media literacy into the school curriculum, and commissions a technical and legal review of how the sector is regulated. The strategy text is not published with the readout, so what counts as abusive content, and what follows an alert, are not stated. Also under Legislation and regulation.

Legislation and regulation

Unlicensed SA finfluencers face enforcement action — ITWeb South Africa, 31 August 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Financial Sector Conduct Authority named unlicensed financial advice on social media one of its highest enforcement priorities, its enforcement head Gerhard van Deventer telling a Regulatory Actions Report roundtable that operating through a platform is no exemption from the licensing law and that penalties have already been imposed on finfluencers. He drew a distinction between financial education and regulated advice, and said the regulator is also looking for a way to engage finfluencers on where that line falls.

Nigeria wants crypto firms to lock up 80% of customer assets offline — TechCabal, 31 August 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's Securities and Exchange Commission opened for comment on 20 August a framework requiring digital-asset firms to segregate customer funds from their own, notify the regulator of material incidents within 24 hours and file a detailed report within 48. Minimum paid-up capital would be ₦2 billion (about $1.5m) for exchanges and custodians, ₦500 million for platform operators, offering platforms and tokenisation platforms, and ₦200 million for virtual asset service providers, each backed by a fidelity insurance bond covering at least a quarter of that. The rules also require a share of customer assets to be held in offline cold storage and SEC approval before a foreign-issued stablecoin may be listed.

Kenya : le Parlement veut rétablir les sanctions contre les fausses informations en ligne — wearetech.africa, 31 August 2026 Kenya

Kenya's National Assembly has asked the Supreme Court to overturn a Court of Appeal ruling that struck down sections 22 and 23 of the 2018 Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, which criminalised knowingly publishing false or misleading data presented as authentic, with penalties up to KES 5 million (about $38,600) and, for some offences, ten years' imprisonment. The Court of Appeal held the wording too broad, capable of catching people who share content unaware it is false as well as journalists and ordinary users; Parliament argues the provisions reach neither opinion, satire nor good-faith error.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Starlink Is Live in Equatorial Guinea — Space in Africa, 31 August 2026 Eq. Guinea

Starlink went live in Equatorial Guinea on 29 August, its 30th African market, with a consumer order page and residential tariffs in CFA francs. The entry is not a permanent licence: it runs under a provisional approval granted by Vice-President Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangue on 29 July, with the state infrastructure company GITGE assessing operations and deciding what regulatory conditions should apply longer term. An earlier framework had confined authorised access largely to oil, gas and strategic infrastructure, and whether that restriction survives the review period is unresolved.

Data Storage

NITDA and Infracorp Strengthen Collaboration on Nigeria’s Sovereign Cloud Agenda — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Nigeria

NITDA's director-general Kashifu Inuwa received Infracorp chief executive Lazarus Angbazo for talks on Nigeria's sovereign cloud agenda, focused on operationalising the Investment Strategy — unlocking local funding, increasing private-sector participation and attracting foreign investment into sovereign cloud and digital infrastructure. No commitment, sum or project is attached to the meeting.

Huawei Launches Agentic AI Cloud in Nigeria to Accelerate AI Adoption — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Nigeria

Huawei launched its Agentic AI Cloud in Nigeria at a summit in Lagos on 27 August, presenting local cloud infrastructure with data residency and low latency as a base for deploying AI agents in finance, telecommunications, government and enterprise. It follows the company's first hyperscale local cloud in Nigeria in 2024 and an industry-focused expansion in 2025. NITDA's director-general Kashifu Inuwa Abdullahi placed it against the National Sovereign Cloud Initiative and the country's ambition to move from consuming technology to hosting AI and cloud innovation. Also under AI.

Energy

Electricity Tariff Overview — Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority, Kenya, 30 August 2026 Kenya

The energy regulator's tariff page sets out its mandate to set and review retail and bulk electricity tariffs. It names no data-centre or large-load category, which dates an absence Corpus had previously recorded only as unsearched: the country is building data centres against a tariff schedule that does not distinguish them.

Integrated Population Registration Service | State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services — State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services (Usajili), 30 August 2026 Kenya

The State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services publishes a page for the service that holds the country's population register, the one queried when an agency needs to confirm who someone is. What the register holds, which bodies may query it and on what basis are not stated on it — a gap that matters more now that the electoral commission has confirmed it verifies voter data against the registration bureau. Digital Payments and Fintech

TTCL and ZFF Launch Digital Push With N-Card E-Ticketing System — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Tanzania

Tanzania Telecommunications Corporation and the Zanzibar Football Federation signed a sponsorship and partnership agreement in Zanzibar on 30 August to introduce e-ticketing at football stadiums through TTCL's N-Card platform. The agreement is also said to cover the federation's wider digital systems and football administration; no value, term or start date for the ticketing service is given.

Samsung SA expands digital wallet ecosystem — ITWeb South Africa, 31 August 2026 South Africa

Samsung South Africa added Scan to Pay QR-code payments, provided by EFT Corp, to Samsung Wallet, four years after launching the wallet locally in November 2022. The company puts Scan to Pay at more than 600,000 acceptance locations in South Africa and inside over 19 million banking, fintech and financial-services app installations; no figure for Samsung Wallet's own local user base is given.

MTN MoMo Uganda and SchoolPay Mark 10 Years of Digital School Fees Payment Partnership — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Uganda

MTN MoMo Uganda marked ten years of its partnership with SchoolPay Uganda for digital school-fee payment, and handed the SchoolPay team a van at the anniversary event. Neither party gives transaction volumes, fee value or the number of schools and parents using the channel.

Looping Binary Launches LBPay Digital Payment Ecosystem in Cameroon — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Cameroon

Cameroon-based Looping Binary launched LBPay, a payments platform for transfers, wallet management and moving money between mobile money networks, with bill and airtime payment alongside. It also offers payment links, developer tools and APIs for businesses integrating payments; the company says Cameroon is the initial market with the intention of scaling elsewhere in Africa, and no licensing or partner-institution detail is given.

Le Béninois Phital Azah transforme WhatsApp et Mobile Money en leviers de vente — wearetech.africa, 31 August 2026 Benin

A profile of Beninese developer Phital Azah, founder of Xèdo, a 2024 company whose platform lets merchants build an online shop by importing a WhatsApp Business catalogue with its photos, descriptions and prices, then centralise orders. It automates WhatsApp notifications through payment and delivery, chases abandoned baskets, settles by Mobile Money or cash on delivery, and carries an analytics dashboard. Azah is also lead mobile developer at Benin's Commission électorale nationale autonome.

Côte d'Ivoire : ANKA relance sa place de marché grand public sous le nom d'Afrikrea — wearetech.africa, 31 August 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

ANKA has separated its businesses at its tenth anniversary and relaunched its consumer marketplace under the Afrikrea name, through which African and diaspora creators sell fashion, art and crafts internationally with cross-border payment and delivery handled on the platform. What the remaining ANKA business now covers is not stated in the report.

Government Digital Payments (eCitizen) | The National Treasury — National Treasury, Kenya, 30 August 2026 Kenya

The Treasury's Government Digital Payments Unit sets out the origin of the country's payments-into-government programme: a 2013 survey found more than a third of receipts still cash-based, a presidential directive that November ordered all payments into government digitised, and a taskforce was gazetted in April 2014. The unit's stated mandate is to digitise every payment into government, raising collection and cutting its cost.

Registries

Business Registration — Rwanda Development Board (Office of the Registrar General), 30 August 2026 Rwanda

Rwanda's registrar general describes company registration as wholly online and free of charge, completed within six working hours on the issue of a certificate of incorporation. There is no minimum share capital, shareholding may be entirely foreign, and a support unit at the board's Kigali offices assists applicants who cannot complete the form unaided.

Sectoral management information systems

ZAMPORTAL and Teams Enable Lupososhi Official to Print Road Tax Locally — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Zambia

An official at Lupososhi District Administration in Zambia's Northern Province printed a road-tax document from his own office rather than travelling to Kasama for it, after the Northern Provincial ICT Unit connected the office and ran orientation on Zamportal, Microsoft Teams and Outlook. The report is a single demonstration rather than a rollout figure: how many district offices have been connected, and which Zamportal services they can now transact, are not stated.

Top ICT tenders: Home Affairs expands revenue admin tender — ITWeb South Africa, 31 August 2026 South Africa

South Africa's Department of Home Affairs re-advertised its tender for the supply, hosting and support of the revenue administration system that handles collection, reconciliation and Basic Accounting System reporting, replacing June's generic "400+" site count with a two-tier footprint of 275 fixed sites and 230 mobile units. The revision reframes transaction channels around point-of-sale devices and mobile offices rather than the standalone kiosks and e-Home listed earlier, so bidders must price deployment, licensing and support against a larger and more dispersed estate. Bidding is restricted to suppliers on SITA transversal contract RFB 1183, with a non-compulsory briefing on 4 September and submissions closing 18 September.

South Sudan seeks Huawei support for digitalisation — ITWeb Africa, 31 August 2026 South Sudan

South Sudan's minister of information, communication technology and postal services, Ateny Wek Ateny, held exploratory talks with Huawei's vice-president for global public business, Eric Ma, in Hangzhou on the margins of the China-Africa Digital Capacity Building and Cooperation Conference. The government is seeking Huawei's participation in e-governance, smart cities, energy and a national data hub in Juba; the report notes the two sides fell out in 2014, when officials accused the company of hacking government email and forging documents, and dates the repair to a Juba meeting in early 2026. Nothing is committed or costed at this stage. Also under China activities.

Smart Africa and Uganda Stakeholders Push Reforms to Boost Digital Entrepreneurship — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Uganda

Smart Africa and Uganda's Ministry of ICT and National Guidance convened a national validation workshop in Kampala on the findings of Uganda's Digital Entrepreneurship and Innovation Country Assessment. Priorities identified were advancing the Startup Bill, strengthening science and innovation policy, widening access to regulatory sandboxes, and using public procurement to create a market for local startups. The work sits under the Africa-Europe Digital Innovation Bridge (AEDIB 2.0); the assessment itself is not published with the report.

Innovation ecosystem

Livraison urbaine : Ebenezer Sasu automatise le transport par drone au Ghana — wearetech.africa, 31 August 2026 Ghana

A profile of Ghanaian aerospace engineer Ebenezer Sasu, founder of Wingxtra Aerospace, which since 2019 has combined drones with electric bikes, motorcycles and vans for last-mile delivery in African cities, switching mode automatically on parcel size, distance, urgency and traffic. An allocation and tracking system matches vehicle position and drone status to each order and exposes real-time tracking to customers and merchants. Sasu co-founded OrcaDynamics in 2025, designing drones and aerial systems through 3D modelling and printing.

Training and skills

NITDA Trains Nigerian Children in Coding, Digital Skills Across Four States — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Nigeria

Nigeria's National Information Technology Development Agency closed its 2026 Kids Long Vacation Coding Bootcamp in Kano, Kwara, Jigawa and Gombe, with training still running in the South-South, South-East and South-West. Participants covered coding, computational thinking and digital creativity and received certificates and devices at closing ceremonies; the number of children trained is not given.

MTN Uganda Graduates 503 Young People in Digital Skills Programme — Tech Africa News, 31 August 2026 Uganda

MTN Uganda graduated 503 young people from its MTN Ace digital skilling programme at the National ICT Innovation Hub, on a hybrid course covering ICT, digital marketing, e-commerce, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. The initiative has now trained 1,191 people in total, and the MTN Uganda Foundation put UGX 5.1 billion through its programmes in 2025, which it says reached more than 60,000 people.

Inclusion

Access to services

Nigeria wants more assistive tech. Who is fixing its inaccessible digital platforms? — TechCabal, 31 August 2026 Nigeria

The Nigerian Communications Commission ran its fourth Hackathon Live Show in Abuja on 26 and 27 August under the theme "Technology Without Barriers", producing prototypes for speech-to-text, real-time sign-language translation, voice-enabled complaints and image-to-voice navigation. TechCabal sets that against the platforms Nigerians are already expected to use: NITDA's guidelines require government websites to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, and the Nigeria Web Design Standards toolkit promised for public feedback within 8 to 10 weeks of its July 2025 launch could not be found published, nor any evidence of Federal Executive Council approval or rollout — while the communications ministry's 2025 Year in Review still lists the launch as an achievement. The National Commission for Persons with Disabilities estimates 35 million Nigerians have disabilities.
