Mozambique steps up AI drive — ITWeb Africa, 28 August 2026 Mozambique

Mozambique's new artificial-intelligence association inaugurated its leadership in Maputo on 27 August 2026, three months after the country published a draft national AI strategy. The national ICT institute's board chairman described it as a technical platform meant to connect government, the private sector, academia, the technology community and civil society, and said a national data governance policy and strategy is under development. That policy is not yet published. Also under AI.

Senegal-Gambie : la cooperation numerique se concretise autour de quatre priorites — wearetech.africa, 27 August 2026 SenegalGambia

Senegal and the Gambia moved their June 2026 telecommunications and digital-economy memorandum into implementation around four priorities — connectivity, cybersecurity, skills and digital transformation — with Senegal's minister travelling to Banjul to agree the first joint work, and the two regulators separately deepening cooperation on frequency coordination, service quality and cross-border roaming. The structural case is the Gambia's limited terrestrial fibre build and its dependence on a single submarine cable, with only limited terrestrial redundancy through Senegal. Also under Regional collaboration and Connectivity.

Rwanda CMA Concludes Dubai VARA Study Tour to Strengthen Virtual Asset Regulation — Tech Africa News, 27 August 2026 Rwanda

Rwanda's Capital Market Authority concluded a two-day visit to Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority as part of a study tour in the United Arab Emirates, on regulatory knowledge exchange around virtual assets and emerging technologies. It follows Rwanda gazetting its first dedicated virtual-asset legislation in May 2026, under which the authority is the primary licensing and supervisory body. Also under New investments and Gulf/UAE activities.

Kenya : les 47 comtes sommes de passer aux marches publics numeriques — wearetech.africa, 27 August 2026 Kenya

Kenya's National Treasury gave all 47 county governments until 30 September 2026 to finish integrating the electronic government procurement platform with the Integrated Financial Management Information System, so that tendering, award and contract monitoring connect to budget planning, spending and payment. The deadline lands on a rollout that has already slipped: a first phase launched in April 2025 for extension to all national and local administrations from 1 July 2025, against resistance from the Council of Governors over pace and service disruption, with more than 900 public entities migrated by February 2026. Also under Other GovTech and e-Gov and Digitalisation of sub-national government.

En Afrique comme ailleurs, les Etats s'emparent de la protection des mineurs sur les reseaux sociaux — Agence Ecofin, 27 August 2026 GabonNigeriaRwandaZimbabwe

African states are moving from a minimum age for opening a social media account towards duties on how platforms are built. Gabon's February 2026 ordinance fixes digital majority at 16 and obliges platforms to limit certain publication and interaction functions, disable contacts from unidentified users by default and provide age-verification mechanisms; Rwanda is preparing a restriction on under-16s, Nigeria is consulting on age limits, identity verification and platform liability, and Zimbabwe has adopted a policy covering cyber-harassment, sextortion and data protection. The African Union's 2024 child online safety policy already asks states to require protection by design and by default, and the constraint named here is enforcement rather than law. Also under Legislation and regulation and Data protection.

Digital assets drive Rwanda-UAE deal — ITWeb Africa, 27 August 2026 Rwanda

Rwanda's Capital Market Authority is benchmarking its new virtual-asset framework against the United Arab Emirates' and the two regulators have begun work towards a memorandum of understanding, after a study tour that included Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority. Rwanda gazetted its first dedicated legislation regulating virtual-asset businesses in May 2026, making the authority the primary licensing and supervisory body; the UAE side frames the cooperation as part of an initiative to expand regulatory partnerships with emerging markets across Africa. Also under New investments and Gulf/UAE activities.

Bolt takes e-hailing compliance push to provinces — ITWeb South Africa, 27 August 2026 South Africa

An e-hailing platform took its compliance programme to South Africa's provinces, holding a North West regulatory engagement in Rustenburg on 18 August attended by more than 150 operators and a Western Cape transport dialogue in Cape Town on 21 August, at which the provincial mobility department's director of licensing and adjudication undertook to add e-hailing as a service option on the operating-licence application. The North West provincial regulatory entity put its pending operating-licence backlog at 3,000 to 5,000 applications. Also under Legislation and regulation, Digital Identity and CRVS and ICT Industry.

Bolt backs Mombasa County push to formalise 400 delivery riders with training and licences — TechCabal, 27 August 2026 Kenya

A ride-hailing platform and the Mombasa county government launched a programme to license 400 delivery riders through Kenya's Recognition of Prior Learning framework, the platform committing KES 1.5 million to it. Riders complete 21 days of accredited training at a certified driving school before the national transport and safety authority licenses them on demonstrated competency, and the platform will share trip and demand data with the county for infrastructure planning including the siting of electric-vehicle charging points. A 2025 parliamentary report puts the sector at more than two million riders. Also under Training and skills, Digitalisation of sub-national government and Access to services.

Legislation and regulation

Three companies now control nearly 70% of Nigeria's ISP market — TechCabal, 27 August 2026 Nigeria

Regulator data released on 25 August put active licensed-ISP subscribers in Nigeria at 420,989 in June 2026, up 19.6% from 352,006 in December 2025, with three providers holding close to 70% of the market — Spectranet 111,384, Starlink 98,642 and FibreOne 56,486 — and points of presence up from 2,508 to 2,893. That whole market is 0.27% of the country's 156.4 million active internet subscriptions, and 126 of the 230 licensed ISPs are reported to have a single active subscriber. Also under Connectivity and ICT Industry.

Consulta Pública do MINTTICS — Proposta de Lei sobre a Inteligência Artificial — Ministério das Telecomunicações, Tecnologias de Informação e Comunicação Social (MINTTICS), Angola, 27 August 2026 Angola

Maroc: les donnees de 70 000 agents de la police et du renseignement publiees apres une nouvelle cyberattaque — RFI, 27 August 2026 Morocco

A hacker group said on 24 August that it had published the personal data of 70,000 Moroccan police and intelligence agents, a leak former members of the intelligence services judge at least partly authentic and which discloses details not previously public, including the date of birth of the head of the police and internal intelligence. Whether it is a new breach is contested — a Moroccan outlet calls it an assemblage of data compromised in earlier hacks — and Moroccan media have covered it only marginally. Also under Cybersecurity.

The data-protection commission continues to hold the operator as a registered licensee. Also under Digital Identity and CRVS.

Regional collaboration

Why Connecting Landlocked Africa Requires More Than Building National Fibre Networks — Tech Africa News, 27 August 2026

The ITU puts internet use across Africa's 16 landlocked developing countries at 25% in 2024 against 68% globally, while 86% of their population is covered by a mobile broadband signal — a gap between availability and use it attributes to affordability and to insufficient international connectivity infrastructure. Landlocked states cannot reach a submarine cable directly, so their international capacity rests on terrestrial routes and commercial arrangements crossing neighbours, which is the part a national fibre build does not settle. Also under Connectivity and Access to services.

ISO - DSNPQ - Direction de Service de Normalisation et Promotion de la Qualité (Guinée-Bissau member organization record) — International Organization for Standardization (ISO), 27 August 2026 Guinea-Bissau

The ISO membership record for Guinea-Bissau's national standards body, held in the trade ministry. It records no technical-committee liaisons at all, which is the measure of how far the country participates in setting the standards its digital sector will be held to.

Confessing to a computer: The hidden danger of AI in vulnerable spaces — TechCabal, 27 August 2026 Kenya

A column argues that accuracy benchmarks are the wrong test for artificial-intelligence systems entering life-or-death decisions in Africa, citing global models misreading Nairobi's altitude and giving harmful clinical advice in Kenyan trials. It opens on the reconstructed chat logs of a woman who died by suicide after months of using a chatbot as a therapist, and its claim is that present standards do not measure the failures that matter where users are most exposed. Also under AI and Research institutions.

Government urged to pick up pieces as UNCDF's 6-year digital inclusion program ends — Pulse Uganda, 27 August 2026 Uganda

The UNCDF's six-year digital inclusion programme closed in Uganda, with district officials and implementers urging the government to take over the systems it built before they collapse. Its stated results since 2019 include more than 3.4 million people registered for supported digital services, over a million active users and more than 21,000 digital agents recruited, concentrated in northern Uganda and West Nile. Also under Access to services and Digital Payments and Fintech.

Technology and business leaders in Nigeria argued that access to technology is no longer the binding constraint and that control of the infrastructure, data and technology businesses depend on is the harder question. The argument was made at the launch of a technology survey and a national database forum. Also under Data Storage and Innovation ecosystem.

Côte d'Ivoire's $68m Rural Connectivity Programme Targets 575 Localities — Tech Africa News, 28 August 2026 Côte d'Ivoire

A late re-report of Côte d'Ivoire's national rural connectivity programme, whose first phase was commissioned at Djebakaha, near Korhogo, on 2 August 2024. The programme's 38 billion CFA franc budget is to reach 575 rural localities in three phases, the second worth 18 billion CFA francs across 240 localities and the third 10 billion CFA francs across 160, both scheduled for 2024 and 2025. From war zones to Kenya's markets: How an ex-soldier built a $1bn lender — TechCabal, 28 August 2026 Kenya

A profile of a Kenyan lender to micro and small businesses, founded in 2013 on the premise that unsecured working capital repaid on time is itself the credit record a trader without collateral or accounts cannot otherwise produce. It said in June 2026 that it had disbursed more than $1bn across over seven million loans at a repayment rate of about 95 per cent, with an algorithmic underwriting engine sizing each subsequent loan from repayment behaviour. Parents keep oversight through their own space, scheduling transfers and watching transactions in real time. The product is still in launch phase ahead of app-store release; no user figure is published. Also under Literacy.
