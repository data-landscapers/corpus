Maturity Assessment

Maturity Assessment: methodology

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS · Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

The scale

Each studied indicator places each of the 54 countries on one five-stage scale. The stages are ordinal: 4 is above 3, not twice 2. The assessment is not a score or a ranking, and nothing is averaged.

Stage Label Meaning
1 Absent Nothing of the kind exists, and a dated source says so.
2 Preparing Announced, drafted, piloted: an intention with an instrument or a pilot behind it.
3 Establishing In service, but limited in scope, coverage or use.
4 Operating In service at scale: funded, regulated, used, maintained.
5 Leading Embedded: interoperable, measured, sustained; at or beyond the continental norm.

No evidence and unplaced

No evidence means nothing held states any of the facts the ladder tests. Unplaced means evidence is held but it meets no rung, so no stage is set. Both are shown in grey. Neither is stage 1: Absent needs a dated source that says the thing does not exist.

How a stage is set

Each indicator is studied whole, across all 54 countries. A study first settles what the indicator covers and splits it into sub-indicators where one label hides two things. It then fixes a ladder: for each stage, the value each coverage aspect must reach. A country is placed on the highest rung whose every test its evidence meets; between two rungs it takes the lower. Every fact carries its date, and a fact older than three years at the as-at date prints its year. A second drafter restages a sample blind, and a rung read two ways is rewritten.

Stages are taken as at the end of a month. A stage changes only on a dated source inside the window; a change from a re-read or a corrected ladder is marked reassessed and is not counted as movement.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

The norm

Africa CDC's Digital Transformation Strategy (2023) and PHC Digitalisation Framework (2025–26): 100,000 facilities connected by 2030 and 90 per cent digitally enabled primary care by 2035.

The ladder

Stage Criteria
1 Absent A dated source states that routine health reporting is not entered digitally at any level of the health system.
2 Preparing A digital reporting system is used by hospitals only or in some districts, or a national rollout has been contracted. Primary clinics do not use it, beyond pilot sites.
3 Establishing A national system takes the clinics' reports, which are keyed in at district level from the clinics' paper forms. A minority of primary clinics enter their own reports, or no figure is published.
4 Operating More than half of primary clinics enter their own reports at the facility. The figure is a published share, or a count given with its total.
5 Leading At least 90 per cent of primary clinics enter their own reports at the facility, on a figure published within the past two years. Reporting completeness is published as well.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

The norm

Africa CDC's Digital Transformation Strategy (2023) and PHC Digitalisation Framework (2025–26): 100,000 facilities connected by 2030 and 90 per cent digitally enabled primary care by 2035.

The ladder

Stage Criteria
1 Absent A dated source states that patient records are kept on paper at every level of the health system.
2 Preparing Electronic patient records are in use in some hospitals or pilot facilities, or a national system has been procured. Primary clinics do not use them, beyond pilot sites.
3 Establishing Electronic patient records are in use in most hospitals, or in primary clinics in some districts, but each record stays in the facility that made it. A minority of primary clinics use them, or no figure is published.
4 Operating More than half of primary clinics keep electronic patient records, entered at the point of care. Each record is held under a unique patient identifier and can be retrieved at another facility.
5 Leading At least 90 per cent of primary clinics keep such shared records, on a figure published within the past two years. A record can be retrieved anywhere in the public network.
