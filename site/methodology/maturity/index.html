Method

The scale · No evidence and unplaced · How a stage is set

The scale

Each studied indicator places each of the 54 countries on one five-stage scale. The stages are ordinal: 4 is above 3, not twice 2. The assessment is not a score or a ranking, and nothing is averaged.

Stage Label Meaning
1 Absent Nothing of the kind exists, and a dated source says so.
2 Preparing Announced, drafted, piloted: an intention with an instrument or a pilot behind it.
3 Establishing In service, but limited in scope, coverage or use.
4 Operating In service at scale: funded, regulated, used, maintained.
5 Leading Embedded: interoperable, measured, sustained; at or beyond the continental norm.

No evidence and unplaced

No evidence means nothing found states any of the facts the ladder tests. Unplaced means evidence was found but it meets no rung, so no stage is set. On the map no evidence is left unfilled and unplaced is grey. Neither is stage 1: Absent needs a dated source that says the thing does not exist.

How a stage is set

Each indicator is studied whole, across all 54 countries. A study first settles what the indicator covers and splits it into sub-indicators where one label hides two things. It then fixes a ladder: for each stage, the value each coverage aspect must reach. A country is placed on the highest rung whose every test its evidence meets; between two rungs it takes the lower. Every fact carries its date, and a fact older than three years at the as-at date prints its year. A second drafter restages a sample blind, and a rung read two ways is rewritten.

Stages are taken as at the end of a month. A stage changes only on a dated source inside the window; a change from a re-read or a corrected ladder is marked reassessed and is not counted as movement.

Strategies, plans and policies

Digital transformation strategy

The norm

The norm is the African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa 2020-2030. Under its enabling-environment pillar it asks states to "develop and implement national and sectorial digital strategies". Its monitoring section covers the continental strategy itself.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A dated source states that the country has no national digital strategy, and none is on record as adopted, launched or in preparation.
2 Preparing A national digital strategy is announced, being drafted, or validated and awaiting adoption. Or one is adopted and its text is not public. Or its period has ended and a successor is in preparation.
3 Establishing A national digital strategy is adopted, its text is public, and its period has not ended. A text the government has published, and no source calls a draft, counts as adopted. A strategy with no stated period stops here.
4 Operating The strategy has a delivery plan with dated actions or costs, and the government has published results against its own targets within the past three years. Without a mandated body and a domestic budget line the stage is capped at 3.
5 Leading The delivery plan is costed, and results against the strategy's targets are published on every pillar within the past two years.

ICT strategy

The norm

The norm is the African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa 2020-2030, which asks states for "national and sectorial digital strategies". It names the subject and measures nothing. The old fifth rung, supersession by a national strategy, becomes the flag.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A dated source states that the country has no ICT or telecommunications sector policy, none is on record as adopted or in preparation, and no national digital strategy is held.
2 Preparing A sector policy is announced, being drafted, or validated and awaiting adoption. Or one is adopted and its text is not public. Or its period has ended and a successor sector policy is in preparation.
3 Establishing A sector policy is adopted, its text is public, and its period has not ended. A policy with no stated period stops here. A national digital strategy is assessed on its own row and does not count.
4 Operating The policy has a delivery plan with dated actions or costs, and the government has published results against the policy's own targets within the past three years. Without a mandated body and a domestic budget line the stage is capped at 3.
5 Leading The delivery plan is costed, and results against the policy's targets are published on every objective within the past two years.

Broadband strategy

The norm

The Broadband Commission's first target is a funded national broadband plan in every country by 2025, which gives questions 1 to 3. The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy sets the 2030 outcome: universal access at 6 Mb/s or more, at no more than one US cent a megabyte. This study asks whether the plan exists, is adopted, is funded and is reported on; it does not measure the outcome.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no national broadband plan and none is being written.
2 Preparing A national broadband plan is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has ended with no successor adopted.
3 Establishing A national broadband plan is adopted by the government or the minister and its period has not ended. It may be part of a larger digital strategy or development plan that states broadband targets. No money tied to it is on record in the past three years.
4 Operating Money has been committed to the plan in the past three years: a budget line, a universal service fund allocation or a financed programme that the source ties to the plan.
5 Leading The plan is funded, and the government has published figures against the plan's own targets in the past three years. Whether the targets are met is not judged.

Data storage / cloud strategy

The norm

The African Union's Data Policy Framework asks each state to set rules for hosting its own data, by category, and to build national or regional cloud capacity; the Digital Transformation Strategy sets a target for hosting the continent's content on the continent. This study asks whether the policy exists, is adopted, names where to host and is being followed; it does not measure the capacity or the localisation.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the government has no hosting or cloud policy and none is being written.
2 Preparing A government hosting or cloud policy is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has ended with no successor adopted. A data centre or government cloud with no policy behind it does not count.
3 Establishing A policy on where the state's data is hosted, or on how public bodies may use cloud, is adopted and in force. It may be a cloud-first policy, a circular, a hosting standard or part of a larger strategy or law. Where public bodies must host is not yet named.
4 Operating The government has named where public bodies must host: a government cloud or data centre in service that the policy tells them to use, or a published list of approved providers.
5 Leading Public bodies have moved onto the named hosting in the past three years: a dated source names or counts the ministries or agencies that have. How good the policy is, is not judged.

Data interoperability framework / roadmap

The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy asks for a framework, with implementing acts, for interoperability and levels of assurance; its Data Policy Framework asks for open data standards, and its interoperability framework for open standards with no dependence on one supplier. This study asks whether a framework exists, is adopted, is compulsory and is being followed; it does not measure the standards.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the government has no interoperability framework and none is being written.
2 Preparing A framework, standard or regulation for how government systems exchange data is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has ended with no successor adopted. An exchange platform with no framework behind it does not count, and nor does interoperability named only as an aim.
3 Establishing A framework for how government systems exchange data is adopted and published. It may be an interoperability framework, a standard, a decree or part of a larger law. No instrument yet makes it compulsory and names who enforces it.
4 Operating Public bodies are required to follow the framework: a decree, law, regulation or circular makes it compulsory and names the body that enforces it.
5 Leading Public bodies are following the framework in the past three years: a dated source names or counts the ministries, agencies or systems that exchange data under it or are certified as meeting it. How good the framework is, is not judged.

AI strategy

The norm

The African Union's Continental AI Strategy, adopted in 2024, says member states must develop national AI strategies, with strategies and governance structures in its first phase to 2026. This study asks whether a strategy exists, is adopted, has a body to carry it out and has money behind it; it does not measure the strategy's content or its ethics rules.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no national AI strategy and none is being written.
2 Preparing A national AI strategy, policy or roadmap is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has run out with no successor adopted. A task force appointed to write it counts. AI named as a priority in a digital strategy does not.
3 Establishing A national AI strategy is adopted and still running: approved by cabinet, or launched or issued by the head of state, the minister or the agency responsible. No body is yet named to carry it out.
4 Operating The government has named a body to carry out the strategy: a council, office, agency, committee or ministry unit set up or named for it.
5 Leading Public money has gone into carrying out the strategy in the past three years: a dated source names money allocated to it, or an action in it started with its funding named. How good the strategy is, is not judged.

Data localisation policies

The norm

The African Union's Data Policy Framework, of 2022, asks states to keep only certain kinds of data in the country and to let the rest move. The digital trade protocol of the African Continental Free Trade Area, of 2024, says a state shall not require computing facilities to be in its territory, with exceptions for public policy. This study asks whether a rule exists, is in force, has an enforcer and has been applied; it does not judge whether the country should have one or how wide it is.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no rule that data must be kept in the country and none is being written.
2 Preparing A rule that data, or a named kind of data, must be stored or processed in the country is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one was repealed with no successor. A minister's stated intention with no drafting begun does not count, and nor does a data centre.
3 Establishing A rule is in force: a law, decree, regulation or regulator's directive that data, or a named kind of data, must be kept in the country. A rule on one kind of data counts; a condition on sending data abroad does not. No body is yet named to enforce it.
4 Operating A body is named to enforce the rule: a regulator, agency or ministry unit that checks compliance or grants exemptions. A regulator that issued the rule for the bodies it licenses counts.
5 Leading The rule has been applied in the past three years: a dated source names an organisation ordered to bring data into the country, penalised or exempted under it, or counts those that have moved data to comply. How wide the rule is, and whether the country should have one, is not judged.

Data governance policy

The norm

The African Union's Data Policy Framework, endorsed by the Executive Council in February 2022, asks each member state to write a national data policy, put it into its own rules and institutions, and monitor it. This study asks whether a policy exists, is adopted, has a body to carry it out and has produced a rule; it does not measure the policy's content against the framework.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no national data policy and none is being written.
2 Preparing A national data policy, strategy or framework is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has run out with no successor. A draft validated at a workshop is still a draft. A tender to hire the writer, or a stated intention, does not count.
3 Establishing The government has adopted the policy and it has not run out: approved by cabinet, or launched or issued by the head of state, the minister or the agency responsible. A policy on sharing or managing data across the whole government counts; a data protection law or an open data policy does not. No body is yet named to carry it out.
4 Operating A body is named to carry out the policy: a council, office, agency, committee, chief data officer or ministry unit set up or named for it. The ministry that wrote the policy being responsible for it in general does not count.
5 Leading A rule or standard the policy called for has been issued in the past three years: a law, regulation, standard or guideline, such as one on classifying data or on sharing it between public bodies. How good the policy is, and whether it follows the African Union's framework, is not judged.

Open data policy

The norm

The African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights' Declaration of Principles on Freedom of Expression and Access to Information in Africa, adopted in November 2019, asks public bodies to publish information of public interest without being asked, including by digital means (Principle 29). The African Union's Data Policy Framework, of 2022, asks states to open government data. This study asks whether a policy exists, is adopted, has a portal and has data published under it; it does not measure the policy against either.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no open data policy and none is being written.
2 Preparing An open data policy, strategy, decree or law is being written or is out for consultation, or the last one has run out with no successor. A draft validated at a workshop is still a draft. A portal with no policy behind it, or a commitment in an open government action plan, does not count.
3 Establishing The government has adopted the policy and it has not run out: approved by cabinet, or launched or issued by the head of state, the minister or the agency responsible. The open data part of a wider data policy counts where a source says it sets the rule; an access to information law does not. No government portal is yet reported running under it.
4 Operating A government open data portal is running under the policy. A statistics office's data portal, or one a donor or development bank runs for the country, does not count.
5 Leading Public bodies have published data under the policy in the past three years: a source names a ministry or agency that has released data, or counts the datasets or the bodies publishing. How good the policy is, and how open the licence, is not judged.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation

The norm

The norm is the African Union's Malabo Convention: Article 8(1) commits each party to a legal framework for personal data, Article 13 sets the principles and Articles 16 to 19 the rights. It names the subject and measures nothing: it sets no test of whether a law is applied, and matching its principles is a reading of two texts, not a dated fact. Ratification is the flag.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A dated source states that the country has no general data protection law, and none is on record as enacted or in preparation.
2 Preparing A data protection law is announced, being drafted, before parliament, or passed and not yet promulgated.
3 Establishing A data protection law is enacted or in force, whether it stands alone or is part of a larger law. No decision under it is on record in the past three years.
4 Operating The law is in force and has been applied: a dated sanction, order, decision or court judgment on a controller within the past three years. Without a constituted supervisory authority the stage is capped at 3.
5 Leading The law has regulations in force under it, and a count of decisions, sanctions or complaints settled is published for a period ending within the past two years.

Cybersecurity legislation

The norm

The Malabo Convention's third chapter commits each party to legal measures against cybercrime: Article 25 on legislation and Articles 29 to 31 on the offences. It names what a law should cover and sets no test of whether one is used. This study asks whether the law exists, is in force and is used.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no cybercrime or cybersecurity law and no bill for one.
2 Preparing A cybercrime or cybersecurity bill is drafted, before parliament or passed. No law is known to be in force.
3 Establishing A cybercrime or cybersecurity law is in force, as an act of its own or as part of a penal or digital code. No use of it is on record in the past three years.
4 Operating The law has been used in the past three years: a dated arrest, charge, prosecution or conviction, or a penalty from the body it sets up. Who it was used against is not judged.
5 Leading The law is used, and a count of cases investigated, prosecuted or decided under it is published for a period ending within the past two years.

Legislation covering digital id

The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy sets a target of digital legal identity for 99.9% of people by 2030, and its Interoperability Framework for Digital ID (§4.1) asks for a harmonised enabling legal framework. Article 14 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol commits each party to adopt or maintain digital identity systems. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, provides for a digital identity and has had rules made under it; it does not measure the law against any of the three.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no national identity law and no bill for one.
2 Preparing A bill for a national identity law is drafted, before parliament or passed and not yet promulgated, or the identity system runs on a decree or order with no law behind it.
3 Establishing A national identity law is in force: a law, ordinance or decree-law that sets up the identity number, the register of persons or the identity card, however old. A data protection law, or a civil registration law that sets up no identity number or card, does not count.
4 Operating The law in force provides for a digital identity: a biometric register, an identity number unique to each person, or an identity that can be checked electronically. A law for a paper card only does not reach this stage.
5 Leading Rules have been made under that law in the past three years: a regulation or decree applying it, or a rule that banks, phone companies or public bodies must check identity against the system. Cards issued, people enrolled and changes to fees do not count.

Digital payments legislation

The norm

Article 15 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol and its Annex on Cross-Border Digital Payments commit each party to interoperable payment systems, and Article 7 of the Malabo Convention to state-approved electronic payment methods. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, licenses non-banks and requires providers to connect; it does not measure the law against either.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no payments law or e-money rules and no draft of either.
2 Preparing A payments bill or draft e-money rules are published, before parliament or passed and not yet in force, or mobile money runs on a central bank's letter with no rules behind it.
3 Establishing A payments law or central bank rules on e-money or payment services are in force, however old. A regional central bank's regulation counts for each member it binds. A law on crypto or virtual assets does not count.
4 Operating Companies that are not banks can be licensed under the law or rules in force to offer payments or issue e-money: a source says so, or names one licensed.
5 Leading A rule requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch. A switch that providers may join by choice, or a rule that binds banks only, does not reach this stage.

Legislation enabling data interoperability

The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy asks for a framework, with implementing acts, for interoperability and levels of assurance, and Article 19 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol for mutual recognition of electronic authentication and digital identities. No African Union instrument requires a country to pass a data sharing law. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, requires sharing and has been applied; it does not measure the law against either.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no law or decree on public bodies sharing data with each other and no draft of one.
2 Preparing A bill, draft decree or draft regulation on public bodies sharing data is published, before parliament or cabinet, or passed and not yet in force. An executive order that legislation is to replace stops here.
3 Establishing A law, decree or regulation on public bodies sharing data is in force, however old. An interoperability framework counts where it is issued as a decree or regulation. A law for one sector, or a data protection law, does not count.
4 Operating The law in force requires public bodies to share: to exchange data, to connect to the government's exchange platform, or not to ask a person for what another public body already holds. A law that only allows sharing does not reach this stage.
5 Leading The law has been applied in the past three years: a regulation, decree or order has been made under it, or public bodies are named as exchanging data because it requires them to.

AI legislation / regulations

The norm

The African Union's Continental AI Strategy, adopted in 2024, asks member states to review and amend existing laws, analyse the gaps and put an enabling framework in place, with independent institutions to oversee it. It sets no date and requires no AI act. This study asks whether a draft exists, a binding rule is in force, a law on AI itself is in force and that law has been applied; it does not measure a law against the strategy.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no law or regulation on AI and no draft of one.
2 Preparing A bill, draft decree or draft regulation on AI exists: published, out for consultation, or before cabinet or parliament. A law that is only announced or planned does not count.
3 Establishing A law, decree or regulation in force names AI and sets a rule for it, in one sector or for one use: AI in higher education, AI-generated content in the media or in elections. A strategy, ethics guidelines or a data protection law's general rule on automated decisions does not count.
4 Operating A law on AI itself is in force and applies across sectors.
5 Leading That law has been applied in the past three years: a regulation or order has been made under it, or the body it names has registered, licensed, decided or penalised.

E-commerce legislation

The norm

Chapter I of the Malabo Convention sets rules for electronic commerce, contracts and advertising, and Article 16 of the AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol commits each party to a legal framework for electronic transactions, Article 27 to protecting those who buy online. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, has rules made under it and has been applied; it does not measure the law against either.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no law on electronic transactions or e-commerce and no draft of one.
2 Preparing A bill or draft exists: published, out for consultation, before cabinet or parliament, or passed and not yet promulgated. A law that is only announced or planned does not count.
3 Establishing A law is in force that gives electronic contracts, records or signatures legal effect, or sets rules for selling online, however old. A law on electronic signatures alone counts, as does the part of a digital code or consumer protection law that does this job.
4 Operating A decree, regulation or order has been made under the law: on electronic signatures, on those who certify them, or on those who sell online.
5 Leading The law has been applied in the past three years: a provider of electronic signatures accredited or licensed under it, online sellers registered or licensed, or an order or penalty made. A government office using electronic signatures does not reach this stage.

Statistics legislation

The norm

The African Charter on Statistics (2009) obliges its parties to bring national law into line with its principles: professional independence, confidentiality, coordination. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, has rules made under it and has been applied; it does not measure the law against the Charter.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no statistics law and no draft of one.
2 Preparing A bill or draft exists: published, validated, out for consultation, before cabinet or parliament, or passed and not yet promulgated. A law that is only announced or planned does not count. A statistics office set up by an ordinary decree, with no statistics law behind it, stops here.
3 Establishing A statistics law is in force, however old: an act, ordinance or decree with the force of law that governs official statistics or sets up the national statistics office.
4 Operating A decree, regulation or order has been made under the law: on the statistics office, the statistics council or board, the approval of surveys or the confidentiality of data.
5 Leading The law has been applied in the past three years: its council or board appointed or meeting, a standard, release calendar or survey approval issued under it, a rule made under it, or a penalty. A census or survey carried out, a strategy or a budget does not reach this stage.

Access to information legislation

The norm

The African Commission's Declaration of Principles on Freedom of Expression and Access to Information (2019) sets out a right of access, a duty to publish and an independent oversight body set up by law, and its Model Law on Access to Information (2013) is the template. This study asks whether a law exists, is in force, has been put to work and has been applied; it does not measure the law against either.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no access to information law and no draft of one. A right of access written in the constitution alone is not a law.
2 Preparing A bill or draft exists: published, validated, out for consultation, before cabinet or parliament, or passed and not yet promulgated. A law that is only announced, planned or called for does not count.
3 Establishing An access to information law is in force, however old: an act, ordinance or decree with the force of law that gives a right to ask public bodies for information.
4 Operating The law has been put to work: a decree, regulation or order made under it, or its information commission or commissioner set up, or an existing body it names taking up the oversight.
5 Leading The law has been applied in the past three years: a decision, order or penalty by the oversight body or a court, published figures of requests or complaints, or a rule made under the law. Training, an awareness campaign or an oversight body merely existing does not reach this stage.

Data protection

Data protection authority

The norm

The African Union's Malabo Convention, Articles 11 and 12, commits each party to an independent authority with stated duties and powers, and the Union's Data Policy Framework asks for authorities that are "independent, funded, and effective". Neither sets a test of whether an authority works. This study asks whether it exists, whether it rules and whether it publishes what it has done; it does not judge independence.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no data protection authority, and no law in force names one.
2 Preparing A law names a data protection authority. No source says its head or members have been appointed.
3 Establishing The authority's head or members are appointed, or an existing body given the job is doing it. No ruling against a named organisation is on record in the past three years.
4 Operating The authority has ruled against a named organisation in the past three years: a decision, fine or order. A court's judgment does not count.
5 Leading The authority publishes a count of its decisions, fines or complaints settled, for a period of a quarter or more ending within the past two years.

National data protection readiness

The norm

The African Union's Data Policy Framework, endorsed by the Executive Council in February 2022, asks each member state for a law, an independent authority and working practice, and asks it to ratify the Malabo Convention. It sets the parts and no measure of them. This study puts the ability to act above ratification.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent No data protection law is in force: there is none, or it is still a bill or has not yet commenced.
2 Preparing A data protection law is in force. The country has not ratified the Malabo Convention and no authority is appointed.
3 Establishing A law is in force, and the country has ratified the Malabo Convention or appointed an authority. Nothing in the past three years shows the authority at work.
4 Operating The law is being implemented: in the past three years an appointed authority has registered organisations, taken breach reports, issued guidance or inspected.
5 Leading The authority is acting: in the past three years it has ruled against a named organisation, or it publishes a count of its rulings. A court's judgment does not count.

Regional collaboration

Regional policy collaboration

The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa (2020 to 2030) asks member states to coordinate their digital policies through the Union and its regional communities, and its policy and regulation initiative, PRIDA, is the working track. This study asks whether a country belongs, takes an active part, has put what was agreed into effect and has it in use; it does not measure a country's policies against the Strategy.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country is suspended from, has left or stands outside the African bodies that make digital policy, and no agreement is on record.
2 Preparing The country belongs to an African body that makes digital policy, or takes part in its meetings, and nothing more is on record. Membership of the African Union or a regional community alone does not count.
3 Establishing In the past three years the country has signed a digital agreement or declaration with another African government or African body, hosted or chaired a meeting of such a body, or won a seat or office in one. Talks, visits and meetings attended do not count.
4 Operating Something the country agreed has been put into effect: roaming charges cut with a neighbour, a joint body set up and meeting, a regional guideline adopted as a national rule, or a shared service opened.
5 Leading What was put into effect is reported in use in the past three years: published figures of its use, a decision of the joint body, or a review of what the agreement delivered. A launch, a renewal or projects agreed do not reach this stage.

Regional legal harmonisation

The norm

The African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa (2020 to 2030) asks member states to ratify the Malabo Convention and to bring their digital laws into line through the Union and its regional communities. This study asks whether a country has signed, ratified, passed a national law and applied the shared law across a border; it does not measure a national law against the shared text.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has not signed, and no other shared African legal text on a digital matter is on record for it.
2 Preparing The country has signed an African legal text on a digital matter, such as the African Union's cyber security and data protection convention, or is bound by a named act or regulation of its regional community. It has not ratified.
3 Establishing The country has ratified or acceded to such a text, by a law or decree approving it or by depositing the instrument. A cabinet approval, a bill or a promise to ratify does not count.
4 Operating A national law, decree or regulation has been passed or amended to give effect to a shared text. A draft, a consultation or a study does not count.
5 Leading The shared law is reported applied across a border in the past three years: a licence or signature recognised, a request between authorities answered, a ruling, or the regional body's finding that the country complies.

Shared regional infrastructure

The norm

The African Union's Programme for Infrastructure Development in Africa and its Digital Transformation Strategy for Africa (2020 to 2030) ask member states to join their networks across borders, to give landlocked countries a route to the sea cables and to share exchange points and data centres. This study asks whether a country has agreed a shared link or facility, started building it, put it in service and has it carrying another country's traffic; it does not measure capacity or price.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no digital link with a neighbour, or stands outside the shared links of its region, and none is on record.
2 Preparing In the past three years the country has agreed to build or join a link or facility shared with another African country: a cross-border fibre link, a sea cable joining two African countries, a shared exchange point, data centre or satellite. Talks, a study or a bid to host do not count.
3 Establishing Building has started in the past three years: a contract awarded, works begun or cable being laid. A tender opened or money promised does not count.
4 Operating A shared link or facility is in service: lit, commissioned or opened to traffic. A ceremony for the start of works, or a test, does not count.
5 Leading It is reported carrying another country's traffic in the past three years: published figures of capacity sold or traffic carried across the border, a named user from the other country, or a fault that cut the other country's service.

Cross-border data transfers

The norm

The African Union's Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (the Malabo Convention), article 14, bars sending personal data to a country outside the Union unless that country protects it adequately. The Union's Data Policy Framework, of 2022, and the digital trade protocol of the African Continental Free Trade Area, of 2024, ask states to let data move across borders under stated safeguards. This study asks whether a rule exists, is in force, has published means of complying and has been applied; it does not judge the rule.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the country has no rule on sending personal data abroad and none is being written.
2 Preparing A rule on sending personal data, or a named kind of data, out of the country is being written: a bill or a published draft that sets a condition for it. A law passed but not reported in effect counts here. A stated intention with no drafting begun does not.
3 Establishing A rule is in force: a law, regulation or directive that lets data leave only on a condition, such as the receiving country's protection judged good enough, the authority's permission, the person's consent or a contract. A data protection law alone does not count, and nor does a rule that data must stay in the country.
4 Operating The authority has published the means of complying: a list of countries data may go to, model contract terms, a procedure for applying for permission, or guidance on transfers. A procedure in the rule's own text, or guidance on the whole law, does not count.
5 Leading The rule has been applied in the past three years: a dated source names a country approved to receive data, or an organisation given or refused permission, ordered to comply or penalised for sending data abroad, or gives a count of permissions. How strict the rule is, is not judged.

The norm

The ladder
Stage Criteria
1 Absent A source from the past three years says the government has no technical standards for its systems to exchange data and none are being written.
2 Preparing Standards are being written: a named set of technical standards for government systems to exchange data is being drafted or is out for consultation. An unapproved edition counts here. A mandate or a stated intention to write standards does not.
3 Establishing Standards are published: the government, the agency responsible or the standards body has issued the data formats, codes, interfaces or protocols its systems must use. 4 Operating Public bodies are required to use the standards: a law, decree, regulation or circular makes them compulsory for the systems public bodies build or buy. A text that says of itself that it is compulsory does not count.
5 Leading A system has been checked against the standards in the past three years: a dated source names a public body, system or product tested, audited or certified against them, or gives a count. How good the standards are is not judged.

The ladder
Stage Criteria

2 Preparing A national standards body exists: a body set up by a law or decree to write or adopt national standards. One announced and not yet created does not count.
3 Establishing The body has issued a national standard on information technology or data: a named standard on information technology, information security, software or data, issued or adopted as a national standard. 4 Operating An organisation can be certified against such a standard in the country: the standards body, or a certifier accredited in the country, offers certification against it or has certified an organisation. A certificate from a certifier abroad does not count.
5 Leading A public body has been certified in the past three years: a dated source names a ministry, agency or state-owned body certified against such a standard by the standards body or a certifier accredited in the country. How good the standards are is not judged.

The ladder
Stage Criteria
