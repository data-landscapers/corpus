Cameroon: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-24

Edition of 2026-08-24  ·  112 systems and instruments tracked, 7 of them not held
↓ PDF
Compiled 2026-08-24 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 112 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 60 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-24, 23 did not, 22 carry no stated baseline, and 7 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 161 sources for this place in the window — 53 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 108 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The clearest movement is in payments, and it is regional. The switch went from about 14.5m transactions worth about FCFA 710bn in 2024 to 20.3m worth FCFA 863bn in 2025, up 40% by volume across 153 participants, and two regional rails that did not exist a year ago now do: an interoperable QR-code standard and membership of the pan-African settlement system.

Two positions worsened. Election-period restrictions took the count from none recorded at the window's start to four shutdowns in 2025, one of them a cross-border cut that took the Central African Republic off a submarine cable; and the lands cadastre modernisation programme was cut 44.2%, from FCFA 1,567,500,000 to a proposed FCFA 874,046,000, below its FY2024 level.

The data-protection authority did not move at either end: created in law, not established, unfunded in both the FY2025 and FY2026 estimates.

The backbone grew and its financing was authorised. Reported length went from about 12,000 km terrestrial on the 2024 reference year to over 15,000 km — figures not reconciled to a single primary — and a decree of 6 March 2026 authorised a CNY 1,350,963,161 loan, about FCFA 108.4bn, for a further 4,000 km, a national data centre and regional links, not confirmed signed. Against that, the Chad border link regressed: a cooperation agreement of May 2025 had promised the section within fourteen days, and the enabling memorandum was suspended on digital-sovereignty grounds with the parties told to revise and re-sign.

Security capability hardened and its measurement did not follow. The incident response team went from operating to running commissioned intrusion-detection hardware and ingesting about 200GB a day; domain-name validation went from no published figure to 56.60% nationally, one operator at 2.75% and most others at 0%, a first-half 2026 target of 98% passing unmeasured. Urban video surveillance drew a fourth tranche of FCFA 39.2bn, taking FCFA 150.8bn mobilised over eight years across three Chinese lenders.

Energy metering moved from financed to deployed: 20,000 advanced meters entered phased deployment from 27 May 2026, with a meter-data centre under construction behind them. The data-centre consolidation policy did not move — the same one-or-two-centre intention, no instrument or site since May 2025.

Identity issuance widened and its registry base began working. The biometric card went from one production centre to two, the second at about 2,000 cards a day. Digital birth certificates went from provided for in the 2024 law with none issued to routine issuance across ten municipalities in three regions, and the digitisation programme behind them went from no named implementation partner to a partner engaged on a 2029 target.

Duty declaration by handset identifier went from not existing to in effect from 1 April 2026; the blocking of unregistered handsets did not happen, two operators refusing on unmet prerequisites and the finance minister making them liable for the duties.

Service platforms advanced on donor and concession money. Consular services went from entry visas online only to second-generation consular cards across 46 posts; the digital health plan replaced an expired 2020-2024 plan that scored 1.8 out of 5 at evaluation, with about FCFA 29bn on eight axes; and the open indicator platform went from built-but-not-launched to publishing.

The largest statistical movement is the census: from a last enumeration in 2005 to a full field round completed between 24 April and 29 May 2026 with 32,059 agents at FCFA 13.28bn against FCFA 64bn initially projected, with no results published.

Both statutes held their position. Loi n° 2024/017 remains in force with no supervisory authority operating, and Loi n° 2010/012 remains in force and invoked to justify network restrictions, civil-society reform recommendations pending. What changed around them is the count of restrictions: from none recorded in the base for the year to August 2025 to four shutdowns in 2025, including election-period restrictions on social platforms.

The satellite licence moved without resolving. From a refusal in March 2025 to a draft concession convention finalised in December 2025 and a file still undecided at the ministry, with the 2024 seizure circular operative at both ends.

Regulatory capability improved: the regulator went from issuing sanctions off manual inspections to holding quality-of-service monitoring platforms handed over in January 2026, with field inspections at Douala in April and no sanction on record since. One regional commitment lapsed: free roaming was mandated for delivery by 30 June 2026 and the base holds no record of implementation.

The AI strategy went backwards in status. A 2040 roadmap on seven pillars was presented in July 2025; a year later cabinet was told it is still being developed, with ten priority areas tabled and no adoption instrument. The AI authority and presidential council remain uncreated, the sovereign large language model has no build, budget or corpus, the national consultations have not reconvened since July 2025, and the readiness benchmark is unchanged at 46.3 out of 100, 30th of 42 African states. What is new is a national high-performance computing centre with no site or cost.

Private technology moved where public technology did not. A smart-port digital concession was signed in November 2025 and took state pension-fund equity in March 2026 on undisclosed terms; an SME funding platform, a local software suite and a climate innovation hub all entered the record from nothing. The state operator meanwhile reached talks only with a global cloud vendor, with no answer on where Cameroonian data would sit.

The two budget programmes moved in opposite directions. The telecommunications ministry's digital programme went from FCFA 10,330,875,000 plus FCFA 1,037,044,000 appropriated for FY2025 to FCFA 19,494,457,000 proposed for FY2026, a 71.5% increase — proposed stage only, the enacted law being unreadable — while the lands cadastre programme was cut 44.2%. Private financing arrived and did not report against itself. A syndicated FCFA 44.884bn was signed for densification, desaturation and extension of the mobile network, and a three-year framework with a foreign incumbent covers fintech support and architecture of a sovereign government cloud with no fee or investment figure attached. The single largest private commitment did not move: US$225m over three years, pledged and never reported against.
