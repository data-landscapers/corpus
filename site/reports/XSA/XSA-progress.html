Shape check, run before the comparison: 56 sources for this place in the window — 10 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-03), 46 in the later (2026-04 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

On 2026-07-27 the SADC-RTGS took a second settlement currency, having settled in rand alone since 2013; on 2026-07-30 the region's central bank governors approved regional frameworks on cybersecurity, climate risk and cross-border crisis management. Both are the work of one institution, the Committee of Central Bank Governors.

The community's own digital instruments did not move: the Digital Transformation Strategy remains the draft tabled to ministers on 12 October 2022, and the 2012 infrastructure master plan reaches its horizon in 2027 with no revision on file.

Cross-border capacity was added by operators: a fibre corridor carrying traffic from 2026-07-06, a subsea system announced on 2025-09-24. Where regional coordination is documented over the period, it is COMESA's rather than SADC's, across an overlapping membership.

The IDEA Project Steering Committee met in Lusaka on 2026-07-30, its third meeting since the programme was approved in June 2024. COMESA describes it as the programme's principal policy, governance and oversight organ, and those attending were other regional economic communities, the ICT associations EACO, SAPOA and SATA, and the World Bank. A regional economic community, not a member state, holds oversight of these countries' digital foundations.

The SADC Financial Inclusion Subcommittee's meeting of 19-20 February 2024, which reviewed the Strategy on Financial Inclusion and SME Access to Finance 2023-2028, is still the newest record the base holds of it.

CRASA is the region's association of ICT and postal regulators, and the base carries one item on it: a secretariat post recording attendance at a continental meeting in July 2026. Nothing it has produced is held, so no position can be stated at either end of the period.

The regulators' association's own year opened on process. It held the first Finance, Risk and Audit Committee meeting of its 2026/27 year over two days in Maseru on 20 and 21 August, hosted by the Lesotho Communications Authority, reviewing financial and audit matters and assessing institutional risk across its regional programmes; no budget, audit opinion or programme list reaches the base from it, and its harmonised regulatory guidelines remain a row the base cannot state.

The instruments that moved were made by central banks. Meeting at Ezulwini, the SADC Committee of Central Bank Governors' subcommittee on banking supervision and financial stability finalised and approved regional frameworks on cybersecurity, climate-related financial risk and cross-border crisis management. Central Bank of Eswatini governor Phil Mnisi said single-country data no longer suffices for regional stability, and urged supervisors to extend oversight to cloud hosting providers, payment processors, and artificial intelligence used in credit scoring and fraud detection.

The Botswana Communications Regulatory Authority announced on 2026-03-13 that Botswana and five other member states — Malawi, Lesotho, Mozambique, Zambia and Zimbabwe — had reduced and harmonised roaming tariffs under the One Network Area framework, with reported cuts of between 10% and 98.6%.

The community's own instruments did not move. The Digital Transformation Strategy and Action Plan is the draft of 12 October 2022, with no adoption decision on record. The Regional Infrastructure Development Master Plan, adopted in August 2012, runs to 2027; with under two years to that horizon the base holds no revision, successor or implementation report.

The SADC-RTGS admitted the Angolan kwanza on 2026-07-27, the first currency added since it began operating in 2013 and the second it settles in. Fifteen of the sixteen member states participate through more than 80 banks, and the system settles R250.7bn a month. The SARB's payments head named the Botswana pula and the Mozambican metical as next, and a SARB spokesperson told Central Banking that Mauritius, Tanzania, Zambia and Zimbabwe are weighing onboarding, potentially by the end of 2027 — intent attributed to the system's operator, not decisions by those central banks.

Retail settlement moved on a smaller scale. TCIB activated its South Africa-Botswana corridor in March 2026, through a single bank. COMESA's Digital Retail Payments Platform, launched on 2025-10-10, is piloting between Malawi and Zambia against a target of under 3% of transaction value, from a stated average of about 8%.

The systems SADC would own are earlier. The cross-border digital-ID and eKYC framework announced in August 2025 with Co-Develop and FinMark Trust covers all 16 member states on paper and follows a South Africa-Lesotho pilot; a year on, the base has no build, budget or launch date for it. The regional internet exchange point stood at a two-bidder shortlist — BoFiNet of Botswana and one Zimbabwean company — on 2025-11-27, with no award in the eight months since.

Capacity that arrived was commercial: the Powertel-Paratus corridor's first phase went live on 2026-07-06, and Seacom announced a 48-fibre-pair Indian Ocean system on 2025-09-24 with no route, cost or in-service date on record.

One regional system entered the record as a procurement rather than a build: the COMESA secretariat has gone to market for a Digital Information Knowledge and Collaboration Platform under contract CS/CC/06/(846-2026), with the technical requirements in annexed terms of reference and no budget, timetable or award published.

Financial inclusion is the only capacity measure the region carries, and it did not move over the window: 119 million adults, 77% of the total, on a 2024 survey that is still the newest held, against 57% in 2011. No later regional figure has been published, so the twelve months add nothing to what was known at their start.

The Botswana Tech Fund announced its launch on 2026-04-21 with a first phase of GBP 5m against a GBP 50m target and a mandate across Southern Africa; the figures are the fund's own, from its launch release.

The region's headline inclusion figure did not move. The newest measurement the base holds is February 2024's — 119 million adults, 77% of the regional total, against 57% in 2011. The period cannot say whether the payment rails that did move have changed it, and the base holds no regional financing instrument or capacity programme of SADC's own.

Five named objects carry no position at either end: a SADC data-protection instrument, a regional AI strategy or position, CRASA's harmonised guidelines, an operational incident-response capability under the supervisory frameworks approved in July 2026, and a regional digital skills programme.

Each was searched on 2026-08-05. Four returned named documents, none of them in the base and none cited above; they are queued for acquisition. On the evidence of the search these are gaps in this record rather than in the region's activity, and the thin sections above should be read that way until the documents are held.

Two further absences have no object to chase. The base holds nothing on Chinese, Indian or Gulf engagement with the region's digital infrastructure, in a period whose two documented external partners were the European Union and the World Bank. And every regional instrument on this ledger is visible through a member state, a central bank, an operator or a lender rather than through SADC's own published record.
