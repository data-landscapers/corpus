This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

On 2 September 2026 eight World Bank specialists opened a four-day working week in Malabo to finish and validate the Estrategia Nacional de Economía Digital. They worked with the Minister of State Honorato Evita Oma, ORTEL, GITGE and CNIAPGE. The week also covered the sector's legal and regulatory framework and what would be the Bank's first operation in the country in 35 years. No completed national digital economy strategy has been found.

ADIGE, the state's named digital agenda, sets 33 action lines for 2019–2025. It is framed against the UN 2030 Agenda, the CPLP Digital Agenda and the African Union's Agenda 2063. In 2024 the World Bank found no implementation roadmap for data exchange or interoperability, and no national strategy for digital public platforms adopted. Equatorial Guinea is the only CEMAC member state without a national financial inclusion strategy (2024).

Ley Número 9/2025 of 9 December 2025, the FY2026 budget, allocates CFA 5,802,246,000 to the transport, telecommunications and artificial-intelligence section. That is 23% less than its 2025 allocation. The ministry's second extraordinary directive council adopted a July–December 2026 plan of 35 strategic objectives under three national priorities. One priority is restoring sovereignty over the national digital space, including the .GQ domain. The plan passes to the interministerial council and then the Council of Ministers, which decide which measures are funded. As at 31 July 2026 the objectives were adopted and not financed.

Decreto 69/2021 of 29 April 2021 adopts the long-run frame, the Estrategia Nacional de Desarrollo Sostenible "Agenda Guinea Ecuatorial 2035". It creates the Consejo Nacional de Desarrollo Sostenible, CONAP, the Agencia Nacional de Desarrollo and the Observatorio Guinea Ecuatorial 2035. In March 2026 a directive council took a draft decree creating a Digital Exclusive Economic Zone at Djibloho. The draft sets no perimeter, incentive schedule or commencement date.

Legislation and regulation

In June 2025 the ministry opened an international tender for a specialist firm to lead an integral reform of Ley 7/2005, the telecommunications law the sector still runs on. The reform is to cover artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, data protection and satellite services. In 2024 the World Bank found the legal framework for the digital economy outdated. Some provisions still rest on colonial-era law from 1965, and laws on data sharing and e-commerce are absent or out of date.

Ley Núm. 7/2024 of 23 December 2024 regulates social-network use, computer crime and cybercrime. It defines 42 terms and sets 40 numbered offences in Título III. Identity usurpation carries three to eight years' imprisonment and fines of 1,000,000 to 50,000,000 CFA francs. Other offences cover child sexual exploitation material, cyberbullying and ICT sexual harassment. Its article 21 creates the Agencia Nacional de Ciberseguridad e Infraestructuras Críticas under the Presidencia del Gobierno, with administrative sanctions of 50,000 to 20,000,000 CFA francs. Articles 56 to 64 provide data-preservation orders of up to 90 days, search and seizure of computer systems, extradition and mutual legal assistance.

CEMAC Règlement n°04/18 of 21 December 2018 governs payment services, in force since 1 January 2019 and applied without national transposition. Electronic signature has been recognised since Ley 2/2017 of 10 January 2017. The most recent telecoms instrument is ORTEL's Orden Ministerial 1/2026 of 13 February 2026, on the broadband-capacity access market. Ley 11/2025 of 12 December 2025 sets up the GE Compras electronic procurement system and a mandatory electronic register of state suppliers.

On 7 May 2025 the Chamber of Deputies approved a land cadastre bill. On 19 November 2025 the interministerial council took an anteproyecto reforming the 1957 Civil Registry Law. On 10 August 2026 it took a draft decree recodifying the tax identification number.

Data protection

The Órgano Rector de Protección de Datos Personales, the supervisory authority created by Ley 1/2016, was still not operational in 2022. The Technical Secretariat and the General Data Protection Registry placed beneath it depend on the same implementing decree. Article 15 of the statute defers the body's composition and powers to that decree.

Ley Núm. 1/2016 of 22 July 2016 is the founding statute. It grants data subjects rights of access, rectification, cancellation, opposition and indemnisation. Its three-tier sanctions regime reaches fines of CFA 15,000,000. Its article 21 lets public bodies share personal data only for historical, statistical or scientific purposes. Private holders need a court order before disclosing it, and no data-localisation or residency requirement applies.

Article 44 of Ley 7/2024, of December 2024, penalises unauthorised use of personal data that breaches confidentiality or security systems. The penalty is four to six years' imprisonment and 1,000,000 to 10,000,000 F CFA. Article 46 penalises disseminating private information without consent at three to six years, more where done for profit. Article 7(c) obliges platform providers to set up technical blocking mechanisms, with a judicially ordered right of retraction under article 8.

Two personal-data regimes are in force at once: a 2016 civil-administrative statute whose named regulator has never been constituted, and a 2024 criminal statute enforced through the courts and a newly created cybersecurity agency rather than through any data-protection authority.

Titre 6 of CEMAC Règlement n°01/20 of 2020 binds banking and payment establishments to lawful collection, confidentiality and security of personal data. Its articles 30 and 31 give customers rights to be told the purpose of any transfer to third parties, to consent in advance, and to rectification or erasure. In March 2026 the Chamber of Deputies approved ratification of the Malabo Convention. The Convention requires a competent data-protection authority and supervision mechanisms.

Regional collaboration

The Chamber of Deputies approved ratification of the African Union's Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection on 25 March 2026. The vote was unanimous across both parliamentary groups, on the report of the foreign policy and treaties committee. The same sitting approved the Convention establishing the International Organization for Mediation. Foreign Minister Simeón Oyono Esono Angue presented the Convention as harmonising African digital legislation and requiring data-protection authorities. Ley 7/2024 had already named it, with the 2001 Budapest Convention, as its model. Equatorial Guinea signed the Convention at Addis Ababa on 28 July 2026, after the vote. No instrument of ratification has been found, so the country is not yet a party.

On 24 September 2025 an ECA/ECCAS workshop at Douala drafted a 2025–2030 regional digital-trade policy and a framework law harmonising data-protection and cybersecurity rules. The framework law provides for free movement of data. Both texts await endorsement by ECCAS's Specialized Technical Committees and then by the Conference of Heads of State.

CEMAC Règlement n°01/20 of 3 July 2020 has bound banks, microfinance institutions and payment service providers since 1 January 2021. It sets fair-treatment, transparency and complaint-handling duties. Its article 52 obliges Equatorial Guinea to put in place a legal framework for personal-data protection. The CEMAC additional act of 1 April 2019 exempts nationals of the six states from visas for stays of up to 90 days. Travellers present a biometric identity card or passport.

Equatorial Guinea and Gabon launched Roaming Cero CEMAC on 18 November 2025, removing extra roaming charges between them. Extending it to Cameroon, Chad and the Central African Republic is an objective of the ministry's July–December 2026 plan. A digital-transformation agreement with Nigeria was signed in February 2026, with both foreign ministers present. In March 2026 INEGE's director-general spoke for the African Group at the 57th UN Statistical Commission. He pressed for consolidated civil-registration and vital-statistics systems.

Standards

BEAC Governor's Instruction N°001/GR/2025 of 17 November 2025 makes ISO 20022 mandatory across CEMAC payment systems. Participants in Equatorial Guinea follow CBPR+/SWIFT rules for international flows and BEAC formats for SYGMA and SYSTAC2. Migration ran from November 2025 to May 2026. A companion circular opened the CEMAC-BANK community on SWIFT's MyStandard portal, where the specifications are published. On 8 April 2026 the UMAC Ministerial Committee at N'Djamena adopted a community interoperable QR-code standard. Under the payment-services regulation, rulemaking and technical standards sit with BEAC and supervision with COBAC, not with a national regulator.

On 10 February 2025 the interministerial council took a draft decree creating a standardisation agency. The agency, AGENOR, presented a seven-stage plan in March 2025 while still pre-launch. The regulator lists two standards instruments in force: OM Núm. 4/2018 of 22 August 2018 on quality of service in electronic communications, and OM Núm. 4/2020 of 3 December 2020 on ORTEL's inspection, sanctioning and revenue-collection powers. Ley 7/2024 defines 42 terms from ciberdelincuencia to ciberterrorismo. In August 2026 the government adopted a national quality-assurance framework for official statistics. It sets a standard protocol for collecting, processing and disseminating socio-economic data.

In November 2025 cross-border radio interference with Gabon was declared resolved. ORTEL and Gabon's ARCEP had completed spectrum-management and frequency-assignment procedures for the border zones. Equatorial Guinea hosted the ITU's Malabo Declaration of 22 May 2026. Signatories commit to safe, ethical, inclusive and responsible use of artificial intelligence, with emphasis on local and multilingual systems.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Since 2 September 2026 Facebook and TikTok have been unreachable without a VPN, with bandwidth throttled. The block followed a viral video accusing the Vice-President of corruption, whose author was arrested. No government order on the block has been found.

Freedom of expression and belief is Equatorial Guinea's most deteriorated of the Ibrahim Index's 96 indicators. It fell 18.3 points over 2014–2023 to 14.4 out of 100, 53rd of 54 African states. Digital freedom stands at 16.8 out of 100, 50th of 54, and media freedom at 31.6, 48th of 54 (2023).

Article 5 of Ley 7/2024 sets eleven prohibitions on users. They include publishing another person's data or images without written consent, creating fake profiles, inciting hatred and spreading false news. Articles 6 to 8 add a judicially enforceable duty of retraction. On 1 July 2026 the Presidency reasserted the law's full force, citing more reports of online defamation and identity usurpation. On 16 September 2026 lawyers for the Vice-President lodged four criminal complaints under it against a digital newspaper's journalist and three others.

In August 2026 the government proposed replacing the 1997 press law with a bill regulating digital media and cable television for the first time. Three bills, on press freedom, audiovisual and digital communication services, and copyright, went to the Cámara de los Diputados on 7 August 2026. The Chamber passed the press freedom bill, and a Senate committee sent it to the plenary by consensus in October 2026. The draft communication law reported on 22 October 2026 would ban content inciting violence, hatred or crime, or harming minors, in print, broadcast and digital media. Streaming, podcast and platform providers would register and answer for what they carry. No adopted text or commencement date has been published. On 8 May 2026 the Senate approved the first access-to-information instrument, scoped to the management of public funds.

On 22 July 2026 the consultancy AVANTE organised a digital-transformation forum in Malabo. Decreto 69/2021 names the Observatorio Guinea Ecuatorial 2035 as the principal channel for consultation. Its article 18 defers the Observatorio's composition and operation to a later decree.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Equatorial Guinea's own resources carry 93.9 per cent of the digital lines in the 2026 budget as appropriated, XAF 23.0 billion of XAF 24.5 billion, against 98.6 per cent of XAF 37.0 billion in 2024; the external share rests on a single project.

The 2026 investment programme gives public-finance modernisation XAF 1.5 billion of external money and nothing from own resources. In 2024 the project had XAF 500 million under the African Development Bank's name. Outside the investment programme the 2026 budget prints no financing source against its digital lines. It carries a XAF 1.26 billion transfer to CNIAPGE, the agency that computerises the administration, in 2026. It also carries XAF 300 million for maintaining the Presidency's applications, licences and programming (2026), against XAF 800 million for that line in 2024.

The 2026 volume prints the 2024 outturn of transfers to the digital agencies. CNIAPGE received XAF 1.17 billion, the business one-stop shop XAF 936 million and the SYDONIA customs-system unit XAF 393 million in 2024. The 2024 budget had voted XAF 1.2 billion, XAF 960 million and XAF 840 million.

The transport and telecommunications ministry's July–December 2026 plan requires frequency fees, port fees and registration charges to be paid in full into the Public Treasury. In September 2026 the Vice-President asked for talks with Meta and other platforms on how they should contribute to public revenue. He also ordered Starlink's sales routed through the regulator ORTEL, with a dedicated bank account for the service's income (September 2026).

The state operators GETESA and GITGE are on the list of public bodies to be privatised under the 2019 IMF programme (2022).

MoUs and other agreements

On 28 December 2025 the Ministry of Transport, Telecommunications and Artificial Intelligence Systems signed a memorandum of understanding with Huawei. It is to accelerate the e-government project under the National Digitalisation Strategy. It covers digital security, network coverage, digital education and complementary technology services. On 9 July 2026 GETESA and Huawei signed a contract at the Jefatura de Estado for a six-month network-optimisation programme. The programme covers existing infrastructure, capacity, data-transmission speed and communications stability.

On 9 February 2026 the government signed a strategic telecommunications agreement with Nigeria's Backbone Connectivity Network. It covers a submarine fibre-optic cable and a commercial data centre. In December 2025 the ministry met the World Bank to work up a strategy for the country's digital transformation. The meeting produced no financing and no timetable.

New investments

China Development Bank signed a USD 70,325,843 commercial loan in 2019 for a video surveillance project. It was undisbursed at 31 December 2019, with no principal or interest repaid. It is the largest external digital commitment still open in the repository. China Eximbank lent USD 402,033,662 in 2015 as a buyer's credit for the CEIBA-2 submarine cable. It paid for a March 2015 contract between the state infrastructure manager GITGE and Huawei Marine Networks. It was a drawing under a USD 2 billion oil-backed facility agreed with Eximbank in 2006. Drawings under the 2006 facility carry 5.5 per cent interest, a five-year maturity and a two-year grace period. The cable loan was fully disbursed by 31 December 2019, with USD 87,944,373 of principal repaid by then.

Equatorial Guinea received USD 11.2 million in official development assistance in 2023, 53rd of 54 African states and USD 6.7 per head. The European Commission has no delegation in the country and runs its cooperation from Yaoundé. ITU technical assistance to eight Central African states, Equatorial Guinea among them, collected ICT indicators from 2021 to 2023 and has closed. On 30 January 2026 EGSA Eloita put a US$50 million proposal to the Vice-President to stand up a new operator, TELGESA. The proposal was under negotiation, not agreed. A World Bank operation with the telecommunications ministry was still in preparation (September 2026).

Three external commitments in all are on the record, over a window from 2015 to 2023: the two Chinese loans, worth USD 472.4 million between them, and the regional ITU project, worth about USD 1 million across all eight countries. Both loans are non-concessional commercial debt rather than grants or credits; the technical assistance is the only non-debt instrument. Across that 2015-2023 window connectivity takes roughly 85 per cent of the money and surveillance equipment the remaining 15 per cent; nothing goes to digital identity, payments, e-government or skills. Every dollar of country-level finance comes from a Chinese state policy bank, and nothing new has been recorded since 2019.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Starlink became available in Equatorial Guinea on 29 August 2026. A data-only mobile broadband plan cost about 10.3% of monthly gross national income per capita in 2022, five times the UN Broadband Commission's target. About 65% of the population was within range of a 4G signal in 2024. Internet use stood at 60.4 users per 100 inhabitants in 2025.

In 2025 the state operator GITGE peered only at foreign exchanges, in Cape Town, Lagos, Accra, Lisbon and London. No domestic internet exchange point operates, so traffic between two Equatoguinean networks is routed abroad. An exchange entered the African Union's project register in 2017 and stalled at construction stage, its cost and financing fields blank. The country joined the ACE cable consortium in 2010 with close to 100 Gbps to Europe. CEIBA-2 is a 400 km cable of 8 Tbps design capacity linking Malabo and Bata, with a branch to Kribi in Cameroon. In April 2026 recurrent incidents on the ASD cable were still degrading international connectivity.

In 2019 the minister described four fibre rings, two insular and two continental, representing more than US$30 million invested. Annobón joined the national fibre network in April 2023 through the ULTRAMAR cable from São Tomé. A digital-rights organisation reported in October 2026 that the island had had no internet or communications service since July 2024. Twenty of a planned 34 towers on the Bata–Mongomo–Ebibeyin–Evinayong road were handed over as operational on 23 September 2025. A CAB-4 fibre interconnection with Gabon was launched at a signing in November 2025. Internet subscriptions rose 10.9% in 2024, while mobile telephony fell 3.2% and fixed telephony 11.8%. In October 2025 the digital terrestrial television project was unbuilt after ten years, with more than half its budget released. On 29 July 2026 the government tendered a Treasury-financed diagnostic, rehabilitation and commissioning of the project.

On 7 October 2026 the Vice-President told the finance ministry to expedite the financing file for GETESA's network agreement with Huawei. Treasury payment arrears to the operator had slowed it.

Data Storage

In June 2026 the Public Finance Modernisation Support Project tendered for a primary data centre in Malabo and a solar-powered backup site in Bata. The two sites are to store, process and back up the state's public financial data. Both must meet the TIA-942 data-centre standard, under a supervision contract financed by the African Development Bank. That contract also covers drafting a business continuity plan and an information security management system. Neither data centre was built or operating as at July 2026. The server-room fit-out tender had its bid deadline extended twice, most recently to 16 September 2026. It was unawarded, with no contractor, cost or delivery date published (August 2026).

In 2024 the World Bank found that GITGE operates national data-centre facilities serving government. No national strategy for digital public platforms or a government cloud had been adopted (2024). In April 2025 CNIAPGE renewed the active and passive equipment of the state data centre. The health ministry runs a separate data centre at its Malabo headquarters, holding the national public-health database (March 2025). No data-localisation or residency requirement applies to any of it.

Energy

Electricity reached 66.9% of Equatorial Guinea's population in 2023. It reached 2.4% of the rural population, against 89.0% in towns (2023). Access to energy scores 43.6 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index, 24th of 54 African states, down 0.5 points over 2014–2023. It is the country's strongest infrastructure measure on that index. A third of firms, 33.3%, reported electrical outages in the 2024 World Bank Enterprise Survey, averaging 0.9 a month.

A Joint SDG Fund programme is establishing solar mini-grids for rural and coastal communities, alongside donor-supported off-grid solar and small-hydro work (2024). The backup data centre tendered for the public finances in June 2026 is to run on solar power. Infrastructure as a whole rose 8.7 points over 2014–2023 to 28.1 out of 100, still 42nd of 54. It is the country's second-fastest improving of the index's sixteen sub-categories (2023).

Technical Capacity

The Ibrahim Index rates the professionalism and delivery capability of the state bureaucracy at 20.3 out of 100 in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states. The score gained 1.4 points over 2014–2023. Public administration rose 7.0 points over the same decade to 26.6 out of 100, still 51st of 54 (2023). The country's standing on the UN's e-government index is comparably low (2024).

In March 2025 the health ministry's routine information system launched with 50 laptops, 30 tablets, 20 desktops, 15 multifunction printers, 10 scanners and 100 digital workstations. More than 100 digital agents were trained (March 2025). From August 2025 officials from across the sectors took an intensive course with UNDP in digital transformation, citizen-centred design and cybersecurity. In July 2023 the government's inspection visits found its regional delegations short of computer equipment, office supplies and transport.

In June 2026 the technical-assistance mission procured for the two public-finance data centres also carried training of the state's technical staff. GETESA's strategic plan for 2026–2030, approved on 3 July 2026, directs the operator to take network maintenance in-house over time. The plan also directs it to train its own staff.

Cybersecurity

In 2024 the World Bank found that no systematic security audit, penetration test or vulnerability assessment of the national systems, the identity system among them, had been carried out. It put a first cybersecurity maturity assessment among its quick-win reforms (2024).

Ley 7/2024, of December 2024, creates an Agencia Nacional de Ciberseguridad e Infraestructuras Críticas. It caps the agency's administrative sanctions at 20,000,000 F CFA. Disclosing a password or access credential for gain or to commit an offence carries five to eight years' imprisonment and a fine of 1,000,000 to 10,000,000 F CFA. The law's criminal-procedure chapter covers data preservation orders, search and seizure of computer systems, extradition and mutual legal assistance.

On 9 December 2025 the Prime Minister brought the telecommunications ministry and the mobile operators together over rising digital fraud and unstable coverage. The operators attributed part of the fraud to abandoned WhatsApp sessions and committed to stricter registration controls. The ministry contracted CIBERTEC to audit national coverage. In October 2026 the Vice-President alleged that a registry official had handed a hard drive of several years of the Presidency's files to a foreign national. The official had used an archive-digitisation project as cover, on the government's account. The government presents it as the country's largest leak of official documents. No judicial finding on it has been found. A China Development Bank loan signed in 2019 for a video surveillance project remained undisbursed at the end of that year.

DPI

Data Exchange

On 7 August 2026 the National Development Agency named limited data-sharing by line ministries as its main obstacle. The obstacle is to monitoring alignment with the National Sustainable Development Strategy. In 2024 the World Bank found no national interoperability framework and no government data exchange layer. Ministries build systems in silos, and cross-agency sharing is ad hoc and manual, by email or USB (2024). No sector system exchanges data with another through a shared government platform. Health, education, justice, tax, social protection, driver licensing, passports, agriculture, employment and electoral registration all stand alone (2024).

The BEAC and the IFC launched the CEMAC credit bureau at Douala on 20 January 2026. Its hub is in Cameroon, with national offices to follow in the other five member states, Equatorial Guinea among them. The tax authority is pursuing interconnection with customs and the Treasury under the PAMFP public finance reform programme, a link inside the Ministry of Finance. In May 2026 a foreign-trade single window took a line in the Treasury's 2027 draft budget. No system, vendor or timetable is described.

The public contracts law of December 2025 establishes the GE Compras electronic procurement information system. The Senate adapted it to CEMAC requirements. No go-live date has been published. In June 2026 the SIGF civil-service steering committee called its main challenge institutional, not technological. It named biometric management, access control and interoperability between institutions. A 2026 study by the state operator GITGE found a wide digital-transformation gap across ministries, interoperability included. In April 2026 the statistical programmes committee recommended giving ministerial statistical services access to administrative registers for statistical purposes. It also recommended a means of exchanging data with INEGE.

Digital Identity and CRVS

In 2024 the World Bank found no functional digital identity or digital authentication system. The identity card's biometric chip is not used for electronic verification, and the e-signature platform planned since 2017 was never built (2024). The ID4D dataset puts holders of the Documento de Identidad Personal at about 63% of adults, higher on Bioko than on the mainland (2022). The card is mandatory from age 18 and required for all public services (2024). CNEDOGE has run an internal issuance database since 2018, interoperable with nothing else. The card is not integrated with the tax register, the civil registry or social insurance, which uses a different number (2024). The civil-registration statute in force is Spain's law of 8 June 1957, which assigns no identifier at birth (2025).

In April 2025 CNEDOGE's Director General told deputies that booklets and plates were out of stock. The centre neither owns the consumables nor is funded for them through the Treasury. A passport cost 7,500 XAF at first issue and 10,000 XAF to renew (2025). On 16 October 2026 the Vice-President authorised modernisation of CNEDOGE's technology, much of it installed in 2017 and 2018. He ordered a technical assessment with the supplier, and new identity card and passport designs. Annobón has no permanent CNEDOGE office and received a seven-day documentation mission from mid-July 2026.

7,706 CEMAC biometric passports were issued through diplomatic missions between 2022 and 2026. Six missions are operational for biometric capture, and others await installation and training (May 2026). The reglamento requires the passport number to equal the identity-card number. It sets no deduplication method, coverage target, audit rule or grievance procedure. The criminal fingerprint database AFIS needs reactivating, according to the security ministry's draft 2026 budget.

In April 2025 the justice ministry announced civil registry offices in every district beyond Bata and Malabo. A multisector commission began redrafting the 1957 Civil Registry Law in January 2026.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Orden de la Presidencia 3/2025 of 10 September 2025 prohibits cash collection of taxes, charges and any other public revenue. Payment goes to Treasury accounts, collaborating banks or official electronic platforms authorised by the government. No list of those platforms has been published, and the order leaves the accounts and channels still to be listed. Its one transitional exception, for sellers of efectos de valor, expires when electronic payment systems are in place, a date the order does not set.

The General Treasury piloted card payment of fiscal obligations at Malabo windows in late 2025. The government has completed a census of state bank accounts and adopted a five-phase roadmap for a Treasury Single Account. It targets production in the first quarter of 2027, built on AMS/X and SINTAG, platforms in use at the BEAC. In 2024 the World Bank found salaries and pensions paid manually and no G2P or P2G digitisation strategy. In September 2025 INSESO told deputies that all social benefits reach beneficiaries' bank accounts, bar the bedridden and severely disabled.

Access to banking scores 7.4 out of 100 in the Ibrahim Index, 45th of 54 African states (2023). The first e-money operator was licensed in mid-2023. A national plan to deploy cash machines and card terminals launched in May 2025, with banks warning that network outages compromise the devices. In February 2026 the Vice-President pressed the five commercial banks and the BEAC to widen terminal and card coverage. In April 2026 he ordered BANGE to repay about 182 million FCFA to some 12,000 customers over improperly timed loan-arrears charges.

SYSTAC 2 entered production on 3 August 2026 across all six CEMAC states. Its instant-payments module was not in production as at 12 August 2026, and SYGMA V10 had no announced date. Equatorial Guinea accounted for 0.41% of CEMAC payment volume in 2024.

Registries

INSESO's social-security roll held 137,445 accumulated affiliates as at 31 December 2024, the one register at national scale. A 2024 pensioner census found 2,282 entries untraceable, unresolved, deceased or fraudulent, saving roughly 4 billion FCFA. No continuously updated population register exists, and the last census was taken in 2015 (2024). There is no social registry and no national social assistance programme, with spending around 0.1% of GDP (2025). The addressing guide filed with the Universal Postal Union in March 2023 carries no postcode field.

SIREC entered service in December 2018 and converted 192,000 birth records covering 1980–2019, all at the central registry. It has not been extended nationwide for want of funding, and the seven regional offices still work on paper (2024). In March 2025 APP RC was issuing electronic certificates carrying QR codes. Vital statistics are assembled separately, from hospital registers and community focal-point forms. The second report recorded 14,667 births and 2,805 deaths in 2024, 64.6% of the deaths outside hospital.

Ley 4/2009 makes land ownership turn on a concession granted by the Presidency, and preserves unregistered immemorial occupation. The Chamber of Deputies approved a Catastro Nacional de Tierras bill on 7 May 2025. Its first field phase opened in Bata's Obispado on 16 June 2026 as a paper document-collection exercise. The Land Registry is connected to no other government register (2024).

Decreto 67/2017 created the Ventanilla Única Empresarial as the Registro Único de Empresas. Since 1 January 2026 Orden de la Presidencia 4/2025 has required a chamber-of-commerce Certificado de Alta before a public entity may contract with or pay an operator. It is a second compulsory register under a different institution, and nothing states which governs when they disagree. Decreto 34/2022 orders a periodic electoral census. It has no biometric capture, and voters present a card not verified against the identity system (2024). The census of June to August 2022 produced a roll of 427,663 voters in 1,486 voting stations for that November's elections.

Sectoral management information systems

ASYCUDAWorld has been live in every customs office since June 2025, on the UN programme's own account. It went live at Bata in November 2024 and at the Malabo port in March 2025. DHIS2 was launched at Baney on 1 March 2025 under a UN Joint Programme. It gives district-level care, vaccination, workforce and medicine-stock data. The pilot in Baney and continental Añisok was declared complete on 25 November 2025, with a national expansion foreseen in 2026. Before DHIS2, ministry staff gathered facility register sheets and transferred them to spreadsheets (2025).

The civil-service system SIGF had reviewed 22 procedures by its steering committee's third meeting on 17 June 2026. SIGF, supplied by Morocco's M2M Group, centralises civil-servant records with biometric attendance and automated payroll. It follows a 2023 enrolment drive covering more than 87,000 public servants and contract workers. The existing HR application, APP SIRH, had more than 20,147 civil servants registered in March 2025. The ninth national yearbook puts the civil service at 20,152 staff in 2025, with 2,731 hires that year, down 20.5%.

SIGREF, the tax system, was contracted on 23 October 2023 under the AfDB-financed PAMFP programme. Completion was foreseen for the first quarter of 2026. On 28 May 2026 the ministry was still reviewing the procedures manual and the system's modules.

The education ministry's most recent yearbook, of May 2023, covered 2020/21. Its statistical unit produces education statistics with PRODEGE, from SIME and the annual school census. Volumes for 2021/22 and 2022/23 were launched on 20 May 2026 as documents, not data. The 2025/26 school census, launched in Bata in June 2026, uses paper questionnaires distributed by inspectors. An action plan of 6 October 2025 covers digitisation of judicial records. Police-process digitalisation is in the draft 2026 security budget.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In October 2026 the Vice-President reviewed the VFS Global e-visa contract ahead of its formalisation. About 35,127 e-visas had been processed since launch on 1 July 2023, some 11,000 a year. The parties had expected 200,000 a year. He asked the departments involved for a report on the system. The country scored 0.2855 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, 176th of 193 member states. On 10 August 2026 the interministerial council agreed to create corporate e-mail accounts for government members and officials.

In March 2025 the director-general of CNIAPGE listed five systems in service: the e-Gob platform, APP SIRH, APP RC, BOE Digital and APP GEX. CNIAPGE runs the programme under the Presidency. The second phase of the informatisation project launched on 10 November 2023, extending case-file digitisation beyond a Presidency and Justice pilot. The 2024 plan set eight objectives, from local networks and a state data-centre building to a fibre ring joining every ministry. It also covered institutional email, IP telephony, videoconferencing and enabling legislation.

In March 2024 CNIAPGE recorded a debt to the connectivity operator GITGE and an incomplete equipment inventory. The Prime Minister asked when the first phase had ended and what the second contained. In 2025 the board gave CNIAPGE control of IT equipment across the administration and barred ministries from buying without its supervision. SIGOB was launched from Bata on 17 June 2021 to let citizens track a procedure online. CNEDOGE enabled online appointments and opened offices at Mongomo and Ciudad de la Paz on 12 June 2026. INAP launched its first institutional website on 10 August 2026. Directives for CONFIN, the integrated public-finance management system, went to the interministerial council on 10 February 2025. The government announced a digital official gazette on 6 October 2026, with free online access and search of past issues.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

On 23 March 2026 the Delegate Minister of the Interior and Local Administration told the Chamber of Deputies that many cities still work from colonial-era or incomplete street and parcel plans. Their streets lack codes and nomenclature. Updated plans are the basis for the cadastre and the property register. The Malabo and Bata study begun towards them was left unfinished. The commission carrying it forward has no stated timetable or budget (March 2026).

At its sitting of 14 July 2023 in Bata, the Council of Ministers recorded that inspection visits found the Delegaciones Regionales short of computer equipment, office supplies and transport. The Delegaciones are the sub-national arm of central administration, and the same sitting recorded their ICT infrastructure as poor.

In March 2025 the e-government services set out for the continental region were in Bata. They covered official training, civil-registry improvements, DGT Digital for vehicle management, and pre-registration of births from reference hospitals. Civil registration ran as a centralised, manual process, with no electronic exchange of records between local offices and the central level (2017).

UNDP lists the ayuntamientos of Malabo and Bata among its government partners in the country (2024). Annobón has no permanent office of the national document-issuing centre and depends on periodic commissions from the mainland (2026). The most recent, a seven-day deployment from mid-July 2026, issued identity documents and passports to residents reaching majority.

Rural digital data capture

Between February and May 2025 a pilot put direct data entry into health facilities in the continental interior district of Añisok and in coastal Baney. It replaced the manual monthly activity sheets in both. The health ministry declared the pilot complete on 25 November 2025 and named it the reference for a national expansion foreseen for 2026. Before the platform, staff gathered facility register sheets and transferred them to spreadsheets (2025).

On 15 March 2026 the Ministry of Education handed the Centro Integrado de Nsok Nsomo in Kié-Ntem a computer room. It holds 30 desktop computers, printers, a laptop, a projector and six-camera CCTV. The national education Anuario for 2021-22 and 2022-23 was launched in 2026, with copies handed to international partners. Its tables are broken down by zona, so the school census collects an urban and rural split (May 2023). ProFuturo's digital-education schools sit in Bata, Malabo and interior schools of the continental region (December 2021).

In 2024 the World Bank found digital services concentrated in urban and capital areas. It put the island-continental divide, with weak mainland connectivity, among the country's cross-cutting obstacles (2024). Rural electricity access is a small fraction of the urban rate (2023). The first phase of a mast programme on the Bata-Mongomo-Ebibeyin-Evinayong road was handed over as operational on 23 September 2025. A further phase is still to be built.

None of the four indicators in the Ibrahim Index's Rural Economy sub-category carries a score for the country in 2023.

Technology

AI

Presidential Decree 61/2026 transferred the Secretary-of-State post for artificial intelligence to Óscar Ondo Ngomo on 23 June 2026. The post dates from 23 August 2024, when Decree 86/2024 named José Eko Owono to it. Ondo Ngomo took office in the transport and telecommunications ministry on 7 July 2026. The ministry also has a Director-General for AI Systems, Reginaldo Boriko Okomo (July 2026).

From August 2025 an anteproyecto proposing a national regulator, the Autoridad Nacional de Inteligencia Artificial y Datos, was in circulation. No enacted artificial intelligence instrument has been found. Ley 7/2024 is technology-neutral and names neither artificial intelligence nor deepfakes. CNIAPGE's 2024 action plan names no artificial-intelligence component among its eight objectives. On 6 June 2025 the health ministry launched the Guinea Salud chatbot as a pilot with MCD Global Health. It answers on vaccination, medical evacuation, clinic licensing and HIV. On 21 July 2025 students began a five-month build of the country's first AI-integrated weather station. No completed station has been reported. The 2026 Africa AI Governance Index places the country 48th of 54, in its Nascent tier.

The second Foro Nacional de Inteligencia Artificial ran on 12–14 May 2026 at the Afro-American University of Central Africa, sponsored by Deep Learning Indaba. In May 2026 the cabinet approved a GETESA modernisation programme committing the operator to AI-based customer-service systems. The country hosted the ITU's Malabo Declaration on the use of artificial intelligence (May 2026). In March 2026 INEGE's director-general argued at the UN Statistical Commission for AI in health systems, with data protection and human rights guaranteed. The 2026 budget cut the section covering transport, telecommunications and artificial-intelligence systems.

ICT Industry

Salvador Nguema Funtam took over as head of the state ICT operator GITGE on 7 August 2026. In July 2026 the merger of the state telecoms holding TELGESA with Loita Group was halted pending a full audit of GECOMSA. By mid-October 2026 the government aimed to sign with the investor before the month's end, with KPMG valuing GECOMSA's assets. Director-general posts at state-participation entities, GITGE among them, are filled by competitive interview (July 2026). Appointments are provisional for six months and terminable if the entity's objectives are missed. GETESA, SEGESA, GECOTEL, GITGE and two others are on the finance ministry's privatisation list, with no completed transaction recorded (2022).

The Senate passed a general law on special economic zones on 8 May 2026, aimed at petrochemical, industrial and energy zones. A copyright and related rights bill reached the Chamber of Deputies on 7 August 2026. It implements the 1997 Berne accession and the 2015 Bangui Agreement, extending protection to new modes of distribution.

Banks and telecom operators, not independent fintechs, dominate the market (2026). In April 2026 digital content creators told a Vice-Presidency meeting that banking and administrative obstacles prevent them being paid. The banks undertook to bring proposals to a later meeting, and Alibaba attended. The transport ministry's plan of July 2026 sets a youth-employment target for the digital economy. The US State Department's 2025 investment climate statement records no bilateral investment or double-taxation treaty with the United States. It records underinvested power generation, and major investment decisions taken at presidential and vice-presidential level.

Innovation ecosystem

Equatorial Guinea is not among the 133 economies ranked in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024. The AfriLabs network lists a single member hub in the country, Dreams Hub in Malabo (2025). A startup tracker shows only a handful of Equatoguinean companies with disclosed funding, together well under a million US dollars (2026).

Dreams Hub opened its La Capsula incubator in Malabo on 30 July 2022, with TotalEnergies Startupper winners as its first cohort. In 2022 UNDP's Guinea ON project with the telecommunications ministry trained some 60 young people and funded ten startups at 6 million CFA each. In March 2026 the IDENTIC Foundation signed incubation agreements with six projects. No startup law is in force, and in February 2026 the foundation and the Bioko chamber of commerce called for one.

The Long-Term Strategic Plan approved in 2024 commits the country to digital literacy from primary level and to innovation centres. In September 2026 a World Bank-supported telecommunications programme was announced. Its planned measures include regulatory sandboxes, a telecoms business incubator, a digital services law, a data policy and strengthening of the regulator ORTEL. A draft CEMAC payment-services regulation under review in June 2026 would open a twelve-month sandbox to fintechs and payment aggregators. It would also require strong authentication and reverse the burden of proof in fraud disputes.

Capacity

Literacy

In 2024 the World Bank found that information technology is not a formal component of the national school curriculum. No data exist to compute what share of the population holds basic, intermediate or advanced digital skills (2024). The same diagnostic records no national ICT education policy.

The country's education enrolment score of 19.3 out of 100 ranks 49th of 54 African states in 2023. It fell 1.3 points over the decade. Equality in education scores 58.6 out of 100, 19th on the continent, up 6.5 points over the same period. The index carries no score for education completion or for human resources in education (2023).

E-tic, a virtual digital-skills training centre, opened to the public on 1 July 2021 under founder-director Rosa Hinestrosa Gómez. It built two online courses on the UNESCO and OECD digital-competence frameworks, for citizens and for teachers. ProFuturo's programme with the Catholic teaching-centres association ACCEGE runs under a November 2017 agreement. It reported reaching around 16,000 children and several hundred teachers across a few dozen schools (December 2021). In January 2026 the Minister of State for Education named computing and communications among the priority technical trades under the Ley General de Educación. In July 2026 the director-general for artificial intelligence said that acceptance, awareness and digital literacy decide whether digital adoption holds.

Five distinct digital-skills and education-technology initiatives are on record since 2021, and none of them has produced the population-level skills measurement the 2024 diagnostic found missing.

Training and skills

In July 2026 fifty trainers were selected from 466 applications in Malabo for the UniPod Formación de Formadores programme. The Ministries of Education and of Telecommunications run it with UNDP's Timbuktoo platform and the incubator IDENTIC. The twenty best trainers from Malabo and Bata are to cascade the training nationally in August and September 2026.

A UNESCO train-the-trainers programme opened in Malabo on 4 July 2026. It runs with the Ministry of Education to 2028, in artificial intelligence, 3D design and printing and Scratch programming. Agriculture and health are its priority application sectors.

In August 2025 the foreign ministry and UNDP launched an intensive Digital Fitness programme for the public sector. It covers digital transformation, citizen-centred design and cybersecurity. CNIAPGE's first phase trained civil servants as informatisation focal points and equipped five training rooms (January 2019). Health professionals were trained under the DHIS2 pilot in Baney and Añisok (November 2025).

The state operator GITGE runs TEG Campus hackathons and the Conéctate schools programme (2024). E-tic has offered online citizen and teacher courses since 2021. The government has rehabilitated the workshops of the Instituto Politécnico Modesto Gené Roig in Bata, and technical centres in Bata, Mongomo and Oyala (January 2026). Computing is among the trades those centres are meant to teach.

Research institutions

The second national artificial-intelligence forum ran at the Afro-American University of Central Africa in Ciudad de la Paz on 12–14 May 2026. Deep Learning Indaba sponsored it, with the ministry, the National University, INSTTIC, MUNI and GITGE on its panel. A ministry delegation met AAUCA, UNGE and INSTTIC on using artificial intelligence in the classroom and on UNESCO's recommendations.

Tertiary engineering and computing teaching is concentrated in the National University of Equatorial Guinea, through its Faculty of Architecture and Engineering in Bata and its engineering school in Malabo (2025). A small number of private institutions sit alongside it. In February 2026 the university installed an artificial-intelligence laboratory with the pan-African charity STEM Power, alongside donated computing equipment.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services scores 12.7 out of 100, 52nd of 54 African states, and has fallen 2.7 points over 2014-2023 (2023).

The social security number is a different number from the national identity number (2024). Civil registry data is not available to social-sector ministries (2024). National identity card coverage leaves a substantial minority of adults out and is thinner in the continental provinces than on Bioko (2022). Documents are not issued free of charge (2024). Issuance has been held up by shortages of booklets and plates rather than by systems (April 2025). The Chamber of Deputies took access remedies from its April 2025 review of CNEDOGE: cutting delays and extending issuance to more diplomatic offices and embassies abroad.

Annobón has no permanent document-issuing office and is reached only by periodic visits (July 2026). Residents otherwise travel to Malabo or Bata. A digital-rights organisation reported in October 2026 that the island had had no internet or communications service since July 2024. It attributed the blackout to the government. Services were extended to further continental locations from 12 June 2026. There is no national social assistance programme and no social registry to target benefits through (2025). Social benefits are now paid directly into beneficiaries' bank accounts (September 2025). Banking access is among the lowest on the continent (2023).

Digital public platforms are nascent and carry no documented accessibility features for disabled or elderly users (2024).

The Ombudsman opened a complaints website on 28 September 2022. An online anti-corruption reporting portal taking anonymous reports was cleared for operation on 24 October 2024. The health ministry's patient-ombudsman office takes complaints through an online form, and none of the three states a volume or response time.

Equatorial Guinea has acceded to the 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol, the 1969 OAU Refugee Convention and the Kampala Convention (September 2026). It has no domestic refugee-status-determination law and no comprehensive procedure for identifying stateless people. Nationality is governed by the Constitution and the Equatoguinean Nationality Regulation.

Digital divides

Women's access to public services fell 6.0 points over 2014-2023 to 9.7 out of 100, 51st of 54 African states. It is one of the country's ten most deteriorated governance measures (2023). Equatorial Guinea has the sixth highest GDP per capita in Africa, USD 7,066.6, on a population of 1.7 million (2023). Its overall governance rank is 50th of 54 (2023).

In 2024 the World Bank found digital services clustered in the capital and other towns, with weak mainland connectivity holding back access. Rural electricity access is close to absent (2023). New towers on the Bata-Mongomo-Ebibeyin-Evinayong road came into service in September 2025, with a further phase still to build. Donor-funded school digitisation runs through Bata, Malabo and interior continental schools, naming neither Annobón nor Corisco (December 2021).

Substantially fewer women than men use mobile internet (2023). Equality in education is one of the country's ten best-scoring governance indicators (2023).

A virtual training centre opened in 2021, pricing its courses for the whole population as a measure against the digital divide. On 7 April 2026 a solar-powered portable digital library that creates its own local wifi network was presented to the finance and education ministries. It needs no internet connection.

Data

National statistics

INEGE's Cifras compendium gives a population of 1,668,768 for 2024, a projection from the 2015 census count of 1,225,377. Decreto núm. 25/2025 of 3 March ordered a fifth Censo General de Población y Vivienda and a first Censo General Agropecuario. On 27 August 2026 the Consejo Nacional de Estadística heard a proposal to run the two as a single operation. It also heard plans for a second Demographic and Health Survey and a second Labour Force Survey. Six INEGE statisticians had studied Burundi's combined, fully digital 2024 census on 10–16 August 2026. On 26 October 2026 INEGE, with UNDP and FAO, opened a workshop to validate the two censuses' methodological documents and data-collection tools. No enumeration date has been found.

The Ibrahim Index scores the statistical system at 33.6 out of 100 in 2023, 44th of 54 African states, up 2.1 points since 2014. It carries no data for 22 of its 96 indicators for the country. Four of the five pillars of the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators fall in the 20-49% band (2023). No agricultural, business or labour force survey has been run in the past decade, against one health survey and one household survey (2023).

INEGE presented the ninth Anuario Estadístico in Malabo on 25 September 2026, covering the last five years. It named weak administrative registers, undigitised processes and poor interoperability among the system's challenges. The series is assembled from line-ministry returns, and the eighth edition of October 2025 was the first also in French. Work opened in April 2026 on a successor to the 2022-2026 statistics strategy, with no adoption date published. A first Catálogo Nacional de Indicadores was launched with AFRISTAT at Malabo on 24 June 2026. In February 2026 a committee began implementing the 2008 System of National Accounts, approving product and activity nomenclatures. In October 2026 the country was elected to chair the UN Statistical Commission for Africa for 2026-2028, INEGE's director-general presiding.

Open data

Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory scores Equatorial Guinea zero out of ten in five domains: digital, built environment, pollution, food security and poverty. Government finance is its strongest category at 7 out of 10, and balance of payments scores 6 (2024). Energy, banking, national accounts, labour, education, health and population score between 3 and 4.5. Crime and reproductive health statistics score 2 (2024).

The Ibrahim Index puts disclosure of public records at 9.8 out of 100 in 2023, 51st of 54 African states and static since 2014. Access to government information rose 8.1 points over the same decade, to 11.8 out of 100 and 45th of 54 (2023).

On 8 May 2026 the Senate unanimously passed the Proyecto de Ley de Transparencia, Acceso a la Información Pública y Buen Gobierno en la Gestión de Finanzas Públicas. It is scoped to the management of public and internationally granted funds, not a general right of access. No enactment has been found.

The ninth Anuario Estadístico was presented to ministries, agencies and diplomatic missions on 25 September 2026. The education yearbooks for 2021-22 and 2022-23 were handed to UNICEF, UNDP, UNESCO and the African Development Bank on 20 May 2026.

Use of satellite data

An ECCAS Situation Room has operated at Douala since September 2024 under the continental AMHEWAS early-warning system. As an ECCAS member, Equatorial Guinea is also covered by a sub-regional WIGOS observing network and a regional meteorological centre at Yaoundé (November 2025). On 10–12 February 2026 the ten ECCAS states' meteorological, hydrological and disaster-risk institutions met at N'Djamena. They formalised communication with the regional centres under CREWS Central Africa and Early Warnings for All.

Ley 4/2009 substitutes a document-by-document concession inquiry for a cadastre and creates no systematic land survey.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

Starlink went on sale in Equatorial Guinea on 29 August 2026. In July 2026 United States offshore oil operators had been cleared to use the service in the country's waters. The national backbone was financed and built by Chinese lenders and vendors (2015).

The State Department's 2025 Investment Climate Statement records no bilateral investment treaty or double-taxation treaty with the United States. It records underinvestment in critical infrastructure, including power generation. It records that major investment decisions are still taken at presidential and vice-presidential level.

In early September 2026 a World Bank mission worked in Malabo with ORTEL, GITGE, CNIAPGE and ministry teams. It covered the national digital economy strategy, the sector's legal framework and a Bank-financed project to follow. In July 2026 a CNEDOGE team installed a passport processing and issuance unit at the consulate general in Houston. It extends the national issuance system to the diaspora in the United States.

China activities

On 28 December 2025 the Ministry of Transport, Telecommunications and Artificial Intelligence Systems signed an e-government memorandum of understanding with Huawei. Its execution is to be coordinated across CNIAPGE, the regulator ORTEL, the civil-service ministry and the operators MUNI and GETESA. It followed a meeting between the President and Huawei's North Africa president in November 2025. GETESA signed its own six-month network-optimisation contract with Huawei on 9 July 2026. In August 2026 Huawei was the first caller on GITGE's new director general. In June 2026 the Vice-President recommended that the electricity utility SEGESA form a company to sell fibre on the power grid. He named Huawei or a comparable firm as its partner.

China Eximbank financed the CEIBA-2 submarine cable in 2015 under an oil-backed buyer's credit facility agreed in 2006. The loan paid a contract between GITGE and Huawei Marine Networks. The cable also joins the ACE system to Cameroon, the two landing stations in Bata sitting together. China Development Bank's 2019 loan for a video surveillance project was still undrawn at the end of that year, and is understood to fund cameras at the Presidential Palace, Malabo airport and the Malabo–Sipopo highway. ZTE is understood to do most of the work.

In June 2025 China committed to support the country's technological development in cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and critical-infrastructure management. The commitment followed the Vice-President's visit to Beijing, under a roadmap to make the country a Central African technology hub. It includes training for more than 200 INSTTIC students with Chinese universities and a cybersecurity and AI forum at Djibloho. Equatorial Guinea is to co-chair FOCAC in 2027. In April 2026 Alibaba joined a government working meeting on e-commerce and digital payments, chaired by the Vice-President.

EU activities

As at September 2026 the European Union has no delegation in Equatorial Guinea and runs its cooperation from Yaoundé. There is no bilateral allocation under the 11th European Development Fund and no NDICI multiannual indicative programme for 2021-2027 (September 2026).

EU support runs through civil-society and human-rights programmes, and through ECCAS on maritime security, AfriIPI and aviation fuel (September 2026). A Team Europe civil-society roadmap is in preparation. None of these programmes is a digital or connectivity channel, and none is bilateral.

On 22 October 2025 the President raised the unbuilt digital terrestrial television project with Spain's chargé d'affaires. More than half its budget had been released over ten years. He asked the two governments to work together on the return of the funds.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf state or Emirati investor held a digital-sector agreement, financing commitment or operating presence in Equatorial Guinea as at September 2026.

India activities

India's April 2026 country brief records 22 Equatoguinean officials on a special ITEC course in computer science and emerging technologies on 12–25 November 2025. India allocates the country 32 ITEC training slots a year, and 35 were used in 2025-26. Bilateral trade was US$463.32 million in 2024-25, almost all of it Indian imports. A Department of Telecommunications delegation visited in July 2018 to explore cooperation. No Indian digital financing commitment, vendor presence or digital public infrastructure agreement has been found.

Digital sovereignty

The transport and telecommunications ministry's July–December 2026 plan creates a National Negotiation Commission to recover the .GQ domain. A foreign operator manages the domain (July 2026). In September 2026 the government cancelled Starlink contracts obtained outside official channels and put sales under the regulator ORTEL. It ordered Equatoguinean technicians trained to install and maintain the equipment.

CNIAPGE signed a licence agreement with Microsoft in June 2015. It works with the Spanish firm Munitecnia on the Experta e-government platform (2025). In April 2025 the social security institute's head told deputies its database has no replica site and depends on a single internet provider. As at September 2026 no instrument fixing where state data is held, or under whose law, had been found. Where the national identity database is hosted had not been published.

The December 2025 e-government memorandum with Huawei has no published terms on data. Nor does the February 2026 agreement with Nigeria's Backbone Connectivity Network, which covers a commercial data centre. Neither text sets out data jurisdiction, portability or exit (September 2026).

In January 2026 at Davos Amini's chief executive proposed data infrastructure and sovereign AI capabilities to the Vice-President. He invited the company to begin a diagnostic in the country. At the Korea-Africa ministerial conference in September 2026 the finance ministry's delegate minister named digital sovereignty among Africa's main strategic challenges. She offered the country's energy and digital infrastructure for co-investment.
