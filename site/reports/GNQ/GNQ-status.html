Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Equatorial Guinea has no published national digital strategy on file. What it has instead is a set of individually dated builds — a civil-service management system, a Treasury Single Account, two government data centres out to tender — and a governance layer being made through corporate control of the state's own telecoms holdings rather than through instruments.

The legal position is the weakest part of the record. Ley 7/2024 on social media and computer crime is in force and its text is not held; the agency it creates, ANCIC, has nothing on record establishing that it operates; the reform of the 2005 telecoms law went to tender in June 2025 with no award published; and the country signed the Malabo Convention on 28 July 2026 without becoming a party. A data protection law has stood since 2016 without a functioning regulator.

A single figure gives the scale of the delivery problem. GITGE's own study of the ministries in July 2026 put interoperability at about 15%, big data at about 21% and data analysis at about 10% — the maturity floor every system on this page is being built onto.

Standards

System or instrument Status ↗ source Milestone
Published quality-of-service or interoperability standards Implemented OM Num. 4/2018 gazetted 22 August 2018, confirmed via ORTEL's own legislacion index

Ley 7/2024 of 23 December 2024 regulates social media, computer crime and cybercrime — under-14s may not join social networks without parental consent, personal data may not be published without express written consent, and identity usurpation through false profiles is criminalised — and the full text is not held. It creates ANCIC under the Presidency, and nothing on record shows that agency operating. A quality-of-service regulation, OM Núm. 4/2018, has stood since 22 August 2018, confirmed only by title and date on ORTEL's own index. The government has also proposed replacing the 1997 Ley de Prensa y Medios Audiovisuales, for the first time bringing digital media and cable television within its scope alongside copyright and related-rights provisions aligned with the Berne Convention and the Bangui Agreement; the bill is with parliament's Public Works and Communication committee, with no adoption date or text published.

Treaty position: the Chamber of Deputies voted on 25 March 2026 to authorise ratification of the Malabo Convention, and the African Union's own register recorded no signature at all as of 2 February 2026. The signature followed on 28 July 2026. The country is a signatory and not a party to the convention adopted in, and named after, its own former capital.

The July–December 2026 consolidated action plan carries 35 strategic objectives under three national priorities, one of them restoring sovereignty over the national digital space including the .GQ domain, which a national commission is to recover from an unnamed foreign operator; the plan is adopted and not yet financed, and the document is not published. Alongside it, directorships at state-participation entities are now filled by competitive interview on six-month provisional terms, and supervising officeholders have been ordered off the boards they oversee.

Open data amounts to one process. EITI data collection for the 2024 report opened at Malabo on 30 July 2026 as the last outstanding measure before validation of the IMF Staff-Monitored Programme. No open-data portal or policy is held.

International capacity runs through GITGE. Ceiba-2, 400 km on the Malabo–Bata–Kribi route, has been in service since 2018 alongside Ceiba-1 and the ACE landing, for which GITGE hosted the consortium's 36th operation and maintenance committee in May 2026. Two additions are prospective: a Medusa landing at Bome or Bata, put at EUR 20–60m for 2029–30 and driven by degradation on the existing ASD cable, and the Nigeria agreement of February 2026, which bundles a data centre and publishes no delivery date.

GETESA's modernisation — 5G, eSIM, public Wi-Fi and fibre reactivation — runs on a Huawei network contract signed on 9 July 2026, which the independent press reads as a rescue and criticises for opaque procurement. The second operator, TELGESA, is not yet constituted: the merger with Loita Group was halted on 22 July 2026 pending a full audit of GECOMSA.

A temporary authorisation signed on 28 July 2026 permits North American companies to use Starlink for offshore operations inside jurisdictional waters; extension to individual subscribers is deferred on stated security grounds, with type-approval and the competences of company and regulator unresolved. The digital terrestrial television project is under tender for a diagnostic and regularisation of what has already been built.

New hosting capacity is prospective: the public-finance modernisation project tendered a primary data centre in Malabo and a solar-powered backup in Bata in June 2026, and a digital economic zone at Djibloho is under evaluation. The only capacity figure held is older: GITGE's own factsheet describes two technical facilities, in Bata and Malabo, each with two generators and ten nodes. Separately, the electricity utility SEGESA proposed fibre-optic grid digitisation with Huawei on 21 May 2026, with spare capacity to be leased to telecom operators; the Vice-President called it welcome but unproven, pending review.

The DIP is issued by CNEDOGE with the most recent figures dating from the 2025 national tour — 7,670 dossiers obtained and 7,275 processed — and the service was extended in June 2026 with two provincial offices and online appointment booking. A digital identity system was recommended by the World Bank in 2024 and does not exist; the civil registry behind it remains paper-based, and fraudulent birth certificates are an acknowledged control problem.

The SIGF is in implementation on an eighteen-month timeline with biometric attendance and automated payroll, supplied by M2M Group. The Treasury Single Account has completed a census of all state bank accounts and adopted a five-phase roadmap targeting production in the first quarter of 2027, to be built on the BEAC's own AMS/X and SINTAG systems. The annual tax licence moved online in January 2026. Against these, no national interoperability framework has been published, and the only cross-system exchange in operation is INTERPOL's border screening at ten sites. Predating this build, the SIGOB administrative-procedure tracker has let citizens follow a request online since 2021, with no usage figure published since launch.

BANGE MOBILE GE and Getesa Money are live without published user figures, no licensed mobile-money operator has been reported, and the national ATM and point-of-sale plan has produced directives rather than a deployment count. The BEAC's own data puts the country at 0.41% of CEMAC payment volume at end-2024, the smallest of the six member states.

The fifth population census and a first agricultural census are in preparation against a last count in 2015, with no enumeration date.

A State Secretariat for Artificial Intelligence, held since 23 August 2024 and passed to Óscar Ondo Ngomo on 7 July 2026, sits within the Ministry of Transport, Telecommunications and Civil Aviation alongside an existing director-general for artificial intelligence. A draft Ley de Inteligencia Artificial proposing an autonomous ANIAD authority is in preparation under the ministry, with no enactment date published.

The consultancy AVANTE, backed by the national development agency ANDGE, brought public institutions, banks and technology firms together in Malabo on 22 July 2026 with artificial intelligence, electronic payments and public-administration reform as its three axes.

A China cooperation roadmap aims to make the country a Central African technology hub, with INSTTIC training and a cybersecurity and AI forum at Djibloho named under it; no delivery or disbursement figure is on record.

The Chinese-financed Video Surveillance Project (2019) has no delivery, siting or oversight arrangement on record; financing detail for the sector is under Finance, below.

The UNDP Digital Fitness programme trained multisectoral officials in August 2025 with no later cohort on record, and INSTTIC training with Chinese universities runs at more than 200 students. National research on public-administration data systems appears once, as doctoral findings presented at the AVANTE forum arguing that structural gaps and elapsed time, not technology, are the constraint.

Access is measured only by a reference study, cited and not absorbed here: DataReportal's Digital 2026 put mobile connections at 45.4% of population and internet penetration at 60.4% in November 2025. Nothing national measures digital literacy or the gap between Bioko and the mainland.

Most of these absences share one cause: access rather than inactivity. The government's news outlet is cookie-walled and serves no body or publication date to automated retrieval, so developments known only by their existence — a Huawei e-government memorandum, an e-government law, the full text of Ley 7/2024, a unique person and entity identification code — have no source of record and need a hand-clip.

The rest are absences of a different kind: no power-supply figure for network sites, no municipal digital service, no national digital strategy, no open-data portal, no literacy measurement and no measured Bioko-mainland gap.
