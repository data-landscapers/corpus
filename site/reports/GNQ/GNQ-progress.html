Equatorial Guinea: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-04-2  ·  122 systems and instruments tracked

Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-04.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy The digital agenda launched in 2019 on 33 lines of action ran to 2025 and has produced no implementation report, budget figure or successor.
Full recordIt was aligned with three international frameworks and is the agenda a World Bank diagnostic assesses against, with the diagnostic's recommendations outstanding (diagnostic, priority actions).

The national development strategy to 2035 commits to gradual privatisation of state telecom ownership, a universal broadband backbone across all regions, an updated telecoms legal framework and a science and innovation fund that may be financed by a special tax on oil and gas exports; the diagnostic's own digital recommendations, a one-stop shop, online business registration and a digitised commercial registry, remain undelivered (strategy text, economic memorandum).

A consolidated action plan for the second half of 2026 was adopted in July with 35 strategic objectives under three national priorities, one of them restoring sovereignty over the national digital space; it is not yet financed and goes to the Council of Ministers, which decides what is recorded and funded (action plan).
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy An international tender to draft a replacement telecommunications law opened in June 2025 and has produced no award, draft or revised timetable.
Full recordThe tender was scoped to cover artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, satellite connectivity and data protection (tender).

Fourteen months on, the 2005 statute still governs a sector that has since acquired a second state operator, an offshore satellite authorisation and a directorate for artificial intelligence, none of which it contemplates.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy The transport ministry set the country's first published target: 85 per cent coverage through 34 new towers by end-2026.
Full recordEquatorial Guinea has had a fibre backbone since the last decade — four rings and an international gateway, over US$30m invested — and no coverage target against it.

The half-year plan of July 2026 supplies one: 85 per cent second and fourth generation coverage through 34 new towers by the end of the year. Twenty of those towers were already live on the mainland trunk road by September 2025.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A negotiating commission is to be created to recover the country-code domain from a foreign operator the release does not name.
Full recordThe commission was named in the July to December 2026 plan (plan).

Nothing is established about the current administration arrangement, so the base holds an intention to recover a domain and no statement of who holds it or on what terms. No cloud or storage policy exists beside it.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap The World Bank diagnostic found no interoperability framework, and none has been published since.
Full recordThe diagnostic records the absence plainly: no interoperability framework and no digital skills data held.

Where the problem surfaces operationally it is named as such: the third steering-committee meeting of the civil service management project in June 2026 reviewed 22 procedures and identified interoperability as the challenge. A national digital economy strategy was still being finalised with World Bank support in September 2026.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy Artificial intelligence acquired a director-general and a state secretariat before it acquired a policy; the strategy is still in drafting.
Full recordThe institutional layer arrived first, and the ledger carries both offices. What the base does not hold is an adopted strategy.

The forum around it is academic: a second national artificial intelligence forum in May 2026 drew ministry, university and international research participation on artificial intelligence policy, and a sovereign artificial intelligence and data infrastructure collaboration was pitched to the Vice-President at Davos in January.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies The 2015-2017 electronic laws cover data protection and retention and set no localisation requirement.
Full recordThe five laws published together in the official bulletin run to data protection, data retention, electronic signature, electronic access and internet communications. None requires data to be held in the country.

Hosting is instead a procurement question: the national data centre stood up under the administration computerisation programme and a backup site is at works stage, with no rule saying what must go into either.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy Data governance rests on the 2015-2017 electronic laws; no policy instrument sits above them.
Full recordThe law package is the whole of the framework: protection, retention, signature, access and communications, enacted between 2015 and 2017 and unamended.

What would sit above it has not been adopted. The national digital economy strategy was still being finalised in September 2026, and the ratification of the regional data protection convention was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in March — an external commitment rather than a domestic policy.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy The first transparency and access-to-information law passed the Senate unanimously in May 2026; no portal follows from it.
Full recordThe Prime Minister opened the parliamentary debate on transparency, access-to-information and public finance bills in February 2026, and the Senate passed the transparency bill unanimously in May.

An access right is not an open data policy. Nothing in the base records a publication scheme, a dataset inventory or a portal following from the law, and the statistics strategy that might have carried one is a production plan rather than a release policy.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation Ley 1/2016 has been in force since August 2016, giving rights of access, rectification, cancellation and opposition and a sanctions regime to CFA 15,000,000.
Full recordIt was gazetted on 22 July 2016 and took effect twenty days later (the law).

Ten years on, no implementing decree, guidance or enforcement action under it is held, and the country signed the continental convention on the same subject only in July 2026.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation Ley 7/2024 on social media and computer crime has been in force since December 2024, its full text unheld and its provisions known from reporting.
Full recordIts provisions were reported in rollout from July 2026 (the law).

The same statute creates the national cybersecurity and critical infrastructure agency under the Presidency, and that body has no premises, staffing, budget or activity on record, so the law's institutional half exists only on paper.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id Identity rests on a 1957 registry law and a regional passport regulation, with reform under review.
Full recordThe statistical questionnaire records the position exactly: civil registration still runs on a registry law inherited from 1957. The biometric credential above it is regional, governed by the regulation applying the CEMAC biometric passport decree.

Reform is under consideration rather than enacted: an interministerial council reviewed reform of the 1957 law in November 2025, and a draft decree would recode the tax identification number for individuals as well as companies.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A presidential order banned cash collection of taxes and fees, routing public revenue to bank accounts and electronic platforms.
Full recordThe order of September 2025 is the operative instrument: cash collection of taxes, fees and other administrative revenue is prohibited and payment must go to bank accounts or electronic platforms.

It bites on benefits too — social benefits are now paid bank-only, and a 2025 census purged 2,282 untraceable pensioners. The regional layer moved separately: the deposit insurance ceiling rose to 8m CFA for individuals and 15m for firms from 10 December 2025.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability A bill creating an electronic public procurement information system went to single-reading study in November 2025.
Full recordNo statute enables data exchange across Equatoguinean agencies. The electronic laws of 2015-2017 address protection, retention and signature, not exchange.

The nearest thing to an enabling instrument is sectoral: the public sector contracts bill creating an electronic procurement information system, opened for single-reading study in November 2025. The business single window remains the only decree that folds several registers into one process.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations A draft artificial intelligence law is being written, setting six guiding principles and proposing an autonomous national authority for the technology and for data.
Full recordA drafting session in August 2025 addressed splitting the proposed authority's functions, and two national institutions are named as prospective training and certification centres under the draft; no enactment, text or adoption date is published (draft law).

The proposed authority would supervise both artificial intelligence and data, in a country whose existing data-protection statute has had no supervisory body for a decade.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation The 2015-2017 laws supply electronic signature and access rules; the state is instead building platforms.
Full recordThe legal base for electronic dealing is the signature and electronic access laws inside the 2015-2017 package. No electronic commerce act has followed.

Activity has run ahead of the statute: national marketplace and e-commerce platforms sit on the ledger, and in April 2026 a foreign platform pitched an e-commerce hub while the Vice-President formed a task force on paying digital creators. Neither has a consumer-protection rule behind it.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The census legal basis was set by Decree 25/2025 and Resolution 006/2025; no general statistics act is held.
Full recordThe base holds no general statistics statute for Equatorial Guinea. What it holds is instrument-by-instrument authority: the fifth population census and first agricultural census rest on a 2025 decree and resolution, with a scientific committee starting work.

The institute's own governance moved in July 2026, when its board adopted a revised staff statute and approved action plan spending above five million.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The Senate passed the country's first transparency and access-to-information bill unanimously in May 2026.
Full recordThe Prime Minister opened the debate before the Chamber of Deputies in February 2026 on transparency, access to information and public finance.

The Senate passed the transparency and finance-access bill unanimously in May. Nothing yet records its promulgation, an implementing regulation, or a request procedure a citizen could use.

Data protection Data protection authority The supervisory body the statute provides for was never created: article 15 leaves it to a decree the government has not issued.
Full recordThe article leaves both the supervisory organ and the registry it would run to that decree, and a continental country register records no supervisory authority publicly identified (the law).

So a statute with a sanctions regime running to CFA 15,000,000 (the law) has had no body able to apply it for ten years, which is the clearest instance on this ledger of a law without an institution.
 No change
Data protection National data protection readiness The Chamber of Deputies approved ratifying the regional data protection convention in March 2026; no supervisory authority operates.
Full recordThe domestic law dates from 2016 and its supervisory authority has never been recorded operating. The 2015-2017 package carries the protection and retention rules it would enforce.

The external commitment moved first: an interministerial council reviewed a proposal to authorise the convention in November 2025 and the Chamber approved ratification in March 2026, four months before the signing ceremony. Ratifying a convention does not constitute a regulator.

Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration The country took part in a regional data governance workshop in June 2026 applying an international toolkit, with no national framework resulting.
Full recordIt was the second such workshop in the Lusophone Africa process (workshop).

No commitment, framework or follow-up is recorded from it, and it is the only regional policy process the base holds outside the continental convention and a bilateral accord with one neighbour.

The central bank adopted a regional financial inclusion strategy for 2025-2029 covering the six member states, validated by the monetary union's ministerial committee on 21 December 2023 and published in February 2025 (strategy); the base holds no member-state implementation plan, target or outturn under it.

Full recordNo instrument of ratification is on file or on the continental register (signature).

The Chamber of Deputies' unanimous vote of 25 March 2026 was a domestic authorisation to ratify, taken four months before the country became a signatory, so the two acts ran in the wrong order and the second has not followed (authorisation).

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers A border-screening connection runs at ten sites including three airports, with no traffic figure and no successor project.

It is the only cross-border data exchange the base holds, and no data-protection arrangement, retention rule or oversight applies to it on the record, in a country whose own supervisory body was never constituted.

Full recordThe World Bank diagnostic records no interoperability framework, and the body that would issue one set out a seven-stage plan in March 2025 and remains pre-launch.

The commitment that exists is declaratory: the Malabo declaration of May 2026 commits to interoperable standards for green digital infrastructure, agreed at a symposium the country hosted.

Standards National quality standards A service-quality regulation of 2018 is confirmed in force through the regulator's own index, with only its title and date held.
Full recordThe index also lists an inspection and sanctions regulation of 2020 and a broadband-market update of February 2026, neither captured separately (regulatory index).

So the country has published quality-of-service rules and the base cannot read them; no measurement, inspection outcome or sanction under any of the three is held.

Standards Adoption of international standards The backup data centre works are tendered to an international facility standard, and the passport follows the regional chip specification.
Full recordTwo adoptions are documented. The biometric passport regulation sets chip, machine-readable zone and issuance-control rules to the regional specification, and the technical oversight tender for the backup data centre names an international facility standard.

The statistical system is adopting one too: the technical committee began rolling out the 2008 national accounts standard and updating the base year in March 2026.

Full recordThe decree adopting the national sustainable development strategy creates an observatory as the consultation channel and defers its composition. Five years on, nothing in the base records it constituted or meeting.

Where non-state voices do reach policy it is through single events: a second national artificial intelligence forum in May 2026 brought ministry, university and international researchers together on artificial intelligence policy.

Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy A reform of the 1997 press law was presented in August 2026, regulating digital media and cable television for the first time.
Full recordIt was referred to a parliamentary committee alongside a separate copyright and related rights bill implementing the country's convention obligations; neither bill text is held and no adoption date is published (presentation, scope).

A commission adopted 11 articles of the draft on 18 August 2026, leaving four outstanding, on the principles of the profession, the qualification required to practise, student interns, and professional secrecy; no plenary vote, text or commencement date is published (commission stage).

Connectivity Mobile penetration A semestral plan targets 85 per cent second- and fourth-generation coverage through 34 new towers in the islands and the interior.
Full recordThe plan was presented to the Prime Minister in July 2026 and also names a zero-roaming arrangement with four neighbours and a national digital postcode system; the plan document itself is not held (connectivity plan).

 Advanced
Connectivity Internet usage No national internet-use series exists; the statistical yearbook publishes administrative counts and no penetration figure.
Full recordThe eighth national yearbook carries 19,586 civil servants, 10,468 online visas approved and public debt at 33.3 per cent of output — administrative counts, not connectivity.

The population it would be measured against is itself estimated: the 2025 compendium still projects population off the 2015 census. The fifth population census now has its legal basis and has not been taken.

Connectivity Mobile affordability The state operator raised prepaid data volumes 50 per cent at held prices in August 2026, its top tier reaching 24GB for 15,000 CFA.
Full recordPrice last moved by agreement rather than regulation: the government and operators halved internet tariffs from 1 September 2022.

In the window the movement was volume: a summer promotion raising data 50 per cent across all prepaid packages at held prices, the top tier at 24GB for 15,000 CFA. Upstream, the regulator updated the primary and secondary broadband capacity market regime by ministerial order in February 2026. No affordability ratio against income is published.

Connectivity National fibre backbone The four-ring backbone may be joined by the electricity utility's 2,000km grid fibre network.
Full recordThe minister's own account puts the backbone at four rings and an international gateway with over US$30m invested.

A second national network exists and is unused for telecommunications: the electricity utility is studying whether to lease its 2,000km grid fibre network to operators, on a vendor study. International capacity may also grow — a study backs a Medusa cable landfall at Bome near Bata at EUR 20-60m, with service targeted for 2029-2030.
 Advanced
Connectivity International internet bandwidth The country lands three submarine systems, has two more under study or agreement, and since November 2025 exchanges traffic overland with Gabon.
Full recordOne regional system was inaugurated in 2018 on a 400 km route between the two main cities and a neighbour, alongside an earlier landing; no later capacity or utilisation figure is held (regional cables).

The state infrastructure operator is landing party for the continental system and hosted its maintenance committee in May 2026, with capacity unchanged on the record (landing party).

A Mediterranean-anchored system is under study at a landing in one of two towns, put at EUR 20-60m and 2029 to 2030 with an alternative route also named, the existing cable reported to degrade quality which is the stated reason for diversifying (study, routes).

A subsea fibre agreement with Nigeria was signed in February 2026 bundling a data centre, with no route, capacity or delivery date published (agreement, signature).

2025-11-10 - the two countries' national backbones were interconnected and inaugurated at Ebibeyin, exchanging traffic on the Bitam-Ebibeyin and Libreville-Bata routes, the first terrestrial international route on this ledger; no traffic or capacity figure is published.

Full recordThe continental infrastructure registry's entry is the only record the base holds: construction stage in 2017, no launch, no operator, no traffic figure.

Connectivity Satellite broadband licensing and availability The satellite service is now available in the country, after a July 2026 authorisation that covered only foreign companies' offshore operations.
Full recordThe July authorisation covered North American companies' offshore operations inside jurisdictional waters and was signed at the presidential palace on 28 July 2026, with extension to individual subscribers deferred on stated security grounds (authorisation).

On 29 August 2026 the operator confirmed the service is available in the country, following the government's opening of negotiations on national digital transformation in March 2026 (availability).

The confirmation is the operator's own, made on its and its owner's social-media accounts. Type-approval and competences were unresolved with the regulator a month earlier (implementation), against an entry signalled in February 2025 that was not then authorised (entry).

So a service that was lawful for foreign companies at sea and unlawful for citizens on land is now stated to be generally available, and the licence, tariff and subscriber figures that would show on what terms are not held.

Full recordThe absence was named by the World Bank diagnostic and no framework, standard or governing body has been published since (diagnostic, priority actions).

At the agency's directorship handover on 7 August 2026, its outgoing Inspector General stated the consequence directly: limited sharing by line ministries is what stops it monitoring delivery against the national development strategy (handover).

Data Exchange Use of digital id in other systems A mobile wallet onboards customers on a SIM and a copy of the national identity document, the only recorded downstream use.
Full recordThe identity document is used for enrolment rather than verification: a fuel retailer and a wallet provider onboard customers with just a SIM and a copy of the national identity document.

The platform arrived in stages — launched at Baney in March 2025 with a ministry data centre and more than 100 digital agents trained, pilot completed in two health districts in November opening a 2026 national rollout. The portal launched in July 2026 is the first citizen-facing layer over it, carrying enrolment, results and a graduate directory. Whether it draws on the same records as the yearbooks, or on a register of its own, is not stated.

The law that might have required better passed at second reading in March 2026 specifying no registry and no management information system. What has changed is payment channel rather than data: benefits are now paid bank-only and a 2025 census purged 2,282 untraceable pensioners.

Full recordThe credential itself is regional and well specified: chip, machine-readable zone and issuance-control rules under the CEMAC passport regulation, with 7,706 issued through missions abroad between 2022 and 2026.

The service around it is less so. The application portal went down for maintenance and was restored on a new host in July 2026, and only six overseas posts capture data with more pending.

The executive has invited parents to register children and adults over 18 to obtain the document (invitation).

The agency's own reach is growing — identity and passport services extended to two further mainland towns in June 2026 — while the underlying registry law dates from 1957 and reform is only under review.
