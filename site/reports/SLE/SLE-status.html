This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In August 2026 the Bank of Sierra Leone, the communications regulator, operators, cybersecurity bodies and civil society held a two-day workshop on mobile-money fraud. It announced public awareness and better inter-agency working, and no new legal instrument. A multi-agency strategy announced on 5 August 2026 set out awareness, institutional strengthening and better investigation. It proposed no liability rule, refund duty or new operator obligation.

The National Innovation and Digital Strategy 2019-2029, published by the Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation, remains the overarching document. It carries interoperability standards, e-government and data exchange as pillars, with no separate strategy for each. It commits that cognitive, mental, literacy or physical ability should not exclude anyone from digital services. Delivery sits with that ministry and the Directorate of Science, Technology and Innovation, anchored in the Medium-Term National Development Plan and the "Big Five Game Changers".

The central bank's National Strategy for Financial Inclusion 2022-2026 sets targets for women, youth, rural communities and smaller enterprises. Cabinet approved the country's first National Data Protection Policy in April 2026. It authorised repeal of the Right to Access Information Commission Act. A first national artificial-intelligence strategy was being developed with the World Bank in May 2026, with none in force. In August 2026 the procurement authority's chief executive called public procurement "a key foundation for effective e-governance". In March 2026 the communications regulator pressed for fifteen- to twenty-year spectrum licences and staggered spectrum fees as the terms for rural expansion.

Sierra Leone scored 53.0 out of 100 for overall governance in 2023, 23rd of 54, after a rise of 1.1 points over the most recent five years.

Legislation and regulation

Every enforcement power the National Communications Authority Act 2022 gives the regulator opens with the words "a licensee". Those powers are compliance orders, sanctions, licence revocation, consumer obligations and data-protection duties. Sierra Leone's principal communications statute therefore reaches only the people it licenses. The Act defines "intermediary service provider" in its opening section in terms that fit a messaging platform. It never uses the term again across its thirty-eight Parts, so the definition carries no duty and no power.

The National Civil Registration Act 2016 makes registration compulsory for citizens and residents alike. It provides the legal basis for national identification numbers and multipurpose identity cards. A review of the Act is expected to be tabled in Parliament at the end of 2026. A Cabinet directive has since made the number a condition of access to public and financial services, enforced more firmly from January 2025. The National Payment Systems Act 2022 lets the central bank organise, own and operate payment and settlement systems, not merely regulate them. It also creates a National Payment System Committee seating mobile money operators, community banks and the Fintech Association. The Cyber Security and Crime Act 2021 supplies the offences an account-takeover engages: illegal access, data interference, computer-related forgery and identity fraud.

The Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill 2025 would create a single independent authority for both data protection and access to information. The country does not have such a body. Its 2026 text was tabled in Parliament by the information minister and completed first reading in November 2026. Mobile money is supervised by the communications regulator "together with the Bank of Sierra Leone", with no lead named and no memorandum between them. Subscribers regulations tying every SIM to its holder's identification number were announced in July 2026 and take effect after a six-month transition. Operators are fined 25,000 new leones for every unregistered or pre-activated card. A Digital Government Bill was being drafted with the Attorney-General's office in September 2026. A public-private dialogue was convened on 27 and 28 July 2026 to operationalise the Electronic Transactions Act 2019.

Data protection

Sierra Leone has no economywide data-protection statute. The telecommunications licensing framework stands in its place. Under those sectoral rules consent is required before personal data may be processed. Subscriber data must be held in a central electronic database inside the country, a localisation mandate carried in licence conditions, not in a privacy law. Personal data may not be transferred out of the country without the telecoms regulator's approval. Those duties bind licensees; they do not reach a controller who holds no licence.

The combined Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill completed its final national validation in November 2025. Cabinet approved a first National Data Protection Policy in April 2026 and left the Bill with the Attorney-General's office for tabling in Parliament. No data protection or localisation law was yet in force. The Bill completed first reading in Parliament in November 2026. Until it passes there is no supervisory authority to complain to. The World Bank identifies the missing framework as undermining safe use of the national identity system (December 2025).

No law requires a payment service provider to notify users or a regulator of a data breach within a set period. The National Payment Systems Act 2022 imposes general consumer disclosure duties and no breach-notification duty. Sharing of identity data rests on the disclosure provisions of the National Civil Registration Act 2016 plus bilateral memoranda. The memoranda are with the road safety authority, and with banks for electronic know-your-customer checks, rather than under any comprehensive national framework. The registration authority itself called in August 2026 for governance standards over how registration data moves between institutions. It asked for integration to be separated from unrestricted access.

The state reaches subscriber records at volume: Orange Sierra Leone answered more than 18,000 police requests for know-your-customer data to support criminal investigations (August 2026). Customers carry the corresponding risk on their own. The loss from an authorised push payment falls on them, and there are no statutory SIM-swap safeguards. The only practical route to recovery is a discretionary freeze by the operator.

Regional collaboration

A free-roaming memorandum with Liberia and The Gambia took effect between Liberia and Sierra Leone on 2 May 2025, and for The Gambia from 1 July 2025. It lets subscribers use a home SIM at local rates under the ECOWAS roaming framework. The communications regulator signed a further free-roaming agreement with Guinea in November 2025, under Smart Africa's single digital market programme. Guinea's and Sierra Leone's national fibre networks were interconnected at Pamelap in June 2025. In June 2026 a four-country consortium agreed to commercialise spare fibre on the TRANSCO CLSG power grid. In March 2026 the World Bank approved the second West Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, covering Sierra Leone with Benin and Liberia.

Sierra Leone's biometric card is ECOWAS-compliant and serves as a regional travel document (2024), with no cross-border authentication of the identity behind it. In March 2026 the regional identification programme's supervisory committee validated a cross-border interoperability strategy and 2026 work plan for ECOWAS states. It commits the region to mutual recognition of national ID credentials and harmonised technical specifications. The strategy had been validated, not put into operation. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger cut ties with ECOWAS and the programme in 2024, and launched an Alliance of Sahel States biometric passport in January 2025.

In June 2026 nine Sierra Leonean banks were live send-and-receive participants in the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, and no mobile money operator was among them. The ECOWAS Community budget for 2026-2028 carries no separate digital or ICT appropriation. Its only ICT-named programme line shows nil actual spending from 2024 through the 2028 estimates.

Standards

In 2025 the Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation ran a Digital Service Compliance review process for government digital solutions. In November 2026 the ministry began training agency ICT officers under a Website Standardisation Framework. It sets common design, branding and technical requirements for official websites, which the ministry builds and hosts on shared gov.sl infrastructure. A 2025 World Bank terms of reference records the core public financial management systems running standalone, with no government service bus or agreed protocols between them. An Interoperability Roadmap completed under the Ministry of Finance's reform programme sets up an X-Road pilot (2025).

In February 2026 the national identity system was being migrated onto the open-source MOSIP platform. In August 2026 the ministry named that platform as the specification for the wider digital public infrastructure programme. The credentials envisaged with Bhutan's national digital identity body are W3C-compliant verifiable credentials. The audit office's new management system localises the regional AFROSAI-E audit platform, and was in the last stage of user acceptance testing in August 2026. It is expected to standardise audit practice across planning, risk assessment, quality assurance and reporting. The planned national mortality system is designed to adopt the World Health Organization's ICD-11 cause-of-death classification (August 2026).

Under the National Payment Systems Act 2022, rules for the national payment switch are issued as central bank directives. They must appear in the Gazette or on the Bank's website at least fifteen days before taking effect. No consolidated public rulebook has been found.

In August 2026 the communications regulator announced network drive tests and fines for network-quality lapses. On 28 October 2026 it took delivery of spectrum-monitoring, spectrum-management and quality-of-service measurement systems, funded by the World Bank. Until the National Monitoring and Evaluation Agency was created, no dedicated body monitored the hundred or more public projects launched each year. The agency is expanding a platform giving authorised staff real-time metrics on those projects.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In 2023 the Ibrahim Index ranked Sierra Leone second of 54 African states for deliberative and participatory governance, at 81.1 out of 100. The score rose 23.9 points over 2014-2023. The same index puts digital freedom among the country's ten most deteriorated measures, down 7.9 points to 79.8 in 2023.

Freedom of expression and belief scored 89.2 out of 100 in 2023, 6th of 54 and essentially unchanged over the decade. The public's own sense of freedom of speech scored 63.3 in 2023, 20th of 54. Media freedom scored 65.6 in 2023, 12th of 54, after slipping 2.6 points.

On 5 August 2026 the communications ministry convened a national dialogue on digital identity and digital public infrastructure, the first of a series. An eighteen-month engagement with the Digital Impact Alliance brought more than 220 stakeholders, civil society among them, into shaping the Digital Economy Programme and the National Data Strategy. The artificial-intelligence strategy process ran multi-stakeholder consultations in 2026 meant to reflect local languages and cultural contexts. The fifth Open Government Partnership action plan, running to June 2028, commits Parliament and civil society to build a portal for public comment on draft legislation. The portal had not been built when the plan was reviewed in February 2025.

On 4 August 2026 Parliament took up the mobile-money fraud wave. The Speaker demanded a remedial plan from the Communications Minister, and the Opposition and Majority Leaders called for oversight hearings and refunds for victims. The House told its Communications Committee to report within a month, and adopted no liability rule at the sitting. Days earlier a former minister argued in public that the problem was unenforced law, not absent law. In August 2026 the head of the civil registration authority warned against parallel identity systems being built alongside its own.

Disclosure and accessibility of public records both scored poorly in 2023, and had barely moved over the decade.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2026 estimates vote NLe401.7m for the communications regulator's operations, against NLe22.0m for the civil registration authority's identity-system project.

The state's own money is 88.6% of the digital lines of the 2026 estimates, NLe637.3m of NLe719.0m, against 94.7% in 2024.

In 2026 a foreign loan for school connectivity carries NLe80.6m, and a World Bank grant to the Digital Transformation Project NLe1.06m. The school-connectivity loan carried NLe30.0m in 2024. The Digital Transformation Project, US$50m from the World Bank, closes on 30 September 2027 and had disbursed 21.24% by August 2025. The 2025 estimates carried a NLe0.5m government counterpart for it. In 2025 the regulator's line paid out NLe425.0m, against NLe323.0m voted and NLe401.0m after the mid-year revision. The identity-system project paid out NLe18.6m of NLe20.0m voted in 2025.

Under 2019 regulations, a universal access and service levy of 0.75% of operators' gross income, with parliamentary appropriations and grants, finances the Universal Access Development Fund. In 2024, 10 of 16 internet service providers paid no levy, and the auditor found no penalty imposed on them. The regulator's revenue rose 46% in 2024 on surcharges under the Finance Act 2024. It paid NLe74.7m, 12% of that revenue, into the Consolidated Revenue Fund in 2024.

On 5 May 2026 Africell paid Sierratel a US$2m upfront sum under the partnership relaunching it as a mobile virtual network operator. The money went to settle long-standing staff liabilities.

MoUs and other agreements

In April 2026 the government appointed Africell as investment partner to relaunch the loss-making Sierratel as a mobile virtual network operator. The model is revenue-share, avoiding heavy capital spending after earlier failed privatisation attempts. Its target market is students and young professionals. No launch date, contract term or revenue split has been found.

A free-roaming memorandum with Liberia and The Gambia under the ECOWAS roaming framework took effect between Liberia and Sierra Leone on 2 May 2025. It extended to The Gambia by 1 July 2025, letting subscribers use their home SIM at local rates. On 17 November 2025 the National Communications Authority signed a free-roaming agreement with Guinea at Transform Africa 2025. It sits under Smart Africa's Single Digital Market programme, with no commencement date or tariff detail attached.

In June 2026 the Directorate of Science, Technology and Innovation and Catholic Relief Services signed a memorandum of understanding on geospatial technology. It sets out no value, duration or funding commitment. On 14 August 2026 the cross-border payments firm Cauridor and BnB announced a partnership to widen digital payments, mobile money and cross-border transfers. It builds on Cauridor's existing links with Orange and MTN, and names no amount, product or delivery date.

New investments

On 11 March 2026 the World Bank approved the USD 137 million second West Africa Regional Digital Integration Project. It covers broadband and digital services in Sierra Leone, Benin and Liberia, with no national share set out.

In July 2022 the World Bank lent USD 50 million on concessional terms for the Digital Transformation Project, running to 2027. In August 2019 China Eximbank lent RMB 207 million on concessional terms for 721.5 km of national fibre backbone.

MIGA's USD 97.2 million political risk guarantee of 2018 runs to 2033 over SONATEL's 50% stake in Orange Sierra Leone. A USD 13.5 million MIGA guarantee of 2021 covers solar-hybrid power at around 600 Orange tower sites.

UNICEF and the Islamic Development Bank are connecting 523 schools under Giga Phase 2, the bank taking 403 and UNICEF 120. An African Development Bank facility for digital interoperability across the West African Monetary Zone does not size Sierra Leone's part.

Forty-seven digital-sector commitments are on record for Sierra Leone as at November 2026, starting from 2015 with windows out to 2033 and worth about USD 318 million in all. By instrument (November 2026), 39 grants are worth about USD 49 million, three concessional loans about USD 105 million and two MIGA guarantees cover USD 112 million of private investment. An operator's self-funded network upgrade accounts for USD 50 million, its self-funded Bo data centre carries no dollar amount, and one USD 2 million payment has no instrument recorded (November 2026). Over a third of the November 2026 total is guarantee cover rather than money flowing in, and two of the 47 are approved or in pipeline, not yet under way. Connectivity takes much the largest share as at November 2026, about USD 251 million across eight commitments, with land registries next at about USD 21 million and every other subsector far behind. MIGA is the largest source by value as at November 2026, ahead of the World Bank at about USD 86 million across three operations.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

By April 2026 Sierra Leone's fibre backbone reached 14 of the country's 16 districts. Internet use stood at 25.1% of the population in 2024. Mobile communications rose 41.0 points over 2014-2023 to 79.4 out of 100, the most improved of the 96 Ibrahim Index indicators for Sierra Leone. Access to internet and computers scored 24.8 out of 100 in 2023, among the country's ten worst-scoring measures.

The National Broadband Strategy 2023-2028 places Sierra Leone 14th of 16 West African states on the cost of a gigabyte of mobile data. In April 2026 the regulator put Africell's network at just over 92% of the population for 2G, 77.48% for 3G and 61.72% for 4G.

In July 2025 a communications ministry delegation assessed a second submarine cable landing routed via Guinea, Sierra Leone having only one. In June 2025 Guinea's SOGEB and Sierra Leone's Leonecom interconnected their national fibre networks at the Pamelap border crossing. The link gives cheaper regional transit and route redundancy. In June 2026 the country joined Liberia, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire in a consortium to commercialise spare fibre on the TRANSCO CLSG power grid. Michcom-IX, a carrier-neutral non-profit exchange, interconnects networks in Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone. A feasibility study for a national internet exchange point was completed in August 2025, with the exchange still at planning and procurement.

In 2025 the World Bank-financed Digital Transformation Project was extending broadband to schools, hospitals, clinics and local government offices in secondary cities.

Data Storage

In April 2026 the National Data Centre was commissioned inside the National Communications Authority's new Freetown headquarters. The ten-storey building, begun in 2015, also houses the communications ministry and the regulator's network operations. A separate government data centre at the Ministry of Finance supports the e-Government Procurement system, with its disaster-recovery arrangements still being finalised.

Orange Sierra Leone has run a data centre in Freetown with capacity for 154 racks since 2018. On 29 November 2025 it opened a second, EUR 23 million facility at Bo. The Bo site replicates the Freetown one as a disaster-recovery hub for a capital exposed to landslide and recurrent flooding. Afcom sells rack and cage space with redundant power and cooling in Freetown, alongside smaller providers including Zoodlabs and Leonecom. There is no hyperscale cloud region in Sierra Leone; the nearest is Google's Johannesburg region, opened in 2024.

At the Bo opening in November 2025 Sonatel's chief executive called the centre "a major step forward for data sovereignty". Subscriber data must be held in a central database inside Sierra Leone under the telecoms licensing framework, not a privacy law. The Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill completed first reading in Parliament in November 2026.

Energy

Access to energy gained 4.8 points on the Ibrahim Index over 2014-2023, to 12.7 out of 100 and 47th of 54 African states. It is one of Sierra Leone's three lowest-scoring measures on the index. Over the same decade the country's mobile score climbed faster than any other indicator the index tracks. Just over a quarter of the population had electricity in 2020. 56.3% of urban residents had electricity in 2023 against 19.0% of rural ones. The gap was 37.3 percentage points. Supply to those who have it is severely unreliable. Renewable sources accounted for 75.28% of electricity generation in 2021. The national electrification plan runs on decentralised supply, mini-grids and stand-alone solar systems, backed by dedicated programmes and duty exemptions. The World Bank is financing solar home systems for 500 schools and 200 health facilities. The aim is to give them the electricity digital services need. Solar-hybrid power is being installed at around 600 Orange Sierra Leone tower sites. It cuts their diesel use by three-quarters.

The distribution utility recorded 18,231 supply interruptions in 2022, more each year since 2019. The police give inadequate and unreliable electricity, limited connectivity and a shortage of computers and printers as the barriers to digitising case records. These barriers bear hardest on rural stations. In rural clinics the same combination of unreliable power and connectivity keeps paper the primary medium for patient records. The RESPITE solar and storage plant was commissioned in July 2026 and is fully operational.

Technical Capacity

Public administration scored 53.9 out of 100 in the 2023 Ibrahim Index, 27th of 54 African states. It rose 1.6 points over 2014-2023, one of the two smallest gains among the index's sixteen sub-categories. Effective administration reached 60.3 in 2023, 19th of 54 and up 8.3 points.

In February 2026 identity officials said any replacement system had to match the legacy system's service from the first day, because institutions already rely on the register. The National Civil Registration Authority depends on external partners for the identity system's core components. The India-based MOSIP foundation supplies the open-source platform, and a local firm produces the biometric cards under a public-private partnership. The agency running the national project monitoring platform acknowledges continuing problems with data entry and reporting. Tertiary ICT and engineering capacity rests on a small number of public institutions, principally the University of Sierra Leone and Njala University.

Cybersecurity

The communications ministry confirmed that 44 website addresses were hit on 25 September 2026 by unauthorised access to the system that administers the national internet domain. They were restored the same day. The road safety authority disclosed, in a notice dated 2 October 2026, an unauthorised intrusion into the systems used to produce vehicle licences. Five people were arrested.

In a ministerial statement in August 2026 the government named the linking of SIM cards to national identification numbers as its main enforcement lever against mobile-money fraud. It conceded that not all subscribers have yet linked a SIM to a verified number. The regulator later set 1 November 2026 as the deadline for that linkage.

Sierra Leone sits in the "establishing" tier of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024. The tier is the band for overall scores of at least 55 out of 100. A National Cybersecurity Coordination Centre works alongside the communications ministry and the science and technology directorate. Together they work on whole-of-government cyber and emerging-technology capacity. Senior officials across ministries, departments and agencies have been put through cybersecurity and emerging-technology training. The ministry and ECOWAS launched a national incident response team and digital forensics laboratory in Freetown in January 2025. An Estonian index scored fighting cybercrime 14 of 16 and incident response 6 of 14 in May 2025. It scored protection of personal data and crisis management at zero. The National Cybersecurity Strategy ran from 2021 to 2025, and no successor has been found.

The ECOWAS directive on cyber and ICT confidence-building measures was adopted alongside the bloc's Digital Sector Development Strategy in October 2024. It urges member states to implement it "without delay upon its publication". The duty begins when a text appears, not when ministers adopted it.

President Bio pressed telecommunications providers on their cybersecurity responsibilities at the Bo opening in November 2025. Orange launched a scam-reporting service on its own network in August 2026. In October 2026 security forces and the regulator raided a suspected international cybercrime network, arresting 18 people and seizing 1,206 phones and 339 SIM cards.

DPI

Data Exchange

In June 2026 the Ministry of Finance recorded an Interoperability Roadmap as developed, with a Government Service Bus to be piloted on X-Road. The roadmap was contracted to PCL International in April 2025 for completion by the end of September.

A 2025 World Bank terms of reference records no government service bus with agreed protocols across the public financial management systems. IFMIS, the Integrated Tax Administration System, ASYCUDA and the central bank's T24 core banking system run as standalone systems. A Japanese Quality Infrastructure Investment grant provided on 26 April 2025 funds a prototype bus. The systems named for connection are civil registration, the tax administration system, LoMEMIS, the Municipal Property Tax System covering 22 councils, and T24. The revenue authority has linked its tax system to ASYCUDA World, the commercial banks and, not directly, the civil registration system. It waits on the National Investment Board to build the system that would join business registration to it.

In July 2026 the Anti-Corruption Commission found over 300 billion leones lost to backend deletion of paid customs declarations from ASYCUDA. Dormant bank-teller profiles had validated transactions after their holders left. The commission found reasonable grounds to suspect a coordinated scheme. In October 2026 the revenue authority began consultations at the Jendema border crossing ahead of launching ASYCUDA World there.

In 2024 the National Civil Registration Authority's verification platform let banks, operators, social security, immigration, health and education bodies authenticate against the national database. They connect by API or web portal. A May 2024 memorandum for a Data Exchange Layer sharing identity data with a line ministry has not become operational. The land register exchanges data with no other government system. Data sharing between the business register, the revenue authority, social security and municipal licensing is written into a 2026 bill, with no system built. In August 2026 the registration authority pressed for governance standards over sharing between institutions, separating system integration from unrestricted access.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Fewer than half a million eID cards had been issued in Sierra Leone as of 2024, against a register holding the great majority of the population. The World Bank attributes the gap to a card fee of roughly six to seven US dollars.

Registration itself is free and compulsory for citizens and residents alike under the National Civil Registration Act 2016, and is open from birth. The card is not free: a standard card for a national has cost NLe165 since 23 February 2026, up from NLe145, and ECOWAS and non-national cards cost more.

A Cabinet Directive ratified by Parliament in March 2022 makes the number a condition of financial services, education, healthcare, SIM registration, immigration and employment. Enforcement was strengthened from January 2025, including identity checks at checkpoints. The number has been mandatory for passports and work and residence permits since June 2022. It has been mandatory for driving licences and vehicle registration since the Road Safety Authority gained platform access in October 2023. The card is ECOWAS-compliant and serves as a regional travel document, though cross-border authentication is not established. In April 2022 the registration authority offered commercial banks real-time eKYC and credit-reference checks against the number, at USD 10,000 a bank a month.

The register is being migrated onto the open-source MOSIP platform, begun with a pilot in early 2024. That pilot is complete and full implementation is unfunded (August 2026). The authority's head has warned against parallel identity systems being built alongside its own.

Civil registration is the country's strongest public administration measure in the Ibrahim Index, seventh of 54 African states in 2023 and unmoved since 2014. 90.4% of children under five were registered in the 2019 Demographic and Health Survey. The authority's first vital statistics report put birth-registration completeness at 52.1% in 2022. The National Public Health Agency engaged suppliers on 7 August 2026 to build a national digital mortality registration and certification system. Issuance remains concentrated at District Headquarters. Public perception of how easy it is to obtain an identity document fell 23.0 points to 27.2 out of 100 by 2023.

Digital Payments and Fintech

The Instant Payment Service launched in February 2025 with seven banks and two mobile money providers connected. It settles across the National Payment Switch, launched on 29 April 2023 and owned and operated by the Bank of Sierra Leone. Since 1 April 2025 its directives have required every bank and mobile money operator to route domestic instant, card and point-of-sale transactions through it.

As of July 2026 no law allocated the loss from authorised push payment fraud, so it fell on the customer. No statutory mechanism recalls or reverses a fraudulently induced transfer.

Around 30% of adults used mobile money in 2019, with 2.2 million active accounts after a 2020 clean-up. The central bank's National Strategy for Financial Inclusion 2022-2026 counted 6.5 million registered mobile money accounts in December 2021. It makes digital financial services a priority area.

In 2022 the revenue authority launched a mobile payment application for non-tax revenue. Income and vehicle tax can be filed and paid by mobile money and wallet. In December 2025 the World Bank reported the flagship social assistance programme shifting its transfers to digital payment. An emergency cash transfer reached 35,000 households by mobile phone, a national first.

As of July 2026 supervision of mobile money was shared between the communications regulator and the central bank, with no lead named. The central bank's consumer protection guidelines, drafted with UNCDF in 2020, still awaited public consultation in March 2021. No issued version has been found. No mandatory fraud data-sharing arrangement links operators, regulators and police, and there are no statutory SIM-swap safeguards. A victim's only route is a discretionary operator freeze. Orange had logged about 390 mobile-money fraud complaints by early August 2026, and opened a Scam Alert reporting channel on 18 August 2026.

Registries

In March 2022 Sierra Leone had no registered land ownership rights, against a target of 500,000 by 2028. The land record is largely paper, with cadastral maps and deeds held manually by the Registrar General's office and the lands ministry. Digitisation attempts in 2014 and 2016 did not progress, and the land information system under the 2022-2028 project is not operational. In September 2026 Cabinet had approved a national land title certification scheme, with Parliament's approval outstanding. Freetown City Council has used Google Plus Codes as a digital address register since its 2019-2020 property tax reform. A 2024 partnership between the science directorate and Google Maps extends digital addressing beyond the capital, though deployment stays urban.

The 2022 Census of Business Establishments counted 165,514 establishments, 71% of them informal. The Integrated Tax Administration System runs nationwide as the digital taxpayer register, with an online portal. A taxpayer number requires the applicant's National Identification Number. The business register offers online name search but is only partially digitised. In May 2026 Parliament ratified a 15-year concession for an online registration system.

For the June 2023 election the voter roll was a fully digital biometric database drawn from civil-registry data and deployed at 3,300 centres. It held 3,374,258 voters against an estimated voting-age population of 4,690,201. In August 2026 the electoral commission wanted the two registers linked before the 2028 polls, with first-time voters completing civil registration first. Under a May 2025 agreement the national digital farmer registry is being authenticated against the identity number. SPRINT, run by the National Commission for Social Action, supported around 65,000 extremely poor households as of mid-2022.

Sectoral management information systems

In mid-2025 more than 1,500 health facilities filed weekly disease-surveillance reports through the DHIS2-based eIDSR system, at submission rates around 85%. Rural primary healthcare units record the care itself on paper. The Digital Health Roadmap 2024-2026 records electronic medical records only in isolated donor-supported pilots in a few districts. It and the National Health Compact set extension to all facilities as a 2030 target. A National Health Information Hub was launched in January 2026. In December 2025 the World Bank recorded that systems handling sensitive data such as child protection are not linked to the national ID and still rely on paper.

The 2022 Annual School Census report records rural primary schools keeping attendance and enrolment on paper, digitised afterwards by visiting enumerators. In November 2026 the EU committed EUR 2 million over two years, through WFP, to monitoring data for the national school feeding programme. The local education management system LoMEMIS is among the systems awaiting a service bus that does not yet exist. The Integrated Tax Administration System is deployed nationwide as the taxpayer register. Its taxpayer numbers are issued against a National Identification Number.

The judiciary's Justice App, a mobile case-management application, was piloted in the Freetown and Makeni courts from 2017. It was reported to have disposed of 3,670 cases by 2022. In April 2025 the Chief Justice said it still runs beside a virtual court for witnesses abroad, while some courts remain on paper. An e-Justice Blueprint launched in March 2025 frames the digitisation of case management, court processes and legal documentation. A consulting team was scoping the work in May 2025, and court records were exchanged with no other government system. The police's 2025-2029 strategic plan commits to moving from paper to digital records, and to deploying e-Case Management from 2026. A 2025 partnership with the science directorate digitises police human resource and payroll administration, not case recording.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

WanGov, a ministry app, launched in February 2025 to take applications, documents and payments for services. These include business registration, tax filing, permits and driving licences. It was deployed at the Gbalamuya border post with Guinea in October 2025, and no usage figure has been found. The revenue authority runs the Integrated Tax Administration System nationwide, with an online taxpayer portal. Income tax and vehicle tax are filed and paid by mobile money and wallet.

Parliament ratified a 15-year build-operate-transfer concession for an online business registration system in May 2026. The investment board's platform was reported nearly complete in October 2026, with November given as its operational date. In November it was demonstrated to the communications minister, with the identity number being integrated into registration. The Office of the Administrator and Registrar-General offers an online business-name search on a register that is only partially digitised. Passports, work and residence permits, driving licences and vehicle registration are gated on the identity number.

The electronic government procurement platform's supplier registration module was live by January 2026. The platform is hosted in the Ministry of Finance data centre ahead of full rollout. The National Public Procurement Authority sent a delegation to Liberia on 6-7 August 2026 to study that country's electronic government procurement platform.

In October 2026 ministries were shown a platform to hold ministerial performance contracts, targets and quarterly progress reports in one reporting system. It awaits validation, testing and implementation. Government digital solutions go through a Digital Service Compliance review run by the Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation.

Sierra Leone sits in the bottom fifth of countries on the UN E-Government Development Index (2024). Equal access to public services has fallen since 2014 and is among the country's ten most deteriorated Ibrahim Index measures. The build is externally financed, through the World Bank's Digital Transformation Project and the regional WARDIP2, approved in March 2026. A National Data Centre was commissioned at the communications regulator's headquarters in April 2026.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

A 2025 World Bank terms of reference names LoMEMIS and the Municipal Property Tax System, covering 22 local councils, among the systems to connect to a planned Government Service Bus. In June 2026 the bus was still to be piloted. Freetown City Council has run a digital address register on Google Plus Codes since its 2019-2020 property tax reform. It identifies buildings that have no conventional street address. In 2024 the science directorate agreed a partnership with Google Maps to digitise addresses more widely, though deployment stays in urban centres. In 2025 the World Bank-financed Digital Transformation Project was extending broadband to local government offices in secondary cities.

Rural representation and participation rose 24.1 points over 2014-2023 to 69.3 out of 100, 24th of 54 African states. It is one of the country's ten most improved measures.

Rural digital data capture

In May 2025 the civil registration authority and the agriculture ministry agreed to authenticate the national farmer registry against the identity number, to strip out duplicate farmers. Rural primary schools keep attendance and enrolment on paper, and enumerators digitise the Annual School Census afterwards on tablets. In 2024 a tablet application for recording attendance reached only a small minority of government primary schools, and its expansion had not been procured. Health facilities have reported disease surveillance electronically since 2019, on devices serviced nationwide in mid-2025. The police's 2025-2029 plan still has the move from paper to digital records ahead of it.

In 2023, 56.3% of urban residents had electricity against 19.0% of rural ones. In 2026 the fibre backbone reached all but a couple of districts without reaching most of the people inside them. In April 2026 the government acknowledged that the rural-urban divide in connectivity and the price of data are unresolved. The World Bank-financed Digital Transformation Project is extending broadband to schools, hospitals and clinics in underserved rural areas. Solar home systems are being financed for schools and health facilities.

The rural economy scored 57.4 out of 100 in 2023, 30th of 54 African states, up 2.4 points in a decade. Rural land and water access rose 14.8 points to 58.9, while rural market access fell over the same decade. In August 2026 the civil registration authority said registration has to be decentralised into rural communities as well as digitalised if the register is to be complete.

Technology

AI

In May 2026 Sierra Leone had no national artificial-intelligence strategy in force. In September 2026 its attorney-general told a UNESCO forum that it has no AI-specific statute, and will treat AI systems affecting citizens' rights as high-risk by default. A readiness diagnostic was reported complete by September 2026, and the communications ministry is drafting the first strategy with the World Bank. Its three-day multi-stakeholder consultations aimed to reflect the country's own languages and cultural contexts.

In June 2026 its developers reported a machine-learning tool allocating national stocks of more than 70 essential medicines, scaled nationwide after a five-district pilot. The education ministry's EDITH assistant for officials was built by March 2026, and was being prepared for handover from pilot. Ten to fifteen AI-driven government workflows are to be built with the Kenyan firm Qhala, under a memorandum signed at the 80th UN General Assembly. In March 2026 senior officials from across government took AI foundations and emerging-technology courses. The civil-service digital skills programme launched in July 2026 counts AI and automation among its six areas.

A fintech firm launched a WhatsApp payments assistant in April 2026. Since May 2026 the science directorate has led a nationwide deepfake and misinformation awareness campaign, with no legislative or platform-facing instrument beside it. Its announced next phase, including an AI-powered verification chatbot, has not been built. A national campaign on deepfakes and digital misinformation was launched in Freetown on 16 September 2026, in two phases across all 16 districts. A national AI roadmap for the justice sector was announced after a justice-sector convening, reported on 5 October 2026.

ICT Industry

Business and competition regulations fell 5.3 points to 56.7 out of 100 in the 2023 Ibrahim Index, 21st of 54 African states. Economic diversification rose 21.5 points to 72.7 in 2023, 14th of 54. The business and labour environment overall scored 41.5, 34th of 54. Technology and innovation were added to a ministry that had been communications alone, less than a year old in early 2024 and with no structures to handle them. The 2022 business census found most establishments outside the formal register. Electricity supply fails for a large part of each month.

Innovation ecosystem

Sierra Leone is not among the 133 economies ranked in the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024, because too few of its indicators exist for the country. Between 2022 and 2024 the startup hubs, principally Innovation SL and the Orange Digital Center, facilitated around USD 1.7m in grants and investment. Formal angel investment was minimal in 2025, and most entrepreneurs worked from personal savings. A Sierra Leone angel network was formed at the end of 2025. Nine active technology hubs were counted at the end of 2020.

An open-access 5G standalone network launched in June 2025. A multi-currency digital wallet with a merchant payment stack launched in April 2025. Four submissions to the global digital public goods registry were announced on 24 September 2026. On 12 September 2026 a Startup Act was said to be expected in the first quarter of 2027. In November 2026 a fintech lab at the university innovation pod gave early-stage startups access to the central bank's regulatory sandbox.

Capacity

Literacy

Adult digital and financial literacy in Sierra Leone scores 26 out of 100 (2025). It is the composite from the first national baseline survey of the two together. In September 2025 the Directorate of Science, Technology and Innovation graduated the first cohort of Tech 101, a two-week foundational course at two university digital learning hubs.

Education is the single largest driver of the country's decade of governance improvement, rising 15.4 points over 2014–2023 to 60.6 out of 100 and 15th of 54 African states. Enrolment reached 62.1 and 6th of 54 (2023), after a 23.7-point rise that is among the country's ten most improved measures. Completion scored 48.1 and 21st of 54, and education quality 44.5 and 26th of 54 (2023). Both rose over the decade, and both stand well below the enrolment score.

Since May 2026 the Directorate of Science, Technology and Innovation, MoCTI and the Sierra Leone Digital Transformation Project have run a nationwide deepfake and digital-misinformation awareness campaign. By July 2026 it had reached Freetown, Makeni, Port Loko, Magburaka, Bo, Kenema and Kono. It convened Paramount Chiefs, district officials, security institutions, councils and universities.

World Bank financing is paying for solar home systems in 500 schools and 200 health facilities (December 2025).

Training and skills

In July 2026 Sierra Leone launched a Digital Skills Programme for the civil service. It is run by MoCTI with the public administration ministry, the Civil Service Training College and the Human Resource Management Office. It aims at 1,000 civil servants in year one, 3,000 in the medium term and more than 5,000 in the long term. It sits under the Digital Skills Development and Gig Economy Pipeline (2025–26), and covers six areas, among them AI and automation and cybersecurity. Its first cohort, administrative staff on a three-week digital-literacy course, was certified in September 2026. The next cohort was due to begin on 9 October 2026.

In March 2026 MoCTI, the science directorate, the National Cybersecurity Coordination Centre and the Kenyan firm Qhala trained more than 50 senior leaders from across government. The courses covered digital literacy, the security implications of emerging technology and AI foundations. The effort follows a partnership with Qhala entered in September 2025. In June 2026 a Spatial Data Science for Development workshop trained 60 leaders in GIS, remote sensing, hazard mapping and inter-agency data governance. Over 3–5 August 2026 Audit Service Sierra Leone and AFROSAI-E trained 25 staff on the final configured build of the SierraSeat audit management system.

By January 2025 twelve universities had been connected to the internet via Starlink through the local partner ELCOME SL, under the National Broadband Strategy 2023–2028. Extension to vocational schools was named as the next step. Tertiary ICT and engineering rests on a small public base. UNESCO counted a modest technology hub sector before the pandemic.

Research institutions

In September 2025 the West and Central African research network announced Sierra Leone's connection to its backbone, with five other states. The European Commission co-funds the connection. Knowledge production in computing and engineering rests on a small number of public institutions. They are the University of Sierra Leone, whose Fourah Bay College awards degrees in electrical, electronic, mechanical and civil engineering, and Njala University. Neither the scale of that output nor Sierra Leone's public spending on research and development had been published as at August 2026.

The Center for Information Privacy, Human-Centered Technology and Ethics Research launched in Freetown on 6 October 2026. It is to research the handling of personal data, train on data-subject rights and feed evidence into data-protection law and policy.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services in Sierra Leone has fallen 9.5 points since 2014 to 45.9 out of 100 (2023). That puts it 22nd of 54 African states and among the country's ten most deteriorated measures. Social safety nets score worse still, at 18.5 out of 100 and 45th of 54 (2023). Citizens' own sense of how easy it is to obtain an identity document has deteriorated sharply since 2014, even as enrolment has climbed. Civil registration and ID issuance stay concentrated at district headquarters. Through 2025 parliamentarians, community leaders and registration officers pressed the National Civil Registration Authority to push services down to chiefdom level. They cited the distances rural residents travel to register a birth. The authority now says itself that the register cannot be completed without decentralisation alongside digitalisation (August 2026).

Those without papers still reach the register through chiefs, teachers and justices of the peace, whose affidavits the registration authority accepts. For much of the rural population the mobile wallet is the first formal financial service they have held, not a substitute for a bank account. Non-nationals, refugees and migrants reach payment accounts the same way. The International Organization for Migration signed a partnership with Orange Money in August 2024 to route payments to migrants and displaced people through it. The state's own side is only starting: the flagship social assistance programme is shifting its transfers to digital payment (December 2025). Policy meanwhile commits that cognitive, mental, literacy or physical ability should not exclude anyone from digital services. It makes accessibility a design requirement. One hundred inmates were registered for national identification numbers on 17 September 2026. The electricity distributor's WhatsApp chatbot has taken fault reports nationwide since April 2026, with a ticket number for each. An app for deaf, hard-of-hearing and speech-impaired people was in validation with the social welfare ministry in October 2026. The Persons with Disability Act 2011 requires sign language or subtitles on television newscasts, and in 2024 the government reported its telephone access duty for people with hearing disability not yet achieved.

Digital divides

In April 2026 the government said the rural-urban divide in connectivity and the high cost of data remain unresolved. The 2015 census found 16% of men and 10% of women aged 10 and over with internet access. Sierra Leone was not among the fifteen countries surveyed individually for GSMA's 2025 mobile gender gap report. That report puts the gap across sub-Saharan Africa at 29% in 2024, down from 32% in 2023. No later national figure for the gender gap has been found.

The universal access fund's rural telephony project, launched in five communities in 2024, reports connecting about 47,700 people in 114 localities. The fund lives on a 0.75% levy on mobile operators, and in August 2025 asked Parliament to raise it to 1%.

In December 2025 the World Bank reported that only a small fraction of adults with an identification number hold the card that proves it. Enrolment is free and the physical eID card is not.

The first national baseline survey put adult digital and financial literacy at 26 out of 100 in 2025. Access to internet and computers remained among the country's ten worst-scoring governance measures in 2023. Access to banking is the lowest-scoring of all ninety-six measures in that index. In 2023, 19.0% of rural residents had electricity against 56.3% of urban ones. The constraint on take-up is affordability more than coverage. The AI strategy process treats local languages and cultural context as a design question.

Data

National statistics

The capacity of Sierra Leone's statistical system gained 21.9 points over 2014-2023 to 53.7 out of 100, 22nd of 54 African states. It is the largest improvement on any of the country's public administration measures. On the World Bank's 2023 Statistical Performance Indicators the country sits in the 20-49% band for data sources and data infrastructure. Data use and data products fall in the 70-89% band in 2023, and data services between the two.

By 2023 a population census and a census of business establishments had been run within ten years, but no agricultural census in twenty. The business census found most establishments informal. Over the decade to 2023 there were three or more agricultural and three or more health surveys, against two household surveys and one labour force survey.

In 2024 school heads still completed Annual School Census questionnaires on paper, digitised afterwards by visiting enumerators. The health system's DHIS2-based surveillance carries weekly aggregate reports, not individual records. Birth registration measured high in the 2019 survey round. The civil registration authority's provisional count for 2025 was 306,057 live births against 17,905 deaths registered. In August 2026 the National Public Health Agency was assessing mortality data capability. The draft 2025 Vital Statistics Report was under stakeholder validation on 10 and 11 September 2026.

Census Night is set for 1 December 2026, for the country's first fully digital census. The statistics agency has deployed the technology, with enumeration by tablet set for 2 to 16 December 2026. A mock census testing the digital data-collection system was ending in selected areas on 19 October 2026. In October 2026 the statistics office put the census budget at nearly US$60 million, against an initial estimate of US$7 million.

Open data

In Open Data Watch's 2024 Open Data Inventory, Sierra Leone's strongest categories reach 6.0 out of 10. They are the built environment, gender statistics and health outcomes. Crime and justice statistics score lowest at 2.0, with health facilities data next at 3.0 (2024). Energy, pollution, international trade and communications and digital data carry no score. The central bank's recurring digital financial services market report publishes account, transaction and participant numbers.

In 2023 Sierra Leone scored 31.9 out of 100 for disclosure of public records, 32nd of 54 African states. It scored 35.0 for accessibility of public records, 19th of 54, gaining 0.5 and 4.9 points respectively over 2014-2023.

Under the 2013 access-to-information Act the statistics office keeps a 2024 scheme listing 22 classes of information it releases routinely. Its publications, metadata and microdata are free of charge. The Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill would create a single independent authority for data protection and access to information. It completed first reading in Parliament in November 2026. Central bank directives governing the national switch must appear in the Gazette or on the Bank's website before taking effect. No consolidated public rulebook has been found. The ECOWAS Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024-2029 stands on the record through the release announcing its adoption at Cotonou on 4 October 2024. The release does not set out what the strategy requires of a member state.

Geospatial data

In June 2026 the science directorate and Catholic Relief Services signed a memorandum of understanding on geospatial technology. A Coordination and Visualization Platform for geospatial data was set to launch the following month. A workshop trained government leaders in GIS, remote sensing and hazard mapping for disaster management and water and sanitation planning. No national space or geospatial agency, ground receiving facility, or space or geospatial data policy had been found as at August 2026.

In May 2025 the agriculture ministry launched the country's first weather index insurance, with payouts triggered from satellite-monitored weather. Starlink launched in Sierra Leone in June 2024. In March 2026 the regulator counted it as licensed and two years in operation. In January 2025 the government used it to connect universities under the National Broadband Strategy. By 2026 a firm had been recruited under the Land Administration Project to build the land information and cadastral system. Records digitisation is under way, with no public digital cadastre in operation.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

The Millennium Challenge Corporation's compact, signed in September 2024, pays for a main and back-up distribution dispatch centre and a national dispatch centre with SCADA upgrades. It gives the utilities centralised visibility of the electricity network. No American platform, cloud or network-equipment presence in government systems has been found.

China activities

In August 2019 China Eximbank lent RMB 207 million on concessional terms for Phase 2 of the National Fibre Optic Backbone Infrastructure Project. The 721.5 km network reaches 15 cities across the northern, eastern and southern regions. The Ministry of Finance on-lent the money to SALCAB, the state backbone company that owns the network. Huawei was the engineering, procurement and construction contractor, under a contract signed in April 2019.

EU activities

The European Union committed EUR 5 million over 2020-2025 to the IT and physical infrastructure of civil registration. It aimed to lift birth and death registration through register service centres and the issue of document extracts. The EU committed EUR 1 million over 2022-2025 to a block-farming and digital extension platform in a pineapple supply chain. It places 250 farmers on half-acre plots of a 50-hectare block farm, with targets of 20-30% women and 40-50% youth over 2022-2025. No standing EU bilateral digital agreement, and no European platform or equipment presence in government systems, has been found.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf-financed digital infrastructure or Gulf bilateral digital agreement had been found for Sierra Leone as at August 2026. No Emirati or Saudi platform presence had been found either.

India activities

No India Stack-derived deployment had been found for Sierra Leone as at August 2026. No Indian digital financing or bilateral digital agreement had been found either.

Digital sovereignty

President Bio told a UN General Assembly roundtable on 21 September 2026 that Sierra Leone has been exploring regional data embassies. Under these, critical public data would be hosted in another participating state while the country keeps legal jurisdiction and control over it. Its principle is that "sovereignty should travel with the law, not simply with the building".

Cabinet approved a National Open Source Software First Policy in July 2026. It aims to cut software costs, improve data security and strengthen digital sovereignty. The next identity platform rests on a memorandum with Bhutan's national digital identity company and the Sign Foundation (July 2026). It is built on open-source technology and W3C verifiable credentials, and stresses local capability and long-term national ownership. It builds on a November 2025 memorandum under which the California-based Sign leads development of "sovereign blockchain infrastructure". It covers national identity, a wallet and asset tokenisation.

The civil registration authority hosts identity data in its own Tier-3 data centre and offers hosting to ministries (2024). The government data centre is at the NatCA tower, with a redundancy site planned at State House (September 2026). A data governance framework is being drafted to set how government data is classified, shared and used. The auditor found that the Universal Access Development Fund's central data centre was managed and controlled exclusively by a foreign contractor (2024). The fund had no administrative access to the system. No instrument fixing where state data is held or under whose law it is processed was in force as at September 2026. In November 2026 the communications ministry set up a working group to move the .sl domain registry onto national infrastructure. The registry has been hosted abroad and run by a private operator since 2007.

On 22 December 2025 the government signed a US$129m health memorandum with the United States that commits it to protecting data sovereignty and regulatory authority. A US freedom-of-information suit filed in April 2026 seeks to have the State Department release its text and related data-sharing agreements, among others.
