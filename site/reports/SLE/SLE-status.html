Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Sierra Leone's digital state is built on one register. The National Civil Registration Authority reports a biometric identity register covering about 93% of the population, each holder with a unique National Identification Number, and payments, banking and SIM registration are being wired onto it. The delivery gap is at the card: the World Bank recorded under 0.5m cards issued against 6.4m NIN holders in 2024, and no issuance figure has been published since.

Two identity architectures are being stood up at once. The ministry is building a credential layer with Bhutan's National Digital Identity and the SIGN Foundation, specified as MOSIP-based with W3C verifiable credentials; the registrar states its own MOSIP pilot is complete and full implementation unfunded, and has warned publicly against parallel systems.

No data-protection law is in force. Cabinet approved a National Data Protection Policy and authorised a Bill on 21 April 2026; it has not been tabled.

Connectivity is the constraint the state names itself: about US$400m invested against a 60% usage gap.

The backbone is built and under-used. Fibre reaches 14 of 16 districts, and the communications minister put the investment at about US$400m against a 60% usage gap, naming affordability rather than coverage as the limit on use. Redundancy runs through neighbours and wholesale carriers: the Pamelap interconnection with Guinea's SOGEB carries 42.5 Gbps, and CSquared added 2Africa West capacity to the backbone that reaches Sierra Leone in July 2026. A second landing of its own has not moved past feasibility since July 2025.

Satellite is filling the state's own gaps. Starlink has been licensed since June 2024, and the health ministry began connecting a stated 300 government facilities on 27 July 2026 with no cost, funder, procurement route or data-residency arrangement published.

Hosting has doubled since 2025. Orange opened a EUR 23m disaster-recovery replica of its Freetown facility in Bo on 29 November 2025, and the National Data Centre at the NaTCA headquarters was commissioned on 17 April 2026 after a build begun in 2015.

Cybersecurity institutions are ahead of the record about them. The regulator confirmed ongoing incidents affecting digital services on 30 July 2026 with no attribution, no named service and no incident count, and its response to the fraud wave is an advisory consumer-education campaign. No founding instrument, mandate or budget is held for the National Cyber Security Coordination Centre named in that response.

Identity is the most complete layer and the most contested. The NCRA reports about 93% coverage on a unique National Identification Number, a Tier III data centre and a live eKYC platform, and states its MOSIP pilot is complete and full implementation still seeking funding. The ministry's track is a credential layer over that register, MOSIP-based with W3C verifiable credentials under a memorandum signed in June 2026 with Bhutan's National Digital Identity and the SIGN Foundation; no framework, legal instrument or timetable has been published. The registrar has argued in public that the register already enrolled should be the anchor. The two positions have not been reconciled.

Interoperability is at pilot stage. The Ministry of Finance is piloting a data exchange layer on X-Road, following a World Bank terms of reference for a Government Service Bus prototype; no production date or connected agency list is published, and no instrument governing exchanges between registers is held.

Payments are interoperable at the rail and crowded at the wallet. The instant phase of the national switch launched on 13 February 2025 with seven banks and two mobile operators. A reported rail re-platforming is Not held — its only source, a tender aggregator, was ruled an inadmissible origin and retired, so the amounts, dates and the fact of award are unestablished. Five card and wallet products have launched above the rail since April 2025, most recently the Afrimoney prepaid Visa card in May 2026.

Sector registers are thinner. The National Digital Farmer Registry has no enrolment figure published since its NIN integration was agreed in May 2025, and electronic medical records exist as an announced programme with one facility connected. Geospatial capability is newer: a platform launched on 6 July 2026 with Catholic Relief Services and a national base map with Japanese support, announced 4 August 2026 with no cost, timetable, hosting arrangement or data licence stated.

There is no data-protection law in force. Cabinet approved a National Data Protection Policy and authorised a Data Protection and Right to Access Information Act on 21 April 2026, repealing the Right to Access Information Commission Act and merging both functions into one authority with investigative and enforcement powers; the Bill is to be finalised with the Attorney-General and tabled, with no parliamentary date set. The draft completed national validation in November 2025, and the policy text is not published. Sierra Leone's bilateral health agreement with the United States, and the data-sharing agreement attached to it, are withheld and in litigation since 2 April 2026.

The Electronic Transactions Act was assented and commenced on 5 December 2019, and a public-private dialogue of 27-28 July 2026 was convened to establish the certification authorities, evidence procedures, dispute mechanisms and penalties that were never created, producing a gap analysis, a policy memorandum and an implementation roadmap, none of them adopted. It worked to functional equivalence and technological neutrality. The Act as enacted and gazetted is not held.

A legal analysis of 23 July 2026 finds that the National Communications Authority Act 2022 addresses its enforcement powers to licensees, leaving foreign platforms outside the perimeter, and that no statutory reversal mechanism or refund duty exists; a former minister argues the central bank's existing know-your-customer rules are adequate and only enforcement is missing. The two accounts have not been reconciled. The government's answer, set out to Parliament on 4 August 2026, is public education, institutional strengthening and better investigation, with no liability rule proposed.

The Subscribers Regulations 2025 fine operators 25,000 new leones for each unregistered or pre-activated SIM, on a transition clock presented as running from the announcement of 30 July 2026, so the phase-out date is unestablished; the instrument's text is not held.

Artificial intelligence policy is at the drafting stage. MoCTI, with the World Bank, is developing a first National AI Strategy and has produced a draft AI readiness diagnostic, with consultations run on local languages; nothing has been adopted. The capacity route runs through a memorandum with Qhala signed in October 2025 to train 500 civil servants and build 10-15 AI workflows; training has been reported and workflow delivery has not.

Synthetic media is being addressed by literacy rather than by rule. A deepfake and digital-misinformation campaign has run since May 2026 across seven towns, with broadcast programming and a verification chatbot announced as the next phase and no budget, funder or completion date stated. No legislative or platform-facing instrument accompanies it.

The only industrial application on the ledger is the dispatch centres and SCADA upgrades provided for in the Millennium Challenge Corporation compact, with no commissioning or delivery report published.

Skills work is running at two levels. A civil service digital skills programme launched in July 2026 with 1,000 places in year one and stated targets of 3,000 and 5,000; in the universities, a curriculum built with UNICEF, the United Nations University and Limkokwing has taught more than 1,100 students and produced 11 student solutions evaluated against the Digital Public Goods Standard, on campuses whose connectivity is bought from a satellite operator — 12 universities through ELCOME SL, with no further site count reported.

Access remains uneven at both ends of the identity chain. Enrolment of the undocumented depends on chiefs, teachers and justices of the peace issuing affidavits the NCRA accepts at face value, per an ethnographic study published in March 2026, while the card that enrolment produces has no issuance figure published since 2024.

External finance carries the digital programme.
