Sierra Leone: monthly update, September – October 2026

Edition of 2026-10-11  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Developments summarised from sources published between the beginning of last month and today.
Summary of the month · Governance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics

Summary of the month

The national campaign against deepfakes and digital misinformation was launched in Freetown on 16 September. The statistics office set census night for 1 December 2026 and stated a US$37m financing gap. A bidder's complaint over the government network contract is under review by the Independent Procurement Review Panel. At the UN General Assembly the government presented its open source software policy, and Cabinet approved a national land title certification scheme that still needs parliament's approval.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

A data governance framework was named as in development for the first time. Meeting United Nations agencies on 5 September, the communications minister named the framework alongside the government data centre at the telecommunications authority tower and a redundancy site at State House. No draft, scope, adoption route or timetable is published for the framework, and the redundancy site appears here with no capacity or commissioning date.

The open source policy Cabinet approved in July was presented by the communications minister at a UNICEF showcase on the UNGA81 sidelines as the answer to duplicated government systems, proprietary-technology costs and vendor lock-in. Its text is still not published.

Legislation and regulation

A Digital Government Bill with data-governance and child-protection provisions is being prepared with the Attorney-General's office, with no draft or timetable published.

Data protection

The communication, technology and innovation ministry, with UNICEF and a digital public infrastructure centre, led a three-day workshop on how government systems handle children's data, reported on 10 September.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

The contract to network government offices was challenged. On 21 September the Independent Procurement Review Panel opened an investigation into a bidder's complaint over the Digital Transformation Project's contract to supply and install wide and local area networks across ministries, departments and agencies, and said it would publish the outcome. No award, supplier or contract value is on record. The procurement review panel dismissed a bidder's complaint over the government network contract and directed the ministry to open financial proposals and proceed to award.

Energy

Three rural mini-grid sites were named on 24 September for a philanthropic demonstration adding computing load to under-used generation, with local demand given priority; no site, capacity or start date is given for the country.

Cybersecurity

The road safety authority disclosed an unauthorised intrusion into the systems used to produce vehicle licences, with five arrests. The communications ministry confirmed that 44 website addresses were hit on 25 September 2026 by unauthorised access to the system that administers the national internet domain, restored the same day.

DPI

Data Exchange

A government delegation completed a two-week learning mission to India on 7 September 2026 under UNDP's digital public infrastructure accelerator, examining identity, payment and data exchange systems. The revenue authority began consultations at the Jendema border crossing ahead of launching ASYCUDA World there.

Digital Payments and Fintech

A self-service route into pensions was announced. The social security trust is to launch an application for pensioners and contributors with a short-code fallback for feature phones, biometric verification, and integration with the three mobile-money services. It is announced rather than launched: no launch date, pensioner count, transaction route or fee arrangement is published, and the short-code fallback is the part that decides whether it reaches anyone outside the smartphone base.

Registries

Cabinet approved a national land title certification scheme, reported on 15 September; it needs parliament's approval before it runs. The lands minister says registration and certification will be free. No cost, timetable or register design is published.

Sectoral management information systems

Government bodies and partners held a workshop on 17-18 September 2026 on a shared health digital public infrastructure architecture, mapping the digital health estate for gaps and duplication; a technical working group is to finalise the implementation plan.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

The audit management system cleared its acceptance stage. It is through user acceptance testing after post-configuration training for 25 audit service staff on 3 to 5 August, built for the state audit service with lender funding and regional audit-body support. No go-live date, module list, cost or audit-cycle coverage is published, and the account comes from the regional audit body rather than from the audit service itself.

Technology

AI

The information minister launched a national campaign against deepfakes and digital misinformation in Freetown on 16 September 2026, in two phases across all 16 districts. The national security office warned of risks from artificial intelligence in the 2028 elections, as the electoral commission considers using it. The judiciary and the attorney-general's office are to draft a national AI roadmap for the justice sector with UNESCO, starting with the security sector; no timetable is published.

Innovation ecosystem

The communications minister said a Startup Act is expected in the first quarter of 2027, alongside an innovation investment fund and a planned tech city. No draft or consultation is on file.

At the same UNGA81 showcase, four Sierra Leonean teams were reported to have met all nine Digital Public Goods criteria and submitted their products to the global registry, with three more part of the way. No product is named.

Capacity

Literacy

The information minister launched a National AI and Deepfake Sensitization Campaign on 16 September with the communications ministry and the science and technology directorate, under the theme of misinformation and the country's information future. No audience, duration or budget is stated.

Training and skills

A first cohort of the civil service digital skills programme was in training in September and due to conclude on 25 September, with 1,000 civil servants planned in year one.

Research institutions

The Center for Information Privacy, Human-Centered Technology and Ethics Research launched in Freetown on 6 October 2026 to research the handling of personal data, train on data-subject rights and feed evidence into data-protection law and policy.

Inclusion

Access to services

The registration authority and the correctional service registered 100 inmates from three Freetown correctional centres for National Identification Numbers on 17 September 2026, their cards issued free. Fieldwork for a research network's survey of how people experience digital identity and payment systems began in the week to 7 September.

Data

National statistics

The census has a date and a hole in its budget. The president set Census Night for 1 December 2026 and put the financing gap at US$37m, against US$24m committed by government of which about US$15m is disbursed. The civil registration authority put a draft 2025 vital statistics report to two days of stakeholder validation.

The census technical committee received the national mapping completion report on 15 September, closing cartographic work for the country's first digital census ahead of Census Night on 1 December 2026. The statistics agency deployed the technology for the digital census, with enumeration by tablet set for 2 to 16 December 2026.

Geopolitics

India activities

India enters this record as a destination. Senior officials from the digital and ICT sector and line ministries joined delegations from four other African states and the African Union Commission on a south-south learning mission hosted by the United Nations Development Programme, studying India's digital public infrastructure and the institutions that built it, with the state government of Karnataka receiving them in Bengaluru on 1 September. No agreement, financing, technology transfer or follow-on commitment is named on either side.
