Edition of 2026-09-06  ·  164 systems and instruments tracked

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A National Innovation and Digital Strategy 2019-2029 is the standing frame, with a Digital Development Policy under it.
Full recordThe strategy runs on a three-part philosophy of digital identity, digital economy and digital governance, and on three principles - mobile-first, country-as-AI-lab and hybrid technology systems (strategy).

The sector policy that operationalises it, Towards a Digital Economy, was issued in 2021 (policy).

Both predate the current ministry, and neither has a published mid-term review, delivery report or successor in the base. The strategy's own window closes in 2029.

Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies Subscriber registration data may not leave the country without the regulator's prior approval, under regulations made in 2020.
Full recordRegulation 22(3) of Statutory Instrument No. 35 of 2020 provides that subscribers registration information shall not be transferred outside Sierra Leone without prior approval by the Commission, and regulation 22(4) requires any such request to justify its purpose; regulation 19 requires the Commission to keep a central subscriber information database (regulations).

That is the whole of the localisation rule in the base: it reaches one data class held by one sector's licensees. No general rule on where government or personal data may be stored is on record, and the data protection Bill that would set one is not enacted.

Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy Cabinet approved a National Data Protection Policy in April 2026, above a National Data Strategy issued in 2023 and updated with donor support.
Full recordThe policy was approved by Cabinet on 21 April 2026, and the same decision authorised the Bill that would give it force (policy).

The data strategy carries January 2023 as the date on the document; the ministry's own index files it as February 2023 and the formal launch was later again, so three dates exist for one instrument. It was updated over five months with donor support and aligned to the continental data policy framework (strategy, update).

Full recordThe extractive industries transparency initiative adopted its own open data policy in 2016, covering the data it holds (policy).

What the base does not hold is a national open data policy, a licensing regime, or any instrument binding ministries generally. The two on record bind one sector and one commission.

Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation Cabinet authorised a Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill in April 2026, and the country has had no data-protection law in force throughout the period.
Full recordThe Bill was finalised in November 2025 and Cabinet approved the policy and authorised the Bill on 21 April 2026 (bill, finalisation).

No data-protection law has been in force at any point in the period. That is the frame for everything else in this unit: a biometric register covering most of the population, subscriber data disclosed to police, an identity migration in pilot and two competing identity platforms, all operating with no statutory data-protection regime behind them.

Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The National Civil Registration Act 2016 is the governing statute, with a replacement Bill validated in November 2025.
Full recordThe 2016 Act establishes the National Civil Registration Authority as an independent body with its own board and a confidentiality duty (Act); a draft Civil Registration and Identification Bill completed final national validation on 19 November 2025 and is not yet before Parliament (Bill).

The subscriber regulations phase-out of unregistered cards was announced on 30 July 2026, linking registration to the national identification number, and the instrument's own text is not held (regulations). Government conceded on 4 August 2026 that the linkage is incomplete (linkage).

That concession matters because the fraud response recorded elsewhere rests on registration and identity verification as its principal control, and the control is admitted to be partial.

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The Right to Access Information Act 2013 is in force, and a Bill that would carry the access regime forward completed national validation in November 2025.
Full recordThe 2013 Act creates a right of access, a proactive publication duty with approved publication schemes, a schedule of exemptions and an oversight commission (Act).

The Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill went through nationwide consultations from September 2025 (consultations), a pre-legislative session in October (session) and final national validation in November (validation).

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation A Nigeria-Sierra Leone Digital Transformation Cooperation Framework was signed in November 2025, naming know-your-customer alignment and ECOWAS-aligned digital trade principles.
Full recordThe framework was signed at a bilateral mission in Freetown on 21 and 22 November 2025, with two technical partnerships and a joint working group on a 30-90-365 day action plan; its stated objectives include alignment of digital know-your-customer standards, shared data and AI governance frameworks, and ECOWAS-aligned digital trade principles (communique).
