Libya: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-05  ·  137 systems and instruments tracked
↓ PDF
Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-05.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Full recordThe strategy period runs to 2027 and nothing on file records a mid-term review, a successor or a costing.

Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy The 2025-2030 digital agenda sets a broadband rollout to 2027 and records the laws behind it as unenacted.
Full recordThe agenda issued in October 2025 is the first standing digital development plan the base holds for Libya, and it puts broadband rollout on a horizon to 2027 while stating that the data protection and electronic commerce laws it relies on remain unenacted.

What is moving underneath it is wholesale access rather than a plan: the regulator and the fixed operator set fibre-pair lease prices and opened access networks to private investment in December 2025.

Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A National Cloud Computing Policy went to national consultation with ESCWA in January 2026; the final text is not published.
Full recordThe consultation was held on 1 January 2026 and the policy is framed as the arrangement for hosting government data and services, pressed on the state's sovereignty over its own data (consultation).

No draft text, timetable or hosting standard has been published since, so the base holds the intention and not the instrument.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap A roadmap proposes an interoperability framework and none had been adopted as at December 2025.
Full recordThe regional commission's roadmap sets out best practice and proposes a Libyan government interoperability framework; nothing had been adopted when it was published.

The governance layer that exists is a method rather than a standard: the information authority's national guide for government data sets a six-stage approach with no interface specification or schema behind it. The mandate to interconnect state bodies has sat with the authority since a 2018 decision.

Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy The prime minister adopted a 2026-2030 National Artificial Intelligence Strategy on 1 June 2026, replacing the 2025-2030 edition published eight months earlier.
Full recordThe first edition, prepared with ESCWA, was published in October 2025 (2025-2030 edition).

The 2026-2030 edition was adopted at an official ceremony alongside an ethics charter (adoption). Its targets are stated rather than measured: 80 per cent of government entities using artificial intelligence, 70 per cent activation of the national digital identity, 10,000 staff trained, 100 startups, half of government transactions automated and 70 per cent of paper records digitised (adoption).

Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies Circular 18 of 2025 bars all cross-border transfer of Libyan banking data and requires certified in-country hosting.
Full recordThe central bank's regulation is the strictest localisation rule the base holds for Libya: banking data may not leave the country, hosting must be certified to international information-security and card-industry standards, and breaches carry a penalty of LYD 100,000 each.

It is sectoral. No economy-wide localisation instrument exists, and the national digital agenda records the general data protection law as still unenacted.

Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id Law 8 of 2014 creates the twelve-digit national number, mandatory from birth for every state service.
Full recordThe law is the statutory basis for everything built since: a twelve-digit number assigned from birth and required to obtain any state service.

The institution beneath it is older — the civil registry authority created by decree in 1998 — and the competences were redistributed by a 2018 presidential council decision that gave the information authority the national database. No amending statute has been passed.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A prime-ministerial decree of 6 March 2026 obliges treasuries and collecting agencies to accept electronic payment, the first such instrument on file.
Full recordThe decree was issued on 6 March 2026 and is reported alongside a cut in the foreign-exchange levy, making electronic acceptance a duty of state collecting bodies rather than an option (decree).

The base holds no implementing regulation, no compliance deadline and no penalty for a body that does not comply.

Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability A 2018 decision hands the information authority the national database and the mandate to interconnect state bodies.
Full recordThe decision is the operative instrument: it redistributes the national number project's competences and gives the authority both the database and the linkage mandate.

The service it produced is running — electronic linkage connecting national bodies to the central database, with no civil registry link named — while the framework that would standardise it is still only proposed.

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations The adopted AI strategy names the legislative vacuum and sets a four-stage law roadmap to 2030.
Full recordThe strategy adopted in June 2026 is unusually direct about its own starting point: it names a legislative vacuum and sets a four-stage roadmap to legislation by 2030. No artificial intelligence statute is in force.

The instrument that arrived first is soft: the regulator reopened the draft national artificial intelligence charter for public comment in April 2026, five weeks before adoption, and the information authority convened a meeting in April to move the strategy from drafting to implementation.

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation Law 6 of 2022 covers electronic signatures, contracts and fifteen disclosures to online buyers; no e-commerce act follows it.
Full recordThe law enacted in October 2022 is the operative instrument for electronic dealing: signature and contract rules, and fifteen matters a seller must disclose to an online buyer.

It is not a full electronic commerce regime, and the national digital agenda of October 2025 records the electronic commerce law as still unenacted alongside the data protection law.

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation Law 16 of 1963 still governs statistics and censuses, its penalty scale unchanged since enactment.
Full recordThe statute creates the central statistics committee and remains the governing law, with penalties still expressed in the currency unit of 1963.

Nothing has replaced it. The statistics development strategy that ran to 2023 carried no adoption decree the base could locate and has not been renewed, so the sector runs on a sixty-three-year-old law and an expired plan.

Full recordThe draft law on the national information system would give Libya a request-and-appeal route to public records. It has not been enacted.

What publication happens is voluntary: the audit bureau published its 2024 annual report on 31 December 2025 after delay, with no right-to-information law compelling it. The constitutional declaration guarantees expression and privacy and creates no access right.

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers Circular 18 of 2025 bans all cross-border transfer of Libyan banking data outright.
Full recordThe central bank's regulation prohibits banking data leaving Libya and requires hosting inside the country certified to international security and card-industry standards, with a penalty of LYD 100,000 per breach.

A flat prohibition is a harder rule than most transfer regimes: there is no adequacy route, no consent route and no derogation on the record. Outside banking, no general instrument governs transfers at all, the data protection law being still unenacted.
