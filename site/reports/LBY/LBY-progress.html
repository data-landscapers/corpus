Libya: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-17

Compiled 2026-08-17 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 77 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 52 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-17, 16 did not, 2 carry no stated baseline, and 7 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 72 sources for this place in the window — 22 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-02), 50 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The payment layer and the register layer moved through the twelve months; the legal layer did not. Electronic transaction value went from LD 136 billion for 2024 to LD 643 billion for the first seven months of 2026, and the real-time payroll went from launch, with the eastern government refusing to share employee banking data, to about 1.7 million employees enrolled, 76%. Five payment instruments that did not exist in August 2025 are in service.

Registers followed: a Unified Tax System, a Unified Economic Number, an export window, a chambers-of-commerce system and an expatriate-worker database. Each was authorised with no data-protection law behind it, and that position is unchanged — none is in force, and the only commitment on file remains the AI strategy's target of 2026.

What did not move: the telecoms licensing dispute, the civil registry's completion figure, the Unified Social Registry and the postal-addressing programme.

5G entered service where nothing existed, Almadar Aljadid launching in central Tripoli in January 2026. Ookla, measuring the four North African markets that launched earlier, found three of them delivering a worse aggregate mobile experience six months in — a dated measurement, cited rather than absorbed. Root servers came into service in October 2025.

Four vendor agreements were added in the twelve months — KBR, ZTE, Nokia and ARABSAT — none of which states a value, a timetable or a coverage obligation. The movement is in the count of memoranda rather than in the network.

The southern network was cut and mostly recovered: floods severed the Ghat-Tahala fibre in May 2026 and 11 of 12 sites, 91.7%, were restored, most on VSAT backup.

Huawei ends the period where it began it. Suspended nationwide on 17 August 2025 and reinstated on 20 August on a compliance undertaking, it was scoping a ministry's internal systems by July 2026. Nothing moved on data-centre capacity, on power supply to network sites, or on the 2019 Tripoli data centre, because nothing is held on any of them.

The payment estate carries most of the period's movement. Terminals went from 150,205 in 2024 to 170,149 by February 2026, and transaction value from LD 136 billion for 2024 to LD 389 billion for 2025 and LD 643 billion for the first seven months of 2026. Five instruments that did not exist at the start of the window are in service at the end of it: QR acceptance on LYPay from March 2026, e-wallets for resident foreigners under Circular 9 of 2026, and SoftPOS, international Visa acceptance and a National Stand-In Processing Service from June 2026. Petrol stations took their first card payments in January 2026. The estate also failed once: 2 million transactions across 67,000 points collapsed the system in May 2026.

Ratibak Lahzi is the single largest movement. It began the window newly launched, with about 2 million records collected and the eastern government refusing to share employee banking data with Tripoli; it ends it carrying about 1.7 million employees, 76% of a 2.2 million denominator fixed at project start, with July salaries executed through it. About 500,000 employees are still outside it and no breakdown of who they are has been published.

Registers were authorised through the window and delivery figures exist for one of them. The Unified Tax System launched in December 2025, the chambers-of-commerce system in March 2026, and the export window reported LD 79.5 million by July 2026, 66% of its orders going to Tunisia. The Unified Economic Number was authorised in July 2026 with no timetable, budget or legal basis stated for the database linkage it is to run on. LY-GOVREP went from preliminary operation with 60 cabinet entities to activation.

Two identity positions did not move: Project Intilaqa's 97% is still a September 2024 figure, and the Unified Social Registry has been draft-approved since June 2025. Enforcement did move: 225 national ID numbers and passports revoked in January 2026, 10 more cancelled at Al-Marj, and the forged-number trade reported still running in April 2026.

The licensing dispute did not move. Law No. 22 of 2010 remains in force, Tripoli continues to read it as reserving all licensing to itself, and the OZON licence granted under it by the eastern ministry remains outstanding and treated as void.

No data-protection law was enacted. The instrument that commits to one was published in October 2025, so the commitment and the vacuum both sit inside this window; a cybersecurity law entered drafting on the eastern track in July 2026.

What was created was administrative rather than statutory: a National Committee for the Information for All Programme under Decision No. (50) of 2026, an open-data platform whose published dataset count went from unstated to 91, a decree requiring state revenue collectors to accept electronic payment, and a prohibition on card-payment surcharges after complaints of spreads reaching 20%.

Libya also took two external positions it did not hold a year ago: the chair of an Arab working group on AI Data and Digital Infrastructure and a declared candidacy for the ITU Council.

Libya's whole AI position was created inside the window. The National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2025-2030 was published in October 2025 and LIBIGPT launched two days before it. Neither has a successor document on file: a 2026-2030 strategy is reported adopted and is not held, and the AI Ethics Charter named alongside it is not held either.

The technology sector itself did not move. Al Itissan Al-Jadeed's Niger contract, won in June 2025, has published no delivery milestone since, and the two bank digital-service build-outs reported in April 2026 rest on the vendors' own accounts.

Training capacity was added three times in the window, each time by an outside body: the AI and cybersecurity master's institute with UNDP in November 2025, cooperation with India on AI and quantum skills in February 2026, and the Islamic Development Bank's programme for central bank officials in July 2026. The research authority's higher-education cloud platform, approved in August 2026, is described by the authority as built on Microsoft 365.

Access moved the other way. Holders of administrative numbers protested exclusion from national-number-gated services in June 2025 and no resolution has been published. The note recall that opened the cash shortage was followed, on 5 August 2026, by branch withdrawal limits of LD 3,000, and LD 4,000 in the south.
