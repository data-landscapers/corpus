Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Libya's digital state is administered from two capitals under one set of statutes, and both administrations invoke the same law. Law No. 22 of 2010 reserves telecommunications licensing to the Tripoli authority; the eastern ministry granted a unified telecoms licence under it in March 2025, which Tripoli treats as void.

The Central Bank of Libya is the most capable digital institution on the record. Electronic transactions ran at LD 643 billion between 1 January and 31 July 2026 against LD 389 billion for the whole of 2025, and about 1.7 million of roughly 2.2 million public employees — 76% as at July 2026 — are paid through Ratibak Lahzi. The same bank rations cash: withdrawal limits start at LD 3,000, and LD 4,000 in the south, from 9 August 2026. No series on file separates the adoption from the shortage.

The identity layer is the weak spine. The National ID Number gates a SIM, a passport, a bank account, marriage and voting, and in January 2026 the Attorney General had 225 national ID numbers and passports revoked after 61 family records were irregularly transferred between civil-registry offices.

No data-protection law is in force. The National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2025-2030 targets one for 2026; nothing has been enacted as at 2026-08-10.

One 5G network is in commercial service, in central Tripoli, launched by Almadar Aljadid in January 2026. The international leg is the stronger part of the estate: the Medusa cable was inaugurated at Misrata in May 2025, and national root servers entered service in Tripoli and Benghazi in October 2025 under the National Telecommunications and Informatics Strategy 2023-2027.

Below that, the position is a set of memoranda. LPTIC signed with KBR in January 2026 and met Nokia in July; LTT signed with ZTE in March and with ARABSAT in August for capacity on Arabsat-6A. None of the four states a value, a timetable or a coverage commitment, and the IPv6 transition convened in April 2026 has published no target either.

Domestic resilience is the thin part. Floods severed the Ghat-Tahala fibre in May 2026 and 11 of 12 sites, 91.7%, were restored, most on VSAT backup. Nothing is held on installed data-centre capacity or on power provision for network sites, and the outcome of the Tripoli data centre announced by Trans-Sahara and Tatweer in 2019 is unestablished.

Huawei operates under a compliance undertaking given to the Tripoli authority in August 2025, three days after a nationwide suspension, and by July 2026 was scoping the Ministry of Civil Service's own systems.

The Interior Ministry recorded 135 cybercrime cases in the first quarter of 2025 and has published no figure since; the Central Bank isolated systems after a June 2026 incident, and a sample of its data was later published on a dark-web site.

Payments are the deepest layer. The terminal estate reached 170,149 POS terminals by February 2026 from 150,205 in 2024, and a single day of 2 million transactions across 67,000 points collapsed the system in May 2026. Transaction value ran at LD 643 billion for 1 January to 31 July 2026, against LD 389 billion for 2025 and LD 136 billion for 2024. Three instruments were added at the payments exhibition in June 2026 — SoftPOS, international Visa acceptance on local terminals and a National Stand-In Processing Service — after QR acceptance on LYPay in March.

Ratibak Lahzi carries about 1.7 million of roughly 2.2 million public employees, 76% as at July 2026, and July salaries were executed through it. About 500,000 employees remain outside it, and no breakdown of who they are has been published.

Project Intilaqa reported 257 offices matched and 1.8 million family records printed, 97% complete, in September 2024, and no later figure is held. Against that, 61 family records were suspended and 225 national ID numbers and passports revoked in January 2026, 10 numbers were cancelled at Al-Marj, and a trade in forged numbers was reported still running in April 2026.

Government platforms cluster on the economic side. The Unified Tax System launched in December 2025, the Unified Digital System for Chambers of Commerce in March 2026, and the electronic Unified Export Window processed LD 79.5 million between April and July 2026 across 354 registered companies and 190 products, 66% of the orders going to Tunisia. The Unified Economic Number, authorised by Decision No. 348 of 2026, is the identifier that layer needs, and states no timetable, budget or legal basis for the database linkage it is to run on. LY-GOVREP moved from preliminary operation with 60 cabinet entities to activation in April 2026.

Two registers are being built to enumerate rather than to serve. The unified national database of expatriate workers, agreed by the interior and labour ministries in August 2026, names no authorising instrument, retention limit or oversight body; the Unified Social Registry has stood at draft-approved since June 2025.

GIA positions LY-NDEX as pillar infrastructure for inter-agency exchange, named at a July 2026 anti-money-laundering workshop, with no legal basis, entity count or transaction figure published. Level 3 biometric LY-DNID was in eKYC trials with the Central Bank and several banks in April 2026, ahead of banking integration.

Two authorities read one statute against each other. Law No. 22 of 2010 is in force and Tripoli reads it as reserving all telecommunications licensing to itself; the eastern ministry granted OZON a unified licence effective 4 March 2025 under the same law.

No data-protection law is in force, and the AI strategy that targets one for 2026 is the only instrument on file that commits to it. A comprehensive cybersecurity law is in drafting on the eastern track, discussed with the National Security Council in July 2026. Government data-governance policy, a vision from February 2026, was applied to the Zakat Fund's platforms in April 2026; no gazetted instrument is stated. A Cloud Computing Policy with ESCWA remains under consultation since January 2026, text unpublished.

Openness has an institutional owner and no statute. The National Committee for the Information for All Programme was established under Decision No. 50 of 2026 and held its first meeting in July, and the open-data platform carried indicators covering 91 datasets in February 2026. Access to information rests on no domestic statutory basis the base holds.

Regulation has lately been pointed at payment behaviour. A prime-ministerial decree of March 2026 requires treasuries and collecting agencies to accept electronic payment, and the Municipal Guard prohibited card-payment surcharges in August 2026 after complaints of spreads reaching 20%. Externally, Libya holds the chair of the Arab working group on AI Data and Digital Infrastructure and is canvassing for a seat on the ITU Council — a multilateral position sought by a state whose own licensing authority is contested.

The National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2025-2030 was published by the General Information Authority in October 2025, prepared with ESCWA. PM Dbeibah adopted a successor, the National AI Strategy 2026-2030, and an AI Ethics Charter, on 1 June 2026, targeting 80% government AI use and 70% digital-identity activation; the Charter centres human decision-making over AI in sensitive sectors. LIBIGPT, launched in October 2025, is the only domestic generative-AI system on the record.

The technology sector outside the state is thin and supplier-shaped. Two banks were reported in April 2026 to be building digital services with foreign vendors — Tadamon Bank with JMR Infotech of India, Seraj Islamic Bank with Network International of the UAE — on those vendors' own account. The single identity-technology export on file is Al Itissan Al-Jadeed's biometric passport and national ID contract in Niger, won in June 2025, with no delivery milestone published since.

Capacity is supplied by multilateral partners rather than by domestic institutions. The Ministry of Planning launched a professional master's institute in AI and cybersecurity with UNDP in November 2025; UNDP's data-systems memorandum with the General Information Authority, signed in July 2025, carries the Seventh National Human Development Report. The Islamic Development Bank ran an executive programme for central bank officials in Jeddah from 27 to 30 July 2026 covering AI-related security risk, four weeks after the bank's own cyber incident, and states no participant numbers, curriculum or standard.

Access is where the record is thinnest. Holders of administrative numbers rather than full national numbers protested exclusion from national-number-gated services in June 2025, and no resolution is published. Withdrawal limits of LD 3,000, and LD 4,000 in the south, took effect on 9 August 2026. No national digital-literacy programme is held.
