This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Stratégie Comores Numérique 2028 was adopted by the Council of Ministers in 2019 and still sets digital policy. Its six strategic axes and thirteen orientations sit under the Plan Comores Émergent. It is costed at 52,995,160,485 KMF, with national broadband the largest line at 32,629,410,800 KMF. It plans fourteen e-government platforms. It designed a Conseil National sur le Numérique, chaired by the Secrétaire général du gouvernement, to steer the sector. ANADEN was to implement the strategy, and an Observatoire national du numérique to measure the sector. ANADEN's board of directors was installed in July 2026 and examined the agency's 2026 action plan. No act constituting the Conseil National sur le Numérique has been found.

On 21 October 2026 the telecommunications minister presented a national data governance strategy for 2027 to 2031 to the Council of Ministers. No adopted text has been published. On 19 November 2026 ANADEN and UNDP launched an assessment of the country's digital readiness. It covers the population, connectivity, government, regulation and the economy, and is meant to set priorities for digital investment.

In June 2024 ANADEN said the measures and strategies to govern artificial intelligence were still to be considered. The UNESCO readiness assessment presented in Riyadh in September 2026 found no national AI strategy, no AI-specific legal instrument and no independent ethics body. It left drafting a strategy to the government as the next step.

Comoros scored 42.8 out of 100 for Overall Governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, 40th of 54 African states. The African average was 49.3. Comoros fell 4.7 points over 2014-2023, and from 28th place. It sits below the continental average on 50 of the index's 96 indicators. Across the decade it improved on 31, declined on 38 and was unchanged on nine.

Legislation and regulation

Décret n°26-023/PR of 2 February 2026 promulgated a new Code de l'information et de la communication. Its Article 231, the country's first statutory rule on artificial intelligence, requires explicit labelling of AI-generated content. Articles 230 to 236 cover the subject, and Article 232 bans AI deepfakes. The labelling duty carries graduated administrative sanctions of up to 1,000,000 KMF. The code, Loi n°25-021/AU, abrogates the 2021 code it replaces.

Décret n°26-070/PR of 4 June 2026 set a legal regime for renewable self-production and mini-grids. It provides net metering, and requires a three-year ARSEE authorisation for mini-grids of 5kW and above.

In December 2018 the Stratégie Comores Numérique 2028 found the legal framework centred on telecommunications. It found a vacuum on digital transactions, electronic money, paperless procedures, electronic signature, cybercrime and personal-data protection. The strategy put 27,000,000 KMF behind closing it. In March 2021 ANADEN set out a programme to consolidate the texts into a code numérique by 2024. Cybersecurity, personal-data and electronic-procurement texts were drafted, and an e-commerce law was still to be written.

Loi n°26-003/AU, a consumer statute, was adopted on 15 June 2026 and promulgated on 21 July 2026. It covers electronic contracts and distance sales, with a 14-day right of withdrawal. It creates a national consumer authority. Loi n°20-005/AU of 23 June 2020 sets the rules for payment services, including those offered through electronic money.

The procurement code as amended by Loi n°22-011/AU of 23 June 2022 institutes an online tendering system, COMORES MARCHES PUBLICS. It gives the system's technical administration to ANADEN. A World Bank-financed consultant drafted the e-procurement implementing decree in September 2025. No adopted decree has been found.

Loi n°23-016/AU of 27 July 2023 mandates computerised processing of civil-registry data. The Code de procédure pénale of 22 December 2014 names no electronic form for a procès-verbal.

Data protection

Since February 2026 Articles 216 to 220 of the Code de l'information et de la communication have let the press regulator CNPA sanction personal-data breaches in the media. They enforce duties on collection, consent and security.

Loi n°14-029/AU, adopted by the Assemblée de l'Union on 26 June 2014, gives supervision of personal data to a Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés. The commission had not been constituted or made operational as of mid-2024. In March 2026 the e-government project's procurement notice listed a consultancy to set it up.

The 2014 statute requires processing to be declared to the commission. Biometric, health and national-identification data need its authorisation. It sets no data localisation rule, and conditions cross-border transfers on the destination state's protection.

The outgoing contractor Semlex held Comorian biometric records offshore until it handed the database to the government on 24 March 2026. The interior minister had called the arrangement abnormal. The identity system gives individuals no way to view their data, and has no binding redress mechanism. It publishes no data-handling or data-sharing rules. The Ibrahim Index's Digital Freedom score fell 8.1 points over 2014-2023 to 65.1 out of 100, 21st of 54 African states.

Regional collaboration

The Smart Africa Alliance welcomed Comoros among six recently joined members at its Council of Ministers on 24 March 2026. Comoros's Regional Integration score in the Ibrahim Index fell 5.5 points over 2014-2023 to 39.0 out of 100, 34th of 54 African states. The country belongs to COMESA, CEN-SAD and SADC.

The Banque Centrale des Comores signed the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System membership agreement in July 2024. It gathered commercial-bank and microfinance heads for a workshop that August. The system's June 2026 coverage map still marks Comoros as live soon.

The press regulator CNPA and the information ministry organised the EMICn colloquium in Moroni on 5 and 6 May 2026, with RIARC. Algorithmic manipulation was among its media-literacy themes, and a Déclaration de Moroni its intended outcome.

Bahrain's e-government authority signed a digital-government cooperation memorandum with ANADEN in June 2026. It held the first knowledge-exchange session under it in October 2026, on the governance and approval of government technology projects.

Standards

A control campaign by the regulator ANRTIC, presented in February 2026, found both mobile operators short on several quality indicators. 4G coverage inside buildings and vehicles was below standard. On 30 October 2024 ANRTIC had presented its 2024 audit of mobile quality of service. It measured Comores Télécom and Telma Comores against 23 indicators over three months. It found coverage gaps and told both operators to redouble their efforts. Director-general Saïd Bouhtane restated the authority's power to sanction, and put the difficulty down to the archipelago's relief. The telecoms minister asked the regulator for a quarterly quality-of-service bulletin.

In May 2025 ANADEN became manager of the .km country-code domain, taking it from Comores Télécom. The transfer, under arrêté n°24-02, requires an ICANN re-delegation step. Décret n°26-070/PR of June 2026 requires installers working on renewable self-production and mini-grids to hold a ministry certification. The certification is valid for three years.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

On 26 December 2025 the Union Assembly adopted the new code de l'information et de la communication by 31 votes of 33. Deputies amended 25 articles and deleted 18. They struck out the government's proposed exception to source protection, « sauf décision judiciaire motivée ». The article, renumbered from 169 to 153, states without qualification that a journalist is not obliged to disclose sources.

In January 2024 the authorities cut internet access and blocked WhatsApp calls after protests against President Azali Assoumani's re-election. The Ibrahim Index's Media Freedom score fell 10.0 points to 58.1 out of 100 between 2014 and 2023, 19th of 54 African states. Freedom of Expression and Belief fell 16.0 points to 44.6 over the same years, 37th of 54. Comoros scored zero on the UN E-Participation Index in 2023. No access-to-information law had been adopted as at January 2026, the code de l'information et de la communication being a press law.

Articles 58 to 66 of the code promulgated in February 2026 create SCEPAC, a holding company supervising and coordinating the public broadcasters. On 24 January 2026 UONI, an association of Comorian diaspora scientists, held a webinar on artificial intelligence. Its conclusions called on public research and innovation institutions to lead in defining a clear national AI strategy.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Comoros's 2026 finance law gives the digital ministry its first capital line in three years, KMF 50m of state money. It raises the state transfer to the digital agency ANADEN to KMF 145m, against KMF 75m voted for 2025. The finance ministry's systems directorate, cut to nothing in the 2025 rectificative, returns at KMF 277.1m for 2026. KMF 70m of the 2026 figure is state investment.

The 2025 finance law printed no donor capital line for the digital ministry. The 2026 law's external investment programme carries the digitalisation project PADEC on African and Islamic Development Bank money. It carries civil-registry modernisation on French money and statistics capacity on World Bank money. PADEC's 2024 appraisal puts the government's counterpart at UA 1.85m in kind, against a total cost of UA 18.47m. The project closes on 31 December 2028. A private operator is to run, maintain and expand the government data centres it finances, under a public-private partnership (October 2024).

In 2024 the telecommunications ministry executed KMF 140m of a KMF 613m transfer provision.

Since February 2024 the amended electronic communications law has funded ANRTIC from a regulation tax on operators' prior-year turnover. Under the 2024 law the tax is 2%, 1% or 0.5% by licence class, and the regulator also takes 10% of licence fees and 40% of fines.

MoUs and other agreements

AXIAN and Mastercard agreed a partnership in November 2025 to issue virtual and physical cards. The cards are issued through AXIAN's Mixx and MVola wallets. Comoros is one of the five African markets covered.

New investments

In August 2026 the African Development Bank and the Multilateral Cooperation Center for Development Finance said they were ready to fund feasibility studies for a national digital-connectivity project. The project, a data centre with inter-island links, is to go through ANADEN.

China Eximbank signed a CNY 567 million concessional loan with Comores Télécom on 11 December 2018. The 2018 loan runs thirty years, with five years' grace, at 1% interest. It finances an USD 81 million Huawei contract to modernise the state operator and extend its network nationwide (2018).

In June 2025 the International Finance Corporation lent €25 million to Yas Comores, AXIAN Telecom's Comorian subsidiary. The loan is for 5G and fibre to homes and offices. In 2025 AXIAN Telecom pledged USD 82 million to expand 4G across Madagascar, Comoros and Tanzania. No Comorian share of the pledge has been published.

The African Development Bank's technical assistance to the PADEC digitalisation programme runs from 2024 to 2028. The Islamic Development Bank committed about USD 7 million to it from 2025. Comoros received USD 164.1 of official development assistance per head in 2023, the third highest in Africa.

In all, 19 digital-sector commitments reach Comoros, starting between 2017 and 2026 with windows running to 2028, and worth about USD 145 million between them (November 2026). Five are active, one approved, one completed and 12 carry no recorded status. China Eximbank's USD 86 million loan of 2018 is the largest, more than three times the next, the International Finance Corporation's USD 27 million for broadband in 2025. Counted by number rather than by value, the Joint SDG Fund leads with eight small grants for statistics, ahead of France's foreign ministry with four. Connectivity takes about USD 113 million, government digital services USD 18 million and policy support USD 10 million (November 2026). Comoros is named in none of the large East African regional digital programmes recorded alongside it, the World Bank's Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project among them.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Comoros's telecoms regulator found both mobile operators deficient on multiple quality indicators when it presented a two-month, 22-indicator control campaign on 26 February 2026. 4G coverage inside buildings and vehicles was below standard. The telecoms minister warned that there would be no further leniency, and floated calling in a third operator before the 2027 Games. The campaign followed the regulator's 2024 national mobile quality audit, presented in October 2024, which had already flagged coverage gaps. On 1 July 2026 consumers held a 24-hour mobile-wallet strike against data prices, with both operators charging KMF 25,000 for 50 GB.

Yas Comores launched 5G in Moroni on 20 May 2025 using Huawei equipment. The incumbent Comores Télécom followed on 2 June 2025 with a fixed-wireless service, inaugurated by the President for the fiftieth independence anniversary.

The 260km Avassa system, delivered in November 2016, links Grande Comore and Anjouan to Mayotte. International capacity also rests on three submarine cables run through the state company Comores Câbles: EASSy, FLY-LION3 and 2Africa. The Nafassi fibre-to-the-home network was built by Huawei for Comores Télécom under a Chinese concessional loan to modernise the state operator. It was launched in March 2022 and carries household internet, television and fixed telephony over that submarine capacity.

Internet use stood at 32.5% of individuals in 2024, against 35.67% in 2023. Take-up lags the equipment. The Ibrahim Index scores internet and computer access at 26.4 out of 100 in 2023, 35th of 54 African states against 69.2 for mobile communications, Comoros's third most improved indicator of 96. Around 21% of primary schools had internet access in 2021, with rural schools furthest behind.

ANADEN became manager of the .km country-code domain in May 2025, taking the national registry out of the incumbent's hands. It did so on an arrêté whose transfer still requires an ICANN re-delegation. A month later, on 12 June 2025, the regulator banned the installation, testing and use of Starlink pending a licence. It acted on a ministerial circular and announced field inspections to identify users.

Data Storage

Comoros opened a national data centre for the public administration on 19 May 2025, a Tier III facility rated at 44.4 terabits and co-managed by ANADEN with Comores Câble. The agency states that it will support the national DNS, digital identity and the state's e-service portals. In June 2025 ANADEN tendered a main data centre under the African Development Bank's PADEC project, with works supervision covering a secondary one. A further, operator-neutral data centre is under construction.

Semlex handed the citizen biometric database back to the government on 24 March 2026. It had been held in Belgium under that contractor, an arrangement the interior minister himself called abnormal. The incoming contractor is to domicile the data in Comoros. In May 2026 the generalised health-insurance scheme said its openIMIS platform was hosted on Amazon Web Services. It was considering a local backup server and the gradual repatriation of parts of the infrastructure.

The data-protection law of 26 June 2014 imposes no data localisation requirement. It conditions cross-border transfers on adequacy and safeguards, without requiring any category of data to be held in the country.

Energy

The government's National Energy Compact of 2025 puts electricity access at 49% on Anjouan, 87% on Mohéli and 95% on Ngazidja. That island divide is wider than the 12.2-point urban-rural gap behind the national figure of 89.8% access in 2023.

Generation is 91% diesel (2025). Supply is unreliable, with frequent prolonged outages, regular load shedding and high transmission losses on an ageing and poorly interconnected grid, rural areas worst affected. About 3,000 power cuts were recorded in 2024. Mohéli was rationing electricity again in early August 2026 after diesel deliveries to the SONELEC thermal plant fell far below its daily requirement. The island depends on maritime resupply without a fuel depot sized to its needs. Power costs about US$0.44 per kilowatt-hour in 2024. Around 48% of primary schools had electricity in 2021.

The 2018 Power Sector Master Plan's target of lifting renewables from 16% to 30% of generation by 2033 has stalled amid unclear institutional arrangements. No binding mini-grid framework existed as at 2025. Three UAE-financed solar plants totalling about 20 MW, with 16 MWh of storage, were inaugurated on 6 September 2026. Décret n°26-070/PR of 4 June 2026 set a legal regime for renewable self-production and mini-grids. The compact makes off-grid and mini-grid deployment the priority route to access on Anjouan and Mohéli. The UNDP/GEF Africa Minigrids Programme is preparing three mini-grid pilots across Grande Comore, Mohéli and Anjouan. In 2026 a UAE-funded solar plant with battery storage was commissioned as a microgrid on Grande Comore.

The World Bank set out about US$5m of additional financing for the Comoros Solar Energy Access Project in a March 2025 project paper. As at April 2026 it had a further operation for SONELEC network strengthening and access expansion in preparation. The compact commits Comoros to universal access by 2030. It plans digital components alongside: smart meters, mobile payment and an ERP system for SONELEC. Access to energy is one of Comoros's better Ibrahim Index measures, up 10.6 points over 2014-2023 to 48.5, 19th of 54.

Technical Capacity

In June 2024 the national digital agency ANADEN said attending the Riyadh AI governance assembly would help it grasp artificial intelligence and advise government on it. ANADEN's Direction Nationale de Cyber Sécurité is organised into a security-technologies department and a security-audit department.

Effective Administration fell 2.6 points over 2014-2023 to 36.4 out of 100, 43rd of 54 African states. The Public Administration sub-category it sits in scored 31.5 in 2023, 47th of 54.

The AfDB's €9.51 million approved in October 2024 pays for a new government data centre to be run by a private operator under a public-private partnership. A coordination unit inside ANADEN runs that project, PADEC. Its June 2025 procurement notice covers institutional support, capacity building and support to higher education. In May 2026 the generalised health-insurance scheme said two local developers had been trained for its openIMIS platform. Its local contractor was not operating, and the scheme still depended heavily on external developers. In June 2026 SONELEC, ministry and regulator staff took a five-day mini-grid pricing and regulation course run by the African Forum for Utility Regulators.

Cybersecurity

Comoros sits in the Evolving tier, the fourth of five, of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024. Loi n°21-012/AU on cybersecurity and cybercrime was adopted on 25 June 2021 and promulgated on 18 January 2022. ANADEN's cybersecurity directorate is the competent authority it designates under Article 6.

The 2021 law defines cybercrime terms and asserts jurisdiction over offences with any link to Comorian territory. It criminalises illegal access, breaches of data and system integrity, and computer fraud. Penalties run from one year and KMF 500,000 to twenty years and KMF 50,000,000 where critical infrastructure is touched.

The Penal Code of 29 December 2020 carries cybercrime offences at articles 449 to 505. They cover ICT offences, online intellectual-property infringement and the liability of online service providers. They also reach cybercafés, minors' access and false information. The justice ministry led the reform with the telecoms ministry, ANRTIC, ACTIC, the interior ministry and the World Bank.

In December 2018 the Stratégie Comores Numérique 2028 found a legal vacuum on cybercrime and personal-data protection. In March 2021 ANADEN set out a programme to consolidate the new laws into a code numérique. By September 2026 Comoros and Russia had initialled a bilateral memorandum on information-security cooperation.

DPI

Data Exchange

In 2026 the Islamic Development Bank issued procurement notices for an interoperability and data-exchange platform. The platform is part of its public administration modernisation project. The African Development Bank's PADEC was launched with a steering committee and an adopted work plan on 27-28 March 2025. The EU and Expertise France launched an e-Administration programme in October 2025. It is to build a single-window service portal and an interoperability layer. In October 2024 an interoperability platform was still to be built under the AfDB grant. The national identity system is not interoperable with other government systems.

A memorandum between the health ministry and the meteorological agency ANACM brings climate data into the health information system (July 2026). Since 2022 the AFD-financed PAGF-SI project has been improving links between the tax system SIGIT, the SYDONIA customs system and other finance ministry systems. The World Bank's 2023 public expenditure review recommends automated exchange of taxpayer information across government. Health, learner, court, voter, social protection and business records, among others, sit in no cross-government system (2025).

The Comores Numérique 2028 strategy carries interoperability as a component, and no standalone data exchange strategy has been found. The national payment switch that went live in August 2025 connects banks, microfinance institutions and mobile-money operators.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Semlex handed the Comorian biometric database back to the government on 24 March 2026. The Belgian contractor had held citizens' biometric data offshore, a position the interior minister called abnormal. GenKey of the Netherlands has taken over production of the biometric passport and identity card. At the end of December 2025 the change was announced for April, with the data to be domiciled in Comoros. The interior ministry suspended biometric document production from early March to 26 March 2026. Card production, concentrated in Moroni, is to begin on Ndzuani (February 2026).

Loi n°14-023/AU of 24 June 2014 instituting the digital biometric identity card was promulgated only by Décret n°14-137/PR of 28 August 2024. Holding a card has been compulsory for every Comorian from the age of 18 since 1987. From 12 August 2025 carrying it and presenting it at identity checks became obligatory. The 1987 law imposes a stamp duty on the card. In January 2018 the biometric card's price rose from 4,000 to 5,000 Comorian francs.

In 2024 the identity system provided no digital authentication function, and no government portal authenticated users against it. It gave individuals no means of viewing their data, and carried no binding redress mechanism. MVola's terms of November 2026 require the identity card or a passport, shown in original, to open a mobile-money account. Verification is by inspection of the document.

On 25 March 2026 the AMECC programme validated preparatory studies for digitising civil registration, which remains largely manual. The France-funded, UNICEF-supported studies make a Personal Identification Number the cornerstone of identity. They also plan interoperability with other state systems. In January 2024 about 95% of births were declared on time, but fewer than 10% of marriages and deaths. The 2026 appropriation gives the directorate for civil status and the criminal record KMF 4,000,000.

Digital Payments and Fintech

The Banque Centrale des Comores launched four payment systems at a ceremony on 4 May 2026: KomorPay/ATS, Mali Ya Wakazi, Komor Switch and the public securities market. Komor Switch runs in three phases: card withdrawal at any ATM, then instant bank-to-mobile-money transfer, then card and phone payment at any merchant.

PayLogic announced on 16 February 2025 that it would build the national interoperable payment switch. A World Bank status report in March 2025 put its completion at end-May 2025, with a pilot and instant payments still to come. The switch went live in August 2025, interconnecting banks, microfinance institutions and mobile-money operators.

In December 2025 the central bank listed two licensed electronic-money institutions, Telco Money SA and Hury Money SA. It granted AXIAN a Decentralised digital Financial Institution licence, announced on 24 February 2026, for mobile-delivered nano- and micro-credit alongside MVola. KomoraLink, a business-to-business and e-commerce platform backed by the chamber of commerce, launched on 24 April 2026. On 20 September 2026 a new phase of World Bank-financed cash transfers began for 55,000 households. Each receives 75,000 KMF in three monthly instalments through the operators Mvula and Huri Money. Official guidance still settles property tax by cheque or cash at the Trésorerie Générale (2026).

The central bank signed the PAPSS membership agreement in July 2024. The June 2026 PAPSS coverage map still marks Comoros as live soon. Since February 2024 one wallet has received transfers from two European apps and sent to Senegal.

About 45% of Comorian adults held an account in 2024, helped by mobile-money providers being allowed to operate. Use of digital merchant payments rose significantly between 2021 and 2024. Access to Banking Services scored 4.5 out of 100 in the Ibrahim Index in 2023, 46th of 54 African states. The central bank coordinates the Stratégie Nationale d'Inclusion Financière 2025-2030.

Registries

The World Bank-supported Registre social unique had 81,075 households registered across 152 of the country's 319 localities in November 2026. The second phase of the French-funded modernisation programme was 84% complete in October 2026 and was extended by three months. The Ibrahim Index's Civil Registration score stayed at 50.0 out of 100 across 2014-2023, 31st of 54. Birth registration completeness was last measured at 87.3%, in the 2012 Demographic and Health Survey.

Land records and cadastral plans in Moroni were kept on paper at the Service des Domaines, with no GIS, in 2019. A 2014 programme to digitise property rights from satellite imagery in pilot municipalities, due to finish in 2018, had not been implemented by 2020. Décret n°23-045/PR of 2 May 2023 created the Direction Nationale des Affaires Foncières et Domaniales. It gave the directorate title registration, the cadastre, the digitisation of land documents and a national property database. In April 2026 the head of the national cartography unit said a land information system was in preparation as a national priority. A household and business addressing and directory programme launched on 19 December 2022 with an end-2023 target. In August 2024 the telecommunications minister said the country still had neither postal codes nor a precise way of locating places. In October 2026 the government approved a national postal-code booklet in principle. Since 15 October 2026 the United States Postal Service has required a five-digit postcode on mail to Comoros, at the request of La Poste Comores.

The Code général des impôts promulgated on 23 July 2011 created the unique taxpayer file. The electoral register is computerised and centralised under the Centre National de Traitement des Données Electorales. It carried 338,940 electors for the January 2024 presidential election. ANPI runs a public online business directory, filterable by island, sector, year of registration and RCCM number.

The computerised national population register, the land and cadastre registry under DNAFD and the electoral register are three separate builds standing on three separate legal instruments, not one converging system.

Sectoral management information systems

DHIS2 has been the national health management information system since 2022. Almost 99% of public health facilities used it for routine reporting in November 2025. A World Bank-funded electronic logistics project launched in 2024 put an Android Real-Time Stock tool into 58 of the country's 74 facilities, 78%, within three months. On 27 October 2026 the health statistics directorate told a coordination meeting that one of the eleven projects in the 2022 digital health plan had seen significant implementation. It cited financial constraints. The October 2025 measles-rubella drive, targeting 101,000 children, was the first national campaign to use ODK digital capture feeding real-time dashboards.

On 5 June 2025 the Ministry of Education and ANADEN launched e-Msomo for online grade entry, automated report cards and unique student identifiers, and e-Shiyo, a digital textbook library. They were education's first individual-level systems. By February 2026 every public secondary school and 45% of public primaries were on e-Msomo. Its digital report card had become the only document accepted for university enrolment.

By April 2025 the World Bank-financed safety-nets project had digitised every technical operation, from targeting to payment statements, on its own management information system. Further modules were due that July. In December 2025 the openIMIS-based Assurance Maladie Généralisée pilot began enrolling beneficiaries in Mohéli. It gave health insurance its first digital claims and enrolment layer. In May 2026 the scheme said the platform had run for a year in seven pilot zones, hosted on Amazon Web Services with two local developers.

From 15 to 17 April 2026 the Direction Générale des Impôts held a validation workshop on the legal corpus for téléprocédures fiscales. These cover online filing, electronic payment and paperless registration. The 2026 finance act imposes phased télédéclaration and télépaiement obligations on large and medium taxpayers, and leaves their implementation dates to regulation. The 2024 Ibrahim Index of African Governance scores Tax & Revenue Mobilisation at 15.2 out of 100, 51st of 54. It scores Budgetary & Financial Management at 26.4, 50th, and both fell over 2014-2023.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

ANADEN's own delivery log still marked the national scholarship portal e-Bourse « En cours » in 2025, after a technical meeting in June 2024. Comoros ranked 180th on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, with a score of about 0.25.

The African Development Bank's PADEC was officially launched on 27-28 March 2025. Its 2025 annual work programme and budget were validated that same month. Its June 2025 procurement notice covers an upgraded government portal, e-services, digital payments for the administration and an interoperability platform, with first bids from July 2025. The EU and Expertise France launched an e-Administration programme in October 2025 to build a single-window portal and an interoperability layer. The Islamic Development Bank issued 2026 procurement notices covering an interoperability and data exchange platform. All three postdate the Comores Numérique 2028 strategy's planned e-government platforms.

In 2025 the justice ministry launched a free online portal of laws, decrees, case law and circulars, searchable by theme and date. ANPI's public online business directory is the operative business-register interface. The tax administration is moving to téléprocédures under the 2026 finance act, with the legal corpus validated at an April 2026 workshop. Property tax is still settled by cheque or cash at the Trésorerie Générale (2026). The national ID system provides no digital authentication function, and no government portal authenticates users against it. The finance ministry's FY2025 framing letter told every ministry to digitalise budget management and extend paperless processing. Business & Competition Regulations rose 10.9 points over 2014-2023 to 43.4 out of 100, 39th of 54.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

Identity cards, produced only in Moroni, were to be made on Ndzuani from April 2026 under the new supplier.

In October 2025 the AMECC project launched a preliminary study on Mohéli for digitising the civil registry. A validation workshop for the preparatory studies was held in Moroni on 25 March 2026. In October 2026 the programme's second phase was reported 84% complete and extended by three months. It had mapped civil-registration centres nationwide and designed new forms. In late 2025 the implementing decree for a national civil-status agency was at the stage of island consultations.

In December 2022 the land-use ministry launched a programme to give every household and business on the three islands a street address and a directory entry. In October 2026 the government approved a national postal-code booklet in principle, and asked for the island governors to be involved.

Rural digital data capture

In March 2026 the agriculture ministry contracted IT equipment for the Centres Ruraux de Développement Économique at KMF 33,840,000. The centres are its decentralised agricultural extension offices.

By November 2025 routine health reporting ran through DHIS2 at close to every public health facility. An Android stock tool from a logistics project launched in 2024 had reached most facilities by then. The October 2025 measles-rubella drive was the first national health campaign to capture its data digitally, on ODK. In December 2025 a pilot of the openIMIS-based health insurance platform began enrolling beneficiaries and processing claims on Mohéli. In October 2026 the health statistics directorate said one of the eleven projects in the 2022 digital health plan had seen significant implementation. It cited financial constraints.

In March 2026 civil registration remained largely manual, its digitisation still at the study stage. In August 2026 the AMECC project consulted midwives, village chiefs, cadis and mayors on Mohéli about registration difficulties. By February 2026 the school platform e-Msomo had reached every public secondary school but only 45% of public primaries.

The Ibrahim Index's Rural Economy sub-category fell 15.9 points over 2014-2023 to 39.0 out of 100, 39th of 54. Rural Representation & Participation fell 48.6 points over the same years. DIMAKOM, an agricultural marketplace and information platform for Comorian farmers, launched in March 2022. It runs standalone, outside any government registry exchange.

Technology

AI

Comoros's first binding rules on artificial intelligence came in the information and communication code promulgated on 2 February 2026. The code requires explicit labelling of AI-generated or heavily modified content, and bans deepfakes and disinformation. It makes publishers liable for what they carry. It creates a technology-and-ethics monitoring cell inside the press and audiovisual regulator. The rules are backed by graduated sanctions and an awareness campaign.

The UNESCO readiness assessment presented in Riyadh in September 2026 places the country at an emerging stage. It finds no structured or general use of AI in public policy, and sets 23 recommendations to 2030.

In July 2026 DHIS2 Climate and Health predictive modelling was being piloted for malaria early warning in Ngazidja. The pilot had World Bank funding through the COMPASS project to June 2026. In May 2026 researchers reported a machine-learning malaria forecasting model built on the same data. On 19 August 2026 two locally built early-warning models pairing satellite imagery with trained AI agents were presented in Moroni. One watches Karthala's seismovolcanic behaviour, the other the spread of juvenile delinquency.

A first national AI forum was held on 31 March 2026, and AI Forward Comores followed in Moroni on 25 August 2026. Private consultancies ran AI Forward with the ministry and both operators. In April 2025 a foundation trained around 40 secondary-school pupils in AI. In January 2026 a diaspora scientists' webinar called on public research institutions to define a national AI strategy. Shikomori speech synthesis is held back by GPU costs, scarce paired text and audio, and too few translators (August 2026).

Artificial intelligence reached Comoros from unconnected directions between January and August 2026 — a webinar, a labelling rule, a language model, an Olympiad delegation, two research demonstrators and a health pilot — and only the health pilot has a named funder, a named end date and a data-sharing instrument behind it.

ICT Industry

Yas Comores launched 5G on 20 May 2025 on Huawei equipment. The state incumbent Comores Télécom followed on 2 June 2025 over WTTX fixed wireless. In February 2026 AXIAN's group plan confirmed 5G and fibre live in Moroni. Comores Télécom reported revenue of roughly KMF 17bn for 2024.

KomoraLink, a private business-to-business and e-commerce platform backed by the chamber of commerce, launched on 24 April 2026. DIMAKOM, an agricultural marketplace and information platform, has run as a standalone service since March 2022. A locally developed enterprise resource planning product was presented at the first national AI forum in March 2026. The investment agency ANPI took gold partnership of the 2026 AI Forward edition.

Company registration and competition rules were among Comoros's ten most improved governance indicators over 2014-2023. In 2024 nothing on the digital economy was published as open data.

Innovation ecosystem

The Technopôle numérique entered its pilot phase on 29 January 2026. The government presented it as a step towards Comorian digital sovereignty. The project runs from 2025 to 2028 with World Bank support, and has trained young people through a partnership with Huawei (January 2026).

In June 2024 the incubators Innov'Lab and OUSSAID took part in a European soft-landing programme for African incubators. By 2023 Innov'Lab, set up in 2017 by the union of chambers of commerce, had incubated dozens of enterprises. It had trained several hundred people.

The Wamanga wa Komor hackathon, launched in April 2026 under a four million euro EU programme, selected ten projects in its first phase. A hundred young people were trained. No start-up statute has been found.

In 2024 recorded startup activity was minimal and no venture capital investment was visible. Comoros is absent from the 133 economies ranked in the 2024 WIPO Global Innovation Index. It last carried a score in the 2020 edition. On 28 July 2026 a Comorian master's student launched a text-to-speech model for shikomori.

Capacity

Literacy

In May 2026 the press regulator CNPA and the Ministry of the Promotion of Gender, Solidarity and Information convened the EMICn colloquium in Moroni. Some 150 participants came from around twenty African delegations. Algorithmic manipulation was on the agenda, and a Déclaration de Moroni its intended outcome.

Comoros scored 48.9 out of 100 on education in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 33rd of 54 and up 0.9 points over 2014–2023. Enrolment scored 49.1, 17th on the continent after a 2.6-point rise (2023). Education quality fell 2.2 points to 36.8 (2023).

In 2021 electricity and internet access in primary schools remained limited, with rural schools worst served. In June 2025 the education ministry and ANADEN launched e-Msomo, for grade entry, report cards and unique student identifiers. They also launched e-Shiyo, a digital textbook library for pre-primary and primary pupils.

In February 2026 the CNPA paired its AI content-labelling rule with a public campaign, "Si c'est de l'IA, ça doit se voir". In April 2025 the Hazi Haki foundation ran a second cohort of AI training for secondary-school pupils. In September 2026 the African Development Bank announced financial and digital skills training for 25,000 women in five countries, Comoros among them. No national digital-literacy programme has been found.

Training and skills

In August 2026 Comoros entered the International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence for the first time. It sent two secondary-school students, chosen from four, to Astana under the Olympiad's GAITE programme for emerging nations. ANADEN, its ministry and ACTIC's academy made the entry together.

In June 2026 the African Forum for Utility Regulators ran a five-day course on mini-grid pricing and regulation. More than 23 staff from SONELEC, the energy ministry and the regulator attended, under UNDP and GEF's Africa Minigrids Program. In April 2026 INSEED trained staff in GIS cartography under STATCAP-KM, for the next census. That month officials also took advanced GIS training from the Regional Centre for Mapping of Resources for Development. In late 2025 civil-status agents joined island consultations on the draft decree for a new registration agency.

The University of Comoros' Institut Universitaire de Technologie is one of six establishments under PROFI, a EUR 21.5 million French grant for 2022 to 2028. PROFI covers five fields including digital, with thirteen new higher-education diplomas planned. The Technopôle numérique, a training hub, entered its pilot phase on 29 January 2026.

Décret n°26-070/PR of June 2026 requires installers of renewable self-production and mini-grids to hold a ministry certification. The certification is valid for three years. AI Forward Comores opened at the Palais du peuple in Moroni on 25 August 2026. Two private consultancies ran it with the digital ministry and the operators Yas and Comores Telecom. The minister named structured data, talent and Comorian content as foundations the country has to build.

Research institutions

On 19 August 2026 a seismologist at the Centre National de Documentation et de Recherche Scientifique presented two locally built AI early-warning models. Both combine satellite imagery with trained AI agents. One aims to detect changes in Karthala's seismovolcanic behaviour, the other to limit the spread of juvenile delinquency. The volcano tool was simulated with real data.

In May 2026 a malaria-forecasting model built by Comorian researchers on routine DHIS2 data was accepted for the 2026 DHIS2 Annual Conference. It tests machine-learning and deep-learning approaches to forecast incidence.

On 28 July 2026 a Comorian master's student launched Komori Tts, a speech-synthesis model for shikomori. Its developer names GPU costs, data volume and a shortage of translators for shindzuani, shimwali and shimaore as obstacles.

In 2026 the start-up Komor-IA published 1,424 French–Shingazidja sentence pairs as an open dataset under a non-commercial licence. On 26 September 2026 it launched a crowd annotation platform for names and parts of speech in shikomori. In December 2024 researchers built Comorian speech-recognition and translation prototypes by transfer learning from Swahili.

On 14 September 2026 the University of Comoros and UNDP signed a memorandum on training, research and innovation. At its centre is a UniPod space for students, researchers and entrepreneurs. In January 2026 a UONI diaspora webinar called on public research institutions to lead in defining a national AI strategy.

Inclusion

Access to services

Comoros scored 30.5 out of 100 for equal access to public services in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 37th of 54 African states. The score was 3.5 points down on 2014. Access to public services for women rose 2.7 points over the same decade, to 37.1 (2023).

A national payment switch went live in August 2025, interconnecting banks, microfinance institutions and mobile-money operators. In May 2026 a central-bank payment stack of KomorPay, Komor Switch and Mali Ya Wakazi was inaugurated at a presidential launch. The Banque Centrale des Comores coordinates a national financial inclusion strategy for 2025-2030. One of its four axes is financial education and consumer protection. In February 2026 the central bank licensed AXIAN as a regulated digital lender. Access to banking services is among Comoros's worst-scoring indicators in the same index (2023).

In January 2018 holders paid 5,000 Comorian francs for the biometric identity card. Production of biometric documents stopped for three weeks in March 2026 while the contractor changed over. Most landowners have not registered their property (2025). In 2025 ANADEN's delivery log still marked the national scholarship portal e-Bourse in progress, after a technical meeting in June 2024.

Under the World Bank-supported safety net project, vulnerable households have received grants and cash transfers through banks, microfinance institutions or mobile money (2023). In November 2026 the Registre social unique held 81,075 households across 152 of the country's 319 localities.

Digital divides

The deepest split in Comoros runs between its own islands: electricity access on Anjouan, at 49%, is roughly half Ngazidja's 95% and well behind Mohéli's 87% (2025). The mobile networks are uneven too. The regulator's 2024 quality-of-service audit found coverage gaps and told both operators to redouble their efforts. ANRTIC's director-general restated the authority's power to sanction, and put the difficulty down to the archipelago's relief. The regulator states that the ICT law established a universal service fund for digital-inclusion projects that are not immediately profitable (July 2025). No levy, accounts or project of the fund has been found.

Most Comorian primary schools had no internet connection in 2021, and around half no electricity, with rural schools furthest behind. Within each island, rural areas are worst served. Rural representation and participation is among the most sharply deteriorated of Comoros's ninety-six measures in the Ibrahim Index over the decade to 2023. The rural population has been losing voice in the arrangements that decide where investment goes.

Mobile coverage, at 77.6%, runs far ahead of internet penetration at 35.7% (early 2025). In 2022, 34.2 per cent of women aged 15 to 49 had used the internet in the previous three months, against 56.8 per cent of men. The constraint on a digital public service is rarely the handset, and usually the connection behind it. Language is a further filter, and one nobody is funding. The first speech synthesis for shikomori is held back by the cost of GPU compute and the volume of text and audio data needed (August 2026). It also lacks translators for shindzuani, shimwali and shimaore.

Data

National statistics

Comoros launched its fifth general population and housing census on 5 June 2026. A pilot enumeration was set for September 2026 and the main count for September 2027. In August 2026 a retreat finalised its methodological and digital instruments, replacing paper enumeration with tablets. In April 2026 INSEED trained staff in GIS cartography with ESRI under the STATCAP-KM programme.

The World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators record a population census within ten years and an agricultural census within twenty, but no business census (2023). They record three or more household surveys, two health surveys and two labour force surveys in the past decade. They record no agricultural or business survey. Products score 70-89% and data use 50-69%, against 20-49% for data infrastructure, the weakest pillar (2023). Data services and data sources return no score.

Capacity of the Statistical System rose 13.7 points over 2014-2023 to 29.6 out of 100 in the Ibrahim Index. It is Comoros's sixth most improved indicator, and still 49th of 54 African states. Eighteen of the index's 96 indicators carry no Comorian data (2023). They include every public-perception measure, education completion and rural market access.

INSEED was a partner in a 2022-2023 UN Joint SDG Fund programme for a national digital statistics repository. ANADEN, the health ministry, Customs and the planning commission were also partners. On 23 August 2026 INSEED's national microdata archive carried documentation and microdata for fifteen datasets. In August 2025 the IAWASuR water programme set out to install 49 automatic weather stations. Loi n°11-003/AU of 26 March 2011 makes INSEED the lead public statistics institute and creates a Conseil National de la Statistique. It sets statistical secrecy, response obligations and penalties for non-response or falsified returns.

Open data

The finance ministry publishes a beneficial-ownership list of public contracts (November 2025). For each award it names the contract title, the beneficial owners, the project or institution and the amount. The single amount column mixes currencies, marked only inline row by row. Most rows are in Comorian francs and a handful in euros, so the table cannot be totalled as it stands.

The Compte administratif for 2024 is dated 14 March 2025. It reports execution at ministry grain, roughly three months after year-end. It is organised by economic nature first, with a separate per-ministry table under each nature. It has no consolidated per-ministry outturn column, so a ministry's total execution can be had only by summing across tables. Appropriation, revision and execution appear together nowhere but in a state-aggregate table. The FY2026 framing letter carries both the economic and administrative budget nomenclatures across its 35 pages, where the seven-page FY2025 letter carried neither. The same letter is where provisional half-year 2025 execution figures appear (June 2025).

No access-to-information law has been adopted (January 2026). The code de l'information et de la communication is a press law. The Ibrahim Index puts Comoros 53rd of 54 for Disclosure of Public Records at 1.4 out of 100, down 18.3 points over 2014-2023 (2023). It puts Comoros 52nd for Accessibility of Public Records, at 3.1.

The 2024 Open Data Inventory's best Comorian scores are the built environment at 7.5 out of ten, and trade and poverty at 7. Balance of payments scores 6.5, and national accounts, prices, labour and reproductive health 6. Among categories with anything published, education facilities rate 2, health facilities 3 and government finance 4. Energy, pollution, natural resources, crime, food security, gender and health outcomes score zero (2024). So does the digital economy category covering ICT and telecommunications statistics, where nothing meets ODIN's criteria (2024). An official data portal, Open Data Comores, was presented at a UN country team meeting on 29 August 2024. No dataset count or licence for it has been found.

Geospatial data

INSEED has set up a digital cartography laboratory to map the whole territory ahead of the next census (October 2026). It works from tablets, satellite imagery and GIS software including ArcGIS Pro, bought under the World Bank's STATCAP project. In April 2026 officials took advanced GIS training at the Regional Centre for Mapping of Resources for Development, a regional African body. Fourteen staff from the three islands attended on World Bank financing. The head of the national cartography unit named a land information system as a national priority. The same month INSEED trained staff in GIS cartography with ESRI under STATCAP-KM, building the mapping base for census enumeration.

Climate data reaches the health information system under a data-exchange memorandum between the health ministry and ANACM, the national meteorological agency (July 2026). A malaria early-warning pilot in Ngazidja combines ERA-5 climate reanalysis with routine case data. The meteorological service publishes wind warnings graded by severity and named by district or island, ten of them between 29 July and 27 October 2026. In August 2026 a seismologist at the national research and documentation centre presented two locally built models combining satellite imagery with AI agents. One of them watches for changes in Karthala's seismovolcanic behaviour.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

The telecoms regulator ANRTIC banned the installation, testing, deployment and use of Starlink on 12 June 2025, stopping the most consequential American technology arrival in Comoros at the border. It acted on a ministerial circular signed three days earlier. It announced a field inspection to identify users already on the service. The regulator framed the ban as provisional, pending the grant of a licence. It required the operator to conform to Comorian law through the same official procedures as any other operator. The ban is a licensing question rather than a policy objection to satellite broadband as such. In December 2025 the regulator and customs at the international airport were reported to have seized Starlink equipment.

China activities

The Technopôle numérique entered its pilot phase at a launch ceremony on 29 January 2026. Ministers presented it as a decisive step towards Comorian digital sovereignty. Its young people were trained through a partnership with Huawei.

China Eximbank signed a CNY 567 million concessional loan, about USD 86 million, with Comores Télécom on 11 December 2018. The 2018 loan runs 30 years, with a five-year grace period, at 1% interest. It finances an USD 81 million Huawei contract to modernise the state operator and extend its network nationally (2018). The Nafassi fibre-to-the-home network the loan paid for launched on 22 March 2022. It extended fibre to 57 further sites and carries household broadband, television and fixed telephony.

Huawei Marine delivered the 260km Avassa cable in November 2016. It links Grande Comore, Anjouan and Mayotte for Comores Télécom and Mayotte's STOI. It gave Comoros a second international link after EASSy. Yas Comores launched 5G in Moroni on 20 May 2025 using Huawei equipment.

Nothing on the financing side comes close: the Chinese loan is more than three times the next largest commitment to the digital estate, and the remainder of the country-level money is mostly single commitments from multilateral lenders, alongside small grants (November 2026).

EU activities

In October 2025 an EU-funded e-Administration programme delivered by Expertise France was officially launched. It is to build a single-window service portal and an interoperability layer for the public administration.

The African Development Bank's PADEC also funds an interoperability and data-exchange platform (October 2024).

On 15 April 2026 a validation workshop opened on the legal framework for the tax administration's téléprocédures. The work is funded through the PAGF-SI project, with support from the Agence Française de Développement. In October 2025 the AMECC civil registration programme, backed by France and UNICEF, launched digitisation studies in Moroni and Mohéli. The studies were validated in March 2026. In October 2026 the programme's second phase was reported 84% complete. In April 2026 a four million euro EU programme launched the Wamanga wa Komor hackathon for young innovators.

Gulf/UAE activities

On 6 September 2026 three solar plants totalling about 20 MW were inaugurated on the three islands. The Abu Dhabi Fund for Development financed them with about AED 84.4m, and Masdar built them (September 2026). In April 2026 the Grande Comore plant was commissioned as a microgrid with battery storage.

The Islamic Development Bank committed about USD 7 million to the digitalisation project PADEC from 2025. The project is to build a coherent range of public digital services.

Bahrain's e-government authority signed a memorandum with ANADEN in June 2026 under its Global Digital Twinning Initiative. It aims to lift the country's UN e-government ranking. The first exchange session under it was held in October 2026. ANADEN attended the Global Assembly on the Governance of Artificial Intelligence in Riyadh in 2024. Comoros presented its UNESCO AI readiness assessment at the Riyadh AI-ethics forum in September 2026.

India activities

No Indian-financed or India-delivered digital programme was under way in Comoros as at September 2026.

Digital sovereignty

On 25 August 2026 Comoros validated a national data governance strategy for 2027-2031 whose first axis is data sovereignty. Under it the state sets out to control the production, storage, circulation, use and protection of the data its administration, citizens and economy generate. The minister presented it as a foundation for a future national AI policy. He took it to the Council of Ministers on 21 October 2026, alongside the takeover, operation and hosting of the state data centre. The strategy was built under the AUDA-NEPAD and German-cooperation Africa Data Governance Initiative, which aligns member states with the AU Data Policy Framework. No instrument fixing where state data is held or under whose law it is processed was in force as at October 2026.

The first public-administration data centre opened on 19 May 2025 under the World Bank-financed RCIP4 project. In December 2025 the finance ministry's AFD-financed PAGF-SI unit tendered for its own primary and backup data centres with network operations centres. The Islamic Development Bank's e-government project procures the interoperability and data-exchange platform, with a national data centre financed by the African Development Bank (March 2026). The central bank put its own data centre into production in 2025, with redundant power, controlled cooling and high-resilience network connectivity. In May 2026 the generalised health-insurance scheme said its data sat on Amazon Web Services. It was considering a local backup server and gradual repatriation.

The national payment switch was awarded to the vendor PayLogic in February 2025. GenKey has taken over identity-document production (March 2026). The terms on which the GenKey identity-document contract and the PayLogic payment-switch contract govern data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at September 2026.
