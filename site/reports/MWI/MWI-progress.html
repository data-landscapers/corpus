The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Over the twelve months to 6 August 2026 Malawi's identity register advanced and its governing instruments did not. Adult identity coverage rose from 12.5 million in May 2025 to more than 13.5 million (registration bureau). Public procurement went from voluntary to mandatory for all public entities on 1 April 2026 (procurement authority). Mobile money transaction value rose 94% to MWK 37.9tn (central bank report).

Against those, the Data Protection Act's twenty-four-month exemption elapsed in early June 2026 with no subsidiary instrument made and no enforcement action of any kind on record (draft regulations); the service bus did not enter service; and a national artificial intelligence strategy, a national digital transformation strategy, a local-authority ICT policy and a government data-sharing regulation all ended the period in draft.

The governing position for personal data changed in scope without changing in enforcement. At the start of the period the Data Protection Act 2024 had been in force for fourteen months with its exemption still running and no subsidiary instrument made; in October 2025 the government was tendering consultancy to design the authority's structure and enforcement framework (terms of reference). By July 2026 the exemption had elapsed, a head of data protection had been named, and draft regulations with a turnover-based fee schedule were out to consultation (consultation account). No fine, order, breach notification, complaint or prosecution under the Act appears in the base at either end of the period.

The rest of the section began the period as nothing and ended it as drafts. Both national strategies did not exist at the start; workshops began in September 2025 and both entered validation in February 2026, neither adopted. A government data-sharing regulation was proposed in June 2026 and not made (ministry account).

Two positions did not move at all. The electoral commission's refusal of machine-readable access to the voter roll, made in June 2025, stands unchanged through the September 2025 elections and after. And the competition authority's July 2025 order requiring a mobile-money operator to open its exclusive e-ticketing platform to other providers within a year reached its deadline in July 2026 with nothing on file recording compliance.

The largest infrastructure movement of the period was procurement rather than construction. In March 2026 the regulator was advancing procurement for more than 90 towers, against a requirement of more than 1,000 cited at the same meeting, and its Director General stated that universal service fund resources remain insufficient (procurement briefing). The tower line does not appear in the project's own appraisal document (appraisal document) — a divergence between appraised design and delivered activity the base cannot document.

Regulatory enforcement began where it had not existed. The tariff-notice requirement carried no record of enforcement at the start of the period; in July 2026 the regulator found both mobile operators in breach and ordered compensation (regulator order). No proposal for a new operator licensing round existed a year ago, and by 24 July 2026 the President had authorised the ministry to identify an investor (parliamentary account).

Satellite connectivity went from an enabling capability to a deployment: at the start of the period the base recorded only that technical assistance had prepared the regulator to enable low-earth-orbit services, and by June 2026 30 rural schools were connected (company announcement).

Identity coverage advanced steadily and its governing statute did not. Adult holdings rose from 12.5 million in May 2025 to more than 13.5 million (registration bureau), and the mobile credential moved from a successfully piloted wallet with no name or launch month to a named product scheduled for September 2026 (registration bureau). Birth registration went the other way: a dedicated campaign did not exist a year ago and now runs across seven phases in 25 districts, but of the MWK 17.8bn required only MWK 5.4bn was allocated (ministerial figures).

Public procurement is the period's cleanest advance, moving from a system built but not required of public entities to electronic-only for all of them from 1 April 2026 (procurement authority). No post-mandate uptake figure or tender count has been published.

Data exchange advanced in use and not in rules. The exchange platform went from delivered to in use by the revenue authority and the registration bureau, with 25 e-services being onboarded (e-government department), while the service bus that would carry government-wide exchange remained unoperationalised. The ICT minister's June 2026 account — that institutions still share citizen data by email, USB drive and paper, and that a binding regulation is the missing piece — is the clearest statement of the distance between the two (ministry account).

Payments grew fast on one measure and not on the one that matters for displacing cash: transaction value rose 94% to MWK 37.9tn, while merchant payments moved only from 6.9% to 7.7% of transactions (central bank report).

Malawi's artificial intelligence position was created inside this period and consists entirely of process. Nothing existed a year ago; consultative workshops began in September 2025, a draft strategy entered validation in February 2026, and a readiness assessment was validated on 22 July 2026 to guide it (validation account). No text has been adopted or published, and the assessment sits with the education ministry rather than the ICT ministry that owns the strategy.

Two rules arrived ahead of the strategy that would frame them. A statutory copyright body decided it will not register wholly AI-generated works, on a date the base cannot establish. And the Defence Minister stated in July 2026 that the defence force intends to adopt artificial intelligence in military operations and learn it from the Chinese army, a subject on which no Malawian doctrine, oversight body or budget line exists.

The sector's financing position also moved from nothing to a proposal: a four-year US$180m roadmap presented in China in June 2026, with funding structure and timeline not made public (ministry account).
