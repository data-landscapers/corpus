This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In February 2026 the Ministry of ICT's Department of E-Government and UNDP opened validation of a Draft National Digital Transformation Strategy and a Draft National AI Strategy at a Lilongwe workshop. A public feedback portal ran alongside it. The AI strategy had entered regional consultative workshops in September 2025, run with UNDP and the Public Private Partnership Commission. No adopted text of either strategy had been found as at August 2026. The government's own readiness assessment recommends developing a national cloud policy to guide government and private-sector cloud adoption (2025). There is no government open source policy and no recorded adoption programme in the public sector (2025).

In 2025 the World Bank recorded that a government institution responsible for coordinating digital transformation is established and a current national digital transformation strategy is in force. MACRA, which holds spectrum and licensing, cut radio-frequency licence fees by 20% across all bands in June 2025. It presented the cut as a digital inclusion measure aligned with the Malawi 2063 agenda. The National Registration Bureau's 2025–2030 Strategic Plan was launched in September 2025. It aims for every birth, marriage and death to be registered by 2030, resting on six pillars from universal civil registration to interoperability and civic trust. The Reserve Bank launched a National Payments System Vision and Strategy 2026–2030 in April 2026. It covers affordability, cybersecurity, resilience to power and connectivity failures, and regional integration.

The February 2026 State of the Nation Address directed all ministries, departments and agencies to digitalise passports, ID cards, driver's licences and procurement "forthwith". A digital identity wallet was then in testing. In July 2026 the ICT minister told parliament the government was considering licensing additional mobile network operators to improve competition and affordability. Technical assistance had prepared MACRA for that work by mid-2025. In August 2026 the finance minister committed the government and the Reserve Bank to secure, reliable and accessible digital financial infrastructure as cheques are withdrawn. He attached no coverage target and no date to the commitment.

Legislation and regulation

Parliament passed four Bills together on 6 August 2026. The Bills of Exchange (Amendment) Bill phases out cheques. Amendments to the Financial Crimes, Payment Systems and Deposit Insurance Corporation Acts remove their cheque provisions as a consequence. The financial crimes amendment also removes references to bearer negotiable instruments.

In July 2026 MACRA found Airtel Malawi and TNM in breach of section 76 of the Communications Act 2016. Section 76 requires an approved tariff change to be published in two daily newspapers seven days before it takes effect. The regulator ordered both operators to tighten their internal compliance procedures.

The Data Protection Act 2024 is the main data protection law, and the Electronic Transactions and Cyber Security Act 2016 regulates the processing of personal data electronically. The 2024 Act leaves its own commencement to the Minister by notice in the Gazette. The 2016 Act provides for electronic transactions, establishes the computer emergency response team and covers computer-related offences and electronic evidence. Draft regulations under it went to public consultation in February 2024, and no regulations made under it have been found. In October 2026 the ICT minister announced a review of the 2016 Act and the Communications Act.

A World Bank mission of September 2026 recorded that the government expects to table a Malawi Information Technology Authority Bill in November 2026 or by March 2027. The authority's creation is an unmet disbursement condition of the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project.

The Value Added Tax (Electronic Invoicing System) Regulations were published on 9 January 2026. The revenue authority later reset enforcement to 1 May 2026. The Tax Administration (Electronic System) Rules 2025 require every registered taxpayer to be registered on the electronic system for filing and settling tax. The Act ratifying the World Bank's Governance to Enable Service Delivery 2.0 grant charges payments on the Consolidated Fund and exempts it from Malawian taxes and charges. As at October 2026 no instrument had been adopted on critical information infrastructure, electronic signatures, e-government and cloud, artificial intelligence or open banking.

Data protection

On 17 October 2025 the authority issued draft regulations on the engagement of data processors, twenty months after the Act was gazetted. Guidelines on personal data breach notifications followed on 22 October. Nothing on record shows either instrument enforced.

Parliament passed Malawi's first dedicated data protection statute on 7 December 2023; it was assented on 31 January and gazetted on 2 February 2024. It provides data-subject rights, mandatory breach notification, an adequacy test for cross-border transfers and reach over processing of Malawian data from abroad. It imposes no localisation duty, though a controller must tell a data subject when data leaves Malawi. Sections 38 to 40 restrict transfers abroad absent adequate law in the receiving country or binding corporate rules.

Section 4 of the 2024 Act makes the Malawi Communications Regulatory Authority the data protection authority, with no standalone body. MACRA holds powers to issue compliance orders, receive breach notifications and handle complaints, and has published a Data Protection Handbook. Registration is mandatory for controllers processing the data of more than 10,000 subjects. In March 2026 it was working with World Bank support to make the authority and the computer emergency response team independent of the regulator. In July 2026 it put draft registration regulations and a fee schedule out to consultation.

In 2025 the World Bank found that neither the regime nor the authority publishes reports on its operation. In August 2024 a detective complained to the authority that her call logs and mobile-money records had reached a bank and its auditors through search warrants. The authority replied that it could not investigate, because the disclosure predated the Act's commencement on 3 June 2024 (July 2025).

In January 2026 the state used Data Privacy Week to declare compliance no longer optional. In July 2026 Airtel Malawi and TNM agreed under communications law to credit affected customers with bundles worth the price difference by 31 July. A consumer petition asked the regulator to investigate data billing and bundle depletion.

Regional collaboration

The SADC regional settlement system's April 2026 statistics record nine Malawian banks and 1,210 settled transactions. The COMESA Digital Retail Payments Platform reached user trials between Malawi and Zambia in October 2025. Deeper regional integration is one of the commitments in the Reserve Bank's payments strategy for 2026–2030, launched in April 2026. In customs, the revenue authority's system has exchanged declarations with Zambia's in real time at the Mwami-Mchinji border since November 2025. A link to Tanzania's customs system launched at Songwe in June 2023. Five customs administrations adopted a roadmap in August 2026 to extend such exchange across the region. The electricity utility signed an agreement in September 2023 to join the national fibre backbone to Tanzania's. No statement since has said the link is live. The national identity card is built to the ICAO standard and used by the Immigration Department as a secondary document at border points (2020).

Standards

In 2025 the World Bank recorded no government interoperability framework, no data-quality or service-availability standards for exchange, and no programme to bring legacy systems onto a common standard. The Reserve Bank's payments strategy commits to migrating the national payments infrastructure to the ISO 20022 messaging standard (April 2026). The National Local Government Finance Committee and the Department of E-Government issued ICT standards for councils alongside the first dedicated Local Authorities ICT policy, validated on 4 May 2026. Malawi has begun adopting the Universal DPI Safeguards Framework as part of its digital public infrastructure rollout (2025). In April 2026 the country's first integrated labour market information system was built around SDMX, the international statistical data-exchange standard, across six institutions. No other international data standard in use in government has been found.

The regional accreditation service renewed the Malawi Bureau of Standards's accreditation to certify quality management systems, effective 14 December 2025. The bureau holds full member-body status in ISO.

The Data Protection Act 2024 requires controllers "of significant importance" to register with the data protection authority. The duty reaches any controller processing the data of more than 10,000 subjects. The registration regulations and fee schedule were still out to consultation in July 2026. The Copyright Society of Malawi, a statutory body, will not register works created entirely by artificial intelligence and will not pay royalties on them (July 2026). No national policy on artificial intelligence had been adopted at that date.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Perceived freedom of speech is the second most deteriorated of Malawi's 96 IIAG indicators, down 41.6 points over 2014–2023 to 49.1 out of 100.0. It ranks 28th of 54 African states. Freedom of Expression and Belief scores 87.1 in 2023, 9th of 54 and up 4.7 points over the decade. Media Freedom scores 65.0, 13th of 54, after a fall of 2.9 points. Digital Freedom, covering online censorship, shutdowns and rights online, is among Malawi's ten best indicators at 82.7, 8th of 54.

Civil Society Space fell 25.9 points over 2014–2023 to 60.2, 21st of 54. It is the third most deteriorated of the 96 indicators. Deliberative and Participatory Governance, which measures consultation and participation in policy-making, stands at 64.2, 16th of 54. It fell 4.2 points over the same decade.

In 2025 every one of the twelve participation and feedback fields in the GovTech Maturity Index was recorded as absent for Malawi. In August 2025 the anti-corruption bureau launched a mobile application letting citizens report corruption by text, audio or image with anonymity and case tracking. In February 2026 the government opened a toll-free national short code for corruption, poor service delivery and abuse of office. Parliament and civil-society organisations met formally for the first time on 18 March 2026, launching a structured platform for engagement. None of the three publishes a count of what it receives or settles.

The two draft digital strategies were put to a validation workshop with a citizen feedback portal in February 2026. Pressure on the regulator over internet pricing came as an online petition in July 2026. The Open Government Partnership's review of Malawi's 2026-2029 action plan, published in 2026, records new public-participation commitments in extractive revenue management and climate action. An access to information law is in force (2025). Malawi's public records are published considerably better than they can be reached and used (2023).

Finance

Financial sustainability

The Digital Malawi Project is appropriated MWK 52.53 billion on the E-Government vote in 2026/27, against MWK 15.50 billion a year earlier (2025/26). A SADC Regional Statistics Project adds MWK 8.76 billion on the statistics vote (2026/27).

The domestically financed share of the digital lines is 55.9% of MWK 139.0 billion appropriated in 2026/27, down from 75.7% of MWK 106.0 billion in 2025/26, while the domestic side itself eased to MWK 77.7 billion from MWK 80.3 billion (2026/27).

The 2026/27 Financial Statement books the acceleration project in Development Part 1, the foreign-financed budget. It books the new financial management system, last-mile connectivity and police digital-radio projects in Part 2, the domestically financed one. The recurrent estimates print no financing source against their digital lines (2026/27). Among them, the National Registration Bureau's identity-management sub-programme is appropriated MWK 12.58 billion in 2026/27, up from MWK 8.31 billion in 2025/26. No outturn against the digital appropriations had been published as at September 2026.

The acceleration project runs for six years in all, from approval in July 2024. In August 2026 the ministry, saying government cannot carry the build alone, sought private investment in towers, fibre and the national data centre. MACRA said the Universal Service Fund it will keep drawing on for further tower sites is insufficient for the gap. No terms for any such partnership had been published as at September 2026.

The National Registration (Amendment) Regulations 2026, gazetted on 31 July, set fees for use of the identity register. They charge MWK 350 to MWK 500 per e-KYC verification and MWK 5,000 for a replacement card. An institution pays MWK 1 million for access to two years of register data (2026).

MoUs and other agreements

National Bank of Malawi and the ICT Association of Malawi signed a three-year memorandum of understanding in November 2025. It covers mentorship and incubation for Malawian innovators. No sum or delivery obligation is attached to it. In June 2026 Airtel Money and the state Agricultural Development and Marketing Corporation began paying farmers directly into mobile wallets. No memorandum behind that service has been published.

New investments

As at November 2026, about US$150 million of Malawi's digital finance — three World Bank operations — is approved but not yet recorded as disbursing. Against it, about US$215 million across nine commitments is active and about US$142 million across six has closed (November 2026). Thirty-one commitments are on the record as at November 2026, with start years spanning 2015 to 2026 and a converted value of about US$514 million. Twelve have no status recorded, eleven of them small grants committed in 2015 and reported to the OECD.

Parliament ratified the Governance to Enable Service Delivery Project 2.0 grant on 29 July 2026, US$80 million of IDA signed on 19 June 2026. It is SDR 58.2 million equivalent, inside a K229.5 billion package of two World Bank financing Bills (July 2026). None of the three gives the grant's digital share. In 2024 the Bank appraised the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project at US$90 million: a US$70 million IDA grant plus US$20 million of unguaranteed commercial finance.

The World Bank is the largest financier on the record to November 2026, with four commitments worth about US$222 million, about 43% of the total; Chinese state lenders come second, with China Eximbank and China Construction Bank accounting for three commitments worth about US$204 million. Connectivity has taken the largest subsector share to November 2026 — six commitments worth about US$347 million, 68% of the total — ahead of other government technology at about US$61 million. Twenty-four of the thirty-one commitments are grants, and borrowing is confined to connectivity infrastructure, most of it Chinese.

In 2018 China Eximbank was reported to finance the National Fibre Backbone Project and its second phase. China Construction Bank finances the Last Mile Connectivity for Rural Areas and National Data Centre project.

Germany's BMZ has an active EUR 28.35 million grant to 2028 for public financial management, the systems' share not stated. The EU has committed EUR 2 million and UNDP US$5.6 million to Inclusive Digital Transformation for Malawi over 2024-2028. The only domestic commitment is National Bank of Malawi's MWK 75.5 million for the 2025 Innovation Jam (November 2025).

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Wholesale bandwidth in Malawi fell from about US$460 per Mbit/s to under US$10 under the World Bank-financed Digital Foundations Project (June 2025). The regulator puts a gigabyte of mobile data at US$0.38 in July 2025 against US$27.41 in 2020. It credits spectrum-fee cuts and lower transit tariffs negotiated with Tanzania and Zambia.

Airtel raised its international fibre routes from three to five in November 2025, adding links through Tanzania and through Mozambique via Nacala. Its primary link through Mozambique had gone down for long periods over the 2024 festive season. TNM bought three further routes in May 2024, when undersea cable cuts had left it a single link via Zambia. In October 2026 the competition commission authorised TNM's MK23.5bn purchase of the failing Open Connect Limited's assets. They are its open-access wholesale fibre network, data centre and contracts. The condition is that wholesale and retail run as separate entities under independent board oversight.

An industry association report published in August 2026 puts fourth-generation coverage at 87 per cent of the population. The same report puts mobile-internet penetration at 12.5 per cent (August 2026). These are the association's own figures, not a regulator series. The Mo Ibrahim Foundation scored Malawi 9.0 out of 100 on internet and computer access in 2023, the second lowest of the 96 indicators it measures for the country.

TNM launched 5G in Lilongwe in July 2025, crediting Huawei for technical support. Airtel launched 5G in November 2025 and reported 111 sites across 10 districts by May 2026. MACRA is procuring some 90 to 100 towers under the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project (March 2026). It says the Universal Service Fund is under-resourced for the rural gap. The project's predecessor connected about 640 government sites and more than 83,000 students through the research and education network. Most schools still had limited or no connection in April 2026. In October 2026 the education minister said 2,000 primary and secondary schools would be connected under the acceleration project. He gave no timeline or cost.

Data Storage

In May 2026 Sparc Systems opened a data centre in Lilongwe pitched explicitly at ending Malawian reliance on offshore hosting. The country's first Tier III site is the National Data Center in Blantyre, opened in 2022. It was built through a partnership between the government and Huawei and inaugurated by the state president in July 2022. It hosted 78 virtual machines for ministries, agencies and private clients in February 2024. A second Tier III centre in Lilongwe is now the government's primary site, with Blantyre as its disaster-recovery site. A 36-month managed-services contract to run it was tendered in February 2025. A stop-gap expansion added 115 TB of storage at the Lilongwe centre in the first half of 2026, and the redundancy link to Blantyre was completed.

In September 2026 TNM was buying Open Connect's Tier III data centre at Kanengo, Lilongwe, to join its Limbe facility as one cloud offering. The competition commission authorised the wider purchase with conditions in October 2026. An edge data centre run by Converged Technology Networks has been live in Lilongwe since February 2026, built with Microsoft's Airband programme, USAID and the Internet Society Foundation. No hyperscale cloud provider maintains a point of presence in Malawi (2025).

In July 2026 the Boma Lathu platform was running on locally hosted servers under the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project. Each department retains ownership of its data and hosts it locally. Immigration services joined the platform on those terms. Proposed rules would route inter-agency requests through the National Data Centre. In 2025 Malawi had published no policy on where government data may be held. The government's own readiness assessment recommends developing a national cloud policy. The Data Protection Act 2024 sets conditions for cross-border transfer and imposes no in-country storage duty.

Energy

Electricity reached 15.6 per cent of Malawians in 2023, among the lowest rates in Sub-Saharan Africa. Access was 57.8 per cent of the urban population against 6.1 per cent of the rural population, a gap of about 52 points (2023). Access to energy is the lowest-scoring of all 96 IIAG indicators for Malawi, at 5.9 out of 100 in 2023. It ranks 52nd of 54 African states, up 0.7 points across the decade. Infrastructure as a whole is the worst of the country's 16 governance sub-categories, at 28.0 out of 100.

Hydropower supplied 1,694 of the 2,224 gigawatt-hours generated in 2023, 76 per cent of the total. ESCOM publishes load shedding as a rolling programme covering six groups across the Southern, Central and Northern regions. The published programme runs from August 2025 to June 2026. The revenue authority's electronic invoicing runs against blackouts and connectivity gaps (June 2026). TNM powers its most remote sites with solar.

Off-grid supply is governed by the National Energy Policy 2018 and delivered through the Malawi Rural Electrification Programme and the Rural Electrification Fund. A 2024 study counted 19 mini-grids with 26 megawatts between them. Several earlier mini-grids had been abandoned over financial and regulatory difficulties.

Technical Capacity

In 2022 the National Registration Bureau had trained thousands of graduates as registration officers and staffed offices in all 28 districts. It depended on donor support for card printers, consumables, connectivity contracts and IT security. The register itself is largely donor-financed. The work of standing up the data protection authority was out to consultancy tender in October 2025. By September 2026 the consultancy was under way, developing a five-year implementation plan for the authority.

The IIAG scores Malawi 56.2 out of 100 on effective administration in 2023, 23rd of 54 African states and up 2.6 points over the decade. Its public administration score of 49.9 fell over the same period. The universities teach computer science and cybersecurity to doctoral level. Developer groups such as Tech Malawi maintain public code repositories and run hackathons. In 2025 the state had no digital skills programme for its own workforce. A development programme reached 5,014 people through digital-skills capacity development in 2025. It does not state how many were public servants.

The older internet exchange has run since December 2008 under the internet service providers' association. Ten of its seventeen providers were peering at the national data centre by 14 November 2024. The Lilongwe exchange stood up the first part of a new leaf-spine switching fabric in December 2025. It took a sustainable peering infrastructure grant in February 2026 to raise port speeds and take more members. Neither exchange publishes a traffic figure, a member count or a port speed.

Cybersecurity

Malawi sits in the top tier of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index (2024). The placing rests on declared commitments, and the rules that would bind anyone are still unwritten. On 6 August 2026 the finance minister told Parliament that stronger cybercrime protection, customer sensitisation and faster reversal of erroneous digital transactions must all come through regulations. Those regulations are yet to be made, and no draft has been published for any of them.

In March 2026 MACRA said the data protection authority and the national computer emergency response team should become independent institutions. Both operate under MACRA, the telecoms regulator. The Data Protection Act 2024 directs breach notification to it within 72 hours. Work on the cybersecurity and data protection frameworks is carrying World Bank support (March 2026). The central bank's payments strategy for 2026-2030 puts cybersecurity and resilience to power and connectivity failure among its objectives.

In October 2026 the lands minister said the ministry's land information system had been hacked about two weeks earlier, exposing title deeds. At the same national cyber drill the justice minister said the government hoped to table a cybercrime bill in the February-April 2027 session. The ICT minister announced a review of the 2016 Act and the Communications Act. The information ministry's National Cybersecurity Strategy ran from 2019 to 2024. Its successor went to regional consultations in December 2025.

DPI

Data Exchange

By September 2026, 14 services had been digitised and onboarded onto Boma Lathu, the national data exchange platform, with 11 more in development. No governance framework was in place to drive their uptake. The Minister of ICT says government institutions still share citizen data by email, USB drive and paper (2026). The binding data-sharing regulation has not been issued. Boma Lathu was implemented under the World Bank-financed Digital Foundations Project. The government's own enterprise service bus was non-operational through 2025, leaving services siloed.

In 2022 the social registry exchanged data through APIs with the management information systems of the Social Cash Transfer Programme and the public works programme. The Business Registration Act gives the Malawi Revenue Authority standing online access to the business registration database. A firm must produce its registration certificate to obtain a taxpayer identification number. The National Registration Bureau offers e-verification and e-KYC to public and private bodies under bilateral memoranda. In August 2026 tax withheld by the copyright society on royalty payments was not reconciled automatically against a rights holder's taxpayer identification number. The holder must collect the documents and file them in person or risk being taxed twice.

In June 2026 the proposed Government Data Sharing Regulation would route every inter-agency request through the National Data Centre. It would mandate encryption in transit and at rest and set a common standard for system-to-system exchange. It would require binding data-sharing agreements with usage limits and full audit trails.

Digital Identity and CRVS

More than 13.5 million Malawians hold a national digital identity, about 99 per cent of everyone aged 16 and over (July 2026). Children are reached instead through birth registration: the Bureau runs civil registration and the national ID as one system. It issues every child a national ID number at birth, carried for life.

Two million voters were registered using offline digital IDs in 2024. Every registrant gives ten fingerprints and an ICAO-standard facial photograph, captured on solar-powered kits. The card carries a unique identification number, a QR code and a machine-readable zone.

The Bureau reports links with more than 33 public and private institutions, including the electoral commission, the civil service payroll, the revenue authority and mobile operators (2025). The tax link rests on a memorandum of understanding. Banks use the ID to verify customers against the register. The agriculture ministry uses it for social assistance, with around 1.5 million Affordable Inputs Programme beneficiaries verified biometrically against it (February 2026). The ID is accepted rather than required for public healthcare (2022).

On 16 September 2026 the Bureau launched the Nzika smartphone wallet, holding national ID, birth, marriage and death credentials. The same day the homeland security minister directed that national ID cards be issued without expiry dates. The wallet complements the plastic card and does not replace it: a person must already hold a national ID to self-register for the digital version. The system is primarily donor-funded, having drawn over US$73 million from UNDP, DFID, the EU, Irish Aid, Norway, USAID and UNICEF (2022). Government meets roughly 40 per cent of project costs and struggles with recurrent salaries, card production and IT maintenance (2022). A World Bank-financed contract for 2,400 biometric registration kits, of about US$5.09 million, was awarded in March 2026 to a Hong Kong-registered supplier (reported September 2026). The award was some 27 per cent above the estimate (March 2026).

The UN agency's own account of 10 October 2026 puts the registration application in pilot at 87 locations across five districts, with 250 planned by 2028. The application links civil registration to identity management.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Malawian mobile money moved K37.9 trillion in 2025, a 94 per cent rise on K19.5 trillion the year before. It carried 2.4 billion transactions, up 31.6 per cent (2025).

The interbank settlement system has run since 2002. NatSwitch launched ATM interoperability in 2015, point of sale in 2016 and real-time electronic funds transfer in 2022. The Reserve Bank joined the continental payment and settlement system in December 2023. Its 2026-2030 strategy schedules an e-KYC platform on the identity register, piloted with the switch in 2026-2027. It schedules a national e-payments gateway in nationwide use by 2028. Under the Payment Systems Act 2016 the Reserve Bank operates the settlement system and licenses payment, clearing and settlement systems. The retail switch is industry-owned and subject to its oversight.

Access to banking services is among Malawi's ten worst-scoring governance indicators, at 17.8 out of 100 in 2023. The national financial inclusion strategy targets 95 per cent of adults reaching formal financial services by 2028. In 2024, 50 per cent of adults held a financial account, below the sub-Saharan average of 55 per cent. The social cash transfer programme's electronic payments had reached ten of its 28 districts by mid-2024. The national e-payment gateway was one of the few government platforms delivered in 2025. In September 2026 the police introduced electronic payment of traffic fines at headquarters and across the six police regions, through commercial banks and both mobile money services. In August 2026 the Farm Input Subsidy Programme returned to manual coupons for the 2026-27 season. Technologists questioned the step on fraud and traceability grounds.

Registries

Roughly 4.6 million Malawian children still have no birth certificate (January 2026). Registering them needs K17.8 billion. K5.4 billion was allocated in the 2025/26 financial year for the first two phases, leaving K12.4 billion unfunded.

The registration bureau's 2025 strategic plan makes a national population register a stated pillar. The register would integrate the civil register and the national identity card. Birth registration still runs on paper forms, with distributed issuance at district level. At rural registration points the informant completes a paper form and a village head signs to verify it. The certificate is printed later at a facility or district office that has a printer. The first vital statistics report drawn from registration records was launched in April 2025. It puts completeness in 2022 at about 13.1 per cent for male deaths and 10.2 per cent for female, on 13,653 deaths registered. Malawi's civil registration score fell 25 points over 2014-2023 to 75.0 out of 100. That is the fourth most deteriorated of its 96 governance indicators.

Eighty-nine per cent of Malawi's roughly 1.6 million micro, small and medium enterprises were neither registered nor licensed (2019). About 11 per cent were both registered and paying tax (2019). In June 2026 the Companies, Registrations and Intellectual Property Centre replaced the Registrar General's department and extended online processing to stamp and estate duty. No count of registered businesses or processing time has been published on either side of the change. Formal land registration is concentrated in three district registries, at Blantyre, Lilongwe and Mzuzu, with most land remaining customary. A Land Information Management System was launched in February 2024. It reached the districts in March 2026, when an implementation support mission trained 647 district land officers. In October 2026 the lands minister said the system had been breached. The minister alleged that the intruders sold title deeds while impersonating ministry officials. Biometric registration from October 2024 to February 2025 put 7,203,390 voters on a roll linked to the national identity register. The social registry covers several million households with rollout continuing (2024).

Sectoral management information systems

By 20 July 2026, 8,552 taxpayers had registered for real-time electronic invoicing, against roughly 9,000 registered VAT operators. The system was established under the 2024 amendment to the Value Added Tax Act. The authority let the statutory deadline pass and rescheduled full enforcement and penalties to 1 May 2026. In use it is constrained by power blackouts and connectivity gaps. In October 2026 the authority said it was building its second integrated tax administration system in-house and was still seeking funding for it. Customs runs on ASYCUDAWorld, whose upgrade to version 4.4 was still in testing and pilot in May 2025. In August 2026 the authority credited its automated risk profiling with faster clearance at Mchinji and Kamuzu International Airport.

In 2025 rural clinics recorded on paper, with Health Passports as the primary medical record. Electronic health records had been feasible mainly in urban, private or larger government facilities. Health data reaches DHIS2 only because clerks transcribe it into paper facility registers that are aggregated and keyed in at district offices. In October 2026 the education ministry presented a five-pillar governance framework for its management information system, including a national data warehouse and offline-capable tools for rural schools. Collection still relies on paper returns and manual aggregation.

In 2025 the financial management system was in use on commercial software, covering treasury and budget preparation, at central government level only. There was a debt management system but no public investment management system. The system reached every ministry, department and agency in July 2021, and most were still transacting outside it in real time (2024).

In 2025 the social insurance system did not use the national identity number and did not exchange data with other government systems. Neither the judiciary's electronic case management system, in place since 2015, nor its 2024 e-court pilot is documented as linked to the national data exchange. In May 2026 government retook full control of the traffic information system from its foreign vendor to enable integration with tax, law enforcement and the national ID.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

A March 2026 directive made e-procurement mandatory for all public entities from April 2026. On 30 October 2026 the procurement authority set 31 December 2026 as the deadline to move onto the system, with no government funding from January 2027 for procurement outside it. By October 2026, 7,704 suppliers had registered, 9% of them small and medium enterprises. The procurement authority reported 216 of 250 targeted procurement plans published on the system at 5 October 2026. The procurement marketplace is integrated with the financial management system, though its integration with a cross-government data exchange is still under development. Malawi's national public service portal is informational only, publishing information about services without allowing most of them to be transacted (2025).

The Department of E-Government is onboarding 25 e-services across ministries, departments and agencies onto the single portal (July 2026). It adds immigration alongside the revenue authority and the National Registration Bureau. That puts immigration transactions on the same locally hosted national stack as the rest. Malawi scored 0.3753 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024.

Across the 149 GovTech Maturity Index fields recorded for Malawi in 2025, 76 report the item as absent and a further 20 as not assessed, leaving 53 that record something in place — so roughly two-thirds of what the index looks for either is not there or could not be established.

In 2025 there was neither an online social protection or benefits portal nor a government jobs and employment portal. The index also recorded no government citizen participation platform or feedback and grievance channel. A 2024 e-court pilot links a small number of court, prison and police sites for remote bail hearings. A real-time constituency development fund dashboard was launched on 31 July 2026. In July 2026 passport printing resumed in Mangochi, with SMS notification, an online portal and WhatsApp integration all deferred until the new system stabilises.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

The National Local Government Finance Committee and the Department of E-Government issued an ICT policy for Local Authorities, validated on 4 May 2026. ICT standards for councils came with it. Until then councils had no guiding ICT framework beyond the 2023 National Digitalization Policy. A 2022 situational analysis had found ICT support weak, ICT projects absent and ICT left out of council procurement.

In March 2026 Blantyre City Council partnered with NBS Bank to digitise its revenue and tax collection and let residents pay for council services electronically. On 31 July 2026 the Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development launched a real-time Constituency Development Fund dashboard in Lilongwe to track project implementation and spending nationally.

The second Governance to Enable Service Delivery project, ratified in July 2026, brings the municipal councils of Karonga, Kasungu, Luchenza and Mangochi into the performance-based grant system for the first time. It covers 32 councils, 28 district and 4 municipal, against 28 under its predecessor. It funds re-engineering of council planning, financial management, procurement and contract management, plus a performance dashboard and ICT equipment.

The Local Authority Performance Assessment sets eligibility for the grants (2026). Of the 28 district councils, 27 qualified under the 2025/26 assessment, with Dowa best-performing. Improvement in a council's score is a headline indicator of the new project. The number of councils with clean audit opinions fell to 18 from 22 in 2023/24. Nkhotakota drew an adverse opinion (2026).

Councils were reported in September 2022 to be operating on a financial system not upgraded since 2014. Their local network reached only chief executives' offices. The accountant general sought expressions of interest in May 2025 for a new council system with a local revenue module and bank links, on a 14-month build.

Rural digital data capture

In December 2025 farmers across 14 districts were entering their details into four platforms that do not talk to each other. The four are the meteorological department's PICSA climate service, One Acre Fund, the government Farm Input Subsidy Programme and ACE's Zaulimi market and warehouse-receipt app. Farmers log into each in turn and lose control of their data. Farming households and their land parcels have been registered and geo-mapped into a national agriculture management information system (December 2024). In June 2026 Airtel Money and the state marketing corporation Admarc began paying farmers directly into mobile wallets and moving commodity payments onto electronic rails.

Health data from rural facilities is transcribed from patient-held Health Passports into paper registers and keyed into DHIS2 at district offices (2025). Digital point-of-care record-keeping in rural clinics has gone no further than pilots. The 2023-2027 education compact still lists digitalising education among its reforms (2024). A birth is still reported on the NR8 form, signed by a village head, with certificates printed later wherever there is a printer. The tablet-based Nzika App meant to replace that chain was a pilot in Lilongwe district in June 2025. By 10 October 2026 the registration application was in pilot at 87 locations across five districts, with 250 planned by 2028.

The Malawi Electoral Commission put 6,500 electronic devices into the field for the 16 September 2025 elections. The devices went offline in rural areas. In 2025 nine in ten respondents to the government's readiness assessment rated broadband coverage in rural and remote areas average, poor or very poor. The Government Wide Area Network that carries the financial, payroll, traffic and health systems faces limited funding and maintenance constraints. The assessment recommends community network models to extend coverage.

Technology

AI

No artificial intelligence was in use in Malawi's citizen-facing government service and feedback channels in 2025. A first national AI strategy went into regional consultation in September 2025. It reached a validation workshop in February 2026 alongside a draft digital transformation strategy. No adopted text of either had been found as at August 2026.

In 2026 the National Data Centre that carries the government's data exchange was being upgraded to an AI-ready state under the World Bank-funded Digital Malawi Acceleration Project. The Ministry of ICT has run AI training for Principal Secretaries. No AI capability runs in the exchange itself.

The meteorological services department hosted a pilot applying artificial intelligence to early warning from May to October 2025. In July 2026 the Malawi University of Science and Technology commissioned an AI fertigation system at its Thyolo campus that doses irrigation and fertiliser from live crop data. In June 2026 a Malawi Low Resource Language Data Trust was launched to assemble licensed Chichewa content as training data, with World Bank and Gates Foundation support. The one AI system found operating in a public service is in health. AI transcription of photographed paper HIV testing registers was in use at 98% of 867 testing sites by September 2024. In September 2026 the communications regulator recommended AI-powered cameras across Blantyre to the city assembly. No cost, supplier or timeline was published.

ICT Industry

In 2025 Malawi had no startup or innovation law, no government startup financing scheme and no SME support programme for the technology sector. Venture funding into Malawian startups is negligible (2024). The technology sector operates largely without formal startup investment (2024).

ICT-services exports more than doubled between 2020 and 2023, making digital services one of the country's growing sources of external earnings. In June 2026 Sparc Systems put forward a blueprint for exporting platforms it builds locally: anti-money-laundering compliance, student records, farmer payment systems and CRM. The same firm opened a commercial data centre in Lilongwe aimed at ending reliance on offshore hosting (May 2026). Most Malawian firms still keep their data offshore, at a cost in scarce foreign currency. Organised developer groups such as Tech Malawi and Golang Malawi maintain public code repositories and run hackathons (2024).

In July 2026 the ICT minister conceded on the record that foreign-exchange shortages are constraining operators' imports of network equipment. In November 2025 TNM named the same shortage as the limit on its network and innovation spending. Malawi scores comparatively well on business and competition regulation (2023). The government's own large build runs on donor money: the national data centre upgrade is financed under the World Bank-funded Digital Malawi Acceleration Project (2026).

Innovation ecosystem

In November 2025 National Bank of Malawi committed K75.5 million to the 2025 Innovation Jam, run with the ICT Association of Malawi on the theme "AI for Sustainable Development". The commitment sits under a three-year memorandum with the association covering mentorship and incubation.

In 2024 a small set of technology hubs was active, including mHub, the MUBAS Innovation Hub and Mzuzu E-Hub. The MUBAS hub was fitted out under a UNDP grant running to 2026. National ICT Innovation Awards were held in April 2026. In April 2026 PayChangu was running an SME payments aggregator offering APIs, Shopify and WooCommerce plugins and hosted payment links. On 30 September 2026 the Reserve Bank licensed it as a payment service provider for payment aggregation.

In 2025 the government reported a public-sector innovation programme of its own. No mechanism for public-private collaboration on government innovation was recorded (2025). There is no open source policy or public-sector adoption programme (2025). Malawi's Global Innovation Index profile puts it in the bottom quartile of ranked economies (2025).

Capacity

Literacy

The supply and qualification of teaching staff scores 86.1 out of 100 (2023), one of the country's ten best governance indicators. Completion stands at 29.8 and enrolment at 38.6, the latter down 3.2 points over the decade to 2023. Education is one of Malawi's weakest sub-categories, at 48.9 and 33rd of 54 African states.

Equality in education rose 11.5 points to 46.6 and education quality 7.0 points to 51.9 (2023). The quality score puts Malawi 14th in Africa.

In 2023 an inclusive digital economy scorecard put general-population digital literacy at 18 per cent and the overall skills component at 37 per cent. No later national measure has been found. In 2025 no government programme taught digital skills to citizens or schools, and nothing was published on the digital skills of the public service. A free digital-learning platform was launched in October 2025. In February 2026 an advocates programme certified 30 university students, each set to train 100 community members. In July 2026 rights advocates including the Centre for Human Rights and Rehabilitation placed digital literacy among the risks of moving the national identity credential onto a smartphone.

The BEFIT tablet programme, running in primary schools from 2023 to 2029, supplies foundational literacy and numeracy software and captures no administrative school record. The records themselves are still kept on paper (2024).

Training and skills

112 of about 180 civil servants completed a six-month digital skills course at the Malawi School of Government in November 2023. The e-government directorate put those trained with the Tony Blair Institute at more than 120 in September 2024. In 2025 there was no public-service digital skills strategy and nothing published on public-service skills.

Fewer than one in ten of the country's tertiary graduates were in ICT fields (2022). The University of Malawi, the Malawi University of Science and Technology, the Malawi University of Business and Applied Sciences and Mzuzu University all run computer science, IT and cybersecurity programmes from bachelor's to PhD level.

In January 2026 the Airtel Africa Foundation's Nzeru Fund began carrying 25 low-income students through undergraduate degrees at the Malawi University of Science and Technology. The degrees are in IT, computer science, software engineering, data science, artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. In July 2026 the defence relationship with China extended into training.

In April 2026 most of Malawi's roughly 9,000 schools had limited or no internet access. The World Bank was separately connecting more than 200 schools, outside any budget line for school connectivity. In August 2026 the CTN initiative carried free long-term high-performance connectivity for more than 20 secondary schools in Lilongwe. The regulator's Universal Service Fund is building 75 ICT laboratories in community day secondary schools under the Connect-a-School project (2024). In 2023 only 241 of 1,610 secondary schools offered computer studies. In October 2026 the education minister said 2,000 primary and secondary schools would be connected under the Digital Malawi Acceleration Project.

Research institutions

In July 2026 the Malawi University of Science and Technology commissioned an AI fertigation system at its Thyolo campus, automating irrigation and fertiliser dosing from real-time crop data. The same project runs an AI-assisted smart tree nursery producing drought- and flood-resilient seedlings under the TagDev 2.0 African agricultural-universities programme, led by Dr Jabulani Nyengere. Both are single-campus installations.

UNDP is funding a US$1.88 million grant, active over 2019-2026, to equip the Malawi University of Business and Applied Sciences innovation hub for ideation, prototyping, mentorship, incubation and testing. The four main universities teach computer science and cybersecurity to doctoral level. More than 83,000 students were connected through the Malawi Research and Education Network (June 2025).

In February 2026 the research and education network launched a national federated repository with digital object identifier support. In April 2026 a university began a twelve-month project evaluating large language models for primary-healthcare settings with the health ministry and the medical council. The country's one dedicated digital-policy think tank is a not-for-profit company whose stated mission is independent advocacy, consultancy and research. None of the three publishes a spend, a headcount or an output count.

A Malawi Low Resource Language Data Trust launched in June 2026 to gather licensed local-language content as training data for Chichewa AI models. The World Bank and the Gates Foundation support it, and digital assistants, translation and education are the named applications.

Inclusion

Access to services

On 21 August 2026 the ICT minister called for the 10 per cent surtax on internet services to be scrapped and the 17.5 per cent VAT on smartphones reviewed. He set a target of lifting mobile internet use from 12.5 per cent to at least 30 per cent by 2030. MACRA is evaluating Universal Service Fund mechanisms to subsidise consumer devices and fund digital-literacy work, including a proposed one-tablet-one-student programme. No budget measure or bill giving effect to the call has been found.

In July 2026 more than 885 people signed a petition in about a day asking MACRA to review internet prices. Days earlier the regulator had approved tariff increases well below what the two operators had asked for. The petitioners also asked it to look into data-billing and bundle-depletion practices and publish what it finds. In late September 2026 MACRA opened an investigation into the depletion of data bundles on both mobile networks. It is testing whether the data deducted matches the data used.

Banks have signed memoranda with the bureau for know-your-customer verification against the national ID (2022). The ID is also used for social assistance, including biometric verification of Affordable Inputs Programme beneficiaries. In 2023 a partnership between Centenary Bank and Crown Agents Bank held 17,420 refugee accounts at Dzaleka, a camp of roughly 49,000 people. Non-nationals may register for a national ID only on a valid immigration permit.

In 2025 the national services portal was informational, not transactional, and there was no social protection or benefits portal. A 2024 study found beneficiaries travel long distances to the nearest mobile money agent, at high transport cost. Malawi scored 36.4 out of 100 for equal access to public services in 2023, 30th of 54 African states. Its Inclusion and Equality score of 44.4 fell over the decade to 2023.

Digital divides

Only 54 per cent of Malawian adults owned a mobile phone of any kind in 2023. In September 2026 the ICT minister put smartphone ownership at 33 per cent of the population and basic internet skills at 12.5 per cent. A mobile broadband basket cost 9.4 per cent of gross national income per capita in 2022. Malawi's score for internet and computer access and affordability sits near the bottom of its whole Ibrahim Index profile.

In 2024 the gap between women and men was 15 percentage points in mobile internet use. The equivalent gap in mobile money narrowed in 2025. The identity register's enrolment ran majority-female in the quarter to June 2026.

The urban electricity access rate is many times the rural one (2023). In August 2026, as Parliament took the Bill withdrawing cheques, an MCP member warned that rural Malawi risks being left behind unless government fixes patchy internet coverage. Sosten Gwengwe, chair of the joint committee that reported the Bill, told the House that "someone in a village should be able to send or receive money without being blocked by poor connectivity". He cited unreliable debit notifications and the difficulty of reversing failed transactions.

In April 2026 about 900 of Malawi's roughly 9,000 schools had no internet at all, and most of the rest had limited access. In July 2026 rights advocates including the Centre for Human Rights and Rehabilitation warned of equal-access, rural-exclusion and digital-literacy risks in the move to a smartphone-based digital ID.

Data

National statistics

In 2023 the World Bank rated Malawi above 89% on data use and 70-89% on data products. It rated data sources and data infrastructure at 20-49%, with data services in between (2023). A population census and a business census had both been run within the last ten years, and the most recent agricultural census within the last twenty (2023). Over the decade to 2023 the country ran three or more household, health and business surveys each, two labour surveys and a single agricultural survey.

The 2018 census counted 17,563,749 people. The next is in preparation for 2028, with geospatial modelling for enumeration-area planning. The 2024 Demographic and Health Survey and the 2024-2025 integrated household survey were both published by mid-2026. The statistics office published its fortieth statistical yearbook in 2023, drawing on the health and education information systems and the road safety database alongside census and survey data. An external review that September found users rated its website outdated, and recorded that a planned release calendar had not been achieved.

The Mo Ibrahim Foundation's Capacity of the Statistical System indicator scores Malawi 48.1 out of 100.0 in 2023, 27th of 54 African states and up 2.2 points across the decade. The bureau's own plan makes death and marriage registration the basis of national vital statistics (2025). The first vital statistics report built from registration records, covering 2022, was published in 2025. A 2025-2027 project with the Ministry of Finance is building a harmonised management information system to pull sector administrative data together for planning.

Open data

Malawi ranks 3rd of 54 African states on disclosure of public records, scoring 57.7 out of 100.0 after a 19.3-point rise over the decade to 2023. It ranks 22nd, at 33.9, on whether disclosed records can be reached and used. In September 2025 the open government secretariat launched a national open data portal for budget, project and service-delivery data. The announcement does not link to the live portal, and states no dataset count or licence. In 2025 there was no government data governance institution and no government data strategy. Nothing was published on how the state governs its own data. The only instrument titled an open data policy covers extractive-industry data. The digital transformation coordinating institution, the national service portal, the e-procurement system and the debt management system all record no public reporting of usage or performance (2025).

In 2025 an access to information law was in force and assessed as effective. It gives the public a right to request government records. In September 2026 65 per cent of 30 requesters surveyed said they had no answer inside the statutory 15 days, and 46.7 per cent gave up. Requests filed with ten public institutions for the same investigation produced the information sought in no case.

On Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory, Malawi's economic and financial statistics all sit in the 3-5 out of 10 band. Population statistics score best, at 5.5, ahead of health outcomes and reproductive health at 5. Agriculture and land use and natural resources score 3.5, and the built environment 4.5. Where publication is routine it is sectoral, as with the Reserve Bank's quarterly national payment systems reports (2024). On 31 July 2026 the Ministry of Local Government and Rural Development opened a real-time Constituency Development Fund dashboard, letting government and the public track CDF project implementation and spending nationally.

Geospatial data

In August 2025 the climate change and meteorological services department installed the 2025 generation of the regional satellite reception system. It gives the department access to third-generation geostationary products, and its staff took operational training on it that October. Its earlier Green Climate Fund project, M-CLIMES, had built a data integration system at the department merging numerical products with satellite information (November 2020). The system sits beside 33 automatic weather stations (November 2020). Separately the agriculture ministry uses a satellite-based in-season yield model. The national committee on crop estimates has recommended incorporating it into the national crop-estimate survey. The meteorological department piloted the WFP-supported PRISM drought tool in five drought-prone districts (August 2025). The tool combines satellite imagery and vegetation indices with socioeconomic data. No national earth-observation strategy or budget line has been published. Nothing connects the meteorological capability to the agricultural use that would be its most obvious domestic customer.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

USAID's Support for Scaling up Mobile Money in Malawi ran from 2012 to 2018. In 2022 the US Trade and Development Agency funded a feasibility study on expanding fixed wireless and fibre broadband access networks. No US hyperscaler held a data-centre region, a cable landing or a government hosting contract in Malawi as at August 2026.

On 11 February 2026 Malawi signed a specimen-sharing agreement within a five-year US$936 million bilateral health cooperation package. The United States embassy says specimens will be shared only during public health emergencies and outbreaks. It says the agreements give no access to personally identifiable information.

China activities

In July 2026 the Defence Minister told a People's Liberation Army anniversary event in Lilongwe that the Malawi Defence Force intends to adopt artificial intelligence in military operations. He said it would learn it from the Chinese army. Both governments described a relationship that now runs into specialised training, equipment supply and institutional development. Further equipment and command-level training were under discussion. No contract or timetable has been announced.

Malawi's first Tier III data centre, the National Data Center in Blantyre, opened in 2022. It was built through a partnership between the government and Huawei and inaugurated by the state president in July 2022. Huawei was credited with technical support when TNM launched the country's first 5G service in Lilongwe in July 2025. The first phase of the national fibre backbone was built on a Chinese government concessional loan. China Construction Bank is financing last-mile connectivity for rural areas together with the national data centre.

Chinese state lenders are the second-largest source of finance for Malawi's digital estate, behind the World Bank and ahead of every other financier, across three commitments.

In June 2026 the ICT Minister presented a proposed four-year roadmap with Huawei. It covers a one-stop digital-government portal for passports, business registration and tax. It also covers a 28-district ring-protected network to carry the Boma Lathu platform. Its funding terms have not been published.

EU activities

Germany's BMZ has an active EUR 28.35 million grant running 2022 to 2028 for public financial and economic management. It works on the legal framework for public financial administration and on the new Integrated Financial Management Information System. The share for the system is not split out. The European Union's own EUR 2 million contract under Inclusive Digital Transformation for Malawi is active from 2024 to 2028. Its stated objective is to strengthen democratic governance and the social contract.

In July 2026 Malawi's national identity programme was run by the National Registration Bureau with sustained external support from UNDP, the European Union, Norway and Irish Aid. On 21 August 2026 the Union launched a EUR 143 million annual action plan for Malawi. It followed a fourth partnership dialogue, four months earlier, covering new gateway investments. The plan's digital share has not been stated. No European-owned network, data centre or hosting contract has been found.

Gulf/UAE activities

Five memoranda were signed during an Emirati ministerial visit to Lilongwe in May 2025. One was a digital-learning cooperation agreement between a Gulf school programme and the government. In November 2025 Malawi launched a Dubai-based climate-data platform built on machine learning and distributed ledgers at the climate conference. Both accounts come from the counterparty's side. No Malawian source states what either commits the country to, what data the platform holds or where. No stake, contract or hosting position in the estate held by a Gulf state, sovereign fund or Gulf-owned operator has been found.

India activities

India's 2025 bilateral brief names the tele-education and tele-medicine network programme and a power-sector memorandum as the standing engagements. Neither carries a delivery figure. In April 2026 officials met for a demonstration of India's unified payments interface, and no memorandum was signed. In September 2026 Malawian officials joined a UNDP learning mission to India to study its digital public infrastructure.

On 29 September 2026 India's High Commissioner reviewed an e-KYC and Digital Identity Project in Malawi with UNDP and UNCDF. The project is funded through the India-UN Development Partnership Fund and implemented with the Reserve Bank of Malawi. The meeting discussed its achievements, findings and transition. No Indian company has been found holding a stake, contract or hosting position in the estate.

Digital sovereignty

In May 2026 the transport ministry retook the traffic information system by changing the passwords. The system, built by South Africa's Fischer Consulting with the local firm Movesa and launched in 2015, was due to pass to the road traffic directorate in 2017. A former Attorney General had blamed the design of the contract for letting a foreign firm capture the system (2023).

The National Registration Bureau moved the national identity system's servers to the new National Data Centre in Lilongwe in January 2026. That centre runs on Nutanix hyperconverged infrastructure, with the Blantyre centre as its disaster-recovery site (2025). Its 36-month managed-services contract requires documented knowledge transfer and a handover to the government's own technical team. In April 2025 the Blantyre centre was operated by the ministry, the power utility ESCOM and Huawei. The terms of the Huawei partnership behind the Blantyre centre, and of the proposed Huawei roadmap, on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at September 2026.

Draft regulations of October 2025 would let a data processor move personal data out of Malawi only on the controller's written instruction and under the Data Protection Act's transfer rules. No instrument fixing where state data is held, or under whose law it is processed, was in force as at September 2026.

In September 2026 UNDP reported country-hosted GPU capacity procured and deployed with Malawian public institutions, one of six African countries in its programme. Farmer advisory services and Chichewa language models are among Malawi's proposed uses. In August 2026 Malawi's customs administration adopted a roadmap for customs-to-customs data exchange with four SADC neighbours. Legal authority and data standards were still unresolved.
