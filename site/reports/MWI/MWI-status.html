Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Malawi holds one digital system close to complete and a set of governing instruments that are not. The national identity register carried more than 13.5 million adult identities by July 2026, about 99% of those aged 16 and over (registration bureau). The Data Protection Act 2024 has been in force since 3 June 2024, but the statute exempted controllers and processors not of significant importance for twenty-four months, and that exemption elapsed in early June 2026 with the registration regime still in draft consultation (draft regulations).

The distance between built systems and the rules governing them is the defining feature of the record. Public procurement became electronic-only from 1 April 2026 (procurement authority), while in June 2026 the ICT minister stated that government institutions were still sharing citizen data by email, USB drive and paper (ministry account).

The record is thinnest where it would need to be strongest to test any of this: nothing is held on energy supply to networks, on technical capacity or on digital literacy, and the regulator publishes no sector statistics the wiki holds.

Connectivity policy is set by statements rather than by published instruments. The regulator cut spectrum licence fees by 20% in June 2025 (regulator announcement), but no fee schedule or gazette notice is held, so neither the base amounts nor the date the reduced fees took effect can be stated. The ICT minister told Parliament on 17 July 2026 that the reduction had been 50%; five contemporaneous accounts of the regulator's own announcement give 20% (parliamentary account).

Market structure has not moved. What the regulator did enforce in the window was notice, not price: it found both mobile operators in breach of the tariff-publication requirement and ordered compensation, while the approved increase itself stands (regulator order).

Rural coverage rests on a tower programme the appraisal document does not contain. The regulator was procuring providers for more than 90 towers in March 2026, against a requirement of more than 1,000 cited at the same meeting (procurement briefing); the project's own appraisal carries no tower line (appraisal document).

Only 3% of web content consumed in Malawi is served from within the country and 62% regionally, mainly from South Africa — the lowest local share among the economies measured (content-locality measurement).

The identity layer is the one part of the record stated with confidence: more than 13.5 million people held a national identity by July 2026, about 99% of those aged 16 and over (registration bureau). A mobile credential was announced for September 2026 (registration bureau); a separate blockchain wallet is in pilot under a United Nations programme, and neither names the other, so whether they are one product or two is unestablished.

About 4.6 million children remain without a birth certificate, against a campaign requiring MWK 17.8bn of which MWK 5.4bn was allocated for its first two phases (ministerial figures).

The national data centre and exchange platform were delivered under the foundations project (project results), and 25 e-services were being onboarded by July 2026, the immigration department integrating while retaining ownership of its data (e-government department). The data-sharing regulation the ICT minister proposed in June 2026 to govern that exchange is not made (ministry account), and the service bus intended to carry it was not operationalised through 2025, leaving passport registration and driver's licence payments unavailable online.

The ministry states that the constituency development fund dashboard, launched on 31 July 2026, carries progress, budgets and locations for more than 2,000 projects; no address for it is established (ministry account). The launching minister named the President, the finance and information ministers and the Office of the President and Cabinet as having real-time access, and said the dashboard depends on councils uploading data manually, warning of no excuse for late uploads (minister's remarks).

Mobile money carried MWK 37.9tn in 2025, but merchant payments were 7.7% of transactions against airtime at 42%, which the central bank reads as continued cash substitution (central bank report).

The Data Protection Act 2024 commenced on 3 June 2024 and designated the communications regulator as data protection authority (the Act). Sixteen months later the government was still tendering consultancy for the authority's organogram, staffing and enforcement framework (terms of reference).

The twenty-four-month exemption elapsed in early June 2026. By then the regulator had published draft regulations and a turnover-based fee schedule from MWK 50,000 for small and medium enterprises to MWK 7 million for the largest controllers and processors, consulted on 13 July 2026 (consultation account). No commencement date for the regulations is on record, and the day the exemption fell cannot be settled: the circulating expiry of 2 June 2026 implies a 2 June 2024 commencement, and the notice is not held.

No fine, order, breach notification, complaint or prosecution under the Act appears in the base, two years after commencement. Malawi's bilateral health agreements with the United States, and the data-sharing agreements attached to them, are withheld and in litigation since 2 April 2026.

A national digital transformation strategy and a national AI strategy, the latter backed by an AI Bill announced on 6 August 2026, both entered validation in February 2026; a local-authority ICT policy was validated in May 2026 with no adoption date; and the proposed data-sharing regulation is not made (ministry account). Parliament passed the Financial Crimes (Amendment) Bill No. 19 of 2026 in the week to 6 August 2026, removing references to cheques and bearer negotiable instruments; assent and commencement are not on record (parliamentary account).

Malawi holds no state-published artificial intelligence document. Every position the wiki carries is known through reporting of workshops and ministerial statements rather than through a text. A readiness assessment run by the education ministry with UNESCO was validated on 22 July 2026 and put 27.7% of the population online against household computer ownership of 4.2% (validation account). The strategy it is meant to guide remains pre-adoption, and the assessment sits with a different ministry from the one that owns it.

The sector's own measures are equally thin. ICT services exports rose from about US$70m in 2020 to US$154m in 2023, on a private-sector managing director's account at an industry expo rather than an official statistic. No statistical release gives the sector's share of gross domestic product or its employment.

The largest technology proposal on record is unsigned: a four-year roadmap presented to Huawei in China in June 2026 seeking US$180m, covering a one-stop government platform and a network linking all 28 districts, with funding structure and timeline not made public (ministry account). Against that, one dated position of a different kind stands: of five countries studied in 2023, Malawi was the only one recorded as having rejected the safe-city surveillance package offered by Chinese companies, though the base holds no account of who rejected it or on what grounds.

A statutory copyright body has meanwhile set an authorship rule ahead of the state, declining to register works created entirely by artificial intelligence, with no published test for how much assistance is admissible and no decision date established.

The measures that would show whether Malawi's digital systems reach people are largely absent, and where they exist they are unflattering. The most recent national internet-penetration figure the wiki holds is around 24 users per 100 inhabitants, from a May 2024 appraisal on a 2022 baseline (appraisal document). No measured value for any date inside the past year is held, against a presidential target of 80% internet usage by 2026 stated without a baseline.

Schools are the clearest case. 85% of Malawi's 9,000 schools have limited or no internet access and about 900 have none at all, with lack of electricity and of devices named as the main barriers; the 2026/27 national budget carries no dedicated line for school connectivity, while the nearest digital allocation is MWK 1.1bn for assembling laptops and tablets at a university (landscape analysis).

The universal service fund exists and is stated to be short: its Director General said in March 2026 that existing funding levels remain insufficient (procurement briefing), and no income, balance or disbursement figure is held for any year.

What is financed is financed externally. Parliament ratified an SDR 58.2m grant for local-government service delivery on 29 July 2026, of which US$12.8m funds business-process re-engineering, system assessments and a performance dashboard (parliamentary approval). There is no domestic budget archive for Malawi, so every appropriation figure the wiki carries is a journalist's or a minister's reading.
