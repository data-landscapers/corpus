This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Cabinet approved the Seychelles Digital and AI Strategy 2026-2031 on 28 October 2026 as the national framework for digital and AI transformation. The strategy was prepared with World Bank support. Cabinet also set up an Inter-Ministerial Digital and AI Coordination Committee chaired by the ICT minister. In August 2026 the President restated a target of fully digitalised e-government by 2030. He named technical assistance from Malta as the route. Cabinet approved a Digital Economic Transformation Forum on 9 September 2026 to agree a national digital architecture and pilots.

In 2025 a government entity focused on GovTech, a coordination body and an entity monitoring digital spending across government were all in place. None of them publishes an annual report on results or spending. There was no dedicated body or strategy for data governance. The public sector had no open source policy, and there was no national strategy on disruptive technologies. In 2024 interoperability was addressed only inside broader frameworks: Vision 2033, the ICT department's strategic plan and the National ICT Policy. It had no strategy of its own.

Cabinet approved a National AI in Education Framework 2026-2030 on 22 July 2026 covering every state school. The Central Bank is retiring cheques on a dated four-stage timetable running to May 2026. The Revenue Commission's mandatory e-invoicing procurement reached its final stage in July 2026.

The 2023 budget address states that the Minister for Finance chairs the Digital Economy Steering Committee and the ministry allocates the ICT budget. The ministry holds no veto over technology spending in other departments, a power a World Bank review recommended and government did not take. Seychelles ranked first in Africa for overall governance in 2023, at 75.3 out of 100.

Legislation and regulation

The National Institute of Science, Technology and Innovation Act 2025 was assented to on 22 December 2025. It establishes NISTI as a body corporate to lead and coordinate science, technology and innovation research. The Act commences only on a date the Minister appoints, and no commencement date has been found. In August 2026 the President described the institute as reinstated, with its secretariat under the Office of the Vice-President. The Seychelles Broadcasting Corporation Act 2026, gazetted on 13 July, requires the broadcaster to keep a policy on its use of artificial intelligence. The National Assembly Administration Act 2026 gives Parliament its own Office of Information Technology, Cybersecurity and Data Security.

The National Payment System (Electronic Money) Regulations 2022 have been in force since 12 December 2022. The Central Bank made them under section 42(1) of the National Payment System Act 2014. They set an initial capital floor of SCR 1,000,000, with ongoing capital the greater of that sum or 2% of the average float (2022). Issuers must hold liquid assets matching the outstanding float and reconcile it to the Central Bank each quarter. They must also spread customer money across two or more banks or credit unions, apart from their own. An issuer needs no banking licence, because receiving funds against electronic money is not deposit-taking. Customer recourse sits in the Financial Consumer Protection Act of 2022 and its 2024 complaint-handling regulations.

Civil registration rests on the Civil Status Act, Cap 34, enacted in 1893 and consolidated as at 1 November 2022. SIM registration has been compulsory since December 2020. Regulations of 2018 set standards for secure digital signatures and certifying authorities. In May 2025 the Supreme Court ruled a digitally signed plaint defective until a structure to verify e-signatures in court filings is in place.

In July 2026 Cabinet decided on amendments to the Foreign Exchange Act 2009 widening authorised dealers to credit unions and licensed payment service providers. It also decided on the Financial Services Authority as supervisor of all public and private pension schemes. Both are Cabinet decisions, not law.

Data protection

Every Seychelles controller and processor has been bound by the Data Protection Act 2023 in full since 22 June 2025. On that date the eighteen-month conformity window in section 56(1) closed and non-compliance became an offence. The Act came into operation on 22 December 2023 and repealed the former Data Protection Act. That 2003 statute was never implemented. Section 5 designates the Information Commission as supervisory authority. The Commission was created under the Access to Information Act and designated afterwards.

Controllers owe GDPR-aligned duties under the 2023 Act: explicit consent, impact assessments for high-risk processing, and a data protection officer for certain organisations. The Act brings the country's first cross-border transfer regime and a wider set of data subject rights. Criminal investigation, national security and purely personal activity fall outside its scope. A breach must be notified to the Commission within 72 hours. The Act fixes neither the length of any extension nor the deadline for telling data subjects. Section 23 treats anyone under 18 as a child and requires parental consent, naming no age-verification mechanism. Sensitive data may be processed on grounds of misconduct, a term the Act does not define.

The Commission may audit, investigate, impose penalties, seek injunctions and issue enforcement notices under the 2023 Act. An aggrieved person may appeal to the Supreme Court within 21 working days. Its 2026-2028 strategic plan puts enforcement of the information and data protection Acts first. In 2025 neither the authority's results nor the complaints it receives were published. Seychelles Commercial Bank confirmed a customer data breach in July 2025. The July 2026 plan to turn public CCTV cameras to vehicle identification named no legal basis, no retention rule and no role for the Commission.

Regional collaboration

The Revenue Commission launched a national electronic Certificate of Origin with the COMESA Secretariat on 4 July 2026 for trade facilitation and border security. Government is urging traders to use it to claim the reduced duties their trade agreements allow.

Seychelles processes cross-border payments on the SADC real-time gross settlement system through the Central Bank (July 2025). Volumes are too thin to give commercial banks a case to join. Seychelles appears nowhere in the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System's June 2026 coverage list. Its own domestic instant payment system is still in development, leaving no rail for a regional scheme to reach.

Seychelles scores 59.8 out of 100 for regional integration in 2023, 11th of 54 African states. Its score rose 7.6 points over the decade. It belongs to both COMESA and the Southern African Development Community. The Information Commission places the country among the earliest signatories anywhere of the Council of Europe's convention on the automatic processing of personal data, in 1981. The NISTI Act's stated objectives include science diplomacy, alongside making research attractive to business and fostering innovation in schools. No commencement date for the Act has been found.

Standards

In 2025 Seychelles had no government interoperability framework, no data quality framework, no uptime monitoring of government systems and no guidance for replacing legacy ones. The ICT department's Policy, Standards and Research Section says it adopts technical standards for the interoperability and integration of e-government services. No published framework has been found.

The 2022 electronic money regulations give interoperability a provision of its own: one system working with others without any intervention by the customer. SeyID integrates with the civil status identity database, the Licensing Authority and the monServis portal using OpenID Connect and OAuth. The health ministry's 2022-2026 strategic plan records no formal standards and no coordination mechanisms for managing health data.

The Seychelles Bureau of Standards is constituted under Act 2 of 2014, whose thirty-six sections set the Board, the standards procedure, offences and penalties. A new eight-member Board was appointed for three years from 1 August 2025. The regional accreditation register shows the Bureau's mass-calibration capability approved on 15 September 2025 and valid to 14 September 2030. No count of national standards adopted, and no annual report of the Bureau, has been found. The Meteorological Authority kept its ISO 9001 certification for aviation weather services after a surveillance audit on 3-4 November 2026. Marine forecasting is next in line for certification.

In 2025 the e-procurement portal published nothing to the Open Contracting Data Standard and exchanged no data with other government systems. It had no governance arrangements covering its operation. Governance arrangements for the human resources and payroll systems existed but were internal and unpublished. None were recorded for the customs, tax, financial management and treasury systems.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Seychelles ranked first in Africa for deliberative and participatory governance in 2023, at 89.6 out of 100. Its score rose 27.7 points over the decade. In 2025 no national platform let a citizen or business take part in a policy decision. There were no petitions, no anonymous feedback, no omnichannel access and no published government responses. Citizens and businesses had no hand in designing the public service, tax and social insurance portals. The 2023-2025 Open Government Partnership action plan's commitment to an e-engagement portal has not begun.

Media freedom fell 5.6 points over 2014-2023 to 79.3 out of 100, though Seychelles still ranked 3rd of 54. Freedom of expression and belief stood at 93.1 in 2023, 2nd of 54 and up 16.7 points over the decade. A June 2026 amendment gave the President greater powers to appoint and dismiss the public broadcaster's board. In a 2025 survey 45% said the media was somewhat or completely free, against 52% who said it was not. The ICT department opened a public consultation on the draft data protection bill in August 2023, four months before the Act passed the National Assembly.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2026 estimates' public investment programme carries SCR 18.7 million of foreign loans and SCR 339.8 million of foreign grants, none of it for a digital project (FY2026). The Appropriation Act 2026 voted the ICT department SCR 93.8 million. The 2026 budget framework allocates SCR 325 million to digitalisation across government over 2026-2028. It covers the human resources, financial management, tax and social protection systems.

The digital lines of the 2026 estimates come to SCR 239.6 million proposed, none of it externally financed, against SCR 276.2 million appropriated in 2025, of which 80.4% was the state's own (FY2026).

The 2024 budget financed SCR 86.7 million of a new integrated financial management system with an Indian government loan, beside SCR 5.0 million of local money (FY2024). The 2025 budget put SCR 42.0 million of local money on it and no loan (FY2025). The project spent nothing in 2024 and was revised to SCR 0.3 million for 2025 (FY2025). A SCR 54.3 million Chinese grant in kind for the Revenue Commission's invoice management system sat in the 2025 budget (FY2025). The 2025 mid-year review cut the same sum from the Commission. The 2026 estimates give the Commission SCR 39.5 million for e-invoicing (FY2026).

The 2025 mid-year review gave the ICT department SCR 6.1 million for a Microsoft subscription and servers for the human resources system (2025). The 2026 estimates carry SCR 8.7 million for Autodesk licences for ministries (FY2026). The 2025 budget carried SCR 5.5 million for the health information system's annual maintenance contract (FY2025). In November 2024 the biometric passport ran on a five-year maintenance contract with a French contractor of about US$153,000 a year. The ICT department spent SCR 73.9 million of the SCR 78.9 million voted to it for 2024 (FY2024).

The Communications Act 2023 provides for a universal access and universal service fund fed by operator contributions. The identity card is free on first issue; in 2024 replacing or amending one cost SCR 150 and an expatriate's card SCR 300.

MoUs and other agreements

Hytera and Cable & Wireless Seychelles signed a memorandum of understanding at Mobile World Congress on 4 March 2025. It covers a resilient, redundant emergency-communications network able to keep working through a disaster. No value, timetable or build commitment has been published, and no deployment has been found.

In June 2026 Seychelles announced it would bring India's Unified Payments Interface into service by the end of the year. The Central Bank, local financial institutions and NPCI International are to implement it. The target date is not an obligation. Neither agreement binds either side to spend, build or deliver.

New investments

The World Bank has a US$25 million digital investment project for Seychelles at concept stage, with approval estimated for 17 December 2026. It is to fund inter-island fibre, shared digital-government infrastructure with AI-enabled services, and digital skills. The African Development Bank approved a US$34 million budget-support loan on 22 September 2026. Its named digital reforms are electronic invoicing for tax, a national e-procurement system and a one-stop investment portal.

The Trade and Development Bank's USD 50 million facility to Cable & Wireless Seychelles was the largest live commitment to the digital sector as at October 2026. In 2019 CWS Investment Ltd bought the incumbent from Liberty Latin America with a USD 70 million syndicated loan the bank arranged. The bank lent USD 50 million of it in 2019, beside Absa Group and ABC Banking Corporation. In 2022 the bank refinanced the USD 48.1 million then outstanding with a USD 50 million package. The 2022 package included a USD 5 million convertible note and a term loan in rupees. In December 2022 the bank took a stake of about 2.9 per cent in CWS.

Four commitments to Seychelles' digital sector are on record (November 2026), spread across 2015 to 2022 and worth about USD 100 million at face value, or nearer USD 50 million counting the 2022 refinancing once. Connectivity takes almost all of it, both bank facilities having gone to the same operator. The only grants are two small awards of 2015 reported to the OECD, from the African Development Bank for quality-of-service standards and from the ILO for labour-market information. No bilateral donor and no Chinese, Gulf, Indian or EU institution is among them.

The 2023 budget allocated SCR 145.1 million over the medium term for digital economy infrastructure. The Ease of Doing Business portal went to a Norwegian registers developer for USD 1,295,452.86 in 2025. Fusepay raised a USD 350,000 pre-seed round backed by Hustle Fund and Everywhere Ventures in 2025. Seychelles is absent from Partech's 2025 tally of African tech venture funding.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Airtel Seychelles switched on commercial 5G on 3 July 2026. Cable & Wireless Seychelles had launched the country's first 5G network with Huawei on 7 July 2020. In December 2021 Airtel had contracted Ericsson to move its network core into a new data centre ahead of VoLTE and 5G. Starlink began operating under a soft launch announced at State House on 21 July 2026. Cabinet had approved its licence in February 2026. It sells direct to customers through its own platform. The incumbent has been Seychellois-owned since 2019. Every SIM must be registered against a national ID card or passport.

Cable & Wireless completed a nationwide fibre-to-the-curb network in August 2012, fed by the 2,000 km SEAS cable to Dar es Salaam. It ended the country's reliance on satellite links. Airtel opened the first phase of its own fibre ring in May 2015. On 20 April 2023 the private operator Intelvision brought ashore 2Africa, the third submarine cable to reach the islands. Intelvision bought over 600 Gbps on it from Vodafone.

87.8% of the population used the internet in 2024, against 79.3% in 2020. A commercial tracker counted 119,000 internet users in October 2026. Mobile subscriptions ran at around 126 per 100 people in 2024, down from a peak near 165 in 2019. Seychelles ranked third of 54 African states for internet and computer access in 2023, at 79.4 out of 100. It ranked 35th of 54 for mobile communications, at 61.3.

Shipping and postal network was Seychelles' worst Ibrahim Index indicator in 2023, at 15.0 out of 100 and 41st of 54. It fell 16.8 points after 2014, the steepest fall of any of its measures. Infrastructure as a whole scored 67.0 in 2023, up 1.1 points over 2014-2023. The country has a single internet exchange point, SEY-IX, with few participants.

Data Storage

No policy governs where government cloud data may be hosted (2025). The state monitors and publishes nothing on government cloud usage, security or savings (2025). No data localisation requirement applies to anyone, government included (2024). No shared cloud platform is available to government entities (2025). No platform type and no cloud services are recorded, and no answer on whether one exists at all. Cabinet approved a sovereign, in-country Government Cloud on 28 October 2026. It is to run on two geographically separated datacentres built in two phases, the primary one first.

Airtel Seychelles' data centre was certified TIA-942 Rated-3 in November 2022, the first such certification in the country. The certificate for its Perseverance facility was valid to 6 November 2025. Airtel moved its national network core into the facility under the 2021 Ericsson contract. Cable & Wireless Seychelles inaugurated the Bon Espoir data centre in November 2024. The Uptime Institute awarded it Tier IV design and construction certification on 4 September 2025. There is no in-country hyperscale cloud region; the nearest AWS and Azure regions are in South Africa. The Department of Information Communications Technology operates the government-wide Electronic Government Network. It has a network operations section and server-room infrastructure connecting ministries. A National Assembly committee inspected it in June 2025.

Energy

In 2023 renewables supplied around 5% of electricity generation, with over 95% from imported fossil fuels. The national target is 15% by 2030, under a newly launched Renewable Energy Acceleration Programme. In 2023 the whole population had electricity, in urban and rural areas alike. Seychelles ranked first of 54 African states on access to energy, at 100.0 and unchanged over 2014-2023.

Firms reported around half an electrical outage a month, roughly six a year, in the 2023 World Bank Enterprise Survey. Residential electricity cost roughly SCR 2 per kWh, about US$0.15, in 2024. The Electricity Act 2023 and regulations of 2024 set up net metering, net billing and feed-in tariffs. The Public Utilities Corporation and the Utilities Regulatory Commission administer them. Utility-scale projects include the 5 MWp Ile de Romainville solar farm and the Port Victoria wind farm.

Technical Capacity

Public administration was Seychelles' worst continental placing of any Ibrahim Index sub-category in 2023, at 10th of 54. It scored 64.4 out of 100, up only 4.5 points over the decade. Seychelles ranks first or third on most of the other measures.

In 2024 the national digital ID ran on a Swiss vendor's proprietary platform. A World Bank assessment credits the ICT department with a solid foundation of in-house technical capacity. The department shared delivery of the relaunched egov.sc portal in June 2026. The 2023-2025 Open Government Partnership action plan records a long-standing shortage of AI and cybersecurity expertise in the public and private sectors.

Cybersecurity

Seychelles Commercial Bank confirmed a customer data breach after an attacker penetrated its network on 5 February 2025. The attacker exfiltrated roughly 2.2 GB of records on 3-4 July 2025. The records held names, dates of birth, phone numbers, addresses, emails, account types and balances. The bank suspended internet banking, apologised to customers and engaged the police.

Seychelles scored 50.41 out of 100 on the ITU's 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index, ranking 132nd in the establishing tier. Coordination runs through the National Cybersecurity Coordination Committee and a Cyber Secretariat, both approved by Cabinet on 10 July 2024. The ICT department launched CERT-SC, the national computer emergency response team, in July 2025. It acted on a recommendation of the 2024 cybersecurity maturity assessment supported by the World Bank. In October 2026 the response team designed and delivered the third national cyberdrill itself, for the first time. The National Assembly approved accession to the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime in July 2026. The accession itself is still to follow. The Assembly passed the Cybercrimes and other Related Crimes (Amendment) Bill unanimously on 15 December 2025. The amending Act, assented to on 22 December 2025, widens the offences and adds search and interception powers.

A March 2025 memorandum between Cable & Wireless Seychelles and China's Hytera envisages a redundant emergency-communications network. It would join mission-critical push-to-talk cores to the operator's public 4G and 5G networks. Its users would be public safety, energy, transport and tourism. The World Bank's digital government assessment flagged thin security investment, notably in regular penetration testing, as a threat to all government systems.

DPI

Data Exchange

In 2025 Seychelles had no government interoperability framework, no data quality framework and no guidance for replacing legacy systems. Nothing was monitored or published about the use, compliance or benefits of the sharing arrangements in place. The tax, customs, human resources and social insurance systems all exchanged data with other parts of government. The financial management system, still being implemented, exchanged none.

In November 2024 the government tendered a Land Management Administration Information System. It is to link the land register with the National Population Database, SeyID, the Immigration Information System and public financial management. The Revenue Commission's customs payment facility has run as a module of the ASYCUDA customs system since June 2024. Since November 2022 the Electronic Border System has fed passenger data into immigration databases in real time. By May 2025 the Agency for Social Protection was synchronising benefit-payment data with the Seychelles Pension Fund.

A 2024 assessment of the e-Services Gateway and SeyGo Connect found little evidence that the country's digital government initiatives talked to each other. An October 2023 national digital health maturity assessment scored standards and interoperability at 2, against a global average of 3. A Health Information Exchange Maturity Assessment was held in July 2025 with Africa CDC support. In September 2026 Africa CDC called for consultants to draft a national interoperability framework for health information exchange.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Since 17 September 2024 work-permit holders have been registered on SeyID and issued a virtual card valid for the permit. Seychellois citizens keep the option of a physical card, which expires every ten years.

The National Population Database was established digitally in 1988 and is updated daily from civil status registrations. The National Identity Number it assigns has been compulsory by law since 1995. Every resident must hold a valid card, and foreign nationals over 16 on work permits must enrol too. First issuance is free and SeyID registration carries no fee (2024). Citizens living abroad can enrol with an identity card or ID letter and a passport.

SeyID launched in November 2022 and was operational by April 2024. WISeKey built it on its WISeID platform, under a partnership the government entered in 2014. A certificate ordered through CertExpress is saved to the holder's E-Doc wallet. Its QR code supports remote verification for opening a bank account. In April 2025 the Central Bank directed every bank and payment provider it supervises to integrate SeyID into customer due diligence. A physical card or passport is still accepted on equal terms. The Agency for Social Protection uses the National Identity Number across its programmes.

SeyID operates under the Civil Status Act, which governs the number, and the Data Protection Act 2023, which governs the data. Registered users can see the data kept about them and which fields reach integrated third parties. The e-passport introduced in November 2022 embeds the holder's facial image and fingerprints. SeyID's onboarding captures a live facial video.

Digital Payments and Fintech

The Revenue Commission limited cheque payments for taxes, duties and levies from 13 July 2026, and plans to stop accepting them by the end of 2028. The Central Bank has withdrawn cheques in stages since the start of 2025, from individuals first and non-residents during 2026. In 2024 Seychelles was listed among countries whose domestic instant payment system was still in development.

In 2023 electronic funds transfers and card payments carried over 10 million transactions, against around 600,000 cheques. The Central Bank operates SEFT, the domestic electronic funds transfer system. It also licenses and supervises the other payment service providers.

The National Payment System Act 2014 and its regulations set out access, technical, conduct and complaint rules. The 2022 electronic money regulations let issuers distribute airtime and data, but bar airtime from being used to pay for anything. Complaint-handling units became compulsory for financial and payment service providers under regulations made in 2024. In 2025 commercial banks recorded 1,957 complaints and payment service providers 25. Fifty customers took their complaint on to the Central Bank in 2025. In March 2026 the Central Bank consulted on regulations for a sandbox to test new financial products under supervision.

In 2025 pensions, disability benefits and welfare assistance went straight into beneficiaries' bank accounts. A World Bank-financed reform required every beneficiary to hold one. Access to banking services scored 48.8 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index for 2023, up only 4.5 points across the decade. Fusepay made its first live transaction in Victoria on 19 November 2025. It sells virtual business accounts and post-dated digital payments to replace the cheque.

Registries

In 2026 neither the land register nor the business register could be tied to a national address register. A company's address is captured only as free text. Cabinet approved a National Addressing System Bill in April 2026. It creates a National Addressing Department and a National Addressing Database Unit. The Bill rests on the international S42 address standard, which Cabinet had already endorsed.

The National Population Database, held by the Civil Status Division, consolidates birth, marriage, death and identity records for the whole resident population. UNHCR records the country as having achieved universal birth registration. A birth must be registered in person within 30 days at one of three civil status offices. Since May 2023 maternity wards have notified Civil Status of each birth electronically within three days. Doctors notify each death within 24 hours. Civil registration scored 50.0 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index in 2023, unmoved since 2014.

The business register offers online search and online registration. Bills gazetted on 3 November 2026 would let the Minister provide for electronic filing, records and fee payment for companies. The Revenue Commission runs online Tax Identification Number registration for individuals and businesses. In February 2026 an African Development Bank appraisal put electronic tax filing under 40 per cent. Registration requires a validated National Identity Number, and for a company the Registrar's certificate. The land register runs an eSearch platform covering title deeds, charges, transfers and leases (2025). Access requires an in-person subscription at the Office of the Registrar General, and registration remains substantially manual. The Road Transport (Amendment) Act 2023 created registers of driving licences and merit points. Courts must notify the Licensing Authority of traffic convictions. Beneficial owners are filed to a national Beneficial Ownership database under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2020 (2024).

The Electoral Commission's digital voters' register, certified for 2025, can be checked online or by SMS against a National Identity Number. Deceased persons reach it as a monthly feed from Immigration and Civil Status. Changes take effect only at annual or pre-election certification, and registration cannot be done online.

Sectoral management information systems

In 2025 customs, tax, human resources, payroll, debt, treasury and social insurance systems were all in use. Customs and debt ran on commercial packages, and tax, human resources, payroll and social insurance on custom software. There was no public investment management system.

In 2025 the integrated financial management system was still being implemented and covered central government budgets only. It captured no non-financial performance indicators. The finance ministry signed the development contract in early 2025 with Linpico in joint venture with PFM Smart System. The system replaces accounting software that needed numerous manual interventions (2024). In May 2026 it was in user acceptance testing, with a pilot in selected ministries from January 2027 and full deployment by January 2028. A government-wide human resource management system, contracted in December 2024 with FreeBalance, is now to start in 2027.

The health ministry's 2022-2026 strategic plan records health data capture as primarily paper-based, on duplicative reporting tools. The electronic Health Information System begun in 2020 stalled. The Education Management Information System produces school-level statistics with no link to civil registration, digital identity or social protection. The ministry has joined the OpenEMIS community and is training ahead of a nationwide rollout.

The Attorney General's Electronic Case Management System went live on 23 June 2026. It offers shared calendars, case-data retrieval and secure chat. The Judiciary launched an e-Service platform on 26 November 2025 for lawyers to file submissions and pay fees by card. It runs under rules the Chief Justice made in September 2025 for electronic filing in civil matters. The Agency for Social Protection launched ASP Connect internally on 15 October 2025 to manage benefit applications and payments. Police case management is deployed across the country's police stations, with search across districts. The employment ministry's Labour Market Information System registers vacancies, jobseekers, placements and redundancies. A second phase has been tendered to link it with the population database, SeyID, immigration and finance ministry payments. In January 2025 the lands department tendered for a land valuation system linked to the population database, SeyID and financial management, and no award has been found.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Seychelles relaunched its national services portal, egov.sc, in June 2026. The portal had gone more than a decade without meaningful review. It worked as little more than an email gateway for public servants.

In 2025 the public service portal reached transactional maturity, so users complete transactions and do not only download forms. SeyID is the mandatory login for monServis, where business and vehicle licences are transacted. Import and export permit applications moved onto monServis from the older e-services gateway on 1 May 2026. CertExpress has issued birth, marriage and death certificates online since September 2024. Customs charges have been payable online since June 2024. In 2025 a resident could not start a business or establish an e-residency through the portal. Nothing was published on how online services perform (2025). The government soft-launched SeyAlert, an emergency-alert app for warnings by app and SMS, on 30 October 2026. The 2026 mid-year review cut SR 10.3m from the human resource system and SR 4.6m from the e-invoicing system for delay.

Large businesses have filed and paid through the Revenue Commission's tax portal since 2023. Other taxpayers are urged to lodge returns by email and pay by bank transfer. A digital acknowledgment notice replaced the paper assessment letter in April 2026. Seychelles scored 0.6773 on the UN's 2024 E-Government Development Index, 92nd of the states assessed. In November 2025 the investment ministry announced an Ease of Doing Business portal bringing services, document submission and payment together.

An eight-week pilot announced in July 2026 turns public CCTV cameras at Roche Caïman on Mahé into automated traffic enforcement. The cameras read the number plate, and an officer issues an electronic ticket delivered by message. Permanent adoption is envisaged with the Australian firm Acusensus.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In 2024 there was no sub-national tier of government: the 26 administrative districts sit below a single national layer. All digital government services operate nationally. In 2025 a police case-management application was deployed across police stations and searchable across districts. No system run by a district has been found.

Rural digital data capture

In 2024 a birth could be registered only in person, at Civil Status offices in Victoria, Grand Anse Praslin and La Digue. Registration is due within 30 days, on production of a maternity certificate and identity documents. There is no remote or online channel for the registration itself. In 2019 a pilot of the African Union's DataPlat platform was introduced because school data collection was inconsistent, unreliable and slow.

Agriculture earned 2.48% of GDP in 2021. By 2023 no agricultural survey had been held for ten years. In 2023 Seychelles had no data for 21 of the Ibrahim Index's 96 indicators, including every Rural Economy indicator. In 2021 57.1% of the population lived in urban areas, above the regional average.

Technology

AI

Cabinet approved a National AI in Education Framework 2026-2030 on 22 July 2026, covering every state school in the country. The framework was drafted with technical support from the World Bank. It is governed by a national steering committee that maintains lists of approved and prohibited tools for schools. It reserves grading, certification and placement decisions to human judgement in its early phases. It rests on teacher capacity, not a new subject.

The Seychelles Broadcasting Corporation Act 2026 requires the public broadcaster to develop and review a policy on the use of AI in content and broadcast operations. No other statutory AI obligation has been found. The NISTI Act 2025 sets the institute an objective of devising strategies to promote artificial intelligence, other emerging technologies and digitisation. The objective covers the country's socio-economic development and sets up no rulebook or regulator.

Cabinet approved a national Digital and AI Strategy 2026-2031 on 28 October 2026, with a government cloud to supply local AI computing. A shortage of artificial-intelligence and cybersecurity expertise across the public and private sectors has left digital projects unimplemented and unmaintained for a decade (2023). No chatbot or AI-enabled forum supports citizen engagement (2025). The revenue commission's audit risk-scoring model, built with UNDP during 2024, is the one analytical model on record. A pilot announced in July 2026 would read offending vehicles' plates off the public CCTV cameras already installed. It would route an electronic ticket to the driver. The tourism minister said an AI travel assistant has run on the official destination website for two years. A forum on 1 October 2026 is to feed a Tourism Digital and AI Agenda.

Fusepay released Fuse360 in July 2026, a trade-business operating platform. Its AI agents automate reconciliation, collections, reporting and credit monitoring, alongside business and customer onboarding checks. In May 2025 the national cybersecurity centre and the media practitioners' association ran a World Press Freedom Day workshop on AI's impact on press freedom, ethics and cybersecurity.

ICT Industry

Cabinet revised the Seed Capital Grant Scheme on 13 May 2026, redefining the youth beneficiary and revising eligibility. In 2024 the national digital ID was built by a Swiss vendor on its own proprietary identity platform. The Ease of Doing Business portal was launched in November 2025 with a foreign supplier, for full service by 30 June 2026. In 2025 government had no policy to support technology startups and gave no innovation financing to startups or SMEs. No procurement policy favoured their bids, and nothing was published on support given to them.

Business and Labour Environment was Seychelles' worst-scoring Ibrahim sub-category in 2023, at 58.7 out of 100. It still ranked 4th of 54 and rose 14.4 points over the decade. Business and competition regulations were its weakest component, at 55.6 and 26th of 54. Partech's tracker recorded no venture funding to Seychelles-based startups in 2025.

Innovation ecosystem

The National Institute of Science, Technology and Innovation Act 2025 commences only on a date the Minister appoints, and no commencement date has been found. NISTI's functions include running a Science Academy and establishing incubation centres, science parks and maker-spaces. Its objectives extend to science diplomacy, making research attractive to business, and fostering innovation in schools. In August 2026 the President described the institute as reinstated as the implementation arm for science and innovation policy. He set it a near-term focus on digitalising public-service delivery.

A government-backed Business and Technology Incubator has run under the Division of Science, Technology and Innovation since 2019. The University of Seychelles established a Centre for Innovation in 2025. In 2025 government had no public-sector innovation lab or programme, and no collaboration with the private sector on one. Ground was broken on 21 July 2026 on a regional incubation and innovation hub at Ile Perseverance, expected to operate in 2027.

The country placed 75th on WIPO's 2025 Global Innovation Index. Fusepay, founded in Seychelles, took its first live business payment in Victoria in November 2025. In July 2026 it extended into an AI-driven operating platform for trade businesses.

Capacity

Literacy

The National AI in Education Framework approved in July 2026 creates no standalone AI subject. AI literacy is to be carried across the whole curriculum, in every state school in the country.

Seychelles ranked first of 54 African states for both equality in education and education quality in 2023. It ranked tenth for education's human resources, a measure up 10.7 points over the decade. Enrolment was its weakest education measure, ninth of 54 in 2023 after a 5.5-point gain since 2014.

In 2025 government offered no digital skills programmes to citizens or schools. A Digital School Initiative launched with the United Arab Emirates in 2025 puts technology into classrooms.

Training and skills

The National Teacher AI Competency Framework of July 2026 requires every teacher in Seychelles to hold an AI micro-credential. Every school is to name an AI Champion under its four levels. The framework is the central mechanism of the country's AI in education policy.

In 2025 Seychelles had no government strategy or programme to improve digital skills in the public sector. Nothing was published on digital skills results or progress. There was no government job portal, so jobseekers were offered no online job-matching. A memorandum with India's National Centre for Good Governance commits to training 250 civil servants over three years. A first cohort of 29 completed a two-week programme on digital public infrastructure and AI-enabled governance on 22 May 2026. A second cohort of 27 left for a two-week course in Delhi and Mussoorie on 5 October 2026.

Only one provider in the country offers ICT and computing programmes: the University of Seychelles, through an academic partnership with Malaysia's SEGi University. From August 2025 a vendor-supplied smart classroom system was deployed in four secondary schools.

Research institutions

The National Institute of Science, Technology and Innovation Act was assented to on 22 December 2025. It establishes NISTI as a body corporate to lead and coordinate research. NISTI is charged with establishing a digital repository of scientific data held on open access. The statute confers the repository, the academy and the science parks as powers. None of them has been found in operation.

Inclusion

Access to services

Seychelles ranked first of 54 African states for equal access to public services in 2023, at 94.5 out of 100. Its score rose 10.3 points over the decade. The September 2024 consultation on withdrawing cheques raised financial-exclusion concerns for the groups that still rely on them. A physical identity card remains an option. SeyID is accepted but not required for banking (2024).

In 2025 no government platform took complaints, requests for information or support for users with disabilities. There were no published service standards and no record of a service changed in response to feedback. There was no citizen-facing mobile app. Since April 2023 the finance ministry has taken complaints and compliments through an online form that requires a national identity number. Protection against discrimination remained one of the weakest-scoring governance indicators in 2023, despite a large gain over the decade.

Starlink subscriptions are taken through Starlink's own online platform, and on 22 July 2026 officials said some people already used kits. The receiver needs a clear view of the sky, and officials named trees and buildings as obstructions.

Digital divides

The 2023-2025 Open Government Partnership action plan committed to raising IT literacy among the elderly. In July 2026 its independent results report recorded that digital-transformation commitment as dropped from the plan.

In 2023 Seychelles had Africa's highest GDP per capita, at USD 17,879. It had the continent's smallest population, around 0.1 million (2023). Its internet access and computer use rose faster over the decade to 2023 than any other part of its infrastructure.

Data

National statistics

Seychelles' Capacity of the Statistical System score rose 14.8 points over 2014-2023 to 71.1 out of 100, 6th of 54. By 2023 no agricultural, business or health survey had been held in ten years. Three or more labour force surveys and two household surveys were held in the same period. An IMF mission in April 2024 was the first government finance statistics assistance since 2011. It was sent to revive compilation after disruption from Covid-19 and high staff turnover. The first national strategy for the development of statistics, for 2023 to 2027, had its mid-term evaluation in February 2026.

By 2023 a population census had been held within ten years and an agricultural census within twenty, but no business census in twenty. Data services was the weakest of the World Bank's five pillars in 2023, in the 20-49% band. Data use scored above 89%, data infrastructure 70-89%, and data products and data sources 50-69% (2023).

The Bureau of Statistics takes restricted feeds from line agencies, among them the Fisheries Information Management System the Fishing Authority launched in 2023.

Open data

In November 2025 the Information Commission named the lack of a clear power to enforce its orders as the access law's most urgent weakness. Holders of information have ignored its directives since 2019, with no recourse available. The Commission recommends reforming the 2018 Act, including open-reuse licensing and a central access-to-information unit.

In 2025 the right to information was in force, requiring information to be made available online, with a body monitoring compliance. No statistics on requests received or granted were published. Accessibility of public records rose 26.0 points over 2014-2023, yet scored only 46.3 out of 100, 11th of 54. Disclosure of public records has stayed at 50.0 out of 100 since 2014, 9th of 54.

In 2025 there was no open data portal and no government entity, strategy or policy for data governance. All sixteen transparency and publication indicators in the 2025 GovTech Maturity Index returned a negative. Seychelles publishes Open Government Partnership action plans.

Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory scores balance of payments, international trade, and money and banking highest, at 6.5 out of ten each. Crime and justice scored 2.5, and education facilities, education outcomes, health facilities and poverty 3.0 each. Telecommunications and digital data, food security and nutrition, and health outcomes scored zero.

In June 2026 the fisheries ministry described its Fisheries Information Management System as giving public access to fisheries statistics, licensing and export data. In September 2020 the extractives transparency board found revenue data published in open format but no open data policy. It set a corrective-action deadline to publish one. No such policy has been found.

Geospatial data

Starlink service began under a soft launch announced at State House on 21 July 2026, with the Communications Regulatory Authority presenting the regulatory framework alongside Vice-President Sebastien Pillay. Satellite reached Seychelles in 2026 as bandwidth, not imagery. The same announcement left a formal launch ceremony involving Starlink representatives still to be held. Cabinet had approved the operator's internet service provider licence on 18 February 2026, subject to conditions. The company had incorporated as a local Seychelles entity and entered the final stage of licensing by May 2026.

The Meteorological Authority's forecasters use EUMETSAT satellite products for nowcasting (2025). Weather forecasting is the routine operational use of Earth observation on record. The authority also runs a WIS2.0 node for World Meteorological Organization data. It has no weather radar and no formal data-sharing policy. In 2023 one of the six surface stations the global basic observing network requires of Seychelles was reporting. A US$3.31 million request through the World Bank to rehabilitate the airport surface and upper-air stations and six outer-island stations over 2027 to 2031 was in the pipeline in October 2026. Geospatial data reaches the public through one service, the lands ministry's WebGIS platform, which gives public search access to land parcels, survey beacons and planning layers. It is compiled periodically rather than live. The Lands Department holds the authoritative survey records separately.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

The Electronic Case Management System launched for the legal affairs sector in June 2026 was developed with United States State Department funding. It was delivered through the UN Office on Drugs and Crime's Global Maritime Crime Programme and UNOPS. The working platform sits in the Attorney General's office.

Cabinet approved an ISP licence for Starlink in February 2026. The company incorporated locally and reached the final stage of licensing by May 2026. Service began under a soft launch announced at State House on 21 July 2026. The Vice-President and the Seychelles Communications Regulatory Authority presented the regulatory framework together. Starlink sells direct to consumers through its own platform.

China activities

In August 2025 the Ministry of Education and Cable & Wireless Seychelles put Huawei's SMART Education platform into secondary schools. It runs on the vendor's own devices and is centrally managed. The same operator launched the country's first 5G network with Huawei in July 2020.

China's Hytera and Cable & Wireless Seychelles signed a memorandum at Mobile World Congress in March 2025. It covers a redundant network to keep communications running during disasters. All three reach the country through Cable & Wireless Seychelles.

EU activities

In June 2026 the EU and four eastern and southern African states, Seychelles among them, concluded an enhanced Economic Partnership Agreement. It carries a binding ban on unjustified data-localisation requirements and on customs duties on electronic transmissions. The 2026-2030 fisheries protocol, published on 11 August 2026, sets data protection rules for the electronic vessel data the parties exchange. The revenue commission's customs ePayment facility was delivered under the Economic Partnership Agreement in 2024. No European Union programme or European vendor had a named role in Seychelles' digital public infrastructure as at September 2026.

The ninth EU–Seychelles political dialogue, in October 2022, agreed to explore cooperation on cybersecurity, disinformation, safer internet use and personal data. In February 2026 the Information Commission's chief executive and Chief Information Commissioner met EUROJUST and the EU Delegation on data protection.

Gulf/UAE activities

In July 2024 the central banks of the United Arab Emirates and Seychelles signed two memoranda on settlement in local currencies and linking instant-payment platforms. They name financial technology and central bank digital currency as areas of cooperation. No linked switch, live corridor or digital-currency pilot between them had been found as at November 2026.

Seychelles launched a Digital School Initiative with the United Arab Emirates in 2025. In May 2026 the Emirates reaffirmed commitments to four desalination plants, a sports complex and housing. They also opened preliminary talks on direct oil procurement. No Gulf state had a role in Seychelles' core public registries as at September 2026.

India activities

NPCI International and the Central Bank of Seychelles signed a memorandum on 29 June 2026 to roll out the Unified Payments Interface by the end of that year. It covers instant mobile and QR payments between bank accounts. It also covers regulatory preparation on standards, cybersecurity and interoperability. It is aimed at the India-source tourist market and at financial inclusion. In February 2026 India's electronics ministry and the ICT department signed a memorandum on sharing digital solutions used at population scale. The specification and the body that governs the Unified Payments Interface are Indian.

Digital sovereignty

A presidential Commission of Inquiry into the Border Management Service Agreement with Travizory Border Security SA opened on 2 September 2026. Travizory's system runs the immigration department's digital border control and the electronic travel authorisation for visitors. In September 2026 the inquiry heard two former ministers describe the established international providers the government considered before Travizory.

Each ministry or agency houses its own data or places it with a cloud provider, and there is no government-wide hosting (2024). Cabinet approved a sovereign, in-country Government Cloud on two datacentres on 28 October 2026. It is to reduce the state's reliance on commercial and overseas infrastructure. The Data Protection Act 2023 requires every processor's contract to let it move personal data to another country only on the controller's instruction. In March 2026 Cable & Wireless Seychelles offered its Tier IV centre as a way for government services to run on infrastructure inside the country.

In March 2026 Cabinet confined PwC's continued work on the ASP Connect social-protection system in 2026 to stabilisation and knowledge transfer to government. It made the ICT department responsible for bringing the system into full government ownership. The immigration department's 2024 digitalisation modules were built with the ICT department within its existing budget. The Central Bank operates the domestic transfer system SEFT itself. SeyID's authentication rests on WISeKey's OISTE public-key infrastructure. Whether the state holds the source code and operating skills for SeyID, or can exit its WISeKey contract, was not on record as at September 2026. The terms of the Travizory agreement, the WISeKey partnership and the 2026 memorandum with NPCI International had not been published as at September 2026. Those terms govern data jurisdiction, portability and exit.
