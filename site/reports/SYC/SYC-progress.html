Edition of 2026-09-06  ·  122 systems and instruments tracked

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Full recordThe restatement came in August 2026 at a credentials ceremony (target).

Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The National ICT Policy of 2007 is still the standing whole-of-government instrument, with no successor, revision or evaluation held.
Full recordThe policy covers ICT infrastructure, the legal and regulatory framework, human resources, industry and government (policy); the document carries no date on its face and 2007 comes from a third-party policy portal.

Delivery has continued without it: the official citizen portal was relaunched in June 2026, timed to the fiftieth anniversary of independence (portal).

Nineteen years on, nothing held records the policy being reviewed, costed or replaced, so what governs ICT strategy is an instrument older than the smartphone.

Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy There is no standalone national broadband strategy; the closest instrument is the regulator's revised wireless-access spectrum policy of 2025.
Full recordThe revised policy governs licence-exempt use of the 2.4 and 5.8 GHz bands for fixed broadband wireless access, superseding the 2004 policy (policy).

The connectivity targets a broadband strategy would carry sit instead in the universal access and universal service provisions of the Communications Act 2023, and nothing held sets a coverage or speed target under either.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy No whole-of-government cloud strategy exists; a spatial data sharing policy provides for distributed hosting, and a Tier IV data centre opened in 2026.
Full recordThe 2020 policy adopts international geospatial principles and provides for distributed data-centre hosting and for OGC and ISO interoperability standards across ministries; it is sectoral, not a whole-of-government instrument (policy).

The country's first Tier IV certified data centre opened at Anse Boileau in March 2026, positioned by its operator as keeping government services and financial institutions onshore rather than on overseas facilities (data centre).

So the capacity arrived before the policy: nothing held states where government workloads are to run, or on what terms.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap No whole-of-government interoperability framework is held; the spatial data sharing policy and the ICT department's standards mandate are the nearest instruments.
Full recordThe 2020 spatial policy provides for OGC and ISO interoperability standards across ministries within its own domain (policy).

The ICT department states that adopted technical standards for the seamless interoperability of e-government services, and the integration of every e-government application and database, fall within its Policy, Standards and Research Section's mandate (mandate); the standards themselves are not published.

What is held is a sectoral policy and a departmental mandate, and no roadmap, target architecture or sequencing plan behind either.

Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy A national science, technology and innovation institute was established by Act with an artificial-intelligence strategy mandate, and the body was reinstated under the Vice-President while no commencement notice for the Act is on record.
Full recordThe Act was assented in December 2025, establishing a body corporate with an artificial-intelligence strategy mandate, a science academy, an open-access scientific-data repository and incubation centres (Act).

The institute was reinstated with its secretariat under the Office of the Vice-President and is described as functioning; no commencement notice for the Act is cited anywhere (reinstatement).

A body operating under a statute whose commencement cannot be evidenced is worth stating plainly. No artificial-intelligence strategy has been produced under the mandate; what exists instead is an education framework, recorded separately.

Full recordThe Act, assented on 9 July 2010, founds the Bureau and the national statistical system and repeals the 2005 statute (Act).

Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy There is no open government data policy; a 2020 corrective action to publish one is not shown closed.
Full recordThe extractives transparency board found in September 2020 that revenue data was published in open format but that the multi-stakeholder group did not appear to have an open data policy, and set a corrective-action deadline to publish one (decision).

Nothing held records that policy being made. In December 2025 the statistics bureau opened two externally financed expressions of interest to modernise its website and improve public access to national data (procurement) — a procurement step, not a portal and not a policy.

Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation The Data Protection Act 2023 is in force in full, its eighteen-month transition having elapsed, and it expressly recognises participation in a global cross-border privacy system.
Full recordThe Act commenced in December 2023 with an eighteen-month transition; that period has elapsed and controllers and processors are now subject to it in full (Act, force, review).

A continental review adds that the Act expressly recognises participation in a global cross-border privacy rules system (reforms).

A statute fully in force with an explicit cross-border interoperability provision puts this unit ahead of most in the frame on the law. What it lacks is recorded in the next cell.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation The Cybercrimes Act 2021 was amended in December 2025, widening offences and adding search and interception powers.
Full recordThe 2021 Act created the first standalone cybercrime offences regime, repealing the Computer Misuse Act, and commenced on 1 February 2022 (Act).

The amendment passed the National Assembly on 15 December and was assented on 22 December 2025; it widens the offence definitions, defines subscriber information as identity, address and billing or service details, and adds search and interception powers (amendment).

Cabinet separately approved a judiciary digitalisation programme in May 2026 expressly requiring stronger cybersecurity and data-management systems around the courts (judiciary). No prosecution, caseload or enforcement figure under the Act is published.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The identity-card statute was amended in 2023 on identity numbers and disclosure; no statute names or governs the SeyID credential itself.
Full recordPart 13 of the 2023 omnibus Act amends the National Identity Cards Act on the national identity number requirement, cards for non-resident citizens and the rules on disclosing information, and does not create the digital credential (Act).

The December 2025 cybercrimes amendment defines subscriber information — identity, address and billing or service details — for evidential purposes, the strongest in-window legal movement touching identity data (amendment).

So the physical card has a statute and the digital credential has none: SeyID rests on administrative practice, which is a different kind of guarantee.

Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation The National Payment System Act 2014 is the statute, and December 2025 regulations made interoperable IT systems a licence condition.
Full recordAct 14 of 2014 commenced on 18 August 2014 and has been amended by the 2022 electronic money regulations and a 2023 amendment Act (Act).

The amendment to the licensing and authorisation regulations, made on 15 December 2025, requires safe and reliable IT systems and adequate interfaces to ensure straight-through processing as a condition of licence for payment, clearing and settlement operators (regulations).

Cabinet approved amendments to the Foreign Exchange Act in July 2026 adding credit unions and payment service providers to the class of authorised dealers (Cabinet); that approval is not yet an enacted instrument.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability The payment licensing regulations make straight-through processing a licence condition; a National Addressing System Bill was Cabinet-approved in April 2026.
Full recordThe December 2025 regulations require payment, clearing and settlement operators to hold safe and reliable IT systems and adequate interfaces for straight-through processing, the clearest statutory interoperability duty the base holds (regulations).

Cabinet approved the National Addressing System Bill in April 2026, creating a National Addressing Department and Database Unit as a shared reference-data layer described as improving cross-agency access to data (Bill).

Both are sectoral. No general statute obliges public bodies to share or expose data to one another, and the addressing Bill is approved rather than enacted.

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations The Broadcasting Corporation Act 2026 requires an AI-use policy for content and broadcast operations — the only statutory AI duty held.
Full recordThe Act, assented on 9 July and gazetted on 13 July 2026, repeals the 2011 statute and requires the Corporation to develop and periodically review a policy on the use of artificial intelligence in content creation, curation and broadcast operations (Act).

It is sector-specific and applies to one state broadcaster. No general artificial-intelligence statute, regulation or code applies across the economy, and the policy the Act requires is not itself held.

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation The Electronic Transactions Act 2001 gives legal effect to electronic records and digital signatures; customs payment rules were digitised in 2026.
Full recordThe founding statute recognises electronic records, digital signatures and electronic dealings with public authorities; the consolidated text held is current to 29 March 2018 (Act), and a 2024 statutory instrument amending its exclusions schedule is not applied to it.

In April 2026 Cabinet approved customs regulations requiring electronic or bank-transfer payment of dues of R50,000 and above and operationalising the ATA Carnet system (Cabinet).

The statute is twenty-five years old and has not been revisited for platforms, marketplaces or consumer contracting online.

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation An access-to-information reform whitepaper recommends a centralised access unit, alignment with the data-protection Act and open-reuse licensing, with drafting targeted for end-2025.

Data protection Data protection authority The Information Commission names no clear power to enforce its orders as its own most urgent weakness, in its own reform whitepaper.
Full recordThe finding is the Commission's own, set out in its access-to-information reform whitepaper, and it marked data protection day in January 2026 (whitepaper, day).

The same body holds both the access-to-information and the data-protection mandates, so the enforcement gap it identifies runs across both.

A regulator publishing that it cannot enforce its own orders is unusually direct, and it is the fact that governs how much weight the fully-in-force statute above can carry.

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers The African Union urged AfCFTA parties to operationalise cross-border data commitments; an EU agreement would bar unjustified localisation.

Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy Parliament amended the broadcasting Act in June 2026 to give appointment and dismissal power over the broadcaster board, and perceived media freedom fell on a survey round fielded in 2025.
Full recordThe base holds no primary text of the amendment; it is reported in a survey dispatch citing two press-freedom organisations (amendment).

So citizen feedback is collected per exercise by whoever is running it, and the standing channel the plan promised is the thing that has not been built.

In August 2026 Seychelles joined a girls-in-space mission training participants in satellite, CubeSat and geospatial data skills, and the address referenced a science, technology, innovation and AI department established in the education ministry earlier that year (mission).

Fourteen years after the policy, no sex-disaggregated figure for internet use, ICT employment or computing enrolment in Seychelles is held.

Access to services Inclusion of persons with disabilities The government itself records no National Accessibility Action Plan and no supporting legislation, against individual building retrofits.
Full recordThe May 2026 statement records concrete accessibility retrofits at the Office of the First Lady and a stadium ramp, and explicitly identifies the absence of a National Accessibility Action Plan and a supporting legislative framework as the structural gap, with a presidential pledge to legislate (statement).

The 2025 voluntary national review states that social protection is being digitised to increase efficiency and reach the most vulnerable, including the elderly and people with disabilities (review).

The absence is stated by the government rather than inferred from silence, which makes it a finding. No accessibility standard applies to any government digital service.

Access to services Inclusion of refugees and IDPs The refugee agency records no known refugees, asylum seekers, stateless persons or displaced people in Seychelles.
Full recordThe agency's own submission to the 38th session of the Universal Periodic Review states there are no known refugees or asylum seekers, no known cases of statelessness and no known internally displaced people in Seychelles (submission); Seychelles has not ratified the 1951 Convention.

This is a dated statement that the population does not exist rather than an undocumented gap, which is a different finding and the reason the indicator does not read as absent evidence. Nothing more recent than 2021 was located.

Digital divides Bridging of digital divides 87.4 per cent are online and 16,800 people are not, against a universal service fund with no disbursement on record.
Full recordThe tracker dated October 2025 puts 117,000 internet users, 87.4 per cent penetration, and an estimated 16,800 people or 12.6 per cent still offline (tracker).

Sections 97 to 102 of the Communications Act 2023 establish universal access and universal service policy, obligations and a fund administered by the communications regulator (Act); the World Bank finds internet cost still a barrier to greater inclusivity despite high coverage (framework).

Three years after the Act, no disbursement from the fund, designated provider obligation or coverage target set under it is held.
