Progress, Oct 2025 – Oct 2026

The progress reports looks for evidence impacting on 115 indicators over the past twelve months. It is a more granular version of the Maturity Assessment.

Movement means that some form of progress, however minor, has been recorded in the period.

The COUNTRIES reports tabulate all indicators for your selected country, grouped by topic. The TOPICS reports tabulate a single indicator across all countries.

Click on a country or indicator to access the detailed report.

Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance across the subjects the repository tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the repository holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this repository, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-10-01 to 2026-10-27.

Progress values. Movement — some form of progress, however minor, has been recorded. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the repository holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the repository holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Movement, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy The 2030 strategy's targets are unchanged, and the high commission seated a scientific committee in October 2025.
Full recordThe strategy was published in May 2025 targeting all public services online by 2030, 500,000 information and communication technology jobs and a fifth of national output from the digital economy, announced the day before. Its targets did not move in the window.
2025-10-23 - the high commission for digitalisation, created by decree in 2023, seated a fifteen-member scientific and technical committee, three of them from the diaspora. It had been operating without one.
No delivery figure against any of the three targets is published, so what has been added is a governance organ rather than a measured position.
2026-09-08 - and what is published instead is description. State media restated the vision as resting on data-protection and cybersecurity laws and on local infrastructure including national data centres, and as aimed at transparency in public management and at combating administrative malpractice. It carries no target, budget or completion date, and sixteen months after publication the repository still holds no delivery figure against the 2030 strategy's own three.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The national digital transformation strategy announced in May 2025 governs, with a high commission and a digitalisation oversight body beneath it.
Full recordAlgeria's strategy is settled and its machinery is what moves. The national digital transformation strategy was announced in May 2025, with a high commission for digitalisation set up on expert-panel advice and a separate oversight body running the national services portal.
Nothing in the twelve months to September 2026 amends or replaces the strategy itself. What the window shows is delivery under it — a national digital services centre inaugurated in July 2026 and a second national data centre at Blida — rather than a new statement of direction.
2026-09-19 - the high commission trained sector information-systems managers on deploying the mechanism for verifying electronic documents issued through the national portal, under a presidential instruction of 12 July 2026 to widen the portal's uses and strengthen cross-sector interoperability. No count of sectors, documents or verifications is published.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy The fibre programme is targeted for completion in 2027, with a universal-service tender for underserved areas in September 2026.
Full recordThere is no standalone broadband strategy document; the target is stated by the minister and delivered by programme. Fibre completion is set for 2027 under the fixed broadband and fibre-to-the-home programme, with new fourth-generation base stations planned and a 400G optical backbone project launched with Huawei.
Coverage of underserved areas was put to the market in the window: a universal-service tender for mobile coverage of 3,000 low-density localities in September 2026, whose calendar, fixed by ministerial order, gives operators 45 days to bid and the regulator 30 days to publish results. International capacity remains measured but unpublished as a target.
2026-10-01 - the posts ministry said Algiers ranked first in Africa and 34th of 50 cities in a 2026 gigabit-city index, a first measurement.
2026-10-11 - the minister put fibre-to-the-home subscriptions above 4 million, with a target of more than 4.4 million by the end of 2026.

Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A second national data centre at Blida took tier-three design certification, and the national cloud is hosted at the Mohammadia data centre.
Full recordAlgeria is building the estate a strategy would govern, and the strategy itself is not published. The national digital services centre opened in July 2026 on dual data centres, and a second national data centre at Blida took tier-three design certification in September.
Beside them sit a research cloud, an artificial-intelligence supercomputing centre at Oran and a private data centre planned at El Affroun. Energy is treated as the competitive argument for hosting here.
The one cloud-specific rule is a 2022 licensing procedure under the general-authorisation regime, and a private provider still describes its cloud authorisation as awaiting regularisation under a deadline that was extended to April 2023.
2026-10-22 — the regulator definitively withdrew a private provider's cloud hosting and storage authorisation for breaching the regime; the breach is not specified.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap On 20 September 2026 the Council of Ministers ordered a presidential decree on a secure interoperability plan, over working exchanges in customs and property transactions.
Full recordAlgeria has more working exchanges than most bases hold and no published framework joining them. The national interoperability system is in development, while individual connections already run: the customs single electronic window, with import and export licences made paperless in August 2026, property transaction data exchanged under a 2025 agreement, and a national system for tracking strategic goods planned.
2026-09-20 - the Council of Ministers ordered a presidential decree on a secure digital interoperability plan, setting the missions and representation of the sectoral bodies under the high commissioner and extended to the wilayas; no draft is published. State media describes a sovereign network, IRIES, linking ministries to the national data centres.
The identifier that would tie them is in place: the national unique identification number established by decree in 2023, now carried into a unified national digital identity.

Full recordThe strategy was adopted by the national artificial-intelligence council in December 2024 on six pillars, and its deployment plan was reviewed by government on 25 May 2026; no Council of Ministers adoption is recorded.
2026-08-05 - a first interministerial meeting fixed priorities: sovereign open-source government models, national high-performance computing, curricula, a research centre and public data hosted in national data centres, with the deployment agreed jointly.
2026-09-08 - a second coordination meeting set up five thematic commissions and a target of 50,000 AI specialists by 2030.
2026-09-22 — a follow-up meeting approved an updated draft decree creating the national AI and data research centre.
2026-10-20 — the fifth follow-up meeting proposed a joint financing fund with companies and a national AI doctoral school with 102 places; the strategy text is still unpublished.
The state's contribution to the UN science and technology commission sets out the national AI council, a strategy dated December 2024 and the national higher school of AI. An analysts' index scored the country's AI policy 16 of 24 (October 2025).

Full recordLocalisation was recorded as mandatory in September 2024, and an April 2026 analysis of digital-sovereignty policy set out the data-protection law that governs data storage and transfer. The instrument imposing it is not itself held.
No later instrument, implementing text or enforcement decision on localisation is held.

Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy A national public-data governance framework was gazetted on 30 December 2025 in twenty articles.
Full recordThe framework did not exist at the window's opening. Presidential Decree 25-320 was gazetted in twenty articles, its article 15 giving the data-protection authority a supervisory hook and article 18 excluding defence, security and judicial data, and was reported as a launch in February 2026.
Its article 19 classification and cataloguing duties are not shown to have commenced. A framework in force whose classification work has not started, and whose interoperability obligations wait on two reference documents that cannot be traced, is a legal position rather than an operating one.
2026-09-07 - a second joint session of the digitalisation high commission and the domestic trade ministry took the data sources themselves as its subject - how they are produced, updated and kept consistent - towards a unified digital mechanism for regulating the national market. It is the first account the repository holds of provenance being treated as the problem rather than the platform.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy The data-governance decree sets no open-data or reuse policy and keeps data flows inside public institutions.
Full recordThe position is unchanged at both ends of the window: the data-governance decree sets no open-data or reuse policy and keeps data flows inside public institutions, and a named expert criticises it for closing data to private and academic users.
It is a cited absence rather than an unexamined one, and it is a design choice rather than an oversight: the decree that governs public data was gazetted inside the window and is built around exchange between administrations on a network kept physically separate from the internet, not around publication.

Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation Law 18-07 stands as amended, its officer and breach-notification duties added by the 2025 amendment.
Full recordThe law was enacted in 2018 and in force from August 2023, and the 2025 amendment added data-protection-officer and breach-notification duties.
Nothing amended it further in the window. What moved is enforcement practice rather than the statute: the authority ran a compliance push on officers, processing registers and audit logs, and issued a deliberation treating images as personal data - both under a law whose own text the repository does not hold.

Full recordThe presidential decree signed on 20 January 2020 is the founding instrument of the national apparatus: it creates the national council for information systems security and the national agency that carries the work.
The security referential the repository already held names this decree without carrying its text, so the instrument behind the framework is now on the record rather than cited at one remove.

Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id Law 26-02 on trust services and electronic identification was gazetted on 17 February 2026, three and a half months after the Council of Ministers approved the draft.
Full recordThe law did not exist at the window's opening. The draft was approved by the Council of Ministers on 2 November 2025, reported in the biometrics press and put before the National Assembly by early December.
2026-02-17 - it was promulgated as Law 26-02 and gazetted, defining electronic identification means at three levels of assurance and creating a national electronic certification authority.
A unified national digital identity is already issued and in use as a single credential across the government services portal; no implementing text tying that credential to the law's assurance levels is held.

Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A first regulation dedicated to payment service providers was followed by one bringing them inside the anti-money-laundering regime.
Full recordThe umbrella statute is the monetary and banking law of June 2023, whose article 166 repealed the prior ordinance.
2025-09-24 - a second regulation explicitly brings payment service providers inside the anti-money-laundering regime, and in November the banking commission issued guidelines on virtual assets. No licence granted under the provider regime is recorded.
2025-08-17 - the operating rules followed the authorisation conditions: an instruction establishing three tiers of payment account by permitted balance at DZD 100,000, DZD 500,000 and DZD 1,000,000, allowing remote account opening by a secured technological process at all three, requiring providers to verify an agent's commercial registration, standing, experience and resources, and confining payment services to Algerian dinars inside the national territory. It also governs admission to the payment systems, reporting duties and the central bank's supervisory powers. No provider has been authorised on the record.
2026-10 — the draft 2027 finance law proposes a 10% tax reduction, to the end of 2028, for small taxpayers who take at least half their receipts electronically, and would make non-resident digital platforms register with the tax authority and remit VAT quarterly.

Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability A national data-governance system was launched in February 2026 under a presidential decree of December 2025.
Full record2026-02-09 - the prime minister launched the national data-governance system established by a presidential decree of 30 December 2025.
The trust-services law of February 2026 is the other enabling instrument: it establishes the national trust-service and electronic-identification framework across 116 articles and requires trust-service-provider data to be hosted nationally.
A digitalisation law covering data, digital services, electronic exchange and system security is in the final stages of study, with no draft published.
None is accompanied by an operating rule, a participating body, a dataset exchanged or a service built on it, so what is established is the legal frame and not its use.

That is nine months of a stated preparation with nothing published, against a national strategy whose deployment roadmap was agreed in the same period and a university assistant announced to steer module allocation and mark student answers from the 2026-2027 academic year. Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation Law 18-05 is in force while most online selling runs through social-media pages outside its obligations, cash on delivery dominant.
Full recordThe law is in force, and the great majority of online selling runs through social-media pages outside its obligations, with cash on delivery dominant.
The assessment is named-analyst opinion from a consumer organisation rather than a state position; the law's own date and text are not held, and no enforcement action or compliance figure is on file. A statute whose scope most of the activity it governs sits outside, with no published enforcement, is the position - and the payment side matches it, with fewer than a tenth of merchants operating a card terminal.

Full recordThe office's own presentation to an international quality workshop names the 1994 legislative decree as the governing statistics law, with its organisation set by a 1995 decree whose article 6 makes it responsible for the technical preparation, conduct, processing and analysis of population censuses.
2025-11-10 - a presidential decree transferred oversight of the office, and a Council of Ministers sitting the same autumn took its modernisation.
The institution is being rearranged around a statute that is thirty-two years old, and no replacement bill is on the record.

That is a press-access provision inside a media statute, not a general right of access. No right-to-information law, no request procedure, no refusal ground and no appeal route open to a citizen appears anywhere on this ledger, and no request, refusal or appeal under the media law's provisions is recorded either.

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation The 1989 Maghreb Union treaty is in force with a binding judicial organ and no summit since 1994.
Full recordThe treaty concluded at Marrakesh in February 1989 has been in force since 1 July 1989 and establishes a presidential council, a council of foreign ministers, a general secretariat, a consultative council and a judicial organ whose rulings bind.
No summit has been held since 1994, so the instrument supplies a legal frame for harmonisation and no working machinery. Harmonisation between Maghreb states therefore proceeds bilaterally where it proceeds at all.

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers The 2018 law's prior-authorisation and adequacy regime was carried into the amending law of July 2025.
Full recordThe founding act created the data-protection authority and, at chapter IV and notably article 44, set the prior-authorisation and adequacy regime governing transfers abroad.
2025-07-24 - the amending law carried the regime forward, in a gazette issue that also carries a virtual-assets amendment to the anti-money-laundering law.
The authority's own compliance guide describes an operational portal and states that processing involving transfer requires its authorisation. No adequacy decision, authorisation count or refusal is published, so the regime's operation cannot be checked.

Standards National interoperability standards An interoperability referential has bound public system purchases since 2019, and a security referential covers 14 domains.
Full recordOlder than the window: Executive Decree 19-271 of October 2019 fixed a national interoperability referential that public bodies must write into the specifications of any information system they buy or develop to exchange data, defence and security systems excepted. The referential itself is annexed to the decree and not printed, and no update order is held.
The clearest national technical standard the repository holds is an information-security referential covering 14 domains including asset management, access control, cloud-services security and incident handling. It is security, not interoperability.
The addressing side does have one: the national addressing procedures guide sets a data model and procedural standard.
No published message standard or conformity test appears anywhere, and the national interoperability system this ledger holds separately is a system rather than a standard.
