Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

5G went commercially live across all wilayas for one operator within four months of the licence awards (Ooredoo, 2026-03-12), the fixed-internet base passed 7 million subscribers with more than 3.4 million on fibre (2026-05, minister's figures), and the state data-centre estate was inaugurated on 2026-07-05 with administrations ordered to interconnect within one month.

Presidential Decree 25-320 makes a national interoperability system the exclusive channel for inter-agency data exchange, but its classification and cataloguing duties start only when two référentiels are published, and none had been traced as at 2026-07-20. Nothing held establishes that the gazetted instrument yet binds anyone.

The national portal held 21 services in 2026-07 against targets of 62 by end-2027 and 350 by 2028 (HCN), and a services-import platform entered operation on 2026-08-01 as the exclusive route for import applications. No source in the base carries a budget figure for any of it.

Three licences were awarded in 2025-12 for DZD 63.9bn, reported as about US$492m at the time (ITWeb Africa), and rollout then ran ahead of a published six-year plan that does not reach nationwide coverage until 2031.

Two things sit behind the build figures. The state now owns the compute base as well as the network — the National Digital Services Centre runs two active-active sites built by Huawei to Uptime Tier III design, while the AI facility at Oran has been under construction since 2025-03 with no commissioning date or capacity figure since. 5G and a satellite-ISP round opened in 2026-04 went to competitive ARPCE tenders, but renewal of the two incumbent GSM licences went to the Council of Government as draft executive decrees. Under article 138 of Law 18-04 the regulator had registered two authorisations for private cabled networks as at 31 December 2024 and had received no applications for registration certificates under the simple-declaration regime (ARPCE).

Presidential Decree 26-07, gazetted in Journal officiel n° 04 of 2026-01-18, requires every public body to stand up a structure covering both information-systems security and personal-data protection, organisationally separate from its IT function. The strategy, the agency behind it and the sectoral incident-response centre all report through the Ministry of National Defence and the Presidency, not the telecommunications regulator.

The national single window launched 2025-10-17 held 21 live services in 2026-07 against targets of 62 by end-2027 and 350 by 2028.

Land titles went nationwide in 2026-01; eleven further sectoral management systems are in operation and not one publishes an adoption or usage figure.

Decree 25-320 makes the interoperability system the only permitted channel for inter-agency exchange, on a network kept physically separate from the internet, and the trade ministry announced the services-import platform in operation from 2026-08-01 as the exclusive route for those applications. The digital-identity and trust-services law was before the National People's Assembly in 2025-12 with no promulgation on record, while all-digital vehicle registration requires the biometric card for transfers.

Payments carry more than 22 million cards and 104,000 terminals at end-March 2026, on electronic payment value of DZD 939bn in 2025 (2026-05-15) — and two announced rails remain unconfirmed: a QR mobile system nothing held confirms went live, and the central bank's domestic instant switch, announced in 2024-10. On 2026-05-31 the Bank of Algeria set the composition and working arrangements of the National Payments Committee by Règlement 26-01, the first detailed framework for a body Law 23-09 created; the national payments strategy it is to prepare is not drafted and the annual report it owes is not published (2026-08).

One official statistical release is held: the regulator's annual report for 2024 puts the combined fixed and mobile base at 60.98 million at 31 December 2024, 47.30 million of the 54.05 million mobile subscribers on 4G, and fixed internet at 6,216,146 (ARPCE). No quarterly observatory or statistics-office series is held, so every later connectivity and payment figure here reaches the base through a minister's statement or the trade press.

Open data

Open government data policy or portal Not held —

The personal-data regime is Law 18-07, in force from August 2023 and amended in 2025 to add data-protection-officer and breach-notification duties (Digital Policy Alert, 2026-01). The authority began a compliance push in 2025-12; no enforcement decision against a named controller is held.

The authority's deliberation no. 04 of 2026-07-15 treats identifiable photographs and videos as personal data, and publication via platforms hosted outside Algeria as an international transfer under articles 44–45 (ANPDP); it takes effect on publication on its own website, with no Journal officiel step. Algeria also requires mandatory in-country storage in a cloud market no hyperscaler has entered.

Presidential Decree 25-320 is the framework instrument. Its article 18 removes defence, national-security and judicial data from it, and its article 19 starts the classification and cataloguing obligations only on publication of two référentiels, unpublished as at 2026-07-20.

A national information-verification system was examined in cabinet on 2026-07-23 with no regulator, legal basis or oversight mechanism named anywhere held; a proposition de loi on platform regulation has been under examination at the assembly since 30 October 2025 and would bind platforms above a million users to a local office, in-country storage, 24-hour takedown and a regulator attached to the Presidency — the outlet's reading rather than the bill's text.

African ICT ministers adopted the Algiers Declaration on fair, safe and responsible digital platforms in Africa at Algiers on 2025-12-07, setting founding principles for a common framework on the global platforms; African Union political adoption is pending and none is held (2026-08). Domestically, Law 18-05 on electronic commerce is in force, and the consumer-protection body assessed on 2026-08-04 that the great majority of online selling runs through social-media pages outside its obligations, with cash on delivery dominant.

Algeria signed at Shanghai on 2026-07-16 as a founding member of a new world artificial-intelligence organisation, according to CARE, which names neither the organisation nor a ratification step.

The National AI Strategy was adopted by the National AI Council in December 2024 on six pillars, and its deployment plan was reviewed by government on 2026-05-25 ahead of a Council-of-Ministers adoption nothing held records. Implementation opened at a first interministerial meeting on 2026-08-05, which fixed a roadmap on sovereign open-source government models, national computing capacity, training, a national AI research centre and public data held in national data centres, and created an interministerial follow-up commission; no budget or timetable is stated. An ethical-AI law has been in preparation since 2025-11 with no draft text or timetable on file since.

In education, from the 2026–2027 academic year an assistant will steer each student's learning pathway and a language model will generate teaching material and mark student answers. No appeal route, accuracy claim, disclosure duty or human-review threshold is reported.

On compute, Djezzy launched a sovereign AI cloud in 2026-04, and cooperation with Huawei on AI and GPUs was reported as talks with no agreement stated. The first start-up cluster opened at Sidi Abdellah in 2026-04. In hardware, ENIE obtained GIE Monétique accreditation, reported 2026-07-31, extending it from its own-brand devices to every payment terminal on national territory whatever its brand.

Ministerial order 1275 lets final-year students convert projects into start-ups or patents, with 673 start-ups, 3,949 patents and 2,339 labels, 2022–2026-07 (El Watan).

No deployed surveillance technology is established: Algeria appears in an academic mapping of Chinese-supplied AI surveillance across eleven African countries (2026-03), but no contract, vendor, system name or site is held.

Penetration stood at 76.9% and 10.9 million people offline in early 2025 (DataReportal). The instrument aimed at that remainder reached 1,400 areas in its first phase (2026-05), with no target, budget or completion date alongside the figure.

The "7.77" programme recorded 64,508 registrations across all 58 wilayas by 2026-05, of which 19,691 — 30% — chose artificial intelligence ahead of professional cybersecurity at 9,678 (2026-05-21).

Fewer than 10% of merchants operate a terminal (GIE Monétique, cited 2026-05), and an Internet Society study found only 13% of Algerian web traffic served locally in 2026-06.

Algeria committed US$1bn to a continental startup fund (2025-10-21) while its own startups raised US$5.3m in the whole of 2025, and an Omani delegation offered partnerships across AI, data centres and fintech in 2026-07 with no instrument concluded. No source in this base carries a budget line for the programme, so nothing here can be costed.
