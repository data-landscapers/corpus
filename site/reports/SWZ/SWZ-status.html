This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In May 2026 the Digital Landscape Assessment found that 20 of Eswatini's 22 assessed ministries had no approved ICT strategy. The ICT ministry endorsed the report on 21 May 2026 as the foundation of the next national e-Government Strategy.

The Digitalisation Strategy 2024-2028, branded 'Government in Your Hand', counts data exchange and interoperability among its aims. In 2025 the machinery to deliver it was only planned or in draft, and delivery ran ministry by ministry. A Digital Government Implementation Roadmap was still under development in 2025, under the World Bank-financed Digital Eswatini project. Terms of reference for a consultant had been published, and nothing had been finalised.

In December 2025 the Central Bank and the Ministry of Finance launched a National FinTech Strategy 2025-2030. It aims to cut reliance on cash and make the country a regional fintech hub. A National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2023-2028 runs beside it. Hosting has no policy of its own: the National Cybersecurity Strategy 2022-2027 stands in for a government cloud policy. The same strategy covers data protection, identification, payments and signatures.

In July 2025 Smart Africa tendered the national data policy on a US$70,000 budget. A first National Data Policy Workshop followed in April 2026. There the ICT minister described data as an asset to be managed like land, water and financial reserves. Stakeholders validated a National Data Governance Policy on 27 July 2026, with an implementation cycle of 2027-2031. In September 2026 the policy was reported adopted with an implementation roadmap and a monitoring and evaluation plan. No published text, adoption instrument or implementing body has been found. In August 2026 it was still undecided whether a standalone law or amendments to existing laws would back the policy. In 2025 the data governance body sat inside another institution, with no data strategy behind it.

Legislation and regulation

In 2025 the legislative basis for sharing data across government had still to be created. Neither the Data Protection Act 2022 nor the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 2022 provides for it, or governs a data exchange system.

The Computer Crime and Cybercrime Act 2022 is the cybercrime statute. It also carries the country's provisions on digital signatures, electronic commerce and digital payments. The Electronic Communications Act governs quality of service and universal access, and reaches into identification, payments and signatures. Legal identity rests on the Identification Order, 1998, which compiles the national population register. The Order provides for an identity document from the age of 16. In 2024 there was no regulation specific to digital identity. Payment service providers are licensed under the National Payment Systems Act 2023.

All four of the ICT Acts of 2022 were tabled with certificates of urgency, which shortened the time for public input. In August 2025 the government said bills on cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, e-commerce, and the governance of AI, robotics and satellite activity were in preparation. In 2025 no right to information law was in force, and a bill remained in draft. On 19 October 2026 the Senate adopted its ICT committee's report on the Critical Infrastructure Protection Bill, backing fines of up to E5 million for companies and ten years' imprisonment for individuals.

In August 2026 the ICT ministry told Parliament it had completed Online Media Publishing Guidelines, which require online publishers to register. The guidelines bind publishers to accuracy, corrections, privacy, safeguards for children and the responsible use of AI. An immigration bill before Parliament in September 2026 would let a system-generated communication bar a foreign national's entry, at a cost put at about E6 million.

Data protection

In June 2026 ESCCOM opened an investigation into the Ministry of Home Affairs over the exposure of citizens' ID documents. The ministry had published unblurred national identity documents on X. It was the authority's first major enforcement action against a government ministry.

The Data Protection Act 2022 has been in force since 4 March 2022, and binds the public and private sectors. It treats biometric data as sensitive personal information. It reaches businesses outside the country that target its residents. As enacted in 2022 it provides for fines of up to E5,000,000 or 2% of annual turnover. It requires a breach to be notified to the authority and to the affected person. It imposes no data localisation requirement. Guidelines of August 2025 set 72 hours for notifying the authority of a breach. The communications regulator also polices data protection, and there is no separate data-protection institution.

In mid-2025 the World Bank recorded that the data protection office had still not been operationalised. By February 2026 the authority was setting out eight data-subject rights in a public campaign. In July 2026 it said it had concluded a complaint over a health practitioner's unlawful disclosure of a patient's information. In 2024 it kept a mandatory register of controllers and processors, and ran a cross-border transfer authorisation process. By 31 March 2026, 511 had registered. It has issued enforcement notices against MTN Eswatini, Liberty Life Swaziland, the Royal Science and Technology Park and the Sincephetelo Motor Vehicle Fund.

In 2024 the Association for Progressive Communications read section 36 as giving the authority grounds to take no action on complaints. The Act applies to payment service providers as a general law, with nothing specific to payment transaction data. Eswatini's Digital Freedom score fell 15.5 points over 2014-2023 to 39.9 out of 100, 38th of 54 African states.

In August 2026 draft sector guidelines under the Act were validated at a week-long workshop. The regulator named the financial sector as the leading source of personal-data breaches. The ICT ministry said in August 2026 that enforcement and oversight of compliance fall outside its mandate.

Regional collaboration

The national switch carries domestic payments only. Interlinking to SADC's Transactions Cleared on an Immediate Basis scheme is identified as a priority, and there is no live retail cross-border corridor. High-value settlement continues to run over SADC-RTGS. A central bank directive in force from March 2025 requires low-value transfers within the Common Monetary Area to move off SADC-RTGS onto that scheme by 31 March 2027. Retail money already crosses the border through wallets. MTN customers in South Africa have sent directly to MoMo wallets in Eswatini since December 2024. The national identity document is not yet interoperable for cross-border recognition. In 2025 the country took part in the SADC initiative working towards a regional digital identity framework.

Transfers of personal data to other SADC member states run under regional data protection standards, and anything beyond the bloc turns on an adequacy assessment. The model is a regional-bloc one, not a purely national one. In October 2026 the ICT minister said the African Union's Malabo Convention on cyber security and personal data protection was before Parliament for ratification.

Eswatini was welcomed into the Smart Africa Alliance as one of six recently joined members at its Council of Ministers on 24 March 2026. In 2025 the alliance tendered the national data policy on a US$70,000 budget. Smart Africa also developed the draft health-data guidelines and sponsors their validation workshops, with technical and financial support from GIZ. Support for the National Data Governance Policy runs through the Team Europe Data Governance in Africa Initiative with GIZ and the African Union. Much of the country's own rulebook for data is being written through these continental and European channels.

Talks with the Central Bank of Egypt in July 2026 covered accelerating Eswatini's accession to the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System. In 2024 Eswatini became the second African state to launch phase two of the UN Economic Commission for Africa's registration and vital statistics mentorship programme. The work includes linking birth and death registration to the national population register.

Standards

In 2025 Eswatini had no government open source policy and no recorded adoption of open source software in the public sector. Electronic government procurement was under implementation in 2025. It published tender notices and awarded contracts, and enforced technical standards on suppliers. The same index recorded service-level standards and a programme to retire legacy systems onto a common standard. It recorded no data quality standard for exchange between government systems. The e-government interoperability framework published in March 2025 adopts XML for data integration across government. The Central Bank publishes the full rulebook of the national payment systems, freely and without registration.

From 28 July 2026 the data protection authority validated draft guidelines on processing personal health data. Its workshops at Ezulwini brought in the health ministry, providers, insurers, pharmacies and medical aid schemes. It intends the guidelines to issue as a regulatory instrument under the Data Protection Act 2022. They cover collection, retention, sharing, breach notification, cloud services, impact assessments and patient rights. The Act already bars processing sensitive data without explicit authorisation, and requires breach notification and a data protection officer.

In 2026 the standards authority's catalogue listed 28 domesticated information-technology standards, among them ISO records management. The Eswatini Standards Authority is ISO's member body for the country, taking part in 2 technical committees and observing 15 (November 2026). On 27 October 2026 the authority launched an AI Safety and Standards Centre of Excellence with AICE Africa. It is to train standards officers, auditors and regulators and develop a proposed AI testing laboratory. The Electronic Communications Act requires licensed operators to run complaint-handling mechanisms. Consumers have a month to complain about postal and courier services, three months for broadcasting and six months for telecommunications.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In 2025 Eswatini had no e-participation or public consultation platform, and no citizen feedback or grievance mechanism.

Eswatini's Deliberative and Participatory Governance score fell 3.9 points over 2014-2023 to 23.7 out of 100, 47th of 54 African states. All four ICT Acts of 2022 were tabled with certificates of urgency, which shortened the time for public input. In 2025 no right to information law was in force, and a bill was in draft.

Media Freedom rose 9.3 points over 2014-2023 to 43.6 out of 100, 40th of 54. Freedom of Expression and Belief fell 18.2 points over the same decade to 36.2, 43rd of 54. Public perception of freedom of speech sat at the index's floor score. During the 2021 unrest the state directed EPTC, MTN Eswatini and Eswatini Mobile to suspend internet services. Social media was restricted again that October.

In August 2026 the ICT ministry said its Online Media Publishing Guidelines 2026 would require online publishers to register. The ministry's reason is that mainstream outlets carry permit and licensing costs and formal oversight, while online platforms compete for the same audience without them. In July 2025 parents and teachers refused a school biometric gate-pass scheme over consent and child-data privacy. In August 2026 a senator told the Senate that data governance is where the country is most exposed. The communications regulator said in August 2026 that cyberbullying is a criminal offence under the computer crime statute.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2026/27 estimates carry the first money of the World Bank's Digital Eswatini loan, E30 million, with no local funds on the project. The 2026/27 estimates also cut the state's own line for the integrated online e-services platform to E1.75 million. That line had stood at E30 million in 2024/25.

The digital lines of the 2026/27 estimates come to about E471 million proposed, 88.5% of it the state's own money and the rest World Bank and Taiwanese financing (2026/27).

The largest of the state's own lines in 2026/27 are E130.5 million for the 2027 population and housing census and E119.1 million for the Royal Science and Technology Park. The Park's sum is a recurrent subvention, and the recurrent estimates print no financing source against their digital lines. Taiwanese grants financed a E36 million 5G government network in 2024/25, with no local funds beside them. Taiwanese grants also pay for rural school computer laboratories and computers, E14.4 million in 2026/27.

In 2024/25 the Universal Access and Service Fund took in about E10 million from the operators' levy and E10 million from the regulator. The fund was set up under the Electronic Communications Act 2013 and is administered by the communications regulator. It spent E14.2 million in 2024/25, E3.7 million of it on last-mile connectivity at 20 sites and E0.8 million on e-government support. Under section 49(10) of the Communications Act 2013, revenue the regulator leaves unused at year-end goes to the universal service programme.

In June 2026 the World Bank's appraisal of Digital Eswatini committed the government to budget for the rising internet costs of schools, clinics and other public institutions. Those costs fall to the line ministries in the project's later stages. The project buys last-mile capacity as indefeasible rights of use that run beyond its July 2031 close.

MoUs and other agreements

In April 2026 Eswatini's memorandum of understanding with Google became a cabinet dispute. Prime Minister Russell Dlamini first dismissed it, saying Google was unaware of it and calling it a national-security threat. He then acknowledged in writing that Cabinet had approved it. The Attorney-General confirmed that Minister Savannah Maziya had consulted Cabinet and the Attorney-General before signing. The partnership was announced in January 2025 and covers Google Workspace for public servants, artificial intelligence, digital addressing and youth digital skills. No other standing agreement with a named technology vendor has been found. An investigation of the row found the memorandum a framework for cloud services and e-government, not a monetisation or revenue-sharing deal.

In February 2026 the ICT minister met Global South Utilities on powering the National Data Centre. No agreement was signed, and no capacity, price or timetable was stated. In July 2026 the central banks of Egypt and Eswatini discussed a pan-African gold bank. The gold bank rests on a memorandum signed on 29 December 2025 between the Central Bank of Egypt and Afreximbank. It is at feasibility-study stage, and Eswatini is not a party to it.

New investments

The World Bank's Digital Eswatini Project is active over 2026–2031, with a digital-sector commitment of about US$20 million. It covers broadband, government platforms and the enabling environment for the digital economy. In June 2026 the ICT ministry was training change agents ahead of a 2027 rollout of the E1.2bn programme. In September 2026, US$26m of the programme was a government-guaranteed loan to the posts and telecommunications corporation. Its borrowing Bills took a second reading that month. On 5 October 2026 the ICT minister told senators that Parliament had approved the guarantee. He said in October 2026 that E26m of the loan goes to the post office pension fund.

The IFMIS and PFM Capacity Building Project, about US$5 million, ran from 2020 to 2023. It rolled the financial management system out to line ministries. A World Bank health project appraised in May 2020 finances the electronic Community-Based Health Information System. Its cost on the system is not split out. In 2021 India's Export-Import Bank signed a line of credit of about US$10 million for a disaster recovery site for the national data centre. World Bank operations also extend the Imbeleko social registry, from 2025, and electrify rural areas for 200,000 people, from February 2025.

Eswatini's digital estate has drawn 19 external commitments, worth about US$61 million on windows running from 2015 to 2031 (November 2026).

The World Bank holds three of the commitments and US$35 million of the money, and India's Export-Import Bank, the only bilateral lender on record, US$10 million (2020-2031). USAID's four grants, the largest for a client management information system, come to about US$15 million (2015-2019).

Sectoral information systems take the most commitments, eight, chiefly health and client-management systems; connectivity has three, led by the Digital Eswatini Project, and data-centre disaster recovery and public financial management one each (2015-2031).

Most of the money is lending: two World Bank loans and India's line of credit carry US$35 million of it, while the 18 grants come to about US$16 million and the health project's instrument is not stated (2015-2031).

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In May 2026 15 of Eswatini's 22 assessed ministries were running on internet connections of 1 Mbps or less. In the same year the state put E1.2 billion behind a Digital Eswatini programme to connect schools, clinics and communities. About E648 million is for broadband and E450 million for the government services platform (June 2026). A nationwide fibre rollout was under way in February 2026. In July 2026 the ICT minister told Parliament that international capacity had reached 120 Gbps and fibre 26,528 home passes. Capacity had stood at 72 Gbps in August 2025.

LTE and WiMAX covered 87% of the population in 2024. 57.6% of individuals used the internet in 2025. Those left out are mostly rural people, the elderly and people with disabilities (2024). Mobile connections ran at 139% of the population on GSMA's count in late 2025. That counts SIMs, not people, and no unique-subscriber figure has been published. The cheapest 2GB mobile plan costs about 3.4% of monthly GNI per capita, against the UN Broadband Commission's 2% target (2025). Fixed broadband costs around 19% (2025).

In 2024 the country had no active internet exchange point, so domestic traffic is handed off abroad. There is no in-country hyperscale cloud region, though zones in the same sub-region sit 6-10ms away (2025). The National Data Centre is carrier-neutral, on two diverse provider feeds. A Starlink Business reseller launched locally in July 2025, and the government commissioned a Starlink gateway in April 2026.

In June 2025 the regulator decided to switch off 2G by 31 December 2028 and 3G by 31 December 2030. It banned imports of 2G- and 3G-only devices from 30 November 2025. The sector runs under the Electronic Communications Act, and SIM registration is mandatory against a national ID or passport. In 2021 the government directed operators to suspend internet services during the June–July unrest. Mobile communications was Eswatini's second most improved of 96 Ibrahim Index indicators over 2014-2023, reaching 65.2 out of 100. Internet and computers reached only 43.9, and public perception of infrastructure fell to 49.1 (2023).

Data Storage

In September 2026 the finance minister told Parliament that the facility at the Royal Science and Technology Park was not a fully operational data centre. On 24 September the Park confirmed an incident at the facility that disrupted at least 12 government services. On 23 October 2026 the ICT ministry said 13 services had been restored, while the government website was still being restored. In May 2026 the state's Digital Landscape Assessment named government's near-universal dependence on the Park a "structural concentration risk". Ministries have no internal fallback if that capacity fails.

In 2025 the Park described the National Data Centre as the country's basic government data centre. It is carrier-neutral on two diverse provider feeds, and backed by four 500 KVA diesel generators. It sells colocation and remote hands, and platform, infrastructure and software as a service. The operator's 2023 brochure describes a Tier III facility at 99.982% uptime. No capacity, named tenant or utilisation figure has been found. In 2024 the wider estate was small-scale Tier I-II facilities only. In 2025 there was no hyperscale region in the country, and the nearest availability zones were 6-10ms away.

The 2021 budget speech recorded construction begun on a Disaster Recovery Site at KaLanga, with E58 million allocated. A US$10.4 million India Exim Bank line of credit signed in 2021 was to finance it. In October 2025 the communications regulator advertised a tender for consultancy on a data centre disaster recovery facility. In September 2026 the finance minister told Parliament that the project had failed to proceed. In October 2026 his ministry said the loan stands with 1% disbursed, and that it had asked the lender to cancel only the commitment fee on the undrawn balance.

In May 2026 nine of the roughly 22 ministries assessed kept no regular backups. Seven backed up daily, three weekly and three monthly, and eleven held no ICT maintenance contract. Water, mining, energy and social-grant data were flagged at risk of unrecoverable loss. The Data Protection Act 2022 imposes no localisation requirement.

Energy

In 2024 nearly three in five Eswatini firms reported electrical outages, at around 1.2 a month. In February 2025 the remaining 12% of the population was still without electricity. Access to energy scored 65.5 out of 100 in 2023, twelfth of 54 African states and up 11.2 points over the decade.

In 2020 urban access stood at 92.2% against 75.8% in rural areas. In February 2025 the World Bank expanded its support for an off-grid and rural electrification programme reaching 200,000 people. In 2026/27 the energy regulator prices a subsidised lifeline block, the first 75 kWh a month, at E1.5685 per kWh. Domestic supply above it costs E2.8624 per kWh, and small commercial supply E3.3920.

In 2024 most generation was domestic hydro and bagasse. Thermal power imported from South Africa is not counted in the domestic generation figures. In February 2026 the ICT minister met an Abu Dhabi clean-energy firm in Dubai about power for the National Data Centre. No agreement, capacity, price or timetable came out of the meeting.

Technical Capacity

In May 2026 Eswatini's assessed government estate had 18 ICT professionals, and 15 of its ministries had none in-house. Eleven held no formal ICT maintenance contract. Ministries relied on the Royal Science and Technology Park for hosting, maintenance and system development, with nothing internal to fall back on.

In 2024 the University of Eswatini offered degrees in computer science, information technology and computer science education, with master's provision above them. Measured e-government readiness rose sharply in 2024. In 2023 the civil service ranked 42nd of 54 African states on effective administration, and had gone backwards over the decade. In 2025 the data protection office still awaited full operationalisation.

In July 2026 Eswatini said small and developing nations should not become mere consumers of technologies developed elsewhere. It said they should invest in local innovation, research, computing infrastructure and data people can trust.

Cybersecurity

Eswatini sits in the establishing tier of the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index 2024. In October 2026 the ICT minister cited its score in that edition as 79.43, up from 18.23. In May 2026 the state's own assessment found critical state information at risk in nine ministries. Nine of the roughly 22 ministries kept no regular data backups, and only seven backed up daily. The Ministry of Finance runs mainframe software past end-of-life and without vendor support.

The National Cybersecurity Strategy 2022-2027 covers cybersecurity, data centres and cloud services, data protection, digital identification, payments and signatures. On 1 October 2026 the ICT minister said a review of the strategy has begun. The Computer Crime and Cybercrime Act 2022 is the binding statute. A Critical Infrastructure Protection Bill was before the Senate in October 2026. Breach notification is mandatory under the data protection regime.

In October 2025 the Prime Minister said the communications commission had been designated the National Cybersecurity Agency. Its incident response team is tasked with protecting critical national infrastructure. In 2026 the agency held the country's first national cyber drill, a ransomware simulation facilitated by Lesotho's regulator. In October 2026 the communications regulators of Eswatini and Lesotho signed a memorandum on cybersecurity cooperation, including coordinated incident response. In October 2026 the Director of Public Prosecutions said her office has set up a Cybercrime Unit. In 2025 a digital forensic unit worked on cybercrime investigation.

In July 2025, six weeks after the Government in Your Hand platform launched, a reporter found other people's data on it. The eGovernment services director conceded the gaps. A government mobile application testing laboratory, funded by Taiwan, opened on 28 August 2026.

DPI

Data Exchange

The one substantial data link running inside Eswatini's government sits in the health sector, where the Population Registry feeds the health service's Master Patient Index over FHIR for real-time patient identification (2026). Nothing comparable joins the rest of the state. Agriculture, the electoral register, employment and labour market data, driver licensing, passports and immigration, education records, justice and case management, health and payments all sit outside any cross-government exchange (2025). They sit outside it because no such platform exists (2025).

The 2024 Digital Readiness Assessment put duplicated processes and a missing interoperability framework at the top of what limits the country's digital public infrastructure. More than half of the ministries assessed lack meaningful interoperability or connectivity to a central repository (May 2026). The assessment's first recommendation to government is to develop and implement a national interoperability framework (2024). Version 1.0 of the e-government interoperability framework was published in March 2025. Compliance with it is mandatory for ministries and agencies.

The legal footing has still to be laid: neither the Data Protection Act 2022 nor the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 2022 establishes authority for cross-government data sharing or governs an exchange system. Where one agency needs another's data it arranges the matching itself. The Revenue Service's 2025 Digital Transformation Agenda checks tax records against data held on properties, trusts, commerce and vehicle registrations to verify declarations.

The World Bank-financed Digital Eswatini project was approved in 2025. It is designed to build the back-end interoperability framework alongside a digital identity and a digitalised National Population Register. The terms of reference for the Government in Your Hand roadmap scope the intended platform across digital identity, data exchange, social registries, public key infrastructure and payments (2025).

Digital Identity and CRVS

Production of national identity cards was suspended in late September 2025 and resumed only on 22 February 2026. The halt disrupted access to banking, employment and travel nationwide. In 2024 the card, issued in physical form since 2003, was not a digital credential. It carries no biometrics, and a holder cannot be authenticated directly or through a government portal. There is no digital-ID regulation and no identity act codifying such a credential. The state's flagship app authenticates users by national ID number and an SMS one-time password.

Under the Identification Order 1998 an identity document is issued from the age of 16. The Order establishes the National Population Register and takes the applicant's fingerprints into it. Under fees effective from March 2021, first enrolment is free within three months of turning 16. It then costs E100 up to age 25 and E250 from 26, with replacement at E300. Public perception of how easily an identity document can be obtained fell 26.5 points to 38.0 out of 100 in 2023.

In 2025 banks took the card as the primary know-your-customer document. A national ID or passport is required to register a SIM card, and the Elderly Grant is not paid without one. In 2022 refugees and non-citizens with valid status were eligible for a 'non-Swazi' document. In October 2025 the central bank launched an electronic know-your-customer registry for banks, financial institutions and some public bodies. In 2024 the Home Affairs ministry assigned a Personal Identification Number on registration of a vital event. Identity data governance rests on the general Data Protection Act 2022, with no identity-specific sharing framework. The Digital Eswatini project's US$25 million Government in Your Hand component is to deploy building blocks including digital ID, on its 2025 design.

A network blackout on 16 September 2026 stopped all identity card and passport processing at Home Affairs and at postal counters, with no offline procedure. In September 2026 the Civil Service Commission found an assistant regional civil registrar guilty of unlawful processing. The registrar had processed a birth certificate and identity card for a foreign national in February 2024.

Digital Payments and Fintech

In 2025 more than 90% of person-to-business payments were still made in cash. The Eswatini Payment Switch's Fast Payments module, built by Paylogic, launched on 11 December 2024. It carries real-time transfers up to E50,000 across banks and non-banks, with single wallet transactions capped at E10,000. In November 2025 it connected the four commercial banks, the Building Society and three mobile-money operators. Participation is mandated for every payment service provider offering local payment services. In September 2026 fast payments were described as fully active, with QR-code merchant payments the next module. Open banking and card, POS and ATM switching are to follow. In 2024/25 the Bank reported 98.6% reliability against a 98% uptime target.

In October 2026 a bank-run wallet, ShareSha, let users register with a phone and an identity document and send money to any mobile number. In August 2025 mobile money was reported as the driver of account growth.

The Revenue Service has handled no cash at its offices since April 2021, when cash had fallen to 2% of tax transactions from 36% in 2018. It takes tax and customs payments by electronic transfer, mobile money, card and bank deposit. In September 2025, 35 post offices became payment points for government services. Payments out are only lightly digitised, against a target of digitising 80% of recurring government payments by 2028. On 9 October 2026 the ICT principal secretary said post offices would become community digital centres for authentication, verification and payments.

Licensing runs under the National Payment Systems Act 2023. Regulations of 2026 on provider licensing, electronic money and consumer protection were before the Senate on 27 October 2026. The Practice Note for Mobile Money Service Providers requires fee disclosure, written agreements, complaints handling, free hotlines and confidentiality. Scheme governance runs through a National Payments Council with no reserved seats for consumers or small providers. In 2025 refugees reached digital accounts only partly, on documents accepted by several banks and by MTN's mobile money. Access points make no accommodation for persons with disabilities.

Registries

By 13 October 2026 more than 15,000 people had registered to vote, a quarter of them young people. Continuous registration ahead of the 2028 elections adds biometric capture against duplicate registration. In September 2023, 584,710 voters were registered for the general elections, about 91.2% of the eligible electorate. In 2023 the elections commission ran the roll on its own biometric system, separate from the national identity and population registers.

In 2024 civil registration covered live births, deaths, marriages and divorces, and was only partly digitised. At a civil registration office the officer fills in the form and the details go into the National Population Register. In 2025, 61% of births and 62% of deaths were registered, against 8.3% and 26.5% in 2016. The 2022 vital statistics report had put registration at 35.6% of births and 55% of deaths. Civil registration scored 50.0 out of 100 in 2023, 31st of 54 African states.

In 2019 the Deeds Registry covered only Title Deed Land, about 25% of the country. The remaining 75% in 2019, Swazi Nation Land, was held under customary tenure and not formally registered. In October 2024 the government told Parliament that a prototype electronic deeds system was 60% complete. Deeds were still registered and stored manually in 2025. The Surveyor General's cadastral maps are on a GIS platform not linked to the Deeds Registry. No national address register has been found. National addressing standards, domesticating ISO 19160, were launched in July 2026. In September 2026 Matsapha became the first municipality to roll out addresses under the national programme.

In 2024, 90.3% of firms were formally registered with the Registrar of Companies when they began operations. In 2024 applicants for a Tax Identification Number presented a certified copy of their identity document. In 2026 the Imbeleko social registry's Shiselweni pilot had registered 44,683 of an estimated 47,766 households. The Lubombo rollout, the second of four regions, began in August 2026 with an E41.7 million data-collection exercise.

Sectoral management information systems

In May 2026 the Ministry of Finance ran IBM mainframe software past end-of-life and without vendor support. The 2026 budget speech allocated E35 million to upgrade the government mainframe, citing frequent downtime.

In April 2026 the Client Management Information System, a government-owned electronic medical record, was live at 233 sites, 73% of health facilities. It opens a patient's record on a unique identity number alone. In February 2026 community data from Rural Health Motivators was moving onto a separate electronic Community-Based Health Information System. On 18 October 2026 the health minister was shown an electronic logistics system giving clinics their stock levels, interoperable with the client system. In 2024 education ran a DHIS2-based management information system in place of paper returns. A learner-record mobile application launched in March 2024 had reached around 60 schools by October 2024.

In 2024 online filing was mandatory for all income taxpayers through the Revenue Service's e-Tax system. In 2025 real-time fiscalisation of retail VAT data was being introduced against an E4.4 billion tax gap. Only the customs system exchanged data with other government systems in 2025.

In 2025 the integrated financial management information system was still under implementation, on custom software, covering central government only. A treasury single account was under implementation and only partly used. A public debt management system was in use, with no public investment management system. Budgetary and financial management scored 47.2 out of 100 in 2023, up 11.1 points over the decade. A data-centre outage kept lenders off the financial management system into October 2026, blocking new loans to civil servants. The ICT ministry listed the system among 13 services restored by 23 October 2026.

In 2025 the public-service human resource system ran on custom software, keyed to the national identification number. Public employees' social insurance and pensions run as a separate scheme. An Integrated Electronic Case Management System linking the police, the judiciary, the Law Society and the Anti-Corruption Commission was launched in August 2025. The courts held about 1,500 virtual remand hearings between January and June 2025.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

An incident at the National Data Centre disrupted at least 12 government digital services in September 2026. They included parts of Government in Your Hand, e-Visa, road transport permits, the elderly grant, deeds registration, company registration and IFMIS. On 23 October the ICT ministry said 13 services had been restored, while the government website was still down. In the second quarter of 2026/27 online company registrations fell 70.3% year on year. In November 2026 the home affairs ministry said it was moving passport applications online.

In May 2026 only 31 of 419 government services were available online. Government in Your Hand launched on 21 May 2025. It lets users track identity and passport applications, register businesses and find education-loan information, and in 2025 vehicle licences became payable in the app by mobile money. In 2025 the roadmap behind it scoped the platform at about 200 services. In 2025 there was no national service portal and no single online front door. Tax, social protection and employment portals were each in use and reaching ordinary users. A Business One-Stop Shop opened on 21 July 2025 for business registration, tax numbers and work permits.

In June 2026 the finance ministry's principal secretary told the Public Accounts Committee that electronic procurement is now implemented. By June 2026 only E0.5 million of the E18.8 million appropriated for it had been spent.

Eswatini scored 0.6081 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, 113th of 193 countries, up from 141st in 2022. In 2025 an institution coordinating digital transformation was established, with coordination and audit functions. In September 2026 the BIKA platform was introduced for anonymous reports of abuse and bribery on public transport. The Auditor-General's strategic plan for 2026 to 2030, launched in August 2026, names AI and digital transformation as the route to stronger accountability.

Of the 32 headline indicators assessed for Eswatini in the 2025 GovTech Maturity Index update, 19 record a system, institution or law in place, five record something under implementation or in draft, and eight record the item as absent.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In 2025 the integrated financial management information system covered central government only. In September 2025 post offices opened as payment points for government services, to cut the distance citizens travel to pay. In June 2026 the Tinkhundla structures were the channel for engaging communities on the digitisation rollout. In May 2026 most assessed ministries ran on internet connections of 1 Mbps or less. In August 2025 the capital, Mbabane, was being fitted out as a smart city with 5G and public Wi-Fi.

In December 2020 the Manzini council added a second mobile wallet for rates, alongside transfer, debit order and card. By April 2024 Manzini offered a USSD code to view balances and pay, with bills sent by text message and email. In May 2026 Mbabane's council took municipal rates payments online.

Rural digital data capture

In 2024 Eswatini's education ministry was replacing paper school returns, entered centrally, with a DHIS2-based system. In 2022 rural civil registry offices such as Hlathikhulu, Hluti and Nhlangano worked a hybrid process. For a birth outside a health facility the family brings a letter from the chief or indvuna. The officer fills in the form by hand, and a typist enters it into the National Population Register. In April 2026 the government-owned electronic medical record supported entry at the point of care. In February 2026 community data gathered by Rural Health Motivators was moving off paper into an electronic system. In June 2026 farm information and livestock tools reached farmers through a new operator platform. On 10 August 2026 agricultural extension officers received 40 tablets and four laptops, valued at E313,504, for crop, livestock and weather reporting.

In 2020 rural households were markedly less likely to have electricity than urban ones. In 2024 digital exclusion was concentrated among rural people, the elderly and people with disabilities. The national programme of 2026 is to connect schools, clinics and communities, and it reaches rural people through the Tinkhundla structures. Customary land held through chiefs, the bulk of the country, sits outside the formal Deeds Registry. In 2024 no national address register had been deployed. Mobile money reaches rural areas the banks do not. Eswatini's Rural Economy score fell 19.6 points over 2014-2023 to 50.4 out of 100, 36th of 54 African states. It was the largest decline of any of the index's sixteen sub-categories.

Technology

AI

In August 2025 an AI diabetic-retinopathy screening system went live in six public hospitals and clinics. From August 2022 to March 2023 an AI-enabled SMS tool for tracking infant immunisation enrolled more than 1,300 mothers. In May 2025 the Revenue Service sought a fiscalisation system using machine learning to flag irregularities and profile high-risk taxpayers. Three technology providers were being integrated into its electronic invoicing system in September 2026. In 2025 no use of AI or chatbots was recorded in citizen-facing government services. In August 2025 AI-enabled cameras were part of the smart-city fit-out of Mbabane. On 19 October 2026 the ICT minister presented a proposed AI-enabled platform for government services and payments, with no implementation date.

In November 2025 the communications regulator urged broadcasters to manage AI ethically and transparently, warning that deepfakes erode public trust. It was guidance to licensees, not an enforceable rule. In August 2026 the ICT ministry said its Online Media Publishing Guidelines 2026 carry provisions on the responsible use of AI in publishing. In August 2025 the governance of AI and robotics was among the areas of legislation announced as in preparation.

Two Eswatini instruments now reach artificial intelligence without being AI instruments: the broadcasting guidance of November 2025 and the online media guidelines of 2026.

In November 2024 the ICT ministry said it would develop a National AI Strategy and a 4IR Strategy in that financial year. What reached stakeholder validation in February 2026 was a review of the science, technology and innovation policy, with a combined 4IR and 5IR strategy. On 16 September 2026 the ICT minister said a consultant had been engaged to draft a national AI strategy. No draft or timetable has been found. In July 2026 the Prime Minister called in Geneva for a human-centred approach to AI. He said small and developing nations should invest in local innovation, research, digital literacy, computing infrastructure and data people can trust.

ICT Industry

Online company registrations fell 70.3% year on year in the second quarter of 2026/27, to 632 of 790 registrations in all. The commerce ministry blamed network connectivity, manual back-end processes and manual payments. The Eswatini Investment Promotion Authority opened the Business One-Stop Shop on 21 July 2025. It brings business and trading-licence registration into a single facility with tax identification number applications through the Revenue Service. Work-permit processing through Home Affairs sits there too. There is no single online front door to government services, and no company formation or business registration available that way (2025). Formality itself is not the barrier. 90.3% of firms were registered with the Registrar of Companies when they began operations (2024). The share is above 89% across most firm sizes and sectors (2024).

A Taiwan-funded platform connecting local businesses to wider markets was handed to the investment promotion authority on 3 September 2026. The commercial hosting a domestic sector would build on exists in one place, the National Data Centre. It sells platform, infrastructure and software as a service alongside colocation and remote hands. What runs on it competes with the power supply: 58.5% of firms experience electrical outages, at around 1.2 a month (2024).

Innovation ecosystem

Eswatini is not among the 133 economies covered by the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024. It carries no rank or score in that edition. It registers no visible venture capital activity in Partech's 2025 Africa tech venture capital report.

The Royal Science and Technology Park Business Incubator is the country's AfriLabs-affiliated technology hub (2025). A government- and UNDP-backed iHub opened in Piggs Peak in 2025. Further hubs are planned across all four regions. MTN Eswatini and the ICT ministry launched a Content Creator Hub in June 2026 offering skills and opportunities to the creative economy. The University of Eswatini announced in October 2026 a E15 million UNDP-funded innovation hub, under construction in its library.

A startup and innovation framework with state startup financing is in place (2025). Public-private partnership arrangements exist for delivering government services online. No state programme supports small and medium enterprises in the digital sector. The same index records a public-sector innovation strategy, a programme and an established institution running it. They cover digital skills and public-service innovation. There is no government open source software policy and no recorded adoption of open source in the public sector. Open source is therefore not a route for local firms to supply government. The central bank governor announced a reconstituted FinTech Forum Eswatini on 23 September 2026. It is widened to government, academia, industry and students. Six entrepreneurs in a private accelerator's first cohort generated more than E1 million in combined revenue within a year. A second cohort was launched in August 2026.

Capacity

Literacy

Eswatini's Equality in Education score fell 7.6 points over the decade to 2023, to 37.1 out of 100, 42nd of 54 African states. It was the weakest of the education indicators measured for the country. Education Enrolment scored 77.3 out of 100 in 2023, third in Africa and up 2.8 points over the decade.

In 2024 the education management information system ran nationwide on DHIS2. Most rural primary schools still recorded enrolment and attendance on paper for later entry, on personal laptops where there was a device at all.

In 2025 Eswatini had a digital skills strategy and a programme focused on basic digital skills. In October 2025 the Prime Minister opened Cybersecurity Awareness Month under a "Secure Eswatini" banner.

Training and skills

By October 2026 the ICT ministry was pursuing training for a combined 500,000 citizens. Of these, 300,000 would be trained through a partnership with the United Arab Emirates and 200,000 through a World Bank-supported initiative. No start date or selection criteria were given. In February 2026 the ICT minister set a national goal of 300,000 digitally skilled citizens, with the Royal Science and Technology Park, the Eswatini Innovators Association and UNDP among the partners. The UAE strand is the Udacity Coding Programme, launched in August 2025 with free certified courses in AI, data science and programming. No enrolment count has been found. In March 2026 the regulator said its 2025/26 National Digital Skills Training Program had trained more than 2,700 young people. No measured baseline of the population's digital skills has been found.

The combined target is about two fifths of the national population (October 2026).

In 2025 the government's standing programme was aimed at basic digital skills. In 2024 the University of Eswatini offered BSc degrees in computer science, information technology and computer science education, and an MSc in computer science. In May 2026 many ministries had no ICT professional in-house. In 2025 a state jobs portal for jobseeker registration and vacancy search was in use.

On 6 August 2026 a second digital innovation hub opened at Macetjeni in the Lubombo Region, aimed at young people, women and people with disabilities. In June 2026 MTN Eswatini and the ICT ministry opened a Content Creator Hub. In July 2026 the UAE's She Powers Africa Digital Academy began training a first cohort of 50 women from government institutions. In June 2026 the ICT ministry was training cross-ministry change agents ahead of the Digital Eswatini rollout. In September 2026 the education minister set out more than 1,140 computers and 60 printers donated by Taiwan to schools.

Research institutions

In 2024 the University of Eswatini's Department of Computer Science carried computer science and information technology through to MSc level. Its research pages list a health informatics group and a cybersecurity and forensics group.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services scored 57.0 out of 100 in 2023, 11th of 54 African states and up 5.1 points over the decade. In 2023 access to banking services scored 14.5 out of 100, 35th of 54, having moved 0.6 points in ten years.

In July 2026 the central bank governor said the headline inclusion figure of 87 per cent dates from the 2018 FinScope survey and measures access, not use. On use, Global Findex put account ownership at 63 per cent in 2025, up from under half a decade earlier.

Under fees effective from 2021, identity card enrolment is compulsory from 16 and free only in a three-month window. In 2025 the elderly grant could not be collected without the card. When card production stopped for five months, until February 2026, banking, employment and travel access was disrupted nationwide. Birth registration has long been incomplete. In 2023 public perception of how easy an identity document is to obtain was among the decade's most deteriorated measures. In 2025 refugee and non-Swazi documents were accepted by several banks and by MTN's mobile money under simplified checks.

In 2025 tax, social protection and jobs portals were in use and reaching ordinary users, with no single government service portal. In May 2026 only a small fraction of government services was online. In September 2025, 35 post offices began taking payments for government services. The social registry was piloted in Shiselweni, and its Lubombo rollout began in August 2026. In 2023 the National Financial Inclusion Strategy found that digital payment systems and access points made no accommodation for persons with disabilities.

Digital divides

In 2024 LTE and WiMAX covered 87% of the population. In 2025 57.6% of individuals used the internet. In 2024 exclusion was concentrated among rural populations, older people and people with disabilities. Mobile connections run well above the size of the population, but they count SIM cards, not unique subscribers (2025). Mobile data costs more than the international affordability target, and fixed broadband several times more (2025). The first national ICT household survey found 67% of households with internet access in 2022, 73% in urban areas against 63% in rural ones.

In 2020 urban households had electricity at rates well above rural ones. The rural economy fell further over 2014-2023 than any other sub-category of the governance index. Equality in education went backwards over the same decade. Where a digital service has reached rural areas, it has usually been mobile money. In August 2025 MTN MoMo and e-Mali reached places banks do not, though an urban-rural gap in use persisted. In 2025 most person-to-business payments were still made in cash. In July 2025 satellite capacity was being marketed at remote schools and clinics.

Far more young people enter the labour market each year than the formal economy has jobs for (January 2026). In 2023 the country's internet-and-computers score had improved sharply over the decade and still sat below the halfway mark. In June 2026 the Digital Eswatini programme planned USSD-based government services so that citizens without smartphones can transact. In 2026 the regional alliance opened a workshop to pilot gender impact assessment tools for digital policy, with Eswatini and The Gambia as pilot countries. A third community innovation hub is planned for the Shiselweni Region, after the second opened at Macetjeni in August 2026.

Data

National statistics

In January 2026 the international statistics partnership's country table recorded Eswatini's statistics strategy as Designing. No strategy title or period stands against its name, where most of its neighbours carry both. The statute beneath it is the Statistics Act of 1967, and no amendment has been found. Its Capacity of the Statistical System score rose 7.0 points over 2014-2023 to 32.3 out of 100, 47th of 54 African states.

In the World Bank's 2023 Statistical Performance Indicators, data sources is the weakest of five pillars, in the 20-49% band. Data use and data products both scored 70-89% in 2023, and data services and data infrastructure 50-69%. A population census and an agricultural census have been run within the last ten years, and a business census within twenty. The past ten years saw three or more labour surveys and two health surveys, but a single household survey. The 2017 census counted 1,093,238 people, with growth slowed to 0.7% a year over 2007-2017. In November 2025 the planning ministry and UNFPA launched the 2027 Population and Housing Census and the 2025/2026 Economic Census.

The Central Bank launched the FinScope Eswatini 2026 Consumer Survey on 24 July 2026. It adds new scope on digital financial services, fraud and climate-related risk. The round is in the field across 3,200 households in 320 enumeration areas. In January 2026 the communications regulator awarded a tender worth about E2.8 million for a State of Communications Sector Study 2025/26. The Central Statistical Office ran the first national ICT access and use survey of households in March to May 2022.

In 2024 the lack of a central open data portal limited what the Central Statistics Office could do with its data. The birth register remains incomplete. In February 2025 the statistics office was setting up a National Data Coordination Centre to manage multiple data sources. The education ministry's first National General Education Progress Report, for 2024, rests on a school census collected on DHIS2. Its 2024 reporting rates were 97.3% in primary and 97.2% in secondary schools.

Open data

In 2023 Eswatini scored 36.2 out of 100 for Disclosure of Public Records, 19th of 54 African states, after a rise of 1.8 points over the decade. It scored 9.7 for Accessibility of Public Records, 47th of 54, after a fall of 4.7 points. That was one of its ten worst-scoring indicators in the index.

In 2025 no right to information law was in force, and a bill was in draft with consultations in progress. There was no online facility for requesting records, and public records were not published online. The country had no national open data portal. The 2024 digital readiness assessment put it 191st of 195 countries on the Open Data Inventory, last in Southern Africa.

In Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory the best categories, government finance, international trade and price statistics, score 5 out of 10. Four score zero: digital and communications statistics, pollution, crime, and education outcomes. The Central Bank publishes its clearing and switch rules, the National Payment Systems Act 2023 and the fast payments directive without registration.

In 2025 an established data governance body sat inside another institution, with no data strategy behind it. A National Data Governance Policy was validated in July 2026 and reported adopted in September 2026. No published text or adoption instrument has been found. On 18 September 2026 two public dashboards of trade, investment and business-environment indicators were launched under EU support. No data licence or update schedule was stated.

Geospatial data

In 2025 the National Drought Management Center, the agriculture ministry and the meteorological service validated a monthly Combined Drought Indicator for each inkhundla. The indicator is built from CHIRPS rainfall and MODIS satellite data, and published openly on the Eswatini DroughtMap Hub. In 2021 drone and Sentinel-2 imagery were piloted together for crop monitoring at four sites for the disaster management agency. In September 2026 the disaster management agency and the meteorological service began rolling out impact-based forecasting for early warnings. The Surveyor General's Department holds nationwide cadastral maps and survey records on a GIS platform built with Japan International Cooperation Agency support. Its Swazi Nation Land records are outdated and incomplete, and the platform has no link to the Deeds Registry.

In April 2026 the government commissioned a Starlink satellite gateway to extend internet connectivity nationwide. It followed the July 2025 launch of Paratus Eswatini, an authorised Starlink Business reseller.

India activities

In 2021 India's Export-Import Bank signed a soft loan of about US$10 million with the Government of Eswatini. It was to fund a disaster recovery site for the national data centre, under Phase 2 of the Royal Science and Technology Park. In October 2026 the finance ministry said the loan stands with 1% of it disbursed. It had asked the lender to stop the commitment fee on the undrawn balance.

India's Export-Import Bank is the only bilateral lender among the external financing commitments recorded for Eswatini's digital estate (August 2026).

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 the finance minister told Parliament that the Royal Science and Technology Park's facility was not a fully operational data centre. He said the state needed a functional primary data centre before a backup site. The Park also operates the Government in Your Hand platform, which is to carry digital identity and payment services. In June 2026 a new government team there was to oversee system integration and procurement for the World Bank-financed build.

In December 2025 Eswatini signed a specimen sharing agreement with the United States. It shares specimens and sequencing data on novel infectious diseases within seven days of a US request. It consents to their onward sharing with up to ten US entities, and US law governs their transfer, use and control. If Eswatini terminates, it keeps no hold on data already shared. No published terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit in the memorandum with Google Cloud EMEA had been found as at September 2026.

In April 2026 the ICT minister said the national data policy then being drafted aims to make Eswatini one of the safest places in the world to store data. No instrument fixing where state data is stored, or under whose law it is processed, had been found as at September 2026. In August 2026 Eswatini's data governance experts joined a UNESCO workshop in Harare. Seven other southern African states took part, working towards harmonised data governance.
