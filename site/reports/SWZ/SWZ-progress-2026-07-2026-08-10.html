Shape check, run before the comparison: 57 sources for this place in the window — 18 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 39 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The twelve months to August 2026 turned Eswatini's data-protection statute into a working institution and its digital programme into an approved loan. The authority went from not operationalised in July 2025 to investigating a government ministry in June 2026 and drafting sectoral health-data guidelines in July. The Digital Eswatini Project's financing was approved.

What did not move is the foundation. The payment switch stood at phase one of three at both ends of the window, no national population register entered the record, and the National Data Centre still discloses no capacity, tenant or uptime tier while the state's dependence on it was formally named a concentration risk.

Capacity moved and use did not follow. International capacity rose from 47 to 72 Gbps on the World Bank-supported rollout, above target; mobile coverage was about 95% at the window's opening and 95% at its close, against mobile-internet subscription of 57%. The regulator's response arrived in the window's last month: a proposal to raise the minimum service standard from 2 Mbps to 10 Mbps, not yet in force.

Satellite went from a reseller channel to state infrastructure. A Starlink Business reseller launched in July 2025; by April 2026 the government had commissioned its own Starlink gateway to reduce dependence on cross-border fibre.

The state's own estate is where the window's most consequential finding sits, and it is the government's own. The Digital Landscape Assessment endorsed on 21 May 2026 found nine of twenty-one ministries keeping no regular backups, eleven with no ICT maintenance contract, the finance ministry running mainframe software past end of life, only 31 of 419 services online, and near-universal dependence on one hosting provider named as a structural concentration risk. Nothing comparable existed at the window's opening, so the position did not worsen — it became visible.

Payments changed on strategy and not on rails. The switch's Fast Payments module was live at both ends of the window and phases two and three were not. What arrived was policy and measurement: the National FinTech Strategy 2025-2030 launched in December 2025 targeting 500,000 e-KYC users and 80% interoperability by 2028; the e-KYC registry itself began construction; and the FinScope survey that will replace an eight-year-old inclusion figure launched in July 2026. The behaviour those instruments target is unmeasured since more than 90% of person-to-business payments were reported as cash in August 2025.

The window's clearest regression is in identity, and it was mechanical. Production of the national identity card was suspended in late September 2025 and resumed only on 22 February 2026, disrupting banking, employment and travel for months — on a card that does not meet the criteria for digital identification in the first place. Electronic IDs moved from conceptualised to planned under the Digital Eswatini Project for 2027. No national population register entered the record at either end.

Service platforms multiplied. Government In Your Hand and TaxPal launched in May 2025; thirty-five post offices opened as government payment points in September 2025; three sector platforms for agriculture, health and the creative economy launched with MTN in June 2026; and community health services moved from paper to digital records. The interoperability beneath them did not: more than half of ministries lack meaningful interoperability or central-repository connectivity.

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-09 Movement
Public account of the Google memorandum Not on the record Established after a public contradiction between the prime minister and the Attorney-General Advanced

The window's real movement is institutional. The Data Protection Act has been in force since 2022, but the office to run it was recorded as not operationalised in July 2025. By February 2026 the authority was running a compliance campaign with a 31 March registration deadline; by June it had opened an investigation into the Ministry of Home Affairs for publishing unblurred national identity documents, a probable breach on which the ministry also missed the 72-hour notification duty; by July it was drafting sectoral guidelines on personal health data and had concluded one complaint against a practitioner.

Policy followed the same path from procurement to text. The National Data Governance Policy was being tendered in July 2025, reached a first national workshop in April 2026, and was validated on 27 July 2026 for a 2027-2031 cycle with its text still unpublished.

Two things were said on the record in the window that had not been before. The Google memorandum was established as a cloud-services and e-government framework after a public contradiction between the prime minister and the Attorney-General, and the sponsoring ministry stated that data-protection implementation and enforcement are ESCCOM's, not its own — the clearest statement of the mandate the base holds.

Eswatini had no AI strategy at the window's opening and has none at its close. What it acquired instead is two instruments that regulate AI without being about it: ESCCOM's deepfake and broadcasting guidance in November 2025 and the Online Media Publishing Guidelines 2026, whose AI provisions are enforced through publisher registration.

The stated posture hardened over the window into a public position at Geneva in July 2026: small and developing nations should not become mere consumers of technologies developed elsewhere.

The partnerships that carry the sector all sit with foreign parties. The Google relationship went from a memorandum nobody would confirm to a publicly acknowledged framework with a national showing at the Google Cloud Summit Africa 2026, value and term still unpublished. A UAE-delivered women's academy took its first 50, and Taiwan and a regional health body added agreements with nothing costed.

The window's financing question was settled at its end: the Digital Eswatini Project's more than E1.06bn was approved on 19 July 2026, E648m for broadband and E450m for the citizen application, against a US$165m opportunity that a January 2026 column noted had gone unmentioned in the prime minister's year-end statement. No domestic appropriation line was held at either end of the window.

Inclusion improved on ownership. Global Findex 2025, cited in August 2025, put account ownership at 63% against below 50% a decade earlier, led by mobile money. Price moved into view rather than down: the committee report of July 2026 is the first dated transaction price the base holds.

Capacity delivery began late in the window and small against the ambition. A target of 300,000 digitally skilled citizens was set in February 2026 in a population of about 1.2m; by August the country had two community innovation hubs and a third planned, cross-ministry change agents in training, and a first cohort of 50 in the UAE-delivered academy.

Nine rows carry no position at either end, and they are the same nine that would have been empty a year ago. Nothing in the window filled a foundational gap: no population register on file, no data-centre capacity disclosure, no AI strategy, no domestic appropriation line, no open-data instrument. The window added systems, financing and an enforcing regulator, and left the layer beneath all three exactly where it was.
