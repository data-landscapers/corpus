This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Cabinet approved the Digital Transformation of Government Roadmap on 26 March 2025. The Roadmap organises the programme into four initiatives with dated milestones. On 21 September 2026 the communications minister said its first phase closes in February 2027 and that government digital systems remain fragmented. Operation Vulindlela's report for the first quarter of 2026/27 says progress "has been uneven and, in certain instances, slower than initially anticipated". In September 2026 the minister said the department had begun a structural review of the State Information Technology Agency's responsibilities.

The National Integrated ICT Policy White Paper of 2016 still sets policy on universal access, e-government, digital identification and cybersecurity. Cabinet approved the merger of Sentech and Broadband Infraco into a State Digital Infrastructure Company in December 2017; in April 2026 the department delayed this taking place until the 2028/29 financial year. In August 2026 the department recorded that the lower prices expected from the 2022 spectrum auction "remain largely unmet". The universal service agency gazetted a discussion document on its 2010 determination, with comments closing on 30 October 2026. It creates no new funding, subsidy or licensing obligation. On 30 October 2026 the regulator gazetted a discussion document finding most of its 470 network-service licences dormant and proposing that new operators apply for licences directly. Comments are due on 6 January.

Communications minister Solly Malatsi withdrew the Draft National AI Policy in April 2026 after it was found to cite fictitious sources. He convened an expert panel to redo the work. The department says the rewrite will issue national guidelines only, with sectors writing their own AI strategies. In October 2026 the deputy minister said the redraft is due at cabinet by the end of the 2026/27 financial year.

A 2025 public service directive requires every department to set up a data governance committee and appoint a chief data officer.

Legislation and regulation

The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 has governed personal data since 2021. The Western Cape High Court held in July 2026 that regulations under the Births and Deaths Registration Act are subordinate legislation. Orders invalidating them therefore need no Constitutional Court confirmation.

On 4 September 2026 the Electoral Commission gazetted a code requiring parties and candidates to label AI-generated content. It received more than 700 misinformation complaints by 20 October 2026, most about posts on X. SAHPRA's definition of a medical device includes software. It has never commenced product registration under regulation 8 of its 2016 general regulations.

On 13 November 2025 the Competition Commission closed its Media and Digital Platforms Market Inquiry with binding remedies for Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, TikTok and X. The remedies are made under section 43D(1) of the Competition Act. Under the November 2025 remedies, Google is required to commit up to ZAR 687.5 million over five years to news publishers, an AI innovation fund, a fund for small and community media, and training. No communications regulator has imposed comparable obligations on these platforms.

ICASA gazetted an amendment to its End-user and Subscriber Service Charter Regulations on 23 January 2026, in force from 23 January 2027. It requires data-depletion alerts, bars out-of-bundle charging without opt-in and rolls unused bundles over at least once. By July 2026 MTN and Vodacom had filed for review to have the rules declared invalid. In August 2026 both operators also filed High Court reviews against the exclusive-use spectrum amendments.

A ministerial policy direction of December 2025 would have let foreign satellite operators substitute equity-equivalence investment for direct Black ownership. ICASA rejected it in May 2026.

Data protection

In April 2026 the Information Regulator obtained High Court confirmation of a fine against Blouberg Local Municipality for publishing an employee's personal information. In June 2026 it was refused leave to appeal a ruling that set aside its notices against the Department of Basic Education. In August 2026 it served an enforcement notice on the South African Bureau of Standards over its 2024 ransomware attack. In October 2026 it fined First National Bank R1 million and Central Johannesburg TVET College R500,000, the college for ignoring a May 2026 enforcement notice. It said on 30 September 2026 that Gauteng's e-Government department had not notified it of the e-Panic Button database exposure. Three of its seats were put out to nomination, closing on 31 August 2026.

The Protection of Personal Information Act of 2013 has been in full effect since 1 July 2021, binding public and private bodies alike. It permits cross-border transfer only on stated grounds, and South Africa publishes no adequacy register. Section 22 requires notice to the Regulator and to affected people of any security compromise, with no materiality threshold. Notices have gone through a mandatory e-portal since April 2025. The Regulator recorded 1,220 security-compromise reports in the five months from April 2026. Its assessments find the public sector putting less effort than the private sector into security measures.

In mid-2026 the GSMA found no AI-specific guidance under the Act and no sector codes for high-risk uses such as credit scoring. A May 2026 analysis of the draft digital identity regulations found no right to erasure of biometric data. On 7 October 2026 the consumer commission launched a national opt-out registry, open to consumers from May 2027.

Regional collaboration

South Africa signed the agreement establishing the China-led World AI Cooperation Organization, set up in Shanghai in July 2026. The AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol was tabled in Parliament on 30 July 2026 and went to the NCOP select committee on 20 October. In November 2026 Nigeria's trade minister said Nigeria, Ghana, Rwanda, Kenya, South Africa and Egypt expected to sign a digital trade accord that month. The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores South Africa 46.0 out of 100 on regional integration, 29th of 54 African states.

The National Radio Frequency Plan 2026 re-bases the allocation table on the ITU Radio Regulations 2024. It aligns to AfriSAP and the SADC Frequency Allocation Plan. A Reserve Bank directive gazetted on 1 April 2025 moves low-value transfers within the Common Monetary Area onto the SADC instant scheme by 1 April 2027.

A private terrestrial fibre route has run from Mombasa to Johannesburg through the DRC since May 2023. The METISS cable has linked Mauritius, Réunion and Madagascar to a landing near Durban since March 2021. Google's Umoja route from Kenya to Johannesburg had its terrestrial path built by May 2024. In October 2025 a 10 Gbps research-network link from Lagos reached the ZAOXI exchange in Cape Town. A private carrier lit a 750 km route from Maputo through Eswatini to Johannesburg in September 2020. In 2019 the state wholesaler Broadband Infraco reached the borders of all six land neighbours.

On 26 August 2026 the revenue service hosted five customs administrations that adopted a roadmap for customs data exchange. The instant payment rail settles across borders through the SADC scheme, with live corridors to Zambia, Zimbabwe, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini. On 16 November 2026 South Africa was re-elected to the ITU Council for 2027-2030 at the Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha. South Africa left the Financial Action Task Force grey list in October 2025, having been placed on it in February 2023.

As at February 2026 South Africa had signed the African Union's Malabo Convention and not ratified it, on a policy institute's reading of the AU status list.

Standards

In July 2026 a Public Service Commission investigation found more than R2 billion in irregular expenditure at SITA. The State Information Technology Agency Act 88 of 1998 gives SITA the mandate to set and certify interoperability standards for government systems. It also found weak governance, unstable leadership and failed procurement. SITA's procurement attrition stood at 25.2%, and some contracts had been stuck for more than 400 days (July 2026). Departments were seeking exemption from using it. In July 2026 the communications minister gave SITA 30 days to produce a recovery plan.

Under regulation 97 of the Public Service Regulations, 2016, the Minimum Interoperability Standards bind national and provincial departments. For local government they are only informative. The government interoperability framework is recorded as only partially used, with its terms unpublished (2025). Operation Vulindlela's first-quarter 2026 report dates the remaining interoperability standards to September 2026. A health laboratory set up with the CSIR was to begin testing patient systems against the gazetted health interoperability standards in January 2026.

In 2026 the national standards body was a participating member of the joint ISO/IEC information technology committee. It sits on the subcommittees for security, identification cards, biometrics and AI. In October 2026 it published drafts adopting ISO/IEC AI standards on transparency, data quality and human oversight. Comment closed on 10 November 2026. In September 2025 the health professions council set disclosure, consent and data-protection duties for practitioners using AI.

In September 2025 SAHPRA set quality, risk and software-lifecycle standards for AI medical devices: ISO 13485:2016, ISO 14971 and IEC 62304. Class C and D applicants must show approval in Australia, Brazil, Canada, the EU, Japan or the United States, or WHO prequalification. It expects local test data and subgroup analysis by sex, age and ethnicity. It fixes no minimum sample size, required subgroup performance or operating point.

In July 2026 TikTok's rule that creators label realistic AI content was the only synthetic-media labelling regime operating in South Africa.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The 2024 Ibrahim Index finds that public perception of freedom of speech in South Africa fell 19.2 points over 2014-2023, to 69.4 out of 100. That placed it 14th of 54 African states, while the index's measured media freedom score rose. Its Rights sub-category ranks South Africa 2nd of 54 at 80.0, with Freedom of Expression and Belief at 92.6. Participation ranks 3rd of 54 at 76.4, and Civil Society Space scores 75.9, up 5.1 points over the decade.

In 2025 the World Bank's GovTech index recorded no online public participation platform, marking 'No' against all five of its sub-measures. A right-to-information law is in force, but the index marks 'No' on whether the public can obtain records and request unpublished ones. On 20 August 2026 Parliament extended its call for submissions on the Electronic Communications Amendment Bill to 21 September 2026. Consultation on the Digital Identity Regulations has closed, with comments under evaluation (2026). Nominations for Information Regulator seats will be published with CVs for public comment before shortlisting.

On 19 August 2026 the Deputy Minister of Justice launched a rights commission policy brief on information integrity, saying government should not become "the custodian of truth" on disinformation. On 17 September 2026 Research ICT Africa and partners launched a study finding the governance of harmful online content fragmented in enforcement. It found platform moderation configured in English alone, leaving out 10 of the 11 official languages.

In 2026 community network operators were trained to file submissions on communications regulation. In July 2026 civil society argued that energy-hungry data-centre growth "necessitates a conversation about prioritising". The same report found no national moratorium or binding resource condition on data-centre development. Objectors to the Cape Town data-centre rezoning may appeal to the municipal Appeal Authority, whose ruling is final (August 2026).

In August 2026 the Department of Social Development was running unannounced ministerial inspections of local SASSA offices, framed as fraud control. No fraud finding, count or outcome was stated, and the minister attributed beneficiary complaints to users not understanding the system.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2026 Budget Review records the second phase of SA Connect as ending in 2025/26, leaving the department to keep 948 government facilities connected each year. The broadband subprogramme that carries it is appropriated R285.1 million for 2026/27. It was appropriated R1.89 billion for 2024/25, of which R734.8 million was spent. The 2026 estimates print no financing source against their digital lines (2026/27).

The digital lines read from the national estimates come to about R13.3 billion appropriated for 2026/27, against about R12.6 billion for 2025/26.

In 2024/25 Home Affairs spent R1,020.7 million of the R1,110.6 million adjusted for its transversal IT subprogramme, and R828.5 million of R1,157.4 million on status services. Its R685.9 million modernisation programme for 2026/27 pays for maintenance and support of the revenue engine, live capture, e-Home Affairs hosting and public key infrastructure. The same programme pays for Microsoft, IBM, HP and Gemalto licences.

Regulations under the Identification Act, in force from 1 July 2025, charge private users R10 for each real-time verification and R1 for each batch field, and state bodies nothing. The department set the price to fund maintenance of the population register.

In May 2026 the communications minister told Parliament that full state ownership of the department's entities is no longer sustainable. He said partial privatisation makes sense in some cases, and private operators should use state-owned assets under commercial agreements.

MoUs and other agreements

In July 2026 Afreximbank and the Industrial Development Corporation signed a three-year memorandum in Cairo. The two will originate deals together against an $8 billion envelope (July 2026). Afreximbank said it would run PAPSS and its MANSA due-diligence repository across the pipeline. In November 2026 Cassava Technologies and Mauritius Telecom signed a memorandum in Cape Town on AI compute and services. They will assess taking Mauritius Telecom's AI tutoring platform into South African schools. No sum was disclosed.

On 3 April 2025 SARS and Home Affairs revived a bilateral agreement in place since 2010. They also signed a four-party memorandum with the Border Management Authority and Government Printing Works. Government Printing Works takes the roles of Certification Authority and Digital Trust Centre. In July 2026 Johannesburg announced a sensor-and-analytics smart-city memorandum.

New investments

The largest single commitment on the record is Amazon Web Services' plan to put a further R30.4 billion into cloud infrastructure by 2029. Almost all the money going into South Africa's digital estate is corporate capital. The largest financiers are hyperscalers and mobile operators spending their own balance sheets, not development lenders.

The largest grants are the two tranches of Germany's Digital Skills for Jobs and Income programme: a EUR 9.6 million grant running 2020 to 2024 and a EUR 6.5 million second phase running to 2027. Concessional finance is marginal. Grants also went to technical assistance for five USTDA feasibility studies between 2017 and 2023, none larger than about $2 million. The development institutions that do lend, lend commercially. The IFC has put a $100 million senior term loan (2021) and a rand-denominated loan of about $58 million (2024) into Liquid Intelligent Technologies' fibre and data centre build.

There are 223 commitments on the record, worth about US$9.9 billion (November 2026). Grants make up 162 of them but only about US$99 million, about 1 per cent of the money (November 2026).

The largest sums that have actually changed hands went to the state: at the ICASA auction of March 2022 Vodacom paid R5.38 billion for 110 MHz, the largest purchase in it, and MTN R5.2 billion for 100 MHz. Everything since has gone into buildings and towers. Teraco is funding a 40MW hall at its Isando campus from an R8 billion syndicated loan arranged through Absa (2024). Equinix has committed $438 million (R7.5 billion) of its own balance sheet to Johannesburg and Cape Town capacity (2026). In towers, Actis completed its R6.75 billion purchase of Telkom's Swiftnet portfolio in March 2025. Chinese money has come as vendor-linked buyer's credit for Huawei equipment, lent to Telkom and Cell C.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In March 2026 a development-bank broadband map found 4G reaching 98% of the population, with roughly 400,000 households still without 4G or better. It put 74% of households within 10km of a fibre node and 12.2% more than 20km from one (March 2026). More than 6,700 of about 15,000 public schools and more than 1,000 clinics lie 10 to 25km from the nearest fibre. The map's underlying dataset is not public.

DataReportal counted 127 million mobile connections in late 2025, 196% of the population. It put internet users at 51.7 million, 79.6% of the population (late 2025). By March 2026 operators had connected 10,851 of the 21,878 public service institutions their 2022 spectrum licences oblige them to reach.

Data took 62.4% of Telkom's group revenue in the quarter to 30 June 2026. Its data subscribers grew 15.5% to 19.8 million, while prepaid ARPU stayed broadly flat at R59 (quarter to June 2026). Group capital expenditure fell 19.4% to R888m in the quarter to June 2026. In the quarter to June 2026 Openserve passed 26,541 homes and connected 26,023, lifting its connectivity rate to 53.9%. The two largest operators are seeking review of Icasa's new data-bundle rules.

In July 2026, 794MHz was assigned of the 1,155MHz with assignment plans or licences. The regulator's roadmap puts national need between about 3,000MHz and above 14,000MHz, depending on how much traffic fibre and Wi-Fi carry. On 25 September 2026 the department withdrew its 2019 policy reserving high-demand spectrum for a wireless open access network, which was never licensed. On 10 November 2026 Icasa opened a questionnaire on licensing six more IMT bands, with responses due on 30 November 2026. A policy direction gazetted on 20 October 2026 orders Icasa to review the 2010 facilities-leasing rules and write rapid-deployment regulations. Access to spectrum is the one common demand the community network sector makes of Icasa (July 2026).

Data Storage

In February 2026 the communications department told Parliament that mission-critical government systems run in two national data centres. SITA operates them at Centurion and Beta, both National Key Points. The department proposed a national integrated government data centre as a medium- to long-term measure, with no budget, site or date. In 2024/25 SITA's plan to offer departments public cloud across several hyperscalers was 55% implemented against a 100% target. Government data is also hosted outside the country, under arrangements that are not published (2025).

Data centre infrastructure used by government entities must sit inside the country; no general localisation mandate applies beyond that (2024). A March 2025 consultation paper would keep payment clearing and settlement data in the country. The Reserve Bank's draft Crypto Asset Manual would let providers host offshore within a group, but not in a sanctioned country (July 2026).

In May 2026 the Human Rights Commission opened an inquiry into whether the frameworks governing data centre development meet constitutional obligations. In September 2026 the commission said it had received more than 250 submissions. It named the key issue as the transparency of information on electricity and water demand, land use and community impacts. It has published no finding, timetable or report date.

South Africa had 68 data centre facilities in 2025. Amazon Web Services opened its Cape Town region, its first in Africa, in April 2020. Microsoft put R20.4 billion into its two South African regions over the three years to March 2025. In April 2026 it committed a further R5.4 billion for land, power and water readiness.

In July 2026 Cape Town's Municipal Planning Tribunal approved the land use for two Equinix data centres by four votes to one. In September 2026 two civil society organisations appealed, saying the application lacked information on electricity, water, emissions and noise. The City says the rezoning is suspended until the executive mayor decides the appeal. The appeal went before the City's advisory panel on 12 October 2026, after which the mayor has 90 days to decide. The City is drafting guidelines for large data-centre applications.

Energy

Load shedding has largely stopped: the year-to-date energy availability factor reached 67.55% by August 2026, the strongest in six years. Unplanned outages nearly halved year on year. Yet households across the provinces went on losing power through 2025, mostly through faults in municipal distribution networks. The constraint has moved from generation to distribution.

Wind, solar and other renewables supplied 17.8TWh of the 221.2TWh generated in 2024, so supply remains overwhelmingly coal-fired. Several thousand megawatts of private generation were registered by mid-2024 after licensing thresholds were lifted to 100MW. Of the population, 87.7% had electricity access in 2023. Rural access reached 94.0% against 90.9% urban in 2023, so the backlog now sits in urban informal settlements.

In February 2026 National Treasury reclassified data centres as infrastructure as critical as electricity, ports and transport, signalling priority grid connections and tax incentives. Eskom's public position is that current data-centre demand is within what the grid can carry (July 2026). Teraco's expanded 50MW Cape Town facility runs zero-water closed-loop cooling (November 2025), and its Johannesburg build wheels utility-scale solar across the Eskom network. Wind power from a contracted producer began reaching its Cape Town data centres over the grid in September 2026. Its own 120MW Sediba solar plant in the Free State is due for grid energisation in December 2026.

Equinix's two planned Cape Town data centres, of up to 160MW, drew a formal objection in June 2026 that the application disclosed neither their water nor their power demand. The planning tribunal deferred both questions to a later stage (July 2026), and there is no national moratorium or binding resource condition on data-centre development. Rural primary healthcare clinics still record encounters on paper because connectivity and power are unreliable.

Technical Capacity

The managing director of the state's central IT agency said in September 2026 that 60% of executive committee posts had been vacant. The agency is rebuilding an executive that stood largely empty. A permanent chief financial officer and executives for client engagement, supply chain, and network, cloud and platform have since been appointed. Its managing director since April 2026, Magatho Mello, is the first permanent holder of the post since late 2023. He was appointed as the minister constituted a new board. The same shortage surfaces wherever a department is obliged to write it down. The Border Management Authority has gone to market for three years of software-development resourcing (July 2026). In the tender it concedes that its internal capacity is insufficient to meet evolving system demands. The deputy minister of justice named ICT human resource capacity, procurement delays and budgetary pressure as what holds back his department's digital estate (July 2026).

The government data-exchange platform MzansiXchange was nine months into pilot and onboarding additional technical capacity to complete it (July 2026). What cannot be staffed is bought in. Home Affairs' own strategic plan describes the department as grossly underfunded and dependent on third parties for critical services. The identity infrastructure it runs rests on foreign suppliers for both fingerprint matching and smart card production. Cyber security roles are largely unfilled.

South Africa's GitHub developer population grew by roughly a quarter over 2024. There is a domestic base underneath the shortfall. A South African firm has opened a Midrand headquarters with a UPS test platform and a cooling test centre certified to European standards (July 2026). It supplies the power and cooling layer beneath the foreign-owned data-centre build. The domain registry counted 1.5 million active registrations across its four commercial namespaces in October 2026. NAPAfrica, the internet exchange in Teraco's Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban data centres, carried more than seven terabits per second of peak traffic with more than 690 networks connected in November 2026.

Cybersecurity

On 4 August 2026 the national CSIRT's site served a maintenance page, and its newest dated item was from September 2025. It carries no statistics, no reports, no advisories archive and no data downloads. Neither the register of sector-CSIRTs nor any national standard the Cybersecurity Hub is mandated to create has been published. The National Cybersecurity Policy Framework, gazetted in December 2015, is still the country's policy instrument. On the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024 South Africa sits in the "advancing" tier.

The industry body recorded 14,897 subscription fraud cases in the twelve months to April 2026, up 307% on the year before. Social engineering over messaging and calls has overtaken technical intrusion, carried by AI-generated voices. A free helpline for victims of online fraud opened in October 2026 as a 12-month pilot, funded by a US cloud provider. Of 309 organisations in the CSIR's 2024 survey, 88% had been breached in the previous twelve months. Third-party connections were the leading root cause, at 48% (2024). Of 291 public-sector institutions surveyed in 2024, 64% rated themselves very prepared and 56% had suffered a data breach.

In April 2026 Home Affairs conceded that its cybersecurity maturity remains low and that it lacks a fully implemented disaster-recovery strategy. In July 2026 the Electoral Commission readvertised a tender to rebuild its mobile app, which it said runs on an unsupported framework and has vulnerabilities. Statistics South Africa was hit by a ransomware attack in March 2026. In July 2025 government made cybersecurity a counter-intelligence priority.

Gauteng's e-government department left the database behind its e-Panic Button app unsecured, exposing users' names, crime reports and locations back to its 2024 launch. The contractor fixed the flaws after a news outlet's alert on 21 September 2026. After a joint meeting on 5 October 2026, legislature committees said the department told the Information Regulator only that morning, two weeks after the alert. The pathology group Ampath confirmed an attack detected on 2 October 2026. In November 2026 the booking platform LekkeSlaap told guests that their names, contact details and booking references had been accessed.

DPI

Data Exchange

In October 2026 the Treasury and the Presidency put completion of South Africa's government data-exchange pilot in December 2026. The National Treasury launched MzansiXchange in October 2025. It is a federated platform built on Estonia's X-Road. It is designed as a real-time rail between institutions, not a central repository. Its first live use case links Home Affairs data to the South African Social Security Agency for real-time verification. It is in testing, not in full production.

The real-time exchange is still to be built, under an inter-departmental working group set up in September 2024. Several partial systems run in parallel: the Treasury's Secure Data Facility, the social security agency's relief-of-distress system, the cooperative governance department's National Strategic Hub and the South African Integrated Data Lake. Tax administration, customs, human resources and financial management are connected both through separate point-to-point interfaces and through a Government Service Bus. A government interoperability framework is in place but only partially used, and its terms are not published. Alongside it sit the minimum interoperability standards that bind national and provincial departments. The integrated social information system meant to join social protection data remained undeveloped in June 2025, with no legal framework for sharing it. Health runs its own exchange, which the health ministry says now carries the patient register and the facility list (June 2026). Trade has no single window yet: the revenue service gave no completion date in September 2026. Only an inspection workflow shared with the border authority since September 2023 runs, in phased testing. The social security agency's grant verification cross-references records held by the revenue service, the Unemployment Insurance Fund, the Government Employees Pension Fund, banks and credit bureaus. In January 2025 the North Gauteng High Court declared unconstitutional and invalid the relief grant's checks against databases that may indicate income. It declared the proxy means test conducted by verifying insufficient means with banks unconstitutional and invalid too. The state has taken the ruling to the Supreme Court of Appeal.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Home Affairs' only digital identity target for 2026/27 is the hosting infrastructure, due complete by 31 March 2027. Verifiable credentials and digital wallets come in later phases, with full digital identity promised "by 2029". The minister showed the first working digital identity credential on his phone on 1 October 2026, with no release date. In October 2026 the department's quarterly report recorded a missed first-quarter target to start procuring the hosting infrastructure.

In February 2026 President Ramaphosa confirmed that the digital identity framework was still awaiting Cabinet approval. Draft Identification Regulations gazetted in May 2026 would create an optional smartphone credential. Comments on them are under evaluation. In October 2026 the communications regulator published draft regulations requiring biometric verification of mobile subscribers at activation and SIM swap.

Home Affairs issued 4,002,964 Smart IDs in calendar 2025, 17% up on 2024. Smart ID replacements ran at 833 bank branches on 18 November 2026, past a 750-branch target for March 2027, with more than 1.16 million applications since 9 March. From that day adults could apply for passports at 480 of them, raising service points from 228 to 708. Green book production ends on 31 March 2027, with 14.7 million green books still in circulation (October 2026). As at November 2025, 4,432,923 South Africans aged 16 or over held neither a green ID book nor a smart ID card.

Since 1 September 2025 the social grants agency has required biometric enrolment for every new grant application and triggered review. It has logged 7,779 complaints about the facial biometric system, whose remedy is a visit to a local office.

In 2024, 83.2% of births were registered within the legally mandated 30 days. In July 2026 the Western Cape High Court declared the birth-registration regulations unconstitutional for four categories of exclusion. Home Affairs conceded three and has 18 months to amend the rules.

In October 2026 the transport department said it would amend the road traffic regulations so that driving and learner's licences no longer count as identification. A Presidency-led working group is exploring digital driving licences within the digital identity framework (2026).

Digital Payments and Fintech

On 20 October 2026 the central bank set its non-bank Authorisation Framework for end-November and a QR+ merchant-code pilot for the first quarter of 2027. Regional payments under R100,000 move from SADC-RTGS to TCIB by end-April 2027. An exemption notice and a forthcoming National Payment System Bill deliver activity-based licensing, letting non-banks issue e-money and reach clearing and settlement directly. A state payment credential and wallet are in development, with government use-case testing dated November 2026. Unregulated payment facilitators acquiring for offshore merchants are to come under a payments directive and an exchange control circular published together. Comments on it closed on 17 July 2026. The modernisation programme uses neither tokenisation nor a distributed ledger.

FinScope found 89% of adults, 40.8 million people, holding a bank account in 2025. Grants reached about 19 million people in 2026, almost entirely into bank accounts and onto cards. SASSA gold cards were due to stop working after 31 August 2026 (July 2026). Free black replacements were issued over the counter at Shoprite, Checkers, Usave, Pick n Pay, Boxer and Spar. Postbank had replaced 172,146 of the 463,836 active cards by 28 July 2026. It assigns sites by the location of each recipient's last three transactions, ring-fencing anyone more than 30km from one.

The Reserve Bank took a 50% stake in PayInc, operator of the instant payment rail, and the February 2026 Budget recast the clearing house as open, shared national payment infrastructure. PayInc's shareholders remain banks only, while 39 banks and more than 480 non-bank payment service providers use its rails (August 2026). PayShap, live since March 2023, processed more than 500 million transactions worth about R486bn in 2025. It had passed six million registered users by May 2026. Owner-bank pricing of R1 to R10 a transaction and the absence of a USSD channel for feature phones held it back.

Dollar-stablecoin flows of about R27bn ran on-chain in the year to 30 April 2026. Licensed providers' monthly trading fell from about R19.2bn in 2025 to about R10.7bn over the first four months of 2026. More than 300 crypto asset service providers are licensed.

Registries

377,060 identity numbers remained blocked on the National Population Register in July 2026, down from 702,267 when the Gauteng High Court found the placement of markers without due process unconstitutional. Home Affairs blames "unserviceable addresses" for failing to trace around 400,000 affected people. The court deadline has been extended to March 2027. There is no authoritative national register of addresses. No commercial, statistical, municipal or postal address set holds a national mandate or complete coverage. This leaves the companies commission to verify company addresses through a commercial geolocation service.

From 1 July 2025 Home Affairs raised the real-time verification fee from 15 cents to R10 a check, to fund an Intelligent Population Register. The industry association for Vodacom and MTN, which calls the rise a regressive tax, has brought it under High Court review. The population register is the identity key that the digital-ID programme, the revenue service and the grant and unemployment-insurance systems read from. The verification service failed up to half of requests in March 2025, before an upgrade that month.

The Electronic Deeds Registration System launched on 1 April 2025 as a controlled beta test. No deed had been registered end to end on it by November 2025. In May 2026 the minister targeted its full function by 31 March 2029. Registers are joined bilaterally. Companies registered through BizPortal are automatically registered with the revenue service, the Unemployment Insurance Fund and the Compensation Fund. The applicant's identity is verified in real time against the population register. Voter registration still depends on a voter presenting an identity document, not on an automated link to the population register. A draft bill would have Home Affairs pass records to the Electoral Commission at 16 and to the roll at 18. Interoperability between the two is targeted only for the 2029 elections.

Errors stay with the citizen: identity numbers stolen to make Covid-era relief claims still show their owners as employed on the Unemployment Insurance Fund register (July 2026). This blocks those workers from benefits. Neither the labour department nor the police will take ownership of correcting the record.

Sectoral management information systems

The National Student Financial Aid Scheme was placed under administration in May 2026, citing delays in ICT modernisation and systems integration. It names ICT as its biggest operational risk. Its board had been dissolved in 2024 over IT-system failures and the fintech disbursement debacle.

By March 2025 the unified electronic medical record had reached beta in 36 primary healthcare facilities and one general practice. By June 2026 it was still in beta and user-acceptance testing. Rural clinics remain paper-based at the point of care, with records entered into national systems such as DHIS2 afterwards. The national digital health strategy ran to 2024. The ministry promised a revised strategy for the last quarter of 2025/26, and it had not been released as at July 2026. The mandated school administration platform, SA-SAMS, is reported at near-universal adoption.

The integrated financial management system mandated by Cabinet in 2005 had cost R2.6bn by September 2024. In November 2026 the public service minister told Parliament that the Treasury had placed it in abeyance pending oversight investigations. A shared public-service HR system was held back with it. The legacy accounting system's cutover to a new chart of accounts on 1 April 2025 strained its mainframe. It left 29 of 162 departments forced to close April 2025's books. Customs still runs under the 1964 Act (2026), and the Customs Control and Customs Duty Acts of 2014 are unpromulgated. The revenue service had auto-assessed more than 6.9 million returns by 30 September 2026, up 20% on the year before.

The police told Parliament in May 2021 that the electronic docket system was live at 998 stations, with 159 waiting on network upgrades. The 2025-2030 strategic plan commits to full docket integration and blames the state IT agency for delays. A ministerial reply of December 2025 records victim-data errors halting the women-and-children crime statistics. Court Online, the judiciary's e-filing and evidence system, was live in 19 courts by August 2025.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In August 2026, three days before the registration deadline, the Electoral Commission put voter registration inside WhatsApp. 1.7 million transactions ran through it over the final weekend (August 2026). The commission's own portal had carried 74% of more than 815,000 registration transactions since June 2026. It declared its results system ready on 28 October 2026, after an independent audit and an audit by five political parties. On 6 November 2026 the MK Party lodged a formal objection alleging a failure in live results capture at the eThekwini results centre.

MyMzansi, the single national portal, was a functional prototype in a controlled testing environment in October 2026. The home affairs minister opened a digital identity credential in the MyMzansi app in a live demonstration on 1 October 2026. The 2026 adjusted estimates allocate R150m to the Presidency's Digital Services Unit, with the digital matric certificate the first use case.

The Electronic Travel Authorisation went live nationally at OR Tambo in August 2026. It is live at four airports, OR Tambo, Cape Town, King Shaka and Lanseria, from 12 August 2026. It runs machine-learning document checks, facial recognition and rules-based automated decisions. In October 2026 the department said a human is brought in where the system detects an anomaly. It had rejected 6,126 fraudulent applications, put at 2.8% of pilot submissions (August 2026).

Home Affairs put the first phase of its digital asylum system live for test cases on 10 November 2026. The portal records intentions to apply for asylum, with facial biometrics.

South Africa ranked 40th of 193 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, entering its "very high" group for the first time. Operation Vulindlela's October 2026 report puts the shared Gov.za website platform only at testing by participating departments. The Auditor-General told Parliament the integrated justice system programme has not achieved its vision after about R4.9bn spent over a decade (2026).

State procurement is still largely paper-based, and the Treasury is building an electronic system in phases under the 2024 Public Procurement Act. End-to-end open tendering is targeted for 2027/28.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT tenders advertised in a single week of July 2026 included a unified contact centre for the City of Cape Town, spanning emergency and non-emergency services. They also included a VPN security upgrade for the Free State Office of the Premier. Under the Public Service Regulations, 2016, the Minimum Interoperability Standards bind national and provincial departments and are only informative for local government.

The City of Johannesburg's citizen call centre went down in July 2026. It is the main route for residents to report outages, leaks, potholes and signal failures. The same month the City was under an Auditor-General regression finding and a Treasury suspension of part of its funding. Its own 2025/26 draft integrated development plan concedes fragmented initiatives and insufficient governance structures. In October 2026 Johannesburg's power utility put out to tender a near-real-time monitoring platform for prepaid electricity purchases. The utility recorded R3.96bn of non-technical losses in 2024/25.

The City of Tshwane and the CSIR launched an Enterprise Data and Analytics Platform on 29 September 2026. It pools municipal departments' service-delivery data, while the live service-delivery dashboard stays internal.

In April 2026 the Information Regulator obtained High Court confirmation of a fine against Blouberg Local Municipality for unlawfully publishing an employee's personal information. In July 2026 Cape Town's planning tribunal created a dedicated land-use class for data centres. The Electronic Communications Amendment Bill would standardise municipal wayleave processes for electronic communications facilities (2026). Most household power interruptions now originate in municipal distribution networks (2025).

A 2020 parliamentary reply named councils budgeting in spreadsheets outside their core financial systems. The Treasury's 2024/25 budget circular required reporting only from the core financial system. The Treasury's 2026 report finds councils still reporting from spreadsheets. One metro's audited accounts disclose an October 2023 ransomware attack that took down its financial system. In 2024-25 the Auditor-General found 44% of municipalities with effective IT governance.

Rural digital data capture

In March 2026 a broadband map placed the least-connected municipalities in Mpumalanga, the Northern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal. By 2023 rural electricity access in South Africa had overtaken urban access. A subsidised solar home system serves deep rural households where the grid does not reach. Rural primary healthcare clinics remain paper-based at the point of care, with records entered into national systems afterwards (2025). Rural school administration follows the same pattern (2025), and case-docket work in rural police stations still runs substantially on paper (2025).

In July 2026 the minister approved the 2026 National Radio Frequency Plan, adding licence-exempt Wi-Fi spectrum for rural and underserved areas. Spectrum is the community network sector's single common demand of the regulator (2026). The Electronic Communications Amendment Bill answers it with a sharing mandate on secondary licensees.

Online birth registration sits in the maternity units of larger hospitals, not in rural facilities (2025). Mobile units are the main civil-registration outreach to remote communities (2025). Where SASSA's facial biometric check fails, the remedy on offer is to travel to a local office and enrol by fingerprint (May 2026). The 2024 Ibrahim Index leaves South Africa unscored on all four of its rural economy indicators.

Technology

AI

In November 2026 President Ramaphosa asked his Silicon Valley AI delegation to identify, within 90 days of returning, a focused set of practical AI initiatives with measurable outcomes. The national AI policy was withdrawn, and its redraft faces a parliamentary committee whose chair is set against the new AI bodies the draft proposed. Those bodies are a regulatory authority, an ethics board, a commission and an ombud. In July 2026 the chair argued that the regulators already in place should be equipped for AI instead.

In July 2026 the Prudential Authority's 2025/26 report built AI, cloud, cyber and data-offshoring into bank supervision. It reported a first nationwide AI-adoption survey, whose named barriers are regulatory uncertainty, AI skills and governance. The five largest banks are taking AI out of pilots and into fraud detection, risk management and customer service. Insurers are writing their own AI frameworks while the national policy is redrafted (October 2026).

In July 2026 SARS was applying machine learning to tax compliance, to find undeclared income and to choose which taxpayers are assessed automatically. Its AI assistant, Lwazi, answered more than 2.7 million taxpayer questions in 2025/26. Home Affairs runs automated risk scoring over travel authorisation applications, and no appeal route has been published. The courts have begun setting the standards for AI-assisted legal work themselves, ahead of any statute.

In September 2025 SAHPRA stated that formal regulatory pathways for AI and machine-learning enabled medical devices are yet to be developed. The algorithm version is locked at licensing, and significant changes must be notified. Among South African SMEs already using AI, 61% have deployed data analytics and customer-facing applications (February 2026). Most use cases have not reached deployment at all. The Ayre Index placed South Africa 13th of 56 Commonwealth states on youth AI readiness in November 2026, second in Africa after Mauritius.

ICT Industry

Openserve, South Africa's largest fibre wholesaler, launched its own internet service provider in July 2026. That puts it into retail competition with the ISPs it sells capacity to. In the six months to 30 September 2026 group revenue at its parent, Telkom, rose 2.0% to R22.5bn, and data took 61.8% of it against 59.1% a year earlier. Revenue at BCX, the group's IT services arm, fell 8.2% in the six months to September 2026. On 16 November 2026 Telkom announced an approach from MTN to acquire all its shares, with talks at an early stage and no certainty of a deal.

In July 2026 a Cape Town biometrics vendor was moving from one-off remote onboarding checks to continuous identity verification. No South African rule allocating who should operate such a service has been found. A Midrand engineering business supplies the power and cooling plant under the foreign-owned data centre build (July 2026). Pepkor is merging its Flash business with Shop2Shop into a single merchant-commerce platform, announced in July 2026 and still subject to regulatory and competition approval. The Finance chapter sets out how the startup layer is funded. The state records a startup and innovation support framework with financing and small-business support in place (2025).

Central ICT procurement through SITA is a national bottleneck, with contracts stalling for long periods and departments seeking exemption from using it (July 2026). In mining, executives rate their own AI readiness and data management poorly, and most have deployed nothing (2026). Economic diversification scores 91.1 out of 100 on the 2024 Ibrahim Index, fourth of 54 African states. It has fallen 6.7 points since 2014.

ICT services imports reached R85bn against R30bn of exports in 2025. About 20 million phones a year were imported over 2020-2025. In May 2025 Mobicel, assembling imported parts in Johannesburg, was the one local smartphone producer. Mara Phones' KwaZulu-Natal plant had gone to auction in early 2022. The business-process export sector reported just under R13.6bn of export revenue and more than 14,000 new jobs in January to September 2024, on its own figures.

Innovation ecosystem

As at 2025 South Africa had AfriLabs member hubs in Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban and Stellenbosch. In June 2025 Gauteng's innovation agency broke ground on an R83.1 million public-private building at The Innovation Hub in Pretoria. In November 2026 a French embassy incubator run with Wits University's Tshimologong precinct took 14 start-ups into its first cohort.

In 2025 South Africa led the continent in both equity funding and equity deal count for the first time since 2017. Its deal flow was spread across stages, and a single megadeal accounted for 15% of its funding (2025). It ranked 69th of 133 economies on the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024.

As at 2025 the state had a public-sector innovation strategy or programme and a dedicated innovation lab. The results of that work were held internally and not published. The Reserve Bank's distributed-ledger work remains confined to two experiments that never moved into production.

Capacity

Literacy

The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores South Africa's Education Completion at 94.7 out of 100, fourth of 54 African countries. Equality in Education stands at 44.9, 32nd of 54, and was effectively unchanged across the decade to 2023. Education overall scores 62.0, tenth of 54 and up two points over the decade. The profile records no score for Human Resources in Education, one of five indicators for which South Africa supplies no data.

In January 2026 the state argued in the relief grant litigation that applicants' complaints on social platforms prove online access is no barrier. Applicants say the system is an insurmountable hurdle for those without digital literacy or with disabilities. A large share of public schools sits far from the nearest fibre (March 2026).

The National School of Government trained 112,855 learners in 2024/25. In April 2025 it signed an agreement with Microsoft to train 100,000 public servants in digital literacy, AI and cybersecurity.

Training and skills

In the CSIR's 2024 survey of 291 institutions, 7% had trained no employees at all and 32% had trained a quarter of their staff or fewer. Just 14% trained more than three quarters (2024). 62% of cyber security roles are partially or fully unfilled (2023).

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a public-service digital skills strategy and a programme delivering against it, from basic digital literacy up to advanced data science. Above them sits the National Digital and Future Skills Strategy, gazetted in 2020. In July 2026 the department told Parliament that the strategy is under review and a National Competency Framework is in development. Asked what AI would do to employment, it declined to forecast a net effect.

In June 2026 a development bank's review with the higher education department found about 7 per cent of ICT and engineering posts unfilled. Software developers, network engineers and ICT security specialists are the hardest roles to fill. Employers attribute 26 per cent of the gap to basic-level training and 24 per cent to graduate throughput (June 2026). The review holds the mismatch to be structural and not self-correcting.

Germany funds a second phase of its Digital Skills for Jobs and Income programme to 2027. Microsoft's expansion pairs data-centre investment with AI skills training (April 2026). Google has committed to a digital innovation centre at a Soweto TVET college (2026). In March 2026 the higher education department agreed with Google an initial 10,000 scholarships for professional qualifications in AI, cyber security and data analysis. An industry-led digital skills impact fund had mobilised R600m and reached 4,000 young people by October 2026. A Google.org-funded programme training lecturers to teach advanced AI launched at Stellenbosch University on 9 November 2026, with ten South African universities among its 30. In 2026 a digital policy school trained 23 community network practitioners to file submissions on communications regulation.

Research institutions

The CSIR has taken delivery of a new 4-petaflop system to replace the national research computing centre's ageing core, its first phase due live by the end of November 2026. The full upgrade is due by the end of March 2027. The national cyberinfrastructure system is ring-fenced at R292m for 2026/27. In August 2026 Parliament's science committee recorded that the Centre for High Performance Computing has reached maximum capacity and cannot accommodate future growth. The science minister told the National Assembly that the late-May 2026 breach of the Lengau supercomputer was caused by vulnerabilities associated with the legacy high-performance computing system. The intrusion deployed crypto-currency mining malware, and a remediation programme has since been run.

Gross spending on research and development reached R44.2 billion in 2024/25 but fell from 0.62 to 0.60 per cent of GDP. ICT research spending rose R853 million, to R5.7 billion.

The University of Cape Town and the Global Center on AI Governance launched the African Hub on AI Safety, Peace and Security on 28 September 2025. The hub sits under the AI4D programme, financed by the UK's FCDO and Canada's IDRC. The same programme is funding a GPU cluster for AI research at the university, to be live within a year of March 2026. The science minister stated that no government-backed home-grown large language model is under development (October 2025). The department funds language resources instead. Four universities are building models for isiXhosa, isiZulu and Sepedi. Cape Town researchers released MzansiLM, trained from scratch on all 11 official written languages (May 2026).

In October 2024 Research ICT Africa filed 20 comments and three recommendations on the draft digital government policy framework. In February 2026 Stellenbosch University's Policy Innovation Lab hosted senior officials on regulatory sandboxes for the three national DPI priorities. On 17 April 2026 the University of Johannesburg signed a memorandum on digital policy with the communications minister, alongside a new UNESCO Chair on digital inclusion for 2026 to 2030.

Inclusion

Access to services

In July 2026 grants flagged by SASSA's verification checks in September 2025 were still suspended and unpaid. There was no effective route to correct a flag. A small unemployment-insurance payment, bank-account income data or a stale employment record can trigger the flag. The beneficiary must produce payslips and bank statements and attend an office in person, and an income investigation can run for months (July 2026).

In June 2022 the Department of Social Development's figures put 13.4 million working-age people with no income and 18.3 million below the food poverty line. When the relief grant's criteria were tightened, applications fell from over 15.8 million to just over 8.1 million between March and April 2022. In 2025 the High Court found SASSA's failure to pay successful applicants unconstitutional.

By the end of the first quarter, SASSA's Western Cape registration outreach had delivered 14 of 56 events planned for the year (August 2026). Beneficiaries' gold payment cards were due to stop working after 31 August 2026. Home Affairs cannot trace the people whose identity numbers it wrongly blocked on the population register (July 2026).

In June 2026 SA Connect's in-year allocation for 2025/26 was reported to have fallen from an expected R1.1bn to R116m, arriving only in January 2026. 7,397 public facilities were connected, and the 100%-access target set in 2022 was out of reach. As at March 2026 only 10,851 of 21,878 public service institutions targeted under spectrum licence obligations were connected, 49.1%. About R652m of the universal service fund was unspent at the end of 2025/26, of R1.29bn collected over five years. The regulator withdrew its telecoms affordability inquiry on 30 September 2026, 26 days after gazetting it. It is to be republished with a draft questionnaire.

On 6 October 2026 Parliament's committee heard that the social development department had a qualified audit opinion for 2025/26 over SASSA payment weaknesses, including grants paid to deceased people. The Treasury credits biometric enrolment with R13.1bn saved, mostly on the relief grant (October 2026).

Digital divides

In March 2026 a parliamentary reply recorded 67,868 social grants suspended and 48,949 lapsed over the first three quarters of 2025/26 for failure to complete review or verification. Most were Child Support Grants: 37,825 of the suspensions and 42,748 of the lapses. The reviews followed income testing of some 6 million Child Support Grant recipients, run with the banks (March 2026).

In January 2025 the North Gauteng High Court declared the regulation confining relief grant applications to the online channel unconstitutional. It read in the words "or at the offices of the Agency". The Supreme Court of Appeal heard the state's appeal on 25 August 2026 and reserved judgment pending further affidavits. It admitted an AI governance organisation as a friend of the court. The department argues that complaints applicants post on platforms such as X prove there is no barrier to online access.

At the start of 2025, 13.6 million people were offline, 21.1% of the population. Women are 14% less likely than men to use mobile internet (2024). In the second quarter of 2025 South Africa ranked 31st of 45 African countries for the price of a monthly 1GB mobile package. The least-connected municipalities cluster in Mpumalanga, the Northern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal (March 2026). An applicant whose facial biometric fails on SASSA's portal is told to travel to an office and enrol by fingerprint.

In August 2026 the Electoral Commission said women made up 61% of online voter registration transactions, and people aged 16 to 29 made up 50%. About 603,000 of more than 815,000 transactions since June 2026 had run through its data-free portal.

Data

National statistics

In 2023 the World Bank found that South Africa had conducted no business or establishment survey in the past ten years. A population census, an agricultural census and a business census all fall inside the same window. So do three or more rounds each of household, labour force, health and agricultural surveys. The World Bank otherwise scores the programme as complete. The 2022 census, the first run digitally, missed an estimated 31.1% of the in-scope population on the statistics office's own post-enumeration survey.

In 2023 South Africa sat in the 70-89% band on all five of the World Bank's pillars: data use, services, products, sources and infrastructure. The Ibrahim Index puts the capacity of the statistical system at 72.7 out of 100, fifth in Africa and 1.6 points up over the decade to 2023. The 474,366 deaths occurring in 2024 were published only in September 2026. The same release counted 14,851 late registrations of deaths from 2003 to 2023. The 863,858 births registered in the 2024 period were published in October 2025.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index several indicators carry no South African score, including the whole rural economy sub-category and the measure of staffing in education. No data is supplied for them. In August 2026 Home Affairs replaced its periodic count of participating Smart ID bank branches with a live locator. The Electronic Travel Authorisation's usage reaches the public through speeches, and no published series has been found (August 2026). The National Cybersecurity Hub publishes no statistics of any kind on the incidents it handles.

Statistics South Africa was itself hit by a ransomware attack in March 2026.

A deputy minister in the Presidency said in a keynote address on 9 October 2026 that Census 2031 is in development. The address called statistical independence a constitutional safeguard. The statistics office's roadmap sets the main enumeration for October 2031, with strategic planning and budget tabling in 2026/27.

Open data

South Africa ranks first of 54 African states on both of the Ibrahim Index's records measures (2023). Disclosure of public records stands at 80.0 out of 100, up 12.4 points since 2014. Accessibility stands at 75.3, up 3.5. Yet in the 2024 Open Data Inventory, health facility data scores between 0.5 and 2.5 out of 10, and education facilities between 3 and 5.

Government finance, poverty and crime each score between 3 and 5 out of 10 (2024). Labour, national accounts, balance of payments, trade, prices, population and agriculture score between 5.5 and 7.5.

A national open data portal is running, publishing basic information and datasets on a mix of annual, quarterly and monthly refresh cycles (2025). A data governance body exists without any published account of how data across government systems is governed (2025). The right-to-information law is in force and rated effective. The same index marks the country down on whether the public can obtain records in practice or ask for what is not already published. National Treasury has published eTender procurement data in the open-contracting standard since May 2021. Its beta portal held 169,559 tenders and 73,447 awards in November 2026.

The dataset behind South Africa's first broadband map is owned by the Development Bank of Southern Africa and has not been released (March 2026). The study built on it criticises government and ICASA for withholding infrastructure data. ICASA has since moved to compel operators to file geo-referenced infrastructure data. A ministerial policy direction of 20 October 2026 told the regulator to build a national GIS database of communications infrastructure. The government data exchange runs with no live public dashboard (2025). Public access to aggregated datasets is planned rather than delivered. The Information Regulator ordered a mining house to disclose its social and labour plan reports in June 2026.

Geospatial data

In July 2026 Deputy President Paul Mashatile launched BrainSAT Satellite Services, a state-backed satellite operator. It is a South African company running on Emirati Space42 capacity, Ka-band broadband and Thuraya handsets. It works under a national SATCOM strategy whose stated destination is an eventually South African-owned satellite. Amazon Leo will enter in 2027 through Herotel as its licensed local partner, an arrangement that raises no ownership question. Starlink has neither. It reached its 27th African country in July 2026 without launching in South Africa. Its rollout is held up on black economic empowerment compliance. The only lawful route left to a new entrant is acquiring an existing licensee under the Electronic Communications Act.

Operational use runs through the Weather Service, which receives EUMETSAT satellite imagery every 15 minutes alongside 14 radars and 214 automatic weather stations (November 2024). In 2025 an early-warning roadmap to 2029 was costed at about R1bn. It found cell broadcast in place but not activated. On the data itself, the state's standing national geospatial holding is the Chief Surveyor General's digital cadastral layer of farms, erven and holdings across all provinces.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In March 2025 Microsoft disclosed R20.4 billion of spending over the preceding three years on its Johannesburg and Cape Town facilities. Its Azure regions there opened in March 2019, the first enterprise-grade cloud regions on the continent. Amazon Web Services put R15.6 billion into its Cape Town region between 2018 and 2022. Equinix entered in December 2022 with a $160 million colocation and interconnection data centre in Johannesburg, since opened. Google's Johannesburg cloud region, its first in Africa, became operational in January 2024.

In August 2022 the US DFC disbursed $83 million to Africa Data Centres, mainly for its South African expansion. In 2015 its predecessor OPIC guaranteed up to $80 million to IHS Fund II SA, a telecommunications tower fund. USTDA funded five ICT and connectivity feasibility studies and technical assistance grants between 2017 and 2023, none larger than about $2 million.

In December 2025 a policy direction let foreign operators substitute equity-equivalence investment for direct Black ownership. It was read as opening the door for Starlink, and ICASA rejected it in May 2026. Amazon's Leo service is due in 2027 through a licensed local distributor. In July 2026 Africa Data Centres' Johannesburg site became an Azure ExpressRoute Metro peering location. In August 2026 voter registration ran in part through a US-owned messaging platform.

China activities

South Africa has signed the agreement establishing the China-led World AI Cooperation Organization, set up in Shanghai in July 2026. In 2015 Cell C took a capital expenditure facility of $240 million plus R1.3 billion from the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China. It financed Huawei radio access equipment and the expansion of Cell C's LTE, mobile broadband and microwave networks. In 2019 Telkom raised a ten-year buyer's credit from ICBC and Standard Bank, insured by Sinosure, to buy 4G equipment from Huawei. It was the first rand funding Telkom had raised from China.

Huawei has operated cloud availability zones in South Africa since 2019, and opened a third Johannesburg zone in January 2023. In September 2026 Standard Bank Group expanded its partnership with Huawei to move its core banking systems onto a hybrid cloud in phases. No value, hosting location or data-residency terms have been published.

EU activities

In October 2025 Team Europe announced a package of nearly EUR 12 billion for South Africa, covering digital connectivity among other areas. Germany's BMZ funded Digital Skills for Jobs and Income with a EUR 9.6 million grant from 2020 to 2024. A second phase of EUR 6.5 million runs from 2024 to 2027. In 2023 Finnfund invested EUR 2 million in Fibertime Group, which sells low-cost fibre in township communities.

In 2025 Germany's DEG put $4 million into insurtech Naked Insurance's $38 million Series B2 round, led by BlueOrchard. In 2023 DEG took part in the Series B round of Lulalend, a digital lender to small and medium enterprises.

Gulf/UAE activities

In July 2026 Deputy President Paul Mashatile launched BrainSAT Satellite Services, a state-backed South African operator. It sells YahClick Ka-band broadband and Thuraya 4 handsets over infrastructure owned by the UAE's Space42. The national SATCOM strategy behind it aims at an eventual South African-owned satellite system. The science department sets the country's yearly spending with foreign providers against the cost of a local satellite with a twenty-year life.

India activities

India held no financing, infrastructure or platform position in South Africa's digital economy as at August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 Home Affairs' plan for a state digital identity listed a certificate authority among the things it will build, without saying who would hold the keys. South Africa's existing trust layer for electronic signatures is delegated to three accredited commercial operators.

The National Policy on Data and Cloud of May 2024 makes SITA the authority that sources data infrastructure and cloud services for government. It requires government data to be stored in unified cloud-enabled data centres. In February 2026 the communications department told Parliament that government data bearing on national security and sovereignty is to be stored only inside the country. The President wrote in July 2026 that government is investing in its own cloud through the CSIR.

In August 2026 the skills authority financing a learner-management system for the post-school sector was advised that the university which built it owns the intellectual property. The authority is seeking co-ownership, and has warned that the lack of government-owned data centres exposes national data. As at September 2026 nothing had been found on whether the state holds the source code for the national identity system. Nor had anything been found on whether it can exit its supplier contracts. No exit terms had been published for the police's Huawei cloud contract.

In September 2026 SITA's board chair told GovTech 2026 that sovereignty means knowing where data is stored and who may access it. The chair said management will report against verified targets for availability, cost, resilience and local participation by GovTech 2027.
