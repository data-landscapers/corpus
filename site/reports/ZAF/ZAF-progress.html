This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-27.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies The only localisation instrument is the same unresolved draft data and cloud policy, which would vest data generated in South Africa in the state.
Full recordLocalisation in South Africa rests on a draft. The draft data and cloud policy declares that data generated in South Africa shall be the property of South Africa and provides for in-country copies for law enforcement; it has not been gazetted and nothing in the window moved it.

What is in force runs the other way. The data protection Act operates an adequacy model — cross-border transfers are permitted where equivalent protection, consent or contractual necessity applies, with the responsible party accountable — so the position a reader would take from the statute and the position the draft policy would create are not the same, and the base holds no decision reconciling them.

Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability Draft data-exchange legislation is a stated deliverable of the exchange pilot, over a 2014 statute that already provides for administration-wide norms and standards.
Full recordThe standing instrument is not a data law. The Public Administration Management Act has been in force since 2014 as the frame for administration-wide arrangements across national, provincial and municipal spheres, including the transfer and secondment of employees and the establishment of common norms and standards — which is the authority under which service standards are set, and it says nothing about data.

Draft data-exchange legislation is one of three named deliverables of the one-year exchange pilot, alongside four task teams and interoperability standards.

So the country is running a federated data exchange in pilot with no statute behind it, and the statute is scheduled to be drafted by the pilot itself. Nothing on this ledger states who would introduce it or when.

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No rules govern state automated decision-making. What moved instead was litigation over an algorithmic grant eligibility screen and a copyright Bill silent on AI.
Full recordThe position is unchanged and stated as such: the base holds no instrument governing automated decision-making by the state.

What the window produced is a court case rather than a rule. The state is appealing a January 2025 judgment that struck down regulations governing the Social Relief of Distress grant, a scheme administered online only and screened by an algorithmic verification system that excluded applicants it read as earning above R624.00; the court found the automation unfairly excluded otherwise eligible applicants, an AI governance organisation was admitted as a friend of the court, and the appeal was set down for 25 August 2026.

The one Bill in the window that touches machine learning does so silently. The Copyright Amendment Bill would replace fair dealing with a broader fair use exception and returned to Parliament in June 2026 after the Constitutional Court upheld most of it; it says nothing explicit about generative AI or text and data mining, which imports another country's judicial answer to those questions along with the doctrine.

Full record2026-08-24 — the electoral commission is moving its updated code of conduct on countering disinformation from draft to enforcement ahead of the local government elections.
