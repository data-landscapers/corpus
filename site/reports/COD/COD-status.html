Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

DR Congo's digital appropriations can be read directly from an enacted budget. The 2026 finance law gives the digital economy section FC 81,698,223,741 of domestic credit and posts and telecommunications FC 53,840,917,417. Against that, the FY2025 chain closed with FC 31,358,784,468 paid, 51.7% of the revised credit, and nothing had been published for the 2026 exercise seven months into the year.

The framework law is in force and its supervisory institution is not. The Code du numérique was promulgated on 13 March 2023 with a third book governing personal data; the authority required by its article 262 has still not been created, and no decree defining its organisation was found as at March 2026. A national identity platform meanwhile runs under a twenty-year exclusive concession, launched on 13 June 2026 and valued at US$97.1m, with no enrolment figure published.

Physical build is measurable and behind. 7,000 km of the transformation project's fibre is deployed against a target of more than 18,500 km, tied to connecting 145 territories by 2030.

The backbone is the one infrastructure object with a delivered figure and a target. 7,000 km is deployed against more than 18,500 km, leaving 11,500 km over a 2030 horizon; the minister attributes the pace to budget constraints and a low disbursement rate on external financing. Around it, eastern DR Congo is tied into the East African terrestrial corridor and the internet exchange now operates as a distributed exchange across two carrier-neutral facilities, with no traffic or member figure held at any date.

Two announced programmes have not moved off the announcement. A rural joint venture's commitment to 2,000 solar base stations was restated with a 2031 date and carries no sites-built count; a sovereign satellite estimated at US$400m has no contract, operator or launch date, and the claim that a bank has mobilised the funds is relayed by the presidency and uncorroborated.

Commercial data-centre capacity is specified: 400 racks over 1,542 square metres at 1.5 MW on US$30m invested, with no utilisation figure. State capacity is an appropriation: the 2026 finance law carries FC 26,756,546,346 for national data-centre acquisition with no procurement or site on file.

The state operator's position is financial: on current finances it could theoretically pay salaries only every four months, which management says rigour has brought to every two to two and a half months. No incident-response capability, technical-capacity base or energy provision for digital infrastructure is held.

Identity is being built twice, in parallel, on different legal bases. The RDC-PASS platform launched on 13 June 2026 under a twenty-year exclusive know-your-customer concession, valued at US$97.1m with US$2.6m raised by the concessionaire for initial rollout. Separately the identification office states operations are about to start on a second national identification, financed by a partnership because the 2026 finance law fell short — the appropriation for national identification acquisition is FC 20,654,178, a figure that cross-foots exactly against its group total. The last national identification was in 1984. A bill setting one legal frame for identification data was tabled on 12 June 2026 and nothing on file connects it to the concession.

Payments are the most active layer and the least measured. A card-and-wallet interoperability service is live and now carries a stablecoin settlement of wallet top-ups, which runs against the central bank's own deadline of 9 April 2027, from which no foreign-cash transaction of any amount is permitted. Account numbers cannot be stated: the base carries 24m, 29m, 30m and 35m across sources, none reconcilable and no central-bank series published.

Government platforms are specified on paper. The digital government platform missed a first-quarter 2026 target with neither launch nor delay announced; the e-government strategy dated 2025 to 2030 has its master plan and interoperability framework still to be defined by a consultant not yet appointed. Where delivery exists it is narrow: sole-trader attestations issue in under 48 hours while full online company creation is not possible.

Tax administration is the sector's most enforced digitisation. The standardised VAT e-invoicing system, mandatory since 1 December 2025, has closed its transitional "fiscal clinics", and the finance minister has ordered immediate targeted sanctions against non-compliant taxpayers and against DGI staff who validate deductions without a normalised invoice; quarterly VAT mobilisation has risen from a CFC 280–290bn average to CFC 340bn, which the minister called still "very largely below potential".

The Code du numérique has been in force since March 2023; the data protection authority it requires is still not created, the minister saying in March 2026 that structuring a professional network precedes its creation. That network is live and is not a supervisory authority with sanctioning powers.

Two bills sit in opposite directions. A bill on access to information was declared admissible on 27 March 2026 and referred to a joint commission with ten days to report, with no commission report four months past that deadline. In the other direction, an information note to the Council of Ministers proposes a classification regime of four levels with access limited to cleared agents.

An interministerial order pricing digital-sector duties took effect on signature on 20 July 2026. The finance ministry announced a stay of its application decided on the evening of 1 August 2026; after a working session on 6 August the sector network reported the minister's position that the order was never suspended and remains fully applicable. Neither position rests on a published government text, and that session agreed a permanent state-industry consultation framework. The ministry's own 8 August statement then confirmed that registered "entreprenant" start-ups are fully exempt under Digital Code Article 384, not merely subject to the US$100 line reported earlier; no gazetted order or amending text is published.

The sectoral digital economy policy 2026-2030 was validated by government on 4 August 2026 and referred to the Council of Ministers; the text is not published and its costing cannot be checked.

Network loss in conflict-held eastern territories is recorded as war damage and fuel-access failure rather than any ordered restriction, with mobile money the first casualty in each outage. Baraka and Fizi have been cut off for months amid operations against Twiraneho/AFC-M23 forces near Épupu, and humanitarian NGOs' Starlink kits, the last backup channel, were seized on the Baraka mayor's order. No data governance strategy, open-data policy or European Union instrument is held.

The national AI strategy was technically validated over 23 to 27 July 2026, then validated by government on 4 August 2026 and referred to the Council of Ministers; the text is not published and no adoption date is established. Its four pillars run from governance, ethics and regulation to industrialisation and value creation. Officials have said on the record that bodies already acting across digital, research, data, cybersecurity and regulation have roles not yet sufficiently articulated in a common steering architecture.

AI was used to mark the 2024-25 state examination, with no scope, vendor, model, accuracy or human-oversight detail held; a state guarantee fund runs a live AI-assisted business-plan platform with no usage figures or data-protection arrangement stated.

Four draft decrees adopted in May 2025 to operationalise the fiscal, customs and exchange advantages of the 2022 ordinance-law remain in preparation, so no exemption regime for startups or digital small businesses is in force.

A Chinese supplier memorandum covers about 2,100 km along the Congo River at a stated US$1.5bn, with more than US$400m required for the first phase and no financing close; a United States partnership covers data centres, connectivity, cybersecurity, financial technology and artificial intelligence with no value or milestones. A claim in a United States federal court seeks up to US$400m over a collapsed payments-modernisation project; the government calls the signed documents a non-binding memorandum, and no ruling is held.

The universal service fund has moved from an instrument civil society described as unavailable to a first funded national tranche. The first pilot connectivity sites were launched in Kongo Central with a community digital-learning centre attached, and the fund then signed subsidy conventions with Airtel, Orange, Vodacom and Africell covering a first tranche of 40 localities, targeting about 258,000 people, against a diagnostic finding nearly 3,000 uncovered localities holding about 4.3m people. A second phase of 300 sites is planned, with a 2030 target of 3,000 localities, 16m people and 100 digital community centres. No per-locality budget or fund appropriation figure is held.

Financial inclusion is stated once and without a trajectory: about 58% against 25 to 30% bank-account penetration, the gap attributed to mobile-money use without a bank account. No earlier dated rate is held.

Training targets exist without enrolments. The national digital plan sets 250,000 young people to be trained in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and programming, at least 3,000 in advanced digital skills and about 1,000 teachers, all to 2030; no source establishes a single enrolment against any of them, and no domestic public funding for digital or AI research exists in the base. The largest single training cohort held is 41 certificates awarded in November 2025.
