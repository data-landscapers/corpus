DR Congo: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-23

Edition of 2026-08-23-2  ·  122 systems and instruments tracked, 13 of them not held
↓ PDF
Compiled 2026-08-23 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 122 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 68 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-23, 14 did not, 27 carry no stated baseline, and 13 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 196 sources for this place in the window — 32 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 164 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Most of what moved in this window was written rather than built. The clearest movement with figures at both ends is fiscal. The digital economy section's domestic appropriation went from FC 60,627,481,411 revised for FY2025 to FC 81,698,223,741 for FY2026, and posts and telecommunications from FC 40,138,356,283 to FC 53,840,917,417 — but the two sections were also renamed and their mandates redistributed, so the rise is substantially a transfer.

The identity platform is the largest system to have changed state: from a twenty-year exclusive know-your-customer partnership signed in June 2025 with nothing live to a platform launched on 13 June 2026, valued at US$97.1m, still with no enrolment figure published. The supervisory authority the code has required since 2023 remains uncreated at both ends of the window.

The backbone acquired a measurable position it did not have. At the window's start the programme had run about a year with no deployed-kilometre figure held, and the June 2025 Council recorded 30.79% mobile-internet and under 0.02% fixed access; by 3 August 2026 it stood at 7,000 km against more than 18,500 km, leaving 11,500 km over a 2030 horizon. Two connectivity assets appeared alongside it: an eastern route to Mombasa and a second presence for the internet exchange, making it the country's first distributed exchange.

State data-centre capacity moved on paper only, from no established appropriation to FC 26,756,546,346 in the enacted 2026 budget, with no procurement or site.

Three positions did not move, each with an announcement at both ends: a rural joint venture's 2,000 solar base stations, restated with a 2031 date and no sites-built count; a US$400m sovereign satellite with no contract or launch date; and a 5G public consultation open since March 2025 with no report, spectrum award or licence seventeen months later.

Identity moved at both ends of the same problem. The concession-run platform went live in June 2026; the state's own identification programme went from a card-production partner under discussion with no financing route to operations announced as imminent, financed by a partnership because the finance law fell short. The appropriation for national identification acquisition is FC 20,654,178.

Payments gained four rails and no measurement. A card-and-wallet interoperability service, a stablecoin settlement of wallet top-ups, a palm-vein payment technology and a microfinance digitalisation agreement covering 10,000 payment terminals and connection to the national switch all entered the record from nothing. Against them, the account base did not move: the only precisely dated figure remains 29m accounts and 34.6% penetration for the fourth quarter of 2024, with 24m, 30m and 35m also on file and none reconcilable.

Three government systems held still. The digital government platform passed a first-quarter 2026 target with neither launch nor delay announced; the single social register has no signed decree, operator or household enrolment twelve months after its draft decree was adopted in council; and the biometric civil-servant registry stands where it did at 118,000 cards, its US$250m commitment five months from closure with no published outturn.

The two positions that define the section are unchanged. The Code du numérique is in force as it was, and the authority its article 262 requires is still not created — the same finding two years running, alongside a twenty-year exclusive foreign concession over citizens' identity data. What did appear is a national network of data protection officers, live since March 2026, described as the first concrete step under article 262 since 2023. It moves the enforcement gap without closing it.

Legislative activity ran in both directions from nothing. A bill on access to information was declared admissible on 27 March 2026 and a bill on the identification of natural persons was tabled on 12 June; in the other direction, a classification regime for official documents was presented to the Council of Ministers. None has been adopted.

The mandate itself was redrawn: the 2026 finance law renamed both sections and moved credits from FC 145.7bn to FC 55.1bn and from FC 60.6bn to FC 226.7bn. Combined, the two are almost flat as a share of the budget. One regional deadline passed unmet: the free-roaming resolution required effective implementation across Central Africa by 30 June 2026, with no implementation or tariff held.
