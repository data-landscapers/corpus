Strategies, plans and policies

Telecom infrastructure-sharing and taxation studies under the connectivity project

PACT et telecoms en RDC : baisse des couts en vue — Numerico.cd, 9 September 2026

A workshop in Kinshasa on 9 September 2026, under the World Bank-supported connectivity and transport project PACT, presented two studies on the Democratic Republic of Congo's telecoms sector: one on infrastructure sharing, roaming and the .cd domain, the other on taxation. Recommendations include requiring operators to give the regulator current infrastructure data and a central infrastructure database updated twice a year.

Legislation and regulation

Ordonnances-lois organising statistical activity

Le Président Tshisekedi fixe les conditions d'organisation de l'activité statistique en RDC - ACP — Agence Congolaise de Presse (ACP), 12 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's President Félix Tshisekedi signed an ordinance-law organising statistical activity, the 98th Council of Ministers reported on 11 September 2026. It governs the production, dissemination and archiving of official statistics and national censuses, names the Institut national de la statistique as principal statistical authority, and applies its confidentiality rules at once to operations under way.

RDC : le gouvernement adopte le projet de loi autorisant la ratification du nouveau cadre de l'activité statistique — Opinion Info, 12 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Council of Ministers on 11 September 2026 adopted a bill to ratify the ordinance-law organising official statistics, submitted by the planning minister. The law sets principles of professional independence and statistical confidentiality, and bars use of data collected for statistics for control, prosecution or sanctions outside cases the law provides.

RDC : le Sénat ratifie à l’unanimité l’ordonnance-loi portant organisation de l’activité statistique — Actualite.cd, 22 October 2026

The DRC Senate on 21 October 2026 ratified ordonnance-loi 26/015 of 7 September 2026 organising statistical activity, by 73 votes to none of its 109 members. The 95-article law gives the national statistics institute the power to issue the statistical visa, puts a council chaired by the Prime Minister at the head of the system, penalises breaches of statistical secrecy and puts the system's budget at about USD 192 million.

Private member's bill on non-consensual diffusion of intimate content

Violence numérique : une proposition de loi de protection des victimes annoncée à Kinshasa - ACP — Agence Congolaise de Presse (ACP), 12 September 2026

Christelle Vuanga, a member of the Democratic Republic of Congo's National Assembly, announced she will table a bill this parliamentary session amending the penal code and the 2023 digital code to protect victims of non-consensual sharing of intimate images. She proposes prison terms of five to ten years with fines, and heavier penalties where blackmail or minors are involved.

Décret 23/13 creating the successor regulator ARPTIC

[Enquête]- ARPTC, un policier illégal et défaillant — Ouragan.cd, 14 September 2026

A joint press investigation calls DR Congo's telecoms regulator ARPTC unlawful and ineffective, noting that the successor authority created by a 2023 decree has never been installed. It details failings on service quality and equipment approval.

Private member's bill on the protection of minors in the digital environment

Assemblée nationale: le député Alfred Dibandi initie une proposition de loi relative à la protection des mineurs dans l'environnement numérique — Actualite.cd, 21 September 2026

Alfred Dibandi Nzondomyo, a national deputy in the Democratic Republic of Congo, filed a private member's bill on protecting minors in the digital environment on 21 September 2026. He said it complements the Digital Code and child-protection law, with rules by age band for under-6s, under-11s and under-15s. The bill's text has not been published.

Bill amending the Code du numérique on minors' access to social platforms

DRC Senate Considers Social Media Ban for Children Under 13 — We Are Tech, 1 October 2026

The DR Congo Senate declared admissible on 28 September 2026 a bill by Senator Yvan Kazadi Kankonde amending the 2023 Digital Code to bar children under 13 from opening social media accounts. Those aged 13 to 15 would need verifiable parental consent, and platforms would have to verify age. The bill has gone to committee and is not law.

Bill revising the public procurement law

DRC Moves to Expand Public Procurement Access for MSMEs and Start-Ups — Ecofin Agency, 1 October 2026

The DRC government adopted on 30 September 2026 a bill to replace the 2010 public procurement law (Law No. 10/010), presented by Budget Minister Adolphe Muzito. It would widen the use of paperless procedures and open public contracts to small businesses and start-ups, and responds to heavy use of direct awards and weak contract monitoring.

RDC: la diffamation dans les réseaux sociaux, punissable par le code du numérique — ACP, 6 October 2026

A Kinshasa lawyer spoke to the press agency on 6 October 2026. He said the 2023 digital code punishes online defamation, with no immunity for journalists or influencers. He cited the September 2026 arrest of a TikTok chronicler. The arrest followed a broadcast about a singer's private life.

RDC : Des personnes poursuivies pour des infractions numériques détenues à Makala, annonce Guillaume Ngefa — LePotentiel, 24 October 2026

The justice minister spoke at a press briefing. He said several people prosecuted for social-media offences are held at Makala prison in Kinshasa. He added that some of them presented themselves as communicators for the presidency. He said nobody is exempt from prosecution on grounds of political affiliation.

Regional collaboration

International declaration on space-based scientific data

International declaration on space-based scientific data. — Élysée, 10 September 2026 AngolaMadagascarMauritiusRwandaTunisiaGlobal

Angola, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Madagascar, Mauritius, Rwanda and Tunisia are among the 49 states that signed, with the European Space Agency, a declaration on space-based scientific data published by France's presidency on 10 September 2026. Helios Towers RDC said on 3 September 2026 that it now operates 3,000 telecoms sites in the Democratic Republic of Congo, 673 of them in rural areas, and that its infrastructure supports mobile coverage for more than 40 million people. Tanzania, DRC agree to fast-track SGR link and fibre-optic cable across Lake Tanganyika — Daily News (Tanzania), 5 September 2026 Tanzania

Tanzania and the DRC agreed to fast-track a fibre-optic cable from Kigoma to Kalemie across Lake Tanganyika, alongside a standard gauge railway link through Burundi and the establishment of a joint economic commission. The commitment was reaffirmed at a farewell meeting for the Congolese ambassador; no route survey, cost, financing or delivery date is published.

Goma-Mombasa fibre route

Paratus expands East Africa connectivity ahead of ITW Africa — TechTrendsKE, 9 September 2026 RwandaUgandaKenya

Paratus said its 2,000km Goma-to-Mombasa terrestrial fibre route is live and carrying traffic, announced at the ITW and Datacloud Africa conference in Nairobi. The route links eastern DRC to the Kenyan coast through Kigali, Kampala and Nairobi, with access to subsea capacity landing at Mombasa. Starlink services have launched in Uganda alongside it, and the company is introducing a Layer 2 connectivity service over low-earth-orbit satellite for the East African market.

Internet subscribers in Kinshasa report slow speeds, frequent interruptions and difficulty reaching online services across all four mobile operators, against the packages they pay for. The regulator is being called on to act.

The Democratic Republic of Congo plans to extend its fibre-optic backbone by 11,600 km through four 20-year concessions tendered internationally, with gap financing for routes whose revenues cannot cover the cost, reported on 26 September 2026. Helios Towers and UNICEF announced a multi-year partnership on 28 September 2026 supporting UNICEF's Giga school-connectivity initiative and its Learning Passport digital-learning programme, starting in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Malawi and Tanzania. DR Congo's government is preparing a commercial 5G launch, which the telecommunications minister said in late September it was working to make available before the end of 2026. Cybersecurity

Security audit of the state's public IT systems

Cybersécurité : Félix Tshisekedi ordonne un audit des infrastructures numériques de l'État — Numerico.cd, 24 October 2026

At the 101st Council of Ministers on 23 October 2026, President Félix Tshisekedi ordered an audit of DR Congo's public IT systems to find security flaws, coordinated by the posts and telecommunications ministry with the digital economy ministry. The interior and defence ministries are to tighten protection of sensitive systems, and the finance ministry is to work with the Banque Centrale du Congo on public-finance platforms, payment systems and critical infrastructure; the order came a day after the digital economy ministry warned of two critical PHP vulnerabilities.

Africa CDC said more than 150 internet devices, including Starlink systems, have been deployed to Ebola-affected health zones in the Democratic Republic of Congo to replace paper-based reporting, with 142 more due within two weeks and 600 being procured. Digital Identity and CRVS

Biometric passport

Semlex to face criminal trial in Belgium over Congo passport scandal — Biometric Update, 1 September 2026

Fourteen defendants in the case of Semlex, the Belgian firm that made the Democratic Republic of Congo's biometric passports from 2015 to 2025, have been sent to a Brussels criminal court on bribery, money laundering and tax fraud charges. Investigators allege about $60 of each $185 passport went to a Dubai firm, nearly $60 million in all; 51 Congolese citizens are civil parties.

RDC: Jean-Claude Mputu dénonce la continuité Semlex-RAM — CongoQuotidien, 2 September 2026

Jean-Claude Mputu, spokesman for the Congo Is Not For Sale coalition (CNPAV), said the Democratic Republic of Congo's passport contract with Semlex, renewed in 2020 when the original five-year deal expired, has never been published. The renewal, meant to last a year, ran until 2025; the only concession won was a passport price cut from $185 to $100.

Diaspora congolaise : lancement à Paris et Bruxelles de la phase pilote de l’identification numérique — B-one télévision, 30 September 2026

Registrants record their education, specialisms and intended contributions. Extension to other missions is planned, with no dates given.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Roadmap for modernising the national payments system

Le pari de la Banque centrale pour moderniser le système de paiements — Ouragan, 4 September 2026

The Central Bank of Congo instructed its technical services to draw up a roadmap for modernising the national payments system, its governor said after a central bank payments conference in Istanbul. The stated aims are the security, fluidity and continuity of transactions and assessing the uses and risks of artificial intelligence, tokenisation, stablecoins and central bank digital currencies; the delegation gave sustained attention to offline payment, given electricity and internet constraints in several parts of the country.

ISYS-REGIES revenue collection platform

Presumee cyber-maffia a la Banque Centrale sur les recettes de la DGI : versions croisees — Congo Guardian, 10 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of Congo's tax directorate wrote to the finance minister on 3 September 2026 alleging that central bank staff siphoned tax revenue by manipulating the ISYS-REGIES revenue platform, and sought their suspension and a judicial inquiry. On 10 September the Banque Centrale du Congo denied it, saying every action on the platform is logged and cannot be altered after validation.

RDC : la BCC dement toute manipulation des recettes de l'Etat et clarifie les ecarts entre Isys-regies et Navision — Actualite.cd, 11 September 2026

The Central Bank of Congo denied on 10 September 2026 that state revenue had been deleted or altered in ISYS-RÉGIES, its revenue-collection platform built since 2021 with the French development agency AFD. It said gaps against its NAVISION accounting system mostly involve the savings bank CADECO, which is outside its clearing house, and are settled in a monthly reconciliation. AFD and the finance ministry signed a €12.5 million convention in 2021 to digitise and link the revenue agencies.

Microfinance transition to digital payments

DRC Microfinance Institutions Seek Digital Support Ahead of Dollar Cash Ban — BANKABLE, 14 September 2026

Microfinance institutions in the Democratic Republic of Congo are seeking financial and technical support to move to digital payments before the central bank's ban on US dollar cash transactions takes effect on 9 April 2027. An August 2026 report by the microfinance and savings cooperative associations, ANIMF and APROCEC, with Innogence Consulting, names the deadline as a main constraint for smaller institutions.

Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS) accession

EAC, PAPSS, IA : la BCC accélère l’intégration de la RDC dans l’écosystème financier africain — Actualite.cd, 21 September 2026

DR Congo joined the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System on 18 September 2026, when the central bank governor, André Wameso, signed the accession agreement in Nairobi. The same day he signed the East African Community central banks' memorandum on currency convertibility and repatriation, which Burundi, Uganda, Tanzania, Kenya and South Sudan had already joined.

National consumer complaints platform

Communiqué de presse N°163/2026 : le Ministère de l'Économie Nationale lance « LOBA », une plateforme numérique dédiée à la protection des consommateurs — Ministère de l'Économie Nationale (RDC), Cellule de communication, 5 October 2026

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ministry of National Economy launched LOBA, a national consumer-protection platform, from 6 October 2026. Run through the Competition Commission (COMCO) with sector regulators covering telecoms, banking, insurance, electricity and health, it lets consumers report abuses, learn their rights and track complaints via a WhatsApp chatbot and a mobile app.

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's standards control office plans a national QR-code system letting consumers scan a product — medicines included — to verify its certification status, with consumer checks stated as opening within weeks and resting on 88 laboratories across 29 sites. The Democratic Republic of Congo's President Félix Tshisekedi gave the government three months, at the Council of Ministers on 25 September 2026, to set up a single identifier for businesses recognised by all public administrations. The plan relies on interoperable public information systems and harmonised legal texts, and a report on the mechanism and timetable is due within the three months.

RDC : l’apport de la digitalisation du secteur foncier présenté à Kinshasa — ACP, 8 October 2026

The DRC's land affairs minister said implementation of the December 2025 land law has begun: digitisation directorates created and more than 350 officials trained in archiving. Three of Kinshasa's nine land districts are in the first phase, toward a geolocated digital cadastre.

Beneficial ownership register (RBE)

ITIE-RDC Données et Divulgation Systématique — ITIE-RDC, 23 October 2026

The data page of EITI DR Congo reports 2023 extractive revenue of USD 5.85 billion, 5.61 billion of it from mining and 234.3 million from oil, from 134 reporting entities. It also publishes the beneficial ownership register set up under Law 25/048 of 1 July 2025 and a ministerial order of 8 July 2026, now listing 202 beneficial owners of 54 companies, two of them politically exposed persons.

Sectoral management information systems

Anonymised mobile-phone data in outbreak response

Health officials turn to mobile-phone data to track Ebola spread in Congo — Reuters, 3 September 2026

The World Health Organization and the non-profit Flowminder are using anonymised Vodacom mobile-phone records to map population movement and predict where the Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak will spread, the first such use in an Ebola response, Reuters reported on 3 September 2026. The data flagged Kisangani as at risk before cases appeared there. The outbreak has infected more than 6,250 people and killed 3,039.

Digital points-of-entry health screening

Au Beach Ngobila de Kinshasa, la technologie renforce le bouclier sanitaire entre Kinshasa et Brazzaville — OMS, Bureau régional pour l’Afrique, 18 October 2026 Congo

WHO's Regional Director for Africa, Mohamed Janabi, visited passenger screening at Kinshasa's Beach Ngobila river crossing on 18 October 2026, where the WHO-backed Preparedness Data Exchange platform supports Ebola surveillance. He said a similar electronic system would go live in Brazzaville the following week.
