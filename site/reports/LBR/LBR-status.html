Status report

This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Liberia published Digital Liberia, its national digital strategy for 2025-2029, in February 2026. It covers e-governance, data sovereignty and interoperability. It contains no data exchange strategy and no implementation roadmap with milestones.

Executive Order 163 of April 2026 created an Office for Technology and Digital Innovation and a National Digital Master Infrastructure. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications' National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 would introduce data classification and localisation requirements. It recommends building machine learning into the government's digital roadmap, with safeguards for automated decision-making. No instrument adopting the policy has been found. The ministry's cybersecurity strategy carries no adoption instrument.

The National Port Authority's RESET Strategic Plan 2025-2030 places port digitisation under its technological-modernisation pillar. An assessment of the Central Bank's 2020-2024 financial inclusion strategy found it weakest on consumer protection and financial literacy. Its 2026-2030 successor went through validation in September 2026 and is to be revised, with no launch date set. The Liberia Telecommunications Authority announced a package of telecom reforms in July 2026.

In August 2026 the Land Authority presented a national spatial data infrastructure as part of the President's ARREST Agenda, as an intention with no funded programme. No operational digital land register has been found.

Liberia scores 49.1 out of 100 for overall governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, 26th of 54 African states (2023). The score is marginally below the continental average and 3.8 points above 2014. Improvement slowed over 2019-2023.

Legislation and regulation

The Cybercrime Act of 2025 was approved on 3 July 2026 and printed on 24 July, six weeks before a ministerial announcement dated its signing to 19 August. It replaced a drafting-stage bill of 2021 as the text on record. In September 2026 police arrested a man under the Act over AI-generated images of the Vice-President. On 22 September a criminal court ordered his release on a habeas corpus petition. The main opposition party said it would ask the Supreme Court to rule on the Act's constitutionality.

The legal basis for digital services rested on the Electronic Transactions Law of 2002, the National Identification Registry Act of 2011, the ICT Policy 2019-2024 and the Central Bank's 2020 e-payment regulations. It had no data protection law and no cross-government data-sharing law. The Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act took effect in March 2026. Liberia has no economy-wide localisation rule, but section 9(c) of the payment regulations requires payment systems to be hosted locally or to give the Central Bank unfettered access. The 2026 Public Procurement and Concessions Act makes use of the e-GP system compulsory. In July 2026 the Telecommunications Authority began rewriting the licensing regime by regulator instrument under the Telecommunications Act 2007.

Executive Order 147 of April 2025 makes enrolment in the National Biometric Identification System compulsory for citizens and for foreign residents of 90 days or more. It makes the National Identification Number the primary identifier, an obligation created without statute. Its enforcement lapsed when issuance was suspended in August 2025.

Executive Order 163, signed in April 2026, gives twelve months for a law on the new digital-government framework to be drafted. The National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025-2029 set a programme from a National Cybersecurity Act by Q3 2025 to an operational package by Q1 2026. Cybercrime, data protection and critical infrastructure laws and sectoral regulations were due by Q4 2025.

Every deadline in that programme had passed by August 2026.

Data protection

On 13 September 2026 the gender and social protection ministry confirmed that leaked records of children in its Save the Future Project are authentic. It said their disclosure was never authorised and opened an internal investigation. Officials had posted enrolment lists of children in social protection programmes, with home addresses, to Facebook (September 2026). No action under the data-protection Act has been found.

In June 2026 the Liberia Telecommunications Authority fined Orange Liberia LRD 4 million, about US$21,900, in Liberia's first privacy penalty. It held the operator liable for issuing a customer's SIM card to a third party without authority. The fine was imposed under telecom regulation.

The Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act of 2024 was approved on 9 March 2026 and printed on 23 March, taking effect on publication. The Act gives existing businesses a one-year transition. A convicted public officer faces up to four years' imprisonment.

The Act gives an Independent Information Commissioner 24 months from March 2026 to issue implementing rules. The data governance policy places complaints and remedies with the Independent Information Commission set up under the Freedom of Information Act 2010. No appointment or rule under the Act has been found. Liberia has no dedicated data protection authority, and the Telecommunications Authority regulates telecoms data only. Liberia has signed the ECOWAS Supplementary Act requiring a national data authority.

The Cybercrime Act of 2025 requires a breach to be reported to the national CERT within 72 hours. Before it, a House probe opened in February 2026 into alleged breaches of data held by the revenue authority, the electricity corporation and the identity registry.

The 2026 Satellite Communications Guidelines require satellite operators to keep data-protection and cybersecurity compliance plans. The Central Bank's 2020 electronic payment regulations carry consumer and data protection sections. Identity data may be released only to the applicant or on a court order, and no specialised tribunal oversees it.

Liberia scores 74.6 out of 100 for Digital Freedom in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 16th of 54 and 9.0 points below 2014 (2023).

Regional collaboration

The ECOWAS Community budget for 2026-2028 carries no separate digital or ICT appropriation. Its only ICT-named line, Youth Entrepreneurship in ICT, shows nil spending in 2024 and nil in the estimates to 2028.

ECOWAS ministers adopted the ECOWAS Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024-2029 at Cotonou on 4 October 2024. The strategy itself has not been found in publication. The same meeting required member states to have every mobile operator apply the ECOWAS Roaming Regulation by the end of 2025.

Liberia's national ID card is ECOWAS-compliant in design, but Liberia has not implemented the ECOWAS biometric identity card (2025). In March 2026 the ECOWAS-World Bank WURI programme validated a cross-border interoperability strategy and a 2026 work plan. Its aim is mutual recognition of national credentials across the region. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger cut their ECOWAS and WURI ties in 2024 and launched a common passport in January 2025.

Liberia's National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025-2029 commits it to the Budapest Convention and the African Union's Malabo Convention. Liberia has acceded to neither. In October 2026 it joined the Smart Africa Alliance as its 43rd member. It was also admitted to the African Network of Cybersecurity Authorities, and agreed with Morocco to pursue a cybersecurity memorandum.

Sierra Leone's procurement authority sent a delegation to study Liberia's e-GP system on 6-7 August 2026. The World Bank approved the US$137 million WARDIP2 operation for Benin, Liberia and Sierra Leone on 11 March 2026. Liberia signed its own US$50 million tranche on 8 June 2026. On 1 October 2026 the central bank governor led a delegation to the Bank of Ghana to study its interbank payment and settlement company. Economic integration scores 31.0 out of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 41st of 54 (2023).

Standards

In August 2026 the Liberia Land Authority told the UN Committee of Experts on Global Geospatial Information Management that Liberia has no coordinated national spatial data infrastructure. Each institution collects its own geospatial data, and one body's data is not systematically shared with others. The Authority sought international partners and technical support to build one.

The instant payment system launched in December 2025 runs on the open-source Mojaloop platform. Mobile money, the instant payment system and the planned national switch sit under the Central Bank's published Mobile Money Regulations. The payment system's own participation, pricing and technical rules are not published. They sit in agreements between the bank, the switch operator and the mobile money operators.

The 2026 Public Procurement and Concessions Act requires public entities to use the e-GP system. In August 2026 the Telecommunications Authority launched an automated licensing and renewal platform, keeping the in-person route open.

The Liberia Standards Authority became a correspondent member of ISO in October 2025, its dues paid through an EU-funded project.

In March 2026 WURI committed ECOWAS member states, Liberia among them, to common specifications for foundational digital identity. No published standard under it has been found.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In October 2026 the Press Union of Liberia called for the immediate withdrawal of the Civil Service Agency's confidentiality agreement for civil servants. It said the agreement upends the Freedom of Information Act, and that the Independent Information Commission had opposed it. The union first challenged the agreement on 5 August 2026.

Participation scores 65.1 out of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 8th of 54 African states (2023). Civil Society Space scores 90.7, 2nd of 54, and Deliberative and Participatory Governance 75.9, 4th. Media Freedom scores 66.0, 11th, down 2.0 points over the decade. Freedom of Expression and Belief scores 70.0, 21st, down 8.2 points.

In April 2026 the President relaunched SMS shortcode 2424 for citizens to report on named officials and stalled projects. It first ran in 2023 as a feedback line on public-service delivery. No count of messages has been published. The Civil Service Agency launched a monthly newsletter on 5 August 2026, beside its website, virtual assistant and magazine. A performance management information system launched in April 2026 promises quarterly results for every institution on one public platform.

In March 2026 the national ID steering committee's report to President Boakai had not been made public. The e-GP system publishes no award, tender, bidder or contract-value data (August 2026).

Ten civil society organisations founded a Digital Advocacy and Rights Alliance in September 2026. They press for an independent data protection authority and amendments narrowing the Cybercrime Act. A Liberian Observer editorial argued that the country cannot build digital sovereignty by outsourcing its digital infrastructure.

Finance

Financial sustainability

OeSD International is to finance about US$54.7 million of identity enrolment and infrastructure under a build-operate-transfer concession (September 2026). The concession is signed but cannot be implemented until the Legislature ratifies it. October 2026 reports put the deal at US$52 million. The company is to recover its investment before transferring the system to government. The registry will disclose the agreement's details only after legislative approval (August 2026).

The 2026 national budget more than doubles the identity registry's appropriation, to US$2.0 million. US$1 million of it is the registry's first funded investment project since 2024. The 2026 budget raises the national postal addressing system to US$1.1 million. It opens 2026 lines of US$1.2 million for the national cadastre, US$355,000 for digitising court records and US$400,000 for the security and data protection act. In 2025 the registry had US$0.8 million and addressing US$250,000 (FY2025). The IFMIS licence fees, US$165,850, sit on the state's own budget (FY2026).

The digital lines of the 2026 national budget come to US$7.1 million appropriated, up from US$5.1 million in 2025 and against US$8.3 million in 2024 (FY2026). Just over half of the 2024 appropriation was spent (FY2024).

The 2026 budget's chapters carry Government of Liberia money only. Its external-resources annex lists aid by partner, sector and modality with no project lines, so no externally financed digital line is printed. Of US$2.76 million voted in 2024 for the revenue authority's project digitising revenue transactions, US$421,401 was spent (FY2024). The World Bank's GREAT digital-government project closes on 30 November 2030. Its appraisal commits research into financing the county one-stop service centres, including private operation.

Since 2015 the Universal Access Fund has taken 0.5% of designated telecoms licensees' annual gross revenue. The Legislature approves its budget each year within the national budget. On 26 October 2026 the regulator announced sites for 15 new towers under the fund, adding to the 15 it already supports. In July 2026 the regulator's chairman declined to disclose what the authority collects in licence fees, spectrum charges and levies.

MoUs and other agreements

The health memorandum of understanding Liberia signed with the United States on 9 December 2025 plans US$124,418,400 of American support against US$50,725,704 from Liberia. It states that it "is not an international agreement and does not give rise to legal rights and obligations". Health data systems are one of the lines it plans, running to 2030.

Of the twelve external commitments touching Liberia's digital estate, only one rests on a memorandum of understanding rather than a grant, a loan or a guarantee — and it is the fourth-largest of them by value.

The December 2025 memorandum is public as a copy posted by a third party, as is the America First Global Health Strategy template it follows, published by Public Citizen. No copy published by either government has been found. The National Port Authority has an agreement with the French port operator HAROPA to digitise state port operations (July 2026). No sum, scope of works or delivery date has been announced.

On 16 September 2026 the National Identification Registry urged the Legislature to ratify its signed US$54.7 million build-operate-transfer agreement with Austria's OSD. The agreement is not yet in force, and its terms have not been published. The copy before the House in October 2026 lacked the signatures of the supplier's chief executive and the telecommunications authority's chairman. The arrangement was restructured into a public-private partnership concession under which OSD finances enrolment, operates the system and then transfers it to the state (May 2026). President Boakai rejected a traditional procurement model in favour of that concession (August 2026). Digital Liberia and the merged motorcycle and tricycle riders' union signed a memorandum in August 2026. It is to build digital identification, a rider and vehicle database and GPS tracking for commercial riders.

New investments

Liberia signed its US$50 million tranche of the regional WARDIP2 digital programme on 8 June 2026. It is part of a US$125 million World Bank package for energy, digital and infrastructure (June 2026). The GREAT digital public infrastructure project was launched in March 2025 with US$30 million of World Bank funding. Part of the World Bank funding for identification was put on hold as the ID rollout stalled (March 2026).

Twelve digital-sector commitments touch Liberia as at September 2026, running from 2016 to 2031, nine of them still live. The recorded value passes US$200 million (September 2026), though much of that is guarantee cover rather than money into the country, and one, a Korean youth digital-capacity grant, carries no US-dollar figure. Four are grants, three are World Bank concessional loans, two are MIGA guarantees and one a commercial loan; two carry no instrument type, among them the fourth-largest by value, the United States health data-systems plan, which is expressly non-binding. MIGA is the largest financier by recorded value and the World Bank second; USAID holds two commitments, and the African Development Bank, the U.S. Department of State, Korea's foreign ministry, the IFC and the AfCFTA Adjustment Fund one each. Connectivity carries the largest committed value, ahead of digital identity and management information systems.

MIGA issued a political-risk guarantee over Orange Liberia on 8 September 2023, for up to eight years. It covers tower construction, fibre installation and mobile financial services. A second MIGA guarantee covers solar-hybrid power at about 612 tower sites. An African Development Bank grant is upgrading the Central Bank's payment systems and data centres. Another funds unique bank identification with Sierra Leone, The Gambia and Guinea. The USAID connectivity and digital investment activities have closed.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

The Liberia Telecommunications Authority signed comprehensive Satellite Communications Guidelines on 19 August 2026, opening licensed satellite broadband to Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Amazon Kuiper. They price direct-to-device service separately from ordinary satellite broadband. They set a US$250,000 D2D service-provider licence fee, a 9 per cent levy on D2D sales and a 0.5 per cent universal-access contribution (2026). They grant five-year landing rights to non-geostationary operators and ten-year rights to geostationary ones.

About 70 per cent of Liberians did not use the internet in 2024, on ITU figures. ITU data put 4G coverage at 93 per cent of the population in 2025. The 2024 IIAG scores Internet & Computers at 21.4 out of 100.0 and 44th of 54 African states, and mobile communications at 58.4 and 40th. No unique-subscriber penetration figure is published for Liberia, and the rate usually quoted counts SIM connections, not people. An entry-level mobile broadband basket falls within the moderate affordability band.

International capacity rests on a single submarine cable, the ACE system, backed since May 2025 by a mobile operator's fibre link to Côte d'Ivoire. The link reaches six other undersea cables and carried all of Liberia's international traffic through a four-day ACE outage. An open-access carrier's 350 km backbone runs from Monrovia to the Côte d'Ivoire and Guinea borders (2024). Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire agreed at Abidjan in June 2026 to commercialise the spare fibre on the TRANSCO CLSG power line, targeting operation before the end of 2026. In October 2025 ECOWAS experts approved the feasibility study for a second cable serving Cabo Verde, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea-Bissau. No route, cost or construction date has been published since. The one operational internet exchange point, in Monrovia, has limited participation.

The regulator announced a package of telecom reforms in July 2026 and revoked Star Cell's operating licence after six years of inactivity. It moved applications and renewals online in August 2026. Orange Liberia launched 5G services in October 2026, disclosing no coverage areas or packages.

Data Storage

The Liberia Telecommunications Authority's cybersecurity regulations of January 2026 require telecommunications providers to store personal data primarily inside Liberia. The Central Bank requires electronic payment systems to be hosted locally or, if hosted abroad, to give it unfettered access to system data. Liberia has no economy-wide localisation law.

The GREAT project, launched in March 2025, is upgrading the National Data Center run by the state operator LTC-Mobile, the country's main hosting facility. No hyperscale or regional cloud zone exists in or near the country, and the nearest AWS region is Cape Town. Neither Google Cloud nor Microsoft Azure operates a West African region.

Enrolment centres in all 15 counties send encrypted identity records to a central data centre, over a network link or on portable media (2024). In March 2026 the Nairobi firm that built the system withheld full database access from the registry over an unpaid balance. The approved 2026 budget made no provision for that balance. An African Development Bank grant running to 2026 is upgrading the Central Bank's main and recovery data centres. The bank invited international bids for the upgrade on 28 September 2026.

Energy

In February 2025 Côte d'Ivoire cut its electricity deliveries to Liberia from 50 MW to 7.5 MW, and load shedding widened across Monrovia. Low water held back the Mount Coffee hydropower plant at the same time. Supply falls short every dry season, from December to April, and the utility covers the gap with imports.

Electricity access doubled over the decade to 2024, to about a third of the population. In 2023, 52.7 per cent of the urban population had access, against 9.3 per cent of the rural population. Access to Energy scores 13.8 out of 100 in the 2024 IIAG, 46th of 54 and among Liberia's ten worst measures. It rose 10.9 points over 2014-2023, the largest gain in the Infrastructure sub-category. Electricity costs about US$0.24 per kilowatt-hour, a level the World Bank treats as high (2024).

Renewable plant made up about 70 per cent of the 126 MW of installed capacity in 2024, led by the 88 MW Mount Coffee station. An off-grid policy is partly deployed, with solar mini-grids for rural communities and results-based financing for solar home systems. A MIGA guarantee of US$12.06 million, running up to ten years from 2020, covers solar-hybrid power at about 612 Orange Liberia tower sites.

Technical Capacity

In August 2026 the procurement commission began giving computers and printers to the first institutions joining the second phase of e-procurement. That phase is meant to reach far more than the six institutions of the first. The telecoms regulator received equipment for its own use of the platform. In July 2025 the National Elections Commission transferred its biometric voter registration kits to the identity registry. A steering committee chaired by the National Security Advisor was appointed the same month to oversee the ID restart. The registry runs on chronic shortfalls and a debt to its technology provider, and government debt has blocked World Bank disbursements.

GitHub's Innovation Graph publishes no developer count for Liberia, which falls below its threshold of 100 developers a quarter (2025). Only a couple of the country's universities offer ICT degree programmes.

Effective Administration scores 50.6 out of 100 in the 2024 IIAG, 28th of 54 and up 4.7 points over 2014-2023. Public Administration rose 10.1 points over the decade to 46.3, driving most of Liberia's governance improvement. It still ranks 36th.

Cybersecurity

The printed Cybercrime Act of 2025 creates a National Cybersecurity Council, with the national CERT as its technical arm. It seats the financial intelligence agency on the Council, requires breach reports to the CERT within 72 hours and protects whistleblowers. In October 2026 one of its Senate sponsors said it was being enforced before the Council and a specialised cybercrime team had been set up.

The National Cybersecurity Strategy carries a July 2024 cover date for a 2025-2029 period, but its running headers read "Draft National Cybersecurity Strategy 2024-2029". No adoption instrument accompanies it. It sets a legislative programme from Q3 2025 to Q1 2026. It commits Liberia to the Budapest and Malabo Conventions, to neither of which it is a party. The ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index places Liberia in its lowest, "building" tier (2024).

The 2021 draft cybercrime bill allowed systems to be designated Critical National Information Infrastructure, then audited and inspected. Its offence schedule opened on attacks against that infrastructure and on unlawful access to computer systems. It carried no duty to report breaches and no whistleblower protection; the enacted Act carries both.

A 2025 study found no documented risk or privacy impact assessment for the biometric ID system, which the NIR Act does not require. Its access rules name security personnel, civil servants, public agents and private individuals without clear protocols. The Central Bank publishes a signed external audit of its 2024 accounts, but no audit of the instant payment system's security. In February 2026 the House opened a probe into an alleged breach of data held by the revenue authority, the electricity corporation and the identity registry.

DPI

Data Exchange

A 2025 study found no operational government data exchange platform in Liberia. E-government systems remain fragmented and incompatible, and the identity registry's bilateral memoranda stand in place of a shared layer. The tax administration system verifies taxpayers' national identification numbers against the registry in real time (2024). The Revenue Authority also reported a real-time payment link with one commercial bank. The registry has interfaces or memoranda with the health ministry, the passport and driver-licence authorities, NASSCORP, the Civil Service Agency and the telecoms regulator (2024). The older programmes were never built to interoperate.

No law requires or governs cross-government data sharing; the legal basis for digital services rests on the Electronic Transactions Law of 2002 and the registry's 2011 Act. The Digital Liberia strategy for 2025-2029 names data sovereignty and interoperability but carries no data exchange strategy. Each institution collects its own geospatial data (August 2026). The statistics institute's survey of data users recommended a formal inter-agency protocol and application programming interfaces (2026). Cross-agency flows still depend on manual working groups.

Government discloses its digital activity through periodic strategy publications and press briefings, with no dashboard (2026). The data-sharing agreement under the December 2025 health memorandum with the United States is being sought through freedom-of-information litigation in Washington (April 2026). The nearest text in public is a model agreement released by an American advocacy group.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Issuance by Liberia's national identity registry was suspended in August 2025. The registry closed entirely in early 2026 after the procurement commission rejected a restricted-bid award to a foreign firm. The Nairobi company that built the system for US$5.9 million then withheld full database access over a US$1.7 million unpaid balance, leaving roughly 640,000 people already registered unable to obtain their cards (March 2026). The registry said in October 2026 that fewer than 800,000 people hold a national identity card.

The registry runs the National Biometric Identification System under its 2011 Act, issuing a unique number and a biometric card to citizens and residents, with enrolment open from birth. Executive Order No. 147 of 14 April 2025 made enrolment compulsory for citizens and for foreign nationals resident 90 days or more. No statute stands behind it. A card costs a citizen US$5 and a foreign resident US$10 or US$20.

The 2011 Act releases registry data only to the applicant or on court order. No specialised tribunal or judicial oversight of the system exists. The card is required for banking, health, education and telecommunications and mandatory for SIM registration. Banks have required it to open or reactivate an account since June 2025. Verification runs on the registry's own platform, by card or fingerprint, with batch checks for authorised institutions (2024).

Civil registration completeness was about 66 per cent (2020). Registration had been decentralised to 58 centres in all 15 counties by December 2024. No operating link between civil registration and the population register exists (2024). The concession with the Austrian State Printing House was signed and sent to the Legislature, whose ratification the registry urged on 16 September 2026. In October 2026 the House referred it to a joint committee and the Senate to three of its committees, each to report within two weeks. On 28 October 2026 the House concessions committee halted its hearing because the agreement lacked the signatures of the supplier's chief executive and the telecommunications authority's chairman. It reset the hearing for 4 November.

Digital Payments and Fintech

On 16 December 2025 the Central Bank launched Pay Na-Na, a national real-time payment system built on the open-source Mojaloop platform. It links the Lonestar MTN and Orange wallets, which had run as separate silos. A transfer costs no more than 1.0 per cent of value between US$1 and US$2,000, and no more than US$25 above that (August 2026). In October 2026 the governor put the system at more than 5.3 million transactions since launch.

That schedule turns regressive above its US$2,000 break: the US$25 cap equals 1.25 per cent of a US$2,000 transfer, so the charge rises at the threshold and then falls as a share of value on every larger transfer, becoming cheaper in percentage terms than the 1.0 per cent band from roughly US$2,500 upward.

A Central Bank directive of September 2025 routes mobile-money government payments and revenue collections through the national switch. Civil-servant salaries moved from cheques to mobile wallets at launch. Taxpayers can pay taxes and fees for 18 ministries and agencies by phone through a separate Revenue Authority platform. The person-to-government leg and the National Electronic Payment Switch were still to follow in July 2026. In September 2026 the finance minister targeted the switch for March 2027.

Operators sit under the Central Bank's Mobile Money Regulations and its 2020 electronic payment regulations, which require a complaints desk with recourse to the Bank. Digital credit guidelines of March 2022 cap a digital loan at US$400. The scheme's participation, pricing and technical rules are not published. The Bank runs the system as well as regulating it, holding the settlement accounts.

Account ownership stood at 52 per cent of adults in 2024. The Central Bank counted 11.5 million registered and 4.3 million active mobile money subscribers in 2024. No audit of the payment system's security, operations or compliance has been published (2024).

Registries

The Central Bank launched an enhanced collateral registry on 27 August 2026. A regulation gazetted on 6 July 2026 extends it to immovable property. Since 2010 it had covered movable assets only. No operational digital land register exists; in October 2025 the Land Authority signed a memorandum with South Korean partners to build a land information system. Only 13.8 per cent of agricultural landholdings have official documentation, from 44.9 per cent in Montserrado to 6.6 per cent in the south-east (2024). A National Digital and Postal Addressing System with an online portal is deployed in part (2024).

Liberia launched a digital beneficial ownership register in 2023. Business registration has moved to an online platform. The Business Registry is not interfaced with the Revenue Authority's tax system or any other government platform (2025). A decentralisation pilot across seven counties opened in Ganta in August 2026, issuing certificates in four days.

Liberia ran biometric voter registration nationwide for the October 2023 elections, producing a roll of 2,471,617 voters. The Elections Commission and the identity registry keep separate databases (2025). In July 2025 the Commission handed more than 500 biometric registration kits to the registry. The Household Social Registry has enrolled over 250,000 households and serves three social protection programmes (2025). It issues its own scannable household ID cards. A household identifier drawing on biometrics, national ID or voter cards was listed as work to come (2023).

Sectoral management information systems

The gender ministry launched an upgraded gender-based violence information system for incident reporting, case management and referrals in October 2026. A community health information system to replace paper-based community reporting was put out to tender in January 2026. On 30 October 2026 the water, sanitation and hygiene commission signed a memorandum with two US partners to build a system cataloguing the country's water points. A DHIS2-based immunisation registry was piloted at 19 facilities in three counties from December 2023, with national rollout described as planned.

The Ministry of Labour launched LiberiaWorks in October 2025 to link employers, jobseekers, universities and government. The justice sector's case management system has run at the National Police since 2021 and was launched at the Judiciary by June 2022. One harmonised system for the whole justice chain is the stated goal. Agriculture is procuring a mechanisation tracking system meant to link to the National Farmer Registry (2026).

USAID's health systems programme built the national health management information system and the iHRIS human-resources register, and closed in 2022. A DHIS2 tracker for client-level HIV data replaced paper registers at 21 PEPFAR-supported treatment sites from 2021. In May 2026 the Ministry launched a nationwide assessment of readiness for electronic medical records. A private offline-first health record platform went into live clinical use on 1 September 2026 and reached seven clinics and health centres in about three weeks.

The Ministry of Education has moved from paper to a digital EMIS 2.0 and validated a costed plan in September 2025 introducing national unique learner and teacher identifiers. The Revenue Authority operates its integrated tax administration system nationwide. It was built out under a US$24 million World Bank project that closed in 2024. The project left it still not interfaced with the financial management system. The Civil Service Agency signed a memorandum in May 2024 to verify government payroll against national identification numbers.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

The e-GP system began as a six-institution pilot and went national in February 2025, with World Bank and African Development Bank backing. The 2026 Public Procurement and Concessions Act requires procuring entities to use it. In August 2026 a second phase began sending equipment to the presidential affairs ministry, the Civil Service Agency, the Water and Sewer Corporation and two ministries. The system publishes no award, tender, bidder or contract-value data.

An eLiberia one-stop portal was launched in July 2018. On 17 and 18 October 2026 ministries validated 16 priority services for digitalisation, towards a new e-Liberia portal and a Government Service Bus. Online services run agency by agency: tax registration, filing and payment at the Revenue Authority, company registration at the Business Registry, address registration and, since August 2026, telecom licensing. IFMIS was last rebuilt under a World Bank project that closed in 2024.

Executive Order 163 of April 2026 created a digital-government framework around an Office for Technology and Digital Innovation. It gives twelve months for a law on the framework to be drafted. The World Bank's GREAT project is building digital-government systems across nine institutions to November 2030. Liberia scores 0.2905 on the UN E-Government Development Index (2024). In October 2026 the deputy information minister said the online police clearance platform had earned more than US$200,000 since May 2026.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

Civil registration was decentralised to all 15 counties by December 2024 and is recorded on the DHIS2 platform. The school monitoring system piloted in county schools from March 2026 is run by the education ministry. So is the online case management system built for the justice institutions with UNDP support, which serves national bodies. In January 2026 a police station in Lower Montserrado had no computers. No digital system run by a county administration has been found.

The 2024 Ibrahim Index places Liberia among the most improved African states on rural representation and participation (2023). That measure concerns how rural interests are heard nationally.

Rural digital data capture

Rural Economy rose 30.9 points over the decade to 2023, to 71.1 out of 100 and 11th of 54 African states. Rural Representation and Participation rose 31.7 points to 65.6, the sixth most improved of 96 indicators. Rural Land and Water Access sits at 47.5, 33rd, despite rising 16.3 points.

Birth registration had been decentralised to 58 centres in all fifteen counties by December 2024, on a DHIS2-based information system. The education ministry's geo-mapping monitoring system, launched in March 2026, has school staff enter geo-referenced data on site. It was piloted to June 2026 in five counties, including rural Rivercess and Gbarpolu.

In May 2026 the Ministry of Health launched a nationwide assessment of facility readiness for electronic medical records. Electronic records exist as scattered deployments, such as OpenMRS at J.J. Dossen Hospital in rural Maryland County. Other facilities report on paper forms keyed by district or county clerks (2023). In January 2026 Nyehn Town Police Station in Lower Montserrado had no computers. Officers elsewhere had earlier begun recording GPS-tagged crime reports on tablets.

Rural electricity access was a small fraction of the urban rate in 2023. The agriculture ministry's planned machinery tracking system will link to the National Farmer Registry only if the registry is assessed ready (2026). Liberia has had no agricultural census in twenty years (2023). Agriculture was its weakest data category in the 2024 Open Data Inventory. In August 2026 the identity registry named Salayea in Lofa County as its first new enrolment office.

Technology

AI

The Civil Service Agency runs an AI-powered virtual information assistant beside its website and monthly newsletter (August 2026). It answers questions about the agency and carries no transaction. In August 2024 four AI X-ray machines for tuberculosis screening, the first in the country, were commissioned in hospitals in Montserrado and Margibi. An offline AI fundus camera for diabetic retinopathy was validated at the national referral hospital (September 2026). The revenue authority is building an AI-powered contact centre for tax and customs enquiries with a local firm (September 2026).

The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications' National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 recommends building machine learning into the government's digital roadmap. It calls for safeguards for automated decision-making. No adoption of the policy has been found, and no law or regulation on artificial intelligence has been found.

In July 2026 UNDP named AI for government among its three digital workstreams in Liberia. It counts AI and cybersecurity teaching at the University of Liberia among its contributions.

ICT Industry

In July 2026 the National Port Authority signed a smart-ports memorandum with the French port operator HAROPA to digitise state port operations. No sum, scope of works or delivery date has been announced. The national identity system was built by a Nairobi firm, and its planned successor is a concession with Austria's OSD.

GitHub publishes no developer count for Liberia, which falls below its reporting threshold (2025). Partech's 2025 report records no venture capital deals in Liberia. A small set of hubs and incubators trains and convenes entrepreneurs.

Innovation ecosystem

In September 2026 the telecoms regulator disclosed a memorandum with the youth and sports ministry to set up 15 digital hubs, one in each county. It has committed more than US$200,000 to the partnership. iLab Liberia and its iCampus, the DKS Incubation Center and Innovations Hub Liberia run training, incubation and civic-technology programmes.

Liberia is one of 117 economies left out of the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024 for want of data. The index ranks 133 economies, so Liberia cannot be compared with its neighbours on any of its pillars.

Partech's 2025 Africa technology venture capital report records no funded venture deals in Liberia. African technology funding rose to US$4.1 billion in 2025.

Capacity

Literacy

Education Enrolment fell 7.6 points over the decade to 41.6 out of 100 in the 2024 IIAG, 22nd of 54 African states (2023). Education Completion rose 17.7 points to 21.8, 37th of 54. Equality in Education rose 17.1 points to 52.1, 25th. The composite Education score is 43.3, up 4.7 points and 39th.

Human Resources in Education scores 78.1 out of 100 in the 2024 IIAG, 30th of 54 and up 4.9 points (2023). It is among Liberia's ten best-scoring measures. Education Quality scores 44.0, 28th, up 9.1 points.

No school data was collected between the 2022 census and the September 2025 review of the education management information system. The system is moving from paper to a digital successor. A costed plan for unique learner and teacher identifiers was validated in September 2025. The 2024 Open Data Inventory found no Liberian data on education outcomes meeting its criteria.

Training and skills

The Public Procurement and Concessions Commission runs an e-GP Clinic twice a week to teach vendors to use the government procurement platform (August 2026).

UNDP's digital work runs through an innovation hub and AI and cybersecurity programmes at the University of Liberia, alongside a digital-skills partnership with Orange Liberia (July 2026). The country's technology hubs, iLab Liberia and its iCampus, the DKS Incubation Center and Innovations Hub Liberia, run training and civic-tech programmes reaching across the counties. No national digital skills programme has been found. The cybercrime prevention agency's Cyber Liberia 2030 project was at pipeline stage in July 2026. It would train at least 20,000 people in digital literacy, cybersecurity and AI from October 2026 to October 2027.

ICT degree provision barely exists across the Liberian university system (2025). Liberia sits below the reporting threshold at which GitHub publishes a developer count for a country (2025). The Ministry of Labour's LiberiaWorks workforce platform links employers, jobseekers and universities through a talent marketplace and a placement programme. It is not connected to digital ID or any other government registry, so it cannot verify the people it lists.

Research institutions

A 2025 review found that ICT degree provision barely exists across Liberia's university system. UNDP runs an innovation hub and AI and cybersecurity work at the University of Liberia (July 2026). No university department with its own technology research funding has been found.

The 2024 IIAG places Liberia's statistical capacity among the lowest on the continent, with no improvement over the decade (2023). The World Bank's statistical performance indicators rate data sources as the weakest of five pillars, well below data use (2023).

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal Access to Public Services scores 15.0 out of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 49th of 54 African states (2023). It is among Liberia's ten worst measures, and moved 0.3 points in a decade.

Executive Order 147 of April 2025 makes the national identification number a requirement for banking, health, education and telecommunications. Banks have demanded it to open or reactivate an account since June 2025. The register covers a small minority of the population (August 2026). Hundreds of thousands of registrants could not collect their cards during a vendor dispute (March 2026). Allegations of bribery at enrolment centres drew a legislative probe (June 2025).

In 2025 the registry named a US$5 card fee and a ceiling of about 5,000 cards a day as its limits. Its executive director backed lawmakers' call for free cards if the government meets the cost (September 2025). Officials say poor first-time applicants will not be charged under the planned concession (August 2026). Non-nationals hold a resident or foreigner card with the same service access (2025).

Social protection beneficiaries were being enrolled biometrically in 2025. Civil servants in three counties are being moved to mobile money salaries (2026). Transfers between the two mobile money wallets became interoperable in December 2025. An entry-level mobile broadband bundle sat within the moderate affordability band (2022).

The civil registry works through county centres and registration points at health facilities (2019). On 4 September 2026 the telecoms regulator opened its first service centre outside Monrovia, at Buchanan. In August 2026 the identity registry named Salayea in Lofa County as its first new enrolment office.

Digital divides

Equal Socioeconomic Opportunity fell 23.0 points over 2014-2023 to 33.5 out of 100, 40th of 54. It is the second most deteriorated of the 96 indicators in Liberia's 2024 Ibrahim Index profile. Over the same decade, legal protection against discrimination and rural voice in national policy were among its most improved.

About 70 per cent of Liberians did not use the internet in 2024, on ITU figures. Rural electricity access was a fraction of urban access in 2023. Liberia's internet and computers score remains near the foot of the African ranking (2023).

A 2022 report on women's online rights in Liberia gives no national measure of the gap between women's and men's internet use. Liberia is not surveyed individually in the GSMA's mobile gender gap reporting. Completion of schooling and equality in education both rose steeply over 2014-2023 from a low base.

Data

National statistics

The World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators for 2023 put Liberia's data use above 89 per cent. Its data sources pillar sits in the 20-49 per cent band, the weakest of five (2023). Data products sit at 70-89 per cent, and data services and infrastructure at 50-69 per cent (2023). The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores statistical capacity at 32.7 out of 100, 45th of 54, up 0.6 points in ten years (2023).

By 2023 Liberia had run a population census and a business census within ten years, but no agricultural census within twenty. Its surveys over that decade included three or more health surveys, two household surveys, one labour force survey and one agricultural survey. There was no business survey.

The statistics institute compiles returns from ministries into national statistical bulletins, the latest published in 2023. Its 2025/2026 survey of data users recommended a formal inter-agency data-sharing protocol and APIs. Cross-agency flows run through manual working groups. In 2019 civil registration was split between the Ministry of Health and other agencies, with no link between the systems. Rural police stations keep case files and crime statistics on paper (January 2026). The 2025/2026 Annual School Census added school-based collection and digital validation.

Liberia was left out of the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024 for want of data coverage.

Open data

The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores disclosure of public records at 44.1 out of 100 and accessibility at 41.3 (2023). They rank 13th and 16th of 54 African states, up 11.9 and 10.1 points over the decade.

In the 2024 Open Data Inventory Liberia's social statistics score between 3.0 and 4.5 out of 10 for openness. Its economic and financial statistics score between 3.5 and 5.0, labour statistics highest and balance of payments lowest. Agriculture, at 2.5, is the weakest category scored at all, against 4.0 for the built environment. The same assessment returns no score for six further categories, among them energy, crime and justice, and education outcomes.

No data category in that assessment scores above 5.0 out of 10.

No adoption of the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications' National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 has been found, and no open data policy has been found. Geospatial information is collected institution by institution with no coordinated national spatial data infrastructure (August 2026). The rules of the national instant payment system sit in agreements between the central bank, the switch operator and the mobile money operators, not in a published rulebook (2025). The electronic procurement platform releases no award, tender or contract-value data (August 2026). The digital beneficial ownership register under the Liberia Business Registry (2023) is the exception.

The Independent Information Commission set up under the Freedom of Information Act 2010 also takes the data-protection mandate. The data-protection Act has been in force since March 2026. The Press Union of Liberia has challenged a new Civil Service Agency staff confidentiality agreement as a reintroduction of secrecy in government (August 2026). The commission's October 2026 survey in Grand Gedeh County found 87.6% of 1,875 respondents had never heard of the 2010 Act and 85.3% did not know how to file a request. The press union cited a commission audit in October 2026 finding that 78 of 103 assessed public bodies had not appointed the information officer the Act requires.

Geospatial data

On 24 October 2025 the Meteorological Service installed a CLIMSA/PUMA2025 Meteosat Third Generation receiving station at Roberts International Airport. It was EU-funded and fitted under African Union Commission contract, and receives model data and high-resolution imagery. The national contribution plan records no compliant surface stations, one staffed station and no transmission to the global system (2024). An earlier reception workstation was underused for want of power and connectivity.

Liberia's second forest reference level, submitted to the UNFCCC in January 2026, samples 5,990 plots for 2020-2024. It reads Sentinel-2, Landsat, MODIS and Planet imagery, with cropland as an explicit class. In January 2026 the agriculture regulator's traceability plan for cocoa, coffee, rubber and palm oil set out farm-level geolocation for the EU deforestation regulation. It has a 2020 forest-cover map and a 2000-2024 change analysis, with at least US$530,000 committed. A nationwide soil mapping campaign began in Bong County in May 2026, aiming at national soil maps and a digital soil information centre.

The Land Authority is digitising Monrovia's hand-drawn cadastral maps and has signed for a land information system (2025). No national space agency or space data policy had been found as at August 2026.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

On 2 April 2026 Public Citizen sued the US State Department in the District of Columbia over sixteen countries' health agreements, Liberia's among them. The suit seeks the memoranda and their appendices, including any data-sharing, specimen and financing agreements. The State Department briefly published some of these memoranda in March 2026 and then removed them. It refused the requester's fee waiver on 30 December 2025 and had made no final determination by 2 April 2026.

The template memorandum on which Liberia's agreement of 9 December 2025 was built is public only as a copy released by Public Citizen. So is the model data sharing agreement for the America First Global Health Strategy, with the country name left blank. The department says these agreements usually pair a memorandum with at least one further agreement on data sharing. The Liberian memorandum states that it is not an international agreement and creates no legal rights or obligations.

USAID's health systems activity, which built the national health management information system, closed in 2022. The Connectivity for Liberia activity and the Digital Invest placement have also closed, the last in 2024. Support for medical records, laboratory systems, disease surveillance and the DHIS2 warehouse is now planned under the December 2025 memorandum.

China activities

No Chinese-financed digital infrastructure, network-equipment supply or bilateral digital agreement was on the record for Liberia as at August 2026.

EU activities

In September 2025 the European Commission proposed terminating its FLEGT voluntary partnership agreement with Liberia after a decade without operative timber licensing. In June 2015 a joint committee under the agreement adopted a rollout plan for a timber legality database, with milestones to 2018. In September 2016 the fourth committee recorded that some of the work was not on track. In April 2017 the fifth put licensing at 2020.

The Meteosat Third Generation station installed at Roberts International Airport in October 2025 was EU-funded under ClimSA.

In July 2026 the National Port Authority signed a smart-ports memorandum with the French operator HAROPA. The national identity rollout is planned as a concession under which Austria's OSD finances enrolment and later transfers the system to government. No adequacy finding or GDPR-alignment arrangement for Liberia had been found as at August 2026.

Gulf/UAE activities

India activities

Digital sovereignty

In March 2026 the Nairobi firm that built Liberia's identity system withheld full database access from the registry over an unpaid balance. The concession signed with Austria's OeSD International to replace it awaited ratification in September 2026. On 28 October 2026 a House committee halted its review because the agreement lacked two signatures. The registry's executive director says identity data remains a national asset under Liberian control. He says the concession carries training and technology transfer so the state can run the system itself. The concession's terms on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at October 2026, nor had those of the data-sharing agreement under the US health memorandum.

The telecoms regulator's cybersecurity regulations of January 2026 require providers to store personal data primarily inside Liberia. They also require an in-house cybersecurity unit in the country. The Central Bank sets a separate hosting rule for payment systems. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications' National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 would keep public-sector data subject to Liberian law wherever it is stored. It would host the identity registry's and revenue authority's data in the National Data Center and in localised cloud such as a Tier III+ facility in Buchanan. No instrument fixing where state data is held had been found in force as at October 2026.

Under a September 2025 directive the Central Bank operates the instant payment system it regulates, holding the settlement accounts. The system runs on the open-source Mojaloop platform.
