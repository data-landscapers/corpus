Summary of position

Liberia's digital programme is being built by executive order and financed from outside, and the register at its centre spent much of the last year unable to issue a card. Enrolment under the mandatory biometric scheme stood at about 740,000 people, under 15% of about 5.3m, at a US$5 fee, and issuance halted in March 2026 when the system's builder withheld database access over a US$1.7m unpaid balance. The fix is a concession: an Austrian firm is to finance enrolment of the whole population in 18 months, operate the system and transfer it, on terms not published and not yet ratified.

No data-protection statute governs any of it. The Personal Information Act has been before the legislature since June 2025, and there is no data-protection authority.

What did get built is payments. The central bank's interoperable instant payment system launched on 16 December 2025 in 73 business days, and the two mobile-money networks are no longer separate.

International capacity rests on one cable and two unbuilt alternatives: a second landing at Buchanan, with partial funding stated as sourced and investors still being sought, and a regional agreement of June 2026 to commercialise spare fibre on the TRANSCO CLSG grid, with no operator, tariff or date attached. Domestic extension is reported rather than measured — the backbone has reached Loguatuo, with private firms extending it toward the south-east — with no kilometre figure or completion date published. A national data centre and 5G service are stated plans without site, cost or timetable, and the ministry's data governance policy records a Tier III+ facility under development at Buchanan for sovereign hosting of the national identity register and revenue authority data.

Starcell's licence and spectrum were revoked with immediate effect on 22 July 2026 after six years without operating and a 90-day suspension, leaving the duopoly in place. The telecoms traffic monitoring concession is suspended and under a corruption complaint at the Anti-Corruption Commission, with a joint legislative committee recommending renegotiation rather than cancellation and operator funds in escrow of undisclosed amount, on which no evidence has been produced and no findings made.

The ministry's National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025-2029, cover-dated July 2024 sets dated deliverables, among them a National Cybersecurity Council by Q4 2024 and a primary National Cybersecurity Act by Q3 2025; neither is held, and the published file's running headers read as a draft rather than an adopted text. The Cybercrime Act 2025 was invoked when the House ordered an investigation into an alleged breach of about 2m citizens' data across the revenue, electricity and insurance bodies in February 2026, an order that also recorded the absence of a national cybersecurity strategy, while the ministry's own data governance policy of March 2026 records the Act as adopted by the Senate in November 2025 but not yet in force. The two accounts have not been reconciled, and no findings have been published.

Unlike the Act, a regulator-made instrument is already in force: the LTA's Cybersecurity Regulations (LTA-REG-0013-2026), made 13 January 2026, require providers to store personal data primarily inside Liberia, stand up a Cybersecurity Unit within a year, notify the LTA of breaches within 24 hours and customers within 72, and file tested incident-response plans; penalties are revenue-linked but apply only from a year after the effective date. The regulator publishes it only as an image-only scan, and the s.10.6 penalty ladder is unreadable in either copy held.

The identity system is the country's central digital dependency and its least settled. Enrolment is mandatory under Executive Order 147, which makes the identification number the primary credential for banking, health, education and telecoms, and SIM registration was tied to it by a regulator-registry memorandum with a July 2025 deadline; no compliance figure has been published since. The register itself is to pass to a concessionaire: OSD International is to finance enrolment of all about 5.3m people in 18 months, operate the system and transfer it back, a structure a Liberian Observer editorial describes as outsourcing the digital spine of the state. Selection and terms are not published and legislative ratification is outstanding. The enrolment push is funded by the US$30m World Bank GREAT project.

Payments are the layer that works. The instant payment system runs across both mobile-money networks, on a published fee schedule of 1.0% of value up to US$2,000 and a flat US$25 above it, which is regressive above about US$2,500. A national electronic payment switch is provided for and undated.

Government systems are being coordinated from the centre for the first time. Executive Order 163 created a whole-of-government office for standards, interoperability, technical clearance of ICT investments and baseline cybersecurity, with 12 months to draft an institutionalising law; the interoperability framework it provides for is not held. Two platforms are in service: e-procurement, with more than 1,200 vendors registered and more than US$400,000 in revenue generated but no contract value, tender count or bidder numbers published, and the Civil Service Agency's fingerprint-verified purchasing platform, live since December 2025 with 5,000 enrolled across 16 entities.

The Personal Information Act reached a House committee in June 2025 and is unpassed; Liberia is an ECOWAS data-protection signatory with no national data authority. The ministry's National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 designates the Independent Information Commission as data regulator rather than creating a new body, a mandate that takes effect only under the unenacted Act, and prescribes ratification of the Malabo Convention and an adequacy-or-explicit-consent rule for personal data leaving the country. The only privacy penalty on record came through telecom regulation: Orange Liberia was fined LRD 4m in June 2026 for issuing a subscriber's SIM to a third party.

The regulator set out a split of infrastructure and service licences, tower-sharing rules, the country's first satellite regulations, an IMEI regime and repatriation of the .lr domain in July 2026; none of it is published as an instrument.

The ministry's Digital Liberia strategy for 2025-2029 sets eight priority areas across four pillars with no costing or delivery schedule. The African Development Bank's country strategy for 2026-2031 cut its sectors from four to three and carries no digital lending line.

The regulator's chairman declined to disclose the Authority's revenue, saying he reports to the President, while the Legislature, the General Auditing Commission and the Anti-Corruption Commission hold statutory claims on it. Liberia recovered its vote at the African Telecommunications Union in July 2026 when US$361,151.36 of arrears dating to 1982 were waived.

Artificial intelligence entered the national agenda from outside government. The President's Youth Advisory Council put it to him at a town hall and he accepted it in principle, conditioned on applications in agriculture, transport and commerce, with no policy, institution, budget line or date attached. The first working system is private: a law library's CJ Lewis chatbot over Supreme Court opinions from 1861 to 2026, launched on 31 July 2026 with OHCHR support, on a corpus that is a private compilation rather than an official publication of Liberian law, and with no accuracy figure, model or vendor named.

The domestic technology sector is thin and partner-supported. UNDP runs the UNIPOD innovation hub under its Timbuktoo platform with no commitment amount disclosed, and the national startup competition was won by a Monrovia cybersecurity firm pitching domestic data custody, whose projections and skills findings are its own. The only industrial application on the ledger is a non-exclusive smart-ports memorandum with HAROPA PORT covering all four seaports, with no amount, date or named system.

The World Bank signed US$125m of agreements in June 2026, of which US$50m is for broadband, cybersecurity, digital governance and digital payments, detailed in Finance below. Domestically, the Youth Entrepreneurship Investment Bank is put at almost US$18m, with a coordinator recruited and a board being constituted and no disbursement stated.

The base is narrow: DataReportal put internet users at 1.84m, 32.4% of the population, with about 3.8m offline in January 2025, with no later national figure published; financial access is wider, at 52% account ownership and more than 11m wallets.

Capacity work is donor-run at every level: schools connected under a programme reported at about 90 of 156 sites with no funder or completion date; the Ministry of Education's digital learning platform and radio service for Grades 1 to 3, launched 6 January 2026; UNDP's announced master's programme in artificial intelligence with the University of Liberia and its first of three media and information literacy sessions for 19 young leaders on 31 July 2026 on Canadian funding, with no budget disclosed.

External finance is the largest channel funding digital and health-data systems. The World Bank's US$125m of agreements signed at Mount Coffee on 8 June 2026 include US$50m for WARDIP2's broadband, cybersecurity, digital governance and digital payments components. The United States' America First Global Health Strategy memorandum, signed 9 December 2025, plans US$26,133,684 of the wider US$124,418,400 in US support for Liberia's health data systems — electronic medical records, a laboratory information system, disease surveillance and DHIS2 — against US$50,725,704 of Liberian financing. The signed text commits Liberia to an open-source electronic medical record reaching 100% of public facilities by 2030 and gives the United States audit access to up to 5% of health facilities; a data-sharing arrangement is still to be negotiated, expected to run ten years, but the memorandum carries no specimen-sharing or personal-data clause. Its text was published in March 2026, then withdrawn, and is the subject of a federal freedom-of-information suit filed 2 April 2026. Domestically, the Youth Entrepreneurship Investment Bank is put at almost US$18m, with a coordinator recruited and a board being constituted; no disbursement is stated.
