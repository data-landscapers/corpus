Liberia's Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection confirmed on 13 September 2026 that beneficiary records from its Save the Future Project circulating on social media are authentic and were never authorised for release, and opened an internal investigation. The records concern children removed from street situations; the ministry rejected claims they list survivors of gender-based violence and said it had shared collated data with funding partners.

Liberia: LTA Embarrasses Boakai — allAfrica.com (The New Republic Liberia), 3 September 2026

The New Republic reported on 3 September 2026 that Liberia's President Joseph Boakai summoned the Telecommunications Authority's acting chairperson, Clarence Massaquoi, to the Executive Mansion over undisclosed revenue activities. Ten CSOs Form Digital Rights Coalition — Liberian Observer, 24 September 2026

Ten Liberian civil society organisations signed a memorandum founding the Digital Advocacy and Rights Alliance. Liberia: Ten Liberian Civil Society Groups Launch Digital Advocacy and Rights Alliance — FrontPage Africa, 25 September 2026

Ten Liberian civil society organisations, including CENTAL and the Liberia Equality Network, signed a memorandum establishing the Digital Advocacy and Rights Alliance (DARA). The coalition is to work on digital rights, privacy, online safety and digital freedoms.

Cybersecurity

Cybercrime Act 2025

Liberia's Free-Speech Balancing Act and What the Cybercrime Law Actually Changed — FrontPageAfrica, 11 September 2026

A FrontPageAfrica commentary compares Liberia's Cybercrime Act of 2025, whose printed text is marked approved on 3 July 2026, with the 2019 Kamara Abdullah Kamara Act that decriminalised sedition and criminal libel of the President. It sets out the Act's speech-related offences, including cyberstalking and harmful data messages, and a judge-ordered interception power. It also notes a pending amendment, introduced in March 2026, to criminalise insulting language online.

Liberia's New Cybercrime Law: The New Digital Police Power and the Fight Over Free Speech — Smart News Liberia, 19 September 2026

Liberia's Cybercrime Act of 2025 has been published as approved law, and its first public test is a warning by the Executive Protection Service director, Sam Gaye, that makers of manipulated material meant to insult or harass could be prosecuted. It followed an arrest over a doctored video of Vice-President Jeremiah Kpan Koung. The article weighs this against the constitution's free-expression guarantee.

Koung Seeks Release of AI Image Suspect — The New Dawn Liberia, 21 September 2026

Liberia's vice-president Jeremiah Koung asked for the release of Jerry Nyantee Wrotto, whom police detained under the Cybercrime Act over AI-made images and videos of Koung, and said he would not press charges. It is one of the first high-profile uses of the Act, signed on 19 August 2026 with the Data Protection Act. The head of the Executive Protection Service defended the arrest, while critics including former finance minister Samuel Tweah said the Act's cyberbullying clauses cover protected speech.

Liberia's Cybercrime Law Faces Its First Real Trial as VP Koung case exposes Liberia's widening fault lines — FrontPage Africa, 21 September 2026

A second account of the Wrotto arrest, summarised under the New Dawn report. It adds a justice ministry prosecutor's view that the state, not the vice-president, decides whether a prosecution goes ahead, and notes that the Act does not mention AI or deepfakes.

Liberia: CDC Prepares to Take Cybercrime Law Fight to Supreme Court After Wrato Release — FrontPage Africa, 23 September 2026

Liberia's opposition CDC says it will ask the Supreme Court to rule on the constitutionality of the Cybercrime Act of 2025. The move follows the court-ordered release on 22 September of commentator Jerry Nyantee Wrato, whom police intended to prosecute under the Act over AI-generated images of the Vice President.

Liberia: Judge Gives Police 'Last Opportunity' in Cybercrime Suspect's Detention Case — FrontPage Africa, 25 September 2026

A Monrovia criminal court gave the police a last opportunity to justify holding Jerry Nyantee Wrato, a suspect under the Cybercrime Act, after no officer appeared for the second time. The judge found the state had not shown the case was lawfully before the court and postponed the habeas corpus hearing to 25 September.

Senator Dopoh Accuses Executive of Prematurely Enforcing Cybercrime Law, Demands Key Safeguards — The Liberian Investigator, 16 October 2026

Liberian Senator Francis Dopoh, chair of the Senate Committee on Posts and Telecommunications, told the Senate on 15 October 2026 that the Executive is enforcing the new Cybercrime Law before setting up the Cybersecurity Council and specialised cybercrime team it requires. He asked the Senate to notify the Executive and demand corrective measures, following the reported arrest of a man accused of posting AI-generated images of the Vice President.

Digital Identity and CRVS

National identification system concession

Liberia: NIR: US$54.7M Digital ID Deal Awaits Legislative Approval — FrontPage Africa, 8 September 2026

Liberia's US$54.7 million concession with Austria's OeSD International to rebuild the national identification system has been signed but cannot be implemented until the legislature ratifies it, National Identification Registry director Andrew Peters said. The project covers biometric enrolment and deduplication, digital and physical credentials and a digital identity platform. No signature date is given.

Liberia to link national ID, CRVS in planned infrastructure upgrade — Biometric Update, 14 September 2026

Liberia's identification registry plans one system linking national ID with civil registration and vital statistics under its proposed US$54 million partnership with Austria's OSD. The signed agreement still needs parliamentary approval, and ID issuance has been suspended since June 2025.

Liberia: Free National ID Cards Only for Disadvantaged Liberians - Says National Identification Registry — allAfrica.com, 17 September 2026

Liberia's National Identification Registry said on 15 September 2026 that free national ID cards will go only to disadvantaged and unemployed citizens, with employed Liberians and foreign residents paying a fee. It is seeking about US$54.7m for the programme, which starts once an agreement before the legislature is ratified. Its director said the financier, OSD, will invest in ICT infrastructure while the registry keeps custody of personal data.

NIR Seeks Legislative Approval for US$54.7M Identity Deal - Liberia news The New Dawn Liberia, premier resource for latest news — Liberia news The New Dawn Liberia, premier resource for latest news, 17 September 2026

Liberia's National Identification Registry asked the legislature on 16 September 2026 to speed ratification of a US$54.7 million identity deal with OeSD International. Under the build, operate and transfer arrangement OeSD would fund biometric enrolment and infrastructure, run it to recover the investment, then hand it to government. No signature date is given.

House Begins Review of Boakai’s Proposed National Digital ID Overhaul — Liberian Observer, 16 October 2026

Liberia's House of Representatives referred President Boakai's proposed concession with OeSD International for a combined national ID and civil registration system to a joint committee, with 14 days to report; the referral is not ratification. Under the build-operate-transfer deal, OeSD would build and run a system issuing biometric ID numbers and cards and recording births, deaths and marriages, then hand it to the government.

House Scrutinizes US$52M National ID Deal — The New Dawn, 16 October 2026

Liberia's House of Representatives has sent a proposed US$52 million concession with OeSD International for a national ID and digital civil registry to a joint committee, which has two weeks to report. This account dates the President's communication to plenary on 15 October 2026 and says the build-operate-transfer system would hold demographic and biometric data on up to 10 million people.

Liberia: Naymote Urges Legislature to Tighten US$52M National ID Deal, Cites Cost, Electoral and Data Protection Concerns — FrontPage Africa, 16 October 2026

Liberian civil society group Naymote, in a statement of 15 October 2026, urged the Legislature to tighten the proposed national ID agreement, which it values at US$52 million. It asks for full disclosure of financing, fees and contract terms, an independent value-for-money review, government ownership of identity data, free first-time enrolment and public hearings, and says the electoral commission must keep control of the voters' roll.

Liberian Senate Orders Review of US$52 Million Digital Identity Concession Agreement - Liberia Public Radio — Liberia Public Radio, 16 October 2026

Liberia's Senate sent President Boakai's proposed US$52 million build-operate-transfer concession with Germany's OeSD International for a national ID, civil registry and vital statistics system (NIDCRVS) to three committees. They are to report to plenary within two weeks, before any vote on ratification.

Liberian MPs begin scrutinizing PPP concession deal for new national ID — Biometric Update, 19 October 2026

Liberia's House of Representatives referred a proposed $52 million build-operate-transfer concession for a national ID and civil registration system to a joint committee on 16 October 2026, giving it two weeks to report. The system would hold records for up to 10 million people, and civil society groups are asking for scrutiny of the financial terms and data protection. The National Identification Registry says fewer than 800,000 people hold a national ID.

National identity enrolment

Liberia: President Boakai Declares Wednesday, September 16, as “National Identification Day — APO Group - Africa Newsroom, 16 September 2026

Liberia's President Joseph Nyuma Boakai declared 16 September 2026 National Identification Day, a working holiday, by proclamation. Multinational - Government Payments (P2G & G2P) Digitization in Mano River Union - Liberia - Project Completion Report — African Development Bank Group, 27 September 2026

The African Development Bank published on 27 September 2026 the completion report for DIGIGOV Liberia, part of its project to digitise government payments in the Mano River Union. Pay Na-Na inclusive instant payment system

Liberia’s Instant Payment System Crosses 5.3M Transactions — Liberian Observer, 1 October 2026

Liberia's instant payment system has processed more than 5.3 million transactions between the two mobile money operators since its December 2025 launch, the central bank governor said. About 4.2 million were in Liberian dollars and 1.1 million in US dollars.

Registries

Enhanced collateral registry

Liberia: Financial Inclusion Must Not Become Debt Inclusion — allAfrica.com (Liberian Observer), 1 September 2026

A Liberian Observer editorial argues that Liberia's newly launched Enhanced Collateral Registry, which lets borrowers pledge inventory, equipment and livestock, needs clear loan terms and real-time credit reporting alongside it. Collateral registry

Liberia's New Collateral Registry Opens Credit Doors for SMEs, Tightens Lending Controls — FrontPageAfrica, 4 September 2026

The Central Bank of Liberia said registration in its enhanced collateral registry is now mandatory for financial institutions, at a training for about 35 loan and credit officers. The registry covers movable and immovable assets, allows remote public searches for prior liens, and is designed to link to the national ID registry, the land registry and the traffic system.

Private credit reference bureau licensing under regulation CBL/SD/003/2010

Liberia: Prime Credit Sues Central Bank, Demands Nearly US$1.2M Over Credit Bureau Dispute — FrontPage Africa, 16 September 2026

Prime Credit Reference Bureau is suing the Central Bank of Liberia for US$1,180,953, alleging the bank never supplied or ordered lenders to supply the loan data its November 2025 licence depended on. It says the bank later wrote that it lacked clear authority to license private bureaux; the claims are untested.

Revenue authority AI-powered contact centre

Transforming Taxpayer Services: LRA Advances AI-Powered Contact Center — Liberia Revenue Authority, 10 September 2026

The Liberia Revenue Authority said on 10 September 2026 that it is developing an AI-powered contact center for taxpayer information on tax and customs matters. AI retinal screening camera at JFK Medical Center

Real-world validation of an AI-based non-mydriatic fundus camera in the diagnosis of diabetic retinopathy in Liberia — Frontiers in Digital Health, 24 September 2026

A prospective study at John F. Kennedy Medical Center in Monrovia tested an offline AI system on a smartphone fundus camera for referable diabetic retinopathy in general outpatients. Among 1,198 participants with a gradable eye it caught 10 of 11 referable cases (sensitivity 90.9%) and cleared 1,183 of 1,187 negatives (specificity 99.7%), though the small number of positives leaves a wide confidence interval on sensitivity.

Civil Service Agency confidentiality and non-disclosure agreement for civil servants

PUL Condemns Government’s Assault on Freedom of Information and Transparency; Calls for Immediate Withdrawal of Confidentiality Policy Undermining the FOI Act - Liberia Public Radio — Liberia Public Radio, 24 October 2026

At a Governance Commission policy dialogue, the Press Union of Liberia called for the immediate withdrawal of a confidentiality and non-disclosure agreement the Civil Service Agency has introduced for civil servants, saying it overturns the Freedom of Information Act and strips the Independent Information Commission, which opposed it, of its authority. Freedom of Information Act awareness, Grand Gedeh survey

A survey of 1,875 people in Grand Gedeh County on 14-17 October 2026 found that 87.6% had never heard of the Freedom of Information Act of 2010. Some 85.3% did not know how to file a request. Some 77.6% said they use the internet regularly. The Independent Information Commission recommended a feasibility assessment for a regional freedom of information hub in the county.
