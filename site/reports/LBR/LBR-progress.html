Liberia: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-05  ·  136 systems and instruments tracked
↓ PDF
Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-05.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy Digital Liberia 2025-2029 was published in February 2026 on eight priority areas across four pillars, with no costing or delivery schedule.
Full recordThe pillars include universal connectivity, digital addressing, cybersecurity and data sovereignty, replacing an ICT policy already past its stated period (strategy, text).

A development bank's country strategy approved the same month still cites the superseded 2019-2024 ICT policy and does not mention the new strategy at all (country strategy).
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy A licensing and spectrum reform package was announced in July 2026, splitting infrastructure and service licences and promising first satellite rules.
Full recordIt also carries tower-sharing rules, a device-identity regime and repatriation of the country-code domain; none has been published as an instrument (reform package).

Licensing has otherwise run unchanged under a 2007 statute with no satellite or device rules, so the package would be the first structural change to the market's licensing in nineteen years.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy No separately titled broadband strategy; the January 2026 infrastructure roadmap commits the ministry and the regulator to one in years 2-4.
Full recordThe broadband and universal-access targets sat in the National ICT Policy 2019-2024, whose stated period expired in 2024 and which the base holds no successor to. The 2026-2029 roadmap adopted after the national infrastructure conference of 19-23 January 2026 places a national broadband expansion plan in years 2-4, which is a commitment to draft rather than a plan.

The base holds no costing, no coverage target and no delivery schedule for that plan.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A cloud-first policy and a government network are named in the 2026-2029 infrastructure roadmap, with no instrument or timetable behind them.
Full recordThe infrastructure roadmap adopted in 2026 names a cloud-first policy and a government network among its digital commitments. Nothing published sets out what would be hosted where, under whose standards, or by when.

The base holds no government cloud policy, no hosting standard and no migration plan; the nearest binding rule is sectoral, the central bank's requirement that payment systems be hosted locally.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap Executive Order 163 requires a first release of a Government Interoperability Framework within 180 days; none is published.
Full recordExecutive Order 163, signed 22 April 2026, creates the Office of Technology, Digitalization and Innovation as the central authority for government-wide technical standards and sets a 180-day deadline for a first release of the framework, alongside an enterprise architecture framework and a digital systems inventory.

The terms of reference for the office's chief technology officer, published in July 2026, assign the duty to develop and maintain the framework with its data-exchange standards. Neither the framework nor a draft of it has been published.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy An artificial intelligence priority was accepted in principle by the President in July 2026, conditioned on agriculture, transport and commerce applications.
Full recordNo policy, institution, budget line or date is attached, and the proposal came from the President's youth advisory council rather than from the executive (acceptance).

It is the only statement of national intent on the technology the base holds, and it originated outside government.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies Section 9(c) of the 2020 payment regulations is the only localisation rule, and it carries an offshore escape clause.
Full recordThe regulations gazetted 8 January 2020 state that a licensee's payment system should be hosted locally, and that where it is hosted abroad the licensee must supply requested information promptly and give the central bank unfettered access.

That is a sectoral norm with an escape, not an economy-wide regime. The base holds no general data-localisation instrument, and the National Data Policy that might carry one was still in multi-stakeholder drafting in November 2025.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy A national data governance policy issued in March 2026 designates the information commission as data regulator and sets an adequacy-or-consent rule for transfers.
Full recordIt prescribes signature and ratification of the continental cyber and data convention, and was developed under ministry leadership with a regional civil-society body and aligned to the continental data policy framework (policy).

It designates a regulator under a statute that was unenacted when the policy issued, so the policy named an authority for a law that did not yet exist; the law has since been signed and the authority still has not been constituted.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy No adopted open-data policy; the statistics office publishes public-use microdata under access conditions instead.
Full recordThe anonymised public-use dataset for the 2024 women's empowerment and nutrition survey was released on the statistics office's own microdata portal under stated access conditions, with the confidentiality basis cited as the 2004 statistics Act rather than an open-data instrument.

That is open-data practice in the absence of a policy. The base holds no adopted national open data policy and no government open-data licence.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation The Personal Information Act was signed into force on 19 August 2026, with no authority yet existing to administer it.
Full recordIt had been referred to a House committee in June 2025 and left unpassed, drafted with a media-development organisation under a European programme and validated in December 2024 (signature, committee stage).

The base holds no authority, commencement order, registration regime or enforcement route under it, so what a data subject can actually do with it is unestablished.

The country is a regional data-protection signatory and had until this month struggled to enforce privacy law at all (enforcement position).
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation The Cybercrime Act was signed on 19 August 2026, seven months after passage, alongside sector cybersecurity regulations already in force.
Full recordThe signature ends a delay carried since January 2026, when Senate concurrence was communicated to the House; no commencement date, implementing regulation or enforcement body is published with it, and an international index still places the country in its second-lowest maturity tier (enactment).

The regulator's own 2026 regulations require telecom providers to store personal data primarily inside the country, establish an in-house cybersecurity unit within a year, notify the regulator of breaches within 24 hours and customers within 72, run annual penetration testing and keep security-event logs for at least twelve months; penalties are revenue-linked but apply only from a year after the effective date, and the penalty escalation ladder is unreadable in both held copies (regulations).

A National Cybersecurity Act scheduled for the third quarter of 2025 by the strategy's own action plan is not held as a draft, bill or enactment (action plan).
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The 2011 Act is the enacting statute for the biometric register and remains unamended.
Full recordThe National Identification Registry Act, approved 1 August 2011, repeals the earlier decree, creates the autonomous registry, and makes the National Biometric Identification System, the biometric card and the Social Security Number the primary government identity number.

What has moved since is executive rather than legislative: Executive Order 147 of April 2025 compels enrolment and verification, with data handling bound back to Part IX of the same Act. No amending statute is on the record.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation The mobile money regulations are in force and unamended, and the interoperable fee schedule of 2026 was approved under them.
Full recordThey are the central bank's standing mobile-money framework (regulations).

No payments statute, electronic transactions act or switch instrument sits above them, so a fee schedule for a national instant payment system rests on a regulation written before that system existed.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability Executive Order 147 compels inter-agency verification against the identity register; no statute enables data exchange generally.
Full recordExecutive Order 147 requires all public and private institutions to use the registry database, the Identity Query Portal and the e-Verification platform, which is the only operative instrument compelling data sharing across Liberian agencies.

It is an executive order resting on the 2011 Act, not a data-exchange statute. Executive Order 163 of April 2026 mandates a Government Interoperability Framework but is likewise executive.
 No change
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No artificial-intelligence instrument: a court reached alleged AI-generated audio through ordinary disclosure rules.
Full recordAt Criminal Court A on 19 December 2025 the defence played an alleged AI-generated recording to argue that social-media audio is inadmissible without forensic corroboration; the judge sustained the objection on disclosure grounds, applying no AI-specific rule because none exists in the reported reasoning.

The executive's own response to synthetic media is an appeal rather than a rule: in July 2026 the presidential press secretary warned of fabricated and AI-generated documents attributed to government and asked citizens to authenticate through official channels.
 No change
Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation The 2002 Electronic Transactions Law is the only enacted e-commerce statute and predates platforms entirely.
Full recordChapter 13 of Title 14, approved 8 January 2002 and published 19 June 2002, facilitates the use of electronic transactions for commercial purposes.

Nothing covering online consumer protection, platform obligations or intermediary liability has been enacted in the twenty-four years since. The base holds no e-commerce bill in drafting.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The 2004 Act stands unamended, and was tested in the House in June 2026.
Full recordThe National Statistics and Geo-Information Act, approved 22 July 2004, creates the statistics office as an autonomous agency, empowers it to demand information from any ministry, bureau or agency, and binds its staff to an oath of confidentiality.

No amendment moved in the window. What did move is a test of the statutory role: in June 2026 the House summoned the director general and his statisticians to justify the population benchmarks behind a proposed expansion of the chamber from 73 to 87 seats.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The telecommunications regulator continues to refuse to publish what the gateway monitoring system earns (report).
Full recordThe telecommunications regulator's board chairman again declined to say what the international gateway monitoring system has earned, and the dispute has acquired a legal edge (report).

Data protection Data protection authority The telecoms regulator fined an operator LRD 4m for issuing a subscriber's module to a third party, the first privacy penalty on the record.
Full recordIt was imposed by the telecoms regulator rather than under data-protection law, which had none in force at the time (fine).

The reform package announced the same month does not name a data-protection function (package), and no data-protection authority exists to which such a case could now be brought.

Data protection National data protection readiness A riders' union memorandum would build digital identity, satellite tracking and a rider database for commercial motorcycle operators, with no data-protection basis reported.
Full recordIt was signed in August 2026 between the union and a private digital services company, aimed at curbing vehicle theft and linking informal-sector riders to formal financial and government services (memorandum).

It is a private arrangement covering location tracking of a working population: no custodian, retention rule or consent mechanism is reported, and no statutory backing is claimed. It was signed three days before the country's first data-protection statute took effect.

Full recordCurrent obligations were arranged and the ratification instrument deposited (waiver).

Separately the central bank governor proposed shared cybersecurity standards, cross-border incident reporting and supervisory-technology adoption among West African regulators, citing the country's own instant-payment launch as a benchmark; nothing is adopted, funded or scheduled, and the proposal is one governor's rather than the college's position (proposal).

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation The Standards Authority Act grounds the apex standards body expressly in ECOWAS quality-infrastructure instruments.
Full recordThe 2022 Act creating the Liberia Standards Authority cites the ECOWAS quality infrastructure — ECOQUAL, ECOSHAM and Regulation C/REG.19/12/13 — and World Trade Organization technical-barriers obligations as its grounding, which is regional legal harmonisation done through domestic enactment.

The February 2026 entry into the ECOWAS free roaming agreement with Ghana and Sierra Leone is the other harmonisation step the base holds, with the regulator stressing that full implementation still had to follow.

Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure Free roaming with two neighbours has run since May 2025, and spare fibre on a four-country power grid is agreed for commercialisation.
Full recordThe roaming arrangement puts a home module at local rates across the three countries, with no traffic or price data published (roaming).

Regional consensus was reached in June 2026 to commercialise spare fibre on the shared transmission grid across four countries, which would give the country its first terrestrial redundancy against sole reliance on one submarine landing; no operator, tariff or date is published (grid fibre).
 Advanced
Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers A rights organisation's reading puts the country among three committing to hand over any data requested for an American audit without explicit privacy safeguards.
Full recordIt is also among two whose data-sharing arrangement is deferred to an unpublished appendix, and outside the five whose text requires unilateral specimen and pathogen-sequencing sharing; the signed memorandum carries no specimen clause (assessment).

The same reading names the country as lacking a binding comprehensive data-protection law, which is what makes the audit-access provision acute. That law has since been signed, and the appendix is still unpublished.

Standards National interoperability standards Executive Order 163 creates a central standards authority and requires an interoperability framework within 180 days.
Full recordBefore April 2026 no body owned government-wide technical standards. Executive Order 163 creates the Office of Technology, Digitalization and Innovation as that authority and requires a first release of the Government Interoperability Framework within 180 days, with an enterprise architecture framework and a digital systems inventory alongside.

The chief technology officer's terms of reference assign the drafting duty. Nothing has been published, so the standards themselves remain prospective.

Standards National quality standards The Liberia Standards Authority has been the apex national standards body since its enacting Act of August 2022.
Full recordThe Act signed 5 August 2022 creates the authority as the apex national standards body, grounded in the ECOWAS quality infrastructure and World Trade Organization technical-barriers obligations.

The base holds no standards catalogue, no accreditation record and no conformity-assessment output from the authority since. The capture of the Act itself is an excerpt, the source scan having yielded only the preamble and enacting clause.

Standards Adoption of international standards ePhyto went live in April 2026, adding to the numbering plan's adoption of ITU-T recommendations.
Full recordThe National Numbering Plan of December 2023 adopts ITU-T Recommendation E.164 for national number structure and E.212 for mobile country and network codes, giving an international standard domestic legal effect through the regulator.

In the window Liberia launched the IPPC and FAO ePhyto exchange on 20 April 2026 under the Regional ePhyto Africa Window, replacing paper phytosanitary certification with an internationally recognised digital certificate in support of the World Trade Organization trade facilitation agreement.

Public debate and participation in policymaking Non-governmental contribution to national policy The 2007 Act's consultation duty is binding and was exercised twice in the window.
Full recordSections 7(1) and 11(4) of the Telecommunications Act 2007 require the ministry and the regulator to consult publicly and take account of the results before adopting policy or exercising authority likely to have substantial sector impact — the clearest binding consultation duty the base holds.

It was exercised on satellite communications guidelines from 23 February 2026, with a public forum, a comment deadline and a published consultation report. Outside the telecommunications duty, a two-day consultation in November 2025 brought civil society, academia, the private sector and the media into drafting the National Data Policy.

Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy The telecoms regulator's own revenue is unpublished, a corruption complaint against it is under investigation, and the police answered a procurement question with a denial.
Full recordIts licensing fees, spectrum charges, regulatory levies and numbering receipts are not published, nor is an audited account of them (revenue secrecy, alarm).

A complaint over the replacement traffic-monitoring arrangement was forwarded to the anti-corruption commission's investigation unit on 30 July 2026, with no evidence publicly produced and no findings made (complaint).

The national police stated in August 2026 that no facility uses satellite internet and that connectivity comes from the state operator under an institutional contract, and confirmed buying used vehicles from an American firm; no contract, value or procurement record is published alongside the denial (statement).

Domestic budget appropriations and expenditure Sustainable domestic financing of digital transformation The Universal Access Fund levy of 0.5 per cent of licensee revenue remains the only standing domestic mechanism.
Full recordLTA-REG-0006 sets the licensee contribution at 0.5 per cent of annual gross revenues, allows extraordinary contributions including national-budget allocations, and requires annual legislative approval for disbursement and audited accounts. Its most recent published deployment is a satellite installation in Vahun District, Lofa County.

The wider fiscal space moved in the window rather than the digital allocation: the general services tax rose from 12 to 13 per cent from 1 May 2026 under the Tax Amendment Act, a prior action the Fund's third review records as closing a US$10m gap for the 2026 fiscal year. No digital line is identified inside it.
 No change
MoUs and other agreements Strategic relationships The Cybaston five-year framework is the standing vendor relationship; three further partnerships were signed in the window.
Full recordThe master service framework signed 23 September 2024 covers undersea capacity of up to 1 Tbps, a data centre for consolidated e-government services, e-visa, e-tax and e-tourism, a security operations centre and an incident response team, with handover to the ministry in September 2029.

Added since: a UNDP and operator memorandum of 5 November 2025 on digital skills, innovation hubs and inclusion beyond Monrovia, with no value stated; a five-year United States memorandum of 9 December 2025 committing US$124,418,400, with integrated digital health information systems named as one funded line; and Liberia's first Digital Village in Lofa County with Huawei, for which no signed instrument, value or duration is published.

Full recordSpectrum, numbering and regulatory privileges reverted to the regulator, the licensee having been suspended for 90 days from February 2026; the market at mid-2026 is estimated at 3.2m subscribers for the leading operator, 2.3m for the second and 14,000 for the state operator (revocation).

Full recordThe reading is a commercial compilation rather than a regulator or statistics-office release (penetration).

Eighteen months on, no regulator subscription series, household survey or statistics-office chapter has replaced it, so the country's connectivity is measured once a year by an outside compiler.

Connectivity Mobile affordability The regulator's own series puts a 5GB basket at 12.1 per cent of monthly income in 2025, down from 13.6 per cent in 2023.
Full recordThe sector series holds the 5GB monthly basket flat at US$8 across 2023, 2024 and 2025 while average monthly income rose from US$59 to US$63 to US$66, giving affordability ratios of 13.6, 12.7 and 12.1 per cent against a 2 per cent benchmark. The whole of the improvement is income, not price.

Tax works the other way: the April 2026 revenue notice raises the general services rate to 13 per cent from 1 May 2026 while holding telecommunications at 15 per cent. Roaming charges among Liberia, Ghana and Sierra Leone were removed in February 2026, with full implementation still to follow.

Connectivity National fibre backbone The domestic backbone is reported extending south-east from one coastal city toward five counties, with no route map or kilometre figure.
Full recordThe line is reported reaching a border town from the capital, with private firms extending it (extension, earlier account).

No completion date is published, and the base holds no national kilometre figure for the backbone at any date.

Connectivity International internet bandwidth Two routes to a second landing are open: one at Buchanan sought with partial funding, and an ECOWAS four-country cable approved at feasibility.
Full record2025-10-22 - member-state experts with their technical and financial partners reviewed and approved the feasibility study for an ECOWAS second submarine cable serving Cabo Verde, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea-Bissau at a workshop the ECOWAS Project Preparation and Development Unit convened in Monrovia; no route, cost, landing site or construction date is published.

2026-07-23 - the minister announced a second submarine landing at Buchanan, with partial funding stated as sourced and investors still being sought at a technology summit; no landing partner, cost or date is published. Neither record says whether the two are the same project.

The country has run on one landing in the capital, which is why the agreement to commercialise spare fibre on the regional power grid matters as much as the cable does: both would end sole reliance on a single route.

Connectivity Internet Exchange Points The exchange launched in 2015 is functional but still under-peered as at December 2025.
Full recordThe Liberia Internet Exchange Point was launched in Monrovia on 6 August 2015 by the African Union Commission with the ministry under the African Internet Exchange System project, which supplied equipment, technical support and capacity building. No separate licence or establishing regulation was located.

At the national peering forum in December 2025 the regulator's commissioner said the exchange remains functional but suffers governance problems and limited provider participation, with many providers still not peering, against upgraded equipment and a newly elected association leadership.

Full recordThe regulator frames the rules around national security and commercial transparency as space-based solutions become the route to territories terrestrial networks have not reached (market rules, guidelines).

Data Storage Local data centre capacity (all providers) The only published Liberian capacity figures are the cable landing station's own rack specifications.
Full recordThe consortium's colocation page offers climate-controlled racks at 2 to 10U at 1kW with a 2kW maximum, 24U at 3kW and 42U at 5kW at the Monrovia landing facility, with uninterruptible and generator power, air conditioning, fire suppression and cross-connect support.

Data Storage Local data centre capacity (national providers) A national data centre was announced in July 2026 to hold government data domestically, with no site, cost, timetable or operator named.

A separate facility at Buchanan is stated as under development for sovereign hosting of the identity registry and the revenue authority, again with no operator, cost or completion date published (Buchanan facility).

Data Storage Off-site backup capacity The central bank's 2026 statement undertakes minimum uptime, disaster-recovery and continuity standards for payment participants.
Full recordThe policy statement commits the bank to establishing minimum uptime, disaster recovery and business continuity standards for payment participants and to requiring regular cybersecurity and operational resilience testing, alongside prefunding and settlement risk controls. Full recordIts action plan dated deliverables from the fourth quarter of 2024; the cover reads July 2024 for a 2025-2029 period while every running header reads a draft for 2024-2029, and whether the posted file is the adopted final text is not established from the document (strategy).

Data Exchange Use of digital id in other systems A joint directive of February 2026 binds financial institutions to identity verification for non-resident customers.
Full recordDirective CBL/FIA/001/2025, signed jointly by the financial intelligence agency and the central bank on 16 February 2026 and effective immediately, requires additional customer due diligence for non-resident, non-Liberian customers, addressing a deficiency named in the 2023 regional mutual evaluation.

Digital Identity and CRVS Robustness of system Card issuance was suspended for more than six months while institutions continued to require the card.

Separately an operator opened a 4G tower in Karluway District, Maryland County, bringing mobile and internet service to Karloken City and Wutuken Township where coverage had been limited or absent; this is commercial expansion, not a fund project, and carries no coverage figure. Liberia's first Digital Village opened in Lofa County in May 2026 with connectivity and skills training, on no published instrument.

EU activities EU MoUs, engagements and commitments The Commission proposed terminating the EU-Liberia forest agreement in September 2025 after a decade without operative licensing.
Full recordThe voluntary partnership agreement signed in 2011 and in force from December 2013 is the only Liberia-specific EU treaty instrument, and its operative content is a system: the chain-of-custody information system and legality assurance database Liberia was to build for timber licensing and traceability.

The licensing target slipped across successive committee meetings — the fifth aide memoire of April 2017 records it as then targeted for 2020 — and in September 2025 the Commission proposed termination after a decade without an operative scheme.
