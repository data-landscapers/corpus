Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Civil registration is the deepest system in the base and the only one with coverage figures. 437 of 629 centres, 70%, are fully deployed on the civil-status software, regional spread running from 98% in one region to 42% in another, and 15.3m civil-status records are loaded in the live national register. The externally funded programme behind that work closed in June 2026, leaving a validated bill unadopted.

The state's own systems have been breached three times on the record — the tax authority in 2025, the identity directorate on 19 January 2026 and the public treasury in May 2026 — and no technical accounting has been published for any of them. Senegal has neither a national cybersecurity agency nor a national response team on the record, and the data-protection law is in force and unamended since 2008.

The technology strategy carries a stated FCFA 1,105bn envelope to 2034 with no breakdown, and no execution, outturn or audit figure exists for any digital line in any year. Nine of the rows below are Not held.

International capacity is the strongest position. The 2Africa landing station entered service in December 2025 with sixteen fibre pairs, 180 Tbps and carrier-neutral open access, and a teleport gateway with sixteen new antennas links a low-orbit constellation to terrestrial networks. Fixed broadband stands at 1.16m subscribers at end-2025, more than 640,000 of them fibre-to-the-home.

Distribution to households is where the position weakens. 3% of rural households have internet access against 4G reaching 97% of the population — a usage gap, not a coverage gap. The state's answer is a satellite kit programme: a five-year renewable authorisation with local-supervision and data-sovereignty clauses, and a tour distributing a first 200 of 1,000 gifted kits, the one-million-beneficiary target moved to end-2026, with no beneficiary count published.

Domestic hosting is a set of memoranda and one facility. The Diamniadio national data centre has operated since June 2021 with no capacity or utilisation figure held; a sovereign-cloud memorandum of October 2024 has nothing on record being built; and a Dakar data centre of 1.2 MW broke ground in January 2025 and has not been commissioned.

Security is the weakest. Beyond the three breaches, the standing score is 67.17 out of 100, tier three of four on the 2024 index edition, which predates the whole breach run. The state's own diagnosis puts the blockage in procurement, where three routes are being floated, including pooled purchasing.

Deployment of the civil-status software stands at 437 of 629 centres with 15.3m records loaded, against 20m acts digitised and indexed of a 30m universe, restated in the June 2026 closing report as 10m digitised and 20m indexed, unreconciled. The bill computerising civil-status management is drafted and validated but not adopted, its implementing decree unsigned. Card production, suspended by ransomware on 5 February 2026, resumed on 1 April; the directorate's communiqués assert integrity, never confidentiality.

A biometric audit of the public-service headcount serves payroll control, and a biometric register of artisanal gold miners carries no data-protection provision in its reported design.

The national interoperability platform, launched in April 2025 with a first phase targeted for end-2025, is still stated as an intention rather than a capability. A government enterprise architecture is being built above it: interim diagnostic results were presented and the inventory of state information systems launched at a workshop on 29 July 2026, with no scope or deadline stated.

The citizen single window is operational for passports, business creation, police-clearance and nationality certificates, and the tax authority withdrew the paper fiscal stamp on 20 July 2026, making the digital service the sole recognised channel with no assisted route stated. A state procurement platform has run since 15 October 2025, and the procurement regulator names its protection as a priority. The national digital addressing project was relaunched on 5 August 2026 to settle its governance, with the pilot targeted to complete within one year; no budget, implementing agency or pilot geography is stated.

The governing instrument is eighteen years old. Loi n° 2008-12 was promulgated in January 2008, in force from 2014, and no amending law has been found; it did not move through the February 2026 breach of the national identity directorate. A revising bill and its implementing decree are in a finalisation workshop, rewritten against the data and AI strategies, with no adoption date and no published text.

The policy layer above it is dense and largely unpublished. A national data strategy is cited as adopted with no text on file and no adoption date established; an AI strategy adopted in 2023 on six orientations is likewise not on file; and a national AI development plan has been instructed for deployment without being an adopted instrument. Whether it supersedes, implements or replaces the 2023 strategy is unestablished. The steering committee created in March 2026 to oversee digital transformation was still described as operational in the near future on 3 August.

One instrument narrows disclosure. Décret n° 2026-807, in force from 22 April 2026, makes contracts concerning critical information infrastructure an enumerated ground for defence classification, gatekept by a presidency-chaired commission with silence after fifteen days counting as refusal; its accompanying report states that administrations had been invoking the classification improperly. No open government data policy, portal or licence is held.

The AI policy layer is largely unpublished: the 2023 strategy is not on file, the development plan is an instruction, and the ethics committee is recorded as planned with no statute, membership or date. The technology strategy's Priority Programme No. 8 was presented in Tokyo as the Senegal Digital Factory and, within it, the Senegal AI Factory, with no launch date, budget, compute location or partner stated. Senegal is absent from the eight African states ranked in a 2026 AI readiness index. It is one of 29 founding states of an intergovernmental AI-governance body and one of six adopting non-binding Francophone West African AI governance guidelines.

The one working AI service in the base is a Wolof and French tutor reachable by ordinary phone call with no internet, its curriculum written by teachers and validated by school inspectors, hosted on servers in Senegal; no user, pricing or evaluation figure is given, and the education ministry's role is asserted by the vendor rather than confirmed.

A sovereign cloud is in testing, to host sensitive national data on Senegalese territory, with no contract, value, hosting-term or data-access terms reported. A June 2025 partnership covering data-centre modernisation, an equipment assembly plant and high-level training has produced no site, cost or groundbreaking since. United States engagement is at working-session stage, no financier or amount reported; nothing is held on Indian or Gulf engagement.

The financing picture is announcements at the top and nothing at the bottom. The technology strategy states FCFA 1,105bn across twelve priority programmes to 2034 with no breakdown; the 2026 ministry vote is FCFA 81.06bn with FCFA 58.2bn to investment, reported as set without stating tabled or enacted, which makes it an announcement rather than an appropriation. No execution, outturn or audit figure exists for any digital line in any year.

External commitments are better documented than domestic ones. The digital economy acceleration project states FCFA 95.05bn committed against US$150m at board approval, and the two amounts are not reconciled in the base; a foundation grant of US$10m targets a universal digital identity, an AI hub and a delivery unit; and a US$50m pre-seed and seed fund has no first close or manager on file.

Skills carry the largest unverified commitment in the base: US$200m announced in 2019 and running to 2030, with no disbursement or beneficiary figure held seven years on. A memorandum to equip 120 primary schools and train 500 women has produced no count at any date, and two AI research laboratories launched in January 2025 have published no output or headcount. No domestic measurement of the digital divide exists at all.

Nine instrument rows carry no reliable statement. Three are institutional absences the breach record makes conspicuous: no national response team or cybersecurity agency, no open government data policy, and no power provision for the data-centre and telecom estate — the one Senegalese statement on that being an industry recommendation of December 2025, never adopted.
