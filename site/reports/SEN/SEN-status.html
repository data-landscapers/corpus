This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

GouvNum, a digital governance committee created in March 2026, was installed under the Prime Minister on 16 October 2026 to harmonise the state's digital projects. The telecoms and digital ministry is its secretariat. It is chaired by the Prime Minister. It sits apart from the Conseil national du numérique, inaugurated on 2 October 2025. Its twenty experts from the public sector, private enterprise and civil society advise on technology trends, digital policy and structural projects.

The New Deal Technologique Horizon 2034, launched in February 2025, replaced Senegal Numérique 2025. Its core is interoperability of government information systems, digital public infrastructure and a centralised integrated digital platform. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye launched it on 24 February 2025 under the Vision Sénégal 2050 framework. It rests on four pillars: digital sovereignty, a digital administration and public services, the digital economy, and Senegal as an African digital hub. Its predecessor, Digital Senegal 2025, was adopted in 2016. It covered networks, end-user equipment, cybersecurity, e-government, e-commerce, market regulation and skills. In March 2026 Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko launched the New Deal's 12 structuring projects, worth about 1,100bn FCFA over 2025–2034.

The Justice Sectoral Policy Letter for 2025-2029 provides for a national digital judicial-record platform and the progressive digitisation of the courts. On 29 July 2026 the Head of State told the government to speed up a national artificial intelligence plan, which had not been adopted. In 2025 the World Bank found that Senegal has an institution with a GovTech or digital government mandate, but that it publishes nothing about its work. The New Deal contains no explicit digital-rights provisions (March 2025). On 8 September 2026 the prime minister's policy statement refocused the New Deal's priorities. Expressions of interest were then called for an infrastructure-needs study for the national interoperability platform, closing on 7 October 2026.

Legislation and regulation

In 2022 the feasibility study for the Identifiant Numérique National found that no law defines digital identity in Senegal. The bill computerising civil-status management had not been adopted as of August 2025, nor its implementing decrees. In June 2026 the Nékkal programme reported validated texts and a national civil-status strategy. A revised personal data protection bill is finalised and awaiting a vote in the National Assembly (2026). Loi n° 2026-09 of 10 April 2026 created the Conseil national de Régulation des Médias, an independent authority attached to the Presidency. Its remit runs beyond broadcasting to print, the online press, content creators and foreign media accessible in Senegal. Three days earlier the Constitutional Council had struck three of its provisions. The Assembly adopted law n° 25/2026 on critical information infrastructure and digital security unanimously on 20 August 2026. On 29 September 2026 the plenary sent back to committee Bill 21/2026, which extends the validity of the first ECOWAS biometric cards.

Act No. 2008-12 of 25 January 2008 governs the processing of personal data and makes biometric data and identity numbers subject to prior authorisation. Law No. 2008-08 of 25 January 2008 sets the legal frame for electronic commerce, proof and signatures. Its Decree No. 2008-718 gives consumers seven working days to withdraw from an electronic contract. Law No. 2021-25 on prices and consumer protection covers digital payments, drones, e-waste and e-commerce. Decree No. 2019-591 organises the telecoms regulator ARTP. Decree No. 2022-1814 assigns e-commerce to the digital ministry. The Startup Act, Law No. 2020-01 of 6 January 2020, gives young innovative firms a statutory regime; its implementing decree was adopted in January 2025.

In 2024 no law imposed general data localisation; the 2008 law restricts transfers to countries without adequate protection. The bill for law n° 25/2026 requires state data to be hosted and processed on national territory (July 2026). No promulgated text had been published as at September 2026. The UEMOA fintech regulation, in force since 23 January 2024, governs payment providers. The Criminal Procedure Code still requires case files to be kept on paper and sent physically to prosecutors.

Data protection

The data protection commission handled 195 files between April and June 2026, 154 declarations and 41 authorisation requests, and received eight complaints. Its quarterly report records a refusal to authorise the Arma Care health platform, which sent data abroad without consent. In the third quarter of 2025 it issued three formal notices and ran inspection missions. The World Bank assessed the law as in force and effective, with an authority in place (2025).

Law No. 2008-12 governs the regime, never amended as at March 2025. A revision is finalised and awaiting a vote in the National Assembly (March 2026). The act carries no duty to notify a breach to the authority or those affected. The Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles enforces it. In May 2025 it signed a memorandum with the digital-rights organisation Paradigm Initiative on awareness, training and redress. Created by the 2008 law and operational since 2014, it authorises the processing of personal and biometric data and conducts inspections. It can impose administrative fines or suspend processing. Its decisions are challenged before the administrative chamber of the Cour Suprême, and breaches of the 2008 law are prosecuted in the criminal courts.

Hackers breached the identity agency DAF's biometric database in February 2026. The state's communiqués said only that the integrity of personal data "demeure intacte" and "est strictement préservée" (April 2026). They did not address confidentiality or volume, or deny exfiltration. In September the directorate's head confirmed a cyberattack and said no personal data was compromised.

Civil-status digitisation carries an information-system security policy and a data-protection strategy developed with the commission (August 2025). In July 2026 the digital ministry adopted a national action plan for protecting children online, built on nine axes. It commits to prevent cyberbullying, counter online child sexual exploitation and train families and teachers. A convention between the state digital operator and the universal health-cover agency states its data commitment as an intention, with no referral to the commission (July 2026). The biometric register of artisanal gold miners was announced with no data-protection provision and no role for the commission (August 2026).

Regional collaboration

The ECOWAS Community budget for 2026-2028 carries no disaggregated digital or ICT appropriation anywhere in its expenditure structure. The only ICT-named programme line in it, Youth Entrepreneurship in ICT, shows nil from 2024 through the 2026, 2027 and 2028 estimates.

Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger left ECOWAS and its WURI identity programme in 2024. They have since assembled a rival Alliance of Sahel States credential stack: a common biometric passport launched in January 2025, and AES-aligned national identity cards since. These give passport-free travel among the three outside the ECOWAS scheme.

The ECOWAS Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024-2029 was adopted on 4 October 2024 at Cotonou. At the same meeting ministers required full implementation of the ECOWAS Roaming Regulation by all mobile operators by the end of 2025. They also adopted a directive on confidence-building measures in the cyber and ICT field, to be implemented "without delay upon its publication". Under the ECOWAS and World Bank WURI programme, a cross-border identity interoperability strategy and 2026 work plan were validated in March 2026. They work toward mutual recognition of national identity credentials, an objective rather than an adopted standard.

In November 2025 Senegal and Guinea signed a memorandum on network interconnection, e-government, cybersecurity and data centres. At the Gambian border, the two customs administrations agreed in July 2026 to install the GAINDE customs system at three shared border posts within three months. They also agreed to deploy SIGMAT for real-time tracking of transit consignments. They are to replace transit agents with a Single Administrative Document and end double escorting on the Wellingara Corridor. They are also to reactivate the EnCen intelligence-sharing platform. Further out, Senegal hosted a Smart Africa and UNESCO regional data-governance workshop in May 2026 bringing in six neighbouring states. Its digital minister spoke for the Global AI Capacity-Building Cooperation Network at the Global AI Dialogue in Geneva in July 2026. The minister called for cooperation that lets developing countries benefit from artificial intelligence. The steering committee of the digital cooperation memorandum with The Gambia held its first session at Banjul on 29 August 2026.

Standards

A full inventory of Senegal's state information systems was launched at the Government Enterprise Architecture project's workshop of 29 July 2026, where interim diagnostic results were presented. Senegal is drawing up the common reference framework for its state systems after building the systems. A Government Enterprise Architecture is being stood up under the New Deal Technologique. It spans public policies, business processes, data, applications and the state's digital infrastructure. The national interoperability platform, PINS, is one of its four components, alongside national digital identity, a government cloud and data governance. Sénégal Numérique S.A. is the implementer and the PAENS programme the funder.

A national data strategy does exist and is published: the 2023-2028 strategy commits under its first orientation to a legal framework adapted to data opening and to a national data-sharing platform. It also commits to drafting a data law and to standing up a data governance authority. What the architecture has to correct is the absence of shared rule underneath. The comparative assessment records that neither the authority nor the law has followed. Senegal has no data governance institution recorded and no government interoperability framework recorded. It has neither a government open source software policy nor any recorded open source adoption (2025). The President ordered an orientation law on data and their security in April 2024. Drafting opened at a ministry workshop in June 2024. The interoperability platform itself is built on X-Road. Its roadmap of a 2024-2025 pilot and national deployment across 2025-2027 was validated by the COCOOR inter-ministerial coordination committee in September 2025.

The telecoms regulator has deployed eight digital regulatory platforms (January 2026). Where published standards do bite, they are mostly sectoral or regional. The platforms are SUNUCARTO for national digital-infrastructure mapping, RESEAU for quality of experience, CATALOGUE, DMARKET for tariffs, NUMFLEX for number portability, CLAIMS, FSVA and VCN. Payment providers are held to the UEMOA fintech regulation in force since 23 January 2024. The health-coverage payments platform is aligned to the UN's nine Principles for Responsible Digital Payments. Public procurement has moved to a single electronic platform, APPEL, live since 15 October 2025.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Senegal's Digital Freedom score fell 27.3 points over 2014-2023 to 45.6 out of 100.0, 30th of 54 African countries. It was the fourth-largest ten-year decline among the 96 indicators in its profile. Freedom of Association & Assembly fell 37.5 points to 37.5 out of 100.0, the second largest decline of the decade. Media Freedom fell 17.9 points to 60.0, and Civil Society Space fell 8.2 points to 81.5, all at 2023. Freedom of Expression & Belief is among the country's ten best-scoring measures at 87.5, 7th of 54, after falling 7.5 points.

Accountability & Transparency is Senegal's worst-scoring governance sub-category at 48.4 out of 100.0, 15th of 54, after falling 8.4 points over the decade (2023). The country scores far better on the accessibility of public records than on their disclosure. An access to information law was adopted in August 2025, and civil society welcomed it as a transparency measure. In 2025 the World Bank found a citizen feedback mechanism in place that meets none of the five further measures applied to it. An online citizen participation platform is in use but meets only one of six (2025).

Consumer bodies, fintechs and some lawmakers opposed the proposed levy on mobile-money transfers (November 2025). They said it would reverse financial-inclusion gains and push users back to cash, and proposed alternatives. In February 2025 public argument about artificial intelligence asked what strategy is open to Senegal and Africa against Chinese and American corporate domination. On 1–2 July 2026 the digital ministry convened public administration, universities, the private sector and other partners to set the country's AI and data priorities. In April 2026 the minister said internet access "is no longer a luxury, it is a citizen's right".

Finance

Financial sustainability

The external-projects annex of Senegal's 2026 finance law carries XOF 30.5bn of loans from the World Bank, BOAD and the African Development Bank on the Digital Governance and Sovereignty programme. Against them stand XOF 3.0bn of the state's own non-personnel credits in 2026. A XOF 14.0bn KfW grant funds the administration digitalisation programme PRODAP, against a state counterpart of XOF 126m (2026).

The state's own money is 28.8% of the digital lines of the 2026 budget as voted, XOF 22.0bn of XOF 76.5bn excluding personnel, against 31.2% in 2025.

In 2026 the treasury transfers XOF 5.77bn to the state digital operator SENUM SA and XOF 2.3bn to the universal-service fund FDSUT. In 2025 the digital-economy programme took up XOF 8.6bn of its XOF 9.65bn of domestic non-personnel credits. In 2024 it took up XOF 11.96bn against XOF 11.59bn. The telecoms regulator's radio-spectrum fees brought the budget XOF 45.6bn in 2025, 131% of target.

The World Bank's digital-economy acceleration project closes on 31 May 2028. It targets a model under which the health ministry takes the staff and system costs the project funds onto national health financing after closure. That model had not been developed as at October 2025. The 2026 restructuring cancelled the planned public-private partnerships to operate SENUM's fibre and build the backbone's missing links. It gave the reason that the government was not ready to support the SENUM reform. In May 2026 the Diamniadio technology park, about 95% built, was held up by its AfDB closing date and by unfinished concession talks with BOAD.

MoUs and other agreements

Visa and the telecommunications and digital ministry announced a two-year memorandum in October 2026, the first between Visa and a Senegalese government institution. It is to explore person-to-government and government-to-person payments, financial innovation and cybersecurity. UNDP and the government signed a memorandum of understanding in April 2026 covering digital governance, digital inclusion and artificial intelligence. Senegal and Finland opened cooperation on cybersecurity, sovereign and resilient digital infrastructure, data-centre modernisation and AI-ready infrastructure at political consultations in Dakar in March 2026. They framed it under the New Deal Technologique and the EU's Global Gateway. A services-federation memorandum with Mauritania was signed through OPTIC during Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko's visit in January 2025. An October 2024 memorandum with Google set out a sovereign cloud keeping the state's critical data hosted onshore. None of the five states a sum or an obligation to deliver.

New investments

Senegal's digital estate has drawn 172 external commitments, worth about US$1.4 billion on windows running from 2015 to 2034 (November 2026). That is money committed, not money moved. Chinese state finance is the largest single source, around a third of the total across four commitments (2015-2021). Grants outnumber debt by count, 151 of the 172, but the debt carries most of the money (2015-2026).

In 2026 Senegal's own digital budget is CFA 81.06 billion, up 38.7% on the year before. The World Bank alone has committed CFA 95.05 billion to the 2026 digital programme. BOAD is lending XOF 30.9 billion, about US$52 million, for the digital transformation of public services over 2026–2028.

In November 2015 China Eximbank lent RMB 530 million, about US$95 million, for the third phase of the e-government project. Two earlier phases laid some 1,500 km of fibre across the regional capitals and built the government intranet and its data centres. A RMB 200 million grant and a RMB 1.05 billion concessional loan were signed in February 2018 for the first phase of Smart Senegal. About US$148 million more finances the restructuring and expansion of the Diamniadio national data centre under the second (2023).

In May 2024 IFC, British International Investment and Proparco arranged an €87 million sustainability-linked loan for Sonatel, priced on network expansion targets. BII put US$31.7 million of debt into Wave Mobile Money in June 2025.

The government's Agenda de Transformation Numérique carries 1,105 billion FCFA across twelve priority programmes over 2025–2034. A US$10 million Gates Foundation grant funds a universal digital ID, an AI hub for health and agriculture and a delivery unit (September 2025). DER/FJ has launched Catalyst, a US$50 million pre-seed and seed fund for Senegalese startups (July 2026).

By value the money concentrates on connectivity, government digitalisation and data storage; national statistics draws the most commitments, 53, but only about US$19 million (November 2026). A US$300m investment commitment by an American cybersecurity company was presented to the president in Washington on 14 September 2026; no contract is on record.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Sonatel commissioned the country's landing station on the 2Africa submarine cable in December 2025. The 45,000km system has a nominal capacity of 180 Tbps, about ten times that of earlier cables. The landing is carrier-neutral and open-access, so operators other than Sonatel can buy capacity through it.

In 2025 GSMA found 4G networks covering 97% of the population, and a 54% mobile-internet usage gap. It counts about 52% of the population using mobile services and 42% on mobile broadband (2025). About 75% of men and 61% of women own a smartphone, and handset cost is the main constraint on growth (2024). Mobile data prices sit well below the African average.

Phase3 Telecom and Sonatel opened a 3,500km terrestrial fibre route from Lagos to Dakar in May 2025. It gives Senegal an overland alternative to subsea routes of the kind broken in 2024. On 5 October 2026 the Senegalese and Malian state fibre operators inaugurated the interconnection of their backbones at Kidira-Diboli. Two internet exchange points operate in Dakar, SENIX and HubSIX (2024). The internet exchange point had 260 Gbps of capacity and six members in September 2026.

Sonatel launched Senegal's first satellite internet offer in January 2026 on Eutelsat's Konnect service. In July 2026 it rebuilt its 1972-era Gandoul teleport in Thiès as a gateway for Eutelsat OneWeb's low-earth-orbit constellation. Starlink was licensed for five years, renewable. The state negotiated 5,000 kits at a preferential price, aiming to connect a million Senegalese free of charge. The first were handed over at Kolda, in Casamance, in April 2026. On 7 September 2026 the Supreme Court suspended Starlink's provisional licence.

In December 2025 rural households were barely connected at all. The statistics agency found 62.3% of people aged 10 and over with internet access in 2024, 79.4% in towns against 41.3% in rural areas. Senegal's Mobile Communications score reached 83.4 out of 100.0 in 2023, 7th of 54. Internet & Computers scored 37.0 and ranked 21st in the same year. The government plans to connect 6,888 schools to broadband, and more than 2,000 more by satellite, under its 2025-2029 digital education strategy (September 2026).

Data Storage

In 2023 government data and platforms sat on foreign servers, to be centralised onshore. The New Deal sets a target of 100% of sensitive government data hosted inside the country (August 2026). In October 2026 the national data centre's manager said 10 to 20 per cent of public data was still hosted abroad, some under ten-year hosting contracts, with no repatriation date set.

The national data centre at Diamniadio is the hosting base for government digital services (2024). It was built by Huawei under Chinese financing and inaugurated in June 2021. The commercial estate around it is a handful of small facilities, all in Dakar (2024). PAIX Data Centres broke ground at Les Mamelles in January 2025. It is a 1.2MW building due online in 2026. AWS opened a Wavelength Zone in Dakar in April 2025, the first in West Africa. The interior minister told deputies in September 2026 that the government is building a data centre for civil-status, identity-card and passport data.

For the sovereign cloud, Senegal signed a memorandum with Google in October 2024 to keep the state's critical data onshore. It signed an accord with Huawei in June 2025 whose first element is modernisation of Senegal's data centres. In November 2026 Alibaba said the private cloud it built for the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games hosts their ticketing and e-visa processing, and will pass to Senegal after the Games as a national cloud platform.

The 2025 GovTech Maturity Index found no policy on where government data may be hosted. The bill for law n° 25/2026 requires data held by state services to be hosted and processed on national territory (July 2026). The National Assembly adopted it on 20 August 2026. No promulgated text had been published as at September 2026.

Pre-launch analysis of the New Deal named a storage and compute deficit among its limits (February 2025). The civil-status system runs on 6 servers totalling 1,000 TB across 3 hosting sites (August 2025), on a grid where most firms experience outages. A capacity expansion of the Stellarix data centre at Diamniadio was reported on 2 October 2026.

Energy

The World Bank's Enterprise Survey of 603 firms, interviewed between July 2024 and March 2025, found 55% of firms experiencing power outages. Those firms averaged 1.8 outages in a typical month.

Senegal's Access to Energy score stood at 49.4 out of 100.0 in 2023, 18th of 54, after rising 5.7 points over the decade. Most of the population has a connection, but rural access remains far behind urban (2023). Rural electrification runs under a national programme of the agency ASER, with significant off-grid deployment under way (2024).

In December 2025 SENELEC's average residential price was XOF 109.39/kWh. Its tariff runs from about XOF 82/kWh for the first 150 kWh to XOF 159/kWh in higher tiers. Renewables account for about 24% of generation capacity (2021).

In 2025 Sonatel ran more than 1,000 solar-powered network sites in Senegal.

Technical Capacity

Since 2016 card production and key technical infrastructure for the national identity system have been contracted to Malaysia's Iris Corporation Berhad, under an arrangement renewed in 2021 (2025). Day-to-day operation sits with the DAF, and production capability with the foreign vendor.

In April 2026 Alibaba was training a cohort of Senegalese cloud engineers alongside the sovereign-cloud work for Dakar 2026. The June 2025 accord with Huawei commits to high-level training and skills transfer in cloud computing, artificial intelligence and 5G.

Public administration is among Senegal's strongest governance sub-categories (2023). In 2025 the World Bank found an established public-sector innovation institution behind both a strategy and a programme, with digital skills as its focus. The digital skills programme for public servants does not go beyond basic skills, and its documentation is unpublished (2025).

The Taouey supercomputer, rated at 537.6 teraflops, entered service at Diamniadio in March 2024. In June 2026 its operator said it runs part-time, because the GPU cell most in demand is not reliably powered. A power-conditioning contract was in procurement.

In 2025 public contributor trackers listed Senegal's active open-source developers in the hundreds. The digital technology park at Diamniadio has been under development since 2015, and its financing commitment remains open. Senegal presented a national AI Factory to an international audience in Tokyo on 3 August 2026.

Cybersecurity

In 2025 the DGID tax authority lost roughly 1TB of data, including tax declarations and identity documents. The air transport directorate was named alongside the tax and identity agencies among the services attacked (August 2026).

In February 2026 ransomware at the DAF identity agency halted production of national identity cards. Production resumed on 1 April 2026. A security researcher confirmed genuine Senegalese birth records and identity cards among the samples the attackers published. The interior minister said in September 2026 that a general audit of the directorate's system had been engaged.

On 20 August 2026 the National Assembly unanimously adopted law n° 25/2026 on the protection of critical information infrastructure and digital security. It mandates in-country storage of state-sensitive data and empowers a National Cybersecurity Authority. On the ministry's account it also creates national and sectoral computer emergency response teams and grades obligations on critical operators by risk. Critical infrastructure faces enhanced requirements on monitoring, backup, encryption, continuity and resilience testing. No promulgation date or gazette reference has been published, and none of those bodies has been found in operation. The National Cybersecurity Strategy (SNC2022) sets the country's cybersecurity framework.

In July 2026 the procurement regulator ARCOP was brought into the critical-infrastructure cybersecurity programme. It puts the difficulty in contracting, not in budget or technical capability, and names the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games as the window the upgrades must clear. No security assessment, continuity arrangement or manual fallback has been published for APPEL (July 2026), the platform those contracts run through.

INTERPOL's African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 names Senegal among the continent's ransomware hotspots (August 2026). It finds attackers increasingly targeting health institutions, public-service providers and government platforms. Senegal scores 67.17 out of 100 on the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, in Tier 3. In November 2026 the cybersecurity directorate DCSSI put online a national single window for reporting cybersecurity incidents, open to administrations, businesses and citizens.

DPI

Data Exchange

In August 2026 the state said its own government platforms remain siloed, sixteen months after the national interoperability platform was launched to connect them. The 2025 GovTech Maturity Index records no government interoperability framework, and marks all five of its sub-measures absent.

The platform is the Plateforme Nationale d'Interopérabilité du Sénégal, built on X-Road, whose 2024-2027 roadmap the COCOOR inter-ministerial committee validated in September 2025. In 2026 the ministry listed Treasury e-payments, government correspondence, the e-Senegal single window, e-Consulat and e-Visa as running on it. Two exchanges are stated in production: health cover reading social-protection data to enrol people in free health insurance, and tax retrieving validated customs declarations. The decree adopting the platform's framework was still presented as a draft in May 2026.

Foreign-trade formalities have run through the ORBUS single window since 2004. Since 2014 the NINEA business identifier has linked the statistics agency, Customs, the tax and finance directorates and the Treasury. Customs and tax cross-check each other's data under a protocol signed in March 2024. In 2025 the civil registration and identity authorities signed a convention binding the civil register to the national identity file. A protocol of January 2026 puts birth, marriage and death data on automated exchange with the statistics agency. Health facilities can now notify the civil-status centre at the moment of birth (August 2026).

Senegal is pursuing interoperability through at least four separate efforts run by different bodies — the national platform, a climate-transparency platform, a social-protection information system and a bilateral customs exchange with The Gambia — three of which opened within a single fortnight of July 2026. Joint work on interoperability between the civil registry and public identification was completed and two options put up for arbitration, announced 16 September 2026.

Digital Identity and CRVS

On 5 February 2026 the Direction de l'Automatisation des Fichiers suspended production of national identity cards after an intrusion into its systems. It resumed on 1 April. The communiqués assert that the register's data integrity remains intact and say nothing about its confidentiality. In September the directorate's head said no personal data had been compromised, with no technical account. A security researcher found genuine Senegalese birth records and identity cards among the samples the attackers published.

The credential, the ECOWAS biometric card, was established by Law No. 2016-09 of 14 March 2016. It may be obtained from the age of five and must be held from fifteen. The DAF runs the biometric population register and issues identity cards, passports and residence documents. In February 2025 the card became the sole accepted proof of identity for voter registration. In 2023 the social register and the universal health scheme both checked identity against it, so people without a card struggled to obtain either. The New Deal sets a target of digital-ID coverage of more than 90 per cent of citizens (2026).

In 2025 the GovTech index recorded the public-service HR system and the social insurance system as not using the national ID. No law defines digital identity (2022). Card production sits with Malaysia's Iris Corporation, under an arrangement running since 2016 and renewed in 2021 at US$19.5 million. The interior minister told deputies the contract, last amended on 29 April 2026, runs to 21 May 2027. He said card and passport production would pass progressively to Senegalese firms (September 2026).

A physical and biometric audit of the civil-service headcount has run since 23 June 2026, with more than 35 auditor teams. No correction route has been found for an employee it fails to find. On 30 July 2026 the mines ministry and the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations signed a convention for the biometric identification of artisanal gold miners and the issue of secure professional cards.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Senegal carried the largest share of UEMOA's mobile-money transaction volume in 2024, 24.1 per cent, up from 22.7 per cent the year before. On value it was second, at 30.2 per cent against Côte d'Ivoire's 34.7 per cent (2024). Eleven e-money issuing establishments operate in the country, second only to Côte d'Ivoire's nineteen (2024). In 2024, 76 per cent of adults held a mobile money account, on 14.65 million active accounts. The central bank's 2025 indicators, released in October 2026, put the share of adults using a financial service at 88.04 per cent once multiple accounts are netted out, against 76.06 per cent across the union. The rate was 83.3 per cent in 2023.

In 2025 the government proposed a 0.5 per cent levy on mobile-money transfers, plus higher merchant charges. The levy was to raise around CFA 220bn over three years under the 2025-2028 Recovery Plan.

On 30 September 2025 the central bank launched PI-SPI, for instant 24/7 transfers between banks, microfinance institutions and e-money issuers across the eight WAEMU states. Nineteen Senegalese institutions are authorised participants (March 2026). Wave was on the participant list as updated on 30 September 2026. It opened PI-SPI to all its Senegalese customers in the week of 6 November 2026, free up to 8,000 FCFA a day. From 2 November 2026 transfers between bank accounts and wallets had to go through PI-SPI, and banks suspended their own bank-to-wallet and wallet-to-bank services. At the end of February 2026 Senegal held 11 of the 31 payment institutions licensed across the union, more than any other member state. Wave holds an e-money issuer licence. It charges nothing for deposits and withdrawals and a flat 1 per cent on transfers, against the operator wallets' 5-10 per cent (June 2025).

SenTresor, launched on 2 June 2025, pays state salaries, allowances, social assistance, pensions and scholarships digitally. The family security grant resumed on 1 October 2026 after a two-year interruption, paying CFA 140,000 per household through mobile-money operators. Taxes, duties and fees have been payable through Orange's Max It application since November 2025. The entrepreneurship fund DER/FJ disburses and collects repayments through the Orange Money and Wave wallets (July 2026).

Registries

By September 2026, 77 per cent of Senegal's 629 civil-status centres had been digitised. The management software was deployed in 74 per cent of them, and the online certificate platform operated in 189 municipalities as of August 2026. The live register held 15,352,830 records at 13 July 2026, against 20 million acts digitised and indexed out of the archives by August 2025. The platform registered 112,881 declarations in 2024 and 108,233 in the first half of 2025. The agency says digitisation secures the paper register without replacing it; centres still receive declarations as the law requires. In 2025 it instructed mayors to stop using unauthorised civil-status software.

Birth registration of children under five reached 81.4 per cent in the 2023 EDS Continue survey, up from just over 50 per cent in 2013. Against the RGPH-5 census, 5.3 per cent of the population and 8.9 per cent of under-fives hold no birth certificate (August 2026). The 2023 census found 45.7 per cent of deaths declared to the civil registry, and 57.1 per cent undeclared in rural areas.

The 2016 enterprise census found about 97 per cent of the country's 407,882 economic units operating informally. Firms get a NINEA identifier through the statistics agency's e-NINEA portal. They register on the OHADA commercial register, which SEN'INFOGREFFE puts online (2026). A decree of 27 August 2025 opened the core data of the extractive-sector beneficial-ownership register free to the public. By 2026, 28 per cent of extractive companies had disclosed their owners.

The 2022-23 agricultural survey found 97.3 per cent of surveyed parcels with no registered land document. The NICAD parcel identifier is mandatory and unique, and 673,000 parcels had been mapped at the November 2025 supervision. The land register is not connected to the national data exchange (late 2025). The tax directorate's Système de Gestion Foncière is being built to interoperate with the communal land information system SIFCOM. There is no harmonised national address register (2025), and the national digital addressing project was relaunched on 5 August 2026. The social register grew from 542,000 households to about one million over 2024.

Sectoral management information systems

In July 2026 the social-protection ministry found that its five main programmes each run on separately built information systems, limiting exchange between them and producing overlapping interventions. They are the family security grants, universal health cover, the equal opportunity card, the national solidarity fund and the single register. A two-day workshop diagnosed the existing systems, fixed requirements and validated a roadmap for an interoperable replacement.

In 2025 the World Bank found the tax administration running off-the-shelf software not connected to other government systems. Customs runs its own package, with all three connection and integration measures absent. The financial management information system was still in implementation and covers central government only. The treasury set 2 January 2026 for its new integrated system to carry all state accounting (August 2025). The social insurance and pension systems do not interoperate with other government systems (2025). SENTAX replaced ETAX for large-enterprise taxpayers and became mandatory for their declarations and payments from 1 September 2026. It opened to the Dakar medium-enterprise centres from 1 October 2026.

In 2025 a study of four districts found maternal and child health registers poorly kept, with few providers trained to record data in DHIS2. A World Bank-funded project is extending electronic patient records to six more regions (April 2025). The health ministry's policy letter makes the shared single patient record the flagship of its digitalisation programme. In 2024 stakeholders found the SIMEN education information system well organised but incomplete and late from some sub-sectors.

A UN-funded e-justice pilot at the Pikine-Guédiawaye court became operational from September 2025. Its final report records five judicial procedures digitised in that one court and more than 40 justice professionals trained (August 2026). No extension to other courts has been announced. The Dakar police data centre opened in August 2022 as the WAPIS programme's tenth. The programme was handed over to the regional community on 8 April 2025.

On 9 October 2026 the agriculture ministry launched nationwide distribution of subsidised seed and fertiliser through the ERP Pass application. The minister said the state now holds a digital register of beneficiaries and allocations.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Senegal withdrew the paper fiscal stamp on 20 July 2026. Only stamps issued through the tax directorate's SenTimbre mobile platform, each carrying a secured QR code, are now recognised by public administrations. The change also ended the private digital distribution that had supplied the stamp. The announcement stated no offline or assisted route for users without a smartphone or an online means of payment.

In March 2026 the e-Senegal single window was handling passport applications, business creation, police-clearance and nationality certificates with online tracking. The Senegal Services portal carries more than 750 digitised administrative procedures (2023). The APPEL e-procurement platform has been live since 15 October 2025 for the procurement of a thousand public administrations. Its generalisation is set for January 2027. Contracting authorities must load their 2027 procurement plans onto it by 1 December 2026. Since 2024 traffic fines have been processed digitally at 50 points across 47 police and gendarmerie brigades. For civil registration, 112 of the 437 connected centres had activated the Sama État Civil service as at 17 July 2026, about 26 per cent.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded the national service portal as transactional, but without company formation. Citizens are not involved in designing it, and it publishes nothing about its own operation. There is no online social protection portal at all. A full inventory of the state's information systems was launched at the Government Enterprise Architecture workshop of 29 July 2026. No date, budget or publication commitment attaches to it (August 2026). A national strategy for receiving and directing public-service users went to technical validation in October 2026. Supporting documents are to pass between administrations by interoperability, with health and security as pilot sectors.

Of the thirty-two headline indicators in the 2025 GovTech index, ten are recorded as absent, and they fall in a pattern: the line-ministry transaction systems for tax, customs, human resources, payroll, social insurance and debt are all in use, while the layer that would connect and govern them is not. The e-Justice programme and the Jokko Ak Yoon platform were launched on 10 September 2026.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In 2023 the Agence de Développement Local's E-Territoire programme validated a national digital-territories master plan and local digital plans for Bargny, Mont-Rolland and Sandiara. A UNDP-funded US$1.5 million rollout of municipal land-information systems was under way in those three pilot communes (2023).

By mid-July 2026 the civil-status management software covered the majority of the country's centres. It is the only central system that has reached most communes. A year earlier much of the installed base was not yet live (August 2025). The equipment, from interconnected centres and office computing to motorcycles and communal coordination plans, came through the Nékkal programme, which closed in June 2026. In 2025 the national civil registration agency told mayors to stop using unauthorised civil-status software in their registration offices.

In 2025 research on Dakar's property tax found that Senegal maintains no harmonised addressing system, and named this among the constraints on collection. KfW is financing decentralised infrastructure and equipment to improve access to land use rights and land titles through 2028.

The treasury presented the Koom-Com application at the 2025 Dakar fair, covering a geolocated taxpayer census through to collection. Its first phase covers 125 urban communes, said to account for about 80% of local revenue (2025).

Rural digital data capture

At the end of 2025 only 3% of rural Senegalese households had an internet connection.

In 2025 a study of four districts found rural maternal and child health registers poorly kept, with few providers trained to record data in DHIS2. Electronic patient records exist only in a small World Bank-funded pilot (April 2025). In 2023 only 15.4% of rural primary schools had internet access, against 41.4% in urban areas. Schools keep enrolment, attendance and teacher data on registers and logs, aggregated through district and regional offices. The DHIS2-based education deployment begun in 2024 remains a district-level pilot producing aggregate data only. Police and gendarmerie officers must send the original of each procès-verbal to the prosecutor, and the Criminal Procedure Code names no electronic form. Their one digital function outside the capital is processing forfeitary traffic fines. In mid-July 2026 the civil-status software ran thinnest in Kédougou, Sédhiou and Tambacounda and close to complete in Thiès, Dakar and Fatick.

In 2023 almost no surveyed agricultural parcel carried a registered land document. Kédougou holds nearly all of Senegal's recorded artisanal gold-mining sites. The state ties the lack of traceability there to water pollution, insecurity and criminal infiltration of the mining zones (2026). Rural Representation and Participation is Senegal's lowest-ranked of 96 governance indicators, 36th of 54 in 2023 after falling 17.2 points over the decade.

The first Starlink kits were handed over in Kolda, in Casamance, on 10 April 2026. They were a first lot of 200 from 1,000 gifted with two years' subscription. The handover tour ran on 9–13 April 2026 through Ziguinchor, Kolda and Kaolack. Rural electrification runs under a national programme with substantial off-grid deployment (2024).

Technology

AI

An AI application for judicial archives began a pilot at the Pikine-Guediawaye High Court, reported on 6 October 2026. On 9 October 2026 the justice ministry judged its first results satisfactory, naming staff recruitment and the regular feeding of the platform as open challenges. The state digital company runs Anta, a chatbot that guides users through some 700 procedures on the public-service portal (August 2025). It is the only AI found in citizen-facing services. In January 2025 the tax directorate's data-mining project was described as using predictive models to detect anomalies and fraud. In May 2026 the health ministry told the World Health Assembly it had begun integrating AI into the national DHIS2 platform.

In July 2025 a social enterprise launched kSanté with the health ministry, an offline AI assistant for community health workers. An AI-based One Health surveillance platform cut detection in the 2025 Rift Valley fever outbreak from 4.6 to 2.8 days. In February 2026 the lab Soynade released open translation datasets, among them 50,000 web texts put into Wolof. A Senegal AI Factory was presented in Tokyo on 3 August 2026. Korea's development agency put the AI transformation centre it is financing at Diamniadio at US$14.5 million over 2026–2033 (October 2025). The Gates Foundation's AI hub for health and agriculture was launched in Dakar on 29 October 2026 as the Senegal AI Scaling Hub.

On 29 July 2026 the Council of Ministers recorded the Head of State directing government to accelerate the deployment of a Plan national de développement de l'Intelligence artificielle. On 1–2 July 2026 the digital ministry set AI and data priorities with administration, universities and business. They include local-language voice translation, remote diagnostic support, mobility, natural-hazard prediction and smart grids. On 21–22 September 2026 the ministry validated a draft blueprint for creating value from data, with Kenya and Lesotho as fellow pilots. A UNESCO readiness assessment names initiatives duplicated across government and slowed by politics and bureaucracy. No law specific to AI has been found. A revised personal data protection bill, the law AI would run under, awaits a vote.

ICT Industry

In 2025 Senegalese startups raised about US$84 million in equity across ten deals, up 137% on the year, and roughly US$223 million including debt. Senegal was one of only three markets outside Africa's four largest to clear US$50 million in equity in 2025. Sonatel is the incumbent operator. In July 2026 it extended its super-app into Dakar's bus rapid transit ticketing. Wave holds a BCEAO electronic money issuer licence for its transfers and merchant payments. Senegal hosts one of the densest fields of licensed e-money issuers in the West African monetary union (2024). Flutterwave took a BCEAO payment institution licence in July 2025. American development finance has backed a US logistics-technology firm's platform in the market.

The June 2025 accord with Huawei provides for assembling IT equipment in the country. The digital technology park at Diamniadio has been under development with African Development Bank financing since 2015, with land still held for private tenants. On 6 October 2026 the telecommunications ministry signed a US$150m partnership convention for local production of electronic devices. No financing schedule or site has been stated. Public contributor trackers record a small developer community (2025). Company formation and business registration cannot be done through the national service portal (2025). The New Deal sets 2034 targets for direct jobs and a share of GDP.

Innovation ecosystem

In January 2025 Senegal adopted the implementing decree of its startup law, five years after Law No. 2020-01 of 6 January 2020 on the creation and promotion of startups was passed. The labelling process it establishes was launched during the New Deal's first year.

In March 2025 DER/FJ, the state's entrepreneurship-finance vehicle, signed a digital inclusion and innovation pact with Sonatel. Under it the Bambey digital campus becomes an innovation hub, outside Dakar. Korean cooperation has funded a start-up incubation centre and makerspace at ISEP Diamniadio. On 29 July 2026 the Council of Ministers asked for a Fonds national de promotion de la recherche et de l'innovation and a Conseil national de la Recherche et de l'Innovation. No statute, budget or date has been set for either.

Senegal ranked 92nd in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024, of around 130 economies. In 2025 the World Bank found an established public-sector innovation institution backed by both a strategy and a programme. It recorded no startup financing or SME support measures, and neither a government open source policy nor any open source adoption. Wave's flat pricing broke the operator wallets' charges and carried it to Francophone Africa's first unicorn valuation (June 2025).

Capacity

Literacy

Human Resources in Education is Senegal's fourth best-scoring governance measure, at 94.5 out of 100 and eighth of 54 in Africa (2023). It was essentially unchanged across the decade. Education as a whole is one of its three weakest sub-categories at 53.0, 26th of 54 (2023). It rose 3.9 points over ten years. Enrolment at 35.1 and completion at 29.7 hold it down — 35th and 33rd, and both among the country's ten worst-scoring indicators (2023). Senegal ranks fifth of 54 on equality in education at 73.3, up 8.3 points over 2014–2023.

The Head of State asked government on 29 July 2026 to accelerate the digital transformation of the education system and of higher-education institutions. The education ministry and the Sonatel Foundation agreed in March 2025 to work on putting digital tools into schools and widening access to them. The national plan on online child protection names digital education in schools and awareness campaigns in national languages among its delivery mechanisms. The ministry launched PLANETE 3, a national school-management platform with modules for pupils, parents and teachers, on 3 October 2025. The civil-registration agency has agreed with the early-childhood agency a check-and-regularise step for undocumented children at preschool entry (August 2026).

The ITU's Digital Transformation Centre in Senegal, the NGO Concept, trained over 41,000 people in 2024–2025 across 65 ICT training centres. Five of the centres are for people with visual impairments and children. Finance 4 All, running from June 2024 to July 2026, trained 265 civil-society trainers across 12 organisations in Dakar and Thiès in digital financial education. Outside the state, the national literacy coalition presses for literacy teaching in national languages to be paired with basic digital skills. It wants them delivered through tools that work where connections are poor. In higher education a national survey by the digital university's AI lab found 33.7% of 5,037 students did not know generative AI (August 2026).

Training and skills

By June 2026 FORCE-N, run by the Cheikh Hamidou Kane Digital University with the Mastercard Foundation since 2021, reported more than 100,000 young people certified in digital trades. It covers all fourteen regions and reported more than 91,000 supported into work. By 17 July 2026, 2,318 people had been trained on the civil-status software across 599 centres, 1,485 agents and 833 registrars. The New Deal commits Senegal to training 5,000 certified digital experts a year to 2034.

In April 2026 Alibaba was to train around 100 Senegalese cloud engineers under the Dakar 2026 sovereign-cloud arrangement. The June 2025 accord with Huawei commits to skills transfer in cloud computing, artificial intelligence and 5G. Several hundred blind and low-vision Senegalese have taken an advanced IT and AI course run by the STMicroelectronics Foundation with the ITU (July 2026). In 2025 the World Bank found a digital skills programme for public servants covering basic skills only, and none for citizens and schools. A digital training plan for state employees, validated on 10 September 2026, aims to reach all 183,502 state agents by 2029. A survey of 1,878 agents had put 57% at beginner level (September 2026).

The World Bank's Senegal Digital Economy Acceleration Project, running 2023–2028, makes digital skills one of its four components. Korean cooperation funded ICT laboratories, faculty training and curricula at the ISEP technical college at Diamniadio. Digital skills are an enabler in the Mastercard Foundation's youth employment commitment to 2030.

In August 2026 the literacy coalition cited an AI and digital-skills pilot for teachers at Mont-Rolland, limited by equipment, connection quality and a lack of locally adapted content. A deficit of AI skills is named as one of three obstacles to Senegal making AI rules (July 2026).

Research institutions

In January 2025 Cheikh Anta Diop University and the Cheikh Hamidou Kane Digital University launched two AI research laboratories, ALIVE and DiCentre4AI, under the AI4D programme. Their applications are in agriculture, education and industry. The 2023 AI strategy counts research, innovation and technology transfer among its orientations. In August 2025 the cybersecurity association called for a dedicated annual AI budget, with collaboration between the state, the army, universities and civil society.

Inclusion

Access to services

On 20 July 2026 Senegal withdrew the paper fiscal stamp and named no offline or assisted route to the digital replacement. No adoption figure was published. Days later the private app that had distributed the stamp free for six years suspended the service and began refunding its users. The state platform is now the only channel for the stamp.

Equal Access to Public Services scored 54.1 out of 100 in 2023, fourteenth of 54 African countries, after falling 11.9 points over 2014–2023. The 2010 social orientation law covers the access of persons with disabilities to ICT. Only two of its fourteen implementing decrees had been signed, and Senegal had not ratified the Marrakesh Treaty (2025).

The New Deal Technologique targets at least 95 per cent quality and affordable connectivity by 2034. The state reaches people through Espaces Sénégal Services, department-level counters that would also deliver universal health cover under a convention announced in July 2026. There is no citizen-facing social protection portal (2025). Enrolment in the social register and verification for universal health cover both turn on the identity card (2023).

Allo Goor Gi, presented on 30 July 2026, is a Wolof- and French-language AI tutor reachable on an ordinary phone call, with no internet connection. On 10 September 2026 the Council of Ministers ordered an information system for monitoring and handling citizens' concerns.

On 20 August 2026 the National Assembly opened a commission of inquiry into the student-computer programme by 129 votes to none. Its hearings opened on 5 October 2026. A former funding director testified that the 2025-26 handover recorded an order for 25,000 computers from two suppliers without the contracts.

Digital divides

Deployment of the civil-status software had reached 98 per cent of centres in Thiès, 92 per cent in Dakar and 90 per cent in Fatick by mid-July 2026. It stood at 42 per cent in Kédougou, 44 per cent in Sédhiou and 47 per cent in Tambacounda (July 2026). The system arrived last in the south and the east, where the share of pupils without civil-status documents was already highest, at 50.9 per cent in Sédhiou (2021).

78 per cent of men and 66 per cent of women used mobile internet in 2024. That gender gap of 15 per cent compares with a sub-Saharan African average of 29 per cent (2024), and women's adoption grew faster than men's over the year. The regulator's own household survey, fieldwork September to October 2024, found women ahead. 59.7 per cent of women had used the internet in the previous thirty days against 50.8 per cent of men (2024). It found 19.4 per cent of households with a home internet subscription, 43.8 per cent in Dakar and about 3 per cent in rural areas (2024). Of unconnected households, 65.6 per cent cited the price (2024). 4G reaches almost the whole population, and most of the people inside that footprint are not on mobile internet at all. The price of a handset is the principal brake on further growth. Rural household internet connections were still marginal at the end of 2025. Free-connectivity kits began reaching Casamance in April 2026. In September 2025 the universal service fund planned 774 mobile sites in four zones for about 436,000 residents. The plan followed a study that found 24 per cent of 250 localities with no network at all (2025).

Equal Socioeconomic Opportunity scored 87.9 out of 100 in 2023, second of 54 African countries. It rose 10.2 points over the decade and is the country's second-strongest governance measure continent-wide. The same profile puts access to banking among its ten weakest and internet and computers at 37.0 out of 100.0, 21st of 54.

Data

National statistics

On the World Bank's 2023 Statistical Performance Indicators Senegal scores above 89% for data use, 70% to 89% for data services, products and infrastructure, and only 50% to 69% for data sources. Data sources, meaning censuses and surveys, are its weakest pillar. The Ibrahim Index puts the capacity of the statistical system at 61.1 out of 100.0 in 2023, 13th of 54 and 12.5 points higher than a decade earlier.

The 2023 population census ran paper-free on tablets across 21,252 enumeration areas, with 96.0 per cent coverage on the post-enumeration survey. Its definitive results, published in July 2025, put the resident population at 18,126,390. Population, agricultural and business censuses have all been held within the past ten years, with three or more household, labour force, health and agricultural surveys (2023). There was one business survey in the same period, and the general census of enterprises dates from 2016. ANSD catalogues the microdata of its surveys and censuses in a national data archive (2026). In January 2026 it signed a protocol with ANEC for automated exchange of birth, marriage and death data.

In July 2026 the environment ministry named persistent gaps in the availability, quality, harmonisation and sharing of climate data. It said they limit the country's ability to show what its policies achieve and to mobilise climate finance. Senegal's project document records capacity insufficient to comply with the Enhanced Transparency Framework of the Paris Agreement (2025). The country has filed three national communications and one biennial update report, and technically validated its third nationally determined contribution. CBIT-Sénégal, funded by the Global Environment Facility with UNEP support, launched in Dakar in July 2026. It is to build an integrated national platform centralising, securing and publishing climate data, from greenhouse-gas emissions to loss and damage.

Open data

Senegal adopted an access to information law, Loi n° 2025-15, in August 2025, which ARTICLE 19 welcomed as a transparency measure. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index scored the country 53.7 out of 100.0 for accessibility of public records, 7th of 54 and up 12.4 points over the decade. It scored 33.9 for disclosure of public records, 26th of 54 and down 2.8.

The 2024 Open Data Inventory scores Senegal 8 out of 10 for banking, national accounts and price statistics and 7.5 for balance of payments. It scores 5 for trade and 4 for labour. Health facilities and reproductive health score 8.5 and education facilities 8, against 6 for gender statistics. Population statistics and education outcomes score 5.5 each. Natural resources score 8, agriculture and pollution 7, energy 6.5 and the built environment 6.

In 2025 the World Bank found that the open data portal's catalogue holds basic information and datasets, not a comprehensive collection. Compliance with the data protection regime is not monitored, and no compliance reporting is published. A full inventory of the state's information systems was launched at the Government Enterprise Architecture workshop of 29 July 2026. No scope, deadline or publication commitment attaches to it.

Across every part of the GovTech estate the index examines, nothing is published: ten disclosure and transparency sub-indicators — covering cloud hosting, interoperability, the service portal, procurement, debt, the digital transformation strategy, data governance, digital skills, the GovTech institution and the data protection regime — are all recorded as absent for 2025, and the eleventh, the data protection authority's own documentation, exists only internally.

Geospatial data

The statistics agency signed a five-year agreement with Digital Earth Africa to calculate cultivated area, crop types and yields from satellite data (February 2026). The ecological monitoring centre publishes a recurring agro-pastoral campaign bulletin analysing the vegetation index against a 1999-2022 baseline (2023). The AgData Hub agro-climatic platform has run on the meteorological agency's servers since its migration in September and October 2025. A rice remote-sensing platform was piloted with the national agricultural research institute (October 2025). The public-private agricultural insurer sold satellite weather-index cover to 612,000 clients in 2022. These are standing uses of satellite data in agriculture. Senegal had no national space or geospatial agency and no policy on Earth observation as at August 2026. Satellite imagery had no established role in the cadastre or in disaster response.

A WMO gap analysis found no Senegalese surface station reporting to the global basic observing network (2023). The national contribution plan of October 2024 upgrades five surface stations and adds two, at Kaffrine and Kidira. An investment request of US$2.52m for the meteorological agency's observing network runs from December 2026 to November 2031.

China activities

A strategic accord signed with Huawei in June 2025, during Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko's visit to China, puts modernisation of Senegal's data centres first. That modernisation covers performance, cybersecurity and green technology. The accord also provides for a domestic plant assembling IT equipment. Talks in March 2025 had ranged wider, over 5G, a sovereign cloud and low-cost smartphones to widen device ownership.

From 2007 three phases of an e-government programme financed by China Eximbank laid about 1,500 km of fibre optic cable across the regional capitals. They also built the government intranet and its data centres, implemented by Huawei and CMEC. Smart Senegal followed on 8 February 2018. Its first phase funded Safe City surveillance, Smart Education and "Maisons du Citoyen" in every department. It also funded a submarine cable landing and public wifi against the digital isolation of territories. Its second phase is restructuring and expanding the Diamniadio national data centre. Huawei built that Tier 3 facility under Chinese financing, and it was inaugurated in June 2021. It is to centralise government data and platforms previously hosted on foreign servers.

After the centre opened in June 2021, President Macky Sall instructed the government to migrate all state data and digital platforms to it. He also instructed it to repatriate national data hosted outside Senegal. Georgetown's Africa-China Initiative reads this as replicating China's model of housing all servers within national borders. Its researchers read the outcome as sovereignty of place, not of technology. The facility was financed by a Chinese policy bank and built by a Chinese vendor on whom its operation continues to depend.

Alibaba built a private cloud on its Apsara Stack that hosts the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games' ticketing and e-visa processing (November 2026). It is to pass to Senegal after the Games as a national cloud platform, with training for Senegalese cloud engineers attached. Beyond Senegal's borders, China handed over the ECOWAS headquarters building in Abuja on 28 April 2026. It is the second international-organisation complex China has built in Africa, after the African Union Conference Centre.

It is now a gateway linking Eutelsat OneWeb's low-earth-orbit constellation to Senegal's terrestrial networks. It digitised the civil register and interconnected civil-status centres, with the stated aim of improving citizens' access to rights and the evidence base for public policy.

GOIN'Digital runs from 2023 to 2029 on governance of the digital sector, its legal framework and feasibility studies for last-mile connectivity. On 26 March 2026 Senegal and Finland opened cooperation on cybersecurity, resilient digital infrastructure, data-centre modernisation and AI-ready infrastructure. It is framed under the New Deal Technologique and the EU's Global Gateway, with misinformation and media literacy among the topics. In March 2025 a French ministerial visit to DER/FJ opened cooperation on technology transfer and funding for digital entrepreneurs.

In August 2026 Senegal was engaging the Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group, convened by the OECD and Japan, as the vehicle for its national AI positioning. The discussion covered trustworthy-AI governance, compute infrastructure, innovation financing and international partnerships.

Digital sovereignty

Senegal's National Assembly adopted law n° 25/2026 unanimously on 20 August 2026. Its bill requires data held by state services, or by private partners on a public-service mission, to be hosted and processed in Senegal (July 2026). It allows temporary storage abroad only by regulatory act for a stated period, and makes hosting abroad in breach of that rule an offence. In August 2026 the minister told the examining committees that exceptions are provided because of the country's technological dependence. Onshore storage will come from state facilities and from private cloud providers approved by the national cybersecurity authority. Hosting rules for other data will be set by decree. The commissioners asked what guarantees cover sensitive data entrusted to the foreign firms that make identity cards and driving licences.

State hosting runs across three SENUM data centres: Orana, built in 2004, Technopole, built in 2008, and Diamniadio, built in 2021. The civil-status register's data is to be backed up at SENUM's data centre (August 2026). On 24 March 2026 the ministry took formal delivery of a national cloud built by Alibaba Cloud on its Apsara Stack product, under a contract signed in Hangzhou on 28 July 2025. It is to host the Youth Olympic Games' applications and then become a national public cloud open to the public and private sectors.

On 8 September 2026 the prime minister's general policy statement made a sovereign cloud hosting the state's sensitive data on Senegalese soil the central axis of the digital programme. It treats the state's data and essential systems as national-defence assets. No terms on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had been published for four agreements as at September 2026. They are the Iris Corporation identity contract, the Alibaba cloud contract, the June 2025 Huawei accord and the October 2024 Google memorandum.
