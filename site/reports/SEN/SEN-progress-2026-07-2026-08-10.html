Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 102 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 56 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-06, 18 did not, 16 carry no stated baseline, and 12 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 145 sources for this place in the window — 27 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 118 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Civil registration produced the window's clearest movement: deployment of the civil-status software went from 235 of about 400 centres connected in August 2025 to 437 of 629 in July 2026 — on a denominator that itself changed — while the archive position did not move from 20m acts of a 30m universe. The externally funded programme behind it closed on completion in June 2026.

The position that worsened is security. Held state breaches went from one to three — the tax authority, the identity directorate and the treasury — with no technical accounting published for any, and the country still has no national response team.

The money moved on paper. The digital-sector appropriation went from FCFA 16.1bn for FY2024 to FCFA 31.1bn for FY2025 and the ministry vote from FCFA 58.4bn to FCFA 81.06bn for FY2026, but the FY2026 figure is reported as set rather than tabled or enacted, and no execution or outturn exists for any year.

International capacity moved from built to working. The 2Africa landing station went from built-but-not-in-service to in service with sixteen fibre pairs and 180 Tbps, and a teleport that had held no low-orbit gateway gained sixteen antennas and a Tier III room. Fixed broadband rose to 1.16m subscribers with more than 640,000 fibre-to-the-home connections.

Satellite access went from no authorisation framework and no live service to a five-year renewable authorisation carrying local-supervision and data-sovereignty clauses, with the regulator enforcing against unauthorised resale. The state programme built on it slipped: from no free-connectivity programme at all to a tour distributing a first 200 of 1,000 kits and a one-million-beneficiary target moved from the first half of 2026 to the year's end, with no beneficiary count published.

Financing appeared where none had been: a senior facility of up to EUR 270m, EUR 170m committed, and an announced US$150m tower-operator intent with no signed agreement behind it. Domestic hosting did not move at all: the sovereign-cloud memorandum of October 2024, the Dakar data centre under construction since January 2025, the national data centre and the exchange reactivation all hold last year's position.

The one position that regressed is the breach count, against an index score unchanged at 67.17 out of 100 on the 2024 edition, which predates all three incidents.

Civil registration moved on deployment and stood still on stock. Centres went from 235 of about 400 to 437 of 629, regional spread running from 98% to 42%, and 15.3m records are now loaded in the live register — a subset of archive stock, not a replacement for it. The archive figure of 20m acts against 30m did not move. The programme closed in June 2026 reporting 26 modern centres, 400 centres interconnected, 1,200 computers, a validated bill and a national civil-status strategy costed at FCFA 68bn; the bill it validated is still not adopted, and its decree unsigned.

Card production went out and came back: suspended by ransomware in February 2026, issuing again from 1 April.

Service delivery advanced and the exchange layer beneath it did not. A citizen single window became operational in March 2026, eight regulator platforms went live, and digital settlement on the Dakar rapid-transit network went from 10% to 39%. Against that, the interoperability platform launched in April 2025 with a first phase targeted for end-2025 is still an intention rather than a capability, with silos named by the state itself.

The fiscal stamp shows both ends of the same move: the digital stamp went from not existing to being the sole recognised channel, and a six-year-old free service closed in the same week.

The instrument that governs personal data did not move: Loi n° 2008-12 remains in force and unamended through a year that included the breach of the national identity directorate. What moved is the reform around it, from in the circuit with no text or timetable to a finalisation workshop for the bill and its implementing decree, still with no adoption date. The protection commission also signed a cooperation convention with Benin's authority on five pillars, with no workplan or first joint action reported since.

Institutions were created faster than they were operated. The national digital council went from twenty members named to inaugurated with no published output; a steering committee created in March 2026 was still described as operational in the near future in August.

Two instruments narrowed and widened disclosure in the same window. Décret n° 2026-807 came into force in April 2026, making contracts concerning critical information infrastructure a ground for defence classification with silence counting as refusal; meanwhile Senegal joined 29 founding states of an intergovernmental AI-governance body and adopted non-binding regional AI governance guidelines. A draft law on a media and social-network regulator has not moved since January 2025.
