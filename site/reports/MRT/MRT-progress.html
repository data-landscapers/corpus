Mauritania: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-17

Edition of 2026-08-18  ·  58 systems and instruments tracked, 4 of them not held
↓ Download PDF
Compiled 2026-08-17 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 58 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 28 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-17, 16 did not, 10 carry no stated baseline, and 4 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 47 sources for this place in the window — 10 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-03), 37 in the later (2026-04 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

This was a year of things reaching service. A national cloud opened in the Tier III data centre; four 5G licences were awarded and a fourth operator entered the market; a second submarine cable completed its coastal landing; the Ijraati and consular portals launched; and every operator switched IPv6 on.

Two positions moved the other way, and both are about the instruments rather than the systems. The National Digital Transformation Agenda's period ran out during this window with no successor held, and the Universal Service Access Fund was regoverned on stated grounds that its object, its financing and its budgetary treatment had never been settled. Neither the replacing decree nor the one it repeals can be read: both national gazette hosts return NXDOMAIN.

The identity layer added scale rather than reach. The register rose and the application's usage figures rose with it, and beneath both the national public key infrastructure went to tender twice extended and remains unawarded.

The cable position advanced but did not change: ACE remains the only cable carrying the country. EllaLink went from a project launched at Nouadhibou in July 2025 to a completed phase-1 coastal landing in May 2026, with offshore laying scheduled for August 2026 and commissioning expected in the first quarter of 2027 — so the single-cable exposure that opened the period closes it too. The financed MEDUSA sub-Saharan component has no landing on record at either end.

Mobile is where the market structure changed. It opened the window with three licensed operators and closes it with four fifteen-year 5G licences awarded to the three incumbents and the new entrant Rimatel, and with Chinguitel — which had completed a network modernisation with Huawei in November 2025 — live on 5G in Nouakchott from May 2026. Satellite went from nothing to a tender opened in January 2026 and three firms provisionally selected in July, with no award or operating framework.

Hosting is the clearest gain. The Tier III data centre was already inaugurated when the period opened; the national cloud launched into it in June 2026, giving the state a domestic place to put government data. No migration timeline, capacity or cost was disclosed with it, so what has actually moved onto it cannot be stated.

IPv6 went from a strategy with no reported operator activation to full activation across every licensed operator, with subscriber migration in the teens against a 25% end-2026 target. Cybersecurity moved the other way: the 2022–2025 strategy's period expired inside the window with no successor and no CERT, and the only operational figure the year produced is the Central Bank's threat-detection count.

National statistics

Identity is the busiest layer and the one where the underlying trust infrastructure is furthest behind. The National Population Register moved from a reported coverage claim to a stated total of 5.1m against 3.4m in 2019, with enrolment made a permanent public service and 238 local commissions installed — a reform whose existence records that a residual of citizens still has no papers. Houwiyeti reported 677,000 activated accounts and 1,511,139 beneficiaries by June 2026, its share of passport requests rising through the period to 35.7% by January 2026. Against that, the national PKI went from not existing to tendered, twice extended and unawarded, and biometric SIM enforcement moved from an unenforced 2023 deactivation order to formal notices with sanctions attached and no reported compliance outcome.

Government services were consolidated rather than multiplied. Ijraati launched in February 2026 with more than 800 procedures, the consular platform in July, and the water utility was attached to the Mes Services portal in August rather than given its own. That is an architectural choice, and its value depends on how many utilities follow; none is named.

The measurement that puts all of this in proportion arrived at the end of the window. Of 642 legally constituted public bodies, 339 are active with the tax administration, and 3 pay through the electronic platform. No comparable figure exists for the start of the period, so this is a reading rather than a trend — but it measures the state against a system the state itself runs.

Payments delivered and were taxed in the same window. PAMIF is reported by its financier as having taken financial inclusion from 21% in 2019–20 to 55% in 2026, and the 2026 finance law introduced a 0.1% levy on electronic transactions above MRU 5,000. Statistics gained a first National Youth Empowerment Index at a preliminary 60.5 out of 100.

Open data

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-17 Movement
National open data portal Baseline not held mauritania.opendataforafrica.org, run by ANSADE, carries 24 downloadable datasets from 4 public providers — last of four benchmarked countries (Mauritania, Morocco, Senegal, Tunisia) on every published measure — with no Open Government Partnership membership; a June 2023 strategy proposes a new governance framework, portal redesign and reviving the decree creating an Agence Numérique de l'État as operator, none of which is reported delivered Baseline not held

Nothing that governs the digital estate got easier to read during this period, and one thing got harder. The Universal Service Access Fund was governed by Decree 2020-152 when the window opened and is now governed by a decree whose terms are not published, adopted on grounds that the 2020 arrangement had never defined the fund's object, its resource allocation or its budgetary treatment. Both gazette hosts return NXDOMAIN, so neither text can be obtained.

The National Digital Transformation Agenda 2022–2025 expired inside the window with no successor held. The data protection authority is in the same position at both ends — recorded as operating under a 2023–2026 plan by a foreign commercial guide, with no decision, register or report of its own on file.

What did advance is standards. The IPv6 strategy is the state's one written obligation on itself in this area, and a framework for public digital service standards entered development in March 2026 with German support and has published nothing. The memorandum with Senegal is where it was.

Financing was where the period was busiest. The Islamic Development Bank agreed US$50m in principle in April 2026, which did not exist a year earlier and is still not a signed instrument; the World Bank's WARDIP programme delivered the national cloud and is still running the unawarded PKI procurement; and Digital-Y and the EIB cable financing ran unchanged. The one domestic figure to appear in the window is the digital ministry's 2026 budget at US$24.2m, up 104.6%.

None of these closed during the period. Two of them are documents that exist and cannot be reached, because the national gazette's two hosts do not resolve — the data protection law and the universal-access fund decrees. That is a publisher failure, and it is why a country with a data protection authority has no enforcement record here.

The rest stood throughout: the Startup Act and the IBTIKAR technology park, both named in the 2023 digital agenda and neither given a dated status since; no open-data portal; no municipal or regional digital service against two national portals; and no measurement of digital literacy, research capacity or the power the national cloud depends on.
