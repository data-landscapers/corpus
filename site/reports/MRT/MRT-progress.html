Mauritania: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-11  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-10.

Progress values. Movement — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Movement, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy The National Digital Transformation Agenda 2022-2025 has run out its period, and no successor agenda is held.
Full recordThe agenda set four axes and its period has since ended, with no successor, evaluation or extension on record (agenda).
The pattern repeats across this unit: the cybersecurity strategy and the university research accreditation both expired in the same way, so what the base records is a set of instruments that ran their term and were not replaced.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The national digital transformation agenda for 2022-2025 has run out, and preparation of a 2030 successor began in December 2025.
Full recordThe 2022 agenda is now held as the ministry's own document, carrying a vision, objectives, strategic orientations, implementation levers and project sheets (agenda).
The ministry announced on 4 December 2025 that work on a National Digital Transformation Agenda 2030 had begun (successor).
No implementation report, budget outturn or project-by-project status against the 2022 agenda's sheets is published, so a four-year strategy closed without the base holding what it delivered.
 Movementthe strategy period expired with a successor only in preparation
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy A broadband promotion and universal access strategy was completed in February 2019 with an action plan beside it, and no successor or implementation report is held.
Full recordThe strategy volume sets quantified coverage and capacity objectives, market segments and an indicative budget, and calls for a National Broadband Plan to be put in place; it proposes regulatory action on the wholesale market, a wholesale catalogue and a tariff structure (strategy, action plan).
Seven years on, the base holds no adoption instrument, no implementation report and no successor, and nothing establishes whether the National Broadband Plan it calls for was ever made.
 Stalled
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy The national cloud launched in June 2026 over the tier-three data centre, and the regulator took up hosting and certification.
Full recordMauritania now has the estate and is writing the rules over it. The national cloud platform was launched in June 2026 and presented to the national assembly the following month, running on the tier-three data centre of 1,372 square metres and 100 racks inaugurated in May 2025.
No consolidated storage or cloud strategy document is held. What governs hosting is the data-protection authority's practice: its June 2026 monthly meeting took up data hosting and the certification of the data centre, after deliberations of 2025 on foreign cloud services. The rule is arriving through the regulator rather than through a strategy.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap The administrative procedures portal launched in February 2026 with a stated three-phase roadmap ending in integration into the government services platform.
Full recordThe clearest cross-government roadmap of the window came with a portal launch. At the 16 February 2026 launch of the national portal of administrative procedures, the minister set out three phases: centralise and publish every procedure on one national platform, keep them updated and reliable in coordination with each sector, then fully digitise the most-used procedures and integrate them into the government services platform. The portal centralises the procedures it publishes on one address.
Two other tracks bear on it: a national framework for public digital services standards, and a national data management system being worked through blockchain workshops in the public administration.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy A National AI Strategy 2025-2029 is recorded as existing by a reference guide, and its text is not held.
Full recordThe strategy is recorded as existing and nothing establishes its scope, commitments, budget or implementing body (strategy).
No draft, adoption instrument, workshop or consultation for it appears anywhere else in the base, so the country's artificial-intelligence position is a title in somebody else's directory.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies Using cloud services hosted abroad for personal data requires prior authorisation from the data protection authority under the amended deliberation of April 2026.
Full recordThe amended text of deliberation 005/2025, published 30 April 2026, requires public and private bodies using cloud services abroad to obtain prior authorisation for the processing, a separate transfer authorisation where the host country is not on the approved list, and contractual guarantees covering compliance with the 2017 law, a data-transfer clause, return of the data on contract expiry and its permanent deletion by the provider (deliberation).
The authority has also taken up homologation of the national data centre at its monthly meeting (hosting). No general localisation requirement, sector rule or list of authorisations granted is held, and the approved-country list the regime turns on is not published.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy Governance runs through the regulator's deliberations rather than a policy: transfers abroad and foreign cloud in 2025, hosting and certification in 2026.
Full recordThere is no single national data governance policy. What performs the function is a sequence of deliberations by the data-protection authority under the 2017 personal data protection law: a 2025 deliberation on transfers abroad with a list of adequate countries, another on foreign cloud services as later amended, and monthly meetings taking up data hosting and data-centre certification and interconnection between the population register and other registers.
The authority works to its own strategic plan for 2023 to 2026. Alongside it sits a 2023 national strategy for open and interoperable data.
 Movement
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy A 2023 open and interoperable data strategy with a thin portal, an electronic gazette portal since November 2025, and systematic extractives disclosure.
Full recordThe policy exists and the publishing behind it is thin. A national strategy for open and interoperable data was adopted in 2023, and the portal it provides for carries little.
Two narrower channels do publish. The electronic gazette portal launched in November 2025 puts the official journal online. And extractive-industry data is released under a 2019 decree requiring systematic disclosure, which the transparency initiative's board scored as moderate against its standard in 2024. A national data portal is among the procurement commitments of the digital programme.
 Movement
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation The data protection law has been in force since July 2017, requiring approved codes of conduct and free, unambiguous consent, with no enforcement decision published.
Full recordThe statute covers both manual and electronic processing and requires codes of conduct approved by the authority and free, unambiguous consent (law).
The authority runs monthly ordinary meetings and sensitisation days for ministries, telecom operators and financial institutions, and no enforcement decision, register entry, sanction or annual report is published (authority). The held extraction of the law cuts off mid-article, so the chapters covering the authority's powers and sanctions are not captured.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation The cybercrime law of 2016 is the instrument in force, its text now held; no amendment is on the record.
Full recordLaw n. 2016-007 defines computer data, subscriber data and traffic data and sets the offences and procedure for cybercrime (law).
Against it, a national emergency response team and security operations centre were put out to international tender in September 2025 and no award is on the record.
The base holds no prosecution count, case record or amendment, so the statute is dated and its use is not.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The civil status code of 2011 created the national population register, and law 2025-011 replaced certain of its provisions in February 2025.
Full recordThe 2011 law repealed and replaced the 1996 code and is the instrument behind the national population register (code); law n. 2025-011 of 24 February 2025 repeals and replaces certain of its provisions (amendment).
Both texts are held in Arabic and the base does not hold a consolidated version, so which provisions the 2025 law changed is not established from the record.
 Movement
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation Law 2021-014 on electronic payment services and means has been in force since July 2021 and is the statute the central bank's payment instructions are issued under.
Full recordThe Official Gazette text of 30 July 2021 covers definitions, electronic payment services and means, payment cards and the contestation of a card payment (law, text).
The held capture is an excerpt cut off partway through Titre III, so the later articles the central bank's own instruction cites are not on file, and no implementing decree, licensing decision or amendment to the statute is held.
 No changea statute in force since 2021 that the base has only now acquired
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability Decree 2022-009 governs electronic exchanges between users and administrations and between administrations, under the electronic transactions law of 2018.
Full recordThe decree was made on the digital ministry's report under law n. 2018-022 of 12 June 2018 on electronic transactions, and is the legal footing the service portals rest on (decree).
No implementing arrangement, register of compliant administrations or evaluation is held,; the national data management system it would serve is still in development, and the base holds no register of administrations exchanging under it.
 No change
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No statute or regulation addressed to artificial intelligence. What exists is a strategy and a sector framework for schools.
Full recordMauritania governs artificial intelligence by policy rather than by law. The national strategy for 2024 to 2029 is the governing document, and the one sectoral instrument beneath it is the national framework for artificial intelligence in the republican school, adopted in January 2026.
Nothing on the base is a statute, a decree or a regulator's binding rule reaching artificial intelligence. Where synthetic content is addressed it is addressed by training: a programme with the audiovisual regulator on deepfakes and synthetic media for journalists in July 2026. The general data-protection statute is the only law that would reach automated processing.
 No change
Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation The transport regulator gave ride-hailing applications fifteen days to bring their authorisations into line in August 2026, warning of suspension.
Full recordThe communique warned that failure within the deadline could bring legal proceedings including suspension of the activity, with no further notice or warning required, and set the warning against growing use of digital platforms for transport services (communique).
No operator is named and no count of non-compliant authorisations is given. It is the base's only record of platform activity being regulated in the country, and it reaches the platforms through transport law rather than through any digital statute.
 Movement
Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation No statistics statute is held. The system runs on the decadal statistics strategy for 2021 to 2030 and the continental charter.
Full recordNo statistics law, ordinance or decree constituting the statistical system appears on this base. What governs it is a strategy and a treaty commitment: the decadal national strategy for statistical development covering 2021 to 2030, and the country's position on the continental charter on statistics.
The institution beneath them is being reorganised by decree rather than by statute: the Council of Ministers appointed the statistics agency's orientation council in March 2026 in the same sitting as the archives bill. The agency publishes to that mandate, including the preliminary poverty results of the 2025 household survey.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation A bill on access to and use of public information is on the record from June 2026, presented as making good a presidential commitment.
Full recordA press-freedom organisation welcomed the bill and asked for strengthened guarantees (bill).
Nothing from the government or parliament on it is on file: no text, tabling date or parliamentary step. An advocacy organisation's welcome is the whole of what the base holds, which is the position this row records.
 Movement
Data protection Data protection authority The data protection authority is established and operating under a 2023-2026 plan, with no decision, register or annual report held.
Full recordIts existence and plan are recorded by a reference guide (guide).
Nine years after the statute, the base holds no decision, no register of declared processing, no enforcement action and no annual report from it, so what can be established is that the institution exists and not that it acts.
 No change
Data protection National data protection readiness The 2017 data protection law is being issued under: deliberations of 2025 set rules for transfers abroad and for foreign cloud services.
Full recordDeliberation 004/2025 sets the conditions and procedural rules for transferring personal data abroad with a list of countries treated as adequate (transfers), and the amended text of deliberation 005/2025, published 30 April 2026, requires prior authorisation for processing personal data through cloud services abroad and contractual guarantees covering return of the data on expiry and its permanent deletion by the provider (cloud).
The authority meets monthly with minutes issued, working on the inspection framework and its sworn agents (inspections) and on hosting inside the country (hosting), against a published strategic plan for 2023-2026.
No budget, staffing, caseload or sanction figure is published, so activity is dated and capacity is not. The amended text does not state which articles changed from the original.
 Movement
Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration A digital cooperation memorandum with Senegal was signed in January 2025, with no deliverable, workstream or funding under it on record.
Full recordThe memorandum was signed in January 2025 and nothing has followed it into the base (memorandum).
It is the only bilateral instrument with a neighbour on file. The country's other recorded international digital engagements run to the Gulf and to European financiers rather than along its own borders.
 No change
Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation The 1989 Maghreb Union treaty is in force with a binding judicial organ and no summit since 1994.
Full recordThe treaty concluded at Marrakesh in February 1989 has been in force since 1 July 1989 and establishes a presidential council, a council of foreign ministers, a general secretariat, a consultative council and a judicial organ whose rulings bind.
No summit has been held since 1994, so the instrument supplies a legal frame for harmonisation and no working machinery. Harmonisation between Maghreb states therefore proceeds bilaterally where it proceeds at all.
 No change
Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure A vendor with two European donors was named for Sahel digital connectivity support in June 2026, with no site, capacity or delivery figure for the country published.
Full recordThe announcement names an equipment vendor working with two European government funders on Sahel connectivity (announcement).
No Mauritanian site, capacity or delivery figure is published, so the country appears in a regional programme whose national share cannot be established.
 Movement
Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers The data protection authority set the conditions and procedural rules for transferring personal data abroad, with a list of countries treated as adequate.
Full recordDeliberation 004/2025 is the instrument (transfers); a companion deliberation sets minimum conditions for using cloud services hosted abroad (cloud), and both were taken up again at the authority's monthly meeting of April 2026 (meeting).
The adequate-country list itself is not published, and no authorisation, refusal or transfer volume under the regime is on the record.
 Movement
Standards National interoperability standards A public digital service standards framework is under development with German support, and the state agency proposed to operate interoperability has never been created.
Full recordA three-day standards and interoperability workshop was run by the ministry in March 2026 with German support; no framework or standard has been published (workshop).
The agency intended to operate national interoperability was proposed in July 2021 as a public establishment under the digital ministry with about fifty staff, EUR 220,000 to set up and EUR 1.4m a year to run, with a draft creation decree (proposal). A June 2023 open-data strategy proposed reviving that decree to name the same body national interoperability operator, and no adoption, decree or launch is on record (strategy).
 Movement
Standards National quality standards Decree 2023-113 created a national standardisation council and an accreditation committee and set the terms for accrediting conformity assessment bodies.
Full recordThe decree of 7 September 2023 creates the National Standardisation and Quality Promotion Council and the Mauritanian Accreditation Committee (decree).
No membership, meeting record, accredited body or published standard is held for either institution, so the instrument exists and nothing on the record shows it working.
 Movement
Standards Adoption of international standards All licensed operators have activated IPv6, with one past 15 per cent of subscribers migrated against the strategy target of 25 per cent by the end of 2026.
Full recordA sixty-eight page national transition strategy set a target of 25 per cent adoption by 2026 with a national transition committee (strategy); it carries no printed adoption date and its economic-impact figures are lifted from a consultancy report rather than measured (strategy).
By July 2026 all licensed operators had activated IPv6 across networks and services, with one unnamed operator past 15 per cent of subscribers migrated (activation). The target is the clearest measurable commitment the country has made on a technical standard, and it is on course to be missed.
 Movement
Public debate and participation in policymaking Non-governmental contribution to national policy Civil society contributed to the fourth-cycle universal periodic review, and a validation workshop settled the electronic commerce strategy.
Full recordContribution here happens where the state convenes it or where an international process requires it. The fourth-cycle universal periodic review drew observations from the national human rights commission on the implementing decrees of the disability law, alongside the state's own national report and a contribution from the education and culture agency.
Domestically the route is the validation workshop: the national electronic commerce strategy was put through one in October 2025, and the continuing training policy through another in May 2026. Both are ministry-convened, and neither publishes who contributed or what changed.
 Movement
Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy The independent score fell to 38 of 100, with free expression down to 1 of 4; deputies questioned the government on media plurality in June.
Full recordThe scored assessment went down. The 2026 country report puts the country at 38 of 100 and partly free, down from 39, with the sub-question on expressing personal views on sensitive topics without fear of surveillance or retribution scored 1 of 4, down from 2, the decline attributed to intensified use of the 2021 law on the protection of national symbols against online dissent. Free and independent media stands at 2 of 4 and government openness at 1 of 4.
Against that, contestation did happen and was reported. At a public sitting on 18 June 2026 two deputies put oral questions with debate to the government spokesman on whether public media reflect political plurality, and on irregularities in the regularisation of media staff; the minister answered that 1,860 collaborators had been made permanent.
 Mixedan independent score fell while policy was contested openly in parliament

Finance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Domestic budget appropriations and expenditure Sustainable domestic financing of digital transformation The digital ministry's budget reached US$24.2m for 2026, up 104.6 per cent, while the universal access fund decree was repealed for unclear object and resource allocation.
Full recordThe ministry's 2026 budget is US$24.2m, up 104.6 per cent on the previous year (budget).
The Council of Ministers approved a decree in July 2026 repealing the fund's 2020 governing decree, on grounds that the fund's object, its resource mobilisation and allocation, and its integration into the state budgetary system were all unclear; the new terms are not published and neither decree is held (repeal).
So the domestic money for digital transformation grew sharply in the same year the instrument for spending it on universal access was found not to work.
 Mixedthe ministry's budget more than doubling while the universal access fund's governing decree was repealed as unclear
MoUs and other agreements Strategic relationships Five memoranda were signed with Oman in July 2026, after a US$50 million digital financing commitment in April.
Full recordThe window added two relationships and reactivated a third. A business forum with Oman in July 2026 produced five memoranda on digitisation, preceded by a ministerial meeting with the Omani delegation. The Islamic development bank committed US$50 million to digital financing in April 2026.

 Movement
New investments Mobilisation of non-state finance The digital programme was extended to artificial intelligence and data in June 2026, alongside a US$50 million commitment and a new investment code.
Full recordAlmost all the money on this base is public and external. The regional digital programme is the largest channel and it grew in June 2026 with an extension covering artificial intelligence and data; a development bank committed US$50 million to digital financing in April; and European lending financed both the data centre and the second submarine cable connection.
What would mobilise private money is newer and untested: the 2025 investment code with its single-window implementing decree, and the startup act. No private or blended investment in a digital undertaking is named on the base.
 Movement
New investments Development-partner project financing A regional digital programme financed the national cloud, a US$30m reform grant was disbursed, and US$50m more was agreed in principle in April 2026.
Full recordThe regional digital programme financed the national cloud launched in June 2026, and its national public key infrastructure procurement remains unawarded (programme, appraisal).
A US$30m grant was disbursed in 2021 as the second of three planned private sector, digital and human capital reform operations, and no third operation is on record (grant, operation).
An agreement in principle for US$50m covering digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, interoperability and skills was reached in April 2026 (agreement); no signed instrument, disbursement or schedule is held (agreement, record).
The data centre behind the cloud was financed separately: a EUR 15m European Investment Bank loan, the facility inaugurated on 8 May 2025 within the World Bank co-financed regional connectivity programme (loan).

ICT Infrastructure

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Connectivity Mobile penetration Four fifteen-year 5G licences were awarded in 2026 and the first commercial network launched at Nouakchott in August, with no coverage or subscriber figure published.
Full recordLicences went to the three incumbents and a new entrant at bids of MRU 252.6m to 305m plus 5 per cent of revenue, breaking the three-operator structure (licences).
One operator launched the country's first commercial 5G network at Nouakchott on 10 August 2026 under the digital transformation minister; no coverage footprint, tariff or subscriber figure is published (launch, account).
The same operator had completed a full network modernisation at Nouakchott with a foreign vendor in November 2025, again with no coverage, capacity or subscriber figure published (modernisation).
 Movement
Connectivity Internet usage Use is measured through the financial inclusion and connectivity survey and the regulator's market observatory, not by a national survey of individuals.
Full recordTwo published series bear on how many people use the internet. The 2025 financial inclusion survey carries connectivity alongside account ownership, and the telecommunications union's development dashboard carries the infrastructure and access series. The regulator publishes subscriptions through its market observatory and annual report.
None of them is a national survey of individuals using the internet, and nothing in the window updates the position. What did move is supply: coverage extended to southern and south-eastern border zones in December 2025 and three satellite service providers selected in July 2026.
 No change
Connectivity Mobile affordability No price series is published. The regulator's market observatory is where one would appear; the statistics agency publishes a communication price index.
Full recordNo tariff basket, income-share measure or affordability index for Mauritania is held on this base. The two places a figure would come from are published and do not carry one: the regulator's market observatory and annual report, and the statistics agency's monthly bulletin, which carries a communication price index rather than a retail tariff.
What the window did add is a cost imposed rather than a price charged: the 2026 finance law introduced a tax on electronic transactions. Affordability of connectivity and affordability of using it are moving in different directions, and only one of them is measured.
 No change
Connectivity National fibre backbone The national infrastructure company operates more than 1,760 km of fibre on three axes, and a regional programme's backbone loan closed in 2022.
Full recordThe kilometre figure is the company's own, given at the inauguration of its headquarters in December 2025, with no route map, capacity, utilisation or wholesale tariff beside it (network).
The regional communications infrastructure programme's second loan closed with an implementation completion report covering the national backbone it financed (completion).
The relationship between the two - what the programme built and what the company now runs - is not established by anything on file.
 Movement
Connectivity International internet bandwidth The one international cable since 2011 is to be joined by a second commissioning in early 2027, with a third financed and unlanded.
Full recordA single submarine cable has been the country's only international connection since 2011 (cable, baseline).
The second cable was launched at Nouadhibou in July 2025 at 669 km and 200 Gb/s rising to 12 Tbit/s, European-financed; phase-one coastal landing is complete, offshore laying was scheduled for August 2026 and commissioning is expected in the first quarter of 2027 (landing, launch).
A third system's sub-Saharan component is financed with no landing, route or commissioning date on record (financing). A country dependent on one cable for fifteen years is the position the other two are meant to end.
 Movement
Connectivity Internet Exchange Points The base holds a 2014 feasibility study for a national exchange point and nothing since saying whether one was established.
Full recordThe final study was completed under the regional connectivity project in June 2014, setting out the state of the internet sector and its market data (study).
Twelve years on, nothing on the record says whether an exchange point exists. A published operator listing or peering record would settle it, and the row is carried as a dated absence rather than as a finding either way.
 No change
Connectivity Satellite broadband licensing and availability Three firms were provisionally selected for satellite internet licences across two lots in July 2026, with no award or operating framework published.
Full recordThe tender opened in January 2026 and nine bids were received, from which three firms were provisionally retained, two of them required to match the leading offer (selection, tender).
Thirteen firms took the dossier, and the leading bid was US$800,000 for the mobile lot and US$3m for capacity against US$50,000 and MRU 6.5m from the other two (bids).
The regulator's own communique is not held and the figures reach the base through a trade account of it. No award, launch date or operating framework has been published.

Data Storage Local data centre capacity (all providers) The Nouakchott data centre runs to 1,372 m2 and 100 server racks, and the national cloud was inaugurated on it in June 2026.
Full recordThe facility was inaugurated by the President on 8 May 2025 as the first national installation of its kind, presented as certified to international standards and stated as extensible (inauguration).
The national cloud was inaugurated at it on 29 June 2026, the minister telling parliament three days later that its launch removes the need to host state services internationally (cloud, statement).
The certification is asserted rather than evidenced, no tier rating or operator is named, and utilisation, customers and load are unpublished. A backup data centre was specified under the earlier connectivity programme and nothing says whether it was built (specification).
 Movement
Data Storage Local data centre capacity (national providers) A EUR 15m Tier III national data centre has been in service since May 2025, and a national cloud launched on it in June 2026.
Full recordThe data centre was inaugurated on 8 May 2025, European-financed at EUR 15m and certified, operated by the international telecoms company (data centre); no capacity, utilisation or migration figure has been published since (data centre, construction).
The national cloud launched in June 2026 to host government data and services domestically and centralise public information technology, with no migration timeline, capacity or cost disclosed; as at August 2026 it was in data-security testing and migration preparation ahead of go-live (launch, testing).
 Movement
Data Storage Off-site backup capacity One data centre and a backup station specified back in 2021. No second operating site is reported.
Full recordThe estate is one facility: the tier-three data centre of 1,372 square metres and 100 racks, inaugurated in May 2025, now carrying the national cloud.
A backup site was specified long before it. The regional connectivity programme's tender documents of February 2021 carry station specifications for a backup data centre, and nothing since reports one built or operating. What the window does record is oversight of the primary site: the data-protection authority's June 2026 meeting took up hosting and the data centre's certification. Energy Sufficient energy and water for data centres The government's own annual infrastructure note covers electricity access, generation and the capital's water programmes, and publishes no reliability indicator.
Full recordThe single official document covering energy, water and digital infrastructure together is the annual sectoral implementation note for the growth-infrastructure pillar, dated May 2025 with 2024 outturn: electricity access rates, generation and network reinforcement, and the water production programmes serving the capital. It carries two cautions of its own — it is marked draft, and it publishes no standardised reliability indicator such as outage duration or frequency.
Nothing connects that supply to the data centre. No facility-level supply arrangement, tariff, generator provision or water requirement is published. The national energy compact and rural mini-grid financing are directed elsewhere.
 No change
Energy Grid reliability The electricity utility's 2024 procurement plan itemises a data-centre fit-out, an enterprise system and smart-metering and dispatch studies, none reported complete.
Full recordThe plan itemises a donor-financed data-centre fit-out launched in July 2023 and due October 2024, an enterprise resource planning and customer relationship system in procurement due June 2025, a second planning study due December 2025, a geo-referenced database for the national electrification strategy due October 2025, and advanced-metering and national-dispatch studies due late 2025; two telecoms engineers were recruited under a development bank's financing (plan).
No item is reported complete. The utility's own data centre is distinct from the government's national facility, so the state runs two data-centre programmes and has published an outturn for neither.
 Movement
Energy Rural electrification Two framework agreements of June 2026 fund 85 rural mini-grids, and the National Assembly passed the financing bills in July.
Full recordThe agreements were signed on 19 June 2026 and the bills authorising the financing passed on 31 July 2026 (financing, ratification).
A national energy compact issued under the continental access initiative sets the country's commitments on electricity access (compact).
No amount, site list, connection target or delivery date is held for the mini-grids, and nothing states whether any of them serve telecommunications sites - which is what would connect this to the network's own power problem.
 Movement
Technical Capacity Robustness of government hardware and software The sector diagnostic records more than 150 unconnected public platforms. No inventory of ministry servers, workstations or licences exists.
Full recordThe most recent whole-of-sector assessment is also the frankest. Prefaced by the digital transformation minister, it carries a chapter on public digital platforms covering government back-office systems, the fragmentation of more than 150 unconnected public platforms, the state of hosting infrastructure and the first tier-three data centre, dependence on a single submarine cable, and the skills and interoperability gaps.
It is a diagnostic rather than an audit: no inventory of ministry servers, workstations or licences was located, and no availability or incident figure is published for any government system. The network those platforms would run over is itself unfinished — the broadband administrative intranet is in development — and a national response team is still only tendered.

Technical Capacity Local capacity to maintain, manage and develop government systems A 390-post civil service competition was run digitally from application to examination in August 2026, inside a 3,000-post recruitment programme.
Full recordApplications were filed through the state services platform and examinations sat on digital tablets at Nouakchott, for a 390-post competition inside a wider programme for 3,000 state employees and contract workers (competition, examination).
It is the first competition the base holds that is digital end to end rather than at the application stage only. No candidate count, marking method, accessibility provision or fallback for a candidate unable to use a tablet is published, and nothing states how the results are audited.
 Movement
Cybersecurity National cybersecurity readiness The cybersecurity strategy ran out in 2025 with no successor and the response team it provided for was never built, while a central-bank platform detected more than 10,000 threats.
Full recordThe 2022-2025 strategy's period has ended and no successor, evaluation or response team is on record; the strategy text itself is not held (strategy).
The national computer emergency response team is planned, with no establishing instrument, staffing or premises on record (team).
A central bank digital reputation protection platform detected more than 10,000 electronic threats in the first half of 2026, of which 470 were confirmed and removed, with 20 digital identities protected (platform). The country's only operating detection capability sits inside one institution and covers that institution's own exposure.
