Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Zambia's digital state collects revenue well and identifies people slowly. Over 400 government services are onboarded across the service bus, payment gateway, national portal and payment platform, with more than K8.8 billion collected cumulatively (Accountant General's office). Against that, biometric enrolment on the national register stood at about 1.3 million people at the end of 2025, and issuance of the biometric registration card was deferred until after the general election of 13 August 2026 (ministry account).

The governing instruments moved in one direction only. Enforcement of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 was declared fully operational on 18 July 2026, twenty-six days before that election (enforcement account). The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner has been operational since early 2025 and the base contains no registration count, enforcement decision, fine or breach notification for it at any date.

Only 33.0% of Zambians used the internet in October 2025, unchanged across two annual readings (compiler analysis), against government coverage claims of 80% to 94% that state no basis.

Zambia's network figures do not reconcile, and the disagreement is between the state and its own financier. The World Bank appraisal of January 2025 records 10,000 km of fibre (appraisal document); the Minister of Technology and Science stated in May 2026 that the network had grown from 15,000 km in 2021 to more than 45,000 km, with national coverage at 94% (ministerial statement). Neither states its counting basis, and the two accounts have not been reconciled.

The same ambiguity attaches to the word connected. In February 2025 the state ICT institute's own provincial officer said site works were complete at twelve Western Province sites but that completion depended on last-mile connections still outstanding; by March 2026 all 116 councils were reported connected and phase one complete (briefing). In August 2026 the institute confirmed that four named border posts still have connectivity gaps, with no budget or completion date published (status update).

Two builds are real. A government-owned private 4G network for public institutions was launched at Ndola Teaching Hospital on 15 July 2026, with the national electronic health record migrating onto it (launch account), and a cross-border fibre interconnector to Mozambique was commissioned on 23 February 2026.

Over 400 services are onboarded and more than K8.8 billion collected cumulatively across the government's payment and service platforms (Accountant General's office); eight of 116 local authorities went live in June 2026 and returned about K21.7 million in their first month (collection figures).

Three enrolment figures on the base do not reconcile — 1.5 million in September 2024, about 1.3 million in December 2025, and about 1.5 million of about 20 million in July 2026 — with no revision reported by any party (ministry account). The paper registration card, covering some 94% of adults in 2021, remains the operative credential, and card issuance was deferred until after the August 2026 election against a funded target of 4 million by October 2030 (appraisal document).

Birth registration stood at 14% in 2024, with 6% of children holding a certificate, against a target of 50% by end-2026 with no measured mid-point.

The exchange layer named at appraisal has not appeared, and no source reports it in operation (appraisal document). Exchange runs through the government service bus instead, and the national electronic health record is deployed in approximately 80% of health facilities (institute account).

A National Carbon Registry was launched on 8 August 2026 as the compulsory route for carbon-credit transactions, administered by the Zambia Environmental Management Agency and reporting into the National Integrated MRV System and the UNFCCC Centralised Accounting and Reporting Platform; no operating rules or access terms are published.

Both 2025 cyber statutes are now held as gazetted and both are court-gated. The Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 makes its investigatory powers subject to a court order, and the companion Cyber Security Act No. 3 of 2025, acquired on 2026-08-09, requires a law enforcement officer to apply ex parte to a judge of the High Court for an interception of communication order under section 29, valid three months and renewable once; interception without a prior order is confined by section 30 to preventing bodily harm, loss of life or damage to property, and the officer must place the written confirmation, an affidavit, the recording and a transcript before a judge within two days. The blanket interception mandate a United States embassy alert attributes to the regime is not a provision of either Act. Section 3(1) does establish the Zambia Cyber Security Agency in the Office of the President, under the President's general direction, and “law enforcement officer” extends to any person the President designates by statutory instrument.

Enforcement of the Cyber Crimes Act was declared fully operational on 18 July 2026, twenty-six days before the general election of 13 August, with compliance obligations extended to financial institutions, telecommunications operators, digital platforms, mobile money providers and e-commerce businesses (enforcement account). No prosecution, charge or conviction count is published. The Law Association was reported in April 2025 as set to mount a legal challenge; no filing, hearing or ruling appears anywhere in the base in the sixteen months since.

The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner became operational with a controller registration deadline of 30 April 2025, and the base holds no registration count, enforcement decision, fine, breach notification or annual report for it at any date. On 31 July 2026 nine civil society organisations asked the Office to examine the source of the contact list behind SMS messages sent to Social Cash Transfer beneficiaries before the 13 August election (civil society statement); no response is on the record. Monitoring current to late 2025 records Cabinet planning to repeal the 2021 Data Protection Act; nothing has followed.

Research mapping records mass surveillance in Zambia despite no terrorism threat and documents it repurposed against political opposition, while no authorising instrument, oversight body, camera count or retention rule is held (research mapping).

A consent judgment entered after the August 2021 platform blocking binds the regulator not to interrupt access outside its authority and to state the reason for any interruption within 36 hours; the technology ministry has committed not to shut down the internet at the August 2026 election (coalition letter).

Zambia's universal-access levy has a statutory basis on file for the first time. Statutory Instrument No. 38 of 2012 caps a licensee's contribution to the Universal Access and Service Fund at not exceeding 1.5% of gross annual turnover, itself capped at 50% of total collectable annual operating fees payable to the regulator, payable annually in advance (gazetted instrument). No later rate, amendment or the Fund's own balance and disbursement record is held.

A third account of the national AI strategy's stage does not reconcile with the other two. The Ministry of Technology and Science permanent secretary said on 7 August 2026 that the current strategy is nearing the end of its implementation cycle and will be reviewed, naming limited computing infrastructure, data quality and the absence of interoperable standards as adoption barriers (seminar account). That statement sits against the Presidential Delivery Unit's account a month earlier that the strategy was nearing completion, and against a continental review that already treats a Zambian strategy as an existing text; the base cannot settle which is current.

The head of the Presidential Delivery Unit said on 17 July 2026 that the national AI strategy was nearing completion and invited stakeholder contributions (statement), while a continental review published on 1 July 2026 reads Zambia's national AI strategy alongside six others as an existing text. No draft, publication date, budget or institutional architecture is held.

No domestic artificial intelligence institution exists; the only one on offer is an international laboratory disclosed as an opportunity in July 2026, with no signature, funding, siting or timetable. The home affairs permanent secretary said in July 2026 that the cyber security agency's establishment had strengthened the response to online scams and AI-enabled trafficking.

A memorandum signed in June 2026 covers AI infrastructure, sovereign cloud hosting, digital public infrastructure for 25 ministries and a co-invested national data centre, with no value, site, capacity or timetable disclosed (signing account). It precedes the strategy that would govern it, and the same vendor built the existing national data centre.

The United States health financing memorandum is not proceeding. The Foreign Minister said in May 2026 that it had stalled over data terms the government considers unacceptable, and it was reported shelved that month after Washington sought signature alongside a critical-minerals pact (news agency report).

29% of sampled Zambian startups have at least one female co-founder, the highest in the sample.

Zambia has one domestic digital budget line in the entire wiki. ZMW 255,000,000 was approved for fiscal year 2026 to upgrade digital infrastructure and quality of service, particularly rural; the record is built from press reporting rather than an appropriation document, the stage is unclear, and no funding source is named (approval account). There is no domestic budget archive for Zambia, where nineteen other countries have one, so no appropriation figure in this report rests on a budget document.

The universal access fund operates without published accounts: no fund size, levy rate, annual disbursement or cumulative tower count is held for any year.

Financial inclusion reached 80.1% of adults in 2025 from 69.4% in 2020, with mobile money use at 76.2% (survey figures). Internet use did not follow: 33.0% in October 2025, unchanged year on year while user numbers grew with the population (compiler analysis). The wiki holds no rural-urban or gender disaggregation of internet use to explain the gap.

External financing carries the programme: a US$100 million grant board-approved on 11 September 2025, of which US$5 million is for digital skills (appraisal document). Against that, the training the base can evidence is 146 public officers in three districts of one province.
