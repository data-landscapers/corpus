This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-03.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A Digital Transformation Authority was established by prime-ministerial decree alongside data, artificial-intelligence and cybersecurity authorities, and no account connects the decree to the platform claims that follow it.
Full recordThree authorities were created at once by decree: digital transformation, data and artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity (decree, roundup).

No account held connects the institutional decree to the platform launches recorded through 2026 — the national platform, the identity credential, the payment switch — so which authority owns which system, and under what powers, is not established anywhere in this base.

That gap runs through the whole unit: institutions are announced by decree, systems are announced by ministry, and nothing published links the two.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The last information and communication technology strategy the base holds ran to 2011, and the e-government plan that followed it expired in 2020.
Full recordThe Knowledge Society strategy approved in 2007 under the twenty-five-year national strategy ran to 2011, with the National Center for Strategic Planning named as the responsible body (profile).

The e-government and smart transition plan that followed covered 2016 to 2020 and has expired; its text could not be captured (plan).

The base holds no successor. What the minister described to the ITU in November 2025 is a programme of work — digitising services, updating regulatory frameworks, developing digital identity and cybersecurity — not a published strategy (statement).

Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy No domestic broadband strategy is held. What moved is an ITU resolution creating a basis for international help to rebuild war-damaged networks.
Full recordSudan's draft resolution was adopted by consensus at the World Telecommunication Development Conference in Baku on 26 November 2025, seeking international support to rebuild war-damaged telecommunications infrastructure (adoption).

The ministerial statement that preceded it names satellite connectivity for war-affected and remote areas as something to explore rather than a planned rollout (statement).

Neither is a broadband strategy: they set a route to external assistance, not a domestic target for coverage, speed or price.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A sectoral data-centre and cloud regulation stands in for a strategy, and the central bank has committed to a hybrid cloud transformation.
Full recordThe regulator names Regulations for the Organization of Data Center and Cloud Computing Services, 2025 as the current sector instrument; the text itself is not held and an acquisition line is open for it (catalogue).

The central bank's five-year plan dated 31 December 2025 commits under its digital-transformation axis to a national disaster-recovery centre for the banking sector, an expanded data-centre network and a hybrid public-private cloud transformation project (plan).

No standalone national cloud or data-storage strategy was located. Both instruments are sectoral, and neither sets a whole-of-government hosting position.

The regulation's own text is now held, closing the acquisition line that stood against it (regulation), as is the 2018 statute the regulator is constituted under (Act). The sectoral instrument can now be read rather than cited; it remains sectoral.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap The nearest thing to a roadmap expired in 2020; interoperability now appears as a platform design principle rather than a framework.
Full recordThe e-government and smart transition plan for 2016 to 2020 is identified as the closest Sudanese instrument to an interoperability roadmap, and it has expired (plan).

The ministry's own product page for the government-institution platform names interoperability among its design principles and lists 23 integrated government institutions (product page).

A platform that integrates institutions is not a framework other systems can be built against. The base holds no published architecture, no data standards and no sequencing plan.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy A Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority was established by decree ahead of any primary data-protection law, and no strategy, sandbox or guidance note is held.
Full recordThe authority was created by the same decree as the other two, and it was created before the country had a data-protection statute (authority).

No artificial-intelligence strategy document, regulatory sandbox or guidance note is held for the country, unlike several other African states the same roundup records (roundup).

A regulator standing up before the law it would enforce is a sequencing choice, and the draft that would supply the law is recorded separately as still in redrafting.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies There is no economy-wide localisation law. Licensing requires disclosure of hosting location, and the digital identity's terms commit to primary storage in Sudan.
Full recordApplicants for an internet-applications licence must state whether their hosting server sits inside or outside Sudan and name the hosting company, and data-centre licence categories separate operators owning facilities inside Sudan from cloud resellers (licensing).

The national digital identity's terms commit to data minimisation and primary storage inside Sudan (terms), and the cybersecurity authority asserted full sovereignty over the platform and its encryption keys (statement).

What the base does not hold is any general requirement covering data outside the telecommunications licence and the identity platform.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy A data and artificial intelligence authority now carries the mandate to regulate national data management; no policy document sits behind it.
Full recordThe prime minister's decree of 5 November 2025 established three authorities under the communications ministry, among them a data and artificial intelligence authority evolved from the National Information Center and mandated to regulate national data management (decree).

A draft data and artificial intelligence law was reported in redrafting in August 2026 and is not in force.

The mandate exists; the policy does not. Nothing the base holds states what the authority's rules over public-sector data actually are.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy No open-data policy is held. The access-to-information law of 2015 is the closest instrument, and its commission was never made operational.
Full recordThe law ratified on 27 January 2015 establishes a citizen right to obtain information from public institutions and a commission with powers to compel publication (law). Available reporting indicates that commission was never effectively established.

A land-sector assessment found no open data available online since the 2023 escalation and recorded Sudan's last Open Data Inventory score as 24, ranking 159th worldwide (assessment).

No open-data policy, portal or publication schedule was located.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation A draft Data and Artificial Intelligence Law is in redrafting with the digital transformation ministry, legislating data protection and artificial intelligence in one instrument.
Full recordThe draft went to the Council of Ministers with two others and was reported back in redrafting in August 2026 (draft, redrafting).

Legislating data protection and artificial intelligence in a single instrument is a design few African states have attempted. Neither text is published, so what it would provide on lawful basis, consent, transfer or supervision cannot be stated.

The authority that would enforce it already exists, created by decree ahead of the statute.

Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation A draft Cybersecurity Law is in redrafting, and its relationship to the cybercrime provisions the country described to a regional experts' meeting is not established.
Full recordThe draft is the second of the three-instrument package and was reported in redrafting in August 2026 (draft, redrafting).

Its relationship to the cybercrime provisions the country separately described to an Arab experts' meeting in July 2026 is not established (meeting).

That matters because the cybercrime statute in force is being used against speech, and whether the new draft replaces, amends or sits alongside it is exactly what no source says.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The Electronic Transactions Act 2007 and the Civil Registry Act 2011 carry the digital identity; two later instruments it cites are not held.
Full recordThe 2007 Act covers digital signatures, electronic authentication and certification, and establishes an electronic licensing committee (Act). The 2011 Act founds the civil registry and its unique national number, and requires the registry to supply data to competent state organs (Act).

The national digital identity's terms, published 21 May 2026, cite both, plus a digital identity regulation of 2025 and a cybersecurity act of 2025 (terms).

Neither 2025 instrument could be located as an independently accessible text, so the legal basis a reader can check stops at 2011.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation Four central bank instruments cover digital payments: mobile payment licensing, banking agency, USSD controls and anti-money-laundering controls.
Full recordThe 2020 regulation sets licensing conditions, capital and insurance requirements, e-money and trust-account rules and agent rules for mobile payment institutions (regulation).

Three instruments were made inside the window. The banking agency regulation of 1 March 2026 lets banks deliver services through licensed agents outside branches (regulation). Circular 7/2026 sets binding consumer-protection requirements for USSD services (circular). Circular 8/2026 extends anti-money-laundering supervision to mobile and phone payment channels (circular).

All four are central bank instruments made under the Banking Business Organisation Act 2004. No primary payments statute is held.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability A draft Digital Transformation Law of 2026 is in redrafting with the ministry, the third of a three-instrument package.
Full recordIt went to the Council of Ministers alongside the cybersecurity and data drafts and was reported in redrafting in August 2026 (draft, ministry).

None of the three texts is published. The platforms the law would presumably govern — the national services platform, the identity credential, the civil-service records system — are already live, so the statute follows the systems rather than authorising them.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No artificial-intelligence statute is in force. The cybercrime amendment of 2020 is the instrument reached for, and it is not AI-specific.
Full recordThe Cyber Crime Prevention (Amendment) Act, 2020 was signed on 22 April 2020 and gazetted on 13 July, sharply increasing penalties across articles 5 to 39 of the 2018 Cyber Crime Law (gazette).

It carries no deepfake or generative-AI provision, and no dedicated instrument was found. A draft data and artificial intelligence law was reported in redrafting in August 2026.

When a manipulated image of the prime minister circulated in June 2026, the information ministry reserved the right to act without naming a provision (statement).

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation A ministerial meeting resolved to review state-level border-trade protocols and replace them with a single national legal framework, with revenue protection the stated driver.
Full recordThe meeting was chaired by the finance minister and resolved to refer the state-to-neighbouring-country protocols to the Council of Ministers, to build one national framework in their place (meeting).

It was paired with the adoption of the national platform as the sole channel for foreign-trade transactions, and the stated driver is revenue protection rather than service delivery.

No draft, timetable or list of the protocols under review is published.

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The Statistics Act 2003 remains in force and was assessed in 2021 as not providing for recent advances in statistical production.
Full recordA joint assessment of the national statistical system, with fieldwork between March and July 2021, states plainly that the Act is outdated, does not adequately provide for recent advances in statistical production and contains no gender-statistics provisions (assessment).

That is the most current authoritative statement of the Act's standing the base holds. No full primary text of the Act itself was locatable, and no amendment or successor bill has been found.

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The Right to Access Information Law of 2015 is in force, and the commission it requires was never effectively established.
Full recordThe law was ratified by the National Assembly on 27 January 2015 and signed on 22 February. It establishes a citizen right to obtain information from public institutions, and a commission with powers to compel publication (law).

Available reporting indicates the commission was never made operational, so the law's practical reach is unverified.

Nothing later amends or replaces it, and no implementing regulation was located.

Data protection Data protection authority A cybersecurity authority was launched in January 2026, and its published mandate is cybersecurity rather than personal-data protection.
Full recordThe authority created by the November 2025 decree was formally stood up in January 2026, mandated to lead national cybersecurity policy, regulatory frameworks and protection of digital infrastructure (launch).

In May 2026 it asserted full Sudanese sovereignty over the national digital-identity platform, and named data governance, encryption-key management and periodic security review as national priorities (statement).

No body with a personal-data protection remit is held, and the draft data law that would create one is not in force. A research series published in July 2026 reads the same gap from the other side (series).
 Advanced
Data protection National data protection readiness A central bank directive suspends bank accounts whose customer data is not updated, its deadline extended to end-2026, with no exception for displaced customers without documents.
Full recordThe directive requires customer data to be updated on pain of account suspension, and the deadline was extended to the end of 2026 (directive, extension).

The directive text is not held. No count of accounts affected, no exception for displaced customers without documents and no appeal route is stated anywhere (flexibility).

In a country where displacement is mass, a rule that conditions keeping a bank account on producing identity documents is an exclusion instrument whether or not it is meant as one, and nothing published measures who it excludes. The country has no data-protection statute in force under which a customer could contest the processing.

Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration Sudan carried a resolution at the ITU development conference and opened cooperation with the regional internet registry on exchange points and address resources.
Full recordThe resolution adopted by consensus in Baku on 26 November 2025 creates a formal basis for international technical and financial assistance to rebuild war-damaged telecommunications infrastructure (adoption).

At the WSIS Forum in Geneva in July 2026 the minister discussed strengthening internet exchange points, IPv6 expansion and internet-resource development with the regional registry and the Internet Society (meeting).

Both are engagements rather than instruments. Nothing yet commits either side to a dated deliverable.

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation A regional cybersecurity harmonisation consultancy was awarded at USD 336,700, moving the work from terms of reference to an appointed consultant.
Full recordThe regional body's 2025 procurement disclosure records the award of a USD 336,700 consultancy for the harmonisation of cybersecurity legal frameworks across member states, Sudan among them (disclosure).

That is a step, not an outcome: the award commits a consultant, not a member state. No harmonised instrument, model law or timetable has been published.

Sudan's own cybersecurity draft law is separately in redrafting, and nothing on the base connects the two processes.

Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure The Horizon Fiber tripartite agreement became binding in February 2026; a longer Port Sudan to Douala corridor remains at pre-feasibility.
Full recordThree operators signed a binding tripartite agreement on 4 February 2026 for a multi-terabit terrestrial fibre corridor running from Djibouti's submarine landing stations through Ethiopia to Sudan, converting a December 2024 memorandum into a commitment (agreement).

The continental programme's record for the Sudan section of a Port Sudan to Douala corridor positions Sudan as a transit route with open-access gateways for landlocked neighbours, at planning stage (record).

One has a signature behind it and the other does not.

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers A regional consultancy on data hosting and cloud-market guidelines was put out to tender in March 2026; no agreement governs transfers today.
Full recordExpressions of interest were invited on 30 March 2026 for a consultancy to develop harmonised regional guidelines on infrastructure sharing, co-location, data hosting and the cloud market across member states including Sudan (notice).

This is procurement, not policy. What the base does not hold is any instrument — regional or bilateral — that presently governs the transfer of personal or public-sector data across Sudan's borders.

Sudan's own sectoral rules reach only as far as hosting-location disclosure in telecommunications licensing (licensing).

Standards National interoperability standards A request for proposals for the instant payment system sets real interoperability requirements; the government platform names the principle without publishing a standard.
Full recordVersion 5.0 of the central bank's request for proposals, dated 7 April 2026, requires interoperability with the existing interbank settlement system and real-time programming-interface integration, alongside enterprise security and compliance standards (document).

The ministry's government-institution platform product page names process engineering, smart governance and interoperability as design principles and lists 23 integrated institutions (page).

A procurement specification binds one supplier and a product page binds nobody. No published national interoperability standard exists for other systems to build to.

Standards National quality standards A telecommunications service quality regulation was gazetted in March 2026, and the standards body publishes an adopted catalogue against international equivalents.
Full recordThe regulation gazetted on 15 March 2026 was made under the Telecommunications and Post Regulation Act 2018 with the minister's approval and repeals the 2021 quality-of-service regulation (gazette). Its substantive quality provisions from page seven onwards are not held.

The national standards body publishes a catalogue of adopted Sudanese Standards with their international equivalents, under the Standards Act of 2008 (catalogue). It is cross-sector rather than an ICT list.

In July 2026 the body began taking certification fees through the government payment platform (integration).
 Advanced
Standards Adoption of international standards A banking cybersecurity framework made ISO and PCI alignment mandatory, and Sudan deposited its passport signing certificate in the ICAO directory.
Full recordThe central bank's framework, issued in January 2026 over the governor's signature, requires banks and financial institutions to align with ISO 27001, ISO 27002, NIST, ISF, Basel and PCI DSS (framework). It is a mandatory instrument, not an aspiration.

On 15 February 2026 Sudan deposited its country signing certificate in the International Civil Aviation Organization's public key directory in Montreal, enabling worldwide verification of its machine-readable passports (deposit).

The USSD circular separately requires encryption to TLS 1.2 and ISO/IEC 27001 (circular).

Public debate and participation in policymaking Non-governmental contribution to national policy A private-sector forum called for a national strategy and a supreme council, and a private-sector engagement strategy was built on 71 interviews.
Full recordThe seventh edition of an operator-hosted forum on 31 May 2026 brought together university academics and external advisers who called for a national artificial-intelligence and digital-transformation strategy, a supreme council for digital transformation, and stronger public-private partnership (forum).

A private sector engagement strategy published on 29 April 2026 rests on 71 key-informant interviews conducted in November 2025 with small firms, banks, associations, displaced firms and diaspora businesses (strategy).

The older precedent is the statistics strategy, whose design process recorded consultation with private-sector actors, civil society and academia (strategy).

Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy A six-year sentence over a private phone conversation was reported in August 2026, one of a series of cybercrime prosecutions for speech.
Full recordA pharmacist was jailed for six years over a private phone conversation (sentence), inside a pattern of anti-war prosecutions under the cybercrime statute (prosecutions), and a journalist was detained under the same Act (detention).

The two accounts date the statute differently, to 2007 and to 2008, and neither the judgment nor the charging instrument is held. So the law being used is one whose year the sources disagree on and whose text this base does not hold.

The draft cybersecurity law now in redrafting does not state whether it touches these provisions.

Full recordThe commercial estimate for October 2025 puts users at 14.9 million, up 407,000 or 2.8 per cent year on year, with penetration unchanged at 28.7 per cent (estimate).
