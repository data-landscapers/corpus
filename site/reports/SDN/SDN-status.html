This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Sudan had no digital public infrastructure policy framework in place as at 2025, on the assessment prepared for UNDP's 2026-2028 country programme. In November 2025 three bodies were created by prime-ministerial decree: the Digital Transformation Authority, the Sudanese Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority and the Sudanese Cybersecurity Authority. All three sit under the Ministry of Digital Transformation and Communications.

In 2026 the ministry launched phase two of its 3x3 digital transformation plan. The plan sets out three pathways: citizen services, private-sector services and government-sector services. Each pathway has named projects and lead ministries. The Central Bank of Sudan's financial inclusion directorate has been given a mandate to formulate a national financial inclusion strategy and a consumer protection framework. Neither document has been found. The finance ministry set its own priorities in August 2026 as automating payroll, invoicing, procurement and electronic collection. No legal instrument has been found for any of them.

Sudan scored 29.6 of 100 for overall governance in 2023, 51st of 54 African states, against a continental average of 49.3. Its Public Administration score fell 12.0 points over 2014-2023 to 24.2 of 100, 52nd of 54. That fall was the second-largest driver of its overall decline. It sat below the continental average on all 16 of the index's sub-categories, and among the bottom ten African states on 13 of them. The central bank published its strategic plan for 2026-2030 with 150 initiatives. Its digital axis commits to a banking disaster-recovery centre, a wider data-centre network and a hybrid cloud. A banker's review of September 2026 found no programmes, budgets or indicators attached to it.

Legislation and regulation

A decree gazetted on 19 October 2026 added publishing through electronic or media channels to the 1991 Criminal Act. The decree is provisional, and the change reached speech, not systems. Activists warn the wording exposes online posts to the death penalty. The digital statute book is otherwise still the pair of Acts passed in 2007. The instruments meant to replace it have not been enacted. Three drafts prepared by the Ministry of Digital Transformation and Communications were before the Council of Ministers on 6 August 2026. On 1 November 2026 the Council approved them as draft bills: a cybersecurity bill, a data and artificial intelligence bill and a digital transformation bill. No text has been published, and none has been enacted. Any of them would be enacted by the interim legislative arrangement set out in the Constitutional Document, not by an elected chamber.

The Electronic Transactions Act 2007 governs electronic signatures, electronic transactions and digital identification. The Cybercrime Act of 2007, amended in 2020, is the principal cybersecurity statute. It criminalises unauthorised access to and disclosure of data. On 13 October 2025 the cabinet approved a further amendment making prison and a fine jointly mandatory. It raises maximum terms to ten years and allows electronic search without urgent judicial authorisation. Identity rests on the Civil Registration Act 2011, which makes registration for the national identity card compulsory for citizens aged 16 and above. Enforcement varies across the country, particularly in conflict-affected areas. The card and number it issues remain the legal proof of identity required for formal employment, banking and other legal purposes.

The three digital authorities announced in November 2025 were created by prime-ministerial decree, not by legislation. No data-protection statute has been enacted at all. A draft data protection bill proposed around 2018 was never passed. A pharmacist was sentenced to six years over a private phone conversation opposing the war (August 2026). He was convicted under the criminal code and the cybercrime law after intelligence officers searched his phone.

Data protection

A draft data protection bill proposed around 2018 was never passed, and no independent data protection authority has been established. On 1 November 2026 the Council of Ministers approved a Data and Artificial Intelligence Regulation Bill as a draft bill. No text has been published, and it has not been enacted. The Sudanese Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority was created by decree in November 2025 inside the communications ministry. No statute gives it powers over the state's own collection. Sudan signed the African Union's Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection in March 2023 and has not ratified it.

The Electronic Transactions Act 2007 and the Cybercrime Act 2007, amended in 2020, criminalise unauthorised access to and disclosure of data. They create no duty to notify users, a regulator or a data protection authority of a breach. Neither imposes a data-residency requirement or any enforceable control over cross-border transfers of government or personal data. Consumer protection for digital payments rests mainly on the 2013 Regulation on Electronic Fund Transfers, with scattered provisions and no recourse built for digital channels. In 2023 a central bank section ruled on customers' complaints against banks and supervised non-bank institutions. Identity decisions reach no dedicated tribunal or appeals body and can be challenged only in the ordinary courts.

In July 2026 the central bank ordered every bank to update the data held on every customer, and to suspend and freeze the accounts of those who do not. The order is a condition on licensed banks under anti-money-laundering rules, and it confers no rights on the customers whose data is compelled. The deadline was extended to the end of 2026, with SudaPass named as the means of completing the update. SudaPass publishes a privacy policy setting out rights of access, correction, deletion and withdrawal of consent. No authority to enforce those rights has been found. Sudan's Digital Freedom score stood at 17.0 of 100 in 2023, 49th of 54 African states.

Regional collaboration

Sudan signed the African Union's Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection in March 2023 and has not ratified it. Its Regional Integration score rose 3.8 points over 2014-2023 to 22.0 of 100, 47th of 54 African states. The score spans its memberships of COMESA, CEN-SAD and IGAD. A 2023 assessment found the national payment rails had no connectivity outside the country. A 400-km fibre cable from Egypt opened in October 2004. In April 2021 the state operator agreed with the Egyptian incumbent to carry international traffic over it. In August 2026 the government resolved to replace its bilateral border-trade protocols with neighbouring states with a single national framework.

Standards

A linkage between the Nazaha procurement platform, the budget execution system and the Baladna trade platform was announced on 9 August 2026. No architecture, standard or timetable has been published for it. The procurement system launched three days later was presented as bringing the country closer to international standards of governance and financial oversight. No named standard was given for it.

A Central Bank of Sudan directive required every bank to update its customers' account data between 30 July and 30 September 2026. Accounts not updated were to be suspended and frozen, on anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing and proliferation-financing grounds. The bank's Field Supervision Department presents the exercise as compliance with international banking-supervision standards. The directive also requires banks to deploy technology that monitors account activity continuously and detects unusual transaction patterns early. One bank pushed the requirement to its customers through the Bankak mobile app. On 17 August 2026 the deadline was extended to the end of 2026.

Electronic certification is governed by a 2018 regulation for certification service providers. Under it the National Authority for Digital Certification runs the SudaPass platform. In public financial management, a team was formed in August 2026 to adopt reform standards, and the standards were not named. Electronic collection of standards and metrology fees reached a second state in September 2026. The White Nile branch issues an electronic receipt at the moment of payment.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In August 2026 an economist called on the Ministry of Digital Transformation to impose mandatory automation across revenue collection, customs, taxes and the gold trade. He named artisanal gold as one of the largest channels moving cash outside the banking system. In July 2026 a named specialist set out a ten-pillar digital microfinance programme for 2027-2030. It rests on a unified national platform connecting banks, microfinance institutions, fintechs and the payments switch. It adds a unified digital credit registry and a new bank for microfinance and community development. No consultative mechanism on digital policy has been found.

In August 2026 bankers pressed for flexibility in the compulsory customer-data update. They argued that it should be measured by customers retained and updated, not by accounts closed. Their case was made on financial-inclusion and anti-money-laundering grounds.

Participation was Sudan's lowest-scoring governance sub-category in 2023, at 10.5 of 100 and 53rd of 54 African states. It fell 4.8 points over the decade. Deliberative & Participatory Governance stood at 19.1 and Civil Society Space at 20.0. Over the same decade Media Freedom fell 5.0 points to 27.8 of 100, 51st of 54. Freedom of Expression & Belief rose 14.6 points to 37.9. In May 2026 Amnesty International reported that journalists have been targeted by both sides with arrests, detentions, abductions and killings. The electoral register has not been updated since 2015.

In July 2026 the Red Sea State cybercrime prosecutor charged at least 15 journalists and two news sites over online reporting on alleged public-sector corruption. The journalists' syndicate counted 64 prosecutions of journalists from April 2023 to June 2026, among them Cybercrime Act complaints against 47 journalists and outlets in a single instance, after which 18 were declared wanted.

Finance

Financial sustainability

No fiscal-year budget for the digital estate had been read as at September 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

In October 2026 the Arab Monetary Fund agreed with Sudan's finance minister to include Sudan in its digital-transformation technical assistance for 2027. The fund also agreed support on securities and sukuk, and more training places. In January 2023 the satellite operator YahClick signed a six-year partnership worth USD 15 million with Canar, a Sudanese operator. It was framed as support for Sudan's digital transformation and broadband goals.

New investments

In May 2025 the World Bank approved ASCENT-Sudan, a USD 76.3 million energy and digital access operation. It became effective in October 2025, and over 30 companies answered its requests for expressions of interest. Its second request for proposals, for energy in the telecom sector, opened on 1 April 2026. Its targets of 150,000 people with new internet access and 50 telecom sites with better power stood at nil in March 2026.

In November 2024 a USD 41.9 million Global Partnership for Education grant was approved for Sudan's reopened primary schools. It covers offline learning servers, tablets, solar power, digital learning hubs and dashboard monitoring.

In November 2026 Proparco, the French development finance institution, put EUR 600,000 into the payments fintech Cashi, its first operation in Sudan. In March 2016 Sudan's Public Financial and Macroeconomic Management project carried US$34.8m from the African Development Bank. Online trading opened on the Khartoum Stock Exchange under it on 24 March 2016. An integrated financial management and information system was among the systems the project was developing.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Sudan had 17.8 million mobile connections by October 2025, 34.1 per cent of the population. That was 2.7 million more than in October 2024. It had 14.9 million internet users in October 2025, 28.7 per cent penetration and up 407,000 on the year. Communications blackouts continued across large parts of the country through 2025. The Ibrahim Index put Sudan's Internet and Computers score at 38.9 of 100 in 2023, 19th of 54 African states, after a 12.4-point rise over the decade. Mobile communications scored 68.3, 27th of 54, and the wider infrastructure sub-category 34.2, 32nd of 54. GSMA measured 4G at around half the population and 3G at around seven in ten in 2022. In November 2026 Zain raised its 100 GB bundle from SDG 68,900 to SDG 115,000, and MTN doubled its 1 GB bundle to SDG 11,000.

Used international bandwidth reached 245 Gbps for a population of 42.8 million in 2019. Four submarine systems, EASSy, FALCON, SAS-1 and SAS-2, land at Port Sudan, and the World Bank judged first-mile connectivity sufficient. A binding tripartite agreement covers a fibre corridor of about 2,800 km from Djibouti's subsea landings through Ethiopia to Port Sudan. The incumbent's group reports over 25,000 km of terrestrial fibre, with 1,586 km added in 2025. The single exchange point, SIXP, carried four members at 1 Gbps each in August 2026. No network had joined or left it in the preceding twelve months.

The government ordered ten internet shutdowns between 13 and 23 April 2026 during school examinations. It was the sixth consecutive year it had cut connectivity for exams. By March 2026, army shutdowns of land-based internet had put the online application for humanitarian group cash out of reach across much of the country. Groups in Darfur sent one member to a town's Starlink terminal to file, at risk on the road and from resellers who may be aligned with a party to the conflict. An armed group ordered satellite internet cut off indefinitely in a West Kordofan locality from 26 August 2026. SIM registration is verified against the national ID platform. The network is concentrated in Khartoum and a handful of urban centres.

Data Storage

Sudan's vehicle and driving-licence records survived the war because they were backed up at data centres inside the states and outside the country, the traffic police said in July 2026. The police named neither the host country nor the arrangement governing it. The Telecommunications and Post Regulatory Authority's data-centre and cloud regulation bars providers from storing data in the two highest levels of its data classification in cloud outside the country (2025). The same regulation exempts government data centres, government cloud services and the public key infrastructure. The one residency rule therefore binds commercial providers and not the state.

In March 2026 the passports and civil registry authority announced a new civil-registry data centre in Port Sudan. Commercial hosting inside the country rests on two providers: the Sudatel data centre in Khartoum, opened in 2015, and colocation capacity from CANARTEL. There is no in-country hyperscale cloud region, so workloads are served remotely from distant regions such as Johannesburg. Both statements describe assets in a city where telecommunications data centres have been repeatedly seized and shut down since April 2023. The paramilitary forces occupied the building housing the state operator's data centres on 23 April 2023, cutting its service. In February 2024 the largest operator's chief said they had stopped the generators at its main Khartoum data centre, cutting communications nationwide.

In October 2025 the Ministry of Digital Transformation said the Khartoum state data centre had been brought back into service after war damage. The ministry said the centre had also been equipped with cloud computing. On the same estate, the national payment switch returned to service after three years of war. It was restored as damaged infrastructure, not built as a new rail.

Energy

In 2025 the World Bank reported that large parts of the national grid, transformers and thermal generation among them, had been destroyed, stolen or left non-operational. Load shedding is frequent, and many regions have no grid electricity at all. Businesses and households fall back on diesel generators at roughly twenty times the cost per kilowatt-hour of grid power. In July 2026 a Bank of Khartoum official named power cuts and weak telecommunications as what holds back digital payments.

About two-thirds of the population had an electricity connection in 2023, and rural connection rates ran roughly thirty points behind urban ones. The Ibrahim Index put access to energy at 62.8 of 100 in 2023, 13th of 54 African states. It rose 19.0 points over the decade, one of Sudan's most improved measures. Renewable sources, mainly hydropower from plants such as the Merowe Dam, supplied most electricity generated in 2021. That share has swung widely across the decade with hydropower's output against thermal. Residential electricity was priced at under five US cents per kilowatt-hour in December 2025.

In 2025 the World Bank and ESMAP scored Sudan's regulation at 61 of 100 for mini-grids and 56 for off-grid systems. UNDP supports a solar mini-grid programme aimed at drawing in private operators (2025). The off-grid solar market is driven mainly by agricultural demand, with providers trying leasing and pay-as-you-go. The conflict has constrained that market but not halted it.

Technical Capacity

committers.top recorded 2,180 GitHub users giving Sudan as their location in 2024. Several universities have dedicated computer science and IT faculties, many of them in Khartoum. In May 2026 the Cybersecurity Authority said the civil registry is managed by the General Directorate of Civil Registration, and SudaPass is developed and operated by Sudanese professionals. The National Authority for Digital Certification partners with the certificate authority Sectigo for globally trusted certificates.

In August 2026 the National Investment Authority described building its own website as a crucial step towards an electronic single window. The meteorological authority has been unable to use its premises since April 2023. It runs from Port Sudan, Cairo and Nairobi on cloud-hosted infrastructure supported by the World Meteorological Organization (2025). It issued a flood warning from exile.

Cybersecurity

In May 2026 the Cybersecurity Authority said SudaPass undergoes regular security review and testing. Its encryption keys and digital certificates are administered by the National Authority for Digital Certification under approved controls. No independent audit of the platform has been published. Sudan sat in the establishing tier of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index in 2024.

The cybersecurity, data and digital transformation authorities were created by prime-ministerial decree in November 2025. On 1 November 2026 the Council of Ministers approved a Cybersecurity Regulation Bill as a draft bill. No text has been published. No breach-notification obligation falls on payment service providers or any other data controller. The prime minister formed a National Mechanism for Combating Information Crimes on 19 October 2026. It is chaired by an assistant attorney-general and mandated to monitor what is published on media and social platforms.

DPI

Data Exchange

In October 2025 the Customs Authority finished a real-time integration of its ASYCUDA system with the Civil Registry. Customs can now check national identity numbers directly, through a link delivered on the government's Baladna platform.

On 5 August 2026 a meeting chaired by the finance minister adopted Baladna as the national channel for all export and import procedures, permits and authorisations. The platform offers company registration, business-name registration and licensing, drawing on four ministries and the Commercial Registrar. Its exports and imports window reaches customs and banking systems. The Commercial Registrar must notify the Tax Chamber monthly of company registrations, amendments and deregistrations. In 2025 the Tax Authority had a project under way to connect its core system to Customs, the Civil Registry, the Commercial Registrar and the central bank.

In August 2026 the National Investment Authority said its electronic single window will operate only once all investment-related entities are linked electronically. It named no entities, sequence or date. On 11 August 2026 the National Economic Management Committee directed the finance ministry and the central bank to expedite the central bank's electronic application system. No architecture, standard or timetable has been published for the announced link between Nazaha, the budget execution system and Baladna.

Five separate government digital platforms — an investment single window, the Baladna foreign-trade platform, the Nazaha procurement system, the central bank's electronic application system and an agricultural-financing platform — were announced, adopted or escalated by five different ministries or authorities between 4 and 11 August 2026, and the only stated link between any two of them is Nazaha's claim to connect to Baladna.

SudaHealth, the one hospital information system found, runs at state level in Khartoum (2026). No health data reaches a national exchange. Humanitarian cash is coordinated through the multi-partner Sudan Cash Working Group outside government systems (2025). No primary law establishes authority for cross-government data sharing. In August 2026 the central bank launched an electronic capital-flows system replacing manual processing. The investment authority opened a service for investors to submit and track project requirement lists on its one-stop platform.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Sudan launched SUDAPASS, a unified national digital identity credential, on 21 May 2026. It is activated with a passport or national number together with face biometrics, so it reaches only people the paper system has documented. It was built with the Civil Registry under the Ministry of Interior and is operated by the National Authority for Digital Certification.

In August 2026 the Central Bank of Sudan named it as the tool for the compulsory update of bank customer data, with no integration detail or uptake figure. Omdurman National Bank became the first bank to switch the link on (September 2026). Its customers inside and outside the country can update their data through its digital channels without a branch visit. SudaPass publishes a privacy policy covering the personal and biometric data it collects and the holder's rights. No equivalent published framework covers the civil registry beneath it.

The registry was set up under the Civil Registration Act 2011, with the identity card mandatory at 16. Records are held digitally against a unique national number. In June 2026 the registry was only partially working, and across vast areas no entity can issue identity papers. In February 2026 the registry authority resumed issuing identity cards at all centres in safe states, after restoring the civil fingerprint system. Sudan's civil registration score fell 50 points over 2014–2023 to 25.0 of 100, 50th of 54 African states. Birth registration still starts on paper, and coverage in Darfur and other peripheries is well below the cities.

Refugees and other non-citizens are recorded under a separate Foreign Number system run by the Commission for Refugees with UNHCR, which the Commission said in 2026 it is reactivating. The national number is required to open a bank account and for know-your-customer checks, and SIM registration requires the national identity card or number.

In October 2026 the TASIS-backed administration, which governs territory outside the Port Sudan authorities' control, announced plans for a national smart identity card. The card is meant for banking, judicial and administrative transactions. No issuer, technology, budget or date was stated.

Digital Payments and Fintech

The Central Bank of Sudan launched the services of the national payment switch on 6 August 2026 with one institution connected, Omdurman National Bank. The switch is operated by Electronic Banking Services, the central bank's operational arm. The plan to connect all banks in turn has no schedule or coverage target. In October 2026 the operator reported 376 million account-to-account transfers to date, at about 10 million a month. It named a national instant payment system with a single payment QR code as its next project. The central bank had opened a tender for that system in April 2026. From 8 November 2026 the central bank ran interbank electronic clearing on every day of the week, with no ceiling on cheque values. Its policy director said that month that the Siraj real-time gross settlement system had been reactivated.

Bank of Khartoum's Bankak had around 7 million users in February 2024, with activations up 85 per cent since the war began. In July 2026 bank apps were the dominant way to settle retail trade in the capital, under currency depreciation, inflation and cash scarcity. Outages of the app continued into September 2026, and disruptions before 9 October 2026 again left customers unable to reach or transfer their money. Up to 45 per cent of humanitarian transfers were reported lost converting app balances into cash in East Darfur (September 2026). The bank launched a USSD transfer service on one operator's SIMs, reported 10 October 2026, for customers without a smartphone or internet. More than 50 customers in West and North Kordofan reported money stolen from their accounts through the app in October and November 2026, losses of SDG 18 million to SDG 45 million each on a local emergency room's count.

In October 2026 Zain's Bede wallet launched a basic bank account number service, letting users send and receive funds across all Sudanese banks. A Saudi-Sudanese joint venture, SDPay, launched offering merchant acceptance, wallets, government collections and remittances (October 2026). The central bank revoked the licence of a mobile-payment company, Sudan Pay Digital, in September 2026 without stating a reason.

Customs duties have been taken electronically since at least 2025. Since April 2026 the finance ministry's My Payment service lets individuals pay some government dues through banking apps and authorised service points. The ministry signed with Sudani on 8 October 2026 for text-message services supporting its collection system, its third such agreement with a mobile operator.

In 2021, 11 per cent of adults used mobile money and 2 per cent were formally registered users. Fifteen per cent were banked.

Registries

Sudan's electoral roll has not been updated since 2015, when 13,126,989 voters were registered against a voting-age population of 19,667,400. The African Union's 2015 pre-election assessment recorded roughly 2.6 million displaced people in Darfur left off the roll, and parts of it carried over unverified from the 2010 register.

In 2021 officials put population registration at close to nine in ten, with lower rates among peripheral and displaced people. In December 2022 the registry's director counted 37,938,429 citizens on it, the last count before the war. In June 2026, across vast areas of Sudan no entity could issue identity papers.

Company records destroyed in the 2023 conflict are being rebuilt (2025). Company commercial registration was added to the national e-government platform, reported 1 October 2026. On lawyers' accounts reported 18 October 2026, a technical fault had almost entirely halted company and business-name filings through Baladna for more than a week. The Tax Chamber runs one registration system for individuals and businesses, with one identification number for every tax including customs duties (2026). In 2019 Khartoum's property records were computerised or scanned and shared a parcel identifier with the cadastral database. Office capacity varied sharply between states, and rural areas largely lacked formal registers. In Darfur, around one per cent of customary land is formally registered.

In July 2026 the traffic police said the national vehicle database was restored, holding more than two million registered vehicles and over three million driving licences. They credited backups at data centres inside the states and outside the country. They are introducing Ansab, a laser-secured platform for transferring vehicle ownership. The poverty registry behind the safety-net system reached six of eighteen states before 2023, and recorded beneficiaries' national identity numbers only in part. In October 2026 a survivors' fund opened a tender for a secure, offline-capable digital victims' registry. The 18-month project from 1 November 2026 is to document about 500 survivors first, with hosting outside the country.

Sectoral management information systems

Sudan's finance minister set the ministry's priorities on 4 August 2026 as payroll automation, electronic invoicing, tax-base expansion, digital procurement and electronic payment and collection.

That list restates a programme financed a decade earlier: payroll automation and an integrated procurement system are presented as new intentions in August 2026, while an IFMIS was already in scope of an African Development Bank-financed project reported in March 2016.

Government clinics and hospitals had no electronic medical record in March 2021 and depended wholly on paper, on a university thesis's account. The Federal Ministry of Health put routine DHIS2 reporting coverage at over 56 per cent by the end of 2025, against 13 per cent at the onset of the crisis. No independent measurement of those figures has been published. In a Darfur pilot, 158 health facilities submitted 752 weekly surveillance reports offline through a mobile tool between September 2024 and January 2025.

A UNESCO-led assessment of 2025 reviewed education data only up to 2022, against a long-standing gap in current, consolidated data. Digital mapping of around 26,000 schools under the Transitional Education Plan 2025–2027 remains at planning and pilot stage. Before 2023 the World Bank-supported safety-net information system ran in six of Sudan's eighteen states. Since the 2023 conflict assistance delivery has fragmented across regions.

Since October 2025 customs' ASYCUDA system has checked identity numbers against the civil registry. From October 2026 customs is extending advance cargo declaration from Port Sudan's southern port to every port and crossing. In August 2026 the accounts chamber began adopting international public-sector accounting standards through the government financial information system. Sudan's tax and revenue mobilisation score fell 16.8 points over 2014–2023 to 11.4 of 100, 52nd of 54. A civil-service platform, Bayan, has launched, built to link with digital identity, e-signature and payroll, with no coverage figure.

The judiciary began a first phase of court computerisation in March 2016, costed at USD 600,000. Its contractor reported that phase finished in December 2016. In August 2026 the judiciary signed a new contract with a national technology company for several digital systems.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Sudan launched Nazaha, a national digital public-procurement and contracting system, on 12 August 2026. It was announced with no coverage figure, no mandatory-use rule and no contract-value threshold. Three days earlier the prime minister reviewed e-tendering, payroll computerisation, accounting standards and a treasury single account against the IMF's debt-relief roadmap. In November 2025 the medical supplies fund ran its first electronic tender since the war, drawing 67 bids.

On 11 November 2026 Baladna's own counters showed more than 141 services from more than 48 institutions and more than 265,785 transactions, with sign-in by SudaPass. Its listing names passports and immigration, higher education, the civil registry, customs, the tax chamber and the central bank, and no basic-education body. The Tax Chamber's portal lets taxpayers register, obtain a number, file returns and request clearance certificates, and its payment channel is being activated state by state. Driving licence renewal runs through the traffic police's Salem platform and Baladna, checked against the national identity number. In July 2026 the Salem service for citizens abroad ran through embassies in 16 countries, up from six, and had issued 1,519 licences.

Those 1,519 licences amount to roughly 0.05 per cent of the 3,161,599 driving licences on the national register (July 2026).

In November 2026 the cabinet's governance sector endorsed making authenticated electronic correspondence the state's sole official channel. No decree, system or timetable was stated. A national budget system covering estimates, execution and analysis is said to be in its final stages (September 2026). It is planned for the 2027 budget and full operation in 2028. Students now apply and pay online for the school certificate. Public service complexes at Omdurman and Bahri have reopened, with cash abolished at their counters through seven partner banks. Import permits have been filed and tracked online since November 2025, and a platform for public-sector companies to report to the finance ministry was launched in August 2026.

Sudan scored 0.2759 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, below the African average. Its effective administration score fell 5.8 points over 2014–2023 to 21.5 of 100, 51st of 54 African states.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

Local government sites in Sudan were at an early stage in 2024, with no integration or interoperability between institutions. The conflict has further cut sub-national digital capacity. Khartoum's civil registry has ceased almost entirely. Local government offices and land registries have been looted or turned into barracks. Across vast areas no entity can issue identity papers. The national information centre's structure includes a general directorate charged with coordinating ICT projects and training at state level.

Khartoum State put its standard systems back into operation (2026). It connected a government fibre network to 36 state units and signed six partnership memoranda (2026). By 20 October 2026 about 93% of its data had been recovered and the move of its systems to the cloud had begun. Its localities are to take all government transactions digital from 1 January 2027. The Northern State is restoring its finance ministry's resource-management system (October 2026). The system was in use from 2017 until the war stopped it in 2023, and the state plans to run its next budget on it. Since May 2026 Khartoum State's localities have taken commercial licence applications fully online through Baladna.

Rural digital data capture

Rural Market Access stood at 78.7 of 100 in 2023, eighth of 54 African states and up 28.3 points over the decade. It was the highest-scoring and most improved of the 96 indicators the Ibrahim Index measures for Sudan. Rural Economy stood at 56.6, 31st of 54. Rural Representation & Participation rose 15.6 points to 63.2, and Rural Land & Water Access fell 9.5 points to 29.8, 41st of 54.

In 2021 government clinics depended wholly on paper records, with no electronic medical record. In 2025 education planning faced a long-standing gap in current, consolidated sector data. On Darfur's mobile disease surveillance pilot, 158 health facilities submitted weekly reports offline (2025). In September 2025 three digital health tools were launched for rollout across all 18 states, planned to reach over 400 facilities and 3,000 health workers. The civil registry takes fingerprints and photographs electronically, and sent more than a thousand mobile units out for the 2011 mass registration. A birth outside hospital is registered only when parents take the midwife's notification to a registry office (2023). In May 2026 a reopened locality registry centre in South Gezira issued its first electronic birth certificate. Formal registration reaches a very small fraction of customary land in Darfur, and rural areas largely lacked formal land registers (2019).

In 2025 Freedom House found digital infrastructure concentrated in Khartoum and a handful of urban centres. About 63 per cent of the population lives in rural areas, with weaker and poorly maintained mobile coverage (2025). Large areas of Darfur and Kordofan have minimal or no connectivity and depend on costly satellite services such as Starlink. The growing off-grid solar market runs mainly on agricultural demand (2025). An agricultural-financing platform built by the Agricultural Bank with two ministries had been announced but not launched as at 10 August 2026.

A stolen-vehicle lookup was added to the police's electronic reporting platform in September 2024. The police counted 80,902 citizen reports since June 2023, 59,115 of them for missing vehicles, at January 2025.

Technology

AI

In September 2026 Sudan's higher education ministry added an Arabic chatbot to its online admissions portal. It answers routine questions and passes complex ones to staff. It is the one artificial-intelligence service running in government. Artificial intelligence is not in use in the state's data exchange (2025). Sudan has a national authority for artificial intelligence. The data and artificial intelligence authority was created by prime-ministerial decree in November 2025. It sits under the Ministry of Digital Transformation and Communications. Its mandate is to plan such capability; it runs no system. No national artificial intelligence strategy had been adopted as at August 2026. In October 2026 the Africa AI Governance Index placed Sudan 52nd of 54, with 0.23 of four points.

In August 2026 ministers considered a combined draft law pairing data and artificial intelligence in one text. It is the instrument that would govern the field. Ministers approved it as a draft bill on 1 November 2026. No text has been published, and there is no statute. The state data centre in Khartoum was returned to service in October 2025 with cloud and artificial-intelligence support systems. The infrastructure and transport ministry has put artificial intelligence and transport-management technology to investors as part of post-war reconstruction (July 2026). Neither is a deployed service, and none is offered to citizens.

Outside government, the groundwork is language data from a 2025 shared task that produced the first public multimodal dataset for Sudanese Arabic. It holds 3,042 captioned images and videos. Researchers at the Sudan University of Science and Technology documented a social-media corpus of 6,755,955 sentences (2026). It comes with sentiment and hate-speech resources.

ICT Industry

Sudan's business and labour environment scored 27.2 of 100 in 2023, 51st of 54 African countries, after a 1.9-point fall over the decade. Business and competition regulations stood at 33.3, 49th. Economic diversification, at 61.7 and 32nd, rose 10.3 points over the decade.

In 2024 committers.top recorded 2,180 GitHub users giving Sudan as their location. No count of the companies employing them has been found. No measure of the domestic software and services industry, its employment or the share of government technology spending reaching local suppliers had been found as at August 2026.

Innovation ecosystem

In 2024 Sudan's technology sector had reverted to an early stage since fighting began in April 2023, sustained largely by donor grants. The country sat in the bottom quartile of the Global Innovation Index 2024, with one identified science and technology cluster. In 2024 the hub layer ran to a single organisation, Impact Hub Khartoum. A social enterprise running a hub has since launched a 500-place youth entrepreneurship programme in Kassala with the state ministry. An African Development Bank and IGAD project of about USD 3.255 million is delivered through the University of Khartoum's incubator (2026). In July 2026 Orange Corners Sudan selected 35 entrepreneurs from six states across nine sectors.

Capacity

Literacy

Over the decade to 2023 Sudan's Education Quality score fell 9.1 points to 13.2 of 100, 49th of 54 African states. Its Education Completion score rose by the same 9.1 points to 70.4, ninth on the continent. The Education sub-category as a whole stood at 35.7, 47th.

The World Bank's 2022 assessment of Sudan's digital economy found no data on digital literacy, and could record its level only as perceived to be low. In July 2026 bankers and economists named low digital awareness, weak telecoms and power cuts as what holds back the move to banking apps.

In 2026 the Baladna platform listed the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research among its institutions, and no basic-education body.

Training and skills

In 2024 a small group of long-established universities taught computer science and IT at bachelor's, master's and doctoral level. No measure of the teaching workforce was reported for Sudan across the decade to 2023. In February 2026 Al-Neelain University raised its information centre to a Deanship of Digital Transformation. The higher education council approved a regulation in 2026 giving e-learning and blended learning in higher-education institutions a formal legal basis.

Research institutions

In 2024 WIPO's ranking of leading science and technology clusters identified a single cluster in Sudan. Sudan University of Science and Technology, Al Neelain University, the National University of Sudan and Future University hold computer science and IT faculties (2024). In March 2026 ISTinaD, a Sudan-based research institute, published a policy paper on digital payments.

Inclusion

Access to services

In August 2026 Bankak users were told in the app to update their information at the nearest Bank of Khartoum branch, on pain of suspension. The deadline has since moved to the end of 2026. Bankers have warned that freezing accounts on this basis risks mass financial exclusion, given displacement, lost documents and disrupted branches.

The national digital credential launched in 2026 is free, but activating it needs a passport or national identity number already held. The register and the national number are restricted to citizens. Non-citizens go to a separate Foreign Number system being reactivated in 2026. In 2022 a refugee's access to an account or a SIM turned on the provider's acceptance of the Commission of Refugees card, and many were dependent on SIM cards registered in others' names.

The Persons with Disabilities Act 2017 provides a legal basis consistent with the UN Convention. In 2025 the World Bank found no accessibility accommodation in Sudan's digital payment systems, such as screen readers, voice interfaces or accessible agents and ATMs. Hardship limits phone ownership, and under-registration of disabled children's births adds to their exclusion.

By March 2026 army shutdowns of land-based internet had put the online application for humanitarian group cash beyond much of the country. The Sudan Family Support Programme's digital transfers were suspended after October 2021, and no programme has since paid at national scale. Humanitarian cash is paid into nominated members' personal accounts, because informal groups cannot hold their own.

Sudan's Equal Access to Public Services score rose 25.9 points over 2014-2023 to 36.3 of 100, 31st of 54 African states. It was the country's second most improved indicator. In 2013 the national identity card was accepted but not required for public healthcare. The Universal Service Fund, re-established under the 2018 telecommunications act, received no government support in 2024.

Digital divides

In 2025 Freedom House reported that large areas of Darfur and Kordofan have been left with minimal or no connectivity, after the war destroyed telecommunications infrastructure and blacked out Wad Madani and Khartoum. Mobile connections covered 34.1 per cent of the population in October 2025. What survives is concentrated in Khartoum and a handful of other urban centres. The rural majority has weaker, poorly maintained coverage and costly satellite services where any service reaches it. In 2022, 42.6 per cent of adults had never used the internet, 51.7 per cent in rural areas.

Sudan's Equal Socioeconomic Opportunity score rose 21.7 points over 2014-2023 to 39.2 of 100, 36th of 54 African states. The series ends in 2023.

In March 2026 a national identity document and a smartphone were both required to open the Bankak account through which humanitarian cash is paid. In July 2026 bank apps had become the dominant way retail trade settles in the capital, taken up by groups that until recently dealt only in cash. Traders without a smartphone route their takings through a colleague's account, and unfamiliarity with the apps produces mistaken transfers.

Data

National statistics

Sudan has run neither an agricultural census nor a business census in the past twenty years (2023). In the ten years to 2023 it ran three or more health surveys, two agricultural surveys, two labour surveys and one household survey, and no business survey. In December 2025 UNFPA reported no recent population census, with a 2024 common operational population dataset used in its place. In July 2026 UNDP published Sudan's first national multidimensional poverty survey.

In 2023 the World Bank put Sudan's data use, data sources and data infrastructure in the 20-49 per cent band. Statistical services and statistical products stood in the 50-69 per cent band in 2023. On the Ibrahim Index, Capacity of the Statistical System stood at 31.2 of 100 in 2023, 48th of 54 African states, after a 6.1-point rise over the decade. No data existed for Sudan on the index's Human Resources in Education indicator.

In April 2026 Egypt's statistics agency began a technical-support programme with the Central Bureau of Statistics, covering training, census and economic-statistics methods. In 2025 the education management information system exchanged almost no data digitally with individual schools, which keep attendance, enrolment and grades on paper. The Central Bureau of Statistics was still producing monthly inflation figures in June 2026.

Open data

Sudan's Accountability & Transparency score fell 7.5 points over the decade to 2023, to 14.5 of 100 and 49th of 54 African states. Disclosure of public records stood at 7.5, 52nd, and accessibility of public records at 10.7, 46th. The Right to Access Information Law 2015 gives citizens a right to obtain information from public institutions, through a commission with powers to compel publication.

The central bank publishes the business operating rules for its real-time gross settlement system, downloadable without registration. SudaPass publishes a privacy policy setting out the personal and biometric data it collects and the rights attached. Since late 2025 the ministry has reported its platform rollout through recurring press conferences. The Sea Ports Corporation posts annual port statistics for 2007 to 2025 as downloadable files, the latest added in April 2026. No open government data portal had been found as at September 2026.

Geospatial data

In May 2025 the meteorological authority based its seasonal forecast on satellite rainfall estimates and rain-gauge data. An FAO assessment put 2025 cereal production at 5.2 million tonnes, 22 per cent down (2026). It used satellite rainfall and vegetation data in place of destroyed weather stations. A July 2026 satellite analysis found nearly 40,000 square kilometres of farmland degraded since the war began. In February 2025 a civil defence situation room opened in Port Sudan, using a national forecast model hosted in Italy since July 2024. No national space agency, ground station or geospatial data policy had been found as at November 2026.

In January 2021 the US Export-Import Bank and Sudan's government signed a memorandum for up to USD 1 billion in export financing. Its sectors include 5G. No transaction under it has been found. No hyperscaler cloud region or government system on a US platform had been found either.

Gulf/UAE activities

In January 2023 YahClick, part of the Abu Dhabi satellite operator Yahsat, signed a six-year USD 15 million partnership with Canar. In January 2026 the Emirati processor Network International signed a payments agreement with the Saudi Sudanese Bank. No volumes or timeline were given. In October 2026 the Saudi payments firm EdfaPay and a Sudanese IT company launched a joint venture, SDPay. No Gulf-owned data centre or Gulf holding in a Sudanese network has been found.

Digital sovereignty

The Telecommunications and Post Regulatory Authority's 2025 data-centre and cloud regulation bars providers from storing data in its two highest classification levels in cloud abroad. The bar covers foreign providers with an agent or contract in Sudan. The second level may go abroad where necessary, with the approval of the requesting body's head and the regulators. The regulation exempts government data centres, government cloud services and the public key infrastructure. Internet-application licensees must declare whether their servers are hosted inside or outside Sudan and name the hosting company (August 2026).

In May 2026 the Cybersecurity Authority said SudaPass is developed and operated by Sudanese professionals, with keys managed by the National Authority for Digital Certification. SudaPass stores its data in government-operated data centres in Sudan, a commitment its terms repeat for primary data. The national root certificate is not included in browsers, so .gov.sd websites are secured through a partnership with Sectigo.

From 3 July 2026 no licensed bank, fintech or switch operator may enter a relationship with another, or exchange data, without the central bank's prior approval. In July 2026 the central bank revoked the payment-system licence of Al-Asjad Digital and Smart Solutions after a technical and legal review.

In March 2026 the communications minister discussed an alternative national data centre with Red Sea State. He called it the largest current investment in the country's technical infrastructure. He said it was meant to restore sovereign national infrastructure and secure all government data. In July 2026 the traffic police said they back up the national vehicle and licence database at data centres abroad. Where that backup is hosted, and under whose jurisdiction, had not been published as at September 2026.
