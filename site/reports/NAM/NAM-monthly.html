Legislation and regulation

Regulations on accreditation of security products, services and providers

Regulations regarding Accreditation of Security Products and Services and Providers of such Products and Services (Electronic Transactions Act 4 of 2019) — Legal Assistance Centre (LAC) Namibia, reproducing General Notice 953 of 2025 (Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia), 17 December 2025

CRAN's regulations on the accreditation of security products, services and certification service providers under the Electronic Transactions Act of 2019 came into force on 15 June 2026. They set out which products and services need accreditation and how providers apply, and are the instrument under which the home affairs ministry later became Namibia's first accredited certification service provider.

Financial Institutions and Markets Act and NAMFISA Act

NAMFISA launches FinTech Square 2026 event to accelerate local innovation — Namibia Economist, 18 September 2026

The Namibia Financial Supervisory Authority announced its FinTech Square 2026 event for 14 October in Windhoek, with the Bank of Namibia and other partners. The event will launch the second cohort of NAMFISA's regulatory sandbox, covering crowdfunding and peer-to-peer lending, after a first cohort on microlending. Twelve exhibitors from the non-banking financial sector are to be selected.

Fintech will drive jobs, economic growth – Namfisa — New Era, 21 September 2026

The head of Namibia's financial regulator, Kenneth Matomola, said fintech could help with jobs and financial inclusion now that the Financial Institutions and Markets Act and a new Namfisa Act are in force. Both took effect on 1 May 2026. He cited youth unemployment of 44.4% in the 2023 labour force survey.

Data protection

United States health data and specimen sharing proposal

US To Phase Out HIV Support For Namibia After Data-Sharing Impasse — Health Policy Watch, 8 September 2026

The United States will stop funding Namibia's HIV programme after 2027, providing $45m for fiscal 2027 for transition, after Namibia rejected US terms for sharing health data and pathogen information. Namibia has received about $1.1bn from PEPFAR over 22 years and already funds most treatment for around 220,000 people. It said the US terms conflict with constitutional privacy rights and its sovereignty over biological resources.

Regional collaboration

AfCFTA Digital Trade Regulatory Assessment

Namibia reviews digital trade framework for AfCFTA protocol implementation — Tech Review Africa, 5 October 2026

Namibia's assessment of its laws and institutions against the African free-trade area's Protocol on Digital Trade went to a validation workshop in Windhoek. It will feed a National Digital Trade Action Plan; no findings are published.

Standards

Harmonised government communication strategy

Namibia's economic armoury: How new government communication strategy could unlock multibillion-dollar investment — Business Express, 13 September 2026

Namibia's ICT minister Emma Theofelus launched the Harmonised Government Communication Strategy 2026–2030 and the Government Corporate Brand Identity Manual at the public administration institute NIPAM on 9 September 2026. Cabinet approved both in March 2026; they set a single voice and brand for ministries, agencies, regional and local authorities and public enterprises.

Membership of the international standards organisation

ISO - NSI - Namibian Standards Institution — International Organization for Standardization, 16 October 2026

ISO lists the Namibian Standards Institution, set up under the Standards Act 2005, as Namibia's member body. As of October 2026 it participates in 21 technical committees and observes 23.

Universal Service Fund annual accounts

Namibia — Universal Service Fund Integrated Annual Report 2025 — Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia (CRAN), for the Universal Service Fund, 1 September 2026

The Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia published the Universal Service Fund's 2025 integrated annual report, which carries the Fund's first financial statements, covering the 18 months to 31 December 2025. Universal service fund phase 2 award to the mobile operator

Namibia accelerates network modernization to meet growing connectivity needs - Namibia Daily News — Namibia Daily News (Xinhua), 28 September 2026

Namibia's Mobile Telecommunications Company said on 28 September 2026 it has extended 5G to Grootfontein, Mariental, Okahandja, Otjiwarongo and Tsumeb and is building 19 new sites in nine regions. It was awarded N$32.3 million from phase 2 of the regulator CRAN's Universal Service Fund for 10 towers in underserved areas; ICT minister Emma Theofelus put 4G coverage at about 89%.

Low earth orbit satellite broadband service

Paratus Namibia launches Eutelsat’s OneWeb LEO connectivity — ITWeb Africa, 7 October 2026

Paratus Namibia launched Eutelsat's OneWeb low-earth-orbit satellite internet service across Namibia on 7 October 2026, with capped and uncapped packages for homes and businesses. Data Storage

National and public data centre

AI to boost digital services: Theofelus — New Era, 25 September 2026

Namibia's ICT minister, Emma Theofelus, said at the launch of the National AI Institute in Windhoek on 23 September 2026 that 4G covers about 89% of the country and internet use is about 62%. She said construction of the National Data Centre at Kenya House has begun. No measurement date for the coverage figures is given.

NCRST opens National AI Institute — New Era, 25 September 2026

Namibia's National Commission on Research, Science and Technology launched the National Artificial Intelligence Institute in Windhoek, with centres for research, software, datasets and AI innovation. The commission said 2,000 students have received AI training and it has funded two GPU-enabled high-performance computing centres at the Namibia University of Science and Technology and the University of Namibia. No cost is given.

Cybersecurity

Regulator as root certification authority

Government told to stop making citizens carry ID copies — observer24.com.na, 1 September 2026

Namibia's National Root Certification Authority, operated by the communications regulator at the top of the country's digital trust chain, is technically live after a key-signing ceremony; the electronic signature provisions of the transactions act took effect on 15 June 2026.

Home Affairs becomes Namibia's first certification service provider — New Era, 3 September 2026

Namibia's communications regulator, CRAN, accredited the Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety and Security as the country's first certification service provider under the Electronic Transactions Act of 2019, announced in Windhoek in the week of 3 September 2026. The four-year accreditation lets the ministry issue digital certificates and encryption keys for government services, following a key signing ceremony on 28 August.

Global Cybersecurity Initiative

Global Cybersecurity Initiative: Unifying Africa via Cybersecurity — The Villager Newspaper, 4 September 2026

DS Tech Namibia launched the Global Cybersecurity Initiative, a private pan-African platform to connect businesses, governments and innovators on cybersecurity, in Windhoek on 3 September 2026. Chaired by Paulinus Sheehama, it starts in Namibia, Kenya, Botswana, Ghana, Nigeria, Zambia and South Africa and aims to cover 52 African countries by 2029.

Ransomware attack on the Namibian Defence Force

Hackers hit NDF: RansomHouse threatens state secrets — The Namibian, 20 September 2026

A ransomware group listed the Namibian Defence Force among its victims and published an evidence pack it says shows access to the force's systems, with a message accusing its IT department of concealing the incident. No confirmation or response from the force is on record, and the defence minister said he had not heard of it.

Namibia’s Defence Ministry hit by double-extortion ransomware attack — Tech News Namibia, 20 September 2026

Namibia's national cyber incident response team, NAM-CSIRT, confirmed unauthorised activity on the Ministry of Defence and Veterans Affairs network and attributed it to the RansomHouse ransomware group, reported on 20 September 2026. It is coordinating investigation and recovery. It did not say what data was accessed, stolen or encrypted.

National computer security incident response team

CRAN appoints Cornelia Shipindo to lead Namibia's cyber incident response — TechNews Namibia, 3 October 2026

Cornelia Shipindo became Executive of the Namibia Cyber Security Incident Response Team on 1 October 2026, the Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia, which houses the team, announced. She will lead national incident response, threat preparedness and protection of critical information infrastructure.

National public key infrastructure

Namibia strengthens digital trust — Market Watch (Namibia Media Holdings), 4 October 2026

Namibia's communications regulator launched DigiNam in Windhoek on 31 August as the public face of the national public key infrastructure, for which the regulator is root certification authority. It is meant to support electronic signatures, digital identity and authenticated online transactions under the Electronic Transactions Act of 2019.

National electronic identity card

Government briefs lawmakers on new digital e-ID card — Namibia Economist, 6 October 2026

Namibia's home affairs and ICT ministries briefed members of parliament on the rollout of a new electronic identity card. MPs asked about system security, cyberattacks, backups and who owns and controls the technology where private providers are involved, and about a proposed Resident Identity Card for long-term foreign residents. Digital Payments and Fintech

Instant Payment Solution

Explainer: What is WayaMe? — The Namibian, 3 September 2026

WayaMe is the public name for Namibia's new instant payment system, operated by Instant Payments Namibia under the Bank of Namibia. It routes payments between participating banks and e-money wallets, which customers reach through their existing apps or USSD. The system is expected over time to carry government grants and pensions. No launch date is given.

Cash social grant payments through NamPost

NamPost grant system faces scrutiny, elderly left stranded — The Namibian, 7 September 2026

Retired Namibian MP Elifas Dingara called on the government and Namibia Post Limited, which pays social cash grants, to address failures in the grant payment system. He said elderly beneficiaries had travelled long distances and queued only to find their names missing at payment points. He asked for answers on missing beneficiaries, delayed and unpaid grants, and complaint timeframes.

NamPost grant system faces scrutiny, elderly left stranded — The Namibian, 7 September 2026

5 700 grant beneficiaries still locked out of payment system — Namibian Sun, 10 September 2026

More than 5,700 Namibian pensioners and other social-grant beneficiaries remain locked out of the government payment system after their grants were suspended during an identity verification exercise earlier in 2026, the gender equality and child welfare minister told the National Assembly. She said 15,785 were affected in all, of whom 10,066 had verified their identities, been reinstated and paid, and defended the exercise as ensuring public money reaches only legitimate beneficiaries while acknowledging it had delayed vulnerable people.

National addressing system

Request for Proposal for Consultancy Services to Develop a White Paper on the National Digital Addressing System for Namibia (SC/RP/NSA-04/2026) — Namibia Statistics Agency, 28 September 2026

Namibia's statistics agency is seeking consultants to write a white paper on a national digital addressing system. The tender notes that the country has no national addressing policy or law.

Marriage records digitalisation

Home affairs minister Iipumbu urges married couples to ensure they are registered — The Namibian, 10 October 2026

Namibia's home affairs minister, Lucia Iipumbu, told the National Assembly that some marriages remain unregistered because registers held by churches and magistrates' courts reach the ministry late or never. The gap came to light during records digitisation begun in 2021; the system relies on paper submission and does not track delivery, and some historical records are still in South Africa. Couples are urged to check their registration.

Child-grant payments to ineligible and deceased recipients

Audit finds N$28.8m in duplicate child grant payments — The Namibian, 13 October 2026

Namibia's Auditor-General found that the ministry responsible for child grants kept paying 32,890 beneficiaries aged 19 to 21, at N$250m a year, in 2024/25, reported on 13 October 2026. The audit also found 3,350 duplicate child records, with N$28.8m paid twice, and more than N$43m paid to deceased recipients, nearly 4,000 of them over two months after their deaths were registered.

E-Policing system

NamPol pushes for digital investment ….ICT overhaul to modernise services — New Era, 16 October 2026

The Namibian Police has begun overhauling its E-Policing System after slow processing and intermittent interruptions, Acting Inspector-General Anne-Marie Nainda told New Era. Government pension fund integrated administration system

GIPF to invest N$40m in new pension administration system — The Brief (Namibia), 23 September 2026

Namibia's Government Institutions Pension Fund plans to spend about N$40m on a new pension administration system built by Kenyan firm Cystech, its chief operating officer, Elvis Nashilongo, said, reported on 23 September 2026. Innovate4Water smart water-metering pilot

Namibia project uses household data, smart meters to rethink water — Science Granting Councils Initiative Africa, 21 September 2026

Innovate4Water, a research project led by the Namibia University of Science and Technology, surveyed 692 households in Windhoek, Rundu and Ongwediva and found only 25% reported uninterrupted water supply. In Ongwediva the team fitted 69 pulse readers to household meters to produce time-stamped consumption data, with AI to flag possible leaks and unauthorised use. No verified reduction in water losses is reported.

AI

Central bank and regulator shared artificial-intelligence programme

BANK OF NAMIBIA, CRAN LAUNCH AI PROGRAMME — Namibian Broadcasting Corporation, 1 September 2026

The Bank of Namibia and the communications regulator CRAN have launched a programme to build shared AI infrastructure for public agencies and find uses in advisory services, procurement, licensing and scam reporting. Agencies are to buy AI hardware and software jointly rather than separately, and the roadmap includes AI in indigenous languages.

Ongiini-Eval-OW translation benchmark for Oshindonga and Oshikwanyama

The Ongiini-Eval-OW Benchmark: A Concept Paper for the Planned Benchmarking of Machine Translation and Large Language Models on Oshindonga and Oshikwanyama — arXiv, 1 September 2026

A concept paper on arXiv announces Ongiini-Eval-OW, a planned 600-item benchmark for English to Oshindonga and English to Oshikwanyama translation, with references from two independent native-speaker translators, a 30% blind split and scoring on chrF++, BLEU and COMET-22 plus a 50-item human evaluation. The authors say no machine-translation evaluation benchmark exists for either standardised Oshiwambo variety, which major commercial services, NLLB-200, MADLAD-400 and the Masakhane checkpoints do not cover; first public release is targeted for Q4 2026 under CC-BY-4.0 for data and MIT for code.

US firm 6th Grain Corporation is demanding N$13 million from Namibia's government after Cabinet ordered its crop-monitoring contract cancelled for failing legal and procedural requirements.
