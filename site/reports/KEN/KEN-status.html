This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Kenya's National AI Strategy 2025–2030 is costed at KSh 152bn over five years, and the 2026/27 national budget gives it no dedicated allocation. The 2026/27 KSh 8.6bn ICT vote goes instead to broadband expansion, digital skills and government digitisation.

On 21 July 2026 the Ministry of ICT and Digital Development opened public participation on the Kenya Artificial Intelligence and Other Emerging Technologies Policy, with submissions due by 4 August. The draft claims jurisdiction over any entity outside Kenya whose AI outputs are used in the country or have direct and foreseeable effects there. It is silent on penalties. A submission to the consultation argues that the draft gives the sponsoring ministry an equity stake in the ventures it supports, making one body investor, regulator and procurer. In September 2026 the Senate's ICT committee, after 143 submissions, recommended that the Senate not proceed with its Artificial Intelligence Bill, 2026 until a national AI policy is published. A second draft, the National AI Policy for Science, Research and Innovation, was open for public comment until 14 October 2026.

The Kenya Cloud Policy entered into force on 2 May 2025 on publication in the Kenya Gazette. In August 2026 the committee that is to accredit providers to host state data had not been constituted, and no list of eligible providers had been published.

The most recent national ICT policy found is that of November 2019, which carries no adoption date. The Digital Master Plan runs to 2032. The National Broadband Strategy's period ended in 2023. A third strategy for 2025–2030 targets 100,000 km more fibre, 99 per cent 4G coverage and half of households connected. A DPI roadmap was launched at an inaugural inter-ministerial steering committee and was still being developed in September 2025. A draft National DPI Roadmap was taken up at the second national-county ICT forum in Naivasha, reported on 30 October 2026. At the same forum the ICT ministry said it would present counties with a draft policy to make wayleave permit rules and charges uniform. No text of the draft roadmap has been published.

Legislation and regulation

Kenya's Virtual Asset Service Providers Regulations were gazetted on 22 July 2026 as Legal Notice No. 134. Under the 2026 regulations, minimum capital is KES 300m for stablecoin issuance, KES 150m for a wallet provider, KES 100m for an exchange and nil for an investment adviser. The text gazetted in July 2026 drops the draft's one-third ownership cap and requires approval for a shareholding above 10%.

The National Payment System Bill, 2026 was published for comment on 21 September 2026. It would repeal the National Payment System Act and license payment providers and system operators by category. The Bill would also oblige interoperability and consent-based open-finance data sharing. Regulations for non-deposit-taking credit providers, gazetted on 29 September 2026, revoke the 2022 digital credit rules. They require separate consent for personal data and human review of AI lending decisions.

Gazette notices of 7 August 2026 set licence conditions for public communications access centres, equipment vendors, network facilities, landing rights and international gateways. From 7 September 2026 every cyber café must record each customer's name and identity number, the terminal used and the session times. It must keep the record for three years. In October 2026 the ICT ministry put a revised draft Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Bill, 2026 out for comment until 17 November 2026. A separate Online Safety Bill, 2026 was published as National Assembly Bill No. 69 in October 2026. Tabled by a private member in November 2026, it requires parental consent for users under 18 and proposes fines on platforms of up to KSh 500 million.

On 27 October 2026 the National Assembly deleted clauses vesting election technology's intellectual property in the electoral commission and requiring local servers. A Bill sponsored by the Senate majority and minority leaders would delete the commission's statutory duty to livestream polling-station results. In October 2026 the National Assembly's petitions committee recommended amending the Law of Succession Act so that land succession can be done through eCitizen.

Since 27 December 2024 non-residents earning through a digital marketplace have paid a significant economic presence tax, an effective 3% of gross revenue. It replaced a 1.5% digital service tax, and the two had raised KSh2.3bn from 454 foreign providers by August 2025.

Data protection

On 13 May 2026 the High Court found evidence of a systemic compromise of subscriber data at a telecommunications operator. It awarded each of eleven subscribers KES 900,000 for the violation of their constitutional rights. By 3 August 2026 the Gambling Regulatory Authority was investigating betting operators named as recipients of the data.

The Data Protection Act 2019 establishes the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner as an independent regulator. It opened 2026 having processed more than 9,000 complaints, issued 184 enforcement orders and awarded more than KES 30 million in compensation. About 15,000 data handlers had registered with it by January 2026. In May 2026 the ICT Cabinet Secretary threatened 185 betting and gaming firms holding children's data with shutdown for failing to register. The Public Service Commission shortlisted 13 of 152 applicants to succeed the inaugural commissioner in September 2026. In November 2026 the President nominated Lydia Sitienei, legal director of the Communications Authority, and sent the nomination to the National Assembly for approval.

The ODPC published guidance notes on cross-border data transfers in September 2026. Personal data processed for the "strategic interest of the state" must be processed in Kenya, or have at least one serving copy stored there. That interest covers identity, elections, public finance, protected computer systems, education and healthcare. On 3 August 2026 the ODPC put draft guidance on artificial intelligence and privacy-enhancing technologies out for comment. It requires an adequacy assessment and a lawful transfer basis before personal data leaves the country for AI processing. Controllers must notify the ODPC of a notifiable breach within 72 hours.

A High Court injunction of 19 December 2025 froze the US–Kenya health framework. The Court of Appeal lifted the order in May 2026, with a final ruling due at the end of October. In September 2026 the deputy data commissioner described a memorandum under which the central bank will license a digital lender only once it holds the regulator's compliance certificate. The communications regulator told a parliamentary committee that deactivation and recycling of inactive numbers now run under its 2026 procedures.

Regional collaboration

In July 2026 East African Community rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection were approved, but must still go to the bloc's decision-making organs before partner states can adopt them. The regional data-sharing framework behind them went to a validation workshop in Dar es Salaam in June 2026.

On 24 July 2026 the 29th EAC Monetary Affairs Committee held the target date for a single currency at 2031. It minuted that no partner state, Kenya included, had met all four primary convergence criteria. Its remedy was a peer review mechanism for macroeconomic surveillance.

As at March 2026 Kenya had neither signed nor ratified the African Union's Malabo Convention, in force since June 2023 and ratified by twenty member states. The Cabinet approved a step towards accession in September 2025, and the data protection commissioner led consultations closing on 6 October 2025. A parliamentary dialogue on accession was called for 7 September 2026. Kenya signed the Digital Cooperation Organization's charter in New York as a signatory member, reported on 28 September 2026.

In 2026 Kenya was selected for a pilot integrating digital public infrastructure into cross-border trade under the African Continental Free Trade Area. Its wholesale settlement system connects to the East African Payment System and the COMESA regional arrangement. PesaLink joined the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System for local-currency transfers in 2026. Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda accept each other's national IDs as travel documents. Kenya's 730 km fibre from Eldoret to the South Sudan border at Nadapal was complete by May 2025. A fibre link with Uganda at Busia was built but awaited an agreement between the two ICT agencies in August 2026. The revenue authorities of Kenya and Uganda have exchanged scanned-cargo images since July 2024. In March 2026 the central bank signed a memorandum with Rwanda's letting payment providers licensed in either country operate in the other. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, regional integration fell 14.1 points over 2014–2023 to 43.7 of 100, 31st of 54 African states.

Standards

In 2026 a submission to the AI policy consultation found that the standards-setting mandate the draft gives a proposed Technology Council overlaps that of the Kenya Bureau of Standards. The Bureau is the country's ISO member body, participating in 129 technical committees and observing in 177 (October 2026). In April 2020 its standards council approved the international standards on data governance and data quality as Kenya Standards. In September 2026 the central bank published a draft guidance note that would set mandatory guardrails for AI and machine learning in licensed financial institutions.

In 2025 the World Bank found the government interoperability framework in place but only partly used. The ICT Authority issued the framework's third edition in February 2026. Performance contracts for 2026/27 require every ministry, county, department and agency to adopt it. Procurement runs through an electronic system used across government to enforce technical standards. The Communications Authority has published guidelines for VoIP services, and no direct-to-cell satellite service had been approved as at 4 August 2026. The ICT Authority set minimum ICT competences and certifications in a standard in force from July 2023.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In July 2026 the High Court declared two provisions of the 2025 amendment of the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act unconstitutional. It struck out the coordination committee's power to shut websites without judicial oversight, and a provision criminalising speech deemed to cause suicide. The Bloggers Association of Kenya's Court of Appeal challenge struck down the Act's false-publication sections. A conservatory order blocks any national internet shutdown pending a full hearing. In August 2026 the National Assembly asked the Supreme Court to restore sections 22 and 23, with fines of up to KSh 5m and up to ten years' imprisonment. An Access to Information (Amendment) Bill would criminalise sharing classified information over personal WhatsApp or email.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, media freedom fell 16.8 points over 2014–2023 to 57.8 of 100, 21st of 54 African states. At the Ol Kalou by-election on 16 July 2026 masked men assaulted NTV, Nation and Star journalists and took their cameras while police stood by.

On 6 October 2026 the cohesion commission launched a roadmap for a violence-free 2027 election. It names the weaponisation of AI and technology-facilitated gender-based violence as key threats. The public consultation on the AI and emerging technologies policy closed on 4 August 2026. A civil-society coalition sat with the regulators at a July 2026 technical review of a proposed joint platform-governance framework. The review recommended mandated disclosure of algorithmic parameters and researcher access to platform data. An online citizen participation platform is in operation. In June 2026 the National Assembly's finance committee took views on the Finance Bill by a QR-code platform and by email, beside 13 county hearings. On 12 August 2026 the National Assembly passed the Public Participation Bill 2025, which lists online forums among the permitted modes and lets the public sue an authority that fails to consult. No presidential assent has been found. Kenya hosts the global Internet Governance Forum in December 2026.

A €1.485m EU grant runs from 2024 to 2028 to bring young people in Kwale and Nairobi into digital policy design. A €1.3m EU grant for civil society as an independent actor of accountability ran from 2023 to 2026. The EU-funded KenSafeSpace digital-rights coalition closed in July 2026 after 30 months.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Kenya's own money carries 86.7 per cent of the digital lines in the 2026/27 national budget as appropriated, KES 40.4 billion of KES 46.7 billion, against 88.8 per cent of KES 49.3 billion in the 2025/26 budget as revised and 66.2 per cent in 2024/25 as appropriated.

The 2026/27 development estimates give the Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project KES 4.2 billion of loan money, and KES 500 million each to the last-mile county network and the Horn of Africa Gateway. The World Bank credit behind the project closes in 2028. The 2024/25 estimates had carried KES 4.95 billion of foreign loan for the Konza data centre and smart-city facilities. No Konza allocation was announced for 2026/27. The data centre's expansion was given KES 175 million for 2026/27.

In 2025/26, KES 3.53 billion of the KES 3.90 billion e-Citizen line was fee income collected as appropriations-in-aid. Draft Treasury regulations of August 2026 would turn the platform's convenience fee into a statutory access fee for its operation and maintenance. An amendment to the NG-CDF Act lets constituencies spend up to 3 per cent of their funds on ward digital hubs (August 2026). In 2024/25 e-government services spent KES 2.41 billion and e-Citizen KES 2.60 billion, against KES 4.69 billion and KES 690 million voted.

Cabinet approved NTSA's smart driving-licence partnership with KCB and Pesa Print in December 2025. As approved in 2025 it runs 21 years on KES 42 billion of private debt and equity, recovered through a KES 3,000 licence charge and instant fines. High Court orders of 12 March 2026 halted automated instant fines, and the authority withdrew the system's launch notice on 27 March. On 27 October 2026 the revenue authority began replacing its own electronic cargo seals with user-owned seals from several vendors.

MoUs and other agreements

In June 2026 the EU–Kenya Digital Partnership put KES 15.3 billion, about US$118 million, behind Kenya's digital infrastructure, connectivity and innovation. The sum was approved, and no disbursement has been found. Tether and the Nairobi Securities Exchange signed a memorandum of understanding on 28 July 2026. It covers blockchain infrastructure for tokenising and settling NSE securities, and an undertaking to assess tether as a settlement layer where permitted. No commitment figure was disclosed.

The December 2025 health cooperation framework with the United States states an intention to fund health data systems, not an obligation. The EcoCloud–G42 agreement of March 2024 for a geothermal-powered data centre is a memorandum of understanding only. The technology track with South Korea, widened in August 2026, carries no amount or instrument. In October 2026 the ICT Authority and the Korea Data Agency signed a letter of intent on preparing government data for AI use.

New investments

The World Bank's US$390 million Digital Economy Acceleration Project, running 2023–2028, is the largest single live public commitment to Kenya's digital estate.

Kenya's digital estate has drawn 353 external commitments, worth about US$4.6 billion on windows running from 2015 to 2033 (November 2026). Of these, 59 are active, 37 completed and 250 carry no recorded status (November 2026). Almost half the money is one transaction, Vodacom's US$2.1 billion purchase of a controlling stake in Safaricom, now suspended, which makes equity the instrument carrying the most money, about US$2.2 billion across 13 (November 2026). Concessional loans carry about US$1.1 billion across ten, grants are the commonest instrument, 304 worth about US$570 million, and ten commercial loans account for a further US$137 million (November 2026). Public recipients take 43 commitments and private recipients 77, and leaving the Safaricom stake aside public recipients take about 65 per cent of the money (November 2026). After that stake, connectivity draws more money than any other part of the estate, 21 commitments worth about US$770 million (November 2026). USAID appears more often than any other financier, 52 times, ahead of the Gates Foundation's 35 (November 2026). The largest active commitment is the World Bank's US$390 million digital economy acceleration project (November 2026).

In April 2026 the Nigerian fintech Moniepoint entered the market by buying the CBK-licensed Sumac Microfinance Bank. Kenya takes all three of the IFC's first Catalytic First Loss Guarantee transactions in Africa. They give US$24.2 million of first-loss cover to local-currency lending by 4G Capital, Equity Bank Kenya and KCB Bank Kenya (August 2026). Korea's development cooperation fund finances a Digital Media City at Konza Technopolis and the second phase of Nairobi's intelligent transport system.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Mobile signal covered 98.0 per cent of Kenya's population in the year to 30 June 2025, with 4G at 97.9 per cent and 5G at 30.0 per cent. The 2023/24 housing survey found internet use of around 35 per cent. Active mobile subscriptions reached 88.0 million at 30 June 2026, a count of SIMs. In 2024, 53.7 per cent of individuals owned a mobile telephone.

Fixed internet subscriptions reached 2.84 million at 30 June 2026, up 32.4 per cent in a year, and fibre carried 1.57 million of them. Safaricom held 69.8 per cent of mobile subscriptions at 30 June 2026. In September 2026 it became the first provider to pass one million fixed broadband subscribers, with a 36.1 per cent share. Starlink held 27,616 subscriptions at 30 June 2026, about 1 per cent of the fixed market. International bandwidth rose 18.5 per cent to 28,950 Gbps in the year to June 2026. The Nairobi internet exchange, managed by the providers' association TESPOK, has been active since February 2001. Two terrestrial routes inland start in Kenya: Liquid's Mombasa–Johannesburg route of May 2023 and Google's Umoja route to Australia, announced in May 2024.

The standing national target is 100,000 kilometres of fibre, and 37,000 kilometres had been installed by 2 September 2026. In October 2026 the ICT Authority tendered framework agreements worth KSh 13 billion for at least 27,000 km of backbone and cross-border links, on World Bank finance. A separate 17,000 km government project is to connect public institutions; no route, cost or completion date has been found. On 13 October 2026 the Communications Authority published a draft satellite licensing framework making landing rights the prerequisite and requiring operators to record every terminal. Axian Telecom completed its purchase of 99.63 per cent of Wananchi Group, owner of Zuku, in November 2025. In October 2026 Airtel Kenya paid KSh 8.4bn for 100 MHz of 5G spectrum in the 3500 MHz band. Bayobab said in October 2026 that it had laid 2,038 km of fibre on the Kenyan and South Sudanese segments of its East to West route, with activation next and no date given.

After Vodacom's suspended purchase of a Safaricom stake, connectivity draws more external money than any other part of the digital estate: 21 commitments worth about US$771 million (November 2026).

Data Storage

The US$1 billion Microsoft–G42 data centre at Olkaria missed its May 2026 launch. President William Ruto said in mid-2026 that its planned 1 GW draw would take a third of national supply. Talks broke down over the capacity the government was asked to commit to lease.

In April 2026 Konza National Data Centre hosted 145 of Kenya's 451 government institutions. It drew about 300 kW of IT load against 706 kW of certified capacity, and its 1,292 TB of storage was fully used. Bids for a transaction adviser on an expansion to 280–300 racks closed on 22 April 2026. In October 2026 the Treasury's PPP facilitation fund agreed to pay for the advisory and feasibility work, with no amount stated and no private partner selected. A High Court award of December 2022 over the National Disaster Recovery Site still stood at Kshs 4,091,619,712 outstanding on 30 June 2025. The first supplementary budget for 2025/26 allocated KSh 2bn for a new data recovery site at Konza.

Africa Data Centres runs a Tier III Nairobi facility with 7.5 MW of site capacity, and iXAfrica's 22.5 MW facility was East Africa's largest announced data centre in August 2026. Airtel's 44 MW Nxtra build at Tatu City is due in July 2027, moved from the first quarter of 2027. Safaricom moved M-Pesa's core onto Huawei Cloud, hosted locally, in September 2025. Sovereign-cloud services opened on iXAfrica's floor from Servernah in March 2026, Baobab in May 2026 and Tata Consultancy Services in September 2026. Oracle was still preparing its Nairobi region there in September 2026.

In 2026 the state spent about KSh 10 billion a year on ICT and data-centre running costs. AWS runs a Local Zone in Nairobi parented to its Cape Town region. It runs several separate cloud platforms on a hybrid model, and holds government data inside and outside the country under arrangements it does not publish.

Energy

Afrobarometer's 2024 survey found 82 per cent of rural respondents living within reach of the national grid, but only 51 per cent of rural households connected. In towns the 2024 figures were 98 per cent reach and 90 per cent connection. About 76 per cent of the population had access to electricity in 2023.

Access to energy was Kenya's third most improved measure in the 2024 Ibrahim Index. It rose 30.9 points over 2014–2023 to 52.8 of 100, 16th of 54 African states.

Renewable sources supplied 80.17 per cent of the electricity mix in the year to June 2025. The World Bank-funded Kenya Off-Grid Solar Access Project began in 2017. It is the main programme extending off-grid solar into 14 underserved counties. Development finance now buys the household receivables outright: Norfund took NOK 177.2 million of a US$130 million securitisation for Sun King Kenya in 2023. It was structured entirely in Kenyan shillings and raised against customer payments on metered solar systems collected through mobile money. The country's flagship hyperscale data centre stalled, the President saying its draw would take a third of national supply. KenGen said in February 2026 that the energy regulator had pre-cleared direct supply from its Olkaria geothermal plants to data centres in its green energy park.

Kenya Power put average interruption time at 113 hours a customer in the year to June 2025, down from 120.6 hours.

Technical Capacity

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, Kenya's public administration score fell 2.5 points over 2014–2023 to 56.1 of 100, 24th of 54 African states. Effective administration held at 69.3 of 100 and 10th place, almost unchanged over the decade.

A May 2025 ranking puts Kenya's software engineering workforce among the largest in Africa. Three senior Safaricom executives in its financial services business left within five months of March 2026. One went to run Stanbic Bank Kenya, and the last left on 31 July 2026 with only an interim successor named. Inside government, ICT staff are graded under a scheme of service issued in February 2007, across eleven grades from ICT Officer III to Secretary ICT. The Treasury's reform secretariat built its public finance reform system, REFORMIS, in-house, and it has run since 2023 across more than 39 ministries and agencies. Makueni County's ICT team built its project-tracking system without external vendors, launched in March 2024.

Cybersecurity

In November 2025 a coordinated attack took the presidency portal offline with the sites of five ministries, the immigration department and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations. On 18 July 2026 attackers defaced president.go.ke and demanded five Bitcoin. State House worked with the National KE-CIRT/CC to restore the site. Kenya reached Tier 1, the role-modelling tier, in the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index (2024/25). The National KE-CIRT/CC detected 2,696,335,021 cyber threat events in July–September 2026, up 14.45 per cent on the previous quarter. Most were exploits of unpatched systems, and government, internet providers and cloud providers were the most affected. Parliament approved the National Cyber Security Agency Order, 2026 in June 2026. It creates a central authority for critical-infrastructure protection, incident response and resilience audits. Its board, chaired by Martin Koyabe, was inaugurated in November 2026 and told to build the operations centre's real-time detection and response capacity. In November 2026 the ICT ministry launched the SecureKE/Konza Sovereign Security Operations Centre, a Konza Technopolis and SecureKE partnership monitoring threats and coordinating responses for public and private institutions. EU money backs a 2023–2029 security and cybersecurity programme and a €3 million Kenya Cyber Resilience project launched in January 2026.

Cybersecurity carries 19 external commitments on record, together about US$10.7 million, US$9.4 million of it in two from the European Union (November 2026).

In July–September 2026 the Communications Authority received 47 complaints on fraud and scams and 49 on cybercrime and criminal use of ICT infrastructure. The regulator blames unauthorised SIM replacement, identity theft and weak customer verification. In July 2026 the High Court held Safaricom 60 per cent and Diamond Trust Bank 40 per cent liable for a KES 4.4 million SIM-swap fraud. The health data exchange's shared resources are designated critical information infrastructure by regulation. Since October 2025 critical information infrastructure may use digital certificates only from certification providers the regulator has licensed. In October 2026 the communications minister told the Senate that the national incident response centre's forensics laboratory had received more than 200 requests, most involving online abuse.

DPI

Data Exchange

The Digital Health (Data Exchange Component) Regulations, 2025 commenced on gazettement on 11 April 2025. They require users of the exchange's shared resources to pay the service fee in the Third Schedule, and make non-payment a ground for action. As served by Kenya Law in August 2026, that schedule carries its heading and no figures. The shared resources include the client, facility and health worker registries and the national logistics platform. A 2020 Ministry of Health framework set out those registries, an interoperability layer and certification of health information systems. On 4 August 2025 the Health Cabinet Secretary said the Health Information Exchange was operational in every facility contracted by the Social Health Authority.

In 2026 Kenya ran more than 20,000 digital government services, with eCitizen as a one-stop portal for several of them. An X-Road test environment run from April 2024 to June 2025 produced a framework document, not a production platform. The World Bank records the interoperability framework as only partly used. Tax, customs, human resources, debt, procurement and financial management connect point to point and through a Government Service Bus.

In 2025 iTax was integrated with the National Registration Bureau, the transport management system and the Business Registration Service. The land register is wired point to point to eCitizen, iTax, the population register and the business register. The social protection single registry checks cash-transfer beneficiaries against their national ID numbers. The trade single window, upgraded in August 2021, is fully integrated with the customs system. Its general regulations have been in force since 8 March 2024. The revenue authority's GavaConnect API platform had onboarded more than 2,500 developers by March 2026.

In February 2026 the electoral commission was still negotiating an API link to the population register. In October 2026 it said it was setting voter-registration targets from registration bureau and immigration data on people holding identity cards.

Digital Identity and CRVS

In late October 2025 a gazette notice waived the KSh 1,000 replacement fee for a national identity card for six months, and the fee for a first card has been scrapped. In January 2025 the High Court ordered more than 40,000 vetted citizens wrongly registered as refugees removed from the refugee database within 60 days.

Section 9A of the Registration of Persons Act, inserted in 2018, is the system's statutory base; no standalone digital identity act has been enacted. The 2020 NIIMS Rules establish the database, the Maisha Namba and card, and make the credential legal proof of identity. Registration is compulsory for citizens at 18. Biometrics are ten fingerprints, facial images and iris scans, DNA and GPS collection having been struck down in Nubian Rights Forum. The identifier is assigned at birth registration, with a minors' card at six and an adult card at 18. Issuance at 18 is moving to automatic generation from civil registration data.

By August 2025 biometric patient registration was operational in all level 4 to 6 public health facilities. The 2025 subscriber rules require it for SIM registration. The number is a shared identifier across education, health, tax, social security, eCitizen and the land registries. In October 2026 registration officers in Bungoma told a National Assembly committee that staff, vehicle and equipment shortages were holding back registration. In September 2025 the Interior Cabinet Secretary said more than one million Kenyans aged 30 and over held no national identity card. At least 557,000 identity cards lay uncollected in November 2026, nearly 400,000 of them for more than ten years.

The courts declared the Huduma Namba system illegal in 2021, and have since suspended the Maisha Namba rollout for want of a data protection impact assessment. In August 2026 the High Court halted the Maisha Card rollout pending an activists' petition. No such assessment for Maisha Namba has been made public, and no access log is open to the data subject. On 6 October 2026 the immigration principal secretary said electronic birth notification would reach every hospital contracted by the social health insurer by December 2026.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Safaricom cut M-Pesa business tariffs by up to half from 7 August 2026, citing the Central Bank's pricing principles. The free threshold on Lipa na M-Pesa Buy Goods doubled from Sh200 to Sh500.

Safaricom reported 40.99m one-month-active M-Pesa customers in Kenya at 31 March 2026, and KES 41.68tn moved across 46.41bn transactions. It held 88.8% of mobile money subscriptions at 30 June 2026. On 15 September 2026 the High Court declared void the government's sale of 15 per cent of Safaricom and restored the shares to the government. Mobile money subscriptions stood at 54.0m at 30 June 2026. In 2024, 90.7% of adults made or received a digital payment.

A draft National Payment System Policy of 21 September 2026 commits the government to a national instant payment switch. The Central Bank owns and operates the wholesale KEPSS system. The retail instant scheme PesaLink is owned by the Kenya Bankers Association and connects more than 80 banks, fintechs, telcos and SACCOs. Kenswitch with Visa, the banks through IPSL, and Nigeria's NIBSS with Ceva have all bid for that role. Mobile money operators are fully interoperable. Kenswitch launched a domestic card scheme on 22 September 2026, naming no issuer, date or price. On 2 November 2026 it announced a 3-D Secure partnership with PayU's Wibmo to authenticate online card payments for connected banks. Since November 2026 customers of other participating institutions can deposit cash at Postbank's more than 600 agent and branch points through PesaLink. From November 2026 Airtel Money users can send money from their wallets to accounts at 30 banks, SACCOs and microfinance banks on PesaLink.

On 30 September 2026 the central bank licensed 29 more digital credit providers, 281 in all since March 2022. The Treasury's eCitizen unit is tasked with making all payments to government digital. Inua Jamii cash transfers have been paid through eCitizen and M-Pesa agents since the January 2025 disbursement, and salaries and pensions moved onto an integrated payments system from August 2025. The Auditor-General found 104,631 Hustler Fund loans worth Sh116.5m issued to borrowers whose national ID details were missing (July 2026).

Registries

Birth registration completeness stood at 77.1% in 2023, and death registration runs far behind births. At the 2022 Demographic and Health Survey only 34% of registered children held a birth certificate. Civil registration fell 12.5 points over 2014–2023 to 50.0 of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 31st of 54 African states.

In 2018 births and deaths were registered on paper forms completed in duplicate, with about 20 million records awaiting digitisation. The electronic civil registration database remains partly deployed. A digital birth notification linking hospitals to Civil Registration Services started as a Nairobi pilot in 2026. After its June 2026 launch it registered more than 270,000 newborns with unique personal identifiers in two months, and nearly 300,000 births by October 2026. The Registrar of Marriages ended manual certificate printing in January 2026. In March 2026 stakeholders validated a bill to make birth and death registration digital.

In 2024 civil registration fed the Maisha Integrated Database, which combines national ID, birth and death, foreign national and refugee records. The Maisha Namba is assigned at birth registration and becomes the national ID number at 18.

Company and business name registration is fully operational online, with certificates issued only as eCitizen downloads since February 2026. Companies must file beneficial-ownership registers on pain of a KSh 500,000 penalty and strike-off. The social protection secretariat runs an enhanced single registry of vulnerable households. The Auditor-General found only Nairobi and Murang'a of 83 land registries fully migrated to ArdhiSasa, and in August 2026 the lands principal secretary said Mombasa Island and Isiolo had since joined. The platform was down again on 21 September 2026 after an outage from 1 September. In September 2026 an EU- and FAO-co-financed programme was digitising ten land registries in Kajiado, Baringo and Machakos counties. Title issuance covers under a third of the country. The voters' roll stood at 22,120,458 after the audited June 2022 revision, 79.41% of the eligible electorate. The electoral commission resumed continuous registration on 29 September 2025, with a biometric registration module added to its election technology and 28.5 million voters projected for 2027.

Sectoral management information systems

By June 2026 the Social Health Authority's system ran in 5,078 facilities, carrying patient verification and electronic claims. An interoperability layer on the OpenHIE framework and HL7 standards connects eight health systems. In November 2023 facilities with internet access entered aggregate data directly into DHIS2, and the rest reported on paper keyed in at sub-county level. The Authority has begun biometric enrolment of dependants aged 7 to 17, issuing each a Maisha Namba. The health ministry ordered contracted facilities onto certified systems by 30 September 2026, then extended the deadline to 30 October for larger facilities. It put migration at 6,427 public, 38 private and 38 of 518 faith-based facilities. On 14 October 2026 Murang'a County lost access to its own records system and sued the ministry and the health agencies. In October 2026 the ministry disabled its All Disease Outbreak Module during the Ebola response, and responders said they had not been told of a replacement. A December 2025 US framework funds named health systems, with running costs falling to Kenya. In November 2026 the ministry ran mass drug administration, malaria and bed-net campaigns on its community health information system, whose household register was still incomplete in many counties.

In June 2026 schools not registered on KEMIS were told they would receive no third-term capitation. In August 2026 the education principal secretary said the latest round ran on manually verified data because KEMIS was incomplete.

In 2025 the World Bank found a treasury single account used by all ministries, departments and agencies. IFMIS operates across national and county governments. Human resource, payroll, debt and public investment systems are custom-built, the HR system keyed to the national identifier. Since May 2022 the Integrated Customs Management System has taken every customs module. Its failure in November 2024 halted clearance at Mombasa and the main airport. E-filing reached every court station in March 2024. In September 2026 work was continuing on the police information management system, the digital occurrence book included. Live biometric capture ran at 26 of 63 police clearance centres by September 2026.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In April 2026 the Revenue Authority launched a WhatsApp tax-filing service that cuts the process from eight steps to three. In April 2026 only about 7 million of more than 22 million registered taxpayers filed. In October 2026 the authority was in talks with Safaricom for M-Pesa to issue electronic tax invoices for small traders.

Only 1,695 services were migrated onto eCitizen in the year to 31 March 2026, against a target of 11,000. Its payment layer was down for much of 26 August 2026, and the tax invoicing service reached through it was down for three days to 31 August. Draft regulations of May 2026 would have the National Treasury host and run the platform. On 27 October 2026 the Treasury said the regulations were before Parliament, approved by its delegated legislation committee. At Huduma centres, cyber cafés chosen by tender charge Ksh150 for assisted access to eCitizen (October 2026). The Auditor-General found that the Huduma secretariat has run since 2014 without the legal framework its founding notice requires.

The 2026/27 Budget Statement made electronic procurement mandatory for all government purchasing from 1 July 2026. On 4 November 2026 the Treasury said more than 1,500 procuring entities and 45,000 suppliers were on the system, with contracts worth about KSh 18.3 billion processed. The World Bank placed Kenya in the top band of its 2025 GovTech Maturity Index. Documentation of the estate is largely internal and unpublished. A verifiable-credentials wallet for civil service qualifications was completed with the Public Service Commission in March 2026. In 2026 Parliament approved a further Sh33.5bn for election technology, taking the programme to Sh74.8bn. On 5 October 2026 the High Court at Marsabit upheld the collection of visitors' travel insurance data through the Electronic Travel Authorisation system.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

As at January 2026 Kirinyaga County had installed 102.4km of fibre connecting all five of its sub-counties to the One Government Network, with nine public Wi-Fi hotspots. Kenya has 47 counties. The Integrated Financial Management Information System operates across national and county governments, on commercial software covering treasury functions and budget preparation (2025). The vendor of the national interoperability pilot found limited local connectivity, non-digitised records and siloed manual systems (2025). In September 2025 the Treasury found most counties ran unintegrated revenue systems, many operated by third parties. Draft regulations of 2025 propose one revenue system for all 47 counties.

In March 2025 the data protection regulator told counties that each department must register separately as a data controller. County governments paid at least Sh8.3 billion in salaries through manual payrolls outside the payroll system in the year to 30 June 2026. The county governments built My County Hub with Safaricom, for emergency requests, business permits and parking payments (2026). On 14 October 2026 Murang'a County lost access to its health records platform and asked the High Court to restore it across more than 176 facilities. The county says 65,000 patient records kept since 2023 are out of reach. In November 2026 Vihiga's governor said procurement, job applications and payments ran fully online, and that a county land information system was 70 per cent complete.

In October 2026 two tuk-tuk associations petitioned the High Court against Mombasa County's camera-based instant fines, saying no published law authorises them. In April 2026 the Council of Governors was piloting a County Digital Maturity Index with VNG International in Kitui, Turkana and Kisumu. Officials took the index up at a national-county forum reported on 30 October 2026. No method or scores for it have been published.

Rural digital data capture

In 2018 births and deaths were registered on duplicate paper forms and digitised afterwards in bulk. In March 2026 police had digitised the occurrence book in several Eldoret stations. Nationally, occurrence books were still due to go digital in May 2026. The national interoperability pilot met limited local connectivity, non-digitised records and siloed manual systems. Mobile signal reached almost the whole population in 2025. In 2024 only 51 per cent of rural households were connected to the grid.

InfraCo Africa invested US$6 million in Mawingu Networks in 2023. The company runs fixed wireless broadband to rural households, schools and clinics from solar-powered towers. In 2016 OPIC had lent it US$4.1 million alongside Microsoft to deploy TV white space. In November 2026 Namelok Wireless launched a community network at Kimuka for schools, government offices, clinics and small businesses, publishing no coverage or price figures.

The African Development Bank financed a Rural Transformation Centres Digital Platform, an XDR 600,860 grant for 2020–2026. It is to connect smallholders, cooperatives, agro-dealers and public services on one platform. On 30 July 2026 Heifer International, Mastercard and KCB Bank Kenya announced the Farmer Visibility Project. Its nine-month pilot digitises milk deliveries, payments and input purchases for 30,000 dairy farmers, so that the record can stand in for a credit history.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index Kenya ranks first of 54 African states for rural economy support, at 96.3 of 100. Rural market access scores 59.8 of 100, 24th of 54, down 6.3 points over 2014–2023. The Communications Authority runs a Universal Service Fund strategy for 2023–2027.

Technology

AI

In April 2026 a constitutional challenge reached the High Court against Safaricom's Zuri chatbot and the automated decisions behind M-Pesa approvals, reversals, fraud detection and credit scoring. A petition over the absence of any AI law was admitted for priority hearing in April 2026. No judgment has been found in either. In October 2026 the Senate ICT committee recommended that the Senate not proceed with the Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026 until a national AI policy is published. The National Assembly gave its innovation committee a month to report on AI in schools, hospitals and the public service.

Kenya published a National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025–2030. In June 2026 Cabinet set up a Standing Cabinet Committee on Artificial Intelligence to steer the strategy and coordinate AI policy across government. The draft AI and Emerging Technologies Policy, issued for comment on 21 July 2026, would tier AI systems by risk. High-risk deployments would need impact assessments and independent audits. It would give people a right to know when an AI system is acting on them. It would also require a public registry of AI systems used in the public sector. In August 2026 the data protection regulator issued draft guidance on offshore AI data transfers.

In July 2026 the Ministry of Health's tender for the KNPHI planning system made a generative-AI module mandatory. The supplier must disclose the model, provider, processing location and retention. In August 2026 the Digital Health Agency issued seven tenders for TaifaCare, led by an AI-driven maternal and child health platform. In November 2025 the President said AI risk engines had been built into tax compliance and piloted in customs. In October 2026 the ICT Authority and the Korea Data Agency agreed to prepare government data for AI and pilot AI in public services. Absa Bank Kenya piloted a deepfake-detection agent at account opening in 2025. Apollo Agriculture underwrites smallholder credit with satellite data and machine learning.

ICT Industry

Kenya's ICT value added rose from KSh 339.5 billion in 2023 to KSh 364.5 billion in 2024. Sector growth fell to 7.0 per cent in 2024 from 10.3 per cent the year before.

In April 2026 Sama cut 1,108 jobs in Kenya after Meta ended its content-moderation and data-annotation contract. Microsoft told staff on 12 October 2026 that it is winding down its Africa Development Centre in Nairobi. Nearly 400 engineering jobs are at risk, with fewer than ten expected to remain. The company confirmed only proposed changes to its engineering operations. In September 2024 the Court of Appeal held that Meta can be sued in Kenya by moderators it engaged through a contractor. The draft AI and Emerging Technologies Policy would set minimum standards for content moderators, data annotators and AI quality evaluators. They cover pay transparency, psychosocial support, grievance procedures and a duty of care against prolonged exposure to disturbing material. They would bind international firms working through outsourcing partners as well as local employers.

Safaricom's Daraja developer portal carried more than 105,000 developers and 66,000 integrations when it was rebuilt in November 2025. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, business and competition regulation reached 81.9 of 100, fifth of 54 African states. A plant at Athi River owned by mobile operators and device makers opened on 30 October 2023 with capacity for 3m units a year. It produced 700,000 devices in the year to March 2026. M-KOPA said its Nairobi plant had made more than 1.5 million phones by November 2024. In August 2026 the Universal Service Advisory Council turned its attention to locally assembled devices. The desktops bought for the Digital Hubs were assembled locally. In October 2026 a press report of M-KOPA's accounts showed the device financier completing its US$8m purchase of a Finnish device-locking software company.

Innovation ecosystem

Kenyan startups raised US$1.04 billion in 2025, debt included, more than in any other African market. They raised US$126 million in the first half of 2026, third behind Egypt and Nigeria and Kenya's weakest half since early 2021. Kenya ranked 96th on the WIPO Global Innovation Index (2024).

The Science, Technology and Innovation Act of 2013 remains the framework for research and innovation, including startup support, administered through NACOSTI. A Start-up Bill would make Kenya Industrial Estates the labelling body for start-ups and incubators and attach tax incentives to the label. The state's own innovation agency recorded the bill still in parliamentary debate after three years (2024). Around the Act sit innovation and technology hubs in the AfriLabs network, from JHUB Africa to Villgro Africa (2024).

An established public-sector innovation body operates with a remit for public service innovation (2025). Kenya has either a public-sector innovation strategy or a programme, not both (2025).

The IFC committed up to US$3 million as a limited partner in Savannah Fund II (2021). The Nairobi fund was raising up to US$25 million for pre-seed and seed companies in fintech, edtech, agritech and digital platforms (2021). Novastar Ventures co-led a US$10 million Series A for Turaco in 2022. Turaco is an insurtech embedding health and life cover into digital platforms, agribusinesses and logistics firms across five African markets. Zerobionics builds humanoid robots that render speech into sign language in real time for deaf students. Its robots are delivered over 2G, so that a teacher without sign language can instruct remotely. Vumah Labs builds content-authenticity detection and consent-management tooling. In May 2026 Qhala, Amini AI, Angani and iXAfrica formed the Qubit Hub to host an African AI research and compute laboratory on a Nairobi campus. The hub is aimed at compute, talent and community for builders across the region.

The draft Kenya AI and Emerging Technologies Policy 2026 extends to blockchain, the internet of things, robotics, autonomous systems, extended reality, advanced communications and quantum-related technologies.

Capacity

Literacy

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, Kenya's school enrolment score of 49.5 ranked 16th of 54 African states, against a completion score of 86.0 (2023). Education as a whole scored 67.0, sixth of 54, up 2.5 points over 2014–2023. Human resources in education is the only one of the index's 96 indicators for which Kenya returns no data.

In 2025 the World Bank found that the state's digital skills programme does not extend to citizens and schools, and that its strategy is not published. In May 2025 the ICT Cabinet Secretary launched a four-level National Digital Literacy Skills Curriculum. KICTANet, ACWICT and the British Council developed it. It aims at 20 million digitally empowered citizens by 2032.

On 21 September 2026 the second phase of the World Bank-supported Digital Literacy Programme began deliveries to 178 of Nyeri County's 377 public junior schools. It supplies smart boards and teacher laptops. EU money pays for last-mile school connectivity. UNICEF, the ITU and the government deliver it under the Giga programme across all 47 counties (October 2023).

In September 2026 the state signed a partnership with Intel to deliver AI literacy through the public library network. A UK-funded programme ran digital literacy and digital financial access work across eight arid and semi-arid counties to March 2026 (2025). The data protection regulator ran its own constituency-level advocacy in a second round of constituencies (March 2025).

In May 2025 KEMIS was set to replace NEMIS and to use the Unique Personal Identifier issued to every child, folding education data into the identity system.

Training and skills

On 22 September 2026 the Digitalization for TVET project, about €50 million from KfW, the French development agency and the EU, was still prequalifying its implementation consultant. Its five years of delivery count from that consultant's start. KfW's share is €18 million over 2025–2030, beside a separate €5 million loan of 2020–2026 for ICT-focused vocational training. The EU is spending €9.9 million over 2023–2028 on courses and facilities in selected TVET institutions. Germany funded a Digital Transformation Centre over 2021–2025 aimed at the workforce's digital skills, and committed €11 million to a successor centre for 2026–2029.

By October 2026 the Jitume hub network had 391 equipped sites and 23,387 devices, with more than 115,000 trainees. Virtual desktops ran in 186 of 253 TVET institutions, and 67 were still awaiting equipment. The Digital Hubs are to deliver digital skills, AI, software development and online work training (August 2026). On 4 November 2026 the MSME state department launched a free ten-week course on bidding for government tenders online, aimed at more than 600,000 young entrepreneurs.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a digital skills strategy and training programme for the public service. The Africa Centre of Competence for Digital and AI Skilling, launched in December 2024, aims to train 100,000 civil servants in its first phase. By February 2026 it had trained 13,790 public servants from 22 counties and 52 state corporations. A course on AI in government graduated 1,239 officers on 8 October 2026 and opened a second cohort for 9,000 on 21 October. In July 2026 parliamentary staff trained in AI-assisted research were using public AI tools with limited monitoring of data flows.

KICTANet's Kenya School of Internet Governance has trained more than 400 people since 2016. Its latest cohort drew 555 applications for 51 places, and 45 completed (August 2026).

Research institutions

In July 2026 a submission to the AI policy consultation named gaps the draft does not reach. They include research investment, AI researchers, affordable computing, secure data infrastructure and local datasets. The Science, Technology and Innovation Act 2013, run through NACOSTI, is the basis for research and innovation. Strathmore University's CIPIT runs the East African AI Policy Hub (2026). Through it, CIPIT is developing an AI adoption framework with the Judiciary. A year-long KICTANet project with Meedan classified over 1,000 Swahili-language posts of online abuse. Some of the most damaging carried nothing a keyword filter would catch (July 2026).

Inclusion

Access to services

Kenya's Financial Inclusion Fund, the Hustler Fund, received Sh300 million of state money in the year to June 2026, against Sh20 billion at launch. The Treasury has added nothing this year, and 15 per cent of the fund's lending is in default (March 2026).

Only 34 per cent of registered children held a birth certificate in 2022, and about 5 million Kenyans lacked a national ID in 2025. The ID number is the shared identifier for education, health insurance, tax and social security. The card is now free.

Legal Notice No. 90 of 30 May 2025 recognises the Refugee ID Card for SIM registration, opening mobile money to refugees. In March 2026 about 30 per cent of Kenya's roughly 836,000 registered refugees held both a Refugee ID and a mobile money account, and fewer than one in ten was banked. Documentation delays, uneven know-your-customer practice and weak links between the two identity systems hold take-up back.

In 2025 research and a Sightsavers-commissioned assessment found USSD codes that visually impaired users cannot dial, and counters and cash machines too high for wheelchair users. The National AI Strategy 2025–2030, the Persons with Disabilities Act 2025 and the ICT policy all recognise inclusion and set no accessibility standard or enforcement mechanism (July 2026). In 2022 the Kenya Bureau of Standards published KS 2952, a two-part standard for accessible ICT products and services. On 30 October 2026 the government told public institutions to audit their websites and online platforms for accessibility. Each is to draw up a funded plan under a roadmap validated that day. In November 2026 the Grassroots Disability Agenda launched AbiliSpace, an online participation platform for persons with disabilities, with a survey of Garissa, Kajiado and Marsabit counties.

The Ombudsman opened the public portal of its complaints management system in 2022. Of the 813 complaints it received against public institutions in 2025, 72 per cent were still pending at year end. Applications for review of access-to-information cases rose from 32 in 2016/17 to 654 in 2025/26.

In 2024 the data-only mobile basket cost under 2 per cent of gross national income per head. In August 2026 the ICT Cabinet Secretary asked the Chamber of Commerce to work with the government on affordable laptop financing for young people.

Digital divides

In TIFA Research's June 2026 fieldwork, lack of an identity card or other documents was the reason unregistered Kenyans most often gave for staying off the voters' roll. In June 2026 only 57 per cent of those aged 18 to 24 were registered. The gaps in the identity register run heaviest through the young.

In its annual report to June 2025 the Communications Authority records affordability and access as critical challenges. National internet use sits far below the signal's reach. At 30 June 2026 there were 52,262,565 smartphones against 27,420,856 feature phones.

In 2022 Tana River's poverty rate was 66.7 per cent. KICTANet's focus groups there found trust, not access, the limit on women's phone use. CIPIT's work with Kenyans with disabilities names device and connectivity cost, exclusion from design, and distrust of how disability data is collected (July 2026). Fuliza overdrafts crossed KES 1.46 trillion in the year to March 2026, up 49.3 per cent. The Daily Nation traces that growth to food, rent and school fees.

In August 2026, 588 Digital Hub sites were committed nationwide, against a target of one in every ward. The programme blames the last generation's failure on electricity, connectivity, maintenance and management costs. British aid gives £4.77 million to March 2027 for last-mile connectivity and £2.3 million across eight arid and semi-arid counties.

Data

National statistics

In 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators recorded no labour force survey and no business survey in Kenya within the last ten years. In 2023 its data sources pillar fell in the 20–49% band, against above 89% for data use. Population, agricultural and business censuses have all been run within ten years. The capacity of the statistical system scores 59.5 of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, flat since 2014.

The 2019 census enumerated 47,564,296 people and was the country's first paperless one. On 28 January 2025 the High Court cancelled the 2019 results for 15 sub-counties of Garissa, Mandera and Wajir and ordered a fresh count within a year. The bureau's director general said a census takes four years to plan. On 12 October 2026 the Senate finance committee reviewed a draft Statistics Bill 2026 that would turn the bureau into a Kenya Statistics Authority. It would also require each county to set up a statistics office.

In April 2026 Kenya had more than 22 million registered taxpayers but only around seven million active filers. The Central Bank counted 82.43m registered mobile-money accounts at December 2024 and 94.35m in July 2026. Safaricom reported 40.99m one-month-active M-Pesa customers at 31 March 2026. In October 2026 the social development directorate drafted a second Inclusive Data Charter action plan for 2026 to 2030.

Open data

Kenya's draft National Data Governance Policy of May 2026 carries a data-marketplace component under which the state would sell public data. It is designed around India's individual consent architecture (July 2026). It leaves open when personal data counts as anonymised and who decides.

In 2025 the World Bank found an open data portal with a full catalogue maintained, its datasets refreshed monthly to annually. The statistics bureau runs a food-security and nutrition data repository. On the 2024 Open Data Inventory, balance of payments, trade and prices score 7.5 of 10. Education facilities and food security score 4.0, and labour, national accounts and energy 4.5. Communications and digital data carries no score at all.

The Access to Information Act 2016 creates the right to request information held by public bodies. On 9 October 2026 the ICT Cabinet Secretary gazetted the Access to Information Policy, 2026. It requires public offices to disclose key records proactively and give written reasons for refusals. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index disclosure of public records ranks 4th of 54 at 55.5, down 6.6 points over 2014–2023. Documentation of the procurement, debt, tax and data protection systems exists internally but is not published (2025).

Geospatial data

In 2021 the US development finance agency gave Apollo Agriculture a ten-year loan to underwrite smallholder credit from satellite data and machine learning. The land record remains only partly mapped and adjudicated. In 2021 the regional climate centre ICPAC in Ngong received a satellite-data station, one of four under a €20 million African Development Bank programme. It feeds high-resolution data into severe-weather forecasts. In August 2024 the Kenya Meteorological Department received the first PUMA-2025 station for Meteosat Third Generation data, with ClimSA systems, under an AU Commission programme supported by the EU.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

On 4 December 2025 Kenya became the first African signatory of the America First Global Health Strategy, with a five-year, US$2.5bn health framework. Its data-sharing agreement gives the US government access to Kenyan health, genomic and pathogen data and to the health-information systems. The money is earmarked for TaifaCare, the national logistics system, KEMSA's iLMIS, laboratory and surveillance systems and health data banks. It follows USAID's 2017–2022 Afya Halisi programme and the Kenya Digital Health Ecosystem Activity, backing DHIS2 to 2026.

On 18 August 2026 AWS signed an agreement with the Konza authority for an Outpost and a startup centre on the technopolis. It carries no capacity, investment figure or delivery date. AWS runs a Nairobi Local Zone parented to Cape Town, and no US provider runs a cloud region in Kenya. Oracle's Nairobi region was still in preparation in September 2026. In June 2025 Safaricom took AWS for generative-AI fraud detection and customer-service automation. Meta has Safaricom as landing partner for its Daraja cable. Meta's exit from content moderation forced mass redundancies at Sama in April 2026. In 2025 the US trade representative listed Kenya's digital presence tax among foreign trade barriers.

In July 2026 the two governments held talks on Kenya's draft Data Governance Policy and cross-border data flows. DFC has lent to poa! Internet for towers and customer equipment and to M-KOPA for smartphone and solar receivables. USTDA funded feasibility studies for a Mombasa–Malaba fibre and tower corridor.

China activities

Kenya began repaying the 2019 China Eximbank concessional loan for the Konza data centre on 31 March 2026, and repayment runs to 2038. The loan, at two per cent, financed a contract with Huawei for a national cloud data centre, safe city and smart traffic systems and a government cloud. China Eximbank loans of 2012 and 2016 paid Huawei to lay the second phase of the national fibre backbone. Kenya has repaid that in semi-annual instalments since March 2019. On 18 August 2026 the Konza authority signed a memorandum with Guodong Network Communications Group on cloud, AI, computing and smart-city infrastructure. It names no project, value, financing instrument or date. In July 2026 the ICT Cabinet Secretary signed the agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization in Shanghai. In November 2026 he and the Chinese ambassador discussed a proposed US$180 million grant for an AI cloud data project at Eldoret, with nothing committed.

Absa Bank Kenya rebuilt its branch network on Huawei SD-WAN in 2025. In May 2026 Safaricom launched Kenya's first fibre-to-the-room service with Huawei.

EU activities

The EU funds Human-Centred Digitalisation in Kenya with €28 million over 2022–2030, under a Team Europe initiative. It funds a 2023–2029 security and cybersecurity programme and a Kenya Cyber Resilience project run by Expertise France with EstDev from January 2026. It also funds school connectivity, vocational training and civil-society grants such as Sheria ya Vijana.

USAID is the most frequent financier of Kenya's digital estate by number of commitments, 52 worth about US$136 million, ahead of the Gates Foundation with 35 and the Korea International Cooperation Agency with 29 (November 2026). The European Union has eight, and the largest individual sums come from the World Bank, China Eximbank and private data-centre developers.

Cybernetica piloted an X-Road interoperability test environment between April 2024 and June 2025. The European Investment Bank's 2018 loan to Telkom Kenya for mobile broadband has closed. Kenya's draft AI Bill 2026 calls itself a transplant of the EU AI Act, with fines capped at KES 5 million. EU and German technical assistance supports Kenya's draft data-governance policy. European support extends to digitising land registration through an ID-linked system. Italy is one leg of the trilateral sovereign AI partnership signed with Kenya and India in February 2026.

Gulf/UAE activities

The Microsoft–G42 data centre at Olkaria missed its May 2026 launch. President Ruto said its planned 1 GW draw would take a third of national supply. In March 2024 G42 signed a memorandum with Kenya's EcoCloud for a geothermal-powered data centre planned at 100 MW.

In January 2026 M-Pesa Africa agreed to work with Abu Dhabi's ADI Foundation on a layer-2 network across its eight markets. The first stablecoin is to be backed by the dirham and regulated by the UAE central bank.

India activities

In February 2026 Kenya, India and Italy signed a letter of intent for a trilateral sovereign AI partnership at the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi. It targets 100 AI deployment programmes by 2030, built on multilingual low-bandwidth voice AI. Airtel Africa's data-centre arm Nxtra broke ground in 2025 on a facility at Tatu City. In August 2026 its opening was put at July 2027, moved from the first quarter of 2027. Tata Consultancy Services' sovereign cloud went live in Nairobi in September 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In August 2026 implementation guidelines for the 2025 Cloud Policy barred state bodies from hosting systems in their own server rooms without the Cloud Adoption Committee's authorisation. They give entities 24 months to migrate to a single accredited provider. Top-secret data must be held in a data centre in Kenya, and restricted data with a provider present locally.

The eCitizen agreement with a consortium led by Webmasters Kenya runs from 2023 to 2026. On termination it lets the suppliers uninstall all their proprietary infrastructure and software. In March 2026 the Auditor-General's special audit found the government may not have full control of the platform. Draft regulations of May 2026 would have the National Treasury host and maintain it. On 27 October 2026 the Treasury told MPs the regulations were before Parliament with the Treasury in charge of the platform.

On 15 September 2026 the High Court declared the sale of 15 per cent of Safaricom to Vodacom void. In September 2026 a court ordered the electoral commission to give parties and observers access to voter-registration source code and audit logs. The commission's contractor had refused on intellectual-property grounds. On 27 October 2026 the National Assembly deleted clauses vesting election technology's intellectual property in the commission and requiring its servers to be in Kenya.

Kenya signed a joint declaration with Anthropic on 22 September 2026. It places any programme involving data access under Kenyan data protection and cybersecurity law. In September 2026 UNDP and the Konza authority agreed to put sovereign AI infrastructure into the Konza data centre. The data terms of the AWS agreement at Konza and the Guodong memorandum had not been published in September 2026.
