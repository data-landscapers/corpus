Summary of position

Kenya has a data-protection statute in force since 2019, a mobile-money rail that moved KES 41.68tn in the year to March 2026, an identity register the Interior Ministry reports as holding more than 42 million digitised records, and a government services platform carrying over 22,000 services. It has no adopted artificial-intelligence instrument, no national count of registered data controllers, and no cloud region in commercial service. The state's own digital spending fell for a second consecutive year, to KSh 8.6bn for 2026/27 from KSh 16.3bn in 2024/25. The National AI Strategy 2025-2030 is costed at KSh 152bn over five years and carries no dedicated allocation in that budget. Service migration onto the eCitizen platform ran at 1,695 of a target 11,000 in the year to 31 March 2026, against 3,458 the year before.

Satellite broadband reached 24,999 subscribers and 0.9% of the fixed market by March 2026, and the operator then suspended new sign-ups across seven counties, diverting orders to a deposit-backed waitlist, with median download speeds down 26% year on year to 34.55 Mbps. Traffic across the London Internet Exchange's Nairobi and Mombasa points rose twentyfold in a year to 330 Gbps, with 55 or more networks connected. Against the 100,000km national fibre target, the only delivered figures published are the regulator's for the universal-service-funded slice: 2,193km under phase one and 1,173 last-mile sites under phase two at 30 June 2025.

The US$1bn Microsoft and G42 facility at Olkaria missed a May 2026 launch and the project's full 1GW draw is about a third of Kenya's roughly 3GW supply. No data-centre tariff, gazetted large-load connection rule or grid-priority instrument is held, so every power arrangement on record is bilateral to one project. The National Treasury told transaction advisers in April 2026 that the Konza National Data Centre runs 706 kW of certified capacity at an actual load of about 300 kW, with 100 of its 144 racks in use and hosts 145 of 451 government institutions; its expansion to 288 racks drew no appropriation at the 2026/27 budget and is being structured as a public-private partnership.

The National Cyber Security Agency Order was gazetted as Legal Notice 89 on 2026-05-15 and announced as approved on 2026-06-22, at a tabled establishment cost of about KSh 4bn. The presidency's own website was defaced on 2026-07-18 with a 5 BTC ransom demand and restored on 2026-07-20, the second compromise inside twelve months; cause, access vector and attribution are undisclosed.

The Interior Ministry reports more than 42 million identity records digitised in 2025/26, with biometric health enrolment extended to dependants aged 7 to 17. Registered mobile-money accounts reached 94.09 million with 564,330 active agents in May 2026, from 87.46 million and 451,157 in August 2025. A High Court ruling of March 2026 recognised a registered phone number as a personal digital identifier, barred recycling dormant numbers without consent, and gave the Attorney-General six months to legislate; that window closes about September 2026 and nothing on file shows a framework tabled.

eCitizen carries more than 22,000 services from over 100 agencies and collects KSh 600m a day, while the Auditor-General alleges KSh 9.4bn was irregularly routed to private entities. A teachers' union reported members turned away and billed in cash when health-claims authorisation failed, with the Authority answering that the Digital Health Agency runs the system on its behalf. Kenya's clearest written interoperability requirement is a procurement document: the health ministry's enterprise-system tender makes financial, tax, insurance, pensions, payroll, laboratory and identity interfaces day-one mandatory and requires primary hosting inside an approved Kenyan government or health-sector environment.

The Vision 2030 Delivery Secretariat scored all 47 counties on service automation, revenue systems and data use in February 2026, from 98.3 for Murang'a to 28.8 for Wajir, with six counties graded as reliant on manual and cash-based systems — a desk review, not a survey of users. The national DPI roadmap is still unpublished; GIZ tendered technical assistance on 2026-07-31 to develop a bankable Kenyan digital public infrastructure project, with offers closing 2026-09-01.

Virtual assets acquired a statute commenced on 2025-11-04 and regulations gazetted as Legal Notice 134 on 2026-07-22, setting paid-up capital by licence class from KES 300m for stablecoin issuance to nil for investment advisers; every existing operator must license afresh by 2026-11-04. The state's proposed data marketplace would license anonymised platform and statistics-office datasets, targeting more than 1,000 datasets over five years at up to KSh 396m, with the pricing method deferred.

Data Protection Services received KSh 917.3m in the 2026/27 original estimates, its first separately visible appropriation. The regulator's own bulletin puts the caseload at 7,128 complaints received and 215 determinations issued to March 2025, with penalties imposed on 10 entities and no later total published. Of 224 betting and gaming firms it identified, 15 were fully registered and 185 were given 40 days to register or lose their licences in May 2026; the gambling regulator confirmed in August 2026 that it is investigating three licensed operators on a complaint dated 2026-05-19. The data-protection regulator is meanwhile reaching artificial intelligence through transfer rules rather than an AI instrument: draft guidance reported on 2026-08-04 would require a lawful transfer basis, a documented adequacy assessment and a written processing agreement before personal data leaves Kenya for AI processing. The note itself is not held.

Kenya's artificial-intelligence position rests on a draft, a bill and a strategy without money. The draft AI and Other Emerging Technologies Policy runs to 226 pages, was open for comment from 2026-07-21 to 2026-08-04, and asserts effects-based jurisdiction over any entity outside Kenya whose AI outputs have direct and foreseeable effects in the country while leaving "high risk" undefined and stating no penalties. Its labour chapter sets minimum standards for content moderators, data annotators and evaluators — pay transparency, psychosocial support, grievance procedures — binding on international firms operating through outsourcing partners, against a reported wage range of US$1.46 to US$3.74 an hour for Kenyan moderators. A separate Artificial Intelligence Bill sits before the Senate on a different legislative track, and the National AI Strategy is costed at KSh 152bn over five years with no dedicated budget allocation.

Deployment is running ahead of the rules that would govern it. A constitutional petition filed on 2026-04-23 alleges that automated M-PESA decisioning and the operator's chatbot offer no guaranteed route to a human agent, no disclosure of decision logic and algorithmic discrimination in credit scoring; respondents had not filed at publication. Venture funding fell sharply: Kenyan startups raised US$126m in the first half of 2026, third in Africa and the weakest half since early 2021, against US$1.04bn across 2025. No held source states Kenya's installed AI compute capacity at any date.

The digital thematic allocation fell to KSh 8.6bn for 2026/27 from KSh 12.7bn and KSh 16.3bn in the two preceding years, and KSh 4.3bn of it — half the thematic — is the World Bank's Digital Economy Acceleration Project, which had disbursed US$33.93m of a US$390m commitment at a portal reading of 2026-06-24.

The Jitume digital hub programme commissioned its 117th hub on 2026-07-20 on the ceremony series while its own officials give an installed base of about 350, and no hub register has been published. Six donor programmes carry commitment values and no disbursement or outturn at any date. The universal service fund connected 20 sub-locations in 2024/25 and 155 cumulatively, reaching about 508,012 people; the Digital Literacy Programme installed 1,170,846 devices in 22,891 public primary schools but had connected 216 schools to the internet when a government-steered review last reported in 2022.

Smartphone connections passed 50 million in March 2026, with feature phones down to 28.5 million. The Hustler Fund has disbursed KSh 83bn against KSh 71bn recovered on a 15% default rate, while state funding fell from KSh 20bn at launch to KSh 300m in the year to June 2026, and repayment is now accepted in privately issued loyalty points. The KenSafeSpace digital-rights coalition closed on 2026-07-28 after 30 months on a EUR 1.3m grant, its 49 members continuing unfunded. The Mastercard Foundation's EdTech Fellowship moved from eight-month growth-stage cohorts to an 18-month early-stage programme in July 2026, selecting 12 Kenyan startups at up to US$100,000 each.
