This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In 2025 the World Bank's GovTech index recorded Ethiopia's digital government strategy as current, and the programme to deliver it as planned or in draft. The national data strategy had not been adopted (2025).

Digital Ethiopia 2030, issued from the Prime Minister's Office, designates data a national asset. It names a national data exchange as an enabler of digital public infrastructure, and sequences its initiatives over horizons of up to five years. It targets a sevenfold rise in digital payments, through Telebirr and a national payment gateway. The Ministry of Innovation and Technology's library holds a Digital Ethiopia Strategy covering AI, the internet, cybersecurity, data centres and cloud. It also covers data protection, digital identification, payments, digital signatures and e-commerce.

The Digital Government Strategy and Enterprise Architecture 2024–2029 went to final review in July 2024, ahead of submission for government approval. The Ministry of Innovation and Technology prepared it with European Union funding. It builds on the strategy before it. An established data governance function takes a whole-of-government view of data (2025). It sits inside another institution, with no autonomous body of its own. An established institution coordinates digital government.

The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores Ethiopia 48.4 out of 100 for overall governance, 29th of 54 African states. The score rose 2.5 points over 2014–2023 but fell 1.5 points over 2019–2023. Ethiopia scores above the African average on 45 of the index's 96 indicators. Over the decade it improved on 55 of them and declined on 37.

The European Union committed EUR 1.2 million over 2020–2025 to the Ministry of Innovation and Technology for its role guiding digital reforms.

Legislation and regulation

On 23 July 2026 the National Bank defined a virtual asset as any digital representation of value that can be traded, transferred or used for payment or investment. It confirmed that use, trading, custody and every other dealing in virtual assets remain prohibited without its express authorisation. The prohibition came by public notice, with no enforcement mechanism announced.

MESOB, the one-stop service platform, ran for nearly a year without a dedicated regulatory framework (July 2026). Its Addis Ababa deputy director has called for a law compelling institutions to digitise before joining it. Its executives have asked which institution regulates the platform and answers for it.

Directive No. 792/2021 has governed telecommunications licensing since 9 July 2021. The Ethiopian Communications Authority made it under the Communications Service Proclamation No. 1148/2019. It creates individual, class and spectrum licences, with only two individual licences: unified telecommunications service and an internet exchange point. Individual licences run fifteen years, with a further fifteen presumed for a holder that has met every obligation. Under the 2021 Directive, a change of ownership taking a shareholder to 25 per cent or more needs the Authority's prior approval. The Directive issues the state incumbent its new licence without competitive selection.

Copyright still rests on a 2004 proclamation, with sentences of one to ten years and fines of up to 25,000 Birr. Amendments were being drafted with Addis Ababa University's law school in August 2026. A comprehensive personal data protection law has been in force since July 2024. On 28 September 2026 the Council of Ministers approved a draft national data management proclamation and referred it to parliament. The draft is framed around data security, sovereignty and data sharing across government systems.

Data protection

Proclamation No. 1321/2024 was approved on 4 April 2024 and gazetted on 24 July 2024. It replaced the country's piecemeal sectoral regulation of personal data. As of November 2025 the secondary directives needed to operate it had not been issued. No enforcement action by the Ethiopian Communications Authority had been made public as of March 2025.

The 2024 law defines personal data broadly and claims reach over foreign entities handling Ethiopians' data. It requires controllers to notify the Authority and affected people within 72 hours of learning of a breach. It obliges personal data to be stored locally. This matches Digital Ethiopia 2030's principle of national anchoring for data, infrastructure and systems.

The 2024 proclamation makes the Ethiopian Communications Authority the supervisor of personal data protection, with no standalone authority. The Authority is to register controllers and processors and impose fines and compliance orders. A year after the law, enforcement was slow, with implementing directives awaiting approval (2025). Neither the regime nor the Authority publishes reporting on how it operates (2025).

The National Bank's Financial Consumer Protection Directive No. FCP/01/2020 has been in force since 25 February 2021. It covers digital financial services, including mobile money. It mandates key facts statements, data confidentiality, complaint handling and refunds for unauthorised transactions. A revised Payment Instrument Issuer Directive of 2023 strengthened governance, transaction limits and consumer safeguards for mobile money.

In August 2026 the Development Bank of Ethiopia's disclosure policy made client credit information permanently exempt from declassification. Ethiopia scores 16.0 out of 100 for digital freedom in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states. The score improved 5.2 points over the decade.

Regional collaboration

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, regional integration is Ethiopia's second most improved indicator of the decade. It rose 33.8 points over 2014–2023 to 49.1 out of 100, 23rd of 54 African states.

The Horizon Fiber agreement between Ethio Telecom, Djibouti Telecom and Sudatel became binding in February 2026. It commits the three to a terrestrial fibre corridor from Djibouti's cable landings through Ethiopia to Sudan's. The corridor entered active deployment in August 2026, its route extended to Jeddah. On 7 September 2026 the three operators presented it in Addis Ababa to Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, carriers and vendors.

Ethiopia joined the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System as its 31st member, reported on 30 September 2026. Membership had been delayed by the government's concern that financial data would be exposed to other members. Four Ethiopian banks were awaiting central bank no-objection letters before they could transact. The central bank governor and the system's chief executive announced the membership jointly on 15 October 2026. In November 2025 the national switch was integrating with the UAE's Aani system. Vodacom's move to take control of Safaricom, which leads the consortium behind Ethiopia's second operator, needed Ethiopian approval.

The FaydaPass identity wallet was launched in 2025, as the programme adopted international wallet specifications. The programme argued for cross-border recognition of national IDs.

Standards

On 24 April 2026 the national standards board approved five mandatory cybersecurity standards and 31 guidelines and frameworks. The security agency prepared them with the standards institute, as the technical framework for the critical-infrastructure cybersecurity proclamation. The texts were still to be published. The standards institute is an ISO member body and an IEC associate member. Its 2026 catalogue lists ISO/IEC 27000 on information security, ISO/IEC 20546 on big data and ISO/IEC 2382 on IT vocabulary as Ethiopian Standards.

The Ethiopian National Enterprise Architecture Framework and the eGovernment Interoperability Framework, both of 2019, are the standing interoperability instruments.

The 2025 GovTech index records most supporting measures tested alongside the interoperability framework as not in place. By March 2025 no government agency had been integrated with the planned national data exchange platform. Data remains fragmented across agencies for want of clear data-sharing rules and standards. In July 2026 MESOB's founding director general said the platform remains intertwined with institutions' systems, some decades old and built to undefined standards. Government has no open source policy (2025).

In 2025 the e-procurement system interfaced with other government systems and enforced technical standards on government purchases. The Auditor General credits it with a decline in cash shortages across federal institutions (July 2026). National livestock data standards were developed with the Ministry of Agriculture and formally endorsed (2026).

On 6 July 2026 the Federal Auditor General presented its EFY 2017 report to parliament, covering 163 federal institutions and 39 performance audits. It recorded repeated breaches of financial rules, including duplicate payments, spending above approved limits and undocumented disbursements. It warned that authorising officials would be held to account and irregular payments recovered.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The media authority revoked Addis Standard's online media registration on 24 February 2026, a decision under court challenge. In August 2026 security forces detained the outlet's editor-in-chief and raided its newsroom. Wazema Radio's licence was also revoked, and Addis Standard's managing editor was held incommunicado for two weeks in April 2026. In December 2025 the media authority permanently suspended two Deutsche Welle correspondents covering Amhara and Tigray.

Internet and telephone services were severely disrupted across Tigray as fighting escalated in September 2026. The state operator's services were completely down and the second operator's partly running. Responsibility for the shutdown was contested between the regional and federal authorities.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, participation is Ethiopia's fastest-improving sub-category, up 12.4 points over 2014–2023 to 41.9 out of 100. Civil society space rose 13.8 points to 39.8. Media freedom fell 7.0 points to 34.1, 46th of 54 African states. Rights is the worst-performing sub-category, at 28.5 and 44th of 54, down 4.7 points. Ethiopians' own assessment of freedom of speech is among the country's five most deteriorated indicators.

The 2025 GovTech index records government participation platforms in operation, though several tested capabilities are not in place. Citizen feedback channels carry complaints to government. A right-to-information regime lets the public obtain public records, with nothing published on how it performs. In July 2024 the justice ministry launched an online consultation platform and a manual for public participation in lawmaking.

By August 2026 draft copyright amendments had been through several rounds of consultation with rights holders and justice institutions. The draft would add procedures for blocking websites and a take-down notice obliging platforms to remove content. Proclamation No. 1185/2020, in force since March 2020, bans hate speech and disinformation and obliges platforms to act within 24 hours of notice.

Documentation and assessments for the service portal, e-procurement, participation platforms and feedback channels are kept internal (2025). An Addis Ababa outlet has argued that the mandatory design of the national digital ID risks surveillance and exclusion. The European Union committed just under EUR 1 million over 2023–2026 against online hate speech and incitement to violence.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Ethiopia's own money, the treasury's and its agencies' retained revenue, carries 79.8 per cent of the digital lines in the 2026/27 federal budget as appropriated, ETB 35.2 billion of ETB 44.1 billion, against 78.4 per cent of ETB 25.0 billion in 2025/26.

In 2026/27 the Immigration and Citizenship Service is to spend ETB 6.26 billion of its own retained revenue on a modern digital system, beside ETB 1.08 billion from the treasury. In 2025/26 it had ETB 698 million of retained revenue for the system. The 2026/27 budget puts ETB 5 billion of treasury capital into an Artificial Intelligence University and ETB 3.05 billion into a national fibre project. In 2026/27 the Communications Authority's ETB 30.1 million data-protection line is paid from its retained revenue.

The 2026/27 budget carries ETB 5.41 billion of assistance for the national identity project, against ETB 300 million of treasury counterpart. The digital health system has ETB 3.10 billion of assistance against ETB 5.7 million in 2026/27. The identity credit closes in January 2029. In March 2026 a World Bank mission recorded the programme being made a state-owned enterprise, FaydaVerse, for long-term sustainability. The enterprise has ETB 10 billion of authorised and ETB 2.5 billion of paid-up capital inside Ethiopian Investment Holdings (March 2026). United States health-data funding tapers to US$1.26 million in 2030, with Ethiopia undertaking to meet licensing, cloud and hardware costs.

In 2024/25 the Ministry of Innovation and Technology spent ETB 990 million of its ETB 2.75 billion vote, 35.9 per cent. In 2024/25 the Digital ID for Inclusion programme, voted nothing, spent ETB 3.06 billion added in-year.

MoUs and other agreements

The Horizon Fiber memorandum of December 2024 between Ethio Telecom, Djibouti Telecom and Sudatel became binding in February 2026. It commits the three to a terrestrial fibre corridor from Djibouti's cable landings through Ethiopia to Sudan's. The corridor entered active deployment in August 2026.

A December 2025 memorandum under the America First Global Health Strategy sets out United States funding for Ethiopian health data systems over 2026–2030. The funding declines each year, totals under US$20 million over 2026–2030 and is subject to appropriations. Ethiopia undertakes to meet the software licensing, cloud, hardware maintenance and replacement costs itself. The memorandum records that it is not an international agreement. In July 2026 Safaricom Ethiopia partnered with the National ID Program to run Fayda enrolment under Lots 3 and 4.

New investments

MIGA's ten-year, US$1 billion political risk guarantee runs from 2023 to 2033. It covers the equity of Safaricom Telecommunications Ethiopia's shareholders against expropriation, war and civil disturbance. The shareholders are Vodafone, Vodacom, Safaricom Kenya and British International Investment. Since 2023 a US$76 million first-loss layer has come from the IDA20 Private Sector Window.

Thirty-nine distinct external commitments to Ethiopia's digital estate are on record, announced since July 2015 on windows closing as late as 2034, and worth around US$2.6 billion — of which the guarantee is nearly two-fifths; three carry no US dollar amount. Thirty-one sit in windows that have not closed; five are approved and not yet operating and one is still at pipeline stage. Ten were announced in 2025 or later, against fifteen in 2023 and 2024.

The European Union is the most frequent financier by count, with seven commitments, ahead of the World Bank on five, the Gates Foundation on four and IFC on three; twenty-one distinct financiers appear in all. By value the order inverts: the World Bank Group's three arms hold four-fifths of everything recorded. Grants are much the commonest instrument, eighteen of the thirty-nine, but together carry less than the single guarantee. After connectivity, much the largest share, the money goes to digital identity, government systems and sector information systems; payments and fintech draw many commitments but little money.

The Safaricom-led consortium won a fifteen-year telecoms operating licence in 2021. It has since taken equity from British International Investment. In 2023 IFC also took equity and lent the operating company money for 4G rollout and M-PESA. IFC later added a second debt facility. The identity programme rests on one World Bank package. Its long-term financing plan is not fully documented beyond card fees and authentication charges. Korea's development fund is rebuilding tax and customs IT to 2027. In June 2025 Better Auth, an Addis Ababa authentication start-up, closed a US$5 million seed round.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Data and internet passed mobile voice as Ethio Telecom's largest revenue line in 2025/26, at 31.1 per cent against 23.5 per cent. The operator closed 2025/26 with 52.5 million data and internet users, 1 million of them on fixed broadband. Group revenue rose 33.2 per cent to 215.8 billion Birr in 2025/26, on 90.1 million subscriptions.

Unique mobile subscriber penetration rose from 26 per cent in 2020 to 33 per cent in 2024. Mobile connections, which count SIMs, reached 68.4 per cent of the population in late 2025. Internet penetration stood at 19 per cent in 2024. In 2022 international bandwidth was 22 kbit/s per internet user. A mobile broadband basket cost 3.4 per cent of gross national income per head in 2022.

Ethio Telecom signed network expansion agreements with Ericsson, ZTE, Huawei and Nokia at MWC 2026. By August 2026, 4G reached 92 per cent of the population across ZTE's footprint. There 124 new sites, 272 refarming sites and 115 rural sites were still outstanding. In October 2026 the operator extended 4G LTE to 80 more towns. Its 2026/27 plan raises 4G population coverage from 82.23 to 95 per cent, across 1,500 towns. Safaricom Ethiopia began laying its own regional fibre in August 2025, on an Afdera–Mekelle route. Domestic traffic crosses one internet exchange point, Addix, with three members (2026).

A 2025 national assessment rated the community health information system's ICT infrastructure at 1.67 out of 5. It found 32 per cent of required tablets supplied, and LAN internet in 28 per cent of health facilities, in 2025. ICT infrastructure is the weakest domain of the national DHIS2 deployment (2024). In 2021 the Safaricom-led consortium paid US$850 million for a fifteen-year operating licence. Its network passed 15 million 90-day active subscribers in September 2026. The incumbent had 72.09 million active mobile subscribers at June 2026.

Data Storage

In 2025 no hyperscale cloud provider had a region or edge node in Ethiopia, the nearest regions being in South Africa. Under the 2024 proclamation, personal data must be stored locally.

Raxio opened a Tier III-certified facility at the Addis Ababa ICT Park in November 2023, with room for 800 racks and 3MW of IT power. RedFox's first modular data centre went live at the same park in September 2022. By May 2022 the state operator's Huawei-built data centre at Gola Sefer had close to 90 colocation customers. Wingu Africa's WCX platform provides local cloud that interoperates with Azure and AWS (2025). Wingu raised commercial finance in 2025 for its rollout across Ethiopia, Djibouti and Tanzania. Data centre and hosting services sit among the class licences of Directive 792/2021, with ISP, VSAT and satellite categories.

In December 2025 government security and AI bodies ran on cloud infrastructure the state built itself. The national identity system runs across data-centre sites of its own (July 2026). The hosting arrangements behind the government cloud are not published (2025).

Energy

The grid delivers an average of 39 interruptions a month, with about 21 hours of outage a month, to smart-metered users, and households on the low-voltage network face more (2025). Access to electricity reached about 55 per cent of the population in 2023. It was 94.7 per cent urban against 43.6 per cent rural in 2023. Ethiopia's Access to Energy score of 30.5 out of 100 in 2023 ranks 32nd of 54 African states, despite a 16.6-point gain over the decade.

The National Electrification Programme 2.0 set a 65 per cent grid and 35 per cent off-grid split for 2025, and progress has been slower than planned. An EU co-funded programme installed five solar mini-grids between 2017 and 2024. The World Bank's US$500 million ADELE electrification project lost US$70 million in a July 2024 restructuring. Cost overruns mean its planned 201 mini-grids will not all be built. The Ministry of Health issued roughly 29,950 power banks alongside tablets to Health Extension Workers (2023). They let the community health information system run where mains power is not available.

After the October 2025 revision the Ethiopian Electric Utility charges 0.76 birr per kWh for the first 50 kWh block, rising to 5.10 birr per kWh above 500 kWh. Almost all output is renewable, at 99.96 per cent in 2015, which ties supply to rainfall. In September 2026 Ethiopian Electric Power cut the electricity it supplies to bitcoin and other data-mining customers by about 75 per cent. That left them around 23 per cent of contracted volumes in September 2026, as reservoir levels fell. Ten months earlier a second operator had brought a 30 MW mining site at the Bole Lemi Industrial Park into service with the utility. In 2024 Agence Française de Développement committed a EUR 80 million concessional loan to modernise Ethiopian Electric Power's networks for a more digitalised economy. It sits inside a co-financed package with the EIB, the French Treasury and EU grants. The record covers electricity and telecommunications networks together and does not split out the digital share.

Technical Capacity

In July 2026 MESOB was losing staff to better-paying institutions, with the attrition unresolved. Its three-year plan, launched on 24 July 2026, makes strengthening institutional capacity a central goal. GitHub counted more than 94,000 developers in Ethiopia in 2023, up 32 per cent in a year.

The National ID Program runs on the open-source MOSIP platform and staffs the system's engineering itself (July 2026). Its executive director argues that governments must understand what is inside identity systems, and not buy black-box turnkey projects. In July 2025 the security agency had a government email client deployed across federal institutions. Its own messaging and meeting platforms were in rollout and pilot, to replace foreign applications. After the 2025 United States aid cuts, Ethiopia's national DHIS2 health systems were reported still online and supported by local staff.

The aLIVE programme worked inside the Ministry of Agriculture's ICT unit for four years (July 2026). It placed three interns there when routine ICT tasks were taking the core staff time needed for system design. A dedicated data steward now coordinates the Livestock Information System across directorates. Ethiopia scores 59.6 out of 100 for effective administration in 2023, 20th of 54 African states.

Cybersecurity

The Critical Infrastructure Cyber Security Protection Proclamation No. 1426/2026 was signed on 21 July 2026. It enters into force one year after its publication in the Federal Negarit Gazette. It designates twelve critical infrastructure sectors, places 18 core cybersecurity obligations on their owners and operators and establishes a Critical Infrastructure Cybersecurity Fund. The Council of Ministers adopted a revised national cybersecurity policy, effective 27 June 2024. It sets out the areas for public-private partnership and guidelines for implementing, monitoring and following it up. The earlier framework on record is the National Cyber Security Framework of 2014.

Offences have been defined since the Computer Crime Proclamation No. 958/2016. The security agency recorded 27,773 attempted attacks on national digital infrastructure in July–December 2025, saying it neutralised 99.03 per cent.

On declared arrangements the country scores well, in Tier 2, Advancing, of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, a measure of commitments and institutions, not of tested defences. The 2024 data protection proclamation requires controllers to notify the regulator and the people affected within 72 hours of learning of a breach, and to document every breach. Cybersecurity frameworks are one of the four workstreams of the World Bank's US$200 million Digital Foundations project, running 2021 to 2026. A financial sector cybersecurity forum, jointly chaired by the information security agency and the central bank, was established on 29 September 2026. It serves threat-information sharing and coordinated incident response. Five mandatory cybersecurity standards and 31 guidelines were approved in April 2026 to give the critical-infrastructure proclamation technical effect. The security agency has run a public key infrastructure since 31 August 2024. It acts as root certificate authority for certificates issued to national institutions and private companies.

The security agency audited 410 institutions in 2024/25 and opened a digital forensic and cybercrime investigation laboratory, on its own account. Independent third-party security and software code audits of the national digital identity system are funded work under the World Bank project, not work already completed (2025).

DPI

Data Exchange

In 2024 the Ethiopia Digital Stack exchange platform funded under the World Bank digital ID project had yet to be delivered. Data remains fragmented across agencies for want of clear data-sharing rules and standards. The one-stop government platform began as a federal pilot integrating twelve institutions (2025).

The SIGTAS tax system is linked to the trade ministry's registration service and the national ID office to verify taxpayers (2025). Five core systems exchange data through point-to-point interfaces and a Government Service Bus (2025). They are financial management, customs, tax administration, public-service HR and e-procurement.

The Ethiopian eGovernment Interoperability Framework of 2019 sets cross-agency data-sharing rules and creates Regional Cells acting as chief interoperability officers. The 2025 GovTech index records most supporting measures tested alongside it as absent. In health, standards and interoperability sit at the middle, defined stage of the national DHIS2 deployment (2024). Automated patient data exchange between DHIS2 and other systems had not been implemented.

In July 2026 two competing integration models were reported under the one-stop platform. In Addis Ababa it has absorbed institutions' systems. In the regions it stays tethered to their separate systems, and users are bounced between platform and institutions. At the same meeting a prime-ministerial board's approval of a national data exchange platform was disclosed. In 2024 five livestock databases were being aligned to an international data standard under a four-year programme.

Digital Identity and CRVS

A public developer sandbox opened for Fayda in October 2026, when it counted more than 52 million registrations and 208 million electronic verifications. By mid-2026, 13 million cards had been printed. In October 2025, 23 million IDs had been issued against a target of 63 million by the year's end.

Proclamation No. 1284/2023 established the digital identification system, with enrolment rights, data protection principles and penalties for fraud. Enrolment is formally voluntary, though relying parties may make it mandatory with their regulator's approval. By October 2025 Fayda was mandatory for new bank accounts, tax numbers, property and notary transactions, SIM registration and national examinations. From January 2026 the National Bank required it for all new bank accounts. In September 2026 the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia restricted unlinked accounts in Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa, Harari and 24 other cities. Customers elsewhere have until 9 November 2026 to link theirs.

More than 150 agencies ran KYC checks against the register in July 2026. Over five million tax identification numbers had been harmonised with Fayda by October 2025.

Since April 2024 Ethio Telecom has made about 32 million registrations, 69 per cent of the total. Safaricom Ethiopia was added as a second telecoms channel in July 2026. Refugees have been enrolled since a pilot launched in Addis Ababa in March 2024. A refugee with the credential can open a bank account, register a SIM and get a tax number without endorsement letters. Children under five remain outside the system.

In August 2026 the programme was inaugurated as Faydaverse, a state-owned enterprise under Ethiopian Investment Holdings. It runs three data centres with more than 50 in-house engineers. It began charging banks 10 to 20 birr a record retrieval and 20 to 40 birr a biometric authentication. The bankers' association asked for a consultation with the central bank, reported on 3 October 2026.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Telebirr carried 4.19 trillion Birr across 2.61 billion transactions for 60.6 million customers in 2025/26. On the 2025 Global Findex, only about a third of adults used digital payments in 2024. Digital accounts multiplied 2.3-fold to 222.1 million by March 2025, while formal account ownership barely moved. At June 2026 there were 157.6 million mobile money accounts, 29.56 per cent of them active. The central bank put digital financial transactions at 33 trillion birr in 2025/26.

EthSwitch launched EthioPay-IPS, the national instant payment system, in December 2025. It connects 32 banks, 12 microfinance institutions and six payment firms for real-time account, wallet, QR and bulk payments. In May 2026 it passed 1 million peer-to-peer transactions in a day. The National Bank co-owns EthSwitch with 33 payment providers and oversees it (2025). On 1 October 2026 the switch presented a unified platform for government bill payments.

M-PESA Ethiopia reported 2.58 million 30-day active customers in the quarter to 30 June 2026. In June 2026 it earned about 2 per cent of Safaricom Ethiopia's service revenue, against 45.6 per cent in Kenya. Telebirr generated 3.6 per cent of Ethio Telecom's revenue in 2025/26.

On 23 July 2026 the National Bank confirmed that its prohibition covers all virtual assets, not only cryptocurrencies. In May 2026 revenue authorities froze and swept the accounts of the payment gateways Arifpay, Chapa and Santimpay over disputed tax. Online sports betting accounts for almost all gateway volume (2025). Safety-net payments are moving to electronic transfer, for programmes reaching more than 9 million people (March 2024).

In October 2026 a licensed payment operator and an international card scheme agreed to roll out 148,000 contactless devices to merchants. On 15 October 2026 the central bank and the system's operator announced Ethiopia's membership of the pan-African payment and settlement system. Non-bank wallets are preparing to connect, each needing a central bank no-objection letter (October 2026). On 8 October 2026 Awash Bank and Oromia Bank launched international payment cards. An amendment of 11 February 2026 had let authorised banks issue such cards to foreign-currency account holders.

Registries

More than 50.5 million voters registered for the 7th General Election by 22 April 2026. Just over 5.5 million of them used the Fayda-linked digital channel in April 2026. That channel is Mirchaye, the electoral board's self-registration system launched in January 2026.

Fayda, the legal ID established under Proclamation No. 1284/2023, holds demographic data and fingerprint, iris and face biometrics (2026). Proclamation No. 760/2012 covers births, deaths, marriages, divorce and adoption nationwide, on a paper process (2025). Digital capture has been piloted in Addis Ababa, Oromia and Amhara. Registrations of children under one reached about 40 per cent of estimated births in 2022/23, excluding Tigray. In July 2025 there was no data link between civil registration and the identity system.

The trade ministry delivered more than 2.9 million business registration and licensing services online in the first nine months of 2025/26. Its integrated company creation service, launched in September 2025, cut registration from 11 steps to two, on its own account. The taxpayer register is computerised and centralised, with a full countrywide view (2025). Since 10 July 2026 legal persons must file their beneficial owners with the trade ministry. The first filings are due on 11 January 2027.

In 2025 the eLand land administration platform listed three cities using it: Bishoftu, Adama and Shagar. The digital addressing system had been implemented in 73 cities by December 2025. A national farmer database is being joined to Fayda and rural land systems, first across 311 woredas (2025).

Sectoral management information systems

In July 2026 the Federal Auditor General reported weak financial controls across several federal agencies despite their digital systems. It also reported billions of Birr left uncollected through weak revenue administration.

The 2025 GovTech index records an integrated financial management system in use across central and local government. Customs and payroll run on custom-built systems, and tax administration on a hybrid. Most public-service HR services are digitised. Customs declarations run through the eCMS system. A nationwide point-based traffic penalty system launched in 2025, linked to financial institutions by API. In January 2024 an e-filing system was in use among large and medium taxpayers, with adoption still lagging.

DHIS2 has been the national health information system since 2018, with automated reporting at all health facilities. The deployment scored 2.81 out of 5 against the country's 2025 target of 4.09. In 2025 the community health information system ran in more than 7,500 health posts, with over 20 million people registered. That is under half of the country's 17,903 health posts. Health workers report keeping parallel paper and electronic records.

Digitisation of the education management information system was completed in 2024/25. In 2024/25 only 31.8 per cent of primary and middle schools had electricity. In November 2025 a DHIS2-based education data system began with UNICEF in four regions. In 2025 the health ministry announced electronic medical records across 130 health facilities. A 2025 review found the property-tax system, built in 2016 for three pilot cities, was never fully implemented.

On 8 October 2026 the revenue ministry re-advertised for a technical lead for its integrated tax administration system. Expressions of interest were due on 29 October. The security agency's electronic invoice system for the ministry was inaugurated on 8 October 2024.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In July 2026 MESOB's founding director general said the one-stop platform remains intertwined with old institutional systems built to undefined standards. Regional officials said services are being onboarded before the systems behind them are digitised. The platform's Addis Ababa deputy director named that premature integration a major hurdle of the year.

In August 2026 the platform's catalogue listed 180+ services from 23 provider organisations. Fayda is its authentication layer. Passport, tax, investment and work-permit services share data in real time through the MESOB Bridge API gateway (2025).

The 2025 GovTech index rates the national service portal at level 3 or 4, with services completed online end to end. A public jobs portal and e-procurement are in use, while the social protection portal is still being implemented. Federal procurement became fully electronic in the fiscal year to July 2025. In 2025/26 it carried more than ETB 597bn of transactions across 172 federal institutions and 135,000 suppliers. The Auditor General credits it with fewer cash shortages across federal institutions (July 2026).

A Federal Supreme Court directive brought electronic filing and remote hearings to federal courts from November 2025. The court platform requires a national ID number. An unmanned police station staffed by AI kiosks had opened by March 2026. On 30 March 2026 Ethio Telecom's teleSign put document authentication online, verified against Fayda. In 2024 Ethiopia scored 0.3111 on the UN E-Government Development Index, 169th of 193.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In July 2026 Addis Ababa ran 11 one-stop service centres, with more than 150 services from over 25 institutions in one system. It is extending access through mobile units under its Addis Mesob programme.

In July 2026 two integration models were reported: in Addis Ababa MESOB has absorbed institutions' systems, and in the regions it stays tethered to them. Users in the regions are bounced between the platform and the institutions. MESOB began as a federal pilot of 12 institutions and 41 services, meant as a model for national expansion. The interoperability framework's Regional Cells, acting as chief interoperability officers, are its route to regional administrations.

The 2025 GovTech index records the integrated financial management system in use in local as well as central government. In November 2025 a DHIS2-based education data system began with UNICEF, working with schools, woreda offices and zones in four regions. In December 2025 the digital addressing system was being implemented in 73 cities, with work begun in Arba Minch, Jinka and Wolayita Sodo. The eLand platform lists three cities using it (2025).

Rural digital data capture

In 2023 health workers were carrying an offline-first mobile application on tablets into communities. The health ministry issued power banks with the tablets so they work without mains power. By 2025 the community health system had been adopted at 7,808 of the country's 17,903 health posts.

In 2025 civil registration at kebele level was recorded on paper vital-events forms, passed up to woreda, regional and federal offices.

In 2026 FAO began a project to 2028 to turn a digital agricultural extension start-up into a long-term programme. Farmer registration is being joined to the digital ID and rural land systems in a woreda-level pilot. Identity enrolment now reaches into the woredas. Safety-net cash transfers have moved from physical to electronic payment.

In 2023 rural economy was Ethiopia's strongest governance sub-category in the Ibrahim Index, at 66.6 out of 100 and 20th of 54. Rural market access scored 85.0, third on the continent. Rural land and water access fell 9.1 points over the decade to 44.4. Rural representation and participation fell 2.8 points to 63.1.

Technology

AI

In 2025 the United Kingdom committed about GBP 4.5 million over 2025–2029 to a facility advising Ethiopian public bodies on AI. It also builds local skills, for healthcare, education and government administration. The 2026 Africa AI Governance Index places Ethiopia eleventh of 54, scoring 2.12 of four, in its Developing tier.

A National AI Policy was adopted in 2024. Council of Ministers Regulation 510/2022 established the Artificial Intelligence Institute. In 2026 UNESCO's readiness assessment found no binding law yet on public procurement of AI, algorithmic transparency or liability for AI harm.

An unmanned police station staffed by AI kiosks had opened by March 2026. In July 2026 a national data exchange paired with a predictive model was reported approved, and not yet built. In October 2025 the revenue ministry and customs launched an AI customs chatbot and AI passenger risk-screening. By August 2026 a call-centre AI aid built on more than 100 ministry guidelines served health extension workers in five regions. In 2025 a ministry fertiliser advisory using machine learning reached 49,000 farmers.

ICT Industry

Safaricom Ethiopia's service revenue rose 54.2 per cent to KSh 9.68 billion in the quarter to December 2025. Its mobile money arm earns a fraction of what the same product earns in Kenya (June 2026). Ethio Telecom's revenue grew by a third in 2025/26. Data overtook voice as its largest earner that year. The deadline for its 10 per cent public share offer, sold through Telebirr, was extended to 14 February 2025.

At MWC 2026 Ethio Telecom signed network expansion agreements with Ericsson, ZTE, Huawei and Nokia. Under Directive 792/2021 the Communications Authority must approve in advance any shareholding change to 25 per cent or more. It must refuse one it judges anti-competitive.

Better Auth raised a US$5 million seed round in June 2025 for open-source authentication technology. In May 2026 the revenue authority froze and swept the accounts of domestic payment gateways over a disputed tax claim. Ethiopia's Business and Competition Regulations score rose 21.4 points over 2014–2023 to 61.2 out of 100, 18th of 54.

Innovation ecosystem

The Startup Proclamation 1396/2025 was gazetted on 2 September 2025. It creates a startup designation, a digital startup portal, a designation committee, a grant programme, a credit-guarantee fund and a fund of funds. Ethiopia ranked 134th in the 2025 Global Innovation Index, down from 130th in 2024.

The 2025 GovTech index records a public-sector innovation institution working on digital skills and service innovation. There is no public-sector innovation strategy or programme for it to work to.

Korea granted US$10 million over 2020–2026, delivered with UNDP, to support ICT startups and small firms in Addis Ababa. The grant also funds innovation clusters, legal frameworks for commercialising research and an innovation fund. UNDP runs the Ethiopian part of its timbuktoo start-up initiative over 2024–2026, with about US$1.7 million. Both close in 2026.

In April 2026 the e-mobility firm Dodai closed a US$13 million Series A, US$8 million of equity and US$5 million of debt. Better Auth closed a US$5 million seed round in June 2025. No startup designated under the proclamation, and no grant from its fund, has been found.

Capacity

Literacy

In 2023 the Ibrahim Index scored Ethiopia's education completion at 36.6 out of 100, 26th of 54, up 10.5 points over the decade. Education enrolment scored 32.4, 37th, up 1.0 point.

Education quality fell 4.9 points to 40.3 out of 100, 31st of 54 (2023). Human resources in education stayed among Ethiopia's strongest measures, at 83.0.

Only 31.8 per cent of primary and middle schools had electricity in 2024/25.

In January 2025 Addis Ababa announced that Fayda would replace the birth certificate for school registration from the next academic year. The education ministry and the National ID Program had migrated student biometric records to issue Fayda-based student IDs.

Training and skills

In 2025 the GovTech index recorded a public-service digital skills strategy and a free training programme. Its content covers basic digital skills and data literacy, not advanced skills. In August 2025 more than 120,000 Addis Ababa government employees had enrolled in Udacity coding courses. Many skipped the material to pass the tests, and completed courses sold at 200 Birr a certificate.

In 2024 the European Union committed EUR 14 million over 2024–2032 to digital skills in technical and vocational training. The UK's EthiopiAI programme for 2025–2029 funds technical assistance to public bodies and local skills-building. The World Bank's Digital Foundations Project, closing in 2026, carries a digital skills component. In August 2026 the government's coder programme was reported as the 7 Million Coders initiative. No enrolment or completion figure for it has been found. In 2023 the Mastercard Foundation's Young Africa Works targeted dignified work for 10 million young Ethiopians by 2030.

In July 2026 more than 300 experts and data collectors had been trained on the National Livestock Data Standards. The Ministry of Agriculture owns the training-of-trainers cascade.

In 2023 health workers using the community health information system named poor training among the barriers. They also named parallel paper and electronic recording, and fear of liability for stolen devices.

Research institutions

In 2020 KOICA granted US$10 million over 2020–2026, delivered with UNDP, for innovation clusters linking universities and industry. The grant also covers legal frameworks for commercialising research and an innovation fund. In 2025 Ethiopia ranked near the bottom of the Global Innovation Index.

Across 305 health facilities, an index of local use of HMIS data rose from 7.2 to 7.9 between 2019 and 2020. In August 2026 a systematic review of Ethiopian digital-payment studies since 2015 ranked trust first among the factors in user satisfaction.

The National ID Program adopted the Digital Impact Alliance's Digital ID Assessment as an official monitoring tool, first run in early 2025. It was re-run in 2026.

Inclusion

Access to services

In 2023 Ethiopia scored 19.0 out of 100 for equal access to public services, 47th of 54 African states. The score rose only 2.2 points over the decade. Access for women scored 23.6, 43rd of 54, up 15.8 points since 2014.

In October 2025 Fayda was mandatory for a new bank account, a SIM, a tax number and national examinations. Enrolment is formally voluntary, and registration and the number are free. By October 2026 the programme counted more than 52 million registrations. For refugees the credential replaces the case-by-case endorsement letters once needed for the same services (2025).

In 2025 Ethio Telecom offered telebirr access by IVR, USSD and voice command, and connectivity discounts of up to 35 per cent, for people with disabilities. In October 2026 the federal ombudsman took complaints, with status tracking, and freedom-of-information requests online.

The fifth phase of the Productive Safety Net Programme targets around 8 million people (2025). Its transfers have been moving to electronic payment. On central bank figures, bank-account ownership rose from 22 per cent in 2014 to about 45 per cent in 2020. An assessment of the second inclusion strategy put adult account ownership at 66 per cent in 2025, against a 70 per cent target. The 2025 Global Findex found formal account ownership had barely moved by 2024.

In July 2026 Addis Ababa's one-stop centres and mobile units brought more than 25 institutions' services into one system. In September 2026 Ethio Telecom put smartphone penetration at about 36 per cent. It secured 15 billion birr from Siinqee Bank in September 2026, four billion of it for low-income users' handsets. It aims to supply 2.6 million devices over the next fiscal year, it said in September 2026.

Digital divides

Rural electricity access trails urban supply by a wide margin, at 43.6 per cent in 2023. In 2024 unique subscriber penetration and internet use ran far below SIM counts. In 2025 high handset costs and limited supply were holding back smartphone uptake. Ethio Telecom is looking to local production to widen reach.

In 2023 Ethiopia had a large gender gap in mobile internet use. In August 2026 the Fayda programme said it had prioritised registering women. It serves people without smartphones through custodian wallets run by agents (2025). Women's access to public services remains among the weaker records in Africa, though it improved over the decade.

In March 2026 Ethio Telecom and Safaricom Ethiopia were required to contribute 1.5 per cent of revenue to the Universal Access Fund. Article 6 of Directive 792/2021 lets the Communications Authority exempt services from licensing after consultation. It names community networks and not-for-profit organisations among them. In October 2026 the national human rights commission had a draft toolkit on eight dimensions of digital inclusion for disabled and older people.

Data

National statistics

On 30 June 2026 parliament approved an Ethiopian Statistical Service Proclamation to strengthen the service's independence. The Council of Ministers had referred the draft on 26 May 2026. No gazetted text had been found by October 2026. The second National Strategy for the Development of Statistics ran to 2019/20. In February 2024 the planning ministry and statistics service launched a three-year programme of economic, agricultural and population censuses. It was costed at about ETB 35 billion to 2026, with the population census held to its final year.

The statistics service says three censuses were held, in 1984, 1994 and 2007, and current population figures are projections. In 2023 Ethiopia had held neither an agricultural nor a business census in twenty years. Only two labour force surveys had been run in the past ten years.

In 2023 the Statistical Performance Indicators put Ethiopia's weakest pillars at data sources and data infrastructure, both at 20–49%. Data use and data products scored 70–89%, and data services 50–69%, in 2023. In March 2026 a US$50 million World Bank statistics project was at concept stage, with approval estimated for 5 November 2026. It would finance censuses and surveys, administrative data and pilots of satellite and mobile-positioning data.

Ethiopia's statistical capacity scored 63.4 out of 100 in 2023, tenth of 54, after a 12.4-point rise over the decade.

In 2024 the national health reporting platform fell short of the government's own 2025 maturity target. Civil registration runs nationwide in law but recorded only a minority of births in 2023. The taxpayer database is computerised and centralised (2025).

Open data

Ethiopia's open data policy is the 2016 EITI Open Data Policy, an extractive-industries transparency instrument. In 2025 a national data strategy was still planned, not adopted. The open data portal carries only basic datasets and is updated largely by hand (2025).

Population and vital statistics score 2 out of 10 in the 2024 Open Data Inventory, Ethiopia's lowest scored category.

Nineteen of the 23 categories scored in that inventory sit between 3 and 5 out of 10, with labour statistics the only one above that band, at 5.5, and pollution and digital and telecommunications statistics at zero.

In 2025 documentation and assessments for five parts of the digital government estate were kept internal. Nothing is published on how the right-to-information regime performs. Nothing is published on how the data protection regime operates (2025).

In August 2026 the Development Bank of Ethiopia's disclosure policy made strategy papers, legal files, security protocols and contracts permanently non-declassifiable. It lets the bank refuse any request requiring it to create or collect data it does not already have.

Ethiopia's disclosure of public records score rose 23.3 points over 2014–2023 to 44.0 out of 100, 14th of 54. Accessibility of public records stands at 37.4, 18th of 54 (2023).

Use of satellite data

In July 2025 the Ethiopian Communications Authority stated that no discussions with Starlink had taken place, were under way or were planned. No application for satellite broadband entry has been found. In December 2025 the Space Science and Geospatial Institute's digital addressing system was in use in 73 cities. No national programme applying Earth observation to agriculture, land or environment had been found by August 2026.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In December 2025 a memorandum under the America First Global Health Strategy set out declining United States funding for Ethiopian health data systems to 2030. Ethiopia undertakes to meet the software licensing, cloud, hardware maintenance and replacement costs itself. It follows about US$60 million that USAID put into the Ethiopia Digital Health Activity over 2020–2025. That activity supported DHIS2, OpenMRS and the layer linking them.

In April 2025, after the United States aid cuts, national DHIS2 systems including Ethiopia's were reported online on local staff. Donor-funded project systems such as PEPFAR's global HIV database were shut down.

China activities

In July 2026 Ethio Telecom and Huawei were discussing extending their network-equipment relationship into cloud, AI, cybersecurity, IoT and data-centre services. The talks cover local software development and system integration. They reach telebirr, covering payments and e-commerce integration and smartphone financing.

In March 2026 Ethio Telecom signed expansion agreements with Ericsson, ZTE, Huawei and Nokia at MWC 2026. By August 2026 ZTE had completed work at 879 sites, across RAN modernisation, microwave links and IP RAN.

EU activities

In 2024 Agence Française de Développement committed a EUR 80 million sovereign concessional loan to modernise Ethiopian Electric Power's networks. It sits in a 2024 package with the European Investment Bank (EUR 83m), the French Treasury (EUR 55m and above) and EU grants (EUR 40m). The record does not split out a digital share.

The European Union appears more often than any other financier in Ethiopia's digital record, and it does so by number of commitments rather than by value (2026).

The Commission committed EUR 1.2 million over 2020–2025 to the Ministry of Innovation and Technology for digital reforms. It committed about EUR 1.35 million over 2021–2024 for a health insurance information system. It committed almost EUR 5 million over 2023–2027 to help lenders reach small firms in the conflict-affected north. That programme does not split out its digital share. Further strands counter online hate speech to 2026 and build digital skills in vocational training to 2032.

In 2018 DEG put EUR 4 million into M-BIRR, its first fintech financing in Africa, alongside the European Investment Bank and Finnfund.

Gulf/UAE activities

In November 2025 Ethiopia's national switch was integrating with the UAE's Aani payment system. In August 2026 the Horizon Fiber corridor's route was extended to Jeddah. No Gulf state or Gulf company had been found holding a financing commitment, infrastructure stake or platform in Ethiopia's digital sector by August 2026.

India activities

Fayda is built on MOSIP, the open-source identity platform incubated at IIIT-Bangalore (July 2026). In December 2025 India's Ministry of External Affairs gave RailTel a work order of Rs 19,83,74,494 for a data centre in Ethiopia's foreign ministry. The work is to be done by December 2029. In September 2026 Ethiopian officials joined a UNDP learning mission to India on digital public infrastructure. At the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai the national payment switch explored links with international payment networks.

Digital sovereignty

In April 2026 the security agency INSA launched Gotera Cloud, offering infrastructure, platform, software and backup services to institutions. It was presented as keeping citizens' and institutions' data inside the country. Digital Ethiopia 2030 includes a sovereign government cloud (December 2025). Since July 2024 controllers and processors must store locally collected personal data on servers in Ethiopia. The government cloud's hosting arrangements are kept internal (2025).

In July 2026 Fayda ran on the open-source MOSIP platform with its engineering staffed in-house. Its operator is a state-owned enterprise inside the sovereign holding company (August 2026). The payment switch is co-owned by the National Bank and 33 payment providers (2025). No statement had been found by September 2026 of who holds the switch's source code, or whether its operating contract has exit terms.

The December 2025 health memorandum with the United States rests personal-data protection on each side's domestic law. Its data-sharing arrangement runs two years beyond the 2030 term. It must be compatible with Ethiopia's agreements with other countries and bodies. No published terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit had been found by September 2026 for the Huawei collaboration or the RailTel data centre.

In May 2026 the AI Institute's director general set out plans for sovereign cloud, a national data lakehouse and indigenous multilingual models. In January 2026 Ethiopia was one of seven states in IGAD's regional digital integration project, which covers cross-border data flows.
