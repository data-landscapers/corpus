Sao Tome and Principe: status report

Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base, from outputs/reports/STP/ledger.csv (48 systems and instruments, 13 of them Not held). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.
Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

São Tomé and Príncipe's automatic electoral roll delivered its first real-world test in 2026 and the result was mixed: built under the EU-funded PReSE project and first used for the 19 July presidential election, the roll's provisional update for the 27 September elections had to add 1,860 eligible voters the automatic civil-registry derivation had missed through a process failure — deriving a roll automatically from the civil registry reproduced that register's own omissions rather than correcting them. Foundational digital identity remains mid-build: a MOSIP-based pilot with system integrator Ooru has run since 2024 with a roadmap targeting January to May 2026, while a parallel "BI para Todos" campaign issued free identity cards to remote localities using a Starlink-linked mobile brigade.

Cabo Verde and Macau both moved on data-protection cooperation with STP's regulator, ANPDP: São Tomé is a founding member of the Rede Lusófona de Proteção de Dados, and Macau's DSPDP signed a cooperation MoU on cross-border data flows and AI supervision on 1 June 2026. Financing for the country's core digital-transformation programme comes almost entirely from two sources — the World Bank's US$21.8m "Digital São Tomé and Príncipe" project, 36% disbursed by September 2025, and a 2017 AfDB payments-infrastructure grant — with no domestic budget line for digital transformation held anywhere in the base.

Thirteen of forty-eight rows are Not held: no compute capacity, satellite-data programme, technical-standards body, open-data portal, ICT-sector policy, or Indian or Gulf-state engagement is on record.

CST's network expansion reached its 35th antenna at Água Izé, Cantagalo, in March 2026, ending what the operator describes as prolonged local network instability, under an expansion plan first announced in January 2025 to prioritise larger and then smaller schools. Starlink activated commercial service in December 2025, after the US chargé d'affaires publicly backed its entry in February 2025 in the same period the US closed its 32-year-old Voice of America relay station in the country; a standard kit costs roughly US$257, with a residential monthly plan around US$79.50. A submarine cable to Príncipe is tracked as an active World Bank project objective with no completion status or timeline given.

A GoSTP data-centre modernisation programme, covering the digital-ID system's servers and disaster recovery, moved from three costed options — a low-cost requalification, a roughly €4.6m new TIER III build, or a hybrid still under study — to an AFAP tender for servers and disaster-recovery infrastructure by May 2026, implementing the higher-tier recommendation; the current site is flagged as high-risk given its sea-level location. A CSIRT's "materialisation launch" is named as a single line item in a September 2025 World Bank mission report, with no operational date or later confirmation found. No compute capacity beyond this tender, no satellite-data programme, and no energy-linked digital-infrastructure policy is on record.

Foundational digital identity has been piloting since 2024 on MOSIP with system integrator Ooru, with VCI, DEX, portal and e-signature prototypes already live by September 2025 and a roadmap running January to May 2026 — no source held confirms the foundational ID card itself has launched. Running alongside it, the "BI para Todos" campaign issued free identity cards to remote localities including Ilhéu das Rolas and Porto Alegre, reached by a Starlink-linked mobile brigade, with diaspora collection available at the STP embassy in Portugal. SEP 2.0, a new passport-issuance system, launched in April 2026 under a four-year contract with Portugal's Imprensa Nacional–Casa da Moeda.

The EU-funded PReSE electoral-roll project delivered its first real test in 2026: built from automatic civil-registry interconnection and first used for the 19 July presidential election, the provisional roll for the 27 September elections had to add back 1,860 eligible voters a process failure had omitted. The Cadastro Social Único single social registry covers around 12,500 households, roughly a quarter of the population, with the agency itself stating this is registration, not a poverty count. INIC's Portal do Cidadão / Citizen Portal beta launched at INIC's 18th anniversary in March 2026, financed by the World Bank and Co-Develop, with a stated pathway toward verifiable tax and police-clearance credentials. A UNDP-backed Trade Single Window and ASYFIVE deployment was announced in January 2026, integrating 22 public and private institutions and positioning STP as the first African ASYFIVE deployer, not yet reported live. No dedicated rural digital-service programme, no district-administration digitisation, and no earth-observation programme is on record.

Open data

System or instrument Status ↗ source Milestone
Open government data portal Not held —

Two enacted laws underpin the digital-governance framework — the Personal Data Guarantee and Protection Law (03/2016), modelled on EU Directive 95/46/EC and enforced by ANPDP, and a National Framework for Interoperability (1/2024) underpinning DEX, the citizen portal and the trade single window — though the only source held describing either is a 2024 secondary legal-survey chapter rather than the primary texts. AGER's own regulatory framework revision and a separate Aggregated Identification Document Law were both submitted for review by September 2025, with no later source confirming passage of either. ANPDP became a founding member of the Rede Lusófona de Proteção de Dados alongside Portugal, Angola, Brazil and Cabo Verde, and Macau's DSPDP signed a cooperation MoU on cross-border data flows and AI supervision on 1 June 2026; ANPDP also holds oversight of the PReSE registry-CEN interconnection, assessment pending an inspection. STP is a Malabo Convention signatory, though ratification status is not established in sources held.

AGER convened the country's first Internet Governance Forum in July 2026, opened by the Prime Minister, covering digital trust, infrastructure, regulation, privacy and AI, with no outcome document published. A National Strategy for Digital Governance (2020, nine axes) and a 2025 Public Administration Reform Strategy naming e-governance its essential pillar are both in force; a Data Governance Capacity Building Workshop on national adoption of the AU Data Policy Framework ran in July 2026, and AUDA-NEPAD's Programme for African Developing Island Economies, covering STP among seven states, launched the same month. No open-government-data portal and no distinct national technical-standards body beyond the interoperability law is on record.

Two financing instruments underwrite almost the whole of STP's digital-transformation programme. The World Bank's US$21.8m "Digital São Tomé and Príncipe" project was board-approved in June 2022 and had disbursed US$7.64m — 36% — by September 2025, rated Moderately Satisfactory, financing the digital-ID, citizen-portal, interoperability, CSIRT and data-centre work recorded above. The AfDB's Payment System Infrastructure and Financial Inclusion Project, committed in 2017 at US$3,335,999, shows a disbursed figure of US$4,268,636 in its IATI record — exceeding the committed amount with no explanation found in the source held, a discrepancy to treat with caution rather than resolve here. UNICEF's PNAD equipment tender, open since July 2026 against grant SC220341, is the only other financing instrument on record. No domestic STP government budget line for digital transformation is held anywhere in the base — the hub's own financing aggregate marks the domestic side "pending that dataset."
