Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Angola has a personal-data law in force since 2011 and an agency that publishes fines, a government data centre and national cloud inaugurated in April 2026, an earth-observation satellite in build, and the kwanza admitted to the regional real-time settlement system. It has no cybersecurity law: the bill goes to its final vote on 13 August 2026. Only 52.4% of the population aged five and over holds the national identity card, 63% urban against 30.9% rural, leaving 14.9 million people undocumented; and the telecommunications ministry executed 3% of its budget in the first quarter of 2026, with twenty of its fifty programmes below 5%.

A subsea branch entered service in December 2025 on a US$43m operator-built landing, allocating 23.6 Tbps to Angola; a master service agreement of June 2026 buys an initial 1.0 Tbps of another cable's capacity indirectly, through a neighbouring landing. Against that, the communications programme was cut 30.5% to Kz 159.2bn for 2026, with the fourth-generation expansion line down 42% and the third-generation coverage rubric deleted outright.

The state cannot state its own fibre length. Five official statements since 2023 run non-monotonic in both directions — 22,000 km, then 20,000, then 30,000, then 22,000, then 28,000 — and the regulator's annual statistical report that would settle it was dropped as unobtainable.

Storage is where the year's clearest change sits. The government data centre was inaugurated on 28 April 2026 at 6,225 square metres and Tier III design, with 208 racks and 1,040 kW live in a modular architecture of about 336-rack capacity, reported at about US$89m, and the national cloud launched with it under a progressive and mandatory migration of public services. No source records how much has actually migrated. A commercial Tier III facility opened in October 2025 at up to 3 MW; a Tier IV project announced in 2023 has produced no source in three years.

Cyber-security has a decree architecture and no operating record. The cyber centre's statute of December 2025 makes it a public institute with regulatory, supervisory, inspection and sanctioning powers, and allocates it 30% of the fines it levies plus 30% to a cybersecurity fund that no held instrument creates. No source records the centre exercising any power or naming a director general. Telecommunications is not designated critical national infrastructure, and the bill that would supply the designation is still a proposal — a gap tested when the incumbent's mobile network went down nationwide on 28 July 2026 and the operator stated that core services were restored on 5 August, nine days later, with no attribution or scope of data loss established.

The identity credential did not change; its measurement did. The card has been issuing on the same platform since 2017, and the 2024 census supplied the first national coverage figure the base holds. What moved is delivery: printing was decentralised to all 21 provinces from 1 July 2026, with one province reporting issuance under 24 hours against one to two weeks before. Two facts sit against that. More than 120,000 issued cards were awaiting collection nationwide as at 15 July 2026. And on 28 July the justice ministry priced at-home processing at Kz 250,000, and at-home delivery at Kz 10,000, in one province only, the minister justifying the fee as a product of exclusivity. Birth registration, the pipeline feeding the card, reached 63% overall but only 39.2% among children aged nought to four.

Payments and exchange moved on standards rather than services. Domestic payments completed migration to the international messaging standard on 10 July 2026, and the kwanza was introduced into the regional real-time settlement system on 27 July — the second currency added since that system launched in 2013. A US$150m contract covering the interoperability platform, key-based signatures, digital identity and structured cybersecurity entered execution on 31 July 2026 on a twelve-month clock, financed under Despacho Presidencial n.º 38/26; it is a sole-referenced award with the procurement route unstated, and the single digital identity it names has no design, standard, pilot or launch date.

A national cybersecurity strategy, a council and the cyber centre all took effect by presidential decree in December 2025. The cybersecurity bill was approved in detail in committee on 7 August 2026, with a final vote set for 13 August. The online falsehoods law, passed on a party-line vote in May 2026, entered into force on 6 August 2026, with prison terms from three years for simple publication up to ten where it compromises electoral processes or state security; jurists call the definition too vague ahead of the 2027 election, and NGOs plan a legal challenge.

The 2011 data-protection law fines individuals USD 75,000 to 150,000, and trebles that for legal persons, putting a company's ceiling at USD 450,000. The same text withdraws the regulator from state processing: a statute or decree substitutes for its prior authorisation, and state-secrecy, state-security and judicial-secrecy processing falls outside the law altogether. Deliberations of 26 June 2026 fined two firms over Kz 600m; no deliberation text or date is published.

The authority applying it is an executive creation. Its organic statute of October 2016 lets the President remove any member at any time by decree; the decree that staffed it in September 2019 delegated superintendence to the telecommunications minister and filled three of seven seats, leaving four never established as filled. A 2021 tariff decree gives it 60% of the fees it charges, and a pay decree moved most of each board member's salary onto that revenue from May 2024 — a switch the base cannot establish was made.

Surveillance has an authorisation regime, and it is prosecutorial rather than judicial. Lei n.º 11/20 of April 2020 has the Ministério Público authorise cellular location and electronic surveillance where premises are private, and the head of the police body authorise urgent cases with notice within 72 hours; private persons must open their own cameras to the criminal police in real time. Under the 2021 video-surveillance regulation the licence fee is split 40% to the State, 35% to the police and 25% to the authority giving the prior opinion.

The artificial-intelligence draft is now held: published for consultation in September 2025, 86 articles, a single critical-AI category rather than a tiered risk pyramid, and no competent authority named. A readiness assessment of 30 June 2026 named the absence of a national strategy as the central gap. Parliament authorised the President on 30 July 2026 to legislate an electronic-signature regime.

The foreign layer is where announcements outnumber deliveries. A US$170m cybersecurity and connectivity agreement signed in June 2025 has no disbursement, contract or delivery on record fourteen months on, and nothing links its US$25m first phase to the cyber centre created by decree six months later. A vendor research centre announced for 2027 still has no location or value. The December 2021 Gulf memorandum has one output: the telecommunications ministry attributes the national cloud project to it, announced in February 2023, though no valuation has been published since.

Domestically the sector is small and the measures point down. The country ranked 138th of 139 economies on innovation in 2025, from 133rd in 2024. The state sold 15% of the incumbent operator for Kz 300.3bn, settling on 28 July 2026. No held source states an installed accelerator capacity, and no supervisory authority for artificial intelligence exists: the draft law leaves that to a future regulation.

Domestic money is now the story, and it sits outside the digital ministry's vote. Two supplementary credits carry the weight: US$218.5m gazetted in September 2025 for identity-card universalisation, and US$275m opened in February 2026 for the adult-citizen database update, more than doubling that ministry's allocation. The ministry's own communications programme was cut 30.5% over the same period, and the first per-organ execution table put that ministry at 3% in the first quarter of 2026.

Inclusion is measured better than delivered. Financial inclusion reached 51.7% in the first quarter of 2026 against a 65% target for 2027, with banking at about 32% or 5.7 million people. The digital acceleration project is contracted to give ten million people digitally verifiable identification by March 2030 on a US$300m market-rate loan; US$112.21m had been disbursed by 10 June 2026 and every one of the project's own outcome indicators read 0 as at 30 April 2026, in the same report that lifted both World Bank ratings to Moderately Satisfactory.

Satellite-inclusion announcements share no denominator: 366,000 citizens in November 2025, 20,000 students in July 2026 and 3,972 the following day. A universal-service fund does exist, funded from 1% of operators' gross revenue since 2010; no accounts or disbursement have been published for any year.
