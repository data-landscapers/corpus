Edition of 2026-08-23  ·  103 systems and instruments tracked, 12 of them not held

Compiled 2026-08-23 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 103 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 70 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-23, 8 did not, 13 carry no stated baseline, and 12 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 111 sources for this place in the window — 38 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 73 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The state built a compulsory revenue stack in twelve months. Certified invoicing went from a voluntary pilot to mandatory for every economic agent; public revenue went from a single window at the postal bank to one national platform on which payment outside it is void; and the tax filing portal became the front end for that mandatory payment.

Two institutions were constituted and neither has produced an output. The data-protection commission went from a bill approved in council in July 2024 to members appointed and officers elected, with no decision, sanction or guidance since; the security agency gained a statutory levy-share basis in the finance law for the first time and its national strategy is now out to international tender.

The clearest regression is in statistics: balance-of-payments data collection fell from 98% for 2024 to 41.81% for 2025 as at 21 April, a figure the regional central bank's national director described as a collective concern.

The backbone changed hands. Earlier phases held as usufruct with no capital entry were contributed in kind at FCFA 143,878,429,945, taking the state operator's share capital to FCFA 157,297,453,418 and absorbing over FCFA 20bn of accumulated losses, staged to prepare a listing. In the same window the regulator went from no enforcement action on file to formal notices on both mobile operators with a six-month clock and per-department scores published — one operator attributing northern failures to the outages of the backbone now being readied for market.

Rural build advanced and stopped reporting. A European limb of FCFA 27bn launched in June 2025 with no operational-site count became 76 sites built and equipped, 20 fully operational, rated unsatisfactory at mid-term, with no later count in ten months. The universal fund went from an intention to mobilise 1% of operator turnover to FCFA 1,000,000,000 appropriated for 2026, spent entirely as transfers — its first appropriation figure on file, and flat for three fiscal years.

The national data centre moved from about 90% complete with inauguration announced for November 2025 to 95% with works suspended over an unreleased state tranche, and was then cited by the minister as inaugurated with no date, capacity or operator behind it.

Cybersecurity capability moved on paper only: a strategy neither held nor in preparation is out to tender, an audit-provider standard reached consolidated draft, and the agency's largest operational mandate arrived through tax legislation putting it into homologation of every invoicing terminal.

Tax and payments are where everything moved. Beyond the invoicing and payment changes, the forest-revenue system's taxation module went live and interconnected with the national financial systems, compulsory for forest-sector actors on pain of sanction; a public-transport insurance data platform launched with insurer participation hoped for rather than mandated; a payment aggregator secured a regulator licence; and a European remittance corridor went live from two European countries.

Identity moved from a stoppage to a dependency. Production of the biometric card was halted, with 10,506 identity cards and 25,549 number cards uncollected in one city, then relaunched; the number is now the authentication key for the public-revenue platform and mandatory for eight economic formalities. No national coverage or issuance figure exists at either end. The secure digital identity framework did not move at all: twelve months, two statements of intent, no instrument.

Registry work reached validation and stopped there. The integrated civil-status system went from no published build state to three modules validated, its principal recommendation being that resources for deployment be made available; a local-authority revenue platform reached three validated modules with no deployment; and postal addressing stalled, adopted by decree in July 2025 and still held at the regulator.

The instruments made in this window are all fiscal. The invoicing decree and its four implementing orders, the public-revenue payment order and a tax on newly imported SIM-capable terminals all went from not existing to in force. Nothing comparable happened in electronic-communications law: the 2009 framework diagnosed as a brake on the sector holds no number, text or revision status at either end.

Data protection was constituted. The commission went from a bill approved in council with no members appointed to installed with a president, vice-president and rapporteur and powers to advise, warn and sanction. Installation is the whole of the movement.

Policy documents multiplied without adoption: a data governance strategy reached landscape-assessment stage, an AI strategy got a technical committee with eleven deliverables and a budget of FCFA 150m to 200m still to be secured, and a ministry roadmap of five ambitions and nine programmes was published in a signed opinion piece. The 2030 digital strategy is still awaiting a decree.

One enforcement track stalled: the regulator's two-month subscriber-identification deadline expired around end-2025 with no compliance result, sanction or follow-up survey on file.
