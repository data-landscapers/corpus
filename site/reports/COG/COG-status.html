Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

The state's digital effort is concentrated in revenue collection, and there it is compulsory. Certified electronic invoicing has been mandatory for every economic agent except the state, local authorities on public-service missions and diplomatic bodies since 1 July 2026, with no phase-in by firm size and no assisted route stated; an order of 22 May makes all public revenue payable exclusively through one national platform, payment outside it void.

Against that, the institutions are thin. The data-protection commission was installed on 22 January 2026 and has produced no decision, sanction, registration regime or guidance since; the country has had a cybersecurity agency since 2019 and a national strategy now out to international tender, bids closing 2 September 2026.

The money is legible and the delivery is not: the sector vote is FCFA 30,139,589,283 for 2026, up 30.5%, on 2025 execution of FCFA 3,158,525,582, and no audited figure exists for any year. Eight of the rows below are Not held.

Connectivity is externally financed and behind its own schedule. The transformation project adopted a 2026 budget of FCFA 21,872,000,000, inside a consolidated envelope of EUR 136.5m to a 2027 horizon — the same annual figure two years running. Its rural build stands at 76 sites built and equipped, 20 fully operational and 56 in advanced stages, rated unsatisfactory at mid-term, with no updated count in ten months.

Coverage is where the position is worst. An industry report puts rural coverage at 33% for third-generation and 21% for fourth-generation service, with almost 80% of the rural population outside fourth-generation coverage and half outside any coverage, against about 86% national coverage. The same work puts backhaul spectrum fees at about 5% of operators' total revenue against a sub-Saharan median of 0.5%, a premium falling hardest where fibre does not reach, with no regulatory response on file.

The backbone changed owner rather than performance. A decree contributed the first three phases in kind at FCFA 143,878,429,945, raising the state operator's share capital to FCFA 157,297,453,418 and absorbing accumulated losses of just over FCFA 20bn, to prepare a listing. The regulator's enforcement file blames that backbone's outages for northern coverage failures, having served formal notices on both mobile operators.

Hosting is asserted rather than evidenced. The national data centre was reported at 95% complete with works suspended over an unpaid state tranche in October 2025, then cited by the minister as inaugurated three months later; no opening date, capacity or operator is held.

The public-revenue platform went live on 3 August 2026 on the national payments switch, reconciled in real time and routed to the treasury, with all taxpayers targeted by end-2026. The treasury single account intended to centralise those receipts was still being finalised on the day the platform launched. Certified invoicing runs alongside it under four orders fixing a four-stage homologation, ten-year invoice retention and real-time tax-directorate data access.

Identity is the least documented layer. The unique identification number is the authentication key for the public-revenue platform and mandatory for eight economic and five administrative formalities; production of its biometric card was relaunched in October 2025 after a halt whose date and cause are not on file, and no national coverage figure exists. A secure digital identity framework and wallet has been named twice in twelve months with no legal instrument or pilot.

The integrated civil-status system has three modules validated and no funding decision on file; postal addressing is adopted by decree and still held at the regulator, untransferred to the postal operator; and a local-authority revenue platform is validated with no deployment recorded.

The ministry that runs the civil-service payroll platform has minuted data security problems, recurrent unavailability and a malfunctioning payslip code, with no remediation date. An inter-administration data exchange platform is a named roadmap foundation with no design or timetable.

A decree gazetted on 6 August 2026 creates a public-administration digitalisation task force under the Prime Minister, with four organs and interoperability of public information systems as a standing objective; its strategic committee's first listed task is to audit the level of digitisation across the administration. No session is on record.

Fiscal instruments are the only ones being made. Beyond the invoicing decree and the payment order, a tax on newly imported SIM-capable terminals has been in force since 1 June 2026, earmarked for connectivity and infrastructure, with no rate or yield published.

Data protection is constituted and inactive. Loi n° 29-2019 is in force, its institutional framework completed by a decree of 31 December 2025, and the commission's members were appointed and officers elected with powers to advise, warn and sanction. Nothing states whether controllers must register or notify, what fees or deadlines apply, or whether any enforcement has occurred.

The policy layer is a stack of unadopted documents: a 2030 digital strategy finalised and awaiting a presidential decree; a data governance strategy at landscape-assessment stage; an AI strategy whose technical committee has eleven deliverables and a budget of FCFA 150m to 200m still to be secured; and a ministry roadmap of five ambitions and nine programmes reaching the record through a signed opinion piece rather than an adopted instrument.

Enforcement stops short of outcomes: the regulator gave the operators two further months to complete subscriber identification after finding cards sold pre-activated in twelve named towns, and the deadline expired around end-2025 with no compliance result or sanction on file.

Artificial intelligence is a research centre and a committee. The African AI research centre, created by law in May 2024 and built with a Chinese counterpart, has joined an international alliance, with Russian financing for its third and fourth phases under validation through a debt conversion; no research output, staff count or budget is held. Its training platform holds about 3,000 free online courses against a 10,000-youth target and has published no enrolment figure in fourteen months.

The technology sector is small and its evidence is corporate. The state operator holds an international quality standard, the first Congolese operator to do so, and states an ambition to lead regional digital transit on which no interconnection agreement or capacity contract is on file. The startup labelling regime adopted in July 2025 has no commission sitting or label awarded recorded since.

External technology relationships run to China and Russia and stop at announcement: a joint AI laboratory with no source recording it built, staffed or operating; satellite navigation on the ocean railway at discussion stage; a five-year contract for a private telecom network at an oil field with no record of the network in service. Nothing is held on United States, Indian or Gulf-state presence.

The domestic vote rose and the spending did not follow. The sector programme is FCFA 30,139,589,283 for 2026 against FCFA 23,348,037,007 revised for 2025, while 2025 execution was FCFA 3,158,525,582 — 13.5% of the revised figure, on a numerator excluding personnel. The treasury banking-function account more than doubled to FCFA 5,968,000,000 while the dedicated public-finance information-systems account stayed flat at FCFA 3,500,000,000. No audited outturn exists for any digital line: the settlement-law series ends at exercise 2020.

Universal service is funded at a fixed figure and reports in inaugurations. The fund carries FCFA 1,000,000,000 for 2026, spent entirely as transfers, flat for three consecutive fiscal years, against a three-year record of 200 sites connected and 2025 targets whose achievement is not established. What is reported is two further towns connected in January 2026.

Skills are targets without outturns. The transformation project has carried the same 1,200-young-people training target for two years with no trained figure published; a national cybersecurity training plan is being procured with the strategy under a 24-month donor-financed mission. No national digital-literacy programme, target or measured rate is held anywhere in the base.

Eight instrument rows carry no reliable statement. The security agency's statutes oblige its director-general to publish an annual report on the state of national cybersecurity before 30 June of the following year; none is on file for any year. The electronic-communications framework law of 2009, diagnosed by an industry report as a brake on the sector, reaches the base with no number, title or text. No open-data portal or dataset-release policy exists at all.

Three are relationships that return nothing: United States, Indian and Gulf-state technology presence. The remaining two are the absence of any Congolese public argument about data governance or digital rights, and of a national digital-literacy programme.

Four measures matter for reading this report: no registration, notification or enforcement regime under the data-protection law; no cybersecurity or ICT workforce stock; no energy cost or reliability figure for digital infrastructure; and no audited outturn for any digital line. Each row is in gaps.csv with what would settle it; none has yet been probed.
