Congo: monthly update, September – October 2026

Edition of 2026-10-01-2  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Developments summarised from sources published between the beginning of last month and today.
Summary of the month · Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Inclusion · Data

Summary of the month

Works on the national data centre were put at 82% at an inspection on 22 September, with operation stated for the end of 2026. The interior ministry received the technical documents for an integrated civil-status system on 22 September. A payment aggregation platform, Yano, launched on 14 September, and the prime minister called for a revision of the 2001 law on freedom of information and communication.

Governance

Legislation and regulation

On 17 September the prime minister said the 2001 law on freedom of information and communication must be updated for social networks, disinformation and artificial intelligence, and the media fair's closing resolutions recommended its reform. The resolutions come from media professionals; no draft has been announced.

Regional collaboration

A seminar with the DRC closed with a Brazzaville Declaration urging digitalised migration and customs procedures along the 2,400km border; it binds neither government.

Finance

New investments

Works on the AfDB-financed data centre are running again, put at 82% at a ministerial inspection on 22 September; whether the unpaid state tranche that halted them in 2025 was released is not stated.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

The universal access and service fund put a cumulative figure on the record for the first time: more than 380,000 people in formerly isolated areas now have communication infrastructure, and more than 250 localities are better linked to urban centres and essential services. The figure was given at a regional workshop on emergency telecommunications, where the country's own plan is still to be written; it covers the fund's whole life rather than the period, and the fund has published no annual series against which to read it.

Data Storage

The national data centre is not in service. On 22 September the vice-prime minister put works at 82% and operation at the end of 2026 or early 2027, with energy tests within a month, and a 12 MW solar farm is to back it because grid power alone would deter clients. The operator's own estate is a different thing.

DPI

Data Exchange

The digital ministry published a national digital public infrastructure architecture on 2 September 2026: data exchange between ministries that each keep their own data, MOSIP for a unique digital identifier built on civil-registry records, and Mojaloop for a national payments switch under central bank supervision. No timetable or budget is published.

Digital Payments and Fintech

A domestic platform, Yano, launched on 14 September for group collections and merchant payments across mobile-money operators through a QR code or link, holding no funds itself, backed by a FCFA 655m incubation vehicle. Its co-founder gives no user or transaction figure, and no licence is stated.

Registries

Control arrived before the system did. A circular of 1 September 2026 from the territorial administration bans antedating of civil-status acts and changes of identity or filiation without a court decision. It is an administrative control placed on a paper process while the validated integrated civil-status software waits for deployment funding, and no volume of irregular acts is published to say how large the problem it addresses is. The system itself moved on 22 September, when the state information-systems agency handed it and its technical documents to the territorial administration, with seven undated steps still ahead of deployment.

Sectoral management information systems

The social affairs ministry and a US NGO met on 10 September on implementing a digital tool to monitor children placed in care institutions; the NGO undertook to support building the platform, training staff and supplying hardware.

Inclusion

Access to services

The state-control ministry publishes an online channel for reporting irregularities in public administration, taking reports of corruption, misappropriation of public funds, favouritism, poor service quality, unexplained absence of officials and excessive delay, under a stated confidentiality guarantee. The page carries no date of its own and no figure for reports received or resolved, so what the repository holds is that the channel exists.

Data

Use of satellite data

Kazakhstan's deputy prime minister said the two countries are working to build and launch Congo's first Earth-observation satellite, to monitor forests, farming, mining and flood-exposed areas, reported on 12 September. A delegation led by the head of state attended the international space summit in Paris.
