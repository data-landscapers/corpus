Compiled 2026-08-14 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 93 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 50 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-14, 17 did not, 13 carry no stated baseline, and 13 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 75 sources for this place in the window — 27 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-02), 48 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Two systems entered service in the twelve months to August 2026 that did not exist at its start: the NimbaPay instant payment rail and the Trésor Pay gateway for state fees. Identity moved from a closed World Bank project to a domestic stack with enrolment running in one commune and the national register established as the electoral file's single official source. Hosting, naming and international capacity all moved: a Tier III data centre and sovereign .gn management in September 2025, and two further submarine landings contracted.

Three things did not move. The payments statute and the payments governance body stood at terms-of-reference stage at both ends of the window, as did the RTGS migration. No data-protection law entered the record. And the position on access to platforms was reversed in the window's final days.

International capacity was the clearest movement. At the start of the window Guinea had one saturated submarine landing after some 13 to 15 years; by its end it had contracted a second through the MEDUSA Africa construction and maintenance agreement in May 2026 and joined a third through a Via Africa co-investment in June 2026. Neither carries a published service date, so the position at the end of the window is contractual rather than operational.

Hosting and naming moved from nothing to something in September 2025, with a Tier III data centre in Conakry and reinstated sovereign management of the .gn domain. Backbone capacity is reported by the ministry to have quadrupled to 200 Gbps from 50 Gbps; no independent measurement is on file at either end.

Two positions regressed. Access to Facebook, YouTube and TikTok was unrestricted at the start of the window and blocked at its end, from 28 July 2026, still unacknowledged on 7 August. And the licensing regime for fixed fibre to the home became a matter of open dispute, with the telecommunications union threatening to strike on 21 May 2026 over FTTH restrictions and the Wifi-Zone regime.

Payments carry the window's largest single change. At its start Guinea had no instant payment rail; the central bank opened a project in December 2025 and NimbaPay went live on 22 July 2026. Trésor Pay entered service on 22 December 2025. Licensed electronic-money institutions rose from six in 2024 to eleven by November 2025.

The rest of the payments programme is where the window shows no movement, and the evidence for that is the central bank's own project register: the RTGS migration to ISO 20022 stood undelivered at both ends, held by unpaid invoices to the supplier CMA and prolonged customs clearance; the unique bank identifier project sat at consultant recruitment; PAPSS participation carried no transaction; and the ECOWAS integrated payment project remained withdrawn from the bank's priorities.

Identity moved from a closed donor project to a domestic build. WURI-Guinea had closed in June 2024 with about 8,000 unique IDs; by March 2026 ONECI was enrolling biometric birth certificates and national IDs in Sanoyah commune, and in January 2026 a DGE-ONECI convention made the national register the electoral file's single official source. The file itself went from 6.77m electors at the September 2025 referendum to 6.95m across 23,681 stations for the elections of 31 May 2026. The biometric payroll published its first audit result in November 2025.

One position within identity worsened as the window closed: free issuance, decreed in February 2025, was alleged by the state's own contractor on 28 July 2026 to be charged for at enrolment centres.

Government platforms went from few to several — TELEMO, LANDAYA, eCNSS, Œil du Trésor — while the interoperability layer beneath them stayed at workshop stage.

The governing instruments moved less than the systems they would govern. No data-protection law entered the record in the window; one was flagged at Transform Africa Summit 2025 and no text has followed. The payments statute and the Conseil National de Paiement began and ended the window at terms-of-reference stage.

What was made was fiscal and institutional. A decree of 21 May 2026 created a Digital Compliance Fee on foreign digital services, a Digital Sovereignty Fund and a monitoring platform, none of which existed at the start of the window; enforcement is deferred by a six-month transition. Drafting of a National Data Governance Strategy opened on 19 June 2026. The digital ministry was merged in February 2026 and survived the reshuffle of 27 July 2026 with its minister.

Guinea hosted Transform Africa Summit 2025 in Conakry in November 2025, 42 states attending and 14 memoranda signed, and signed a free-roaming protocol with Sierra Leone at the regulators' council held alongside it; no implementation date or tariff for that protocol has since been published.

The base holds no national AI strategy at either end of the window. What changed is that it adopted a shared six-country Francophone West African governance framework on 6 July 2026, a document that explicitly complements rather than replaces a national strategy. The DouIA programme moved from its first to its second phase in April 2026, on a PR-wire account with no primary document behind it, and cooperation with Rwanda in AI and e-health was identified but not signed in July 2026.

In the sector, Cauridor is the one Guinea-founded firm the base establishes in more than one market.

The private payments market is where inclusion moved. CORIS Money entered in February 2026, Orange Money gained wallet-to-bank interoperability with BNG in March and KS-Wallet followed in April, against six licensed institutions in 2024 and eleven by November 2025. Visa opened talks toward a central-bank memorandum in April 2026; nothing has been signed.

Training capacity gained a private campus with Holberton Guinée in July 2026; the ministry's own review reported more than 10,000 people trained across 2025, a figure with no independent check in the base.
