Compiled 2026-08-17 from the Data Landscapers source base, from outputs/reports/GIN/ledger.csv (86 systems and instruments, 13 of them Not held). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.
Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Guinea's digital build is concentrated in payments and identity. The central bank opened NimbaPay, a national instant payment rail, on 22 July 2026. The identity stack is being rebuilt domestically after the World Bank's WURI project closed two years early in June 2024; biometric birth certificates and national IDs are enrolled in one commune.

The instruments that would govern either are absent: no data-protection law, no cybersecurity statute, no payments statute and no national AI strategy. The payments law and the payments governance council both sit at terms-of-reference stage under a World Bank project.

Two things ran the other way. Facebook, YouTube and TikTok have been blocked since 28 July 2026, unacknowledged by any authority. The state's own biometric-enrolment contractor stated that fees not provided for in the regulations were being taken at centres issuing documents the government had declared free.

International connectivity rests on a single submarine cable. GUILAB, the landing company, described the ACE cable as saturated after some 13 to 15 years as the country's only landing. Two further landings were contracted within the period — a construction and maintenance agreement for MEDUSA Africa in May 2026 and a co-investment in Via Africa agreed in June 2026 — neither carrying a published service date.

The ministry's 2025 review reported the backbone quadrupled to 200 Gbps and 585 schools connected under GIGA, against a 2,200-school target for end-2026; no independent measurement is on file.

Hosting and naming came under national control in September 2025, with a Tier III data centre and sovereign .gn management. The state has held 87.5% of MTN Guinee since 30 December 2024.

FESATEL threatened to strike on 21 May 2026 over FTTH and Wifi-Zone restrictions, and stated that Starlink is not recognised in Guinea; no Starlink licensing decision is on file. Facebook, YouTube and TikTok have been blocked since 28 July 2026 on independent tests by the bloggers' association ABLOGUI, still running 7 August 2026; a consumer-protection union additionally reported WhatsApp restricted and bandwidth reduced for months, with operators saying the decision was not theirs. ANSSI is named as an established agency, but no founding instrument for it is held. Guinea's cybersecurity statute, Loi L/2016/037/AN of 2016, is now held — an image-only scan whose cybercrime and evidence-collection provisions are known only from ANSSI's own summary.

A decree of 30 July 2026 converted the distance-learning institute ISFAD into the Universite Numerique de Guinee (UNG), a public scientific establishment mandated for online higher education and digital-pedagogy support; no programme or budget is published.

NimbaPay opened on 22 July 2026, making banks, electronic-money institutions and microfinance institutions interoperable nationwide, built in 127 days on Mojaloop by Guineenne de Monetique. Tresor Pay, for state administrative fees, launched on 22 December 2025. The BCRG has also gone to tender for a resilient SWIFT infrastructure, bids due 31 August 2026. The BCRG's own project register — undated, its latest dated entry 28 January 2025 — records the rest: RTGS migration to ISO 20022 undelivered, held by unpaid invoices and customs delays; the unique bank identifier project at consultant recruitment; PAPSS participation with no transaction recorded; and ECOWAS EPSS withdrawn from the bank's priorities after one 2024 evaluation meeting.

WURI-Guinea closed on 28 June 2024 with about 8,000 unique IDs issued; ONECI began enrolling biometric birth certificates and national IDs in Sanoyah commune in March 2026, phase one covering 2,000 certificates. The RNPP became the electoral file's single source under a January 2026 DGE-ONECI convention, which carried 6.95m electors across 23,681 stations for the 31 May 2026 elections. The biometric payroll FUGAS confirmed about 130,000 of some 277,000 registered civil servants; the unique personal identification number called for then is still not on record. Free issuance is policy and contested in practice: the state's technical partner Multimedia Glory Guinee stated on 28 July 2026 that unprovided-for fees were being taken at enrolment centres, with no ministry response.

The platform layer is populated, from TELEMO for procurement to LANDAYA for industrial licences; the layer beneath it is not: GovStack is at workshop stage, the NG Tech Innovation platform signed 24 July 2026 discloses no value, duration or scope, and RETSSM has software but no legal provisions, security posture or user base.

A data-protection law is now held, Loi L/2016/037/AN — see Infrastructure, Cybersecurity, above — previously only flagged at Transform Africa Summit 2025 with no text, date or authority recorded. Guinea ratified the African Union convention on cyber security and personal data protection by decree on 26 July 2017, a date the Union's own list puts at 31 July 2018; both the ratifying decree and the promulgation decree for the authorising law are now held, in the same August 2017 gazette, though the law itself remains held only by title. Loi L/2016/035/AN of 2016 frames electronic signature, digital archiving and online commerce, and a 2021 decree makes the telecoms regulator the supervisor of electronic transactions including mobile money. Absent are a payments statute and a Conseil National de Paiement, at terms-of-reference stage awaiting the World Bank's no-objection.

A decree of 21 May 2026 created a Digital Compliance Fee on foreign digital services, tiered from an initial flat rate, a Digital Sovereignty Fund financed from it, and a Digital Analysis and Regulation Platform to monitor foreign services; neither fund nor platform is on record as operating, and a six-month transition runs before enforcement. Drafting of a National Data Governance Strategy opened on 19 June 2026.

The day after NimbaPay opened, the consumer body AGUIDUTEN published twelve conditions — a tariff grid, complaint deadlines, security audits and fraud-statistics publication — with no regulator or operator response on file.

The digital portfolio held through the reshuffle of 27 July 2026, MCENI keeping the minister gained at its February 2026 merger; a World Bank tender that week still names the predecessor, MPTEN.

No official explanation for the block is on record; a consumer-protection union said operators and ISPs told it they were compelled to comply, without naming the authority.

No national AI strategy is held. A July 2026 account of a six-country Francophone West African framework names Senegal and Cote d'Ivoire as the two participants with adopted national strategies and places the others, Guinea among them, as still developing their governance frameworks. Guinea did adopt that shared framework, announced on 6 July 2026 and submitted to the United Nations' first Global AI Dialogue as a written contribution.

The domestic AI work on record is the DouIA programme, whose second phase was launched with a Manifesto of Conakry; the only account held is from a PR-wire outlet and no primary document is on file. Cooperation with Rwanda in AI and e-health was identified at a Conakry meeting on 20 July 2026, with nothing signed.

In the sector itself the largest named object is Cauridor, a Guinea-founded cross-border payments infrastructure operating in 36 countries. No startup act or innovation-financing instrument is on file.
