Status report

This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Pillar 3 of Simandou 2040, launched in November 2025, makes digital the backbone of industrialisation across infrastructure, transport and technology. At its November 2025 launch it set a target of 20 per cent of GDP for the digital sector. ICT is one of the programme's five pillars. The state digitalisation agency ANDE was created by decree in April 2022. The sector's own plan, the National Strategy for State Digitalisation and Development of the Digital Economy, runs only to 2027. Its structuring projects include the government data centre, under ANDE and the digital economy ministry.

The digital portfolio sits in a merged Ministry of Communication, Digital Economy and Innovation, whose minister Mourana Soumah took office in February 2026. Mourana Soumah kept the post through the presidential reshuffle of 27 July 2026. Mourana Soumah was then dismissed by presidential decree in August 2026 with no reason given. The ministry's secretary-general held the portfolio on an interim basis until Fana Soumah was named minister by a decree read on 26 October 2026. The 27 July reshuffle put Djénabou Touré at the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralisation. From there she delivered Guinea's address for the Africa Day of Decentralisation and Civil Registration on 10 August 2026.

Guinea named stronger local data hosting among its digital priorities for 2026 (January 2026). The only data localisation mandate in law covers credit-bureau data. On 14 August 2026 the central bank, with the AfricaNenda Foundation, advertised a four-month consultancy to draft a National Payment Systems and Instruments Strategy for 2027–2030. The call closed at the end of that month.

Guinea scores 41.9 of 100 for overall governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index (2023 data), 42nd of 54. That is below the continental average of 49.3; over the decade the African average rose and Guinea's score did not. It sits below the African average on 71 of the index's 96 indicators. Its largest gain is in Foundations for Economic Opportunity, up 5.0 points over 2014-2023 to 45.0 of 100. The foundation attributes the gain to infrastructure and the rural economy.

Legislation and regulation

Décret D/2021/092/PRG/SGG of 25 March 2021 makes the telecoms regulator ARPT the supervisor of electronic transactions, mobile money among them. The same decree lets ARPT charge on the electronic-payment traffic it supervises. It sits under Loi L/2016/035/AN, the framework statute for electronic transactions. It also obliges e-commerce sellers to show prices in Guinean francs, inclusive of taxes and delivery.

In 2025 Guinea had no payment-systems statute and no national payments council. Both are to come from the World Bank-financed WARDIP project, and their terms of reference awaited the Bank's no-objection. In 2021 the World Bank's digital economy diagnostic found the legal and interoperability framework for e-government inadequate. It recommended regulating the interoperability of digital payments. The electronic transactions law requires prices and contract terms to be stated clearly. It has no dedicated regime for electronic money disputes or chargebacks. Central bank instruction 006/2018 binds microfinance institutions, e-money issuers and postal financial services to disclosure and written contracts. It also gives clients access to their own data.

A presidential decree announced in May 2026 makes cybersecurity audit, certification and incident notification mandatory for entities conducting electronic transactions. ARPT supervises it. A decree of 19 August 2026 gives an electronic signature the same probative force as a handwritten one. Nine draft texts framing the state's electronic signature were under review in October 2026. In May 2026 a telecoms union federation argued that the telecoms law lets global-licence holders provide fibre to the home. It was contesting a restriction imposed on that service.

Data protection

The authority provided for in the 2016 cybersecurity and personal data law has never been established. In 2024 a draft law creating the Autorité de Protection des Données Personnelles was still before the National Transition Council. In October 2025 the digital minister said a personal-data bill creating a supervisory authority had been submitted. No record of its adoption has been found. Law L/2016/037/AN of 28 July 2016 covers sensitive categories, biometric data among them. It sets out rights of access, rectification and erasure.

Under the credit bureau law of 30 December 2019, credit databases or their backups may move abroad only with prior central bank authorisation. The bank grants it only where the host country's security measures equal or exceed Guinea's. No comparable mandate applies in other sectors. In January 2026 the government named stronger local data hosting among its priorities for 2026. In April 2023 Guinea signed the WURI governance framework with other West African pilot states. It set cross-border data-sharing and mutual recognition arrangements for identity credentials.

Regional collaboration

In 2025 Guinea took part in the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System but had recorded no transaction on it. The central bank attended an evaluation meeting on the ECOWAS EPSS payment project in July 2024. It then removed the project from its strategic priorities for 2025.

Guinea's WURI project closed on 28 June 2024, two years ahead of schedule. Its early exit leaves Côte d'Ivoire as the programme's advancing partner. In 2024 Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger severed their ECOWAS ties. They have since launched Alliance of Sahel States biometric credentials, starting with a common passport in January 2025.

In June 2025 Guinea and Sierra Leone interconnected their national backbones at the Pamelap crossing. The link falls under the two countries' 2024 Digital Pact. In June 2026 Guinea's officials joined Liberia's and Sierra Leone's in agreeing to commercialise spare fibre on the TRANSCO CLSG power network. In June 2024 the state backbone company and Mali's transmission company agreed to interconnect their networks. A link to Côte d'Ivoire's network is planned with no timeline.

The World Bank's second WARDIP phase, announced on 11 March 2026, covers Benin, Liberia and Sierra Leone. Guinea is in the first phase, with The Gambia, Guinea-Bissau and Mauritania. The ECOWAS budget for 2026-2028 records nil for its Youth Entrepreneurship in ICT line from 2024 through 2028.

On 4 October 2024 ministers, Guinea's among them, adopted the ECOWAS Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024-2029 at Cotonou. They urged full implementation of the ECOWAS roaming regulation by the end of 2025. A regional digital roadmap followed in January 2026, committing the bloc to a digital single market. On 26 September 2026 Guinea's and Niger's audiovisual regulators committed to press platforms for algorithm-transparency protocols. They acted under the regional regulators' platform, whose presidency had passed to Guinea.

Standards

The central bank publishes its payment-systems framework, including a 2017 instruction on real-time gross settlement. The framework also covers the automated clearing house and Swift. Migration of the RTGS to the ISO 20022 messaging standard began on 3 June 2024. It has been held up by unpaid invoices to the supplier and slow customs clearance.

In March 2026 Guinea's biometric civil registry and identity system ran on the open-source MOSIP platform with TECH5 biometrics. The state digitalisation agency publishes its interoperability rules and reference standards. In April 2026 Guinea held a GovStack workshop under the World Bank's regional digital integration project. Its aim was an interoperability foundation for digital public services. The national digital health strategy for 2021-2025 lists standards and interoperability among its action areas.

In March 2026 the WURI supervisory committee assessed how far participating countries had harmonised their technical specifications. The aim is mutual recognition of identity credentials across the region.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In September 2026 the communication authority banned France 24 on every broadcast and online platform. It withdrew the correspondents' accreditation and ordered the telecommunications regulator to enforce the ban. It then banned ten web TV channels and two news sites for operating online without authorisation.

In July 2026 the firm deploying biometric civil registration alleged that unofficial fees were charged at enrolment centres. Official declarations say biometric documents are free. The firm asked the territorial-administration and security ministries for unannounced inspections and sanctions. It also asked for a campaign restating that the documents are free, and a freephone reporting line. Neither ministry had responded at publication.

Digital Freedom fell 19.7 points over 2014-2023 to 40.6 of 100 in 2023, 35th of 54. It is one of Guinea's ten most deteriorated Ibrahim Index indicators. In 2023 freedom of expression and belief scored 58.5 (30th of 54) and media freedom 56.1 (23rd). Both fell over the decade, by 11.2 and 7.2 points.

In 2023 disclosure of public records was among Guinea's worst Ibrahim Index indicators. The July 2026 memorandum on the security of the national identity register was announced with no value or duration. In August 2026 registry officials named information gaps and material constraints as barriers to obtaining civil-status documents.

Before the 2025 referendum a platform gave the draft constitution in national languages, with lawyers answering questions within 48 hours. The Secretariat-General of Government posts draft laws online for comment, the revised labour code bill among them. Comments on them run to single figures. The government's Guinée Connect app takes citizens' questions to ministers and runs a monthly thematic consultation.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The state's own money carries 49.4 per cent of the digital lines in Guinea's 2025 initial finance law as appropriated, GNF 320.4 billion of GNF 648.0 billion, against 39.7 per cent of GNF 637.8 billion in 2024.

In 2024 external money for digitising and securing the land cadastre stood at GNF 192.2 billion. The agro-pastoral digitalisation project had GNF 126.2 billion in 2024. In 2025 the two fell to GNF 160.0 billion and GNF 46.5 billion. The treasury's line for the tax directorate's information system rose to GNF 52.5 billion in 2025, from GNF 8.0 billion. The 2025 law carries GNF 23.4 billion for the regional digital integration programme without naming a funder. That programme's USD 60 million IDA credit to Guinea sits in a first phase that runs to 2028.

A decree of 21 May 2026 sets a 3% Digital Compliance Fee on foreign streaming, cloud, software, digital advertising and e-commerce services. Twelve months after the May 2026 decree the fee moves to tiered rates of 1.5% to 7%. The decree creates a Digital Sovereignty Fund financed mainly from the fee. The fund is for infrastructure, cybersecurity, training and innovation. The 2015 telecoms law finances universal service from an operators' contribution that the regulator collects into a separate account. Collection and disbursement are left to two presidential decrees.

In January 2026 the central bank tendered for fitting out and certifying a Tier III data centre of its own, paid from its own resources.

Guinea opened 2026 on provisional twelfths that carried the 2025 appropriations forward. The 2026 initial finance law was enacted in March 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

ONECI and NG Tech Innovation signed a memorandum of understanding in Conakry on 24 July 2026. It covers an identity-verification and interoperability platform built over the Registre national des personnes physiques. It was announced with no value or duration.

On 5 December 2024 the central bank and AfricaNenda signed a memorandum to stand up a national payment switch, with Guinéenne de Monétique as partner. The Guinea–Sierra Leone fibre interconnection is carried out under the two countries' 2024 Digital Pact. Visa opened talks with the Finance Ministry about digital payments in April 2026.

New investments

A 2025 China Eximbank concessional loan of CNY 385,329,021, about USD 54 million, finances Safe City surveillance at 324 sites in eight cities. On 18 December 2023 MIGA issued a EUR 366.33 million guarantee to Sonatel of Senegal over its equity in Orange Guinée. Of that 2023 guarantee, about USD 373 million covers the telecoms company and about USD 26.5 million the mobile money arm. Both are political risk cover, not money disbursed.

Guinea is one of two beneficiaries of the World Bank's USD 122 million WURI Phase 1 programme, 2018 to 2026. It is one of four in the USD 266 million first phase of the regional digital integration programme, 2023 to 2028. Its own share is a USD 60 million IDA credit, appraised on 7 November 2023. USAID is funding the Notre Santé health programme, DHIS2 included, with about USD 29 million to 2028. The African Development Bank names Guinea in an XDR 6,060,000 grant for West African unique bank identification, 2023 to 2026.

Eleven digital-sector commitments touching Guinea are on record, seven to the country directly and four regional operations naming it, spanning 2018 to 2030. The direct seven come to about USD 529 million as at August 2026, roughly USD 400 million of it risk cover rather than money moving. Four are concessional loans, four grants, two guarantees and one equity, and the Orange Guinée guarantee is the largest single live commitment. Connectivity and digital payments take the most, the rest going one or two apiece to identity, health and local-government information systems, rural data capture, the ICT industry and innovation; nothing goes to data storage, cybersecurity or digital skills. MIGA leads by value, ahead of China Eximbank and USAID; the World Bank is the most consistent presence, funding a USD 60 million broadband allocation and naming Guinea in two regional programmes worth USD 388 million (August 2026).

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In May 2026 Guinea signed the construction and maintenance agreement for the MEDUSA Africa cable. The state landing-station operator GUILAB is the landing party for this second submarine cable. In June 2026 GUILAB's director said the single cable used for some thirteen to fifteen years, ACE, was saturated.

Guinea has moved from one submarine cable landing to two further cables under commitment within roughly a year (June 2026).

Facebook, YouTube and TikTok were blocked from 28 July 2026, with no authority acknowledging it. The block was still in place on 7 August 2026. In August 2026 providers and operators said the decision was not theirs, and a consumer union reported WhatsApp restricted too.

By 2025 a fibre backbone of nearly 12,000 km reached all administrative regions. More than 600 unserved white zones are the priority for 2026. In June 2025 it was linked to Sierra Leone's at Pamelap at 42.5 Gbps, with a 100 Gbps upgrade planned. On 20 May 2026 the backbone's first loop was raised to 400 Gb/s from 200 Gb/s. The target is 600 Gb/s across the network by the end of 2026.

4G networks covered about 78 per cent of the population in 2023. Mobile connections equalled 95.1 per cent of the population at the start of 2025. At the start of 2025, 26.5 per cent of Guineans were using the internet. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index scored Internet & Computers at 10.0 of 100, 48th of 54. Mobile Communications was Guinea's fourth most improved of 96 indicators over the decade to 2023.

MTN's Guinean business, with just over 3 million subscribers, passed to the state in a deal made official on 30 December 2024. A decree of 11 December 2025 made the state sole owner of Areeba Guinée, trading as MTN Guinée. In July 2026 the state pressed Orange Guinée to improve quality, extend coverage and cut the cost of access. In May 2026 the telecoms union threatened a strike over limits on fibre to the home and the Wifi-Zone regime.

Data Storage

In September 2025 Guinea opened a national data centre in Conakry, certified Tier III by the Uptime Institute. In September 2026 the prime minister found it still not fully operational. He put it at about 90% complete in September 2026, on nearly US$10m of investment, with one payment outstanding. The state secured sovereign management of the .gn country domain in 2025. The centre is a structuring project of the National Strategy for State Digitalisation to 2027, under ANDE and the digital economy ministry.

In January 2026 the government listed stronger local data hosting among its digital priorities for 2026. The one binding localisation mandate is the 2019 Credit Bureau Law. Under it, credit databases and their backups may leave Guinea only with prior central bank authorisation.

Energy

In 2025 the grid reached 53 per cent of the population, close to the 55 per cent targeted for 2027. In 2021 grid access was about 89 per cent in urban areas and about 21 per cent in rural areas. In 2025 the World Bank's second electricity access project planned private hybrid mini-grids for 26 remote rural localities. The rural electrification agency AGER supports that work.

In August 2026 daily power cuts in Conakry left cybercafé computers switched off through the vacation season. That season is the peak of student demand for computer training and online applications. In 2023 renewables made up around 66 per cent of installed capacity, overwhelmingly hydropower.

In 2018 residential tariffs of roughly 107 to 453 Guinean francs per kilowatt hour were among the lowest in West Africa. In 2018 households spent about 1.2 per cent of monthly income on electricity at the subsidised tariff. On 2018 figures, full cost-recovery tariffs would push that share past 10 per cent. Access to Energy scored 22.8 of 100 in 2023, 39th of 54, up 7.1 points over 2014-2023. Infrastructure scored 28.9 of 100 in 2023, 40th of 54, one of Guinea's three worst sub-categories. It had gained 9.5 points over the same decade.

Technical Capacity

When the World Bank-financed identity project closed in mid-2024, Guinea's institutional arrangements for it were still not fully consolidated. It closed in June 2024, two years early, and undisbursed identification money was cancelled at the government's request. Re-engagement was left to a later financing instrument.

The RNPP identity-verification platform signed in July 2026 brings NG Tech Innovation teams to Conakry to transfer skills to national staff. In August 2026 the president said he would follow the GEDA decrees-and-orders platform from his own office. His stated aim was faster processing.

Cybersecurity

A presidential decree announced on 21 May 2026 makes cybersecurity audit, certification and incident notification mandatory for every public and private entity conducting electronic transactions in Guinea. The telecoms regulator ARPT supervises it. An incident affecting many users requires an initial alert within 24 hours and a detailed report within 72. Security audits fall due every three years, and foreign companies serving Guinean residents are caught by the decree. The resulting audit reports stay confidential, so no one outside ARPT sees the evidence the audits produce on the state of the country's systems.

A national cybersecurity policy and strategy for 2022–2027 was validated in April 2022. The cybersecurity agency launched DJAMA SUTURA, a national platform for reporting cybercrime, in November 2024. In its May 2025 stocktake the digital ministry said it had begun setting up a national computer emergency response team and security operations centre. It had also begun setting up a national public key infrastructure. Decrees of 22 June 2026 fixed the procedures for auditing, controlling and certifying the information systems used in electronic transactions. The state's public key infrastructure was being built by the national digital agency's teams in October 2026. Law L/2016/037/AN of 28 July 2016 on cybersecurity and the protection of personal data doubles as the country's general data protection statute. Its provisions are set out in this report's governance chapter. Draft texts to add cybersecurity duties to it went to a national consultation in August 2026.

Guinea scored 56.55 of 100 on the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index, up from 20.53 in 2020 (2024). That result predates the 2026 decree. The National Cyber Security Index scored the country 34 of 120 in September 2026. It found nothing on incident response, crisis management or critical infrastructure. In October 2024 ECOWAS ministers adopted a directive on confidence-building measures in the cyber and ICT field. They urged member states, Guinea included, to implement it without delay once published.

DPI

Data Exchange

NimbaPay was launched on 22 July 2026 to move money instantly between banks, e-money and microfinance institutions nationwide. In 2025 Guinea still lacked a secure, unified system for exchanging data across ministries and agencies. Platforms such as eTax and the foreign trade single window worked in isolation, without shared APIs or interoperability standards.

In December 2023 the SYNERGUI platform linked the commercial registry, the tax number and the social security fund in one company-creation workflow. The 2024 upgrade plan for the tax system behind eTax provides a joint tax and social security declaration. It also provides for electronic VAT invoicing. In June 2026 the foreign trade single window was linked to commercial banks, the central bank, customs and the port authority. A January 2026 agreement makes the National Register of Physical Persons the single official source for identifying voters. Before the polls of 31 May 2026 the roll grew partly by recovering enrolment records that had not been synchronised.

In November 2024 the largely biometric PISCES X border platform began synchronising watch-list and immigration data with INTERPOL, IOM MIDAS and US Customs and Border Protection. An arrêté of 25 June 2026 obliges airlines to transmit advance passenger and passenger-name-record data. The data must be hosted in a state-approved data centre and kept identifiable for six months. On 17–23 October 2026 the transport minister held talks in Washington on building the national system.

In April 2026 the state digitalisation agency convened a World Bank-supported workshop on e-services and interoperability. It named passport issuance and identity documents among the priority services for a government-wide exchange. A GovStack workshop followed the same month. The agency publishes an interoperability rules and standards framework. By March 2025 no public or private service had been linked to the foundational identity system. In August 2026 an X-Road exchange platform run by the agency was shown to the prime minister as work in progress.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Guinea's foundational identity pilot issued only about 8,000 unique identification numbers before its World Bank project was wound up in 2024. The project closed on 28 June 2024, two years early. Undisbursed identification money was cancelled at the government's request. As of March 2025 service providers had performed no digital authentications. No public or private service had been linked to the system. The national identity card was established by a 1995 decree.

An identification law adopted on 25 October 2023 sets up a personal identification number and biometric procedures. Under an April 2025 decree on the register, everyone must hold a number from it when dealing with any administration. The World Bank says Guinea's identification arrangements remain unconsolidated. It says the 2023 laws "did not fully reflect the principles of identification for sustainable development".

A nationwide biometric census for civil registration began on 15 April 2025. In March 2026 Sanoyah commune began issuing biometric birth certificates and enrolling citizens for the biometric card. A first phase had 2,000 certificates, on the MOSIP and TECH5 stack. The birth certificate is the foundational document for the card. On 24 July 2026 the identity office signed a memorandum with NG Tech Innovation on securing the national identity register. It was announced with no value or duration. In 2013 only about a fifth of citizens required to hold an identity card had one. In 2023 the ease of obtaining one scored 53.4 of 100, 18th of 54, down 8.1 points since 2014. A June 2026 presidential decree provides for a national platform certifying the identity of SIM cardholders and telecoms users.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Guinea's central bank launched the NimbaPay instant payment system on 22 July 2026, run by Guinéenne de Monétique. It moves money instantly between banks, e-money and microfinance institutions. The Mojaloop Foundation, AfricaNenda and Thitsa Works supported an open-source build that keeps the state in control. By 15 October 2026 seven institutions were connected and about 67,000 transactions worth over GNF 68bn had been processed. Design work had opened at a workshop on 18 December 2025.

In July 2026 the central bank said more than 70% of payments were still made in cash. About 26 per cent of adults held an e-money account in November 2025. Licensed e-money institutions had risen from 6 to 11 in the year to November 2025. Access to banking services scored 11.5 of 100 in 2023, 37th of 54. Coris Bank International launched bank-issued e-money in February 2026. BGB Solutions launched its KS-Wallet app in April 2026. Orange Money linked wallets to accounts at the Banque pour le Commerce et l'Industrie in December 2025 and the Banque Nationale de Guinée in March 2026.

Trésor Pay launched on 22 December 2025 for paying fees, duties and charges to the state by e-money. Orange Money began collecting motor vehicle tax, reconciled to the Treasury, in March 2017. After a December 2023 circular moved pay to bank accounts, civil servants still paid in cash missed their April and May 2024 salaries. Since September 2020 the inclusion agency has paid cash transfers to more than 150,000 households. In 2023 it took US$25m of the IMF food-shock window for more transfers.

In 2025 Guinea had no payment-systems statute, no national payments council and no transaction on the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System. In October 2026 the central bank said it was preparing a payment-systems bill with World Bank support. A 2027–2030 payments strategy went to consultancy tender on 14 August 2026. On 1 October 2026 a regional integration project sought a firm to design a national payment scheme over nine months.

Registries

In August 2026 a national stockout of secure paper left almost every commune unable to issue digital birth extracts for about a month. The certificate can be printed only on that paper, so a supply failure halts issuance nationwide.

Birth registration completeness rose from 28 per cent in 2018 to 64 per cent in 2023. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index scored civil registration 50.0 of 100, 31st of 54, down 12.5 points since 2014. In June 2026 an EU-funded civil-registry application in pilot communes was not yet interoperable with national identification. In February 2025 only 22 per cent of 59 mapped registry centres had internet. By decree, the population register is fed from the national civil status register. On 28 October 2026 the territorial administration ministry launched, through the identity office, the project to build that register, RENEC. It is funded from the national development budget and built with the firm Digitech, to run to 2028. First centres open in 2026, and each citizen is to get a unique identifier from birth. An extension of the persons register opened in September 2026, with a first identity-verification platform due by the end of 2026.

In 2022 an estimated 2.5 to 3 per cent of landowners held a registered title. In 2026 the urbanism minister announced full digitalisation of the land cadastre. The mining cadastre has been a public online register since 2017. Company records are searchable through the OHADA regional registry. In 2023 the unified social registry held more than 977,000 households and 4.86 million people in extreme poverty. A 2019 decree made it the single gateway for social-protection programmes. In July 2025 the electoral roll was presented as a permanent biometric file. It held 6,948,894 voters before the double poll of 31 May 2026.

Sectoral management information systems

In November 2025 a biometric audit had confirmed only about 130,000 of roughly 277,000 registered civil servants. The government puts savings since 2024 at 233 billion Guinean francs. The prime minister said the register "must integrate a unique personal identification number".

DHIS2 has run in all health districts since 2022, but data quality and server security remain weak. Cross-system exchange is not operational. In 2024 routine reporting into it was 99.8% complete, but only 83 of 1,325 approved private facilities reported. In 2025 rural community health workers recorded visits on paper, for typing up at the district office. In March 2022 only 59 of 444 health centres used the electronic vaccine-stock system. A workshop on health information system interoperability was held in November 2025.

The tax authority has run the eTax portal for online filing and payment since September 2020. The social security fund launched an online contributions platform in July 2025. In August 2023 the treasury presented an integrated state accounting system replacing manually kept accounts. It sends payment orders to the bank electronically and links to the tax, customs and central bank systems.

The last security ministry statistical yearbook, for 2018, was compiled from station registers, some missing or poorly kept. The police fingerprint system in Conakry has digitised the paper fingerprint archive. The Conakry commercial court's hearing roll and decisions are online through Judicalex. A consultant contract to further computerise the court was signed on 7 September 2026. In 2024 the government cancelled World Bank results that required school data in a national database for 90 per cent of schools.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In 2025 thirty-nine public service applications were deployed and passport issuance was fully digitised. The government portal offers online civil registration, identity card, passport and driving licence services. In 2024 Guinea scored 0.4006 on the UN E-Government Development Index.

At least seven distinct government digital platforms entered service or procurement between June 2025 and August 2026 — the digital legal gazette, the online social contributions platform, a vehicle-attestation platform, LANDAYA, TELEMO, GEME and GEDA. The two August 2026 launches are internal state-process tools built around control and traceability, a different shape from the one-stop-shop and e-procurement builds.

LANDAYA launched in January 2026 to issue industrial permits within 72 hours. TELEMO, the national e-procurement platform, launched in Conakry on 22 May 2026. In October 2026 a review found its price references and partnerships not yet integrated. A single window for public services, modelled on Azerbaijan's ASAN centres, was agreed in March 2026. In October 2026 the civil service minister presented it to the mayors of Conakry's 13 communes.

On 14 August 2026 the president said he would monitor GEDA, the decrees platform, from his own office. The same week GEME was presented to digitise state mission orders. No budget, vendor or timetable was stated. The livestock ministry launched Ngaynaako, a single digital window for the sector, on 22 October 2026. On 26 October 2026 the fisheries ministry launched KONKÖE, a platform for fishing licences and certificates. It also tracks vessels, landings and fishing revenue.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In August 2026 the territorial administration minister presented civil registration digitalisation as part of decentralisation. The programme covers the 375 communal councils, the Conakry FODECCON fund and the national FNDL and FODEL funds. Work has begun on making the regions decentralised collectivities in their own right.

Full digitalisation of the general land cadastre became a ministerial priority in February 2026. In 2025 the government was piloting digital addressing with the start-up FindMe.

On 25 March 2025 a revenue-management tool tested in Benin was presented for Conakry's 13 urban communes, Coyah and Kindia. Financing for the pilot was still to be found.

Rural digital data capture

In June 2024 the government cancelled, for implementation delays, disbursement-linked results in a World Bank-financed basic education project. They required school data entered by mobile into a national database for at least 90 per cent of schools. They also required dashboards in use by at least a fifth of decision-makers.

In 2025 rural community health workers recorded visits on paper, delivered or typed up at the district office each month. By March 2022 the electronic vaccine-stock system had reached barely any rural centres. The 2018 security yearbook was compiled from station registers, some missing or poorly kept. Since April 2025 biometric enrolment centres have been deployed into rural prefectures. The implementing decree lets registrars record events on paper or electronically. In 2025 a donor-backed programme was putting offline tablet records into selected rural health centres. By September 2026 a pilot had registered 20,463 producers and mapped 23,507 farms in Kindia, Kankan and Mamou. Each producer receives a card with a QR code and NFC chip.

Rural Representation and Participation is Guinea's most improved Ibrahim indicator, up 34.3 points over 2014-2023 to 78.1 of 100. Rural Economy scored 67.8 of 100 in 2023, fifteenth of 54. Rural Market Access fell 10.3 points to 53.5 of 100 in 2023.

Technology

AI

On 12 October 2026 Guinea's prime minister said AI would check road-construction prices against the market and flag overbilling. Public procurement is to be digitised, with Rwandan technical support. A national AI roadmap for 2026–2035 was validated in December 2025. It runs in three phases, from regulatory foundations to AI in public services. In July 2026 Guinea was among six francophone West African states adopting a joint AI governance framework. Since April 2021 customs has selected declarations by a fraud-risk calculation on historical data. In the first quarter of 2022 it covered 78 per cent of declarations. In September 2025 the cybersecurity agency warned of liability for AI-generated images of public figures. In November 2025 the broadcasting authority barred media from carrying AI-generated content during the campaign. A decision of 25 April 2026 banned AI-manipulated content and deepfakes from campaign coverage.

A 2025 national legal report found no AI law or bill, leaving liability to the civil code. In 2026 Guinea ranked 40th of 54 on an African AI-governance index, scoring 0.76 of four and best on strategy. In November 2025 the research ministry signed a memorandum on sovereign, low-carbon AI with a Canadian-led consortium. In September 2026 a start-up offered translation APIs between French and Pular, Malinké and Soussou. In June 2026 another said its Pular-to-French translation model was half trained.

In 2025 Guinea still lacked a secure, unified system for exchanging data across ministries and agencies. An ECOWAS digital roadmap of January 2026 commits members, Guinea among them, to a Regional AI Governance Framework.

ICT Industry

In August 2025 Guinea awarded the build of its national vehicle-attestation platform to two Ivorian startups, SIIN and DIGITECH GROUP. A 2025 China Eximbank concessional loan carries the Safe City surveillance programme across eight cities.

The state took over MTN's Guinean business at the end of 2024. It became sole owner by decree in December 2025. In May 2025 the digital ministry counted a Technopark at Sonfonia among its digital-economy projects. Cauridor, a cross-border payments company founded in Guinea in 2022, operates in 36 African countries. In April 2026 it hired a former Flutterwave executive as chief operating officer. Coris joined the mobile money market in February 2026. The state launched LANDAYA in January 2026 to issue industrial permits digitally.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, business and competition regulation scored 56.7 of 100, 21st of 54, up 1.8 points. In 2023 the wider business and labour environment scored 36.3, 42nd of 54, after a 1.5-point fall.

Innovation ecosystem

Guinea ranked 133rd of 139 economies in the 2025 Global Innovation Index. In 2025 three organisations in the country appeared in the AfriLabs hub directory. They are Trustee Sas, Dreams Tech Hub and Incubateur Saboutech. They work with start-ups in ICT, renewable energy, agribusiness and digital education.

Capacity

Literacy

Guinea's education score stood at 40.4 of 100 in 2023, 42nd of 54, up only 1.5 points across the decade. In 2023 equality in education scored 24.8, 50th of 54. Enrolment scored 29.4 in 2023 after a fall of 3.2 points, and quality 28.7. Human resources in education reached 89.1 in 2023, 18th of 54, almost twenty points above 2014. That measure counts staff numbers and qualifications.

By 2025, 585 schools had been connected to the internet under the GIGA programme, with 2,200 targeted for 2026. In 2024 a World Bank-financed result to put data from 90 per cent of schools into a national database was cancelled.

Training and skills

In 2025 nine private institutions listed computer engineering, computer science or MIAGE degrees on the national admissions portal. In May 2026 digital-skills training for civil servants was among nine projects to modernise public administration. A private coding school opened a Conakry campus on 16 July 2026. In September 2026 a feasibility study for a national digital academy was under validation. It would offer short certified courses in information systems, AI and cybersecurity, first to civil servants.

In August 2026 students taking computer training in Conakry cybercafés could not reliably finish, as power and connection both failed.

On 5 October 2026 an operator's foundation and UNICEF signed a protocol to equip 50 schools in six regions with connected learning hubs. It also plans to train 500 adolescents in science, technology and digital skills. No budget or delivery date is stated.

Research institutions

Univ Connect, run by the agency ANSUTEN, is linking 17 universities and institutes, 13 of them live at its December 2025 launch. In 2025 the programme had reached more than 80,000 students and staff. In 2026 the Cité des sciences et de l'innovation and the higher-education ministry led Guinea's use of the UN blueprint for national AI strategies. Guinea is the second African country to use it, after Lesotho.

Inclusion

Access to services

A 2025 decree on the National Register of Physical Persons gives foreign residents, Guineans abroad and refugees an express right to be registered. Under its 1995 decree the national identity card is issued only to Guinean nationals.

In August 2026 civil-registry officials named information gaps and material constraints as barriers to obtaining civil-status documents. That month communes in Conakry and the interior could not issue digitised birth extracts for want of secure paper. Ratoma kept a small reserve for urgent cases, such as new baccalauréat holders and people travelling for medical care. In July 2026 the technical partner on biometric civil registration asked for a national campaign restating that the documents are free. It also asked for unannounced inspections of enrolment centres and a freephone reporting line. No ministerial response has been found.

In August 2026 cybercafé users in Conakry could not reliably submit job applications, as power and connection were intermittent. Access to public services scored 33.5 of 100 in 2023, 33rd of 54, near-flat across the decade.

Digital divides

Guinea's score for equal socioeconomic opportunity fell 13.4 points over the decade to 23.9 of 100 in 2023, 45th of 54. Its Inclusion & Equality measure stood at 35.7 in 2023, 42nd of 54.

In 2025 the universal service agency ANSUTEN ran inclusion initiatives such as "200 Femmes dans les TIC". In November 2025 the agency's first women's hackathon trained more than 130 young women in Conakry, Labé and Mamou.

In 2021 grid electricity reached about 89 per cent of urban areas and about 21 per cent of rural areas. By 2025 the fibre backbone reached every administrative region, with unserved white zones still on the list for 2026. At the start of 2025, 26.5 per cent of Guineans used the internet. In 2023 internet and computers was among Guinea's ten weakest Ibrahim indicators.

Data

National statistics

The fourth general census began its main enumeration on 5 July 2025. Preliminary results were published on 25 February 2026. They put the population at 17,521,167.

Capacity of the Statistical System rose 11.6 points over 2014-2023 to 38.9 of 100, 38th of 54. It is Guinea's most improved public-administration measure on the Ibrahim Index.

For 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators put Guinea's data products in the 70-89 band. Data services and data sources sat in the 20-49 band, and data use and infrastructure at 50-69.

As of 2023 Guinea had held a population census within ten years, but no agricultural or business census in twenty. Over the decade to 2023 it ran three or more health surveys, two household surveys and one labour force survey. It ran no agricultural or business survey. A presidential decree of 18 September 2026 instituted a first general census of enterprises. It is to count and geolocate formal and informal units and build a national business register.

In 2025 rural health figures still started on paper and were typed up at the district office. Routine health reporting runs on DHIS2 across every health district. Police and gendarmerie files are written into paper registers, the basis of the 2018 security yearbook. The statistics institute's 2024 yearbook, published in October 2025, compiles 2016–2024 series from administrative data.

Open data

Guinea's Journal Officiel went online in June 2025 as a free legal gazette. It carries more than 1,100 issues and over 6,000 legal texts.

Accessibility of Public Records scored 33.1 of 100 in 2023, 23rd of 54, up 6.6 points over 2014-2023. Disclosure of Public Records scored 17.3 of 100 in 2023, 43rd of 54, among Guinea's ten worst indicators. It gained 1.5 points over the same decade.

On the 2024 Open Data Inventory Guinea's strongest category is money and banking, at 6 of 10. Its weakest is crime and justice at 2, and no category reaches 7.

Across the twenty-two 2024 Open Data Inventory categories for which Guinea has a recorded score, the average is 4.2 out of 10 and twenty of the twenty-two fall between 3 and 5 — a flat, uniformly low profile rather than one with real strengths and real gaps.

The mining cadastre has been a public online register of licence areas and holders since 2017. The central bank publishes payment-system time series for 2023-2024. The state digitalisation agency posts its interoperability rules. The July 2026 agreement on securing the identity register came with no stated value or timetable. Nor did the GEME state-mission platform in August 2026.

Use of satellite data

In August 2025 the government was working with the start-up FindMe to pilot digital addressing for postal services and territorial connectivity. A 2023 zoning atlas from the agricultural statistics agency maps land cover for 2005, 2015 and 2020 from SPOT 6 and Sentinel-2 imagery. The 2023 atlas puts arable potential at 13.7 million hectares, 56% of the territory. The national meteorological agency publishes Meteosat products, among them dust imagery, storm nowcasting and precipitation maps. A CREWS project that closed in October 2025 connected 28 revised stations to the WMO Information System.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In November 2024 the PISCES X border system was installed at Conakry's international airport with United States embassy support. It synchronises watch-list and immigration data with INTERPOL, IOM MIDAS and the US Customs and Border Protection targeting system. On 17–23 October 2026 Guinea's transport minister held talks in Washington, including with a US border-technology firm, on a passenger-data system.

USAID's Notre Santé programme committed about USD 29 million over 2023 to 2028. Its components support the national health information systems, DHIS2 and health data governance.

China activities

A 2025 China Eximbank concessional loan finances Guinea's Safe City urban surveillance and security programme. It puts CCTV, command centres and urban security management platforms into Guinean cities.

On 28 April 2026 China handed over the China-aid ECOWAS Headquarters Building in Abuja. China's ambassador presented it as an outcome of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation.

EU activities

In May 2026 Guinea signed the construction and maintenance agreement for MEDUSA Africa, a second submarine cable landing. The state landing-station operator GUILAB is the landing party. The MEDUSA system is designed to link southern Europe with North and West Africa and the Middle East.

By 2023 an EU-funded pilot run by the Belgian agency Enabel had digitised more than 105,000 civil registry records in 10 communes. DXC Technology was its technical partner.

Gulf/UAE activities

In 2019 the government signed a memorandum with the Arab League's Council of Arab Economic Unity on projects under the joint Arab vision for the digital economy. The head of the Arab federation for the digital economy was named the government's special adviser on digital transformation. The United Arab Emirates supported and followed the initiative but did not sign it. No record of its implementation has been found.

In November 2025 Guinea's planning minister signed a protocol with Saudi Arabia on cooperation in planning and statistics. It names no telecommunications, data-centre, cloud or digital-identity deliverable.

India activities

On 2 August 2019 Guinea's higher education ministry signed a memorandum with Telecommunications Consultants India Limited on the e-VBAB Network Project. The project upgrades India's Pan-African e-Network tele-education and telemedicine satellite platform. No report of its operation or of any successor has been found.

Digital sovereignty

Opened in 2025, the national data centre was built to bring home state data hosted abroad at an estimated cost of nearly US$5 million a year. In September 2026 the prime minister was looking for qualified staff and, if needed, an interim manager to run it. In July 2026 the digital ministry set up a technical committee to examine COREX group's proposals for operating it.

In May 2026 a World Bank-supported study on hosting public data and a sovereign cloud was presented to state bodies. Drafting of a National Data Governance Strategy began in June 2026. The 2016 data protection law allows transfers to non-ECOWAS states only with the Autorité de Protection des Données's authorisation. The receiving state's protection must be equivalent or higher. That authority has never been established.

In July 2026 the digital minister's talks with Huawei in China covered the national fibre network, a national data centre and a sovereign cloud. They are to lead to a memorandum and a framework agreement. Huawei is the technology partner for the Safe City surveillance network, whose financing Guinea ratified by law in June 2026. The terms on which the Safe City network and the July 2026 identity-register memorandum govern data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at September 2026.

The May 2026 decree on foreign digital services requires providers to appoint a local representative within 90 days. It also creates a platform to monitor foreign digital services used in Guinea.
