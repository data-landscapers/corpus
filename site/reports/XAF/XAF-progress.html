The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The year's continental movement was institutional rather than operational. Bodies were constituted, positions adopted and memoranda signed across artificial intelligence, connectivity and payments, and very little of it yet carries a delivery figure. The Africa AI Council was established, an Algiers declaration on telecommunications sovereignty was adopted, a continental payments rail passed 190 institutions in 28 countries, and the regional trade protocol on digital trade still has no ratification count. Where numbers exist they are mostly one side's own: an operator's ranking, a company's training pledge, a foundation's cohort call.

Two facts run underneath the rest. Official development assistance fell sharply in 2025 with a further fall expected, and the agency that said so told its board its model is shifting from delivering aid to mobilising investment — which bears on almost every programme in this report without naming one. And the continent's dependence on cloud, compute and capital sited elsewhere is now argued in public by the people building the alternatives, which is a different thing from being measured.

The institutional layer moved most. A council of 42 heads of state was established for artificial intelligence, a secretary general was elected at the telecommunications union, a continental sovereignty declaration was adopted for 2026-2030, and a parliaments project opened with a six-legislature cohort. The continental free trade area now counts 49 of 54 signatories as having deposited instruments of ratification.

What has not moved is the digital trade protocol's own domestication: state parties committed to expedite it under eight calls to action and the base still holds no ratification count. The regional registry remains under an appeal-court order and a receivership. A mandate is cheaper to adopt than to work, and the difference between the two is what this section cannot yet show.

The shared-infrastructure record is the one place where continental numbers exist, and they are uncomfortable. The trade secretariat's own estimate is that digital trade requires several times the number of data centres the continent has, and a facility here takes two to three times as long to reach the occupancy that finances it as one in Europe — so the gap is not only unbuilt capacity but the demand curve that would finance it.

Against that, an African operator's AI factory took a place in the top fifty of the global supercomputer list, which is a capability statement rather than a utilisation one. A connectivity declaration was adopted at Abuja, a harmonised broadband mapping project runs to 2028, and a continental infrastructure report reached its first country pilots as an open tender. The union commission meanwhile put its own privileged-access management out to bid, which is the smallest and most concrete item in the section.

Continental finance moved in two directions at once. Memoranda were signed with a search company on AI and cloud, with the telecommunications union across seven workstreams, and with an Indian technology company over five years; a development financier's African portfolio was shown to sit overwhelmingly at Series B or later, which is a later-stage position than a venture label implies.

Underneath, the money is contracting. Official development assistance fell in 2025 with more expected, the union's own budget is still only about a quarter member-financed a decade after the decision that was meant to change it, and the European programme's mid-term review cut its digital transformation component by roughly three quarters. A year of signatures against a falling base is what this section records.

What the base cannot state at continental level is mostly the effect of what it can. There is no ratification count for the digital trade protocol, no budget for the union's own digital programme, no continental measure of workload concentration on foreign cloud, and no delivery figure against any of the training pledges made this year.

The pattern is consistent enough to name: continental instruments are adopted and announced in public, and their implementation is reported nationally or not at all.
