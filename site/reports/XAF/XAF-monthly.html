African Continental Health Data Governance Framework

Africa CDC calls for stronger health data governance across Africa — Tech Review Africa, 1 October 2026

Africa CDC called on member states to strengthen national health data stewardship and adopt interoperable digital standards, at a consultative session at the Africa HealthTech Summit in Kigali.

Africa CDC official: Continent lacks infrastructure to run its own health AI — The New Times, 3 October 2026 Rwanda

The head of digital health at the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention said most health AI models presented to her are hosted on a foreign cloud, because the continent lacks the infrastructure to run them. The agency is drafting a continental health data governance framework with more than 40 member states, setting who may access health data, for what purpose and for how long.

Foundations First: Transform Health at the Africa HealthTech Summit 2026 — Transform Health, 13 October 2026

More than 100 stakeholders from 70 organisations, including health ministries from 11 countries, commented on the draft African Continental Health Data Governance Framework at a consultation in Kigali. AU member states are to validate it in Addis Ababa on 21-22 October, ahead of proposed adoption at the AU Summit in early 2027.

AU Member States Validate African Continental Health Data Governance Framework — Africa CDC, 22 October 2026

AU member states validated the African Continental Health Data Governance Framework in Addis Ababa on 22 October 2026, the first common continental approach to governing health data, proposing a federated model for sharing across borders. Africa CDC will finalise it with the agreed amendments and take it through AU policy processes, with consideration expected in 2027.

Yaoundé Declaration on E-Governance and Artificial Intelligence in Africa

The Yaoundé Declaration, adopted on 17 October 2026 at the close of the E-Gov'A conference, is a non-binding statement by governments, institutions and companies under a "Local First, African First" approach to e-government and AI. It sets an aim of hosting all critical or sovereign public data locally by 2030 and creates an Observatory of e-Government and AI in Africa.

Yaoundé Declaration Targets 100% Local Hosting of Critical Government Data by 2030 — Business in Cameroon, 23 October 2026 Cameroon

The Yaoundé Declaration on E-Governance and Artificial Intelligence in Africa, adopted when the E-Gov'A conference closed on 17 October 2026, asks African governments to host all classified critical or sovereign public data inside their own borders by 2030, and where domestic capacity is lacking to prefer another African country over providers off the continent. The organisers, Smart Click Africa and Digital Business Africa, are to run an African Observatory for E-Government and AI; the hosting target is a recommendation and binds no government.

PwC's second edition of its guide to VAT on digital services in Africa counts 32 of 54 countries that now tax electronic services sold by non-resident suppliers, against 21 in the 2024 edition. It sets out the rules country by country for 28 jurisdictions where the firm operates.

Space in Africa released its African Satellite Manufacturing and Launch Market Report 2026 on 2 November.
