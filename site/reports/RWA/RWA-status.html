Status report

This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech update rates Rwanda's digital government strategy as in place but due for updating. It records the whole-of-government transformation programme as planned or in draft. The national data strategy is in progress, not adopted, and its governance documents are unpublished (2025). The data governance function itself is established, run by an autonomous institution for government as a whole.

The Ministry of ICT and Innovation published the National Artificial Intelligence Policy in April 2023, with RURA and external partners. It carries fourteen recommendations and an implementation plan phased from FY23/24 to FY27/28. Its six priority areas are skills and AI literacy, compute, a data strategy, public-sector adoption, private-sector adoption and ethical guidelines. It recommends a Responsible AI Office and an annual AI Readiness Index, neither of them a standing body or published instrument. Cabinet approved a National AI Agency on 8 June 2026. In October 2026 the ICT minister of state said the agency is being brought into operation through 2026. He said the digital and AI strategies are to be brought together under one coordinated programme. No text or timetable for that programme has been found.

The 2022 broadband policy is technology-neutral. It spans last-mile connectivity, spectrum and skills. It dropped the single-wholesale-network model, let operators refarm spectrum and allocated more spectrum in the 700, 900, 1800 and 2100 MHz bands. A working group of MINICT, RURA, the operators, government institutions and the financial sector runs the legacy-network transition (July 2026). Cabinet approved a National Data Sharing Policy in May 2025, and a national digital public infrastructure strategy was launched in March 2026.

Rwanda scores 58.7 of 100 for Overall Governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 14th of 54 African states. The score rose 0.8 points over the decade, was flat over the last five years, and fell three places in rank (2023).

Legislation and regulation

The law on virtual assets was gazetted in May 2026, the country's first dedicated legislation for virtual-asset businesses. It makes the Capital Market Authority their licensing and supervisory authority. The Chamber of Deputies had approved the rationale of the draft in April 2026. The Single Digital Identification Law makes the digital credential the only government identity a citizen may hold. The cards issued since 1962 are void from June 2027.

The ICT Law of 2016 remains the sector's governing statute, covering cybersecurity and universal access. The 2018 cybercrimes law covers digital signatures alongside criminal offences. On 5 June 2025 the Supreme Court upheld its offence of publishing rumours online against a constitutional challenge. RURA sets much of the detail by regulation: e-waste in 2018, radio frequency management in 2019 and SIM card registration in 2024. The SIM regulation binds every SIM to a biometrically verified national ID. Population registration runs on Law N° 029/2023. Payments run on Law N° 61/2021, which lets the central bank own and operate the systems it regulates.

Presidential Order n° 025/01 of 12 May 2023 repealed the 2004 order behind the Universal Access Fund. It widens the contributing base beyond telephony to network, application, content, broadcasting and postal services. It caps annual contributions at 2.5% of turnover for each category of regulated service (2023). RURA's Regulatory Board sets the rate actually paid and fixes collection and sanctions. The Fund has no legal personality: RURA staffs it, and RURA's Director General manages its money. Its annual account of the projects it financed goes to the Regulatory Board each 30 September.

The rules on whether public bodies share their data sit in the National Data Sharing Policy of 2025, a policy instrument and not a statute. The data protection law has been fully enforceable since its grace period expired in October 2023.

Data protection

The World Bank recorded in 2025 that neither Rwanda's data protection regime nor its authority publishes any account of how it operates. It rates the law in force and effective, with compliance monitored. The supervisory authority sits inside the National Cyber Security Authority under Law N° 058/2021. It oversees the law, registers data controllers and processors, and can impose administrative sanctions. It declared 2026 a year of accountability, in which registered controllers must prove compliance through documented policies and audits. No formal investigation, fine or sanction had been disclosed in the one to two years to April 2026, and no complaint volumes are published.

Under the 2021 law a processor must tell the controller of a personal data breach within 48 hours. The controller must notify the Data Protection and Privacy Office in the same window, with a full written report to the authority within 72 hours. Affected people must be told where the breach poses a high risk to their rights. Personal data must be stored in Rwanda unless the authority authorises otherwise. The authority published standard contractual clauses for transfers abroad in February 2024, and an application form asking for the contract, an impact assessment and a data-flow diagram. It keeps no list of adequate countries and assesses each transfer case by case (April 2026). The Act reaches any global platform processing Rwandans' personal data, wherever it is established. Payments have no separate privacy statute and fall under the general regime.

UNDP found in 2026 that no standing national body assesses AI risks or monitors AI errors and harms. In July 2026 the EAC's regional rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection still had to pass the Community's decision-making organs.

Rwanda's Digital Freedom score fell 6.9 points over 2014-2023 to 37.7 of 100, 43rd of 54 African states. A restriction on social media use by under-16s was in preparation in August 2026. No text of it has been found.

Regional collaboration

Kenya and Uganda have taken up Rwanda's Electronic Billing Machine, Biometric Update reported in July 2026. Guinea has adopted the Telemo procurement platform and is co-developing it with Rwanda. No revenue, contract value or licensing terms have been disclosed for any of these exports.

Rwanda and Kenya held talks in May 2026 on AI, data centres, digital infrastructure and talent. Rwanda and Egypt signed a memorandum on AI and digital transformation at WSIS in July 2026. On 20 July 2026 the information society authority and Guinea's digital-economy ministry named AI, e-health and e-commerce as areas to explore. In March 2026 the central banks of Kenya and Rwanda signed a memorandum on licence passporting for payment service providers. A provider licensed in one may operate in the other on a letter of no objection from its home regulator.

The national ID has been a recognised travel document for Kenya and Uganda since January 2014, for stays of up to six months. The EAC's regional rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection remained unadopted in July 2026. In July 2026 the EAC's central bank governors called for regulatory harmonisation on AI and digital finance. Smart Africa was preparing an Internet Governance Blueprint for its September 2026 steering committee. It has proposed a Council of African Internet Governance Agencies. In August 2026 Ugandan officials said the fibre link between the two countries at Mirama Hills was built but not yet switched on. The EAC's central bankers agreed to build a peer review mechanism for macroeconomic surveillance under the Community's 2026/27–2030/31 strategy.

Rwanda ranks first of 54 African states on regional integration in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, at 75.1 of 100 after a 5.8-point decade gain. On 21 September 2026 Rwanda was selected to host the seventh UN World Data Forum, expected in late 2028.

Standards

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech update records no government interoperability framework in force in Rwanda. It records no operational standard over data quality or system uptime, and no programme retiring legacy systems to a standard. The e-procurement system is in use, interfaces with other government systems and holds purchases to technical standards (2025).

RURA spent Frw 500,000,000 in 2020/21 on equipment to measure the quality of 4G LTE and internet services. Its published technical rules include radio frequency management, from 2019, and e-waste, from 2018.

The World Bank recorded no open source policy for government in 2025. More than 300 public websites ran on one open-source content platform built by Rwandan developers in 2024. The domestic payment rail is built open-source on Mojaloop.

In October 2026 the Rwanda Standards Board, the country's ISO member body, participated in 28 ISO committees and observed 12. It is a participating member of the subcommittee on IT service management and governance. It observes the joint information technology committee and its security subcommittee. The EAC's Secure Data Sharing Framework was validated at a workshop in June 2026. No adoption of the rules that would follow it has been found.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Rwanda scored 0.0 of 100 on freedom of association and assembly in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, the floor of its range (2023). Its Participation sub-category fell 5.2 points over the decade to 30.3, 38th of 54. Civil society space fell 11.8 points to 29.5. Rights scored 30.0, 42nd of 54, down 4.0 points over the decade. Media freedom scored 30.5, 50th, and freedom of expression and belief 32.2, 45th. Digital freedom fell with them.

The World Bank recorded in 2025 a right-to-information regime in force and rated effective, under which the public can obtain public records. Rwanda's public records measures are among the continent's strongest and rising (2023). The government operates participation platforms, including a separate petitions portal, and citizen feedback channels (2025). The 2023 national AI policy drew on eight workshops and eight stakeholder surveys.

In 2025 the World Bank found citizens involved in designing services on the main service portal and the tax portal. They are not involved on the social protection portal or the public jobs portal. Documentation and assessments across the digital government estate are held internally and not published. This covers the service portal, e-procurement, data governance, the participation platforms and the right-to-information regime itself.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Rwanda's own money carries 45.6 per cent of the digital lines in the 2026/27 finance law, RWF 92.1 billion of RWF 201.7 billion, against 50.0 per cent of RWF 176.0 billion in 2025/26 and 47.2 per cent of RWF 145.5 billion in 2024/25.

The loan line for digital identification and authentication doubled to RWF 26.6 billion in the 2026/27 finance law, from RWF 13.3 billion in 2025/26. The World Bank credit carrying Single Digital ID enrolment, software and hardware was due to close in December 2026. Its envelope had been cut by EUR 18.4 million in June 2024, with initial issuance targeted at 15 per cent of the population. In 2026 Parliament ratified EUR 17m of additional financing for digital identity, public data management and cybersecurity, and moved the deadline to 30 June 2028. The old identity cards are void from June 2027.

In 2024/25 the ICT-for-development programme spent RWF 6.42 billion, national identification RWF 3.05 billion and cybersecurity standards and skills RWF 106 million. The 2024/25 finance law had appropriated RWF 8.72 billion, RWF 2.17 billion and RWF 921 million to them from domestic funds.

A 2023 presidential order levies the Universal Access Fund on regulated services. Irembo earns a commission on each paid transaction under a 25-year agreement, in place of a government fee. Eswatini, Chad, Guinea and Kenya were paying to adopt Rwandan-built government platforms in July 2025.

MoUs and other agreements

In March 2026 the National Bank of Rwanda and the Central Bank of Kenya signed a memorandum on licence passporting for payment service providers. A joint committee oversees it. In May 2026 Broadband Systems Corporation, the state broadband operator, signed with Oman Data Park to invest jointly in an AI-ready Tier III data centre. Omantel's Otech signed a second data-centre memorandum with BSC in August 2026. Rwanda and Egypt signed a memorandum on AI and digital transformation at WSIS in July 2026.

In September 2026 the ICT ministry signed a memorandum with the chip-design company ChipMango to build local semiconductor design skills. Rwanda and Jordan signed a digital transformation agreement at the UN General Assembly on 23 September 2026. A joint technical committee is to meet quarterly. On 3 November 2026 RES Synesis announced an exploratory memorandum with the ICT ministry on systems modernisation, AI, cybersecurity and training. Any activity under it needs a separate implementation agreement, and no sum is stated. No sum has been found attached to any of these instruments.

New investments

On 20 August 2026 the Chamber of Deputies approved two financing agreements worth a combined RWF 279 billion. They fund broadband expansion and a labour-market information system among other measures. On 18 September 2026 Cabinet approved a bill to ratify an IDA loan signed on 11 July for a digital-adoption project, with no amount stated.

In 2022 the Mastercard Foundation committed $275.7 million to a partnership with Carnegie Mellon University and the government, running to 2035. Of it, $175 million endows CMU-Africa in Kigali and $100.7 million funds a centre for inclusive digital transformation (2022).

The World Bank approved $200 million for the Rwanda Digital Acceleration Project in November 2021. AIIB is lending a further $100 million (2022). The World Bank's mission put disbursement at 49.73% on 13 March 2026, up from 40% in November 2025. In 2026 Parliament ratified EUR 17m of additional financing and moved the project deadline to 30 June 2028. Rwanda is not a borrower under the World Bank's $162 million Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project (May 2023).

AFD made its first sovereign digital-transformation loan to Rwanda in 2023, a concessional EUR 37,000,000 for e-government including Irembo. KOICA grants paid for the Revenue Authority's electronic billing system in 2018, for $6 million, and ICT capacity at the Standards Board in 2024, for $8 million. KfW funds an ICT Facility of EUR 12,500,000 from 2021 and a second phase of EUR 10,010,000 from 2022. JICA signed a JPY 2,009,000,000 grant in October 2023 for traffic control at twenty Kigali intersections.

In all, 180 distinct commitments are on the record, starting between 2015 and 2025, with funding windows running to 2035 (November 2026). Twenty-one are active, 12 have closed, two are approved and 145 carry no recorded status. Most are grants, 169 of them, alongside seven loans, two technical assistance projects, one guarantee and one of unrecorded type. Counted by number rather than by value, the most frequent financiers are Korea's KOICA with 67 commitments and USAID with 35, ahead of the Gates Foundation with nine and the Mastercard Foundation with seven.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In July 2026 MINICT set 30 June 2027 as the date for a nationwide 3G shutdown. The sequence runs from pilot shutdowns in 2026, with 4G expansion and VoLTE checks, to migrating critical systems in the first half of 2027. Any extension is temporary and needs the regulator's approval. December 2028 for 2G is a target the ministry is working towards, conditional on the 3G shutdown and the handsets still in use. MINICT says running four generations "takes up valuable radio spectrum, increases network maintenance costs and limits investment in newer technologies".

4G covered 99% of the population in 2022, and 34.4% of people used the internet in 2024. Unique mobile subscriber penetration was 39% in 2023, against the 86.2% SIM-connections figure usually quoted. Smartphone penetration was 22% in 2023, below the East African average of 32%. A mobile broadband basket cost 3.0% of gross national income per head in 2022. Mobile communications scored 79.4 of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 12th of 54, and internet and computers 27.2, 33rd.

Rwanda had about 20 kbit/s of international bandwidth per internet user in 2022. Its exchange point RINEX, whose members include MTN and Liquid, averaged 1.53 Gbps inbound and 1.97 Gbps outbound in 2020/21. A 2,000 km protected fibre route from Goma to Mombasa through Kigali, Kampala and Nairobi was carrying wholesale traffic in September 2026. An earlier terrestrial route from Mombasa to Johannesburg through Rwanda launched in May 2023, and Google's Umoja route to Australia runs over it. Uganda, Kenya, Rwanda and South Sudan opened a joint account in August 2026 to fund a study for a regional communications satellite.

The 2022 broadband policy abandoned the single-wholesale-network model, and RURA ended KT Corporation's 4G monopoly in 2023. KT sought a US$152m exit from the joint venture in March 2026. On 20 October 2026 the government filed a US$386m counterclaim at the Permanent Court of Arbitration. Neither claim has been decided. MTN's data subscribers grew 14.1% to 2.8 million (March 2026), and it began carrying voice over 4G in August 2026.

Data Storage

The World Bank recorded in 2025 that Rwanda runs a government cloud and does not publish the arrangements behind it. The platform is in use across central and local government, as a hybrid offering infrastructure as a service. It sits beside a national data centre and a cloud-first strategy with a national public cloud (2024). Government data must be kept inside the country (2025), as the data protection law requires of personal data. Firms report grid reliability as a constraint (2023).

Rwanda was East Africa's smallest colocation market in 2024, with about four live commercial data centres. Google's first Africa cloud region opened in South Africa in January 2024. An October 2025 digital FDI roadmap targets cloud and data infrastructure. In May 2026 the state broadband operator BSC and Oman Data Park agreed to invest jointly in a data centre for cloud and AI workloads. ICT ministers and RISA's chief executive witnessed the signing. A second memorandum, with Omantel's Otech, followed in August 2026. No site, capacity, cost or date has been published for either.

In March 2026 the New Times argued that Rwanda needs a water policy for data centres before it scales them. The acceleration project tendered IT equipment for a government disaster recovery site in October 2025. No location or award has been found.

Energy

Access to energy rose 18.9 points over 2014-2023 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, yet scores only 27.4 of 100, 34th of 54. It is among Rwanda's ten most improved and ten worst-scoring measures. The utility reported household access of 84.6% in July 2025, 59.6% on the grid and 25.0% off-grid. Access was around 6% in 2008, and the target is universal access by 2029. Urban access stood at 99.9% in 2023, against 57.9% in rural areas.

Firms reported about 1.8 outages a month in 2023, about 22 a year. From 1 October 2025 regulated residential tariffs charge RWF 89 per kWh for the first 20 kWh a month. They charge RWF 310 between 20 and 50 kWh, and RWF 369 above that. About half of generation and capacity was renewable in 2023/24.

In March 2026 electricity supply, affordability and maintenance were named as the obstacles to connecting the remaining schools. Infrastructure is Rwanda's weakest of the four foundations for economic opportunity in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, at 40.0 of 100 and 25th of 54. It barely moved over the decade.

Technical Capacity

The finance ministry completed an upgrade of the integrated financial management information system in October 2026. The upgrade replaced legacy components that had reached the end of their support. The same blueprint is to be applied to the schools data system and the SACCO core banking system.

Rwanda had around 1,000 active GitHub users in 2024. Its public administration ranks first of 54 African states in the 2024 Ibrahim Index.

In 2024 the information society authority's own software division maintained and upgraded four central government systems free of charge. Irembo is built and maintained by an insourced local engineering team. The national data-sharing platform pairs an external firm with internal experts, so that knowledge stays with the state.

The World Bank recorded in 2025 a digital skills strategy for the public service and a free training programme delivering it. It covers advanced skills as well as basic literacy. A study of one rural district found some case documentation still taken on paper before digital entry. It names ICT literacy gaps and limited connectivity and computer access as the cause. A UNDP regional review of September 2026 reports country-hosted AI compute deployed under its programme. It identifies 42 priority applications across eight sectors and gives no capacity or host institution.

Cybersecurity

Rwanda sits in the top band of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, the tier it reserves for role-modelling states. The cyber security authority has published a National Cybersecurity Strategy for 2024-2029. Its first pillar is cyber resilience and the protection of critical information infrastructure. The earlier instrument on record is the National Cyber Security Strategic Plan of 2015. The National Cyber Security Index of March 2026 gives full marks on critical information infrastructure, personal data and the fight against cybercrime. It gives none on cybersecurity research. The binding law is newer: the Law on Prevention and Punishment of Cybercrimes of 2018 is in force and also covers digital signatures. Breaches of personal data must be notified on short statutory deadlines.

Under the data protection law of 2021, the National Cyber Security Authority is also the data protection supervisory authority. It registers controllers and processors and imposes sanctions. The agency charged with defending the state's estate is the same one that polices how the state handles people's data. Its reach extends to the networks, with SIM registrations verified against the national biometric database. It extends to the funding instruments too: protection of Rwandan cyberspace is an eligible use of the Universal Access Fund.

The National Cyber Security Authority opened a Cyber Security Centre of Excellence, CyberHub, at the University of Rwanda's College of Science and Technology in October 2025. It combines a cyber academy, an innovation hub and technical labs, with partners including Cisco and GIZ. In October 2026 MTN Rwanda agreed by memorandum with the authority to support it with cybersecurity and data-protection skills training. Cabinet approved a post-secondary institute in AI and cybersecurity in July 2026. The prime minister said reported fraud complaints over digital payments were about 30% lower in the second quarter of 2026 than the 4,128 of a year earlier.

The central bank reported on 8 October 2026 that fraud cases fell to 2,879 in the second quarter of 2026 from 4,128 a year earlier. Only 3.6% of the Rwf471 million involved had been recovered (2026).

DPI

Data Exchange

In October 2026 the information society authority described the Data Sharing Platform as an interim system while a shared government data hub moves into implementation. The platform envisaged by the National Data Sharing Policy was still being procured and developed in 2025. Cross-government integration runs meanwhile over RISA's Government Enterprise Service Bus and bilateral system-to-system links.

In 2023 sixteen government systems interoperated through the bus, among them the identification agency, the revenue authority, the central bank and the social security board. The financial management, customs, tax, HR, social insurance, debt and procurement systems exchange data through direct interfaces and a Government Service Bus (2025). In 2024 the identification agency and the electoral commission confirmed that the voter register updates automatically as citizens turn 18.

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech update records no government interoperability framework and no operational standard over data quality or uptime. It records nothing published on the data-sovereignty terms of government data exchange.

Cabinet approved the National Data Sharing Policy on 26 May 2025. It treats government data as open by default and institutions as its stewards. It adopts the Five Safes framework for controlled access. Implementation is phased to 2029, with the data-sharing platform project running from 2025 to 2027. The statistics institute's Data Governance Unit owns standards, and RISA runs the platform.

A Centre of Digital Public Infrastructure was established in 2025, and a national DPI strategy launched on 9 March 2026. The government's stated reason is that its platforms were built independently and were not designed to communicate.

Digital Identity and CRVS

On 4 October 2026 the identification agency reported about 13.7 million people biometrically registered for the Single Digital Identity, with the system 76% built. Nationwide enrolment opened on 7 August 2025, with biometric capture from mid-September. The country is preparing a full transition to digital identity by 2027. The legacy plastic cards are to be phased out by June 2027. The agency suspended the photography exercise for the card, naming no resumption date, while pre-enrolment continues online (2026). In October 2026 the World Bank approved an additional €17 million for the digital acceleration project, much of it for the digital identity infrastructure.

In March 2026 the credential was described in three parts: a Single Digital Identification Number for life, a transaction-specific token derived from it, and a card with a QR code readable only after authentication. It is built on the civil registration system, linking parent–child family trees, and collects a deliberately small biographic set. The 2025 Auditor-General's report found the legacy system issued a fresh 16-digit number on every correction or replacement. One person could so hold several numbers, breaking banking, land, SIM, voting and pension records that assume one each.

In 2023 the population register underneath it was a centralised electronic database covering about 98% of the population. Law No. 14/2008 makes the card obligatory from age 16 and the legal proof of identity. A bank account, community-based health insurance, public healthcare and social protection registration all required it in 2020. Every SIM registration is verified biometrically against the agency's records.

In 2021 no identity-specific judicial oversight existed, and grievances ran through administrative review before the agency, then judicial review. Institutional access to the database rested on memoranda of understanding, not on binding legislation. Over 50 ministries, departments and agencies had an API integration with the national ID database in 2024. In October 2026 Rwanda, Ghana and Benin announced that they had connected their identity systems through the Smart Africa Data Exchange and verified identities end to end on synthetic data. An operational border pilot awaits national approvals.

Digital Payments and Fintech

All domestic interoperable retail payments moved onto eKash, the national instant payment rail, on 14 July 2026 under a central bank directive. eKash launched on 27 February 2025, built open-source on Mojaloop by RSwitch with the Rwandan firm Wiredin. It carries instant transfers between any bank or e-money provider, and merchant payments over existing USSD, app and internet-banking channels, up to RWF 10 million (2026).

By September 2026 eKash had carried more than 10.5 million transactions worth over RWF 960 billion, at a 98.6 per cent success rate. The central bank acknowledged that users still report delays and failed transfers. It put active accounts on the national switch at 10.3 million in September 2026, against 2.03 million a year earlier. The RWF 20 fee cap binds only off-net transfers between institutions, and on-net tariffs are outside it (August 2026).

In October 2026 the central bank governor said a pilot linking eKash to Tanzania's instant payment system was under way. Payments within the EAC go through the regional EAC payment system, and those beyond it through PAPSS. In October 2026 the Bank of Ghana said it is testing payment interoperability with Rwanda, including stablecoins and mobile wallets.

Law N 61/2021 empowers the central bank to establish, own and operate clearing and settlement systems. The bank runs the real-time gross settlement system and settles eKash positions through it once daily (2023). eKash was the only instant payment system in Africa publishing its scheme rules in 2023. The law requires operators to appoint an accredited external auditor. No audit findings specific to eKash have been published. A central bank directive of 8 December 2025 sets binding rules for digital credit, remote account opening and digital savings.

In September 2026 the central bank opened a campaign against cash, on data showing 85.3% of Rwandans with a digital account and 92% mainly paying in cash. A digital agricultural lending platform is in a six-month pilot with four lenders, the governor said in October 2026. Vision Umurenge cash transfers began moving to mobile money in three districts in August 2025.

Registries

The Imibereho social registry, live since early 2024, had handled 360,000 classification appeals by August 2026 and corrected 93% of them. It covered 13.8 million people in 2025. It classifies households by checking self-reported income against social security and tax records. It is deduplicated against national ID data and integrated with more than ten government systems (2025). Its main source of error is stale asset records: households still scored as owners of land or vehicles they sold.

Tenure regularisation demarcated 10.4 million parcels, a 2026 UN case study records. It issued 8.8 million titles and put every land record in a digital registry under the e-Title system (2026). The Land Administration Information System connects to more than 14 other government systems (2026). A Kayonza correction drive issued more than 28,000 titles to 17 June 2026, leaving about 4,700 untitled over disputes, mortgages and wetland wrongly registered.

Only 36,706 of an estimated 278,060 enterprises were formally registered in 2024, 13.2%. Registration is fully online and free, with a certificate within six working hours, and a Taxpayer Identification Number is issued in the process. A 2023 amendment to the companies law set up a central register of beneficial owners, kept and verified by the Registrar General. Individual taxpayers' records are tied to the national identity system.

Birth registration completeness reached 92.9% in 2025, from 90.3% in 2024. Civil registration is digital at every level, from health facilities and cells to districts and embassies (2024). Registration generates a national identification number linked to the population register. Since spring 2026 village leaders notify community deaths from a basic phone with the deceased's ID number, about 10,000 in the first months. Community deaths were first notified on paper in 2023. The voter register is an electronic extract of the population database, every citizen entered on turning 18 (2024).

Sectoral management information systems

The World Bank recorded in 2025 a custom-built financial management system covering central and local government. The revenue authority runs the e-Tax platform, Electronic Billing Machines reporting sales in real time, and API links to banks and mobile-money operators. A senior official has said data fragmented across systems still limits full interoperability. A treasury single account is used by all ministries, settling through the real-time gross settlement system and a clearing house. Customs and tax run on hybrids of custom and commercial software, and public debt and investment on two custom systems. The HR and payroll systems are custom-built and use the national identity number. Social insurance runs on a hybrid platform.

The health information system has run on DHIS2 since 2012, with completeness rising from 88% to 95% and timeliness from 60% to over 90%. A National Health Intelligence Centre has been established. A 2024 assessment of 215 health centres found a minority with any electronic medical record. By April 2026 the eBuzima record was in the majority of public health facilities. In September 2026 Irembo's operator described a patient-facing health platform. No launch date or user figure has been published.

The School Data Management System covers pre-primary to technical education (2023/24). A digital attendance system to flag pupils at risk of dropping out was introduced in December 2025. The justice sector began using an upgraded Integrated Electronic Case Management System in January 2025. The Chief Justice named technology a major focus of the 2026/27 judicial year, opened on 4 September 2026. The health ministry's 2023/24 statistical booklet was compiled from the health information system.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

IremboGov carried more than 600 government services in July 2026, more than 240 of them paid. The paid services generate the commission that funds the platform. Citizens can pay through it by mobile money, bank card or bank transfer.

Irembo launched in 2015 as a single-window platform from a partnership between a private company and the government. It runs under a 25-year agreement, funded by a commission on each paid transaction. About 4,000 digital ambassadors transact for citizens who cannot use it themselves. Irembo won Best Government Service in the World at the GovTech Prize in February 2026. Civil-status certificates issued through it rose from 683,560 in 2020 to 1,977,628 in 2024.

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech update rates the national service portal transactional. Services can be completed online end to end, and a business can be registered through it. The e-procurement system covers open tendering and contract management and enforces technical standards. The 2022 procurement law makes e-procurement the default; offline award needs the procurement authority's authorisation. E-passports are issued through the same platform with biometric capture. The HR platform serves ID-verified employee records to partner systems through an API.

Rwanda ranks first of 54 for public administration in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, at 77.2 of 100. It sits in the top group of the 2025 GovTech Maturity Index with Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Egypt. It scored 0.5799 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, in the High band. Documentation across most of the estate is held internally and not published (2025).

The 13th Rwanda Governance Scorecard of October 2026 scored digitally delivered services at 60.98%, against 77.83% for services overall. Irembo scored 81.80%, down 5.60 points, and electronic court filing 65.40%, down 13.60 (October 2026). Only 11% of government services were fully digitised from start to finish, the scorecard's lowest variable (October 2026).

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In June 2026 biometric kits were to be deployed permanently to sector offices, so that ID registration continues after the national campaign. Civil registration is decentralised to cell and sector level and has been kept only in electronic form since February 2020. The cell executive secretary registers community events on information from the village head (2023). The financial management system covers local as well as central government (2025).

In September 2026 the New Times reported 44 per cent of 522 public services decentralised. The local government ministry was said to be digitalising local services and reviewing the capacity of the cell. In October 2025 the government announced Mbaza, a platform for citizens to raise and track issues with local institutions. It integrates with Irembo and the civil registration system and replaces the older e-Citizen platform. The local government and ICT ministries trial-launched Mbaza in January 2026.

KfW supports the decentralisation agency's monitoring and information system for the lower tiers of administration to 2028. French lending in January 2023 covered local alongside central e-government systems.

In 2023 the revenue authority described its local government tax system, in service since 2015, as linked to the land registry by parcel identifier. The system is integrated with the identity agency and banks, and property tax is about 30% of local revenue (2023).

Rural digital data capture

A solar-powered connectivity and power unit for health posts, launched on 1 October 2026, had reached 65 health posts in 21 districts. It is deployed with the health ministry and a vaccine alliance, with 85 more posts planned.

In 2023 community births and deaths were registered by the cell executive secretary on information from the village head. Facility births and deaths are captured at the point of service. A study in rural Nyaruguru district found the electronic case management system improving access to justice. It named ICT literacy and infrastructure limits as the challenges.

The health ministry's replacement for paper registers at health centres was short of national coverage in April 2025. By April 2026 the eBuzima record was in the majority of public health facilities. Nationwide tenure regularisation has put the rural parcel base into one register (2026), and correction drives are reworking its boundary errors district by district.

Rural electricity access was 57.9% in 2023, against 99.9% in urban areas. In March 2026 power, affordability and maintenance were named as the obstacles to connecting the remaining schools.

In 2020/21 the Universal Access and Service Fund subsidised bandwidth at 193 rural and remote institution sites, at Frw 100,000,000 a year. The same year it paid Frw 96,000,000 for two towers in Nyungwe National Park, at the request of local authorities.

Rwanda's rural economy scored 77.8 of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, fourth of 54 (2023). Rural land and water access ranked first at 93.1, and rural economy support second at 90.1. Rural market access fell 15.5 points over the decade to 59.8, its second most deteriorated measure. Rural representation and participation also fell, to 68.1.

Technology

AI

In February 2026 Rwanda signed a three-year, non-binding memorandum placing Anthropic's Claude across its health ministry, public agencies and education. It supplies Claude, Claude Code and API credits to government developers. A Claude-based learning companion built with ALX reaches students in Rwanda and other African countries (March 2026).

Cabinet approved a national AI agency on 8 June 2026. In October 2026 the ICT minister of state said it is being brought into operation through 2026, with a technical unit called the AI Factory. UNESCO's assessment puts the AI policy's cost at USD 76.5 million over 2023–2028, with USD 1.2 million mobilised. It records the recommended implementation office as not yet operational. The policy covers AI and machine learning alongside e-government services, e-commerce and digital market regulation. The central bank's AI Guidelines 52/2026 took effect on 4 June 2026, with six months to comply. They require financial institutions to inventory their AI systems and assess their impact and fairness.

In January 2026 the Gates Foundation and OpenAI funded a programme applying AI to primary healthcare, beginning in Rwanda. The statistics institute built a model coding labour force survey responses in three languages, 96 per cent automatically in one test (October 2026). In November 2025 the infrastructure ministry released an AI agent checking building designs against code and zoning rules. Multilingual AI agents for citizen complaints were announced in February 2026. MTN Rwanda and Viamo launched Miss Baza in October 2026, a voice assistant reached by dialling 845 on ordinary phones. It answers in Kinyarwanda, English, French and Kiswahili.

An open Kinyarwanda voice dataset of over 2,400 hours sat on Mozilla's Common Voice in 2024. A compute-grant foundation launched in Kigali in May 2026 had made 42 grants in 14 countries by September. Its October 2026 memorandum with the ICT ministry commits no public capital.

ICT Industry

MTN Rwanda returned to profit in the first quarter of 2026, with Rwf 10.8bn (about $6.8m) after tax. Service revenue rose 14.7% and the EBITDA margin was 35.8%, carried by data and mobile money (2026). ICT-service exports ran at only a few dollars per head in 2025.

Rwanda scored 85.7 of 100 on business and competition regulation in 2023, third of 54 and up 3.7 points over the decade. A business can be formed and registered online through the government portal (2025).

Rwanda had around 1,000 active developers on GitHub in 2024. Rwandan startups raised about USD 26 million in equity venture funding in 2024. Germany's development ministry funds a project pairing the German IT-SME federation with Rwanda's ICT Chamber to 2027, after an earlier partnership between the same two bodies. KOICA funds a project with the Standards Board putting ICT tools behind SME standards compliance to 2028.

Innovation ecosystem

Rwandan startups raised about USD 26 million in equity venture funding in 2024, among Africa's top ten markets. Rwanda ranked 104th of 133 economies in the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024, leading the low-income group.

The Rwanda FinTech Centre was inaugurated in March 2026, run by the ICT ministry with the ICT Chamber, the Kigali International Financial Centre and others. A FinTech Strategy 2024–2029 aims to diversify the sector beyond payments. A fintech regulatory sandbox held its second Demo Day in 2026. In May 2026 the ministry's Hanga Ventures Ignite programme showcased inclusion-focused startups, among them Kayko and Save Directly.

Kigali Innovation City, a 60-hectare technopark built as a public-private partnership, broke ground in September 2024. The EU funds Hanga Hubs in Muhanga, Nyagatare, Rubavu and Rusizi to 2026. JICA set up the 250STARTUPS incubation mechanism and three secondary-city hubs, and runs a follow-on project to 2026. The AIIB-financed acceleration project carries a digital innovation and entrepreneurship component.

The World Bank recorded in 2025 a public-sector innovation programme with an established institution behind it. eKash was developed domestically on an open-source architecture.

Capacity

Literacy

97.4% of schools held at least one computer in the 2023/24 school year, and the teacher-to-computer ratio had improved to 2:1. In 2023/24 just under two-thirds had internet connectivity, and only around a third reported actively using ICT in teaching and learning.

In 2023/24, 12.8% of Rwandans aged 15 and over could use a computer with confidence, up from 8.9% in 2016/17, with 27.5% in urban areas against 6.6% in rural.

Rwanda's human resources in education score reached 91.7 of 100 in 2023, 13th of 54 in Africa and up 7.3 points over the decade. The Education sub-category as a whole scored 59.1 in 2023, 18th of 54, and rose only 1.4 points across the same ten years. Enrolment, at 40.2, and completion, at 41.3, were its two weakest components (2023). Education quality fell 3.8 points over the decade to 50.5, and equality in education fell 6.2 points to 71.7 (2023).

School internet access divides sharply between rural and urban Rwanda (March 2026). The classrooms most dependent on digital delivery are furthest from it. The connectivity for unconnected schools written into the Digital Acceleration Project is the standing answer to it. An undated education ministry notice, first found in October 2026, reports the first phase of the Smart Education Project complete, with 1,500 schools connected to high-speed internet. It launches the DigiTruck, a solar-powered mobile classroom to visit all 30 districts over three years.

Primary schools, rural ones included, report through the School Data Management System. The system covers pre-primary, primary, secondary and technical education. In December 2025 the education ministry introduced a digital attendance system to flag pupils at risk of dropping out. The teaching side has been built by donors. Korean grant funding through 2024 established ICT smart classrooms as centres of excellence in secondary schools. It also trained teachers in ICT integration and produced an ICT competency curriculum.

Training and skills

Cabinet approved a Rwanda Institute of Computing on 24 July 2026, an institute in AI and cybersecurity hosted by the University of Rwanda and due to open in September 2026. Its first two intakes are limited to graduates of the Rwanda Coding Academy. No report of its opening has been found as at November 2026.

A National Digital Talent Policy dates from 2017. The World Bank recorded in 2025 a public-service digital skills strategy and a training programme delivering it. AI courses were made compulsory for every public servant, with a 31 January 2026 deadline. Nearly 200,000 public servants had completed the flagship course by May 2026, on the provider's count. The 2023 AI policy puts skills and AI literacy first of its six priority areas. In March 2026 the ICT ministry ran AI training for government Chief Digital Officers, facilitated by Cenfri.

The cyber authority's CyberHub opened at the University of Rwanda's College of Science and Technology in October 2025. In October 2026 MTN Rwanda signed a memorandum supporting its cybersecurity and data-protection training, and Airtel Rwanda opened internships and placements for its students. GIZ runs a Digital Transformation Center in Kigali with the information society authority. The Mastercard Foundation's partnership with Carnegie Mellon, running to 2035, is the largest skills commitment in the sector. Tertiary ICT teaching rests on the University of Rwanda and its predecessors.

The Digital Ambassadors Programme has taught basic digital skills since 2017. Its Korean-funded final phase concluded in May 2026. No replacement of comparable reach has been found. In September 2026 a first cohort of 24 teachers completed training to teach cybersecurity in technical and vocational schools.

Research institutions

In 2022 the Mastercard Foundation committed $275.7 million to a partnership with Carnegie Mellon University and the government, running to 2035. It gives CMU-Africa in Kigali a $175 million endowment and the Upanzi Network, a centre for inclusive digital transformation, $100.7 million (2022). It targets 10,000 young Africans in digital skills and careers. The same campus holds a $6 million Gates Foundation grant for 2021–2026 on scaling the digital stack in Africa.

In 2022 the University of Rwanda and its predecessor institutions carried the national research base. Rwanda placed 104th of 133 economies in the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024, ahead of every other low-income country.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services scored 27.3 of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 41st of 54 (2023). Equal socioeconomic opportunity scored 20.4, 47th, down 10.2 points over the decade. Enrolment in Mutuelle de Santé, the community health insurance scheme, fell to 51% in August 2026 from 63% a year earlier.

In 2020 the ID card was required to open a bank account, join community health insurance and register for social protection. It was not issued free of charge. The digital ID covers residents, refugees, asylum seekers, migrants and stateless people. Enrolment reached the roughly 70,000 residents of Mahama refugee camp in June 2026. The immigration authority ran a registration drive for foreigners and refugees in Kigali on 7–15 February 2026.

In July 2026 MINICT published USSD codes letting any subscriber check whether a SIM is 4G-capable. eKash runs over existing USSD, app and internet-banking channels, with no new app needed. Field agents transact for citizens who cannot use the service platform (2026). More than nine in ten adults are financially included, the central bank's deputy governor said. Formal banking access remains one of the country's weakest measures (2023).

UNDP reported in 2026 that failed digital authentication can require in-person verification within limited service hours. The acceleration project funds device affordability, digital literacy and connectivity for unconnected schools, hospitals and markets. On 8 October 2026 the development board announced a planned Business Obstacles Alert platform for firms to report service obstacles.

Digital divides

In March 2026 about 27% of rural schools had internet against about 75% of urban ones, and 812 schools remained unconnected. 62% of schools were connected by late 2024, against a national target of 80%. Electricity, affordability and maintenance hold the remainder back.

The 2023/24 household survey records a persistent urban-rural digital divide, with Kigali far better connected. Rural electricity access was 57.9% in 2023, against 99.9% urban. Women are less likely than men to use mobile internet, by a margin small by regional standards (2025). Persons with disabilities are markedly less financially included, and their formal saving has fallen since 2020. A central bank campaign closed in September 2026 having brought 615,000 women into mobile financial services, against a target of 150,000.

In 2025 the networks reached effectively the whole country, while internet use and smartphone ownership sat far below. The 3G switch-off plan of July 2026 expects many people still on 2G handsets late in the decade.

Inclusion and Equality is Rwanda's worst-ranked measure in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, at 30.4 of 100, 44th of 54. In 2020/21 the Universal Access and Service Fund financed 68 passive-infrastructure sites at Frw 2,698,636,500. It identified 45 more, at Frw 1,158,668,676, that the operators' business case would not carry. A 2022 assessment found some users still depend on assisted access.

Data

National statistics

Rwanda's Capacity of the Statistical System score reached 74.3 of 100 in 2023, fourth of 54, up 5.5 points over the decade. On the World Bank's 2023 Statistical Performance Indicators it scores above 89% on data use. It scores 70–89% on data services, products and infrastructure, and 50–69% on data sources, its weakest pillar (2023). Population, agricultural and economic censuses were all run within the previous ten years, with three or more of each main type of survey. On 15 October 2026 the statistics institute's microdata catalogue held 75 datasets and 74,146 variables, the newest the 2025 labour force survey.

The 2025 vital statistics report put birth-registration completeness at 92.9%, against 90.3% a year earlier, on 356,838 births. The Imibereho social registry reaches close to the whole population (2025). Most enterprises counted in the 2024 business survey are not formally registered. In 2025 the Auditor-General found the legacy ID system let one person hold several identity numbers.

The 2024 IIAG profile carries no data for 14 of its 96 indicators on Rwanda. Rwanda scores above the continental average on 56 of them. The statistics institute leads the Data Governance Unit under the 2025 National Data Sharing Policy. In September 2026 a UN regional hub for big data and data science was hosted in Kigali with the institute, supporting African statistical offices.

Open data

The World Bank recorded in 2025 that Rwanda's open data portal carries only basic datasets, updated mostly by hand. Neither its holdings nor its licensing terms were published. The national data strategy is planned and not yet adopted. Since November 2024 the procurement authority's open contracting portal has published Umucyo contract data back to 2016. It uses the Open Contracting Data Standard, by download or API.

On the Open Data Inventory's 2024 assessment, population and vital statistics score 7.5 of 10 and national accounts 7.0. Pollution and health facilities score 4.5, and no category reaches eight.

Across the 23 categories the Open Data Inventory scores for Rwanda in 2024, the mean is 5.3 out of 10, or 5.6 setting aside the unscored communications and digital infrastructure category.

The right-to-information regime was in force and rated effective in 2025. Accessibility of public records scored 61.5 of 100 in 2023, third of 54, and disclosure 54.3, fifth. The data protection regime and its authority publish no reporting on how the regime operates (2025). No revenue, contract value or licensing terms have been disclosed for the platform exports (July 2026). eKash was the only instant payment system in Africa publishing its scheme rules in 2023. The central bank reports payment-system performance twice a year.

Geospatial data

In February 2026 the Rwanda Space Agency certified the state-linked Broadband Systems Corporation as a Starlink reseller. It runs a GeoHub applying satellite and geospatial data to agriculture and urban planning (2025). In January 2026 satellite and field data were combined into maps showing where each crop is grown. An AFD package agreed in January 2023 covered the GeoHub, the agency and a drone operations centre at Huye. The meteorological service takes EUMETSAT feeds but uses them mainly as static images for forecasters (July 2026).

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In February 2026 Rwanda signed a three-year, non-binding memorandum placing Anthropic's Claude across its health ministry, public agencies and education. Claude, Claude Code and API credits go to government developers. The region's first AWS Outposts deployment, by Sand Technologies, went into Rwanda in 2026.

In January 2026 the Gates Foundation and OpenAI funded Horizon1000, a programme applying AI tools to primary healthcare, with implementation beginning in Rwanda.

China activities

Bank of Kigali joined China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System as a direct participant on 8 September 2026, the first bank in Rwanda to do so. It gains renminbi clearing and settlement without intermediary banks.

China Eximbank extended a concessional loan to the government in 2022 for a Smart Education Project. An undated education ministry notice, first found in October 2026, reports its first phase complete, with Huawei as a partner. The first phase connected 1,500 schools to high-speed internet and built two data centres for a national education network.

EU activities

KfW funds the ICT Facility Rwanda, a EUR 12,500,000 grant for 2021–2030 on the efficiency of state action through ICT. A second phase of EUR 10,010,000 runs 2022–2030. A EUR 8,000,000 KfW grant for 2019–2028 builds a monitoring and information system for the lower tiers of administration through LODA. BMZ funds Digital4Rwanda, EUR 8,000,000 over 2024–2027, for women, rural people and others excluded from digital services. It pairs the German IT-SME federation BITMi with Rwanda's ICT Chamber over 2025–2027.

AFD made its first sovereign digital-transformation loan to Rwanda in 2023, for digital public services including Irembo and digital ID. The EU's Hanga Hubs grant of EUR 1,137,500 for 2021–2026 creates innovation hubs in four secondary cities. A EUR 2,000,000 EU programme for 2020–2030 works on youth skills and digital opportunities in four secondary cities. Over 2022–2027 Sweden channels support through Lantmäteriet, its land-registration authority, to Rwanda's National Land Authority.

Ten of the 32 financiers on Rwanda's record are European public bodies (November 2026), more than from any other region, and they hold 28 of its 180 commitments, about US$119 million.

Gulf/UAE activities

Rwanda launched its Digital FDI roadmap at the 9th Future Investment Initiative in Riyadh in October 2025. It did so alongside the Saudi-headquartered Digital Cooperation Organization, and the roadmap targets cloud and data-infrastructure investment.

In May 2026 Broadband Systems Corporation, the state broadband operator, signed a memorandum with Oman Data Park to invest jointly in an AI-ready Tier III data centre. ICT ministers and RISA's chief executive Antoine Sebera witnessed the signing. Omantel's Otech signed a second data-centre memorandum with BSC in August 2026. No site, capacity, cost, financing or date has been published for either.

India activities

In September 2026 senior digital officials joined a UNDP-hosted learning mission to India on its digital public infrastructure. No Indian-financed digital infrastructure or India Stack-derived system had been found operating in Rwanda as at August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

Rwanda's 2024–2029 ICT sector plan puts only 14.9 per cent of the government's 4,660 websites and applications hosted in the country (2025). That is despite a 50 per cent cut in hosting prices at the National Data Centre (2025). Government data must be kept inside the country (2025), and personal data must be stored in Rwanda unless the supervisory authority authorises otherwise.

The government data hub RISA took to market in November 2025 puts its core hardware in colocation at the National Data Centre. Cloud components, such as model training on GPUs, may run on public cloud outside Rwanda. The region's first AWS Outposts deployment, by Sand Technologies, went into Rwanda in 2026 to keep health and administration data in the country. The terms of that deployment and of the February 2026 memorandum placing Anthropic's Claude across government on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been found as at September 2026.

eKash was rebuilt open-source on Mojaloop with a Rwandan firm, replacing a proprietary first version (July 2026). Irembo is maintained by an insourced local engineering team.

In September 2026 the prime minister set out three tests of AI sovereignty. They ask whether a state can govern its citizens' data, reach infrastructure on predictable terms, and see African firms earn margin. He named affordable compute and energy as the country's biggest constraints. In September 2026 Huawei offered Rwanda a three-tier "trustworthy and sovereign AI" model, starting from a base model built locally. In March 2026 Rwanda hosted the EAC meeting on a shared AI roadmap framed as regional sovereignty. The Community's cross-border data rules remained unadopted in July 2026.
