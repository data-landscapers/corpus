Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Rwanda runs one national instant payment rail carrying every domestic interoperable retail payment at a maximum of RWF 20 between institutions, a biometric identity programme 4.4 million enrolments into a roughly 14 million target, and a data-protection law in force since 2021 that has never produced a published enforcement action or register of controllers. Third-generation networks are dated for nationwide switch-off on 30 June 2027. Not one new Rwandan statute reached the books in the twelve months the base can evidence, and the domestically financed share of the ministry's development programme fell for a third consecutive year while its externally financed share rose to RWF 65.8bn.

The ministry fixed 30 June 2027 for the third-generation shutdown on 28 July 2026, with operator pilot shutdowns during 2026, and named December 2028 as a working target for the older network — expressly conditional on coverage, voice reliability, mobile money continuity, dependent-system migration and affordable handsets. A government-commissioned study found more than two million people could still be on that network in 2030 without a planned transition; the study is unpublished, so its method is unestablished. Voice over fourth-generation networks, the technical precondition, switched on days later, with no coverage or handset-base figure published.

Fifth-generation service launched in June 2025 with no site or subscriber figure since, and the most recent internet-penetration figure is a projection for end-2025 rather than a measurement. Regulator statistics for the first quarter of 2026 give 14,001,080 mobile-cellular subscriptions at 97.1 per 100 inhabitants and 10,738,906 internet subscriptions. The largest connectivity instrument, a US$200m acceleration project, was last reported 55% complete in July 2025 and has published no figure since against a December 2026 closing date.

A hyperscaler hybrid-cloud deployment went live in April 2026, with deployment count and host facilities undisclosed — the only onshore compute named. The ministry's sector plan records a 50% hosting-price cut at the national data centre and 14.9% of 4,660 government websites and web applications hosted in the country, and dates the data centre and cloud service directives to 2023. The national incident response team's profile of 30 June 2022 gives its constituency and its statutory basis in the 2017 authority law, and publishes no caseload, resolution time or service-level target.

Effective 14 July 2026 the central bank migrated all domestic interoperable retail payments onto one state-owned open-source rail by directive, connecting 22 institutions; a bank-to-wallet transfer that could previously cost as much as RWF 5,000 now costs at most RWF 20, about one US cent — a maximum that applies between institutions, with each operator's own tariff on transfers inside its network unchanged. Reconcile settled the point on 2026-08-09: the banking industry's own public notice of 14 July 2026 puts it as a maximum fee of RWF 20 with institutions free to charge less or nothing, on every transfer between banks and electronic money issuers, so it is a ceiling on interoperability rather than a flat retail price. The instrument behind it is not held. BNR Directive No. 45/2026 is absent from the central bank's document library as enumerated on 2026-08-09 (493 documents), its issue date is given variously as 9 January and 9 June 2026, and nothing on file states what governs on-net tariffs if the ceiling does not. A bank and a mobile-money operator launched instant loans of up to RWF 2,000,000 and wallet saving over a short code on 2 August 2026, with no pricing, interest rate or eligibility rule published. The physical layer moved the other way: the second operator's market share fell from 16.9% to 14.3% and its active agents from 53,316 to 48,847 over the same year, in the cash-in and cash-out network the new rail does not touch.

Identity is enrolling against a hard deadline. Nationwide registration opened on 7 August 2025 and reached 4.4 million by June 2026; no figure has been published since, in the run-up to a June 2027 sunset after which legacy cards become invalid and banks, hospitals, telecoms operators and government institutions are expected to use the new authentication. The consent and access-log layer on which the privacy design rests has no go-live date, no live status and no user figure.

The exchange layer is the gap the country's own analysts name most often. A national data-sharing policy was approved in May 2025 and its governance unit is now staffed, but the platform itself has no go-live announcement with its 2026 target year eight months gone. The stack is being sold abroad: two neighbours have adopted the electronic billing machine, a third is mid-adoption, and one state has taken the electronic procurement platform and is co-developing it — with no revenue, contract value or licensing terms disclosed for any adoption.

The data-protection law has been in its enforcement phase since October 2023, carries extraterritorial reach and fines to RWF 5m or 1% of global turnover, and requires the supervisory authority to authorise every transfer of personal data out of the country. In five years it has produced no published enforcement action, fine total, breach register or count of registered controllers. Its appropriation is RWF 130m for 2026/27, flat and down from RWF 150m before that, of which the 2024/25 vote was spent at 35.1%, all in one quarter; the national identification programme executed at 140.7% of its appropriation.

The sector's plan for 2024/25 to 2028/29 replaced the 2018 to 2024 plan, with no progress report against its nine outcomes held. A national artificial-intelligence agency was approved by Cabinet on 8 June 2026 with its structure, operations and timeline not made public and no establishing law held. A virtual-assets bill had its rationale approved on 1 April 2026, with nothing on record since. Rwanda's bilateral health agreements with the United States, and the data-sharing agreements attached to them, are withheld and in litigation since 2 April 2026.

A development agency reported in July 2026, from engagements run to March 2026, that Rwandan participants said no dedicated national organisation conducts risk assessments, evaluations or active monitoring of artificial-intelligence errors and harms. No review framework is triggered by a commercial partnership either.

The commercial stack arrived before the institutions meant to govern it. A three-year non-binding memorandum with a frontier artificial-intelligence firm was signed on 17 February 2026 covering the health ministry, public-sector developer teams and education; the vendor's own announcement is the only primary held and no value is published. A US$50m primary-care health pilot announced the previous month begins in Rwanda and scales to 1,000 clinics by 2028, with no clinic count published since. The national agency was approved in June, and an institute of computing was approved on 24 July to open in September 2026 — its first two intakes limited to graduates of a single secondary academy.

The measurable side is employment and it is genuinely moving off a small base. The information and communication sector employed 19,000 workers in the second quarter of 2026 against about 12,000 a year earlier — up 62.6% against 2.7% economy-wide, still about 0.4% of total employment, against a target of 50,000 digital jobs by 2029. The fisc moved the other way: the science, technology and innovation appropriation fell 7.3% to RWF 856.6m for 2026/27, all recurrent with no domestically financed development.

The continental instruments Rwanda hosts have not converted into delivery. A declaration adopted in the capital in April 2025 endorsed a council and a US$60bn fund; sixteen months on, neither has a constitution, capitalisation, manager or first disbursement.

Formal financial access reached 96% of adults in 2024 against 48% in 2008, with 68.5% using a mobile wallet and about 5% holding a bank account. The agent network lags its market: the second operator's agent count fell over the year interoperability was meant to help. The urban and rural internet-use gap, 57% against 19%, is unchanged: the survey has not been repeated.

4,184 of 4,996 schools were connected as at March 2026, leaving 812 mostly rural — rural connectivity at about 27% against roughly 75% urban, against an 80% target. Nothing has been published since.

The acceleration project and a regional bank operation both close in 2026, the first last reported 55% complete in July 2025 and the second with no disbursement figure at any date. Capacity money moved in opposite directions inside one budget: an education technology integration line rose 23.1% to RWF 28.96bn while a second fell 34.4%, and the cyber-security standards and skills line sat flat for a second year after spending 11.5% of its 2024/25 vote. A digital-ambassador programme concluded its financing phase in May 2026 having trained 1,567,034 citizens, with no successor financing held. A universal access fund is on the record: a presidential order of 12 May 2023 caps each licensee's annual contribution at 2.5% of the turnover of each regulated service category; the rate actually set is a regulatory-board decision the base does not hold. Its latest accounts on file, for 2020/21, report Frw 3,828,837,334 spent against a Frw 6,372,536,516 budget.

The consent and access-log layer is the safeguard the privacy-by-design case for the identity programme rests on, and it is the one component with no delivery evidence — a gap that matters more each month enrolment scales toward the June 2027 sunset. And the government data-sharing platform, on which the interoperability of everything else depends, has no go-live announcement with its target year two-thirds gone.
