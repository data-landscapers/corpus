This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

A digital budget of 82bn CFA francs was presented for 2026. The 2026 finance law voted 62.4bn CFA francs to the digital-economy programme. The supplementary law of 17 July 2026 cut it to 49.8bn. In February 2026 disbursement on the World Bank-financed eGabon and Gabon Digital programmes was still too low. The ministry judged the foundations in place and interministerial coordination in need of strengthening. For 2027 the ministry has asked the National Assembly for 37.2bn CFA francs, against 53.7bn for 2026 on its own comparison.

The World Bank's GovTech Maturity Index 2025 update records a current national digital transformation strategy for Gabon. Its whole-of-government approach to public sector digital transformation is only planned or in draft. On 3 April 2026 the digital economy minister presented the orientations of a national digitalisation strategy to the state's information-system directors. Its five axes are regulation, infrastructure, digitised public services, local skills and data governance, with sector master plans to follow. No strategy text has been found. A ministry leads the work, and senior digital officials meet in a cross-government forum. Progress against the transformation programme is reported internally and not published. The index records no government entity responsible for data governance and no data governance strategy or policy.

A comité de pilotage held its fifth session in February 2026. In June 2026 a World Bank mission met ANINF to review e-Gabon's closure and Gabon Digital's execution. A presidential directive of 3 October 2025 called for the financial agencies to be digitised. The SecBOM cybersecurity initiative is driven from the Presidency and combines technical, legal-compliance and sovereigntist objectives. The digital economy minister presents the e-government ordinance as a reform bringing public services closer to citizens and stimulating digital sovereignty. In July 2026 the ministry set its next-phase priorities: governance of digital projects, the GabIX exchange point, infrastructure, an institutional content strategy and digital diplomacy.

Public Administration is Gabon's lowest-ranked IIAG sub-category, at 38.4 of 100 in 2023 and 45th of 54 African states. Its overall governance score for 2023 is 46.6, 32nd of 54 and below the African average of 49.3.

Legislation and regulation

Law n°003/2026 of 5 October 2026, published in the Journal Officiel of 9 October, ratifies and amends the social-media ordinance of 8 April 2026. It obliges every user to identify themselves formally, without saying how. The ratified text bans deepfakes of public or private persons and gives editors twelve months to label AI-generated content. A host or editor that fails to combat illicit content faces a year in prison and a fine of FCFA 5-50 million (October 2026). In July 2026 civil society asked for provisions it judged disproportionate to be revised.

Article 75 of loi n°025/2023 of 12 July 2023 prohibits outright the collection or processing of biometric and genetic data. The ban also covers racial and ethnic origin, political, philosophical and religious opinion, union membership, health and sexual life. The derogations listed in article 76 are written against "l'interdiction prévue par l'article 74", which governs children's data. Under article 83, state processing of biometric data to authenticate or check identity is authorised by decree in the Council of Ministers after the authority's opinion. The law binds controllers from its publication on 15 July 2023, with no transition period. It amends the 2011 statute that first created the regime.

Electronic communications run under Law No. 026/2018. A dedicated law regulates electronic transactions, taking in data protection, digital identification and digital payments. The 2023 cybersecurity and cybercrime law imposes data localisation on electronic communications operators. Enrolment for the personal identification number is compulsory for every person present on Gabonese territory. The 2025 electoral code builds the roll from the national biometric register. Law 008/2022 provides for a centralised, digital and biometric civil registry.

An ordinance published in the Journal Officiel of 7 September 2025 gives e-government its first comprehensive legal basis. It requires public administrations to digitise services, interconnect systems and bring in the private sector. Gabon has no right-to-information law, and none in draft. From 1 October 2026 draft texts on digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, digital services and the institutional framework went to a validation review of about ten days at Nkok. The texts have not been found.

Data protection

The 2025 finance law instituted a data-protection levy on every person whose data a public or private body in Gabon processes, forecasting 2.92bn CFA francs of receipts. Its rate has not been found. An ordonnance of 31 January 2020 had set the earlier levy at 1,000 FCFA per person per year, split 40% to the state and 60% to the authority. Loi n°025/2023 of 12 July 2023 is supervised by the Autorité pour la Protection des Données Personnelles et de la Vie Privée, an independent authority over public and private processing.

Under the 2023 law, processing biometric data is barred outright. State processing of biometric data to authenticate identity needs a decree after the authority's opinion. Automated processing must generally be declared to it. In April 2024 the authority fined Citibank Gabon 90m CFA francs for diverting employee data from its declared purpose in an internal inquiry. Deliberation No. 026 of 14 November 2023 approved extracting personal data from the IBOGA biometric system for the electronic identity card. It named the directorate handling access and rectification requests. On 20 January 2026 the authority oversaw the destruction of the personal files of unsuccessful 2022 public-tender bidders held by the public procurement directorate. Its rapporteur told public bodies to destroy sensitive documents before witnesses under the authority's supervision.

Under the 2023 law, access to one's own data is a free written request (Article 43), with a copy charged at no more than cost (Article 44). Breach notification to the authority is mandatory and without delay. Transfers to countries without adequate protection are barred, and no adequacy list has been published. In March 2024 the authority authorised Citibank Gabon to transfer staff data to Sweden and Canada for one year. Nothing has been published on complaints or on the regulator's performance. A covenant of the World Bank-financed identity programme requires the biometric population registry, the civil register and the identity card database to stay functionally separate. Digital Freedom fell 19.5 points over 2014-2023 to 38.3 of 100, 42nd of 54 African states. It is the fourth most deteriorated of Gabon's 96 IIAG indicators.

Regional collaboration

GIMACPAY, the CEMAC regional instant payment system, has been live since July 2020. Gabon's payments build sits on it; GIMAC runs it, with the central bank BEAC as majority owner and settlement agent. It treats cross-border transfers within the monetary union on the same basis as domestic ones. The backbone was built under the regional Central African Backbone programme, whose Gabon operation closed in 2020.

Regional Integration scored 25.4 of 100 in 2023, 46th of 54 African states, despite rising 9.7 points over the decade. ECCAS is Gabon's only regional economic community membership. In October 2026 Gabon signed a host-country agreement with the African Union for its technical committee on communication and ICT. The committee's sixth session opened in Libreville.

Gabon signed the United Nations Convention against Cybercrime in September 2026 and has not yet ratified it. It took part in Interpol's Serengeti 2.0 cybercrime operation alongside 17 other African countries between June and August 2025. On 3 September 2026 the digital economy minister presented Gabon's candidacy for the International Telecommunication Union Council at the WTPF-26 forum in Nassau. The election takes place at the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha in November 2026.

Standards

The CI-SIS health information interoperability framework was first issued in 2019 under the 2017 master plan for health information systems. Across government, the World Bank's 2025 GovTech index records no interoperability framework setting the rules for data exchange between state systems.

The 2025 GovTech index records no data quality framework for government information systems and no monitoring of their uptime. It records no guidance for replacing legacy systems, and nothing published on interoperability compliance. There is no e-procurement portal, so tendering and contract award are not conducted online. Procurement enforces no interoperability or technical standards. There is no open source policy or action plan for the public sector, and no body deciding on open source adoption or procurement.

AGANOR was created by decree in June 2014 as the national body for standards, conformity assessment, metrology and quality. In October 2026 ISO listed it as Gabon's member, participating in 3 technical committees and observing 18. No information-technology standard from AGANOR has been found.

The 2025 GovTech index records a unified budget classification and chart of accounts covering central government only, not sub-national government.

Of the core administrative systems Gabon does operate — financial management, tax and customs among them — none has recorded governance arrangements covering compliance, security and audit trails over its operations (2025).

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Deliberative and Participatory Governance is the second most deteriorated of Gabon's 96 IIAG indicators, down 22.0 points over 2014-2023 to 46.5 of 100 in 2023. In 2023 it ranked 35th of 54 African states. Over the same decade Civil Society Space rose 9.9 points to 67.8, 17th of 54. The Participation sub-category fell 5.2 points to 38.4.

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech index records no national platform through which citizens or businesses can take part in policy decisions, submit petitions or give anonymous feedback. It records no platform for complaints, suggestions or information requests about service delivery. It records no published service standards and no demonstration that feedback is acted on. Gabon scored 0.1233 on the UN E-Participation Index in 2024, far below its overall e-government score.

None of the four public service portals the GovTech Maturity Index assesses in Gabon involves citizens in the design of its services (2025).

Freedom of Expression and Belief fell 17.0 points over 2014-2023 to 60.1 of 100. On 17 July 2026 the presidency defended the social-media suspension, then nearly five months old, as temporary. It rejected a purely political reading and framed the measure as regulation and protection of the young. On 25 July 2026 APSAD presented a report on civic and digital liberties in Libreville, built from two months of consultations with civil-society organisations, journalists, bloggers and digital-sector operators. It asked the state to end blanket social-media suspensions and to revise provisions of the regulation in force it judges disproportionate. It also asked the state to strengthen the Haute Autorité de la Communication's independence and open a standing dialogue with the sector. On 6 August 2026 the ministry postponed the Kimba Connect final over start-up concerns. It described the objections only as concerning the programme's organisation, and no start-up was named or quoted.

On 24 October 2026 the Haute Autorité de la Communication lifted the social-media suspension. On 13 October 2026 Paradigm Initiative asked the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights to call on Gabon to amend the April ordinance's blanket ban on under-16s using social media.

Finance

Financial sustainability

On 14 September 2026 the state holding SPIN signed a 20-year public-private partnership with Gabon Fiber S.A.. SPIN leases the company about 1,800 km of existing network, which remains SPIN's property. The company is conceded a 1,700 km extension that it designs, finances, builds and operates. The extensions revert to SPIN at the end of the term, and operators get open, non-discriminatory access. In September 2026 the concessionaire planned to raise more than 41 billion CFA francs on international markets so that the build does not touch the state budget.

Domestic money carries 60.7 per cent of the digital lines in Gabon's 2026 budget as revised by the supplementary law of July 2026, 31.3 billion of 51.6 billion CFA francs, against 52.9 per cent of 48.7 billion in the 2025 finance law as voted.

The 2026 laws attribute 6.82 billion CFA francs of spectrum-management receipts to ANINF. They return 4.01 billion of ARCEP's regulation receipts to the digital-economy programme (2026). They fund 3.81 billion of universal-service spending from a special account (2026). They concede to the data-protection authority 2.92 billion of its levy on data controllers (2026). Under the 2025 finance law, the universal-service account is fed by the universal-service levy, a spectrum fee and ARCEP's receipts. Since August 2025 a ministry that has not sent its annual digitalisation plan can be refused the budget entry and the public contracts for its digital projects.

In July 2026 the supplementary law cut the Gabon Digital drawing from 8 billion to 5 billion CFA francs. It added a 6 billion commercial loan from Standard Chartered and Citi for a national data centre and other digital works (July 2026). The World Bank's Digital Gabon credit had a closing date of 30 June 2026. No outturn against the digital appropriations had been found as at September 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

On 14 July 2026 Gabon signed an accord with the Société Gabonaise de Garantie to establish guarantee mechanisms cutting the risk banks bear in lending to young innovative firms. Its director-general, Alban Etho, put risk-sharing as the condition for improving start-up access to bank credit. No guarantee ceiling, coverage ratio, eligibility test, term or participating bank has been published. The same day Gabon signed a skills accord with Huawei Technologies Gabon. It covers smart rooms for innovation, training in networks, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and software development, and access to the company's certifying training kits. No room count, trainee target, cost or duration has been published.

Gabon signed a memorandum of understanding with Huawei in 2024 for a smart-cities project and other digital services. It carried a pledge of spending, not a financing agreement. On 23 December 2025 Gabon and Huawei signed a broader memorandum on national digital transformation, which expands the Connected Schools programme. In July 2025 ANINF and ST Digital signed a framework agreement covering secure digital infrastructure, cloud, cybersecurity, digital identity and e-government. It includes local capacity-building, and no value, delivery date or committed scope of work attaches to it. A partnership with Visa, agreed in May 2025, was followed up in Libreville on 18 July 2025. It is directed at digitalising public services and modernising how the state collects payments, with no value attached.

In December 2025 Gabon signed an agreement with the UN Capital Development Fund to modernise its financial services and widen financial inclusion.

New investments

The World Bank approved the Digital Gabon Project, about US$68 million of IBRD lending, in July 2021. Its closing date was 30 June 2026, and it covers connectivity, digital government services, digital financial infrastructure and the ICT sector. Its 2021 design puts US$24.5 million, the largest component, into modernising legal identity systems. It puts US$20 million into the enabling environment, US$17 million into public administration and services and US$7 million into citizen engagement, implementation support and monitoring (2021). eGabon adds about US$56 million, approved in June 2016 with a closing date of August 2025. Most was appraised for a national health information system and the digital innovation sector. US$50 million for statistical system modernisation was approved in 2017 and closed in 2023. About US$23 million of additional financing for the Central African Backbone build closed in 2020.

In December 2021 MIGA approved about US$28 million of political-risk cover over an equity stake in Airtel Mobile Commerce's Gabonese subsidiary. MIGA's record lists the guarantee as closed in 2026. The U.S. Department of State is funding about US$1.25 million of cybersecurity capacity-building with ANINF over 2024-2025. The ITU ran a CHF 1.24 million regional project on ICT indicators in 2021-2023 with CEMAC regulators and statistics offices, Gabon among eight target countries.

Nine commitments and pledges are on the record, made between 2016 and 2026 by six financiers. Around US$227 million of that is finance actually committed as at October 2026, and about US$197 million of it is four IBRD loans — non-concessional sovereign borrowing, with grant money amounting to two small awards, one of undisclosed size. Five commitments were still live as at October 2026, four of them worth roughly US$126 million and the fifth undisclosed; the smallest of them is the one private equity round. Connectivity and e-government take most of the recorded total, and the largest figure of all is a private pledge under a memorandum rather than a financing agreement.

The lender's restructuring paper of June 2026 records US$13.38m disbursed by 31 May 2026. It rates progress towards the objective moderately unsatisfactory and leaves the civil-registration digitisation component unchanged.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Some 98% of the population had at least a 4G signal in 2022. The regulator put telephone and internet coverage at 97% in 2024. Its figures show 2,405,614 internet subscriptions in the first quarter of 2026, 94% of them mobile. There were around 124 mobile connections per 100 people in 2025. About 71.9% of the population used the internet in 2023. Rural clinics kept patient records on paper in 2022, citing poor connectivity and too few computers. On 24 October 2026 the Haute Autorité de la Communication lifted the nationwide suspension of social media ordered on 17 February 2026.

Gabon's Internet and Computers score rose 23.3 points over 2014-2023 to 48.5 of 100, 14th of 54 African states. The country scored 76.1 on the ITU's ICT Development Index in 2024, 11th in Africa.

5G was officially launched in Libreville on 26 October 2026, after the regulator approved frequency assignments to Airtel Gabon and Moov Africa Gabon Télécom. Masts are going up in Greater Libreville and Port-Gentil first.

On 24 August 2026 several sections of the national fibre backbone were cut along the railway near Lopé. Internet and telephony went down across four interior provinces. In July 2026 a national seminar on satellite communications cited 88% forest cover as a limit on fibre, opening a national policy process on satellite communications. GABIX, the national internet exchange point, had 8 members in September 2026. The sector operates under Law No. 026/2018. The backbone was built under a regional programme that closed in 2020 and the World Bank's Digital Gabon operation, whose closing date was 30 June 2026. In September 2026 the state signed a twenty-year partnership for a private operator to run and extend the backbone. The operator said in September 2026 it intends to raise more than 41bn CFA francs for 1,650 km of fibre. In October 2026 the incumbent operator and the ministry signed a convention ceding to the State the 196 km Fougamou-Mouila fibre section. The state infrastructure holding and Cameroon's state operator signed a memorandum on a terrestrial link between Cameroon, Gabon and Congo (October 2026).

Data Storage

In July 2026 President Brice Oligui Nguema inaugurated Gabon's first Tier III certified data centre, in the Nkok special economic zone near Libreville. The Cameroonian operator ST Digital built it on a site of about 3,000 square metres, with room for around 3,000 physical servers. Its halls serve cloud, colocation and private suites, and its technical staff are all Gabonese. It commissions 92 racks on 1 MW of installed power and connects to the ACE and the South Africa Far East/West Africa submarine cable systems. It cost about 8 billion CFA francs, some US$14 million (July 2026). Ground had been broken in August 2025.

In July 2026 the state presented the launch as a way to store sensitive government and business data at home and reduce reliance on foreign digital infrastructure. The data protection authority, the APDPVP, spent months verifying the project's compliance before commissioning. ST Digital thanked it and the government for help in obtaining the authorisations the launch required.

In June 2025 the head of state formalised a convention with the US firm Cybastion for a national data centre for ANINF. In January 2026 Cybastion signed a strategic agreement with the digital economy ministry for a 20 MW facility. No cost or completion date has been published.

Law No. 027/2023 requires network operators to run a management centre for their infrastructure and data on national territory (Article 22). It requires confidential or sensitive data to be hosted only in Gabon, absent a written exemption (Article 27). Every operator must keep a copy of its data in the country (Article 36). In 2025 the World Bank recorded no shared cloud platform available across government entities. It found nothing published on where government data may be held or on government cloud use. The World Bank's eGabon operation, approved in 2016, lists a government data centre among the e-government infrastructure it develops. In September 2025 Gabon had no direct cloud on-ramp for AWS, Azure or Google Cloud. Enterprises reached those services over interconnects to Lagos or Cape Town, at latencies above 100 milliseconds.

Energy

Frequent outages and regular load shedding in the capital drew public anger in 2025. In 2023, 94.1% of the population had access to electricity. Access to energy is one of Gabon's ten best-scoring governance indicators, at 91.2 of 100 and 7th of 54 African states in 2023.

Electricity reached 99.2% of the urban population in 2023 but only 29.0% of the rural population. Residential electricity cost about US$0.214 per kilowatt hour in December 2025. At that price a benchmark 100 kWh month cost roughly 3% of income per head (December 2025).

Technical Capacity

In 2025 the World Bank's GovTech Maturity Index recorded a ministry leading public sector digital transformation. It also recorded a cross-government forum where senior digital officials address digital, data, technology and capacity questions. Progress against the transformation programme is reported internally and not published (2025). The index recorded an established GovTech entity, a body coordinating GovTech initiatives and an entity monitoring digital spending across government. That entity's annual progress report is also internal (2025).

Public Administration was Gabon's lowest-ranked governance sub-category in 2023, at 38.4 of 100 and 45th of 54 African states. It improved 3.0 points over 2014-2023. Effective administration scored 46.7 in 2023, 33rd of 54, and was almost unchanged across 2014-2023. ANINF's deputy director-general coordinates the World Bank-financed eGabon-SIS project and Gabon Digital programme. In June 2026 ANINF opened its first office outside Libreville, in Franceville. In 2025 the index found no data quality framework for government information systems, no uptime monitoring and no guidance for replacing legacy systems. It found nothing published on interoperability compliance.

In 2025 the Université Numérique du Gabon, a public distance-learning university, awarded state-recognised degrees in disciplines including computer science and engineering sciences. In 2024 the electronic identity card and its digital identity management software and hardware were supplied by the French company IN Groupe. The US State Department is funding about US$1.25 million of cybersecurity capacity-building with ANINF across 2024-2025. The programme is aimed at national frameworks, incident response and digital resilience.

Cybersecurity

On the night of 14 to 15 June 2026 a cyberattack brought down close to 95% of the information systems of the water and electricity utility SEEG. On 4 August 2026 SEEG reported billing and payment restored in Libreville and seven interior centres. In September 2026 the government raised its vigilance level after more than 900,000 intrusion alerts were recorded in sixty days. ANINF said the figure shows a system able to see threats, not 900,000 compromises (September 2026).

In 2024 Gabon scored 39.85 of 100 on the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index, up from 11.38 in the 2020 edition. It sits in Tier 4, with full marks on legal measures and weak technical, organisational and cooperation scores. In December 2024 a dedicated security operations centre for government systems was still planned, not running. The legal measures rest on the 2023 cybersecurity and cybercrime law. Under the data protection regime, controllers must notify the authority of a breach without delay. Where a breach poses a high risk to their rights, affected individuals must be told. Public administrations must separately report incidents as soon as they become aware of them.

In June 2025 ANINF launched SecBOM, a tool to map, control and protect the state's sensitive digital assets. In June 2026 the agency was working with the US-based MITRE Corporation on the design of Gabon's cybersecurity architecture. Gabon is not a party to the Budapest Convention; its delegates at the Council of Europe's October 2026 conference explored joining.

From June to August 2025 Gabon took part in Interpol's Serengeti 2.0 cybercrime operation alongside seventeen other African countries, with UK backing. In October 2025 Banque de France auditors ran some fifteen cybersecurity and IT audit workshops at BEAC, the central bank serving Gabon. In November 2025 ST Digital held the second national Cybersecurity Forum, on critical infrastructure, with ANINF, CN Consulting and BGFI Services. In October 2026 an accord between ANINF and Kaspersky was reported for a Libreville centre for threat monitoring, detection, analysis and skills transfer. The national strategy also plans a CSIRT and a security operations centre (October 2026).

DPI

Data Exchange

In September 2026 Gabon's government interoperability platform was one of four projects under way at the sixth steering committee of the World Bank-financed programmes, held on 8 September. In June 2024 no government body was exchanging data on it, against a target of five by 2026, and the platform had not been deployed. On the same date the framework to govern the exchange was unpublished, with adoption targeted for June 2026. In 2025 the World Bank's GovTech Maturity Index recorded no government interoperability framework of any kind. A 2025 ordinance requires public administrations to digitise their services and interconnect their systems. In 2025 Gabon had no dedicated body for data governance and no data governance strategy. It had no data quality framework for government information systems, no uptime monitoring and nothing published on interoperability compliance (2025). Business registration data moves automatically from the investment agency's one-stop platform to the tax administration and the two social funds. In 2025 the social insurance and pension system exchanged data with no other government system.

In February 2021 an inter-ministerial committee was created under the Prime Minister's office to coordinate all initiatives to digitise public administration. In 2025 ANINF ran a proof of concept linking the tax, customs and treasury systems through a message broker. ANINF presented it as a trial, not a final technical choice. In May 2026 Gabon signed a concession with the US firm Securiport for an advance passenger and passenger-name-record system. It is financed by a US$30 levy on each international passenger. By October 2026 its equipment was installed at Libreville airport, with commissioning still to come.

A legal covenant of the World Bank-financed Digital Gabon project (2021) requires the biometric population registry, the civil register and the identity card database to remain functionally separate. In mid-2025 the institutions were still drawing up the operational roadmap to interconnect them through a common biometric reference.

Digital Identity and CRVS

In June 2024 Gabon issued its first electronic national identity card. On 2 June 2026 the interior minister told the National Assembly that 335,384 people had enrolled, 164,944 cards had been produced and 147,224 collected. Another 92,614 files were in processing and 78,500 were classed as disputed (June 2026).

Fewer than half of those enrolled hold a card: collections come to about 44% of enrolments (June 2026). Disputed files amount to roughly 23% of everything enrolled to June 2026, which makes data quality a structural feature of the build rather than an administrative backlog.

In 2024 the card ended an eleven-year gap in national identity card issuance. Law 027/2020 makes enrolment for the 14-character personal identification number compulsory for everyone on Gabonese territory, including non-nationals. Registration, the attestation and the first card are free, and enrolment opens at 16. The attestation is the prerequisite for applying for the card. Behind it sits IBOGA, the biometric master database with automated deduplication, begun in 2012.

In December 2024 the World Bank's indicator for Gabonese aged 16 and over holding a NIP stood at zero, against a 90% target for June 2026. Health insurance beneficiary records tied to a NIP also stood at zero, against a target of 250,000 (December 2024). In 2025 most social insurance records were keyed on the schemes' own identifiers, not the national number. The 2023 data protection statute allows the state to process biometric data only by decree after the authority's opinion. In November 2023 the authority gave a favourable opinion on extracting IBOGA data to issue the card. Perceived ease of obtaining an identity document scored 29.1 of 100 in 2023, 28th of 54.

Production of a digitalised driving licence began on 24 March 2026. On 21 September 2026 the transport ministry suspended enrolment, with nearly 7,000 licences uncollected in Libreville. Enrolment resumed on 26 October 2026 at five Libreville sites, on a migrated platform. By October 2026 attribution of the personal identification number had been suspended for several months. A review meeting at the end of September 2026 prepared its resumption, with no date announced.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Of 1,101,434 bank card transactions in June 2026, 62.41% were ATM withdrawals and 24.61% payments at merchant terminals. At end-June 2026 Gabon had 326 ATMs and 1,535 merchant terminals. Banks had 457,377 cardholders among 673,793 clients (June 2026). Of their cards, 440,869 carry an international scheme and 16,508 are domestic private-label (June 2026).

The merchant terminal estate is nearly five times the size of the ATM estate yet carries under half the ATM network's transaction traffic (June 2026).

In 2024 Global Findex put digital payment use among Gabonese adults at 66.5% and mobile money account ownership at 61.6%. Gabon's digital payments run on the CEMAC GIMACPAY system, operated through GIMAC and the central bank. GIMACPAY has been live since July 2020. In 2025 it had 153 participants, among them 56 banks and financial institutions and 16 payment and mobile money institutions. It treats cross-border transfers within the monetary union like domestic ones. In 2023 BEAC owned 99.2% of GIMAC, chaired its management committee and acted as settlement agent. GIMAC's scheme rules are not public (2023). It had established no effective consumer recourse, and no body in its governance represented consumers or low-income users (2023).

In December 2025 the state launched MADIGIPAIE to modernise public payments, letting people pay by mobile money by scanning a GIMACPAY QR code. More than 1,000 interoperable QR codes had reached merchants by December 2025. The scheme reached the TaxiGab transport network on 28 February 2026. An October 2026 account put it at more than 3,273 merchants and 1,382 GIMAC QR codes in July. No transaction volume has been published (October 2026).

In 2024 the tax directorate reported at least 80% of tax revenue collected through its Digitax remote-payment platform. Electronic filing and payment have been mandatory for medium and large enterprises since April 2024. BEAC reports annually on payment services in CEMAC, and its latest edition covers 2024. On 8 October 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a bill ratifying and amending the digital payments ordinance. The bill regulates payment-solution providers and moves payments progressively to digital form.

Registries

In December 2024 no administrative department had a civil registration office registering vital acts digitally, against a project target of fifteen by June 2026. In 2023 registration was still paper-based and decentralised at the point of registration. Law 008/2022 provides for a centralised digital and biometric civil register run by a new national civil registration centre. In 2023 the law was still being put into effect. Gabon's civil registration score in the Ibrahim Index rose 12.5 points over 2014-2023 to 37.5 of 100, 47th of 54 African states. On 28 September 2026 an inter-ministerial workshop on opening the national civil registration centre began at Ntoum. The workshop closed with a diagnosis of badly kept archives, irregular data entry and a Civil Code dating from 1972 (October 2026).

In 2021 the biometric population registry was to be seeded from the IBOGA biometric voter file of roughly 690,000 unique records, about half the adult population. The electoral register held 846,822 voters before the August 2023 elections, 16,093 of them registered abroad. The 2025 electoral code makes the roll a permanent biometric file built from the national biometric register. It registers anyone already holding a personal identification number automatically on turning 18. In 2025 companies registered online through ANPI-Gabon's investment window, which handles registration, payment and the single registration record. The RCCM commercial register at the Ministry of Justice was still being moved online (2025). The RCCM number and the taxpayer number are issued within about 48 hours of each other (2025).

In 2012 formal titling covered a very small share of land, and the cadastre operated in practice only in urban areas. In 2025 the government began modernising the cadastre towards an integrated urban land management system. In May 2026 the unified social registry was still being built, with World Bank technical support. The government judged its register of economically weak citizens not reliable enough to target social protection. It announced a second census of them to begin in September 2026. In 2026 Gabon had no national standardised address register to which land or business records could be tied.

Sectoral management information systems

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech assessment records Gabon's financial management information system as in use and custom-built. It covers only treasury execution, and only central government. The tax management system is custom software and exchanges data with other government systems. Customs uses off-the-shelf software that also exchanges data, under an administration separate from tax. A debt management system is in use; no public investment management system is recorded. SIGFIP, a public finance platform launched in January 2026, links state payments and revenues to the Treasury, banks and payment operators.

By August 2026 the civil service's payroll file, the Fichier unique de référence, had been near-inoperative for months, blocking tenure confirmations, reclassifications, recruitments and promotions. On 25 August the vice-president of the government ordered an emergency restoration plan. On 12 October 2026 the ministry reviewed preparations for SIGRH, a single state-employee register linked to payroll, budget and national identification. By 25 October the ministry had asked for FCFA 5.38bn for 2027 to deploy it. In 2025 HR records had no self-service portal and no link to the national identity number. The social insurance and pension system exchanges data with no other system.

DHIS2 was launched nationally in December 2024. A 2022 assessment of 71 facilities found only 45% had any digital tool for collecting and storing data. Nearly all processed data by hand. The eGabon hospital information system reached two pilot hospitals from December 2023, and a 2025 second phase adds eight more. Health also has its own interoperability framework, the CI-SIS.

In March 2025 the World Bank undertook to supply tablets, and UNESCO proposed software, for school data collection. In February 2025 police stations and gendarmerie brigades kept custody records on paper registers. Digitising court registries is a 2026 priority of a Japan-funded UNDP justice project, with no system or timetable stated. Gabon's Ibrahim Index score for tax and revenue mobilisation fell 17.5 points over 2014-2023, to 30.8 of 100 and 46th of 54. On 29 September 2026 the Gabon Connect supplier presented an integrated customs operations system whose first lot covers mineral clearance and the hydrocarbons warehouse.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In 2024 Gabon scored 0.5741 on the UN E-Government Development Index, above the Central African and African averages. In 2025 the World Bank recorded no one-stop shop, no government mobile application and no online business registration. It found no published performance data on online service delivery. The tax portal offers taxpayer registration and filing. The social insurance and pension portal handles registration, benefits and payments. A government job portal offers registration and job search. In 2025 the Interior Ministry offered an online check of electoral registration and polling station by personal identification number. There is no e-procurement portal, so tendering and contract award are not conducted online.

A free three-month tourist e-Visa runs on a platform developed in 2025. The announcement gave no dates for the free window or application volumes. The food-safety agency is to take certificate applications online through its Nexus platform from 2 November 2026. Applicants abroad met repeated rejections from the online NIP attestation channel, which officials blamed on network saturation. In September 2026 cameras to detect speeding, red-light and parking offences were installed across Grand Libreville ahead of going live.

In 2025 Gabon had a GovTech entity, a GovTech coordination body and a monitor of digital spending. The institution's annual progress report is internal and unpublished. ANINF's deputy director-general coordinates the World Bank-financed eGabon-SIS project and Gabon Digital programme. A ministerial meeting in January 2026 launched SIGFIP with electronic invoicing, a national public-revenue platform and an extension of the Gabon Digital loan. An ordinance published in September 2025 gave e-government its first comprehensive legal basis.

The single window, Gabon Connect, entered deployment in October 2026 with its payroll-declaration and TresorPay portals. It groups five services, with full deployment due by December 2027. In August 2026 a government services portal was being prepared for a pilot in five ministries, with at least ten services due by December 2026. In October 2026 the postal company presented a Gabon-hosted application combining payments, public services, postal services and insurance. On 19 October 2026 the National Assembly launched its GTLAN legislative work platform, issuing tablets to members.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ANINF opened its first provincial office, in Franceville in Haut-Ogooué, in June 2026. It was the agency's first office outside the capital since its creation in 2011.

In June 2024 no government body, national or local, was exchanging data on the planned interoperability platform, which had not been deployed. An inter-ministerial committee to coordinate the digitisation of public administration was created under the Prime Minister's office in February 2021. In 2025 the integrated financial management system in use covered only central government, and only treasury execution. The unified budget classification and chart of accounts likewise cover central government only.

In July 2026 the mayor of Libreville agreed to act as facilitator for MADIGIPAIE among the city's traders. The city kept its own revenue digitalisation system, built with the Treasury. The city began collecting market fees by mobile money in June 2026. Four months later, in October 2026, traders said they had returned to cash or never used it. The city hall called the system operational.

Rural digital data capture

The 2024 Ibrahim Index of African Governance gives Gabon no score for its Rural Economy sub-category. Nor does it score any of the four indicators: rural land and water access, rural market access, rural economy support, and rural representation and participation. The IFAD data these measures draw on were unavailable for Gabon in 2023.

In 2022 health facilities were compelled to collect data on paper records where computers, software and a reliable connection were lacking. In February 2025 police stations and gendarmerie brigades kept custody records on paper. Digitalising custody records was discussed as a future aim, with infrastructure limits flagged in remote areas. In 2023 civil registration was paper-based and decentralised at the point of registration. A centralised digital registry was presented as a later stage of the IBOGA identity project. In March 2025 the World Bank undertook to supply tablets, and UNESCO proposed software, for school data collection. Some schools were already equipped.

In 2023 rural electricity access was far below the near-universal urban rate. In February 2024 some villages had no mobile or internet coverage at all. Forest cover across most of the country means fibre cannot reach everywhere (July 2026). In February 2024 a second phase of the universal service programme was announced, to extend coverage to 200 villages in all nine provinces. It is financed by a special fund the telecoms operators pay into each year. In August 2025 regulator teams with Moov Africa Gabon Télécom were identifying transmitter sites in remote villages. In June 2024 the digital identity programme carried an explicit rural coverage target alongside its gender one.

Technology

AI

Moov Africa Gabon Telecom presented MIA on 21 August 2026, an assistant that answers spoken questions for anyone dialling 333. It works from an ordinary phone, with no smartphone or internet connection needed. The government launched Missamou on 15 July 2025, a Facebook Messenger assistant answering questions on official news, public services and administrative procedures. In August 2026 two officials of the forward-planning directorate were trained in Lusaka in using generative AI tools to monitor and report on the African Union's Agenda 2063.

Preparatory work for a national AI strategy closed in Libreville on 30 September 2026 with 22 challenges and 36 recommendations. They came from six working groups supported by the UN development programme. Drafting is the next step, and no strategy has been adopted. In October 2026 a provisional UNDP AI landscape assessment presented to the workshop scored Gabon 1.9 of 5, and 1.7 on regulation and ethics.

In January 2026 the telecoms regulator ARCEP ran a national workshop on internet governance and digital sovereignty that covered artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. Its president set the aim of making the country a hub of digital soft power. In 2025 the public Université Numérique du Gabon taught computer science among its disciplines, in person and by distance learning.

ICT Industry

In 2025 the World Bank recorded no policy to support govtech start-ups or private investment in them. It found no government financing for innovation by small firms and no procurement rule favouring bids from start-ups or SMEs. Nothing on start-up or SME support had been published. In May 2026 Gabon was outside the African fintech markets that draw sustained venture capital. The country's first Tier III data centre, opened at Nkok in July 2026, was built by a Cameroonian operator. It is run by an entirely Gabonese technical staff.

Innovation ecosystem

Kimba Connect, launched in July 2026, connects established firms to local start-ups so corporate business problems become the specification for digital solutions. It closed applications on 24 July 2026, with its final set for 4 August. On 6 August the digital economy ministry said it would move that date after concerns raised by participating start-ups. Their objections were described only as concerning the programme's organisation. A revised agenda of 11 August put the final at late September or early October 2026. Selected firms were promised finance, technology support, a quality label and market access. On 14 July 2026 the state signed a guarantee mechanism with the Société Gabonaise de Garantie to share banks' risk in lending to young innovative firms. It signed a skills accord with Huawei, and neither accord has published terms.

In July 2026 counts of Gabonese start-ups ran to roughly 150 to 200 firms. In 2025 a commercial tracker listing about that many found fewer than one in ten with disclosed funding. It found one disclosed round in the year to October. That round, of EUR 650,000 in October 2025, was led by Saviu Ventures in the fleet-telematics start-up POZI. In May 2026 the UN Capital Development Fund placed Gabon in the third tier of African fintech markets, outside those drawing sustained venture capital. In 2025 Akewa Accélérateur, the Société d'Incubation Numérique du Gabon and Ogooué Labs operated as innovation hubs, alongside an active angel investor network. In August 2026 the Société d'Incubation Numérique du Gabon lost its director general after an audit and was placed under interim management. A state takeover, a parapublic model or absorption into the national innovation centre were under discussion. In September 2026 the former Woleu-Ntem governorate at Oyem was being converted into a pilot training and fabrication centre for digital skills, robotics and entrepreneurship.

In 2025 an entity carried a public sector innovation remit, with no strategy or programme and nothing published on its results. There was no open source software policy or action plan for the public sector, and no body deciding on adopting or procuring open source.

Capacity

Literacy

In 2023 Gabon ranked 2nd of 54 African states on equality in education, scoring 76.6 of 100. It ranked 35th on education completion, with 28.0 of 100 in 2023. The completion score rose 4.1 points over 2014-2023.

In 2023 Gabon scored 42.7 of 100 on the Education sub-category, 40th of 54. That score changed by 1.0 point over 2014-2023, while the equality score fell 0.5.

In 2023 Gabon had no score for education enrolment or for human resources in education. The UNESCO data both indicators draw on carry no value for the country. In March 2025 the World Bank undertook to supply tablets, and UNESCO proposed software, for school data collection. By June 2026 UNICEF and Airtel Gabon had supplied primary schools with tablets and connectivity for learning content.

In 2025 no digital skills programme was freely available to citizens or to schools. No results on digital skills had been published.

Training and skills

In 2025 the World Bank recorded no government strategy or programme for digital skills in the public service. It found no freely available programme for citizens or schools, and no published results.

On 14 July 2026 Gabon signed a skills accord with Huawei Technologies Gabon, giving the company a technical role in the Kimba Connect start-up programme. The accord covers "salles intelligentes" for innovation, training in networks, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and software development, and access to Huawei's certifying training kits. Its terms, including room count, trainee target, cost and duration, have not been published.

In 2025 the public distance-learning Université Numérique du Gabon awarded state-recognised degrees in disciplines including computer science. The Université des Sciences et Techniques de Masuku awards diplomas from technician to doctoral level in technological and fundamental sciences. A 2025 workshop worked to embed UNESCO Global Skills Academy digital skills certifications into university curricula. It ran alongside the UNESCO China Funds-in-Trust programme for higher education. In 2025 a government job portal offered registration and job search. In September 2026 a connected-schools programme using a digital learning platform entered its scale-up phase, its pilot growing from nine schools to 69. Teacher training for the scale-up opened at Mouila.

DGDI and the tourism caravan's coordinators closed a series of e-Visa familiarisation sessions for the tourism trade in Libreville on 31 July 2026. The sessions were for tour-operator federations, guides, agencies, hotels and restaurants. The second national Cybersecurity Forum met in November 2025 on critical infrastructure. In 2025 three identified innovation hubs and incubators also supported founders.

Research institutions

The Université des Sciences et Techniques de Masuku has awarded diplomas from technician to doctoral level under the licence-master-doctorate system since 2007. It was founded in 1986, when the science faculty and the Libreville engineering school moved to Franceville. It calls itself the country's first scientific establishment.

Inclusion

Access to services

On 24 October 2026 the Haute Autorité de la Communication lifted the nationwide suspension of social media ordered on 17 February. At a Libreville press conference on 17 July 2026 the presidency had defended the suspension as temporary and conservatory. Digital freedom is among Gabon's most deteriorated governance indicators over the decade to 2023.

A gigabyte of mobile data cost about 4.09 US dollars in 2023, roughly 1% of monthly income per head. In December 2025 residential electricity at benchmark consumption took a low single-digit share of income per head.

Enrolment for the personal identification number (NIP) is compulsory and free for every natural person on Gabonese territory, citizens and non-nationals alike, and the 2022 civil registration law reaches foreign residents and people in transit. From the campaign's opening in April, the online channel for a NIP attestation repeatedly rejected applicants abroad. Officials put the rejections down to network saturation. Electronic identity card issuance began in Libreville in June 2024. In June 2026, 17,720 finished cards had not been collected by their holders. Public perception of how easy an identity document is to obtain improved markedly to 2023 but remains low.

In 2025 the World Bank found no national online portal or one-stop shop for public services, no government mobile application and no published performance data on online delivery. Sectoral portals for tax, for social insurance and pensions, and for jobs are in use. It found no platform for complaints or information requests on service delivery. It found no published service standard and no support for users with disabilities in the existing channels. In July 2026 the government said its register of economically weak citizens was not reliable enough to target social protection, and called a new census. In February 2026 the state-backed MaDigiPaie digital payment service reached taxi fares through TaxiGab.

Digital divides

The regulator put national telephone and internet coverage at 97% in 2024. In February 2024 some 1,253 villages, home to about 117,600 people or roughly 6.5% of the population, had no mobile or internet service of any kind. Internet and computer access is among Gabon's most improved governance indicators over the decade to 2023. Gabon scored 76.1 on the ITU's ICT Development Index in 2024, 11th in Africa.

The World Bank's June 2024 identity project document sets a target that half of personal identification number holders be women. It also sets rural coverage of the number to reach the national rate by 2026, from a 2020 baseline of zero. The GSMA's 2025 gender-gap survey covers twelve low and middle income countries, and Gabon is not one of them. No Gabon-specific measure of the gender gap in mobile access has been found.

In 2023 rural households reached the electricity grid at a small fraction of the urban rate. The government says forest cover means fibre cannot physically reach everywhere. In November 2025 the great majority of mobile connections ran on 3G or better. No measure of smartphone ownership in Gabon has been found. In 2025 the World Bank found no state strategy or programme to build digital skills, in the public service or for citizens and schools, and no published results on them. Gabon ranks near the top of the continent for equality of access to education. In July 2026 the ministry said its plan on skills was to strengthen the training institutions it oversees.

Data

National statistics

In August 2026 the Constitutional Court sent teams to all nine provinces to verify the census figures. On 27 August 2026 it homologated a population of 3,518,621. By 21 October 2026 the detailed report had not been made public. On 18 September 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted decrees amending the statistics institute's statutes and reorganising the National Statistics Council. A third decree reorganised the Special Fund for Statistics to reduce dependence on external financing.

In the ten years to 2023 Gabon ran no agricultural or business survey, one household survey, one labour survey and two health surveys. In the same period it held agricultural, business and population censuses. In 2023 data use and data products scored in the 50-69% band of the World Bank's statistical performance pillars. In 2023 statistical services, data sources and statistical infrastructure scored in the 20-49% band. The capacity of the statistical system scored 39.4 of 100 in 2023, 37th of 54 African states. That was a gain of 12.0 points over the decade, one of Gabon's ten most improved governance indicators. The second national strategy for the development of statistics, for 2023-2025, was validated in October 2023 at a cost of FCFA 50.26bn. No successor strategy has been found.

The 2024 IIAG profile gives no value for education enrolment, human resources in education or the rural economy sub-category. The economy ministry's social-indicators dashboard for 2013-2021 records difficulty obtaining statistics from many sectoral administrations. The 2022 health yearbook was compiled from data only 63% complete. An education yearbook was still at the planning stage in July 2024. In 2025 the state's financial management system recorded no expenditure linked to the Sustainable Development Goals. It carried no non-financial performance indicators for government programmes. Health reporting went national on DHIS2 in December 2024, fed by staff transcribing paper registers. In July 2026 the government said its register of economically weak citizens could not reliably target social protection, and planned a second census from September.

Open data

In 2025 the World Bank found no open data portal publishing government datasets for reuse. Disclosure of public records scored 11.7 of 100 in 2023, 49th of 54 African states. It is one of Gabon's ten worst governance indicators and barely changed across the decade. Accessibility of public records reached 21.2 of 100 in 2023, up 7.7 points over 2014-2023.

In Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory, government finance is Gabon's strongest category at 6 out of 10. National accounts, trade, labour, prices, poverty, population, education and health facilities score between 3 and 5, and energy 2.5. Four categories score zero: digital connectivity, built environment, pollution and food security. In those four, nothing meeting the inventory's coverage and openness tests is published.

Of the sixteen transparency tests the GovTech Maturity Index applies, Gabon fails fourteen (2025): nothing is published on cloud usage, interoperability compliance, public investment projects, digital skills results, data governance, data protection complaints, regulator performance, online service delivery, right-to-information implementation, public sector innovation, startup support, government responses to citizens or public service standards. On the two it passes — digital transformation progress and GovTech institutional performance — reports are produced and kept internal.

In 2025 Gabon had no right-to-information law, no body monitoring such a right, and no figures on requests received or granted. The 2023 data protection law lets an individual ask in writing, free of charge, for confirmation and a copy of data held about them. In 2025 the World Bank found no dedicated data governance entity and no data governance strategy or policy. It found nothing published on how government data is governed.

Use of satellite data

In July 2026 Gabon opened a national policy process on satellite communications at a seminar bringing together AGEOS, ANINF and the regulator ARCEP. The seminar was framed as digital sovereignty and produced strategic recommendations for government. The government's case rests on forest cover that fibre cannot reach past. Gabon hosts one of four regional stations retransmitting high-resolution meteorological satellite data, installed under the African Development Bank's SAWIDRA programme (2021). The space agency AGEOS built the first national reference land-cover map from SPOT and Sentinel-2 imagery (2021). The map put forest at 89% of the country in 2015. In March 2023 the agriculture ministry signed with 6th Grain to register every farmer digitally and monitor holdings by satellite.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In June 2026 ANINF and the US-based MITRE Corporation were jointly designing Gabon's operational and institutional cybersecurity architecture for critical digital infrastructure. The work was still at the design stage. The U.S. Department of State gave ANINF a cybersecurity capacity-building grant of about US$1.25 million, running 2024-2025. The grant was aimed at national cybersecurity frameworks, incident-response capability and digital resilience.

Gabon and Visa signed a partnership in May 2025 and held a follow-up meeting in Libreville that July. The partnership is directed at digitalising public services and modernising the state's collection of payments.

The World Bank is by some distance Gabon's principal digital financier of record, with close to US$200 million committed across four operations; the American grant is marginal beside it (August 2026).

China activities

On 14 July 2026 Gabon signed a skills accord with Huawei. It follows a broader digital-transformation memorandum with the company, signed in December 2025. On a ministerial mission to Huawei's sites in June 2025, Gabon had opened an exploration of a strengthened technology partnership. A 2024 memorandum with the company covered a smart-cities project and other digital services, with a large headline spending pledge. In October 2026 the civil service minister asked China's ambassador for expertise in digitalising the public service and training its staff. The ambassador offered to examine support.

Everything Chinese on Gabon's record is a memorandum rather than a financing agreement, including the largest single figure in the whole picture, which is a pledge (August 2026).

EU activities

In 2024 the French company IN Groupe was supplying Gabon's electronic identity card and its digital identity management software and hardware. As Imprimerie Nationale, the firm signed a five-year partnership with the documentation and immigration directorate in November 2015. It covered maintenance of the secure-title issuance platform the firm had delivered to the state in 2013. It also extended the main supply contract for passports, visas, residence permits and refugee cards.

Gulf/UAE activities

The Presidency records three memoranda Gabon signed in Abu Dhabi in February 2026, on mining, digital, and ports and logistics. The digital memorandum renews an existing one with Presight, a G42 subsidiary. It is to support digital transformation of the public administration and strategic State services using artificial intelligence, advanced analytics and big data. The Presidency frames it as reinforcing Gabon's digital sovereignty. It states no amount, term, deliverable or system name. No Gulf investment or data-centre commitment naming Gabon has been found.

India activities

In April 2026 India's High Commissioner offered scholarships for Gabonese public servants and a preparatory mission on digitalising the public administration. Gabon's civil service minister proposed framing the cooperation as a four-to-five-year capacity-building convention. India's April 2026 country brief records memoranda on holding a Joint Commission Meeting and on foreign-service training cooperation. It also records an investment memorandum between the Confederation of Indian Industry and Gabon's investment promotion agency. It lists a 2007 housing line of credit and ITEC and ICCR training places. The Pan-African e-network set up tele-education and tele-medicine links in Libreville. A draft memorandum on its e-VBAB successor was reviewed in December 2020. No signed Indian digital instrument or supplier contract naming Gabon has been found.

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 Gabon's data protection authority authorised the ST Digital data centre, with its main site and a mirror in Haut-Ogooué. It did so after an impact study covering the location, purpose and security of the data hosted. In July 2026 about 95 per cent of the data generated in Gabon was hosted abroad. ACE Gabon SA, a state company, is building a 160-rack Tier III carrier hotel linked to the submarine cables. It is financed by the local banking sector and due between late November and early December 2026.

The 2025 digitalisation ordinance requires public digitalisation contracts to carry local-preference or local technological inclusion clauses. The local start-up Zaghi Digital built the administration.ga platform with the Centre Gabon Innovation (August 2026). Ownership of its source code passes to the administration free of charge. No statement of where the IBOGA biometric register and the identity-card database are hosted, or under whose jurisdiction, had been found as at September 2026.

In September 2026, after a Chinese government delegation's visit, La Poste's Charbonnages site in Libreville was named as a possible AI data centre for the public administration. The proposal is conditional on a Gabon-China partnership being finalised. No published terms on data jurisdiction, portability or exit had been found as at September 2026 for the Presight memorandum, the Huawei memoranda or the IN Groupe identity-card supply.

In August 2026 Gabon hosted an Africa CDC workshop that set a roadmap for a convention on cross-border health-data sharing among the nine Central African member states.
