Compiled 2026-08-17 from the Data Landscapers source base, from outputs/reports/GAB/ledger.csv (102 systems and instruments, 27 of them Not held). Each section opens with its ledger; specific events are covered in the monthly updates. Figures are dated because most are time-varying.
Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Gabon's first data centre certified to the third tier entered service at Nkok on 3 July 2026, with 92 racks over 3,000 square metres and 1 MW installed for XAF 8bn (figures given at the inauguration). A second facility of 160 racks, built by a state company and financed by the local banking sector, is due between end-November and early December 2026 (ministerial site inspection). Both are hosting capacity; neither publishes utilisation.

The electronic identity card has run since June 2024 and stands at 335,384 enrolments, 164,944 cards produced and 78,500 files classed as disputed (the interior minister to the National Assembly, 2 June 2026). The data-protection statute of 2023 prohibits biometric processing outright, and the derogation that would permit it is cross-referenced in the gazetted text to the article on children's data (Loi n 025/2023). No authorisation of the card's processing by the data-protection authority is on file.

Social media has been suspended since February 2026 under a decision of the broadcasting authority whose text the base does not hold and whose start date is inferred from a five-month count (declaration of nine civil-society organisations, 11 July 2026).

The World Bank operation intended to build the digital state had disbursed about US$13.4m of US$68.5m when the project record was read on 24 June 2026 (project record), past an original closing date of 30 June 2026 for which no closure record exists.

Hosting is the part of the estate that moved. The Nkok facility is peered to the ACE and SAT-3/WACS submarine systems and draws about 22 per cent of its site energy from a photovoltaic plant, with water-free cooling and a dual 15 kV feed (technical account); the share is operator-stated rather than metered, and power usage effectiveness is not published. The operator states that about 95 per cent of data produced in Gabon was hosted abroad in 2026 — a claim by the party that built the alternative, which no independent measurement in the base tests.

Local interconnection has restarted. The national internet exchange point carried two members and about 80 Mbps of local traffic, unchanged since 2019, until the two large mobile operators reconnected on 13 May 2026 and traffic passed 250 Mbps (relaunch account); a national workshop with the Internet Society and AFRINIC ran from 3 to 8 August 2026 (opening). The member count after the relaunch has not been published.

Backbone reach is stated at 2,000 km of fibre, with 88 per cent forest cover named as the limit on extending it (satellite seminar) — a ministerial figure given at a seminar rather than a regulator or operator return. Satellite service is prohibited: the regulator declared in October 2025 that the low-earth-orbit operator holds no authorisation and that sale, installation and use are illegal (communique), and no seizure, fine or prosecution has followed on the record.

Cybersecurity is institutionally thin. The asset-inventory method launched in June 2025 is designed to feed a national security operations centre and incident response team (the method) that no source establishes exists, and architecture work with an external partner has defined twelve priority sectors without a completion date, cost or instrument (account). No national cybersecurity strategy is published. A sabotage attack on the state water and power utility SEEG collapsed about 95 per cent of its information systems on the night of 14-15 June 2026, taking down the prepaid-meter top-up platform (the collapse); by its 4 August progress briefing the utility described its network as healthy and secured, with billing and payment restored in Libreville and seven interior centres and restoration continuing elsewhere (SEEG's progress briefing). The account is the utility's own; no forensic, criminal or damage-cost finding is on record and the attackers are described only as unidentified.

The electronic identity card stands at 335,384 enrolments, with 92,614 files in processing and 78,500 disputed at 2 June 2026 (the minister's answer), and the figures rest on press accounts of a spoken reply rather than a published return. The digital identity system and the biometric register of natural persons have been in implementation workshops since June 2025 with nothing operating (the three projects). Who supplies the card was settled by reconcile on 2026-08-09: IN Groupe's own annual review lists an identity card and passport in Gabon among its delivered solutions, and the DGDI is the issuing authority that enrols and personalises in-house at Oloumi, as it does for the passport — two layers, not two suppliers. The Thales attribution carried by December 2023 reporting was a plan and is confirmed neither by Thales nor by any award; the Semlex contract was broken in 2015. No Gabonese procurement notice, award publication or gazette entry for any of this exists on the public record as searched on 2026-08-09, and none is held: the supplier is established by the supplier's own publication, not by the state's.

The domestic payment scheme launched in December 2025 with more than 1,000 codes deployed (launch communique) and became the pilot for the regional standard now in force. The regional switch carried 20.3m transactions worth FCFA 863bn across 153 participants in 2025 (2025 figures). Of 1,101,434 card transactions in June 2026, 62.41 per cent were cash withdrawals against 24.61 per cent terminal payments (bank association figures).

Inside government, the public finance system entered service in January 2026 connected to the Treasury (launch). An internal platform for secure messaging, meeting management and confidential document exchange between civil servants was presented to the Vice-President on 3 August 2026, built with a Gabonese start-up, with the state holding the source code at no cost and no contract, cost or timetable stated (presentation). The layer between systems is absent: the indicator for government entities securely exchanging data stands at zero (report).

The data-protection framework is in force and unenforced against the state. Loi n 025/2023 was published on 15 July 2023 and binds from publication, article 219 giving no compliance grace period (the gazetted law). Article 75 prohibits biometric processing outright, and article 76, which sets the derogation route, cross-references the article on children's data — a defect in the operative text rather than a reading of it. The authority created by article 7 has two dated acts on record: supervised destruction of unsuccessful bidders' files in January 2026 (account) and pre-commissioning compliance for the national data centre in June 2026 (account). It publishes no register of authorisations, declarations or sanctions.

Electronic government has a legal basis and no published text. An ordinance published on 7 September 2025 mandates digitisation, compulsory interconnection between administrations and a national-preference clause in digital public procurement (account); the base holds two secondary accounts and no article text or gazette reference. A second ordinance, of 8 April 2026, regulates digital platforms and is known only through a civil-society advocacy report that recommends revising it (the report).

The suspension of social media has run about five months. The Presidency describes it as temporary and conservatory; nine organisations describe it as unconstitutional and beyond the authority's remit and demand abrogation (their declaration). Both accounts are on the record, and the base holds neither the decision nor its date.

Two instruments are moving. Five implementing texts on the digitalisation of teaching were validated by consensus and forwarded to the competent authorities, not adopted (workshop close); eleven recommendations on satellite and space communications were adopted, with an integrated national strategy recommended rather than drafted (seminar close).

The national open-innovation programme launched on 14 July 2026 addresses a population its organisers put at 150 to 200 start-ups, and offers finance, technology, labelling and market access (launch). Two accords were signed the same day: one with a state guarantee company on credit guarantees, one with an equipment supplier on smart rooms and training in networks, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and software development (account of both). Neither carries a ceiling, coverage ratio, eligibility test, trainee target, cost or duration. The final set for 4 August 2026 was postponed on 6 August 2026, the ministry citing start-up concerns about the organisation of the first edition and giving no revised date (ministry statement). The labelling criteria are unheld, which leaves unstated how the ministry chooses the firms it labels.

The only instrument in the base whose stated subject is artificial intelligence is a protocol with Morocco on exchanging expertise and on capacity-building for digital skills, signed at an exhibition in April 2026 with no budget, term or named programme (account). Gabon has no published national artificial-intelligence strategy, no named deployed system and no compute figure for either data centre. The digital economy minister has argued in his own commentary that the greater risk is joining the technology too early rather than missing it, and named a forthcoming start-up and digital enterprise act as the sequencing instrument — an instrument no Gabonese primary source in the base names.

Social protection has a register at first-deliverable stage. Technical work on the unified social register closed in August 2025, and the first deliverable was presented on 29 July 2026 alongside a commitment to full digitalisation of non-contributory social protection under the 2026-2030 development plan (presentation). Twenty-eight of this ledger's rows carry no reliable statement of status. Where they cluster is itself the finding.

The heaviest cluster is artificial intelligence and the technology sector, with seven unheld rows: no national strategy, no named deployed system, no compute figure, no labelling register, no measured sector weight, and no Indian or Gulf instrument of any kind. The second is inclusion and capacity, where the universal service fund, a named digital-literacy programme, the institutions the ministry supervises, the connected-schools programme, the gender divide, the rural and urban access split, the enacted appropriation and any named research funding are all absent.

Three unheld rows bear directly on positions this report does state. The authority's authorisation for biometric identity processing would settle whether a three-year-old programme operates lawfully under a statute that prohibits it by default. The national incident response team and security operations centre are the stated destination of an inventory method already in force. And the grid supply position is unestablished against the hosting load committed by the end of 2026.

Each row is named in gaps.csv with the document, dataset or statement that would close it.
