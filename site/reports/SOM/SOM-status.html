This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

The Digital Transformation Strategy for 2025-2030 was validated at a national workshop in October 2025 with ITU support. Its November 2025 text, published by the communications authority, sets seven pillars and about 40 initiatives. No cabinet adoption has been found. It is meant to coordinate every national digital initiative, interoperability included. It is drawn as a single national framework covering education, health, agriculture and finance. The National ICT Policy and Strategy 2019-2024 had set Somalia's positions on broadband, cybersecurity, data centres and cloud services, data protection and digital payments until 2024. No separate data exchange strategy has been found.

The Ministry of Communications and Technology issued an eGovernment Strategy for 2025-2029 in August 2025. It carries the plan for government hosting and digital service delivery. The Council of Ministers approved an Inclusive Digital Policy in January 2025. A National Data Policy Framework was validated in March 2026. It covers how data is collected, shared, classified and retained, and its sovereignty. On 27 October 2026 the Prime Minister's Office and the communications ministry held a workshop to validate a National Digital Coordination Framework for federal government ICT.

The National Trade Facilitation Committee held a two-day induction and roadmap workshop in Mogadishu on 3 August 2026. Sadaq Mohamed, National Trade Facilitation Coordinator in the Office of the Prime Minister, leads the work. The committee mapped nineteen priority legal and policy instruments to run alongside the trade-facilitation digital systems. They include rules of origin, competition and consumer protection, special economic zones and intellectual property. No timetable, budget or vendor has been stated for any of those systems.

Somalia scored 25.5 out of 100 for overall governance in 2023, 53rd of 54 African states. That followed a ten-year gain of 6.8 points. It sat below the continental average on all sixteen sub-categories, and in the bottom ten on thirteen. It was above the African average on only six of the index's 96 indicators. GDP per capita was USD 643.8 in 2023, 46th of 54.

Legislation and regulation

Law No. 009 of 2023 gives citizens a right to an identity credential, imposes no obligation to obtain an ID and criminalises only fraud and obstruction. On that ground, a published legal reading argues that the September 2025 presidential proclamation ordering universal use turned a right into an obligation without legislative debate. Law No. 41 of December 2020 created DADSOM. The Identification and Registration Act of March 2023 established the National Identification and Registration Authority. The identification and data-protection laws both took effect in 2023. Law 46 of 2026, on population registration and civil status statistics, was published in the Official Bulletin on 5 April 2026.

Telecommunications rests on the National Communications Law of 2017. It provides for broadband, spectrum management including TV white spaces, and universal access. On 21 October 2026 the House of the People approved the communications ministry's Digital Transactions Bill, covering digital transactions and mobile money, by 137 votes. No record of its Senate stage has been found. On 11 January 2026 the Upper House passed the National Payment System Bill, which lets the central bank license and supervise payment systems. No record of its presidential signature has been found. As at December 2025 the Cybercrimes Bill had completed its first reading in the House of the People and been referred to committee for a second. Parliament approved the Cybersecurity Law on 26 January 2026. A six-chapter cybersecurity bill had its first reading in the Upper House on 29 August 2026, short of the second and third readings that final approval requires. A technical working group opened in June 2026 to develop a submarine-cable regulatory framework, alongside outage management and competition rules.

A cross-sector Data Protection Act has been in force since March 2023. The 2025 audit recorded 66 legal-compliance breaches by government bodies. They included US$18.5m of contracts awarded in 2025 without following committee procedures. In October 2026 a draft Penal Code to replace the 1962 code was before Parliament, with provisions for offences involving digital platforms and information systems. No timetable for a final vote had been set.

Data protection

In June 2026 Somalia's Data Protection Authority issued a notice under Article 32 of the Data Protection Act requiring all data controllers and processors to complete mandatory registration. It began accepting applications and issuing compliance certificates, the regulator's first nationwide compliance step.

The statute behind it, Law No. 005 of 2023, has been in force since 23 March 2023. It is grounded in Article 19 of the 2012 Provisional Constitution. It is a general cross-sector instrument, setting out lawful bases for processing, data-subject rights, data-protection impact assessment and data-protection-officer duties. It sets a registration requirement for "controllers of major importance", alongside cross-border transfer restrictions and breach notification duties. Article 25 gives controllers 72 hours from becoming aware of a breach likely to risk rights and freedoms to notify the Authority. It requires notice to affected individuals where the risk is high, and breaches carry fines of 2 to 4 per cent of worldwide annual turnover under the 2023 Act. The Authority opened an online channel for reporting breaches in June 2026.

The Authority was inaugurated on 24 February 2024. It has power to issue regulations, investigate, impose fines and register controllers and processors of major importance. It has since issued implementation guidance and an alternative dispute resolution procedure. Its leadership is appointed by the executive. The Council of Ministers approved the regulatory framework operationalising the Act on 8 January 2026. Somalia imposes no data localisation requirement on personal, government, financial or critical infrastructure data. It applies instead an adequacy-based cross-border transfer regime. Under it, controllers assess protection in the receiving jurisdiction or rely on contractual safeguards or consent. Redress runs through the constitutional right the Act rests on and the rights of access, rectification, deletion and objection it grants. It also runs through the alternative dispute resolution procedure issued under Article 41. In payments, conduct duties rest on the Central Bank's Mobile Money Regulations as amended in 2020, with no comprehensive payments consumer protection statute or formal redress framework.

Regional collaboration

In July 2026 East African Community bodies had approved regional rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection. The rules must still clear the Community's decision-making organs before any partner state adopts them. No adoption date has been found. Somalia has been a full EAC member since March 2024, one of the bloc's eight partner states.

Somalia signed ratification of the African Continental Free Trade Area in early August 2026. Later that month the AfCFTA Secretariat agreed in Mogadishu that Somalia would become the 50th state party on depositing its instrument in September 2026. The agreed sequence runs through the electronic tariff book, preferential tariffs in the customs administration, outstanding EAC commitments and a first shipment. The continental payment system and the AfCFTA Adjustment Fund were named as the instruments available.

In July 2026 the EAC Monetary Affairs Committee held the single-currency target at 2031. It recorded that no partner state had met all four primary convergence criteria. Somalia belongs to COMESA, CEN-SAD and IGAD. It scored 38.5 out of 100 on regional integration in 2023, 35th of 54, up 7.6 points over the decade.

In September 2025 Puntland and Jubbaland declined to implement the federal national ID. Puntland created its own identification authority, accusing Mogadishu of overreach on data control, election influence and regional autonomy. A 15-member delegation's ten-day mission to Pakistan on digital identity closed in September 2026 with three working groups and an action matrix. A delegation studied Tanzania's labour and employment digital systems in August 2026.

Standards

In April 2026 a Somali analysis named the risk of digitising the existing fragmentation. It called for role clarity, interoperability standards and accountability rather than more procurement. The World Bank found no whole-of-government data exchange platform and no shared enterprise architecture or interoperability layer. In June 2025 NIRA's Director General said siloed databases and the absence of interoperability standards remained a major obstacle.

In July 2026 the Central Bank said the instant payment system runs on the ISO 20022 messaging standard. A national standardised QR code is layered on it. Hormuud's EVC Plus was re-certified to the GSMA Mobile Money standard in August 2025.

In August 2026 the official charged with trade delivery said clear mandates and compatible procedures must accompany the digital systems. Automating confusion, he wrote, would move the same problems online. In August 2026 the auditor general's office completed the third phase of a cooperation programme on auditing government information systems. The national bureau of standards is Somalia's member body of the International Organization for Standardization (2026).

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Media freedom is among Somalia's ten most deteriorated governance indicators, having fallen 6.7 points over 2014-2023 to 38.9 out of 100, 44th of 54 African states. Freedom of expression and belief scores 19.3 out of 100 in 2023, 49th of 54. It fell 0.3 points across the decade. Digital freedom scores 39.7, 39th of 54, equally flat over the same period. In December 2025 a civil-society coalition said Somalia still lacked a national social media policy and called for one to be finalised.

The Digital Transformation Strategy went to a national multi-stakeholder workshop in October 2025. The National Communications Authority convened a national consultation in April 2026 to develop a cybersecurity risk-management and compliance framework. In March 2024 the Speaker launched a digital platform, built by the constitutional review commission, for citizens to comment on proposed constitutional amendments. Starlink's entry was fast-tracked and its full foreign-ownership model accommodated without major public debate (August 2025). The information ministry said on 3 September 2026 that the federal government had closed 82,000 social media pages during the year. It alleges that the pages carried armed-group propaganda. Amnesty International reported in May 2026 that journalists in Somalia were attacked by security forces and subjected to threats, harassment, arbitrary arrests and prosecution. It said the government had stepped up attempts to control reporting on insecurity, corruption and forced evictions. On 19 October 2026 the information ministry warned that anyone using the media or social media to aid, finance or encourage terrorism would face the law. The journalists' syndicate reported a rise in journalists' accounts restricted and posts deleted after reporting on the conflict in South West State.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The central bank's 2024 accounts attribute a US$1.26m rise in system-licence costs to the Switch Payment System licence. The World Bank previously paid that licence; the bank now pays it from its own budget.

The treasury's operating budget carries 15.4% of the digital lines of the 2026 appropriation, US$6.2m of US$40.6m, against 10.4% in 2025; external project grants carry the rest.

The 2026 appropriation carries US$8.0m for the World Bank's SCALED-UP grant and US$13.6m for the regional digital integration project EA-RDIP. In 2025 the two carried US$36.3m and US$11.8m. The treasury's own 2026 lines are running costs: US$2.07m for the identification authority, US$1.32m for the communications ministry and US$0.68m for the regulator. The 2026 lines also include US$0.24m for the Data Protection Authority and US$0.18m for government digitalisation.

In 2025 the identification authority spent US$1.29m of its US$2.03m and the communications ministry 97% of its line. In 2025 EA-RDIP disbursed US$3.12m of its US$11.79m, and the Data Protection Authority's US$1.83m project budget disbursed nothing. US$10m of EA-RDIP's financing for Somalia was cancelled in April 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

The EU and Germany's BMZ co-financed the D4D Initiative for Digital Government and Cybersecurity for the Horn of Africa, a EUR 3 million programme that ran from 2022 to 2025. It covered Somalia, Kenya and Djibouti. GIZ led on e-government, Expertise France on cybersecurity and FIIAPP on change management. In March 2026 Somalia's cybersecurity build-out rested on partnerships with Turkey, Malaysia and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime. No financing figure has been found for those partnerships.

New investments

No other institution comes close to the World Bank: six of the 33 digital-sector commitments on record are its own, about USD 250 million of the roughly USD 300 million on record, around 83 per cent (November 2026). The 33 started between 2015 and 2026 and run to 2030. They comprise 27 grants, four concessional loans and two technical assistance operations, with no equity or guarantees; all four loans are the World Bank's (November 2026). European financiers hold eight of the 33 but only about USD 31 million of the value (November 2026). Statistical capacity is the largest destination by number, taking 12, ahead of geospatial data, government systems and sectoral management systems with four each.

The World Bank approved the SCALED-UP credit of USD 31 million in March 2019. It added USD 70 million in June 2021. The project runs to 2026 across digital skills, financial services and job creation. Digital ID is a major pillar of the World Bank's SPRING initiative, a programme of over USD 100 million (December 2025). Somalia and South Sudan are the two sovereign borrowers under the USD 162 million Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, approved in May 2023. A USD 50 million public financial management project, closed in 2023, rolled IFMIS out across the federal government, Puntland and Somaliland. What it spent on the systems themselves has not been found. A USD 15 million trust-funded grant, closed in 2023, built the telecom policy and regulatory environment.

In 2024 the European Union committed EUR 4.5 million (about USD 5 million) of IT equipment to security and justice institutions, to 2028. Sweden funds SEK 45 million (about USD 5 million) for the DHIS2 national health information system to 2026. The UK funds GBP 3,312,013 (about USD 5 million) for statistical capacity to 2026. The African Development Bank funds XDR 3.0 million (about USD 4 million) of statistics work to 2026.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Mobile Communications is Somalia's tenth best-scoring indicator on the Ibrahim Index at 45.0 out of 100 in 2023. It rose 20.8 points over the decade and is among the country's ten most improved measures. The same scorecard puts Internet & Computers at 21.9 out of 100, 43rd of 54, on a decade gain of just 6.1 points. The broader Infrastructure sub-category scores 21.5 out of 100, 49th of 54 and below the African average, having moved 6.4 points in ten years.

About 27.9 per cent of the population used the internet in 2024 on ITU data. Unique mobile subscriber penetration sat below 40 per cent (2023), well under the raw subscription count that multi-SIM ownership inflates. Hormuud, the dominant operator, said in June 2026 that 4G covers more than 70 per cent of the population. It said nearly half its own device base still uses feature phones.

Satellite has filled part of the rural gap since Starlink was licensed in April 2025. AstaanConnect became the first official Somali reseller in September 2025, selling and installing locally on plans from $36.90 a month. In February 2024 the regulator listed four submarine cables serving Somalia: Gulf2Africa, DARE, PEACE and EASSy. Fixed capacity lands at Mogadishu, Berbera and Bosaso, but outside the cities last-mile electricity supply constrains what any of it can serve. Traffic exchange inside the country runs through an exchange point in Mogadishu, established on 20 November 2018 and listed as active (October 2026).

The National Communications Authority is accelerating national migration to IPv6 under a strategy adopted in February 2025, ahead of IPv4 exhaustion. In June 2026 it opened, with the IFC, Somalia's first Submarine Cable Landing Technical Working Group, meeting in Nairobi under the IFC Broadband Infrastructure Project. Voice calls interconnect across six licensed operators under a 2023 agreement, while mobile-money wallets remain closed to one another (October 2026). Berbera is being positioned as an alternative regional gateway (September 2026). Its capacity and landing arrangements have not been found.

Data Storage

In April 2024 the communications ministry said the National Data Center being built in Mogadishu would "eliminate dependencies of national data on international data storage solutions, that is stored outside the country". It repeated the rationale in May 2025. The centre is intended as the central repository for the data of all government institutions. After a site visit on 6 May 2025 the minister described construction as nearing completion. On 7 August 2026 the State Minister for Communications described the centre as planned and not yet built.

In March 2025 Pakistan's NADRA delivered data centre infrastructure, ID card personalisation machines and secure ID cards for the national identification system. NIRA holds enrollees' fingerprints and facial images in a centralised government database. In November 2025 Hormuud's chairman put its estate at 11 data centres with 10 megawatts of combined capacity. Some drew up to 95 per cent of daytime energy from solar in 2025. A national infrastructure provider advertises off-site backup in Mombasa and Mogadishu with real-time replication (August 2026). It also offers colocation in its own data centres in Mogadishu and Nairobi on a 10 Gbps backbone (October 2026).

Wingu Africa's carrier-neutral Tier III data centre at Berbera in Somaliland was commissioned in 2021, outside federal jurisdiction. No hyperscale provider operates a cloud region in Somalia; the nearest are in Bahrain, the UAE and Qatar. Somalia imposes no data localisation requirement and runs an adequacy-based cross-border transfer regime under the Data Protection Act.

Energy

Electricity averaged about USD 0.61 per kWh in 2023 and exceeded USD 1 where supply came from private diesel micro-grids. The comparable figures were roughly USD 0.15 in Kenya and USD 0.06 in Ethiopia.

Around 50.3 per cent of the population had access to electricity in 2023. In January 2026 the National Bureau of Statistics published the country's first official SDG 7 energy indicators report. It draws on the energy ministry, the national electricity authority and World Bank data. Access to Energy scores 26.0 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index for 2023, 37th of 54, and moved 0.5 points in a decade.

Supply was overwhelmingly diesel in 2023, with solar and other renewables a minority of generation. The Ministry of Energy and Water Resources is developing a National Mini-grid Policy and Regulation with UNDP and GEF support. Deployment runs through projects including an AfDB-backed solar and grid programme in Bosaso. The World Bank's Somalia Enterprise Survey, fielded from June to November 2025, asks firms about infrastructure and services. No published measure of supply reliability has been found.

In February 2026 staff at Mogadishu's Electronic Immunization Register still recorded on paper and uploaded later, because of power cuts and unreliable connectivity.

Technical Capacity

Public Administration gained 20.1 points on the Ibrahim Index over 2014-2023 to reach 30.1 out of 100. That was the largest gain of Somalia's sixteen sub-categories. It covers civil registration, statistical capacity, effective administration, tax and revenue mobilisation and budgetary management. It ranks 48th of 54, as does Effective Administration on 30.7 out of 100 for 2023, up 15.9 points.

In May 2025 NIRA's Director-General said HUBIYE, the Certificate Delivery System and the eAqoonsi mobile ID were "entirely developed by Somali engineers at NIRA". The identity system's core technology and hardware come from Pakistan's NADRA, its primary technology partner.

In June 2026 a National IPv6 Center hosted at Jamhuriya University was supporting the national IPv6 migration. SOMCIRT opened in March 2026 to coordinate prevention, detection and response across institutions. In 2023 developers outside government organised in communities such as Dugsiiye and Somalis in Tech.

Cybersecurity

Parliament approved the Cybersecurity Law on 26 January 2026. It gives policy responsibility to the communications ministry, technical oversight to the National Communications Authority and compliance duties to critical-infrastructure operators. The bill had its first reading in the Upper House on 29 August 2026. The Somalia Computer Incident Response Team, mandated by that law, became operational on 7 March 2026. Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre opened it to coordinate prevention, detection and response and to issue national alerts. Before it, Somalia scored 37.39 out of 100 in the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, placing it in Tier 4 of five.

In April 2026 the National Communications Authority convened a national consultation to build a cybersecurity risk-management and compliance framework. It covers risk identification, regulatory compliance and institutional responsibilities. The Cybercrimes Bill completed its first reading in December 2025 and was referred to committee for a second. The Data Protection Act's 72-hour breach notification duty is in force. The statistics bureau found no national records or reports on cybercrime when compiling its 2024 governance statistics. A draft Penal Code with provisions for digital offences was before Parliament in October 2026.

Somalia signed a cybersecurity cooperation memorandum with Turkey on 10 February 2026 and has closer ties with Malaysia and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime. Before that, the EU and Germany financed the EUR 3 million D4D Initiative for Digital Government and Cybersecurity for the Horn of Africa, which ran to 2025. The Ministry of Finance tendered for a firm to assess security at NIRA headquarters and registration centres. No results of that assessment have been found.

DPI

Data Exchange

The Auditor-General's 2025 annual audit found seven revenue and financial-management systems not integrated with the Somali Financial Management Information System. They are business registration, e-tax, road tax, rental income tax, e-visa, non-tax e-revenue and digital financial services collection. A January 2025 IT audit of the customs system found no integration with the government's other revenue-collection systems. The World Bank found no whole-of-government data exchange platform and no primary legislation giving authority for cross-government data sharing.

In July 2026 the mobile money providers were still not integrated into the national instant payment system, and no date had been published.

In 2025 integration of the digital civil registration system with the national ID, health, immigration and education systems was under development with UNICEF and German support. In October 2025 government and humanitarian coordination documents described linking the Unified Social Registry to the identity system as a future goal.

Somalia held its first National Digital Public Infrastructure Summit in Mogadishu on 14-15 February 2026. It brought the Central Bank, the communications authority, NIRA and the Data Protection Authority together to map interoperability. NIRA opened a National ID and Public Service Integration Working Group with UNDP in June 2026. In August 2026 the accountant general's office moved to link the government payroll system with the national identity database. A pilot across five institutions runs from 1 October 2026, with full implementation targeted for 31 December 2027. On 16 October 2026 the communications ministry invited firms to build a government API gateway, single sign-on and a national digital government portal. The regional digital integration project finances that work.

Digital Identity and CRVS

The identity authority counted more than 1.4 million people registered for the national ID in March 2026. The interior minister counted more than 1.5 million in September 2026. About 700,000 cards had been issued by 31 March 2026. The programme targets 15 million issuances by the end of 2029. In June 2025 NIRA's director general said about 85 per cent of citizens held no legal identity. The eAqoonsi app had passed 50,000 downloads by August 2026, and activation still requires an in-person visit to a NIRA centre.

The National Identification and Registration Act of March 2023 created NIRA and set its mandate, eligibility rules and biometric requirements. Pakistan's NADRA supplies the core identity system and hardware. In late October 2026 NIRA put the consent-based HUBIYE verification platform into operation. UNDP put it at 25 integrated services and about 1.2 million citizens in May 2026.

An April 2025 prime ministerial directive makes enrolment mandatory and requires institutions to integrate the ID into service delivery. A November 2024 resolution made it compulsory for secondary students and teachers. It is required for bank accounts, driver's licences and vehicle registration, and from March 2026 for travel between federal states. In September 2025 the president ordered every agency and business to use it. Two federal member states have declined to implement the federal ID. The travel rule was enforced from 1 October 2026, when passengers at Garowe in Puntland were refused boarding. NIRA keeps no registration offices there.

In 2020 around 3.5 per cent of children under five were registered and 0.3 per cent held a birth certificate. A digital civil registration service for births, deaths, marriages and divorces was piloted in 19 districts on 10 August 2025. By January 2026 it reached around 30 districts and 160 linked hospitals, with more than 28,000 births registered where they occurred. SIM card access is to depend on the national identity credential. In September 2026 the deputy prime minister asked for social media accounts to require the national identity number. Cabinet approved a third-generation e-passport plan on 6 August 2026, with no issuance date.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Integration of the mobile money providers into SIPS remains outstanding, with no published date (July 2026). Mobile money is worth roughly $8bn a year, about 36 per cent of GDP, across some 650 million transactions annually (January 2026). Private operators Hormuud and Telesom built it almost entirely, in the absence of state provision. The central bank's 2026 financial access survey, its first nationally representative one, found 87 per cent of adults using mobile money and 19 per cent holding a bank account.

SIPS launched in January 2025 as the first nationwide instant payment system. It carries instant transfers from mobile and banking apps and QR payments under a national standard, SOMQR. It is operated by the Somalia Payment Switch, a joint venture between the Central Bank of Somalia and 13 commercial banks. It already carries person-to-person, merchant, government-to-person and person-to-government payments (July 2026). Business-facing services are still in development. The Central Bank signed the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System membership agreement in March 2026 and is undertaking technical onboarding. Somalia is listed as "live soon" and not yet live (June 2026).

The non-formalised, non-security wage bill fell from 36.3 per cent in 2021 to 0.03 per cent by the end of 2025, so salaries, the dominant government-to-person flow, now move almost entirely through digital channels. A 2022 Public Financial Management Regulation requires all public-sector payroll to be paid by electronic funds transfer. Sales tax is deducted at 5 per cent from businesses using mobile-money merchant accounts through the Electronic Tax Administration System (2024). Rental-income tax collection is digitised too. Customs duties cleared under the Somali Customs Automated System still require a manual bank receipt and cashier step (2024). The safety-net programme's regular window pays 50,000 of the poorest households in 21 districts US$20 a month, quarterly, by mobile money (2025).

Hormuud's EVC Plus holds GSMA Mobile Money re-certification (August 2025). It is one of only about ten platforms worldwide to meet the standard, alongside M-PESA and MTN MoMo. Consumer protection rests on the Central Bank's Mobile Money Regulations as amended in 2020, with no comprehensive payments statute behind them.

Registries

Between April and September 2025 the electoral commission registered roughly 919,000 to one million voters in Mogadishu and Banadir. It registered about 216,000 in Galmudug ahead of a vote set for 20 July 2026; Puntland and Jubaland stayed outside. Registration uses the commission's own biometric fingerprint capture, on infrastructure built by NOVA GROUP (2025). Registrants present a passport, national ID card or birth certificate.

Since March 2022 the commerce ministry has run an online business registration and licensing portal, built with IFC and World Bank support and extended to Jubaland in 2024. The 2024/25 establishment census counted roughly 48,000 formal business establishments.

In September 2025 the Unified Social Registry was operational and used to target Baxnaano cash-transfer beneficiaries. Targeting combines a proxy means test with district distress ratings built from food insecurity, malnutrition and rural concentration. In October 2025 linking it to the national identification system was planned but not yet operational. The Revenue Directorate runs a digital tax administration for individual and corporate taxpayers, alongside the Somali Customs Automated System (2025).

In December 2024 Somalia had no unified national digital land or title register. Garowe and Bosaso register ownership and transfers locally, and UN-Habitat GIS tax databases cover five towns. By June 2025, 1,150 displaced families resettled in Baidoa and 1,000 in Garowe held title deeds, each plot registered by the municipal land registry. The largest of those databases carries no ownership information. In June 2024 the communications minister announced a Somali Postal Service with a digital addressing system. By December 2024 a diaspora startup had piloted digital addresses in several cities under memoranda with four states and regions. The migration agency, the federal government and the Netherlands launched a programme in 2026 to support civil registration and legal identity. The first unified national registry of health facilities was launched in Mogadishu on 23 August 2026. In September 2026 Somaliland's central bank agreed with banks and mobile-money operators to lead a credit information bureau.

Sectoral management information systems

In 2025 the Revenue Directorate was still developing and procuring a fuller Integrated Tax Administration System. Until then the Financial Management Information System provides only basic revenue receipting and taxpayer numbers for inland revenue.

In August 2025 UNICEF and the University of Oslo's HISP Centre agreed a further three years of support for the national DHIS2 health platform. An Electronic Immunization Register built on it gives each vaccinated child a unique identifier. In February 2026 it had been piloted in 14 Banadir facilities and was being scaled to more than 80. By 2026 the CaafimaadPlus system had grown from a five-facility pilot to more than 40 facilities in nine regions, feeding DHIS2 in real time. In 2022 most health facilities recorded patient data in twelve paper registers, summarised monthly and keyed into DHIS2 by district officers. The health ministry counted 579 public health facilities reporting to DHIS2 in July to September 2023, against 532 a year earlier.

In 2024 the national Education Management Information System collected school data once a year, and headteachers filed school records by hand. An Integrated Electronic Case Management System launched on 9 October 2025 with US funding and IDLO support. It links the Criminal Investigation Department, the Attorney General's Office, the Supreme Court, Benadir courts and Mogadishu Central Prison, with phased deployment. In February 2026 the police announced a second phase linking 20 Banadir police stations with investigators, prosecutors and courts.

On the Ibrahim Index, Tax and Revenue Mobilisation rose 22.2 points over 2014-2023 to 34.7 out of 100, 40th of 54. Budgetary and Financial Management rose 20.8 points to 37.5, 46th of 54. On 8 September 2026 the planning ministry launched a system for monitoring plan implementation across all 26 federal ministries. A Pandemic Fund project on health emergency preparedness was launched in Mogadishu on 20 August 2026.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Somalia scored 0.1468 on the UN E-Government Development Index for 2024, ranking 191st of 193 member states. That placed it in the index's low group. Since June 2025 the Ministry of Transport and Aviation has required a NIRA-issued national ID card to obtain a driver's licence, register a vehicle or use any other service it offers. The rule took effect immediately, consistent with the April 2025 prime ministerial order.

The Somali National Job Portal for Employment Opportunities is a centralised, domestically hosted platform (February 2026). Public-sector vacancies are advertised and applied for through it. Halmeel, the unified public service delivery centre, was created by Cabinet resolution on 23 April 2026. It plans to launch a single digital platform for about 70 government services by the end of March 2027, with the national ID as its only credential. Until then it serves people at a physical centre in Mogadishu. The House of the People suspended sittings for two weeks from 31 October 2026 to install an e-Parliament system for members' online requests, digital attendance and electronic voting. The Revenue Directorate offers downloadable personal income, corporate income and withholding tax return forms for manual submission. No electronic filing is linked to the QR payment channel that already accepts tax and fee payments. At state level Hirshabelle has replaced a manual revenue voucher reconciliation cycle of 30 to 45 days with automatic reconciliation in its digitised tax collection system (July 2026).

The ministry's eGovernment Strategy for 2025-2029 sets out the plan for domestic hosting and digital service delivery. The National Data Centre meant to carry it was not yet operational at the responsible minister's site visit in May 2025. In August 2026 it was described as planned and not yet built. No timetable, budget or vendor has been stated for any of Somalia's named trade-facilitation digital systems, including the planned National Single Window (August 2026). The federal government was negotiating in August 2026 to exempt one region's residents from the electronic visa fee while keeping the entry system in place.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In July 2026 Hirshabelle's SUMER platform, built by the Mogadishu firm Tabaarak ICT, was collecting checkpoint revenue through card terminals. The terminal holds the tariff schedule: the officer enters vehicle type, origin and category, and the device sets the amount due. Payment moves by mobile money straight into the government account, and a QR-coded receipt posts to a central system in real time.

Before SUMER, paper vouchers were carried up to 90km and keyed in by hand (July 2026). The state finance minister put reconciliation at 30 to 45 days, and shilling cash sat 48 hours in the treasury before deposit. The state reports a many-fold rise in collections at equipped checkpoints in the first quarter of 2026 against 2020.

Local administrations in five federal member states take part in the 19-district digital civil registration pilot announced in August 2025. In 2024 Garowe and Bosaso ran municipal land registration on their own, alongside UN-Habitat GIS tax databases in five towns.

In March 2021 a UN-Habitat drive in Baidoa mapped and registered 21,400 properties on a GIS and named and signed the roads. In August 2021 a UN agency reported that Garowe's local revenue had tripled on mobile tax, and that the scheme had been extended to four more districts. A 2023 governance brief found only Puntland councils running such a system. The UN programme's final report counts about 22,000 Garowe properties geo-referenced and paid by mobile money.

Rural digital data capture

In 2025 rural police stations kept occurrence books, station diaries, crime registers and property registers in hard copy. The police Basic Recruit Curriculum trains officers in paper books and forms, with no digital tool in it. In November 2026 the internal security ministry began designing a Data Analysis Centre to replace paper filing and pool police, immigration and intelligence data. In 2022 most health facilities wrote patient data in twelve standard paper registers, which district officers keyed into DHIS2 each month. In 2024 school records were still sorted and filed by hand. Headteachers in schools supported by a Global Partnership for Education programme have been trained to upload student data directly.

In 2026 a donor-run health programme ran in more than 40 facilities across nine regions, feeding DHIS2 in real time. It records medicine dispensing on handheld devices. In August 2025 the civil registration service put district offices in five federal member states online. E-agriculture has been a stated goal of the National ICT Policy and Strategy since 2019. No agricultural data system in use in the field has been found. About half the population had access to electricity in 2023, at some of the highest tariffs in Africa.

On the 2024 Ibrahim Index, Rural Land and Water Access scored zero out of 100 for 2023, 45th of 54. It is Somalia's most deteriorated indicator. Rural Economy Support stood at 26.5, 43rd, and Rural Representation and Participation at 24.5, 42nd, both unchanged across the decade. Rural Economy is one of only two sub-categories moving against the country's overall improvement.

Technology

AI

Somalia established a National AI Center in October 2024. In July 2026 it moved from the communications ministry to the Somali National University. It works on research, education and industry capacity-building. At the communications authority's request, UN-ESCWA advertised in 2025 for a consultant to draft a national AI strategy. None had been published as at June 2026. The East African Community is developing an AI policy roadmap to cover all eight partner states, Somalia among them. It was still under discussion as at March 2026. Somalia is covered by the bloc's German-funded AI research and training network, whose 2025 pilot reached 3,900 students across 100 universities. A national AI readiness assessment is under way with UNESCO (August 2026). A predictive food-security platform released in April 2026 informed assistance to 48,000 people. A Somali-built climate early-warning system is deployed with a state disaster-management ministry (September 2026).

In May 2026 a Mogadishu lab released a Somali speech-recognition model trained on 320 hours of audio. In January 2026 a university AI institute was preparing datasets and prototype text and speech models for public services and content moderation. In April 2026 Jamhuriya University and the University of Djibouti launched a Somali-language AI lab whose first project is a fake-news detector. In November 2026 the communications minister laid the foundation stone of a SIMAD building to house a four-university National AI Hub.

ICT Industry

Somalia's business and competition regulation scored 13.6 out of 100 in 2023, 53rd of 54 African countries. The score rose ten points over the decade. Hormuud and Telesom built the country's mobile money system in the absence of state provision. Hormuud's EVC Plus holds GSMA mobile money re-certification. In May 2026 Hormuud launched smartphone financing that scores customers on SIM tenure and mobile-money spending. In 2023 the domestic software developer community was small, organised around groups such as Dugsiiye and Somalis in Tech.

Innovation ecosystem

Somalia is not among the 133 economies covered by the Global Innovation Index 2024, so it has no rank or score. In 2022 the hub network included iRise Hub, HarHub, Ganacsikaab, Tilmaam i-HUB and SIMAD-iLab. The hubs are concentrated in Mogadishu, Hargeisa and Garowe. Commercial tracking records several hundred Somalia-linked startups, several dozen with outside funding. Fresh rounds closed in the first quarter of 2026. The World Bank's SCALED-UP project supports digital entrepreneurship, skills and ICT sector development to 2026.

Capacity

Literacy

Education is Somalia's worst-scoring governance sub-category, at 10.0 out of 100 for 2023, last of 54 African states. It rose 8.9 points over the preceding decade. Enrolment scores 4.4, 51st, and equality in education 8.8, 53rd. Education quality moved furthest, from zero to 16.7 over the same ten years.

In 2024 schools kept their records on paper, and the national education management information system collected school data once a year.

Training and skills

Between 28 March and 8 May 2026, government ICT staff took internationally certified cybersecurity training in Mogadishu and Nairobi. The National Communications Authority ran it in partnership with the World Bank Group. It covered incident management and response, cloud data security and the auditing of security policies. On 17 October 2026 the communications ministry's National Institute of Telecommunications and Technology began digital skills training for government employees. It opened with a workshop on data protection. In October 2026 the communications minister said a National Cybersecurity Academy would be established later in the year. Other training sits inside infrastructure programmes: the national incident-response team (March 2026) and the technical base for IPv6 migration hosted at Jamhuriya University (June 2026). The World Bank's SCALED-UP work carries digital skills as one strand, alongside financial inclusion, entrepreneurship and ICT sector development.

Somalia is named among the eight member states of the East African Community's regional AI research and training network (August 2026). Germany's BMZ funds it with EUR 5 million (August 2026). It is intended to equip 50,000 young people with digital skills, alongside an EAC artificial intelligence strategy for 2025-2028. Mogadishu University has run a faculty of computer science and information technology since 2003. Computer science and IT was the largest field among 25,230 first-degree graduates from 69 universities in 2022, at 3,136.

A technology firm and the national AI centre signed a memorandum to set up an AI Academy in Mogadishu for continuous AI training and internships (November 2026).

Research institutions

Somalia established a National AI Center in October 2024 with a remit of research, education and industry capacity-building. It moved from the communications ministry to the Somali National University in July 2026. Somalia is one of eight member states covered by the East African Community's AI research and training network (August 2026). Germany's BMZ funds the network with EUR 5 million (2026). Its 2025 pilot reached 3,900 students across 100 universities, alongside an EAC AI strategy for 2025-2028. In October 2026 Mogadishu University said it had introduced a specialisation in cybersecurity and artificial intelligence.

Inclusion

Access to services

Somalia scores 14.2 out of 100 on equal access to public services and 20.1 on equal socioeconomic opportunity (2023). It ranks 50th and 48th of 54 African countries on those measures. The first has risen by under two points in a decade (2023). Obtaining a service often requires a document most people do not hold. The great majority of Somalis have no legal identity (June 2025). Birth registration has been close to absent (2020). Even the mobile ID app requires an in-person visit to a NIRA centre before it can be activated (April 2026). A one-month pilot in Baidoa funded by UNDP registered 38,026 displaced people for the national identity card, with the fee waived (2025). For some groups the document barrier is written into the rules. Refugees and asylum seekers cannot open a formal bank account in Somalia. Neither the federal anti-money laundering framework nor Somaliland's 2019 AML/CFT Act recognises refugee identity documents as valid customer due diligence evidence. The 2015 Money Transfer Businesses customer registration regulations leave them tiered access to lower-threshold mobile money accounts (2022). Those accounts are the only formal channel open to them. Provision for disabled users is charitable rather than designed. A diaspora organisation distributes speaking mobile phones to blind people in Mogadishu to support mobile banking use (2025).

Formal banking touches a small minority of Somalis while mobile money reaches most of them (2026). Government charges and humanitarian cash alike ride on operator infrastructure. Social assistance is paid into mobile wallets (2024). Cost sorts the rest. Mobile data is relatively inexpensive at around $0.50 per GB (2025). The satellite service that covers the places the networks do not costs $390 for hardware and $70 a month, or $210 for the mini terminal (August 2025). The electricity to run any of it is among the most expensive in Africa (2023). The consequences show up in basic services: most Somali children have received no routine vaccination (2026). The electronic immunization register is being scaled to close that gap.

Digital divides

In May 2026 most of Hormuud's network carried 4G while a large part of its subscriber base was still on 2G or feature phones. Through Get-Phone it finances smartphones on a $19 deposit and $0.60 to $1.60 a day, repaid through EVC Plus. Each handset comes with 1GB and 40 minutes, and the target is 100,000 handsets by the end of 2026. More than 2,500 had been sold on credit by September 2026.

In April 2026 women, the elderly and people with disabilities were named as the groups most at risk of being left out of the digital ID rollout. SMS and USSD channels were urged as an alternative to the app, whose downloads were a small fraction of registrations. In June 2025 missing birth certificates, insecurity, and the absence of roads and electricity were named as hurdles.

In 2022, 30.8% of males and 24.7% of females aged 10 and over used the internet. Internet users remained a minority of the population in 2024. Somalia's internet and computers score on the Ibrahim Index sits in the bottom quarter of the continent (2023). Electricity reached about half the population in 2023. Satellite service has been available across the whole country since August 2025. The 2017 National Communications Law provides for universal access.

Data

National statistics

Somalia's statistical capacity score rose 29.1 points over the decade to 2023, reaching 35.1 out of 100. It ranks 42nd of 54, and is the fifth most improved of the country's 96 indicators. As at 2023 the World Bank recorded no population, agricultural or business census within twenty years. In the ten years to 2023 it recorded no household, health or business survey, one labour survey and two agricultural surveys. Data sources was the weakest pillar, under 20%, against 50-69% for data products and 70-89% for data use (2023).

The National Bureau of Statistics' Business Establishment Census for 2024/25 identified roughly 48,000 formal business establishments. In August 2024 the bureau launched a platform through which ministries report SDG indicator data. In November 2024 it compiled the first biennial Statistical Abstract from surveys and ministry records. Its second Joint Data and Statistics Forum, in June 2025, drew over 130 government and partner representatives. Limited data-sharing platforms was one of the two most cited barriers in the bureau's 2025 user satisfaction survey.

The 2024 Ibrahim Index holds no data for Somalia on 19 of its 96 indicators, including all its public-perception measures. Civil registration had registered 3.5 per cent of children under five as at 2020. British and African Development Bank commitments to statistical capacity run to 2026.

Open data

In 2024 five of the 23 data categories the Open Data Inventory assessed for Somalia scored zero out of ten, among them banking, energy and crime statistics. Government finance was the strongest, at 6.5 out of 10. Price statistics, the built environment, education facilities and gender statistics scored 5.5 each.

Across the 23 categories assessed in 2024 the mean score is 3.7 out of 10, the whole distribution squeezed between a floor of zero and a ceiling of 6.5.

Disclosure of public records rose 19.1 points over 2014-2023 to 22.1 out of 100, 41st of 54. Accessibility of public records rose 13.2 points to 13.7, 44th of 54.

The national open data portal, data.gov.so, was launched in 2018 under the Somalia Open Government Initiative. In March 2025 the statistics bureau described difficulty persuading agencies to release non-sensitive data. In November 2026 the finance ministry's website carried a fiscal data dashboard, a citizens' budget and debt bulletins.

The Auditor-General's 2025 annual audit covered 30 internationally financed projects and 22 government bodies holding 60.1% of the national budget. It found 66 breaches of law and procurement procedure. It could not verify the revenue share reported from the Mogadishu port and airport concession, because the records were not available to it. The Central Bank publishes audited financial statements prepared under IFRS, with an independent audit opinion. In September 2026 a national consortium led by the research and education network began letting universities mint persistent identifiers for research outputs.

Geospatial data

A partner-run remote-sensing service estimates cultivable area and production and maps land use (August 2026). No national programme for the state's use of earth-observation or geospatial data was in place as at August 2026. One state body reads satellite data itself. The civil aviation authority installed a Meteosat Third Generation receiver in Mogadishu and a PUMA-2025 workstation, replacing a decade-old platform (July 2025). The equipment tracks lightning and convective storms. No Somali surface weather station was transmitting data to the WMO's global exchange systems in 2023. In March 2026 the CREWS initiative approved support to rehabilitate automatic weather stations, and in May 2026 support to set up a national meteorological agency. A national Early Warnings for All roadmap was validated in 2025.

The African Risk Capacity's drought policy, modelled on satellite rainfall, paid about US$2.18m to the government and the Start Network after the poor 2024 Deyr rains. The World Bank-financed DRIVE livestock insurance, triggered by a satellite pasture index, paid US$3.88m to 17,734 Somali pastoralists in March 2026 with no claims filed.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In March 2026 Somalia's border management ran on PISCES, the US-backed biometric screening system. In January 2025 the FBI's Criminal Justice Information Services division sought vendors to run Somalia's police biometric identification system at Aden Adde International Airport.

Somalia launched an Integrated Electronic Case Management System on 9 October 2025. The US Bureau of International Narcotics and Law Enforcement Affairs funded it, and IDLO implemented it. Deployment is phased and does not yet run nationwide.

China activities

In April 2026 Somalia's space and satellite department signed a memorandum with China's land satellite remote-sensing centre on satellite imagery and technical cooperation. In February 2026 the identity authority's director met China's ambassador on technical cooperation for the national identity system. No Chinese-financed or Chinese-operated digital infrastructure was in place in Somalia as at August 2026.

EU activities

European public financiers are among the more frequent funders of Somalia's digital sector and among the least consequential. Eight of the 33 commitments on record are European — the European Union, Sweden's Sida and the UK's FCDO — and between them they account for only about USD 31 million, a fraction of the World Bank's (November 2026).

In 2024 the European Union committed EUR 4.5 million, about USD 5 million, to the ICT Support to the Security, Justice, Governance and Accountability Project. It supplies IT and office equipment to security and justice institutions at federal level and in two to three member states. It runs to 2028.

The D4D Initiative for Digital Government and Cybersecurity for the Horn of Africa covered Somalia, Kenya and Djibouti, as a EUR 3 million programme co-financed by the EU and Germany's BMZ from 2022 to 2025. GIZ led e-government, Expertise France cybersecurity and FIIAPP change management.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf state or Gulf-owned operator held Somali digital infrastructure or a government platform contract as at August 2026.

India activities

India had no digital infrastructure, platform or vendor presence in Somalia as at August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

A January 2025 IT audit of the SOMCAS customs system found no escrow agreement for the system's code and no formal backup and recovery processes. The Auditor-General said this posed significant legal and operational risks to the system's continuity.

The communications ministry's eGovernment Strategy of August 2025 found institutions saving government data on their own servers for want of hosting guidelines. It proposes a national data centre as cloud infrastructure for all government services, with disaster recovery for shared services. It rates dependence on technology vendors a high risk, to be met by in-house capacity and vendor management. In June 2026 the World Bank recorded no guidelines on government data hosting and cloud adoption. A National Data Policy Framework validated in March 2026 is meant to protect national interests in how data is processed and managed. No instrument fixing where state data is held, or under whose law, was in force as at October 2026.

Pakistan's NADRA built the civil registration and vital statistics system and handed it to the Somali authorities in November 2024. In September 2026 a UNDP-convened mission validated a Somalia-Pakistan action matrix on digital identity and digital public infrastructure. The data, portability and exit terms of the NADRA partnership and the US-supplied PISCES border system had not been published as at September 2026.
