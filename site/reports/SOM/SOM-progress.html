This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-27.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A Digital Transformation Strategy 2025-2030 was in finalisation with international support, while domestic analysis names interoperability as the central risk.
Full recordThe strategy was in finalisation in October 2025 and no adopted text has entered the base since (strategy, context).

A domestic analysis and the national digital public infrastructure summit communique both name interoperability between government systems as the central risk; the analysis argues the danger is digitising the existing fragmentation rather than failing to digitise (analysis, summit).

That argument is the sharpest domestic critique in this ledger, and it is aimed at a strategy that has not yet been published.

Data protection Data protection authority The Data Protection Authority took its first nationwide enforcement step in June 2026, mandatory registration under the Act, and its independence is qualified by executive appointment and override powers.

Data protection National data protection readiness The regulator asked a messaging platform to delay usernames, on grounds of impersonation risk to mobile money and loss of phone-number traceability.
Full recordThe request was made in a regulator statement in July 2026 and restated by the minister later that month (request).

Full recordThe mid-term review ran January to April 2026 and raised the rating from moderately unsatisfactory to moderately satisfactory. Digital Identity and CRVS Robustness of system A mobile identity application passed 50,000 downloads on a renewed adoption call, and a consent-based verification platform runs alongside it.
Full recordThe adoption call was renewed in August 2026 with the application past 50,000 downloads, against about 2m registrations (adoption, application, tools).

The gap between registrations and downloads is the finding: the credential is being issued considerably faster than the means of using it is being taken up.

A consent-based verification platform launched alongside the application and a certificate delivery system, and nothing has entered the base about it since (verification).
 Advanced
Digital Identity and CRVS Registration of entire population About 2m people were registered for the national identity credential by April 2026, against an estimated 19.7m population of whom about 85 per cent held no legal identity at the 2022 survey.
Full recordRegistration passed one million in November 2025 and reached about 2m by April 2026, under a mass registration drive launched in September 2025 (registration, million, drive).

At the 2022 household survey about 85 per cent of an estimated 19.7m population held no legal identity and fewer than 16 per cent a government one (survey), so the base against which this is progress is very low.

Two member states have not adopted the national system, one operating its own identification authority, so the register is federal in name and partial in territory (member states, centre).
 Advanced

Full recordThe cabinet approval came in August 2026 (plan), after clearance in February to adopt and print the regional bloc's electronic passport (clearance) and a stated overhaul target the previous September (overhaul).

No cost, supplier, issuance target or personalisation site is published, and nothing states whether the passport draws on the national identity register.

Digital Identity and CRVS Use by other systems The credential was enforced at the main airport for inter-state travel from March 2026, and a legal analysis holds the proclamation behind it exceeds the Act, which confers a right and imposes no duty.
Full recordEnforcement began at the capital's airport in March 2026 (enforcement, overhaul).

Digital Payments and Fintech Governance role of central bank The central bank set out the bank integration sequence for the instant payment system in July 2026, on a switch it deliberately chose to run as a joint venture rather than as central-bank infrastructure.

Digital Payments and Fintech Population uptake Mobile money runs about 155m transactions a month worth US$2.7bn against formal banking reaching about 15 per cent of the population.
Full recordThe dominant service was re-certified against the industry mobile money standard in August 2025 (certification).

Nationally, mobile money runs about 155 million transactions a month worth US$2.7bn on lender figures, against formal banking reaching about 15 per cent of the population (scale).

That ratio is the whole payments picture here: a private mobile-money system carrying a monetary mass many times the reach of the banks, which is the system the instant payment rail and the identity credential are both being built to sit alongside.
