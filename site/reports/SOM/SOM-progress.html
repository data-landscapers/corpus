Somalia: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-03  ·  147 systems and instruments tracked

Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-03.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A Digital Transformation Strategy 2025-2030 was in finalisation with international support, while domestic analysis names interoperability as the central risk.
Full recordThe strategy was in finalisation in October 2025 and no adopted text has entered the base since (strategy, context).

A domestic analysis and the national digital public infrastructure summit communique both name interoperability between government systems as the central risk; the analysis argues the danger is digitising the existing fragmentation rather than failing to digitise (analysis, summit).

That argument is the sharpest domestic critique in this ledger, and it is aimed at a strategy that has not yet been published.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The 2019-2024 policy has expired and a successor went to national consultation in October 2025 without being adopted.
Full recordThe first cross-cutting national ICT policy and strategy, adopted by Cabinet for 2019 to 2024, documented five major mobile operators employing about 25,000 people and mobile money at 150 million transactions a month (policy). It has expired.

A two-day multi-stakeholder workshop opened in October 2025 to validate a successor Digital Transformation Strategy for 2025 to 2030 (consultation).

As of the last record the base holds, that successor remains at validation stage rather than adopted, so the country has been without a current ICT strategy since the end of 2024.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy No broadband strategy is held. A feasibility and security-risk consultancy launched in October 2025 to map infrastructure and design backbone routes.
Full recordThe ministry launched a technical feasibility study and security risk management consultancy under the regional digital project, mapping existing broadband infrastructure and designing backbone and cross-border routes (launch).

That is design work, not a strategy: no coverage, speed or price target for the country is published anywhere the base can reach.

The regulator separately convened the first submarine cable technical working group in June 2026, on outage management, competition and the cable regulatory framework (workshop).
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy Government data-hosting and cloud guidelines stand at their November 2022 baseline against an April 2028 target, recorded by the financing lender as not achieved.
Full recordThe guidelines are a results indicator of a regional digital integration project and the lender records them as not achieved (guidelines).

They matter more here than the target date suggests: the ministry's stated rationale for a national data centre is to end reliance on storage outside the country, which is also its statement that government data was held offshore when it spoke. There is no rule governing where it sits.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap The e-government strategy published in August 2025 states the federal government lacks an interoperability framework, and sets out the architecture that would supply one.
Full recordThe strategy and roadmap for 2025 to 2029 states plainly that the federal government has no enterprise architecture or interoperability framework, and prescribes the correction: standardised solutions with open interfaces, a data-exchange hub, a national enterprise service bus, shared services and a whole-of-government approach (strategy).

A document that names the gap is not the same as a framework that closes it. Nothing on the base records the hub or the service bus being built.

What exists in practice is bilateral: the school certificate system connects identity, payment and registry building blocks for one service (system).
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy A national artificial-intelligence readiness assessment gained a university participant in August 2026, with no methodology, participant list, score or publication date stated.
Full recordThe university joined the assessment in August 2026 (assessment).

Nothing about the exercise is published beyond the fact of it. A readiness assessment is the usual first step toward a strategy, and no strategy, policy or guidance on artificial intelligence exists in this base for the country.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies There is no domestic storage mandate. The Data Protection Act instead prohibits transfer outside Somalia absent adequacy or an approved safeguard.
Full recordArticles 30 and 31 of the 2023 Act establish a conditional cross-border regime: transfer outside the country is prohibited unless an adequacy finding or an approved safeguard applies (Act). That is a transfer condition, not a localisation requirement.

The implementing regulations published in April 2026 carry the same regime at Article 24 (regulations).

No instrument requires that any category of data be held on infrastructure inside the country, and none of the government hosting documents states where public data sits.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy The implementing regulations for the 2023 Act were gazetted in April 2026, and the authority opened registration and licensing in June.
Full recordThe full regulatory text was published in the Official Bulletin on 5 April 2026, setting registration and licensing, data-protection-officer duties, consent, breach notification, compliance audit, complaints and the cross-border transfer regime (regulations).

The authority then published its operational registration and licensing regime for controllers and processors under Articles 10 and 14, with forms, guidelines, a compliance checklist and renewal templates (regime, guidelines).

That is a governance regime for personal data. No policy governs public-sector data as an asset.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy No open-data policy is held. The operative statement is the statistics bureau's portal terms of use, which set licensing and format rather than obligation.
Full recordThe terms published in July 2024 govern the statistics platform: a CC-BY 4.0 licence, machine-readable formats and a stated update cadence (terms).

That is a licence, not a policy: nothing obliges any public body to publish, sets a default of openness, or names datasets that must be released.

The government's own open-data portal was carrying eight datasets across ten ministries at the time of the probe, against a vision statement targeting 2020.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation A Data Protection Act has been in effect since March 2023, and a regulatory framework under it was approved by the Council of Ministers in January 2026 and adopted the following month.
Full recordThe Act has been in effect since 23 March 2023 and its implementing regulations were reviewed (Act).

The regulatory framework was approved on 8 January 2026 and adopted in February (approval, adoption).

A statute in force since 2023 with regulations adopted in 2026 and an authority taking its first nationwide enforcement step in June puts this country ahead of several larger units in this frame on the sequence, if not on capacity.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation The lower house approved a Cybersecurity Law in January 2026, and the upper house took only a first reading on 29 August 2026.
Full recordThe House of the People approved the law on 26 January 2026, assigning policy to the communications ministry, technical oversight to the regulator and obligations to critical-infrastructure operators (approval, coverage).

The Upper House gave a six-chapter, 33-article cybersecurity bill its first reading on 29 August 2026, at the first sitting of its eighth term (first reading). The chamber's own published procedure requires three readings before a bill passes it (procedure).

So the instrument is short of final approval, and nothing on file states whether the bill now before the upper house is the one the lower house approved in January. The country ranked in the international index's fifth tier at 37.39 of 100 in 2024 and is a signatory to the continental cyber convention (index).

The law's text is not held, and no implementing regulation, commencement date or enforcement action is on record.

Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The 2023 identity law gained its implementing regulation in June 2024, and a civil status law followed in April 2026.
Full recordLaw No. 009 of March 2023 is the founding statute for the identity authority and the national digital identity system (law). Its implementing regulation was made by the Prime Minister on 8 June 2024 and gazetted that October (regulation).

Law No. 46, published 5 April 2026, adds the civil-registration and vital-statistics layer beside the identity infrastructure (law).

The base now holds all three primary texts. What it does not hold is any provision setting out how the identity system and the civil register are to be joined.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation The National Payment System Bill cleared both chambers by January 2026, replacing a regime that rested on 2020 mobile money regulations.
Full recordThe lower house passed the bill 154 to 2 on 8 November 2025 (vote) and the upper house carried its third reading 28 to nil in January 2026 (vote). It empowers the central bank to license and supervise payment systems and digital-money platforms.

Until it is signed, the operative instrument remains the Mobile Money Regulations amended in 2020, covering licensing, conduct of business, safeguarding of consumer funds, supervision and winding-up (regulations).

No record of presidential signature was found.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability Law 46 of 2026 puts civil registration on a statutory footing beside the identity system; nothing yet compels one public body to share data with another.
Full recordThe Population Registration and Civil Status Statistics Law, gazetted 5 April 2026, establishes the civil-registration and vital-statistics layer alongside the national identity infrastructure (law).

The Data Protection Act supplies the conditions under which personal data may move, including outside the country (Act) — permission, not obligation.

What carries interoperability in practice is administrative: a cross-institutional working group launched in June 2026 to integrate the national identity and its verification platform across public and private services (working group).
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No artificial-intelligence instrument is in force or in draft. A readiness assessment was still recruiting its lead expert in October 2025.
Full recordRecruitment opened in October 2025 for a national expert to lead a readiness assessment and produce a policy-oriented report to inform a national governance framework, working under a government-side steering committee (call). Somalia was listed at inception stage.

The national artificial intelligence centre names advising on ethical policy formulation among its objectives (centre), which places the drafting capacity somewhere but produces no instrument.

The Data Protection Act is the only statute reaching automated processing at all, and it is not AI-specific (Act).

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation A national e-commerce strategy stands at its November 2022 baseline against an April 2028 target, with the lender recording that no strategy exists.
Full recordIt is a results indicator of the regional digital integration project, and the lender records that no strategy exists in this country, in a neighbour, or at the regional body (strategy).

Three parties to the same project have none between them. That is a regional gap rather than a national one, and it is the only place this base can say so.

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The National Statistics Law of 2020 remains the governing statute, having replaced a 1970 law; its own text is not held.
Full recordLaw No. 24, assented on 24 February 2020, established the national statistics bureau and repealed the Statistics Law No. 35 of 1970 (bureau).

No direct link to the statute's own text was found; the bureau's institutional statement is the fullest account the base can reach, which is a real limit on what can be said about its provisions.

Law No. 46 of 2026 adds a vital-statistics mandate alongside it without amending it (law).

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The constitution has required an access-to-information law since 2012; the bill drafted in March 2024 was still before the lower house in October 2025.
Full recordArticle 32 of the Provisional Constitution guarantees access to state-held information and directs Parliament to enact an implementing law (constitution). Fourteen years on, none is enacted.

A ministry official told a September 2025 event that Cabinet had passed the law and it awaited presentation to the lower house (event).

A civil-society submission to an international review a month later recorded the March 2024 bill still before the house, criticising its broad national-security and confidentiality exemptions and its lack of independent oversight (submission).

Data protection Data protection authority The Data Protection Authority took its first nationwide enforcement step in June 2026, mandatory registration under the Act, and its independence is qualified by executive appointment and override powers.
Full recordThe authority was inaugurated in February 2024 and moved to mandatory registration of controllers and processors in June 2026 (enforcement, inauguration).

Its independence is qualified by executive appointment and override powers, which is the standing limit on everything it does and is stated in the record rather than inferred.

No registration count, penalty or completed investigation is published.
 Advanced
Data protection National data protection readiness The regulator asked a messaging platform to delay usernames, on grounds of impersonation risk to mobile money and loss of phone-number traceability.
Full recordThe request was made in a regulator statement in July 2026 and restated by the minister later that month (request).

The stated grounds are impersonation risk against the mobile-money system and loss of phone-number traceability in the campaign against an armed group. Both are traceability arguments, which is a state asking a platform not to reduce the identifiability of its users.

No response from the platform, legal basis for the request or outcome is on record.

Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration An instrument of ratification for the continental free trade area is to be deposited in September 2026.
Full recordA government delegation studied labour and employment digital systems in Dodoma (visit), with a stated intent to learn from the wider public-service digitalisation model (report).

The secretariat's own release dates the deposit; its standfirst gives 2 September 2026 while its body says only September, and the wider month is what is carried (accession).

Accession has digital consequences the release does not address — the country has no e-commerce strategy, no adopted digital transformation strategy and no data-hosting rule — and none of those is named as a precondition anywhere on file.

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation A regional study to harmonise cybersecurity, data protection and digital identity law was commissioned in May 2024 and nothing on the base reports its output.
Full recordThe terms of reference commission a comparative study and recommendations to harmonise cybersecurity, cybercrime, data protection, digital identity, e-commerce and digital-authentication frameworks across member states, Somalia among them (terms).

No report, model law or adopted instrument has been published since.

Somalia's own national instruments have moved independently of the process — the data protection regulations in April 2026 and the cybersecurity frameworks in July — with nothing stating how either relates to a regional standard (frameworks).

Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure A binding grant agreement of SDR 59.5 million funds cross-border links and national backbone, and operators were consulted on regional roaming in September 2025.
Full recordThe financing agreement signed on 21 June 2023 is the operative instrument for participation in cross-border terrestrial links, national backbone, regional exchange-point support and coordinated spectrum and cybersecurity platforms (agreement).

The regulator convened licensed operators in September 2025 to review a national roadmap for joining the regional One Network Area, setting phased alignment of national law and tariffs with regional standards (consultation).

Both are commitments to build rather than infrastructure in service; the feasibility study designing the routes only launched in October 2025.

Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers The 2023 Act prohibits transfer outside Somalia absent adequacy or a safeguard, and the April 2026 regulations put that regime into operating detail.
Full recordArticles 30 and 31 of the Act establish the conditional regime (Act), and Article 24 of the implementing regulations gazetted on 5 April 2026 carries it into practice alongside registration, breach notification and audit (regulations).

The authority's registration and licensing regime, published in June 2026, is the machinery through which a safeguard would be approved (regime).

Civil society is separately pressing for ratification of the African Union convention that would supply an external adequacy reference (position).

Standards National interoperability standards The e-government strategy records that no interoperability framework exists; the certificate system shows what standard building blocks look like in one service.
Full recordThe strategy for 2025 to 2029 states the federal government lacks an enterprise architecture and interoperability framework, and prescribes standardised solutions with open interfaces, a data-exchange hub and a national enterprise service bus (strategy).

The high school certificate system connects registration, identity verification and consent, payments and a digital registry so exam results are retrieved automatically and certificates issued digitally (system).

One service built to a pattern is not a published national standard, and nothing states that the next service must follow it.

Standards National quality standards The operative quality instrument is type approval for terminal equipment, mandatory since July 2020 and tested against European and international standards.
Full recordIssued under Article 59 of the Communications Act 2017, the guidelines require regulator approval before radio and telecommunications terminal equipment — handsets, network equipment, radio and satellite gear — is marketed or used (guidelines).

The filename and hosting path indicate a later republication, but the captured text carries no separate revision, so the 2020 issue is what the base can state.

Nothing else the base holds sets a national quality standard for a digital service, and no service-quality regulation for telecommunications exists outside the consumer-protection draft now in consultation (consultation).

Full recordThe regulator published a National Cybersecurity Risk Management Framework and a National Cybersecurity Compliance Framework with download links in July 2026, reported as aligned to the international information-security management and risk standards (publication).

The national bureau of standards holds member-body status, participating and observing on technical committees and observing on a policy committee (registry).

A government information-systems audit cooperation programme sits alongside both (programme).

Public debate and participation in policymaking Non-governmental contribution to national policy Civil society issued its own advocacy position in July 2026, three months after the regulator opened a draft framework to multi-stakeholder review.

The July position is different in kind: an advocacy forum of civil-society organisations, digital-rights advocates and legal practitioners issued a formal submission calling on the ministry, the regulator and the data protection authority to sign and ratify the African Union convention (position).

The digital inclusion policy was separately finalised after a stakeholder validation workshop (policy).

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Domestic budget appropriations and expenditure Sustainable domestic financing of digital transformation The 2026 budget framework funds e-government, digital signatures and payments infrastructure from the general local fund, with no standalone digital appropriation.
Full recordThe finance ministry's framework paper, prepared under the Public Finance Management Act, shows digital government activity — e-government, digital signatures, national payments infrastructure and an ICT infrastructure pillar — financed through the general Government Local Fund rather than a dedicated line (paper).

It states domestic resourcing of these as a medium-term objective, which is an admission that they are not domestically resourced now.

Every large digital programme the base holds — identity, backbone, social protection — is externally financed.

In September 2025 the regulator agreed with Saudi Arabia's commission to develop a framework for cooperation on regulating artificial intelligence and space technology (agreement).

In February 2026 it signed a bilateral memorandum in Ankara covering satellite regulation, cybersecurity, spectrum, broadband, network resilience and regulatory capacity (memorandum).

All three are regulator-to-regulator. None carries a value.

New investments Development-partner project financing A regional digital integration project had its development-objective rating raised at mid-term, and private-sector investment mobilised stands at US$0 against a US$10m target; the identity rollout needs about US$125m more.
Full recordThe mid-term review ran January to April 2026 and raised the rating from moderately unsatisfactory to moderately satisfactory. Registration has since reached about 2m against a population estimated near 19.7m, so the funding gap and the coverage gap are the same gap.

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Connectivity Mobile penetration 11.5 million connections at the end of 2025, 58.1 per cent of the population and up 757,000 year on year, with three-quarters on broadband networks.
Full recordThe most recent figure the base holds puts active cellular connections at 11.5 million against a 19.8 million population, of which 75.7 per cent are classed as broadband, growing 757,000 or 7.0 per cent over the year (estimate).

Connections are not people: no unique-subscriber figure is published, and multi-SIM use in a market with five operators makes the penetration rate an upper bound.

What the market is now addressing is the handset, through a device-finance scheme targeting 100,000 units by the end of 2026 (programme).
 Advanced
Connectivity Internet usage Internet use and price were reported in October 2025, and the same account ranks the country 191st on online government services.
Full recordThe figures establish a baseline the base did not previously hold (use).

The ranking on online government services sits alongside them in the same account, which is the useful pairing: connectivity and price are one problem, and what a connected person can actually do with government is another.
 Advanced
Connectivity Mobile affordability Average mobile data cost fell from USD 5.75 to USD 5.10 between 2023 and 2024, and an operator launched cheaper tiered bundles in April 2026.
Full recordThe regulator's price basket, built on the international methodology, records average mobile data cost falling 11.3 per cent from USD 5.75 in 2023 to USD 5.10 in 2024, with one gigabyte of residential mobile broadband around USD 5.65 at purchasing-power parity (report).

An operator expanded tiered low-cost bundles — internet-only, calls-only and combined — reachable by short code in April 2026 (launch).

Affordability has two halves and the handset is the other: a financing scheme launched in May 2026 to put devices in hands without a bank account or credit record (programme).

The consultancy launched in October 2025 is mapping existing broadband infrastructure and designing backbone and cross-border routes (launch), funded by the regional project's grant agreement (agreement).

Energy Sufficient energy and water for data centres There is no national transmission grid: isolated city networks, diesel generation and distribution losses reported up to 50 per cent.
Full recordThe national assessment endorsed in July 2025 describes isolated island networks with no national transmission grid, several vertically integrated private suppliers per city, generation dominated by high-speed diesel and electrical losses reported up to 50 per cent within urban radial distribution (plan).
