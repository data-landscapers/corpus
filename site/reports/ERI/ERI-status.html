Summary of position

Eritrea's digitally-tracked state consists of a small number of discrete projects rather than a connected system: a customs platform delivered with UNDP support in January 2026 (ASYCUDA World), a completed national household survey using digital data collection (EPHS 2025), and a domestically built offline AI application (Eri-Chat). No foundational digital-ID system, mobile-money service or data-protection law is held on record. Connectivity is among the lowest recorded on the continent — 23.7% of the population held a mobile connection in late 2025 — and the state telecom monopoly EriTel reports no submarine cable landing and, as of July 2026, no announced Starlink launch date. A July 2026 legal notice ordering all cash deposited into banks by 31 July or "face legal action" is the period's most consequential governance development, issued against a cash-based, under-banked economy. The evidence base itself is unusually constrained: the outlets producing dated material are limited to Eritrea's Ministry of Information (Shabait) and the diaspora/opposition outlet Awate.com, neither neutral, with harder figures coming from UNDP/UNECA and reference indices.

The one governance development on record is a Bank of Eritrea legal notice, issued around 30 June 2026 and reported by the diaspora/opposition outlet Awate.com with the notice's own text embedded, ordering individuals and institutions to deposit cash held outside banks by 31 July 2026 "or face legal action." The notice defines neither an "excessive amount" nor an enforcement mechanism. No data-protection law, bill or regulator designation is held on record.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

System or instrument Status ↗ source Milestone
EriTel national network expansion programme Implemented, ongoing expansion Minister reports 184 towers installed, ~90% geographic mobile coverage, service 'reaching 4G level' (government claim)
Starlink satellite internet service Not held Starlink's own coverage-availability map lists Eritrea 'No planned launch date'
Submarine fibre-optic cable landing Not held Cable industry calls Eritrea a 'black hole' that does not answer landing requests; still cable-less as of Apr 2026

Eritrea's international connectivity runs entirely on satellite. The country holds no submarine fibre-optic cable landing, and cable firms describe the government as unresponsive even to a Red Sea route sought after Houthi attacks made Yemeni waters unusable — a position unchanged as of April 2026 and one that leaves Eritrea, on the industry's own undated count, as the sole non-landing state among Africa's 38 coastal countries. Starlink, available in half of Africa's sovereign states by July 2026, carries no announced launch date for Eritrea, one of only seven African states — all in the north or the Horn — in that position; no licence application or refusal has been found. Domestically, the state telecom monopoly EriTel reports 184 towers installed and roughly 90% geographic mobile coverage as of May 2026, alongside international bandwidth risen to about 1,536 Mbps over the preceding decade — figures from the responsible minister that cannot be independently verified.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Mobile money / digital payments service Not held —

The clearest concrete step in the period is the January 2026 launch of ASYCUDA World, UNCTAD's automated customs platform, replacing software in use since 2007; the deployment came with a full transfer of source code to the Eritrean government, a national-ownership condition rather than a licensed or hosted arrangement. Statistical infrastructure has also advanced: the National Statistics Office completed the Fourth Round Population and Health Survey in 2025, covering 9,794 households across all six regions using tablet-based interviewing rather than paper forms, with the full report due in 2026 under a UNDP/UNECA-backed programme that has funded Eritrean statistical-capacity building since at least 2019. No foundational digital-identity system is held on record — an analytical profile finds the country lacks one and sets out what a future system would have to overcome — and no mobile-money or digital-payments service is held either, consistent with the cash-based, under-banked economy discussed under Governance below.

Innovation ecosystem

The base holds one concrete AI/innovation data point: Eri-Chat, an offline artificial-intelligence mobile application built by a Ministry of Information software developer and launched in April 2025, storing conversation data only on the user's device. It was showcased to ambassadors and UN officials at Asmara's UN Day event in October 2025; it remains English-only, its developer citing insufficient training data for Eritrea's other languages.
