This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Eritrea completed its first National Development Plan in 2025. In April 2026 a domesticated UN Integrated Geospatial Information Framework action plan for Eritrea was filed with UNECA's African Centre for Statistics. A five-year civil registration and vital statistics plan for 2024 to 2028 also stands (July 2026).

In 2019 the ITU and Broadband Commission's global inventory recorded no national broadband plan, strategy or policy for Eritrea. Nor has a national AI strategy or a national digital transformation strategy been published (June 2026).

The Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework for 2022 to 2026 was prepared under a joint government and UN steering committee, with the Ministry of Finance and National Development leading on the government side. It seeks to expand access to health coverage, education, social protection, energy and digital connectivity (2021).

Eritrea's Overall Governance score was 27.5 out of 100 in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states. It rose 1.9 points over the decade, 1.5 of them in the last five years, when the African average gained 0.1.

Legislation and regulation

Eritrea's 2015 Penal Code was drafted to provide for computer crimes. It had still not been enacted as at April 2026.

The Public Procurement and Asset Management laws drafted under the African Development Bank's public financial management project remained in stakeholder consultation and unenacted in February 2026. A draft Law on National Archives and Libraries has been prepared with expert input from the Research and Documentation Centre and the University of Pavia (November 2025). Eritrea acceded to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities on 20 December 2024.

Neither the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Commerce nor the Model Law on Electronic Signatures has been adopted, so electronic signatures carry no legal equivalence in Eritrean law (2022). The country is party to none of the three instruments governing refugee protection. It has no national legal framework for refugees or asylum-seekers (2026).

In July 2025 the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group's mutual evaluation found major technical compliance deficiencies in the anti-money-laundering regime. It also found no operational financial intelligence unit.

Data protection

No comprehensive data-protection law was in force in Eritrea as at August 2026. No supervisory authority had been established and no enforcement decisions had been published. The only provision in point is Article 18 of the 1997 Constitution, a general right to privacy with no implementing statute (2026). Personal data is not defined in Eritrean law, and no body holds a mandate to receive complaints or enforce data-protection obligations.

No data-localisation requirement applies to the storage or processing of personal or government data (2026). Personal data is collected at the point of service all the same. Activating a prepaid mobile or fixed line with EriTel requires a copy of the subscriber's national identity card (2025). The Communication Department, the only body with power to supervise and regulate the communication sector, has little to no decision-making authority (2022).

Article 15 of the Samoa Agreement of December 2023 commits Eritrea, as an OACPS party, to building a personal-data-protection regime overseen by an independent supervisory authority. In June 2025 the Horn of Africa Initiative placed Eritrea with Djibouti, South Sudan and Sudan at a nascent stage, without data-protection legislation or regulatory oversight. It put its regional target for national data-protection frameworks back from December 2025 to December 2026.

In July 2026 Eritrea had no independent children's ombudsperson or commissioner mandated to receive and investigate children's complaints. The African Committee of Experts on the Rights and Welfare of the Child has recommended that one be established in line with the Paris Principles.

Regional collaboration

Eritrea is the only one of the African Union's 55 member states not to have signed the consolidated text of the African Continental Free Trade Area agreement (July 2026). Forty-nine states have both signed and deposited instruments of ratification.

In January 2026 Eritrea was not covered by the Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, IGAD's programme on connectivity, data governance, cybersecurity and cross-border data flows. The project gives its coverage as Djibouti, Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan and Uganda. In June 2021 the World Bank found that cross-border links in the Horn would need cross-border collaboration on licensing. Eritrea has not signed the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.

Eritrea is a party to the Samoa Agreement, the EU-OACPS partnership agreement published in December 2023.

Two independent institutional accounts spanning 2021 to 2026 — a World Bank regional survey and IGAD's live regional digital programme roster — each place Eritrea outside the Horn of Africa's shared digital infrastructure and data-governance tracks.

Standards

In January 2026 Eritrea inaugurated the UNCTAD-built ASYCUDA World customs platform, replacing a legacy customs system in place since 2007. Full source code was transferred to the government.

Proclamation No. 75/1995 established the Eritrean Standards Institution as the sole autonomous national body for standardization, quality assurance and metrology. It empowered the Institution to issue compulsory Eritrean Standards and to certify imports and exports against them. It also adopted the International System of Units as Eritrea's legal system of measurement. The Institution is a correspondent member of the International Organization for Standardization, observing on four technical committees (October 2026).

In 2022 Eritrea had no interoperability framework and no data quality framework inside government. Its wire-transfer regulations deviate from international security standards such as ISO/IEC and EMV (2022). Broadcasting has no independent regulator (January 2026). Eritrea has made no submission to the ITU's telecommunications indicators questionnaire since 2017 (March 2026).

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In July 2026 Eurac Research's NBS4Water project held two rounds of geodesign workshops. More than 20 experts and stakeholders from government, academia, the private sector and NGOs took part. The workshops co-developed water-security planning scenarios for Asmara.

Eritrea's deliberative and participatory governance score rose 7.4 points over the decade to 14.1 out of 100 in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states.

Article 19 of the 1997 Constitution guarantees access to information. The 1996 Press Proclamation requires all media to be licensed and publications to be approved before release. No freedom-of-information law has been adopted (January 2026). Eritrea ranks last of 180 countries and territories in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index, the third consecutive year in that position (May 2026). The index names the Eritrean journalist Dawit Isaak as jailed without trial for 25 years. The country's digital freedom score was 1.2 out of 100 in 2023, last of 54 African states and down 6.6 points over the decade. Its freedom of expression and belief score rose 12.6 points to 12.7 out of 100 over the same period, also last.

In June 2015 a UN Commission of Inquiry found that the state runs a complex, multi-layered system of surveillance of the population inside and outside the country. The system is used to instil fear and keep the population under control. The ABC Journey publishes some eighty consultation drafts of Acts, codes, regulations and rules, prepared against the 1997 Constitution for public debate and not as enacted law.

Finance

Financial sustainability

No fiscal-year budget for the digital estate had been read as at September 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

Greenfield solar power plants at Tesseney, Berantu and Kerkebet were under discussion at the African Development Bank's mission to Eritrea in April 2026. The Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework 2022-2026 was signed with the Government of the State of Eritrea and covers United Nations cooperation. It was prepared under a joint government and UN steering committee.

New investments

The African Development Bank appraised Eritrea's Energy Integrated Project in February 2026 on two grants. They are UA 27 million from the African Development Fund and UA 15 million from the Transition Support Facility (February 2026). The Bank approved UAC 9,000,000 on 4 July 2016 for a public financial management and statistics technical assistance project, in force from 10 October 2016. The project stood at 81.65 per cent disbursed at the Bank's supervision mission of 30 November to 30 December 2025. Its Fragile States Facility approved UAC 2,000,000 on 19 June 2019 for the finance ministry's tax administration project. The United Nations Development Programme has financed national accounts and other macroeconomic statistics work with the National Statistics Office since 2019.

Since April 2024 the Global Partnership for Education's US$11.82m System Transformation Grant and US$2.2m System Capacity Grant have funded a foundational-learning reform. UNICEF is the grant agent. One strand is strengthening data collection, processing, storage and use in the education management information system. The reform is directed at the zobas holding nomadic, semi-nomadic and remote rural communities, which lag the national average on education indicators.

Eritrea's external development finance for digital and data systems is exceptionally thin (October 2026): three commitments in all, spanning 2016 to 2026, from two financiers — the African Development Bank with two and the United Nations Development Programme with one. Every one of them is a grant or technical assistance to the public sector; there is no loan, no guarantee, no equity and no commercial or private-sector finance. The subsectors reached are public financial management and tax administration systems and official statistics, and nothing at all has been committed for connectivity, digital identity, digital payments, data centres or digital skills. Eritrea is also named in none of the 26 regional East African digital commitments on the same record: the East African Community and IGAD-anchored programmes for regional digital integration, AI research, digital skills, cross-border payments and connectivity all run past it.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Eritrea was still the only one of Africa's coastal states with no submarine cable landing in April 2026. By June 2024 it had rebuffed approaches from submarine-cable firms seeking to use its waters, even at a price. Houthi attacks had left cables in Yemeni waters unrepaired and new ones unlaid, and Eritrea's waters lie at a depth the industry regards as optimal. In June 2021 the World Bank found that the regional submarine and festoon cable projects planned for the Horn of Africa, and the 2Africa cable, carry no commitment to serve the region as a whole. It named Asmara among the places left out.

There is no active internet exchange point in the country (2026). In 2021 the ITU recorded no part of the population covered by a 4G network. The newest international bandwidth figure the ITU holds, 8 kbit/s per internet user, is from 2017, the last year Eritrea filed telecommunications data at all. In July 2026 Starlink had no planned launch date for Eritrea, one of seven African states with no announced timeline.

The internet and computers score reached 23.5 out of 100 in 2023, 40th of 54 African states and up 22.4 points over a decade. The mobile communications score, at 32.6, ranks 51st of 54, from a 2014 base of zero. Fewer than a quarter of the population used the internet in 2023.

In 2024 limited internet infrastructure had led the Ministry of Health to run its health information platform offline over its own local networks. Data is entered at zone and sub-zone level and analysed centrally.

Data Storage

The state operator EriTel offers a private cloud service hosted entirely in its own data centres (August 2026). It comes in tiers of 100, 250 and 500 gigabytes for 20, 50 and 100 users. Each tier carries a 90 per cent uptime service level and a recovery-time objective of up to six days (August 2026). It is the only provider-hosted storage found, and no capacity figure, facility count or location has been published for it.

The one government data-centre build found is in the statistics office: African Development Bank financing approved in 2016 equipped the National Statistics Office with its own data centre.

In January 2026 customs had all its stations interconnected over a secure internal intranet, on infrastructure of its own. Data can be exchanged and processed without continuous internet access. In 2022 Eritrea operated no government service bus, the shared middleware through which public bodies would route data to one another. It imposes no data localisation requirements on the storage or processing of personal or government data (2026).

Energy

Eritrea's interconnected grid has an installed capacity of 157 MW, of which only 52 MW is reliably available against demand of 74.3 MW (February 2026). Generation depends almost entirely on imported diesel, with renewable sources contributing 6 MW. Generation and distribution assets are ageing and breakdown-prone, and shortages of spare parts constrain operations. Mining companies run 60 MW of captive generation of their own, outside the national grid.

Captive generation held by the mining companies is of the same order as the reliably available capacity of the entire national grid.

54.4% of the population had access to electricity in 2023. Access to energy is Eritrea's best-ranked infrastructure measure, at 30.5 out of 100 and 32nd of 54 African states in 2023. Supply is concentrated in the major cities and their surroundings (2018). 11.4% of electricity generation came from renewable sources in 2023. The 2018 National Energy Policy targets 20% renewable supply by 2030. The solar resource is 2,275-2,314 kWh/m2 a year at Tesseney, Kerkebet and Barentu.

The Energy Integrated Project appraised in February 2026 would build 34 MW of solar with battery storage serving Tesseney, Kerkebet and Barentu. It is the third African Development Bank energy intervention in the country, after a 30 MW plant at Dekemhare and a 12 MW mini-grid programme at the same three towns. The Dekemhare solar plant is operational and supplying the national grid (April 2026).

In January 2026 the President set out a national energy plan of twelve independent mini-grid power centres targeting 360 MW, each growing to 30 MW. The centres are to be interconnected into a national grid eventually reaching about 2,000 MW. No site, cost, financier or timetable has been found for the plan.

In February 2026 the financier of the financial management system said that its government-wide rollout depends on reliable power, IT infrastructure and connectivity upgrades.

Technical Capacity

In January 2026 Eritrea's customs digitisation came with full transfer of the ASYCUDA World source code to the government. A ten-month capacity-building programme trained 100 customs officers, IT specialists and private-sector clearing agents. In 2024 a UNDP assessment scored implementation capacity and systems at their lowest stage: an analogue position with no skills, no outsourcing, no strategy and no budget.

In May 2026 computer-assisted personal interviewing replaced paper collection for the Fourth Round Population and Health Survey. In February 2026 a four-week UNECA workshop trained staff of the Ministry of Land, Water and Environment in GIS and remote sensing in Asmara. In June 2025 the Ministry of Education rated its own teacher management system weak, and its technological infrastructure and connectivity inadequate.

In 2022 technical and ICT capacity for civil registration was inadequate at both national and regional level. The tertiary pipeline runs through the Eritrea Institute of Technology at Mai-Nefhi, which carries the country's computer science and related engineering programmes (2025). In 2024 Eritrea ranked near the bottom of the UN e-government ranking, 190th of 193.

Cybersecurity

Eritrea sits in the lowest tier, Building, of the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, the tier for scores of 0 to 20. In 2024 no national cybersecurity strategy had been published. The information ministry reported a large cyberattack on the national internet system on 24 May 2024. It said defensive measures stopped the attack without interrupting service, and it named no attacker.

The 2015 Penal Code was drafted to provide for computer crimes but had not been enacted as at April 2026. In January 2026 Eritrea was outside the Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, IGAD's programme on data governance, cybersecurity and cross-border data flows.

In January 2026 the President said that secure platforms and a data-protection architecture must be established before detailed personal data is collected for a planned diaspora database.

DPI

Data Exchange

The one working data exchange went live in January 2026, in customs: all of the country's customs stations are interconnected over a secure internal intranet. Data moves between them without depending on continuous internet access.

In 2024 the UN Development Programme's Digital Development Compass placed Eritrea's data exchange at its lowest stage, describing government data handling as offline and paper-based with no systems in place. In 2022 there was no government interoperability framework and no data quality framework, and no government service bus. None of the financial management, human-resource, customs, tax or social-insurance systems exchanged data with any other government system. There was no GovTech or digital transformation strategy and no government body in charge of data governance. In June 2025 the Horn of Africa Initiative placed Eritrea's data-protection policy at a nascent stage, with no legislation and no regulatory oversight.

In 2018 an identity-card application required a supporting letter from the applicant's kebabi administration and a birth certificate. It was lodged at one of 12 branch offices and forwarded to headquarters in Asmara for approval.

A domesticated UN Integrated Geospatial Information Framework action plan for Eritrea was filed with UNECA's African Centre for Statistics on 6 April 2026.

Digital Identity and CRVS

In November 2025 most Eritreans still held the older hand-written identity card, with a long backlog of applications for the newer one. The Department of Immigration and Nationality has issued the plastic, holographic, fingerprinted card to citizens aged over 18 since late 2015. It carries a laser-engraved photograph, the holder's fingerprint and a unique serial number (2018).

In January 2021 each of the six zoba registers recorded a per-resident identification number alongside biographic and civil-status fields. Birth registration passes up from the Kebabi family register through Nus-Zoba to Zoba level.

Proclamation No. 21/1992 remains the operative instrument for issuance. It addresses neither digital identity, biometric data nor data protection, and no dedicated digital identity legislation has been enacted since. Proclamation No. 175/2014 requires financial institutions to set customer identification and acceptance policies, for business relationships and for cash transactions above US$10,000. The Bank of Eritrea is the supervisory authority.

In March 2026 the UK passport authority confirmed that the identity card remains a precondition of passport issue. The card is required of every Eritrean aged 18 and above. EriTel requires a copy of it for a fixed line, and for a prepaid mobile line a regional administration letter, itself obtained with an identity card copy (2025). A new card costs 100 nakfa in Eritrea and US$25 at the embassy in Washington (2018). Limited access to documentation heightens the risk of statelessness (2026). Since September 2024 applicants in the United States can start an identity-card application on the embassy's online consular platform and finish it in person.

Digital Payments and Fintech

A Bank of Eritrea legal notice of June 2026 ordered cash held outside the banking system deposited by 31 July 2026, on pain of legal action. Legal Notice No. 134/2026, in force from 9 September 2026, bars an individual from holding more than 25,000 nakfa in cash for longer than 15 days. Private institutions are capped at 50,000, 100,000 or 150,000 nakfa by grade. In July and August 2024 anti-money-laundering assessors found no automatic teller machines, no debit or credit cards and no internet banking.

M-Nakfa, run by a money transfer company under Bank of Eritrea licence, holds value backed by cash deposits (2025). It carries person-to-person transfers by phone number and merchant and utility payments, with registration at a branch on a national identity card or passport. In 2025 EriTel transferred airtime between mobile numbers by short code at 2 nakfa a transfer, and took fixed-line bill payments only in cash or cheque. In June 2026 Eritrea was not a participant in the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, directly or through its extended network. Sending GBP 120 from the United Kingdom cost 8.42% on average in the third quarter of 2025. In 2024 the Digital Development Compass scored payments at its lowest stage. Cash and cheques dominated, digital payments were rare, and none was integrated into a government service.

Electronic payments, credit cards and internet banking are not legally restricted (2022). The financial system is owned and controlled by the State (July 2025). It is small, simple and less integrated with the global financial system, owing largely to past UN sanctions and domestic restrictions on financial flows. Proclamations No. 32/1993 and No. 173/2013 require all transactions in Eritrea to be conducted in local currency. UNECA reads this as discriminating against foreign firms seeking to settle payments locally. In November 2025 the African Development Bank named an underdeveloped financial sector among the structural weaknesses limiting economic diversification.

Registries

As at 2025 Eritrea's national vital statistics were still derived from Demographic and Health Surveys of 1995, 2002 and 2010. The country had no functional and complete civil registration and vital statistics system. Civil registration is the country's most deteriorated governance indicator of the past decade, down 37.5 points over 2014-2023 to 12.5 out of 100, 53rd of 54 African states.

Since 2001 six separate zoba-level digital population registers have run, each maintained independently, with no central national file (January 2021). They are used to print residence cards and family identity cards. Registration forms differ across regions, making records very difficult to collate into one national database. Civil status offices have yet to be established in some parts of the country, and birth registration is rare in rural areas. A birth must be registered within 90 days at a civil registration office, late registration carrying a penalty, and a birth certificate issues on registration.

All land vests in the State, and the Cadastral Office of the Ministry of Land, Water and Environment is the sole national land register, created by Proclamation No. 95/1997. By 2023 it had moved registration to digital records except for the spatial part. Scanning of old records had stalled on delayed equipment procurement, and registration made mandatory in 1997 remained voluntary in practice. The Business Information Register Proclamation No. 124/2002 obliges the Ministry of Trade and Industry to keep a national business register. Businesses must report to it each year by 15 January. In 2020 registering a business and obtaining a tax identification number were separate steps with no system integration.

No election has been held since 1993 (April 2026). A draft Electoral Law, its proclamation number left blank, provides for an independent Electoral Commission, a voter register and voting cards.

Sectoral management information systems

By the end of November 2025 Eritrea's Integrated Financial Management Information System had completed pilot rollout and user acceptance testing at the Ministries of Finance, Education and Agriculture. It was generating reconciled reports and had been declared ready for go-live. Its seven modules cover budgeting, procurement, payroll, cash management, accounting, asset control and reporting. Under the same project the country has been reporting public debt automatically each quarter and each year on the DMFAS platform since 2019.

In September 2023 the Inland Revenue Department's integrated tax-administration system went to tender, for deployment to every Inland Revenue office in the zobas with legacy data migrated from the current system. In June 2026 the Bank rated implementation satisfactory, with licences, hardware and installation complete and the Release 1 requirements specification finished. Eritrea's tax and revenue mobilisation score fell 22.2 points over 2014-2023 to 29.2 out of 100, 48th of 54 African states.

Since 2015 the Ministry of Health has deployed DHIS2 across the national health system, installed locally and run offline over its own networks. Data is entered at zone and sub-zone level and analysed centrally, and the ministry has introduced a tracker program for patient-level data (2024). The 2025 Joint External Evaluation scored early-warning surveillance at 3 out of 5. It pressed for a digitised, USSD-based national electronic disease-reporting system spanning health and agriculture.

Analysis of the Education Management Information System was used to diagnose the barriers behind the 2023-2027 Partnership Compact. In June 2025 the ministry rated its own teacher information system weak. A grant report of April 2026 records that the ministry developed new software to digitise the previously manual collection and management of education data. It also completed the 2022-23 and 2023-24 statistical yearbooks.

In 2022 the social insurance and pension system run by the National Insurance Corporation of Eritrea neither exchanged data with other government systems nor used the national identity number to identify beneficiaries.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Eritrea scored 0.1576 on the UN's E-Government Development Index in 2024, 190th of 193 countries and in the survey's low group.

In January 2026 Eritrea inaugurated ASYCUDA World, the UNCTAD-built customs platform used in more than 100 countries. It replaced a legacy customs system in place since 2007, and the implementation transferred full source code for the platform to the government.

In September 2024 the embassy in the United States moved its consular work online, on a platform built by the foreign ministry's own IT staff. Applications for identity cards, visas and passport renewals start on an online form and are finished in person or by post.

In 2022 there was no tax online service portal and no e-filing or e-payment service. In 2024 digital payments were integrated with no government service at all. Article 13 of the Samoa Agreement, which binds Eritrea, commits the parties to accelerate e-governance and digital public services and to digitalise public registries.

Eritrea's effective administration score rose 7.7 points over 2014-2023 to 32.7 out of 100, 46th of 54 African states. Public administration as a whole fell 7.3 points to 26.8 out of 100 and 49th of 54. That sub-category covers civil registration, statistical capacity, administrative effectiveness, and tax and budget management, and holds the country's overall governance back the hardest.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In July 2026 the government reported a geographic information systems course in Gash Barka. It was meant to address gaps in the region's information system and its documentation practices. In September 2025 the Anseba regional administration ran an earlier course with the national Research and Documentation Center. It covered document organisation, preservation, evaluation and digital document management.

Two such events are on the record between September 2025 and July 2026, in two of the country's six regions.

Rural digital data capture

Health data from the countryside is entered into the national DHIS2 platform at zone and sub-zone level (2024). The platform runs offline over the Ministry of Health's own networks because internet infrastructure is limited. In October 2025 a WHO workshop in Asmara introduced VigiMobile, a smartphone application for reporting adverse events after immunisation and adverse drug reactions. More than 60 national and zonal focal points and specialists attended, and the tool is to move that reporting from paper to a digital system.

In January 2021 civil status offices had yet to be established in some parts of the country, and birth registration was rare in rural areas, where many Eritreans hold no civil status documents. Each zoba keeps its own digital population register.

In 2021 there was no commercial 4G network in the country at all.

In April 2024 Eritrea's education reform programme targeted the zobas holding nomadic, semi-nomadic and remote rural communities, which lag the national average on most education indicators. In August 2025 a digitised national electronic disease-reporting system spanning health and agriculture was still a recommendation.

The rural economy score rose 18.1 points over 2014-2023 to 32.9 out of 100 in 2023, 42nd of 54 African states. It is the single largest driver of Eritrea's decade of governance improvement.

Technology

AI

In April 2026 a wave of AI-generated images and video falsely showing Ethiopian forces occupying Assab and Eritrea's president fleeing circulated. The material was made with consumer tools and widely shared by pro-Ethiopian accounts. Eritrean-aligned users answered with AI video of Ethiopian troops surrendering.

In June 2026 Eritrea had no dedicated AI law, no published national AI strategy and no AI regulator. In October 2026 the Africa AI Governance Index scored Eritrea 0.08 out of 4, last of 54 states.

On the vendor's account, the health ministry extended CAD4TB to eight more sites in 2026, after six installations in 2024. CAD4TB is offline software that reads chest X-rays for tuberculosis, taken up to screen people in remote areas. In 2026 GeezLab's research programme released Tigrinya and Tigre datasets and models openly, outside the state. They include a 10.6K-pair question-answering benchmark and a named-entity corpus led by Mai Nefhi engineering students, with base language models in progress.

ICT Industry

Polytech, with three branches in Asmara and one in Massawa, teaches computer technology, electrical and electronic technology and audio-visual technology (July 2026). Mining companies run 60 MW of captive generation outside the national grid (February 2026).

Every external commitment on Eritrea's digital record is a grant or technical assistance to the public sector: there is no loan, no guarantee, no equity and no commercial or private-sector finance at all (September 2026).

Innovation ecosystem

In August 2026 the information ministry's outlet reported that Shambko Apps Group, a self-organised collective, had released five Tigrinya-language mobile applications built to the national curriculum. Afro Verse, a virtual-reality platform rendering traditional Hidmo housing in three dimensions, was shown in a first demo version on 27 August 2026.

The Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Hub is based with the National Union of Eritrean Youth and Students, and has run since 2023. In March 2025 it held a national science and innovation fair with all six regions taking part. It gives programming, robotics and electronics training to secondary-school students. AI-generated displays appeared among the innovation exhibits at the National Festival in Asmara (July 2026).

In 2026 the country's startup base was still nascent. Eritrea is not among the 133 economies ranked in the 2024 WIPO Global Innovation Index, the underlying data required for inclusion not being available. The statutory research mandate over information science and technology sits with ESTDA, under its founding proclamation of 2002.

Capacity

Literacy

Eritrea scored zero out of 100 for education quality in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states. The score fell 16.7 points over the decade, one of the ten most deteriorated of the 96 indicators the Ibrahim Index tracks for the country. The same index places human resources in education among Eritrea's ten best scores, at 80.9 out of 100 in 2023, 29th in Africa and up 4.7 points over the decade.

Education enrolment stood at 27.0 out of 100 in 2023, 44th of 54. The education sub-category as a whole stood at 38.7, 43rd, down 4.1 points over 2014-2023. A laptop programme for high-school students was launched in 2019. The Ministry of Education launched an ICT in education initiative in January 2005. Its 2018-2022 sector plan still found ICT hard to bring into schools for lack of facilities, equipment and teacher training.

Training and skills

Mai-Nefhi College of Engineering and Technology conferred qualifications on 297 graduates on 18 July 2026. They took 289 first degrees and eight diplomas, and a quarter were women. The list included degrees in computer science and engineering and diplomas in computer application technology and in computer hardware and networking technology. The college succeeds the Eritrea Institute of Technology, established at Mai-Nefhi in 2004 and restructured into autonomous colleges in 2018. By its 2019 commencement the institute offered a computer engineering degree and diplomas in computer, communication and electrical and electronics technology. Its College of Science offered a computer application diploma.

In January 2026 a ten-month programme accompanying the customs digitisation trained 100 customs officers, IT specialists and private-sector clearing agents. UNECA's African Centre for Statistics trained more than 25 Ministry of Land, Water and Environment staff over four weeks in Asmara, closing on 27 February 2026. The course covered remote sensing, satellite land-use and land-cover classification and watershed analysis using ArcGIS Pro and QGIS. It was the second phase of a multi-year partnership with that ministry. In July 2026 government employees across the Gash Barka regional administration and its sector branches completed a course in geographic information systems.

In November 2025 instruction given to new recruits and serving police officers covered general computer maintenance and networking, and not any electronic records or case-management system.

In June 2026 the Sawa and higher-education branch of the National Union of Eritrean Youth and Students ran a three-month vocational programme for college students. Computer technology was among its trades. Polytech, a technical institute with sites in Asmara and Massawa, runs AI courses for beginners and content creators. About 50 students completed them in 2026. The state technical and vocational system grew from two schools with roughly 350 students in 1991/92 to six with 4,640 in 2010/11.

Research institutions

Eritrea's National Health Laboratory has built genomic-sequencing capacity since 2024 with WHO support (July 2026). It trained 15 professionals, 13 from the Ministry of Health and two from the Ministry of Agriculture. It has applied sequencing nationally for the first time to malaria and antimicrobial-resistance surveillance, producing resistance-pattern data not previously available at national level.

In November 2025 the Research and Documentation Centre had been supported through three successive EU-funded projects. Working with Italy's University of Pavia, it was equipped to digitise and preserve historic archives, manuscripts and audiovisual material. A draft Law on National Archives and Libraries has been prepared with expert input from the centre and the university.

Proclamation No. 122/2002 established a National Science and Technology Council chaired by the President. It also created the Eritrean Science and Technology Development Agency and a National Science and Technology Advisory Board. The proclamation gave the agency's research office an explicit mandate over information science and technology among its research domains. It also empowered the agency to found research institutions, educational institutions and research foundations of its own. The Eritrea Population and Health Survey 2025 was completed using digital data-collection tools, through the statistics office.

Inclusion

Access to services

More than 679,000 Eritrean refugees and asylum-seekers were known to UNHCR worldwide by December 2025, hosted mainly by Ethiopia, Sudan, Germany, Uganda, Egypt and Switzerland. In April 2026 the agency issued a Guidance Note on Eritrea, replacing eligibility guidelines in place since 2011. Eritrea hosts 119 refugees under UNHCR's mandate (2026). It is party to none of the three refugee conventions and has no national law on refugee or asylum-seeker protection.

Equal access to public services scored 19.0 out of 100 in 2023, 47th of 54 African states and 0.8 points below its level ten years earlier. In 2024 obtaining a SIM card required government approval. The UNDP compass places civic engagement at its lowest stage, technology not being used for interaction between citizens and government. The UN's 2024 e-government survey ranked Eritrea 190th of 193, reflecting very limited online service provision. EriTel runs an internet complaint hub, a web form for reporting connectivity and web-access faults.

In March 2026 the UK passport authority confirmed that the identity card is a precondition of passport issue. It is required of every Eritrean aged 18 and above. In November 2025 the card remained in limited circulation, most citizens still holding the older hand-written version. In January 2021 civil status offices had yet to be established in some parts of the country. Limited access to documentation raises the risk of statelessness (2026).

Eritrea acceded to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities in December 2024. In July 2026 the African Committee of Experts on the Rights and Welfare of the Child found no independent children's ombudsperson able to receive and investigate complaints, and recommended one. In August 2024 there were no automatic teller machines, no debit or credit cards and no internet banking.

Digital divides

In March 2026 the ITU reported that Eritrea's most recent household survey of ICT use dates from 2002. That is the oldest household-survey data year of any country in the ITU's Africa subregional dataset. The Ibrahim Index put inclusion and equality at 23.4 out of 100 in 2023, 51st of 54 African states and 3.7 points below its level a decade earlier.

The mobile communications score reached 32.6 out of 100 in 2023, 51st of 54. It was the second most improved of the index's 96 indicators for Eritrea. The whole ten-year gain was measured from a score of zero in 2014. Internet and computers stood at 23.5 out of 100, 40th of 54, up 22.4 points over the same period. In 2021 there was no commercial 4G network in the country at all.

In 2018 electricity, on which school ICT depends, was concentrated in the major cities and their surroundings, remote areas lacking any supply unless communities install diesel generators.

The Global Partnership for Education's April 2024 programme carries US$11.82m in system transformation funding and US$2.2m in system capacity funding, with UNICEF as grant agent. It targets the zobas holding nomadic, semi-nomadic and remote rural communities that lag the national average. Women remained a minority of the graduates at Mai-Nefhi College of Engineering and Technology's July 2026 commencement.

Eritrea is named in none of the 26 regional East African digital commitments on its finance record — the East African Community and IGAD-anchored programmes for digital integration, AI research, digital skills, cross-border payments and connectivity all run past it (September 2026).

Data

National statistics

In May 2026 the National Statistics Office finished fieldwork on the fourth round of the Eritrea Population and Health Survey. It covered 9,794 households across 405 enumeration areas at a 98.4% completion rate (May 2026). It was the first national survey of its kind in fifteen years, run on computer-assisted interviewing in place of paper. UNFPA carried half of its US$535,000 budget (2025).

In 2023 Eritrea had held no population, agricultural or economic census in the past twenty years. Nor had it held a household, labour force, health, agricultural or business survey in the past ten. In September 2022 it was one of two African states not to have scheduled a census at the start of the 2020 round. On the World Bank's 2023 reading the data infrastructure pillar scores below 20 per cent, data use between 20 and 49, and data products between 50 and 69. Data services and data sources are not scored at all.

In January 2007 a draft National Statistical Proclamation making the office the national statistical centre had been submitted to government but not enacted. No later enactment has been found. The office's capacity score reached 35.9 out of 100 in 2023, 39th of 54 African states and up 18.3 points over the decade. The index holds no Eritrean data for eighteen of its 96 indicators. UNECA ran a two-week training in Eritrea in January 2026 on GDP compilation under the 2008 System of National Accounts and on its national accounts database system.

In April 2026 the African children's rights committee found that data on child labour, violence against children, child marriage and children in conflict with the law is not systematically collected. In July 2026 UNICEF's country programme for 2027 to 2031 named digital modernisation in the statistics office among the efforts to close gaps in data on children. It also named an updated Statistical Master Plan.

Open data

Eritrea scored 0.0 out of 100 for accessibility of public records in 2023, last of 54 African states. The score fell 6.3 points over the decade. It scored 12.5 for disclosure of public records, 47th of 54 and unchanged across the same ten years. No freedom-of-information law has been adopted (January 2026).

In 2022 UNDP's Digital Development Compass placed the open-government dimension at its lowest stage: closed, with no information sharing and no feedback or evaluation mechanisms. Nothing is disclosed publicly about government data systems, data-sharing arrangements or interoperability work. In July 2026 WFP maintained subnational rainfall indicators for Eritrea on the Humanitarian Data Exchange, refreshed roughly every two weeks.

The general part of the 2023 Samoa Agreement, to which Eritrea is party, commits its signatories to using digital solutions to widen public access to information. In November 2025 a draft Law on National Archives and Libraries had been prepared with expert input from the Research and Documentation Centre and the University of Pavia.

Use of satellite data

IGAD's regional climate centre issued the most recent dry-conditions alert covering Eritrea, a Climate Watch advisory of 10 August 2026. It was valid to 11 September and raised alert levels on May-to-July rainfall deficits. It used standardised precipitation analysis alongside soil-moisture and vegetation-stress monitoring. In December 2024 Eritrea's delegation to the UNCCD's sixteenth conference of the parties listed setting up a modern national meteorological service among the country's forward strategies against drought and desertification.

In July 2026 WFP computed forecast, preliminary and final rainfall indicators for Eritrean subnational units. It draws on CHIRPS satellite precipitation imagery blended with station data and short-term forecasts. They are published on the Humanitarian Data Exchange and refreshed roughly every two weeks. In July 2026 Eurac Research took high-resolution hydrological modelling and its geodesign platform to Asmara's Toker and Mainefhi catchments across two rounds of workshops.

In 2019 the Ministry of Agriculture created an Agricultural Strategic Information System Division. It brings GIS and remote sensing, IT, an agricultural innovation unit and an expert system under one division. It runs an agro-meteorological station network whose readings are passed to agricultural extension workers. In September 2025 FAO trained its desert locust information officers, who file monthly locust bulletins, on the eLocust3 survey tool and satellite image analysis in Google Earth Engine. UNECA's training of Ministry of Land, Water and Environment staff in remote sensing and satellite land-cover classification closed in Asmara in February 2026. A domesticated UN Integrated Geospatial Information Framework action plan for Eritrea was filed with UNECA on 6 April 2026. In 2023 the Cadastral Office's conversion of land registration from manual to digital had still not reached the spatial component.

Most of the satellite and geospatial work bearing on Eritrea is run from outside the country, by UN agencies, the regional climate centre and a European research institute; the agriculture ministry's information division is the state's own share of it.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In August 2026 the United States was moving toward removing sanctions it imposed in November 2021. They cover the Eritrean Defense Force, the ruling PFDJ, the Hidri Trust and the Red Sea Trading Corporation. Other US restrictions and individual designations stood under separate legal authorities.

China activities

No Chinese-funded or Chinese-built digital or data project was under way in Eritrea as at September 2026.

EU activities

In July 2026 Eurac Research's NBS4Water project ran two rounds of geodesign workshops in Asmara. They applied high-resolution hydrological modelling to the city's Toker and Mainefhi catchments. Its money comes from Italy's Autonomous Province of Bolzano, not from Brussels.

In November 2025 the Research and Documentation Centre in Asmara had been supported through three successive EU-funded projects. Working with Italy's University of Pavia, it was equipped to digitise and preserve archives, manuscripts and audiovisual material. The same work has produced a draft Law on National Archives and Libraries, prepared with expert input from the Centre, the University of Pavia and an in-country team.

The treaty frame is the Samoa Agreement, published in the EU Official Journal in December 2023. It lists the State of Eritrea among the OACPS parties. It asks of Eritrea what it asks of every party: accelerating e-governance and the digitalisation of public registries and strengthening national statistical systems. It also asks for a personal-data-protection regime overseen by an independent supervisory authority, and for digital solutions that widen public access to information. All four commitments sit in the agreement's General Part, which binds the whole membership alike.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf or Emirati investment in Eritrea's data or digital infrastructure was under way as at September 2026.

India activities

No Indian government or industry involvement in Eritrea's data and digital sector was under way as at September 2026.

Digital sovereignty

The ASYCUDA World customs platform inaugurated in January 2026 came with complete transfer of its source code to the government, and national IT staff had already begun customising it. Its central servers sit in a solar-powered server room serving every customs station over an internal network. A ten-month programme trained 100 customs officers, IT specialists and clearing agents to operate it.

No data-localisation requirement applies to the storage or processing of personal or government data (2026). In 2022 there was no shared cloud platform for government entities and no government cloud hosting policy. EriTel's Nextcloud private-cloud service is hosted entirely in its own data centres (August 2026). African Development Bank financing equipped the National Statistics Office with a data centre of its own. As at September 2026 no statement had been found of where the six zoba population registers and the identity-card database are hosted, or under whose jurisdiction.

In June 2026 Eritrea had no published AI strategy, no AI law and no AI regulator. In January 2026 it sat outside IGAD's Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, the region's programme on data governance and cross-border data flows.
