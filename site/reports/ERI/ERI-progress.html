This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-01.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap A national action plan to domesticate the UN geospatial information framework is the first interoperability roadmap on the base (action plan).
Full recordEritrea produced a national action plan in April 2026 to domesticate the United Nations Integrated Geospatial Information Framework, intended to improve the integration and interoperability of geospatial information and national data systems (action plan).

The position it moves from is comprehensive absence: a 2022 structured assessment records no government enterprise architecture framework, no government interoperability framework, no data quality framework and no government service bus platform (dataset).

The plan is geospatial in scope rather than whole-of-government, and the base holds no adoption date, budget or implementing body for it.

Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation A verification pass dated August 2026 confirms no comprehensive data-protection law in force (redetermination).
Full recordA dated redetermination as of 1 August 2026 confirms the position is unchanged: no comprehensive data-protection law in force, no supervisory authority established and no enforcement decisions published (redetermination).

A regional ministerial assessment places Eritrea's data-protection policy area at a nascent stage without legislation or regulatory oversight, grouped with Djibouti, South Sudan and Sudan against neighbours that have enacted frameworks with supervisory authorities (policy matrix).

A trade regulatory profile records that Eritrea lacks a comprehensive legal framework for data protection, with no data-protection officer, impact-assessment or retention requirements (profile).

This is a searched absence rather than an unexamined one: three independent assessments across four years agree.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation Computer offences sit in two articles of the general Penal Code; no dedicated cybersecurity statute exists (Penal Code).
Full recordEritrea's only identifiable computer-offence provisions are Articles 374 and 375 of the 2015 Penal Code, covering unauthorised use of a computer — unauthorised access, interception, alteration or destruction of data, interference with lawful data use — and an aggravated form (Penal Code).

A country cybersecurity assessment records, item by item, no cybercrime legislation, no cybersecurity regulation, no officially recognised national computer incident response team, no national cybersecurity strategy, no governance roadmap and no responsible agency (assessment).

Nothing in the window changed either position. Criminal provisions exist; the institutional apparatus a cybersecurity regime needs does not.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id No digital-identity statute exists; the closest instrument is the 1992 nationality proclamation (proclamation).
Full recordNo dedicated digital-identity statute was located. The closest identifiable legal instrument is the Nationality Proclamation 21/1992, still in force, which provides the legal basis for nationality certification through the Department of Internal Affairs and contains no digital-identity, electronic-identity, biometric or electronic-transactions framework (proclamation).

Identity cards are nonetheless required by other systems: anti-money-laundering law obliges financial institutions to identify every customer (proclamation), and a national identity card is required before a passport is issued (country guidance).

So the card carries statutory weight without a statute defining it, and nothing regulates what data it holds or who may read it.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A regional peer assessment records a state-owned financial system with no cash machines, cards or internet banking (evaluation).
Full recordA regional anti-money-laundering peer assessment, adopted in July 2025 after an on-site visit in 2024, states that Eritrea's financial system is state-owned and offers simple and less sophisticated services, with no automatic teller machines, debit or credit cards, or internet banking (evaluation).

There is therefore nothing for payments legislation to regulate, and the base holds no payment systems act, no e-money regulation and no licensing regime. The only draft text addressing digital finance is an unofficial consultation draft (drafts).

The one instrument on the ledger is a central bank legal notice ordering cash deposits, which is currency management rather than payments law.

Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability No law enables data exchange between government systems; a 2022 assessment records no service bus and no cross-system exchange (dataset).
Full recordA structured government-technology dataset for 2022 records no government interoperability framework and no system that exchanges data across financial management, human resources, payroll, customs, tax or social-insurance systems (dataset).

A regional ministerial assessment places Eritrea's data-protection policy area at a nascent stage without legislation or regulatory oversight (policy matrix), so there is also no legal basis on which systems could lawfully share personal data.

The one system that did move is closed rather than connected: the customs platform runs on an intranet architecture independent of continuous internet access (account).

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations A review dated June 2026 records no dedicated artificial-intelligence law and no national strategy (review).
Full recordA legal-status review dated 8 June 2026 states that Eritrea has no dedicated artificial-intelligence law, no national artificial-intelligence strategy, no data-protection statute and no data-protection authority, and that the nearest legal anchor is the privacy article of the 1997 constitution, which has never entered into force (review).

The 2025 government artificial-intelligence readiness index, in its January 2026 edition, scores Eritrea zero on policy capacity and ranks it 193rd overall on a score of 13.69 (index).

The absence is total rather than partial: no instrument, no draft and no institution with a mandate.

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation No electronic transactions framework is in force; the only relevant texts are unofficial consultation drafts (drafts).
Full recordA diaspora legal-reform initiative published around eighty consultation-draft acts, codes and regulations intended to reconstruct Eritrea's legal order, including a proposed commercial code and a payment systems and digital finance act addressing electronic transactions (drafts). These are unofficial and carry no legislative status.

The standing assessment is that Eritrea lacks a comprehensive legal framework in the digital-trade area, with the only body holding sector-supervision power having little to no decision-making authority (profile).

Nothing on the base records a government bill, a consultation or a stated intention to legislate on electronic transactions.

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The statistics office has never operated under an enacted statistics law; a draft proclamation was recorded as pending (review).
Full recordA mid-term review of statistical capacity support records that the statistical proclamation, the equivalent of a statistics act, had been submitted to government and that the formal issue was still pending (review).

No later record of enactment was located at the probe date of 31 August 2026, so the national statistics office has operated for at least two decades without a statutory mandate.

This matters for the surveys the base does hold: a population and health survey was carried out without a law defining who owns the data, who may access it or on what terms it is published.

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation No freedom of information law has been adopted, a UN review body states plainly (review contribution).
Full recordA formal contribution to Eritrea's universal periodic review states that no freedom of information law has been adopted, and that the constitutional guarantee of access to information sits in a 1997 constitution whose operative reality is the 1996 press proclamation (review contribution).

The 2026 world press freedom index places Eritrea last of 180 countries and territories for the third consecutive year (index).

The absence is legislative and practical at once: there is no statute, no request procedure and no appeal route, so nothing measures how much information is released.

Full recordThe proclamation establishing the Eritrean Standards Institution expressly adopts the International System of Units as Eritrea's legal measurement system (proclamation).

Full recordA water project held two rounds of geodesign workshops in Asmara in July 2026 with more than twenty government, academic, private-sector and non-governmental participants, using a geodesign platform with high-resolution hydrological modelling for two catchments, alongside a modelling training session for Eritrean technical experts (workshops).

Full recordThe 2026 world press freedom index places Eritrea last of 180 countries and territories for the third consecutive year, alongside China and North Korea, describing a state of press freedom that has hardly changed (index).

There is no forum on the record — no independent press, no permitted civil society, no online space — in which digital or data policy could be publicly argued.

Registries Population register Population registration runs on six separate regional registers with no central national register (technical description).
Full recordEritrea's population registration has been digital at regional level since 2001: six separate registers, one per zoba, each recording an identity number, family and resident numbers, nationality and civil data, from which residence cards and family identity cards are printed as extracts. Birth registration feeds them through the local family register. There is no central national register (technical description).

A regional treaty body, reviewing Eritrea's fourth periodic report in April 2026, recognised the development of a five-year strategic civil registration and vital statistics plan for 2024-2028 (concluding observations).

A country-of-origin report carries a dedicated section on the register and the cards drawn from it (report).

The base holds no coverage rate and no statement that the six registers reconcile with one another.

AI Use of AI in sectoral management information systems One agricultural expert system, described by the ministry as part of artificial intelligence, gives farmers guidance when extension workers are unavailable (account).
Full recordThe agriculture ministry's strategic information system division, established in 2019, includes an agricultural expert system that the ministry itself describes as part of artificial intelligence, used to give farmers guidance when extension workers are unavailable (account).

It is the only documented instance of a named artificial-intelligence component inside an Eritrean sectoral management information system, and the account is the ministry's own with nothing testing its use or effect.

The base holds no later statement of whether the system still runs.
 No changea first statement of the position and the only such system on the base
AI Development of national / regional AI systems Eri-Chat, an offline AI application built by a ministry developer, launched in April 2025 and was shown at an Asmara diplomatic event in October.
Full record2025-11-01 — a profile of the developer Naod Goitom Isaac describes Eri-Chat, an offline AI application launched in April 2025 and shown to ambassadors and UN officials at an Asmara UN Day event on 24 October 2025. It works in English only; local-language support waits on training data the developer says does not exist in sufficient quantity.

It is one application written by a single software developer at the Ministry of Information rather than a national programme, and it is the only AI system of any kind on this ledger.

AI Control of AI abuse AI-generated war imagery circulated widely, met only by external fact-checking; no domestic law or takedown route exists (fact check).
Full recordA fact-checking service documented AI-generated images and videos through 2026 — tanks entering Assab, the president pursued by a foreign army chief, troops surrendering — circulating from both pro-Ethiopian and pro-Eritrean accounts, drawing large engagement and inflaming war narratives (fact check).

The response on the record is entirely external. Eritrea has no artificial-intelligence law, no national strategy and no regulator (review), and no data-protection authority to which a person depicted could complain (redetermination).

There is no domestic mechanism of any kind on the base for identifying, labelling or removing synthetic media.
 No changea first statement of the position and the only response on the record is external

Innovation ecosystem Technology hubs One technology hub is documented: a youth union training centre teaching electronics, programming and robotics (account).
Full recordThe Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Hub, established in 2023 by the national union of Eritrean youth and students, runs three levels of six-month technical training in electronics, Python, Scratch, robotics and soldering, primarily for students in grades 9 to 11 (account).

It is the clearest documented operating technology hub in Eritrea, and it is a training centre rather than an incubator: nothing on the base records a company founded through it, investment placed or a product taken to market.

No commercial or university-attached hub is recorded anywhere.
 No changea first statement of the position and one hub on the base
Innovation ecosystem Tech startup ecosystem A self-organised collective released five Tigrinya-language education applications built to the national curriculum, with no stated funding or distribution channel.
Full record2026-08-15 — an interview carried by the state information ministry describes five applications from the Shambko Apps Group: literacy, puzzle, cultural-heritage and arithmetic tools written against the national curriculum, with a stated plan to extend them to Eritrea's eight other national languages.

The group is a private collective rather than a state programme. Nothing is stated about download counts, distribution channel or funding, and no hub, incubator, investor or company registration appears elsewhere on the ledger — so this is one collective on the record, not evidence of a startup base.

Literacy Digital literacy (civil service) One regional training reached 108 government employees in computer technology and networking, 63 of them women (report).
Full recordA workers' confederation branch provided vocational training in computer technology and computer networking to 108 government employees and force members, 63 of them women, reported in November 2025 (report).

It is the clearest documented evidence that digital training reaching government employees exists, and it is a regional activity rather than a permanent nationwide scheme; no standing instrument was located at the probe date. The 2005 education ICT policy requires training only for education-sector staff (policy).

The base holds one course and no measure of civil-service digital skills.

Training and skills DT-related university facilities and qualifications The engineering college showed 26 research papers and 29 demonstrations from 1,694 students, 39% of them women (report).
Full recordMai Nefhi College of Engineering and Technology held its engineering day in May 2026 under the theme of innovation for national development, showing 26 research papers and engineering solutions, 32 illustrations and 29 engineering demonstrations, from a college of 1,694 students of whom 39% are women, across five-year degree and three-year diploma programmes (report).

The qualifications themselves are longstanding: the institute of technology, established in 2004 and restructured into autonomous colleges in 2018, offers computer engineering degrees and computer, communication and electrical technology diplomas (account).

The base holds student numbers and event output, and no graduate count or employment destination.

Access to services Citizen feedback portals The state telecommunications provider runs a web complaint form for internet faults; no whole-of-government channel exists (complaint hub).
Full recordThe sole state telecommunications provider operates a live web form for internet-connectivity, website-access and web-application problems, with telephone and email alternatives (complaint hub). It is the clearest operational digital feedback channel located, and it is narrowly sector-specific.

Nothing on the base records a government complaint portal, a service-standards charter or a published response record, and there is no freedom of information law under which an unanswered complaint could be pursued (review contribution).

The base holds no volume of complaints made or resolved.

National statistics Censuses and surveys The fourth Eritrea Population and Health Survey completed fieldwork in 9,794 households across all six regions, with the report due in 2026.
Full record2026-05-08 — the National Statistics Office reported the fourth Eritrea Population and Health Survey complete: 9,794 households across 405 enumeration areas, a 98.4% household completion rate, and collection by computer-assisted personal interview.

Around 400 tables over 20 chapters are drafted, with writing, printing and dissemination scheduled through 2026, so the fieldwork is finished and the published output is not. Technical support came from UNECA and an Oslo consultancy under the UNDP-led Joint Programme on Data for Development. It is the most substantial statistical work the base holds for Eritrea.
 Advanced
National statistics Statistics from administrative data The statistics office compiled national accounts for 2023 and began collection for 2024, the only administrative statistical production the base records.
Full record2026-05-08 — alongside the household survey, the National Statistics Office is reported to have compiled the 2023 national accounts and started data collection for 2024.

That is the whole of the ledger's evidence on statistics drawn from administrative sources, and it is second-hand: the accounts themselves are not on the base, and no statement of which administrative systems feed them appears either. No statistics law and no national strategy for the development of statistics is on record for Eritrea, so this production rests on the office's own practice.
