Eritrea: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-10-2  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository

Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-10.

Progress values. Movement — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Movement, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy No national digital transformation strategy is held. What the government has adopted is sectoral: a policy for technology in education and a geospatial framework action plan.
Full recordThe base holds two adopted policy instruments touching digital questions and no overarching one. The first is the policy for technology in education, which governs one sector. The second is the domesticated geospatial information framework action plan, which governs one class of data.
Against that, the telecommunications union's own survey of national plans records the country as having no adopted broadband strategy. A country with a sector policy for schools, an action plan for maps and nothing for the network they both run on has no strategy in the sense this indicator asks about, and nothing on the base records one being drafted.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The sector rests on a 1998 communications proclamation and its regulations; the only sectoral policy named is for education.
Full recordThe base holds no national digital or ICT strategy for this country. What it holds instead is the sector's legal furniture, listed in a country profile and not held as text: Communications Proclamation 102 of 1998, which set the legal basis for communications, the Regulations on Telecommunications Networks and Services issued as Legal Notice 43 of 1998, and a 2003 proclamation establishing the state telecommunications corporation.
The one sector-specific policy named anywhere is a National Policy for ICT in Education setting goals for connectivity, ministry systems, staff training and school use of technology. The 2018-2022 National Education Sector Plan carries it forward, aiming at technology infrastructure and e-learning options, on the back of an ICT in education initiative the ministry launched in January 2005.
The sector plan states its own result: incorporating technology in education remains difficult for want of facilities, equipment and teacher training. Neither the policy nor the plan is held as a document, and the profile carrying them is a legacy platform its publisher states is no longer updated.

Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy The telecommunications union's own survey of national plans records the country as having none. Nothing later on the base records one being adopted.
Full recordThe absence is documented rather than merely unfound. The telecommunications union's survey of broadband strategies, policies and plans carries the country in its table with no plan against it; neighbouring entries in the same table carry theirs, so the reading is unambiguous.
What governs the sector instead is the communications proclamation of 1998, which makes the ministry's communications department sole regulator and sets the development of national communications capability as an objective without setting a target for it. Nothing published since names a broadband target, a coverage obligation or a date.

Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy No storage or cloud strategy is held. The nearest thing to a stated position is the state operator's own private cloud service, hosted in its own data centres.
Full recordNothing on the base states where government data should be held, on whose equipment, or under what terms. The one document that describes domestic hosting at all is a commercial one: the state operator's private cloud service page, which offers subscription storage hosted entirely in the operator's own facilities.
That makes domestic hosting the practical default without anyone having chosen it as a policy. No public body's hosting arrangement is published, no procurement rule addresses it, and no residency or localisation instrument was located.

Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap A national action plan to domesticate the UN geospatial information framework is the first interoperability roadmap on the base (action plan).
Full recordEritrea produced a national action plan in April 2026 to domesticate the United Nations Integrated Geospatial Information Framework, intended to improve the integration and interoperability of geospatial information and national data systems (action plan).
The position it moves from is comprehensive absence: a 2022 structured assessment records no government enterprise architecture framework, no government interoperability framework, no data quality framework and no government service bus platform (dataset).
The plan is geospatial in scope rather than whole-of-government, and the base holds no adoption date, budget or implementing body for it.

Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy The government scores zero on policy capacity in the 2025 artificial-intelligence readiness index, ranking 193rd (index).
Full recordThe government artificial-intelligence readiness index for 2025, updated in January 2026 across 195 governments, scores Eritrea zero on policy capacity and ranks it 193rd overall on 13.69 (index). Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies No localisation or residency rule is held. Domestic hosting happens anyway, because the only provider-hosted cloud on the base is the state operator's own.
Full recordNo instrument states where public or personal data may be held. There is no hosting duty on public bodies, no rule on transfers out and no authority that could grant or refuse one, the data-protection law itself being absent.
What produces localisation in practice is the shape of the market rather than a rule. The state operator's private cloud is hosted in its own data centres, and no foreign provider is recorded serving the country. Data stays where it is because there is nowhere else for it to go, which is not the same position as a country that has chosen it.

Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy The geospatial framework action plan is the first data-governance instrument on the base (action plan).
Full recordThe April 2026 action plan to domesticate the geospatial information framework covers governance arrangements for national geospatial data, the first instrument of its kind on the base (action plan).
Against it, a 2022 structured assessment records no data quality framework and no system exchanging data across financial management, human resources, payroll, customs, tax or social insurance (dataset), and there is no data-protection law or authority (redetermination).
A sectoral plan is not a national data-governance policy, and nothing on the base claims one exists.

Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy No open data policy, portal or licence is held. Government data reaches the public as survey reports, not as datasets.
Full recordNothing on the base names an open data policy, a dataset catalogue or a licence under which government data is released. The national population and health survey of 2025 is the largest data-collection exercise in fifteen years, and its output was a report scheduled for the following year rather than a published dataset.
The pattern holds across the base: the geospatial capacity programme produces trained analysts and classified imagery for ministry use, and nothing produced under it is published. Data here is produced for the state's own use, and the question of releasing it has not been put.

Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation A verification pass dated August 2026 confirms no comprehensive data-protection law in force (redetermination).
Full recordA dated redetermination as of 1 August 2026 confirms the position is unchanged: no comprehensive data-protection law in force, no supervisory authority established and no enforcement decisions published (redetermination).
A regional ministerial assessment places Eritrea's data-protection policy area at a nascent stage without legislation or regulatory oversight, grouped with Djibouti, South Sudan and Sudan against neighbours that have enacted frameworks with supervisory authorities (policy matrix).
A trade regulatory profile records that Eritrea lacks a comprehensive legal framework for data protection, with no data-protection officer, impact-assessment or retention requirements (profile).
This is a searched absence rather than an unexamined one: three independent assessments across four years agree.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation Computer offences sit in two articles of the general Penal Code; no dedicated cybersecurity statute exists (Penal Code).
Full recordEritrea's only identifiable computer-offence provisions are Articles 374 and 375 of the 2015 Penal Code, covering unauthorised use of a computer — unauthorised access, interception, alteration or destruction of data, interference with lawful data use — and an aggravated form (Penal Code).
A country cybersecurity assessment records, item by item, no cybercrime legislation, no cybersecurity regulation, no officially recognised national computer incident response team, no national cybersecurity strategy, no governance roadmap and no responsible agency (assessment).
Nothing in the window changed either position. Criminal provisions exist; the institutional apparatus a cybersecurity regime needs does not.
Nor is there a strategy behind the missing statute: a country profile records no public national cybersecurity strategy, so neither the legislative nor the policy layer exists on the record.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id No digital-identity statute exists; the closest instrument is the 1992 nationality proclamation (proclamation).
Full recordNo dedicated digital-identity statute was located. The closest identifiable legal instrument is the Nationality Proclamation 21/1992, still in force, which provides the legal basis for nationality certification through the Department of Internal Affairs and contains no digital-identity, electronic-identity, biometric or electronic-transactions framework (proclamation).
Identity cards are nonetheless required by other systems: anti-money-laundering law obliges financial institutions to identify every customer (proclamation), and a national identity card is required before a passport is issued (country guidance).
So the card carries statutory weight without a statute defining it, and nothing regulates what data it holds or who may read it.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A regional peer assessment records a state-owned financial system with no cash machines, cards or internet banking (evaluation).
Full recordA regional anti-money-laundering peer assessment, adopted in July 2025 after an on-site visit in 2024, states that Eritrea's financial system is state-owned and offers simple and less sophisticated services, with no automatic teller machines, debit or credit cards, or internet banking (evaluation).
There is therefore nothing for payments legislation to regulate, and the base holds no payment systems act, no e-money regulation and no licensing regime. The only draft text addressing digital finance is an unofficial consultation draft (drafts).
The one instrument on the ledger is a central bank legal notice ordering cash deposits, which is currency management rather than payments law.

Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability No law enables data exchange between government systems; a 2022 assessment records no service bus and no cross-system exchange (dataset).
Full recordA structured government-technology dataset for 2022 records no government interoperability framework and no system that exchanges data across financial management, human resources, payroll, customs, tax or social-insurance systems (dataset).
A regional ministerial assessment places Eritrea's data-protection policy area at a nascent stage without legislation or regulatory oversight (policy matrix), so there is also no legal basis on which systems could lawfully share personal data.
The one system that did move is closed rather than connected: the customs platform runs on an intranet architecture independent of continuous internet access (account).

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations A review dated June 2026 records no dedicated artificial-intelligence law and no national strategy (review).
Full recordA legal-status review dated 8 June 2026 states that Eritrea has no dedicated artificial-intelligence law, no national artificial-intelligence strategy, no data-protection statute and no data-protection authority, and that the nearest legal anchor is the privacy article of the 1997 constitution, which has never entered into force (review).
The 2025 government artificial-intelligence readiness index, in its January 2026 edition, scores Eritrea zero on policy capacity and ranks it 193rd overall on a score of 13.69 (index).
The absence is total rather than partial: no instrument, no draft and no institution with a mandate.

Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation No electronic transactions framework is in force; the only relevant texts are unofficial consultation drafts (drafts).
Full recordA diaspora legal-reform initiative published around eighty consultation-draft acts, codes and regulations intended to reconstruct Eritrea's legal order, including a proposed commercial code and a payment systems and digital finance act addressing electronic transactions (drafts). These are unofficial and carry no legislative status.
The standing assessment is that Eritrea lacks a comprehensive legal framework in the digital-trade area, with the only body holding sector-supervision power having little to no decision-making authority (profile).
Nothing on the base records a government bill, a consultation or a stated intention to legislate on electronic transactions.

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The statistics office has never operated under an enacted statistics law; a draft proclamation was recorded as pending (review).
Full recordA mid-term review of statistical capacity support records that the statistical proclamation, the equivalent of a statistics act, had been submitted to government and that the formal issue was still pending (review).
No later record of enactment was located at the probe date of 31 August 2026, so the national statistics office has operated for at least two decades without a statutory mandate.
This matters for the surveys the base does hold: a population and health survey was carried out without a law defining who owns the data, who may access it or on what terms it is published.

Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation No freedom of information law has been adopted, a UN review body states plainly (review contribution).
Full recordA formal contribution to Eritrea's universal periodic review states that no freedom of information law has been adopted, and that the constitutional guarantee of access to information sits in a 1997 constitution whose operative reality is the 1996 press proclamation (review contribution).
The 2026 world press freedom index places Eritrea last of 180 countries and territories for the third consecutive year (index).
The absence is legislative and practical at once: there is no statute, no request procedure and no appeal route, so nothing measures how much information is released.

Data protection Data protection authority No data-protection law and no authority. A legal survey and a regional policy matrix agree that neither exists.
Full recordThere is nothing to supervise and no one to supervise it. A legal survey records no data-protection statute, and a regional policy matrix places the country at the nascent end of its own scale. A digital trade regulatory profile reaches the same finding from a different direction.
The absence has consequences elsewhere on this ledger. Without an authority there is no route for a complaint about personal data, no body that could authorise a transfer out of the country, and nothing that could act on synthetic or manipulated media. Each of those reads as a separate gap; all of them are this one.

Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration The sole non-signatory to the continental trade agreement, and a member of the regional body that its digital integration programme does not cover.
Full recordOf 55 member states of the continental union, only this one has yet to sign the consolidated text of the free trade agreement, with 49 having signed and deposited ratification instruments. That agreement carries the continent's digital-trade and cross-border data provisions.

Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation Nothing to harmonise with. The country has not signed the continental trade agreement and is outside the regional digital programme.
Full recordHarmonisation runs through the two frameworks the country stands outside. The continental free trade agreement, whose protocols carry the digital-trade and data rules member states align to, has not been signed here. The regional body's digital integration programme, which is where harmonised data governance and cybersecurity rules are being drafted for this part of the continent, names seven participating states and not this one.
There is also little on the domestic side to bring into line. No data-protection law, no electronic transactions framework and no cybersecurity strategy exist to be harmonised with anything.

Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure Regional planning still treats Eritrea as a gap: cable and data-infrastructure projects carry no commitment to serve Asmara (overview).
Full recordA regional digital development overview records that planned submarine and festoon cable and regional data-infrastructure projects lack a commitment to serve the region as a whole, including Asmara (overview).
The regional infrastructure master plan treats Eritrea as a connectivity gap whose interconnection to Sudan, Ethiopia or submarine landing points should be implemented in the short term — language describing a planned solution rather than an existing connection, and Eritrea is not listed as an existing landing point (master plan).
Six years on from that plan the base still holds no cable landing and no cross-border interconnection.
 No change
Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers No transfer rule, because there is no data-protection law to carry one, and no participation in the framework that would supply one.
Full recordCross-border transfer is governed by nothing. There is no data-protection statute under which a transfer rule could sit, no authority that could authorise or refuse one, and no adequacy or localisation position of any kind.
Nor is one arriving from outside. The continental agreement whose protocol addresses cross-border flows has not been signed, and the regional programme drafting harmonised rules does not cover the country. The financial system shows what that isolation looks like in practice: the regional peer assessment records a sector largely disconnected from the global financial system, with none of the instruments that would move data across a border in the first place.

Standards National interoperability standards A standards institution since 1995 and a regulator charged with setting technical standards. Neither has published an interoperability standard.
Full recordThe institutional basis exists twice over. The standards institution was constituted in 1995, and the communications proclamation of 1998 makes the ministry's communications department sole sector regulator and sets the establishment of technical standards among its objectives.
What neither has produced is a published standard for how government systems exchange data. Nothing on the base names an interoperability framework, a data dictionary or a common identifier convention, and the systems that do exist run separately: customs on its own platform, tax on another, health on a third, with no exchange layer between them.

Full recordA water project held two rounds of geodesign workshops in Asmara in July 2026 with more than twenty government, academic, private-sector and non-governmental participants, using a geodesign platform with high-resolution hydrological modelling for two catchments, alongside a modelling training session for Eritrean technical experts (workshops).

ICT Infrastructure

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Connectivity Mobile penetration Active connections reached 859,000, or 23.7% of the population, up 8.2% year on year, among the lowest rates in Africa.
Full record2025-11-08 — DataReportal's Digital 2026 report for Eritrea puts active mobile connections at 859,000, 23.7% of the population, 49.3% of them broadband, and up 8.2% on the year. The previous edition is the comparison.
The direction is upward and the level is among the lowest in Africa. Both statements are true, and the second governs what sits downstream of it: a foundational digital ID, mobile money and any service assumed to reach a citizen through a handset all rest on this figure. No operator or regulator return on the ledger tests it.
Coverage is separately reported and much higher than connections: 85% of the population is covered by a mobile-cellular network but only 35% by at least third-generation service and 0% by at least fourth-generation, on 2023 international figures. Nearly a quarter of the population holds a connection inside a network that reaches five-sixths of them.
 Movement
Connectivity Internet usage About 27% of the population uses the internet, with fixed broadband subscriptions effectively negligible.
Full recordA country profile carries the 2023 international telecommunication union figures: 85% of the population covered by a mobile-cellular network, 35% by at least third-generation service and 0% by at least fourth-generation, with fixed broadband subscriptions effectively negligible, and puts internet use at about 27% of the population on a separate estimate.
Coverage and use are two different numbers here and the gap between them is the finding. Five-sixths of the population live inside a mobile-cellular signal, about a third inside one carrying third-generation data and none inside fourth-generation, while about a quarter use the internet at all. With no public submarine cable landing station, the operator's internet connectivity relies on geostationary satellite links, which is the ceiling under all three.
These are first figures for this base with nothing earlier behind them, so no direction can be stated.

Connectivity Mobile affordability An entry-level mobile broadband basket costs 85% of monthly income per head, against a 2% affordability benchmark (country tables).
Full recordCountry-table data on 2022 pricing records Eritrea's entry-level mobile broadband basket at 85% of monthly gross national income per head, against the international affordability benchmark of 2% (country tables).

No tariff per minute or per gigabyte is published.

Connectivity International internet bandwidth Bandwidth is reported at about 1,536 Mbps after a decade of growth, and it is carried by satellite: no submarine cable lands in Eritrea.
