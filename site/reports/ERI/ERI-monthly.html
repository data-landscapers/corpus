Eritrea: monthly update, September – October 2026

Edition of 2026-10-01  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Developments summarised from sources published between the beginning of last month and today.
Summary of the month · Governance · Technology

Summary of the month

The central bank's Legal Notice No. 134/2026 of 9 September caps the cash an individual may keep beyond 15 days at 25,000 nakfa, and private institutions at up to 150,000 nakfa; it is known only through a human-rights group's statement. Two small software efforts reached the state press: on 15 August the information ministry interviewed Shambko Apps Group, which has released five Tigrinya-language mobile applications built to the national curriculum, and on 7 September its outlet reported Afro Verse, a virtual-reality platform rendering traditional Hidmo housing in three dimensions.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

The month adds the sector's policy furniture rather than a new instrument. The one sector-specific policy named anywhere is for education: an education profile last modified on 2 September records a National Policy for ICT in Education, a 2018-2022 sector plan aiming at technology infrastructure and e-learning, and an initiative the ministry launched in January 2005, and the sector plan itself states that incorporating technology remains difficult for want of facilities, equipment and teacher training. The profile carrying it is a legacy platform its publisher states is no longer updated. No national digital or ICT strategy appears at any date, so the sector is governed by three texts from 1998 and 2003 that the repository does not hold.

Legislation and regulation

The central bank tightened its hold on cash a second time. After the June order to deposit holdings by 31 July, Legal Notice No. 134/2026 of 9 September caps the cash an individual may keep beyond 15 days at 25,000 nakfa, and private institutions at 50,000, 100,000 or 150,000 nakfa by grade, in force on publication. The notice is known through a human-rights group's statement rather than its own text, and that statement records no cash machines or cards and thin branch access outside the main towns. Neither what counts as an acceptable reason for holding more nor the penalty is defined.

Technology

Innovation ecosystem

On 7 September the same outlet reported Afro Verse, a virtual-reality platform rendering traditional Hidmo housing and its surroundings in three dimensions, shown in a first demo version on 27 August at an event a private company sponsored. It runs mainly in English, and no user figure is stated.
