This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In December 2025 UNDP's Digital Readiness Assessment found that 93% of the experts it surveyed had appropriated the Plan Stratégique Numérique 2023-2028, but only 7% judged it fully on track. Only 21% considered the digital ministry and the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale fully operational (December 2025). The assessment scores the country 2.3 on Government, 2.0 on Digital Public Infrastructure, 1.5 on Connectivity, 2.3 on Regulation and 2.7 on Population. It was handed over in Antananarivo on 17 December 2025 as the evidence base for a national digital roadmap.

As at 1 August 2025 AUDA-NEPAD was advertising for consultancy firms to draft Madagascar's first national data policy. The assignment runs in three steps: a national dialogue, a roadmap for domesticating the African Union Data Policy Framework, then the policy design. It was scoped at four months, with expressions of interest due 8 September 2025. No adopted national data policy has been found.

Drafting of a multisectoral national cybersecurity strategy began at an Antananarivo workshop in mid-December 2025 under the PRODIGY programme. It covers child protection, gender-based violence, justice and forensic cooperation, with a three-month delivery target. The transition government's programme of 17 November 2025 commits to a national digitalisation policy, a digital-governance mechanism under the Prime Minister's office and a digital electoral roll. That mechanism had not been established, convened or resourced at the programme's publication.

In September 2025 the technical and vocational education ministry adopted a UNESCO-backed digitalisation strategy, with 22 priority projects and a target of 40,000 people trained by 2028. The digital ministry's secretary-general also chairs the board of the telecommunications regulator ARTEC (September 2025). Madagascar scores 45.3 out of 100 for overall governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 34th of 54 African countries and below the continental average of 49.3 (2023). It scores 9 of 16 points against the Open Government Partnership's 12-point eligibility threshold as it seeks to rejoin (2026).

Legislation and regulation

Loi n° 2014-006 of 17 July 2014 is still Madagascar's cybercrime law in force (August 2026). The Council of Ministers adopted a complete recast, projet de loi n° 042/2026, on 8 July 2026. It revises the electronic-evidence rules and provides for connection-data retention and the freezing of suspects' assets. It makes cooperation with justice mandatory for telecom, internet and digital-finance operators. It would create an Unité de protection et d'investigation numérique and raise penalties to two to ten years' imprisonment (September 2026). On 4 September 2026 the deputies deferred it, saying they needed longer on texts touching both security and fundamental freedoms. The extraordinary session opened on 17 September 2026 without it. It is back on the second ordinary session's agenda, with committee examination set for 2 November 2026.

The electronic-communications and digital-infrastructure framework in force dates from 2005. The government filed projet de loi n° 040/2026 on 28 August 2026. It was not on the National Assembly's agenda when the ordinary session opened in October 2026. Loi n° 2014-025, adopted on 5 November 2014, gives an electronic signature the legal weight of a handwritten one. In April 2025 the digital ministry reopened consultation because the digital laws had stalled for want of implementing texts. A draft décret giving the signature law effect went to a validation workshop on 13 September 2025.

Loi n° 2014-024 of 10 December 2014 governs electronic contracts and payment by card and online. Loi n° 2016-056, in force since 2 February 2017, sets up the CSBF-licensed electronic-money institution. Loi n° 2020-011 of 1 September 2020 extends CSBF licensing and supervision to electronic-money providers. Décret n° 2025-1959 sets the institutional framework for interoperability. A decree of 2 July 2025 made electronic invoicing operational through a central tax platform.

On 8 July 2026 the Council of Ministers ordered internet service providers to block pornographic websites on public-order grounds. The order is a cabinet decision, not a statute. It names no timetable, technical mechanism, regulator or route of appeal.

Data protection

By February 2026 the Commission Malagasy sur l'Informatique et des Libertés was exercising its full statutory powers. It was issuing warnings, sanctions and fines and seizing non-compliant databases. It had registered several dozen compliance cases while still recruiting staff. The commission was created by décret n° 2023-1541 of 6 December 2023. It became functional only when its members were sworn in on 5 August 2025, eight of them named by décret n° 2025-814 of 30 July 2025. In February 2025 a francophone peer-review mission had found the authority still not created, eleven years after the statute.

The statute is loi n° 2014-038, adopted on 16 December 2014 and dated 9 January 2015. It establishes the CMIL as the independent supervisory authority and gives data subjects rights of access, rectification and opposition. It sets rules for cross-border transfers and provides administrative and penal sanctions. A June 2025 decree repealed article 90 of the commission's 2023 decree. Article 3 of the law bars judicial, administrative and private decisions taken solely on automated profiling. Transfer of personal data abroad is permitted subject to authorisation. The law imposes no duty to notify a personal-data breach, and its full application awaits an implementing decree (2024).

The commission's budget for 2026 is Ar 2,411,579,000, under Programme 138. It published a 73-page operating-procedures manual on 3 December 2025. Its first annual report, cover-dated January 2026, was announced on 27 March 2026. The deputy who chairs the Assemblée nationale's Digital Development committee also sits on the commission (August 2025).

On 28 July 2026 the national police launched a platform for reporting gender-based violence and disappearances. Its launch named no data controller, retention rule or supervisory authority for the sensitive data it collects. An August 2026 digital-health assessment names medical-data protection as a distinct weakness.

Regional collaboration

Negotiations on an enhanced EU-ESA Economic Partnership Agreement concluded on 10 June 2026, with Madagascar one of four signatories alongside Comoros, Mauritius and Seychelles. Its Digital Trade chapter bars customs duties on electronic transmissions between the parties and strengthens online consumer protection. Its customs-assistance protocol carries updated personal-data-protection provisions. Entry into force needs European Parliament consent, a Council decision and ratification by each ESA state. No signature date or ratification timetable was stated for Madagascar as at 11 June 2026.

Madagascar has held the rotating chair of the Southern African Development Community since August 2025. It hosted the bloc's first gathering on digital issues alone, five days on data governance and e-commerce in Antananarivo ending 23 September 2025. A GIZ data-policy training in the city on 24 September 2025 drew over 50 delegates from SADC and three regional sector associations.

The Haute Cour Constitutionnelle confirmed on 10 July 2024 the constitutionality of the law authorising ratification of the Malabo Convention. Madagascar is moving to ratify the UN Convention against Cybercrime, opened for signature at Hanoi in October 2025. On 4 August 2026 the Council of Ministers approved entry into a cybercrime-cooperation network initiated by China. That membership was not yet in effect. The data-protection authority has acceded to AFAPDP, the network of francophone data-protection authorities (January 2026). The METISS cable, landing at Fort Dauphin, became operational in March 2021 under an Indian Ocean Commission agreement of December 2017.

Standards

On 2 March 2026 the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale commissioned a two-phase performance and security audit of the national identity management system. It is assessed against loi n° 2014-038, ISO/IEC 27001, 27701 and 24745, NIST SP 800-63, World Bank ID4D principles, the EU GDPR and the Malabo Convention. The card is specified as a chipless, laser-personalised polycarbonate ID-1 card to ISO/IEC 7810, carrying a W3C verifiable-credential QR code.

Under the unit's Cadre d'Interopérabilité of 2022, the public sector's interoperability architecture is based on X-Road. The framework gives the unit the coordinating role across every public-sector interoperability project. Its interoperability norms and standards, version 1.0.0, last updated in November 2022, set API, web-service and security rules for every public service.

The e-Facturation decree of 2025 rests on article IV-21 of the Code des procédures fiscales. It sets obligatory invoice content, validity conditions, technical infrastructure, data security and the homologation of third-party systems. In July 2026 BSI certified Stellarix, a Malagasy digitalisation and cybersecurity company, to ISO 9001, ISO 22301 and ISO/IEC 27001. It also holds PCI DSS, the payment-card industry data security standard.

Magistrates adopted an AI ethics charter on 19 June 2026, defining which uses of artificial intelligence are authorised and which are prohibited before the courts. The Conseil d'État president under whom it was developed described it as anchored in the magistrates' code of ethics, not a statute.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The data-protection authority held a two-day public debate on data protection and biometric-identity privacy in Antsiranana on 28-29 May 2026. It drew local authorities, universities, civil society and the private sector. Antananarivo hosted the Indian Ocean Artificial Intelligence Summit on the same two days, with the Primature and the digital development ministry. Digital development minister Mahefa Andriamampiadana said Madagascar's youth should share in AI as an opportunity.

The Forum on Internet Governance Madagasikara held its first edition on 27-28 August 2020. The ISOC Madagascar chapter organised it with the regulator ARTEC and a 17-member multi-stakeholder committee. It issued policy recommendations. The National Assembly runs online public consultations on bills and budget priorities, the Electoral Code reform among them in February and March 2026. In July 2026 a civil-society round table proposed that any national AI policy be co-designed through localised citizen conventions.

i-Toroka, the anti-corruption bureau's anonymous whistleblowing platform, received 242 reports in 2024, up 27.36% year on year. In 2024, 80.53% of them could be investigated, while the count converted into a first-information report fell 5.83%.

Digital Freedom scores 73.6 out of 100 in the 2024 Ibrahim Index, 17th of 54 African states and down 4.0 points over the decade to 2023. Media Freedom stands at 54.9, 27th and down 3.4 points. The 2026 order to block obscene sites at internet-service-provider level came with no route of appeal. The access-to-information bill was left off the order paper of the extraordinary session opened on 25 August 2026. Deputies had promised at the close of committee work to reschedule it.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The telecommunications and ICT development fund FDTIC took Ar 100.1 billion in contributions in 2025 and granted Ar 100.4 billion. In 2024 it granted Ar 91.3 billion against Ar 62.9 billion of contributions. In 2023 it granted Ar 175.2 billion against Ar 88.9 billion. The fund sits outside the finance law. The annex budget for posts and telecommunications had been voted Ar 7.5 billion of its own receipts for 2024 (FY2024). The 2025 finance law abolished that annex from 1 January 2025 and paid its balance into the general budget. The digital ministry's staff it had paid moved onto the general budget.

The 2026 supplementary finance law funds the digital ministry's programme for the digital transformation of public services with Ar 24.1 billion of external money (FY2026). For 2026 it gives the finance ministry's IT, statistics and accounting directorate Ar 36.3 billion from a special Treasury account.

The state's own money is 69.9% of the Ar 80.1 billion of digital lines in the 2026 supplementary finance law, at the revised stage, and the external part is that one programme (FY2026).

In December 2025, as PRODIGY approached its June 2026 close, the World Bank urged the government to arrange financing to sustain the biometric database. It named the capacity of the civil-registration and identity centre CNECI a major constraint. The second enrolment phase, launched on 16 September 2026, is financed by the World Bank through DECIM. DECIM's connectivity targets run to March 2028.

MoUs and other agreements

Egypt and Madagascar signed five memoranda of understanding at El Alamein on 29 July 2026. One of them covers artificial intelligence and digital transformation. The Minister of Digital Development, Posts and Telecommunications signed it for Madagascar, and the communications minister Raafat Hendi for Egypt.

The Direction Générale de la Météorologie and the China Meteorological Administration signed a memorandum in Shanghai on 18 May 2026. It covers early-warning systems, tropical-cyclone forecasting, radar and satellite capacity-building, and AI-related innovations.

Neither of the two 2026 agreements that name artificial intelligence attaches an amount, a timeline or a named activity to it.

In November 2025 Mastercard and AXIAN Digibank & Fintech agreed to put virtual and physical cards and cross-border payments behind the Mixx and MVola brands. In July 2026 Airtel Money integrated Baobab Bank into its Mbanking service. Under a March 2026 cooperation agreement, Rwanda transfers its Electronic Billing Machine tax system to the Malagasy tax administration.

New investments

The World Bank approved US$400 million for the Digital and Energy Connectivity for Inclusion project in March 2023, for a run to 2028. After a 2024 restructuring partially cancelled financing, it carries US$375 million, of which US$135 million is for digital work (November 2025).

The World Bank approved US$225 million for PRODUIRE2, an urban resilience and jobs programme, on 6 July 2026. Digital land services are a small part of it, aiming at documentation for 50,000 parcels in Greater Antananarivo and Greater Toamasina by 2032. It approved US$250 million for ASCENT Madagascar on 6 May 2026, for electricity and modern cooking. ASCENT aims to reach more than 2.5 million people by 2030.

In April 2025 the European Investment Bank lent AXIAN Telecom US$100 million, US$40 million of it for 4G and 5G in Madagascar. China Eximbank's CNY 337,998,700 concessional loan of February 2020 runs at 1.5 percent over 30 years. It funds a Huawei 4G eLTE network across public safety and government agencies in Antananarivo. In July 2026 Indonesia's Indo-Aid released more than IDR 3.4 billion for a Maritime Single Window at the ports agency APMF.

Eighty-one digital-sector commitments to Madagascar are held with a start year between 2015 and 2026, from 23 financiers (November 2026). The European Investment Bank and African Development Bank loans to AXIAN Telecom are held as regional operator financings and are not among them.

All 81 carry a dollar amount, and together they come to about US$442 million as at November 2026 — committed sums, not disbursements.

The World Bank holds five of the 81 and about 81 per cent of the money; China Eximbank's loan, at about US$55 million, is the largest from any other financier (November 2026). USAID made 33 of the commitments, together worth about US$14 million.

Connectivity takes the largest share of the money, about US$190 million, ahead of digital identity at US$143 million and digital payments at about US$53 million (November 2026). National statistics has the most commitments, 34, worth about US$45 million together.

The June 2025 development policy operation and PRODUIRE2 are not among them: digital is one reform area in the first and land services a small part of the second.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In March 2026 the Cour des comptes asked that all disbursements from the telecommunications and ICT development fund FDTIC be suspended. It found unjustified expenditure, untraceable equipment, missing documents and a suspected conflict of interest. It asked for an overhaul of the fund's legal framework and an independent manager. In September 2026 the regulator posted the fund's financial statements for 2024 and 2025.

In February 2026 one exchange point, MGIX, was active, with four members. Under 1% of the country's networks were reachable through an exchange point, against 65% for Africa (February 2026). The same measurement put internet resilience at 41 out of 100. MGIX had been suspended since 2019 until the Internet Society Foundation gave iRENALA USD 49,958 to restart it (January 2025).

4G reached 34.6% of the population in 2024, against an African average of 75.2%. 2G reached 88.5% in 2023 and 3G 69.2% in 2024. 18.68% of the population used the internet in 2024, against cellular connections equal to 66% of the population at the start of 2026. The GSMA put unique mobile internet penetration at 14.3% in 2025. It found 66% of the population inside mobile broadband coverage without using it (October 2026).

Mobile internet cost 6.28% of GNI per capita a month in 2023, more than three times the 2% affordability target. The regulator ARTEC pressed operators to cut rates from late 2024 and again in October 2025. In October 2026 operators put taxes at 47% of an entry smartphone's cost, with an 8% excise on mobile services.

Orange inaugurated a 558 km fibre backbone between Antananarivo and Mahajanga in December 2025, after Yas and Blueline. It had bought the first 15-year global licence, covering national and international fibre, for EUR 30 million in December 2023. International capacity comes from 2Africa, brought ashore at Mahajanga by Telma, and from EASSy, LION and METISS. In September 2025 ARTEC published the first region-level 4G figures, from 46% in Atsimo Andrefana to 30% in Anosy. DECIM targets three million broadband users and 1,500 schools, health centres and ministry departments connected by March 2028.

On those regional figures, three of the four southern regions are covered above the national 4G average for 2024, and one, Anosy, below it.

Data Storage

PRODIGY safeguard documents of April 2026 place the primary data centre and the identity-card personalisation centre at Tsimbazaza. They place the secondary data centre at Iavoloha, and renovate the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale's building at Andafiavaratra.

Cooling equipment for the Iavoloha site went to national quotation in August 2025. Five further tenders opened in December 2025, covering biometric-enrolment supplies and the rehabilitation of both sites. Their closing date was moved to 30 June 2026. A three-lot national tender for the data centres' IT infrastructure was opened publicly on 20 March 2026, with delivery set at 90 days. It requires the winning bidder to disclose its beneficial owners.

Three state properties and at least three separate procurement and disclosure actions are on the record in the thirteen months to April 2026.

A 2025 market brief finds no hyperscale cloud provider operating in the country, with hyperscale services served at latencies above 100 milliseconds. It puts the live estate at a couple of facilities running well below capacity, against national demand in single-digit megawatts. It names electricity cost as the limit on data-centre development. The largest domestic operator is Stellarix, an Axian Group subsidiary running two Tier III-ready facilities in Antananarivo, TNR1 and TNR2.

Managed disaster recovery is sold commercially on local hosting, with the backup copy held outside the country (2025). The law makes transfer of personal data abroad subject to authorisation, not prohibited. PRODIGY has a published disposal plan for electrical and electronic equipment waste.

Energy

In July 2025 JIRAMA put its production cost at about 1,107 Ariary per kWh, against a selling price of about 785. It said it did not expect visible improvement in reliability before the end of 2026, and only if its recovery plan is executed.

That is a sale at roughly 71% of production cost, a loss of about 322 Ariary on every kilowatt-hour sold (2025).

Around 30% of what JIRAMA produced was lost to technical faults, fraud, theft and unpaid bills (2025). A shortfall of 40 to 80MW persists on the Antananarivo Interconnected Network. Its Plan de Redressement Financier 2025-2028 puts the 2023 operating deficit at roughly 1,080 billion Ariary a year. The plan relies on renewables, tariff adjustment and debt restructuring.

Scheduled rotating load-shedding has run since mid-2024, driven by fuel shortages and low water at the Andekaleka dam. Daily cuts lengthened from around two hours to three to six by 2025, and fatal nationwide protests followed in September 2025. By August 2024 shops in Antananarivo were losing five hours of an eight-hour working day to cuts. Rotating cuts returned to the capital in late August 2026 after a fault halted the TAC 1 unit at Ambohimanambola.

Cyclone Gezani took 91% of the electricity network in Toamasina offline in February 2026. JIRAMA contracted eleven local firms to rebuild it under the World Bank-financed DECIM and LEAD projects. By August 2026 most service was restored, with about 4MW still offline and load-shedding continuing.

37% of the population had a regular source of electricity in 2025. 75.6% of the urban population had access in 2023, against 14.6% of the rural population. Renewables, mostly hydropower, produced 53% of the 2,087 GWh generated in 2023. DECIM targets new electricity connections at 2,250 sites by March 2028. Solar installed for the Smart Village hubs also runs refrigeration and school-canteen e-cooking.

Technical Capacity

In November 2023 the SOFF peer review found the meteorological service's fluctuating budget made activity planning difficult. It left DGM unable to hire the personnel its mandate requires or to maintain its technical equipment. The same review found the service's objectives more ambitious than the global observing network's own criteria. Its staff are trained through the University of Antananarivo and the WMO regional training centres it hosts.

In December 2025 the expert panel for the Digital Readiness Assessment named a skills deficit as the principal brake on digital progress, ahead of budget and ICT infrastructure. Only a minority of the panel consider the digital ministry and the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale fully operational as institutions.

In August 2026 JIRAMA rebuilt Toamasina's storm-damaged grid through eleven local contractors. The customs administration launched its own data-science and artificial-intelligence laboratory, CEDIA, in May 2025. CEDIA automates anomaly detection and refines risk targeting. Stellarix runs a security operations centre based in and staffed from Madagascar. Almost all of its engineers and technicians were trained in the country. In 2024 the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale sought a firm to supply developers for at least 250 expert-months, up to USD 300,000.

Cybersecurity

Drafting of a national cybersecurity strategy began in December 2025, on top of a national cybersecurity body created in 2023. Its announced scope covers child protection online, digital justice and international cyber-cooperation. The ITU's 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index scores the country 31.70 out of 100, up from 23.33 in the 2020 edition. It names gaps in technical infrastructure, institutional coordination, skills and international cooperation.

Loi n° 2014-006 remains the cybercrime law in force (August 2026). Cabinet adopted a recast on 8 July 2026, and a text authorising ratification of the UN Convention against Cybercrime was tabled alongside it. In June 2026 the digital ministry told the first Cybersecurity Symposium that the overhaul was nearly complete. The deputies deferred the recast on 4 September 2026. It is back before committee, with examination set for 2 November 2026.

In December 2025 the data-protection authority CMIL and the national CIRT were both recorded as operational with key posts filled. The cybercrime centre created in 2019 resumed work in June 2026, and its teams in all 24 regions were equipped on 21 October 2026. Terms of reference of 2 March 2026 commission a security audit of the national identity management system. The audit applies ISO/IEC 27001, 27701 and 24745, NIST guidance, the EU GDPR and the Malabo Convention, which Madagascar ratified in June 2024. In July 2026 BSI certified the private firm Stellarix to ISO 9001, ISO 22301 and ISO/IEC 27001. The data-protection law still imposes no duty to notify a personal-data breach.

On 3 October 2025, during the protest crisis, the foreign minister said the country faced a massive cyberattack directed from abroad. He named no affected system and gave no technical evidence.

DPI

Data Exchange

Madagascar's national data exchange went live on 28 April 2026: Rahaka is built on the Estonian X-Road under Décret n° 2025-1959 on the interoperability of public information systems. It opened with the Economic Development Board, the social security fund CNaPS, the statistics institute INSTAT, the Ministry of Justice and the tax directorate as its first operational participants. The first usage convention was signed at the launch between the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale, several ministries and private-sector actors. The UGD delivered the first stage of the layer with the World Bank-financed PRODIGY project and the digital-development ministry.

The UGD's Cadre d'Interopérabilité set the X-Road design for the public sector. The architecture is older than the launch. It gave the unit the coordinating role across every public-sector interoperability project. It named Orinasa as the country's first interoperable layer. Orinasa is the secure exchange running between the Economic Development Board, the tax directorate, the justice ministry and INSTAT. Company formation has run over that layer for several years. The tax directorate, the Trade and Companies Registry and the statistics institute exchange data in real time, and registration was cut from about two weeks to a matter of hours.

By the April 2026 launch 14 institutions were connected and more than 162,000 exchanges recorded. Health, education, civil registration and social security are named as next. The government is building an online single window across administrations, institutions and businesses on Rahaka. The objective is that a single identity document suffices to reach the whole of the information held. A national performance dashboard was in preparation as at December 2025. It draws on the HR database, civil-registration and passport services and the land-office single window. The frame around the exchange is the weaker part. The country's digital readiness assessment concentrates its regulatory recommendations on modernising data-protection, e-commerce and cybersecurity law. It also recommends strengthening interoperability between government systems.

Digital Identity and CRVS

At the World Bank's supervision mission of 1–4 December 2025, the proof of concept had reached about 5,500 people in three districts. National enrolment had slipped two to three months after the change of government in September–October 2025. The incoming government had suspended ID-card printing procurement. Phase one of the national identity operation closed in July 2026 with more than six million adults enrolled, against a two-million target. A second phase opened on 16 September 2026 with 1,000 more kits, aiming to enrol more than 10 million adults by July 2027. On 1 November 2026 the interior minister said just under seven million adults had enrolled, with the second phase starting in 16 districts.

The Izaho Tokana campaign was launched on 27 January 2026 at Iavoloha, with 2,500 kits across 1,729 centres six days a week, free to the citizen. Its statute, loi n° 2025-019 on the identification of natural persons, was declared constitutional on 26 August 2025. The Haute Cour Constitutionnelle set necessity and proportionality safeguards on biometric identification. On 21 April 2026 the National Assembly opened birth registration to adults with no certificate while enrolment runs. The Court cleared that law on 13 May 2026 as a derogation from the état-civil statute.

Décret n° 2023-515 created a random ten-digit unique identification number. It requires the data-protection authority's prior authorisation before the civil-status and identity registers are linked to another public database. In 2020 the electoral commission traced repeated identity-card numbers to a card register that was not centralised.

The system captures fingerprints, facial images and iris, where the legacy card carried a single fingerprint (January 2026). The card is a chipless polycarbonate ID-1 card carrying a W3C verifiable-credential QR code. As at October 2024 the register enabled neither authentication nor electronic know-your-customer checks. PRODIGY financed the programme with a USD 140 million IDA credit and a USD 3 million Global Financing Facility grant (August 2025). It closed in June 2026 after an extension for implementation delays. Its closing report recorded no biometric card yet activated against a ten-million target, with printing expected around December 2026.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Under a December 2021 contract the state bought 250,000 e-poketra payment cards for 9.18bn Ariary. Of these, 76,073 reached the education ministry and the security forces, and even those went unused. The project was suspended in July 2024 when the sponsoring bank ended the contract, leaving about 173,917 cards in stock. In April 2026 the Council of Ministers abrogated the card's founding decree, giving no explanation.

Mobile-money platforms handled close to 342 million transactions worth about USD 8.5 billion in 2023. There were 6.12 million active accounts in 2024 against more than 20 million registered. The three operators have been interoperable for person-to-person transfers since 2016. Registration requires a national identity card, and the country remains a cash economy with very limited card acceptance. MVola, the largest platform, replaced its core platform in May 2026, with outages in May and June.

On 11 June 2026 the central bank governor told the National Assembly it was auditing electronic-money institutions after numerous user complaints. The audit covers technical compliance, platform security and whether e-money is fully backed by real funds. JIRAMA customers billed twice can obtain a refund on showing the transaction reference. On 15 September 2026 the finance ministry announced a task force with the banking supervisor and the telecoms regulator on lost money and failed transactions.

Since 9 May 2025 non-tax revenues can be paid to the state through MVola, Orange Money and Airtel Money. The Financial Inclusion Project's digital payment of teachers' salaries and student grants was cancelled in 2022. The development fund pays cash transfers by mobile money, and in October 2026 tendered for providers in seven lots. The central bank opened a ten-month eAriary pilot in May 2025 with the Treasury, the tax administration and Jirama. In October 2026 the capital's urban commune began digitalising its markets, with merchant cards debited by payment terminals.

In October 2026 the largest mobile-money operator made merchant payments free for customers, the fee borne by the merchant. It reported close to 4 million users and more than 50,000 accepting merchants (October 2026).

Registries

Madagascar printed and distributed 5,882,085 Karinempokontany QR-code booklets against a target of 5,817,968 households as at 9 September 2025. The Cour des Comptes finds mauvaise gestion on 21.54bn Ariary spent between 2022 and 2025, 18.74bn of it on printing and distribution. It states that the project is not yet fully operational.

Around two-thirds of civil registration offices had no stable internet access in 2023. An offline application was being designed for them. By December 2025 SIECM digitisation had reached 336 communes, 40% of its target. By then 61,892 birth certificates were linked to an identification number. By December 2025 the e-Fokontany census had covered 5.09 million households and 19.7 million provisional identifiers. Eighty per cent of children were registered with the civil-status service in 2025.

On 5 February 2026 the social registry's technical committee set a September 2026 completion target. By September 2026 the registry covered about 2.7 million households, nearly 12 million people. A new survey was extending it into urban areas. Its designed linkage to the identity system is not operational.

Only around 7% of the country is formally titled, and 1,518,000 local land certificates had been registered as at October 2024. The National Land Programme's 2026 documentation confirms that no integrated national land information system exists.

The electoral commission is rebuilding the voter register under Decree No. 2026-942 of April 2026, for completion by April 2027. By 16 October 2026 it had provisionally registered 7,163,714 adults, 93.23% of them door to door. In October 2026 Japan and UNDP gave it US$750,000 of data equipment to prepare a biometric list. The Gendarmerie is building a secure digital Fichier Central with UNDP support, with fingerprint comparison and facial recognition planned.

The tax directorate launched a central register of beneficial owners on 16 June 2026. It moved into operation on 18 September 2026, when four protocols linked it with the trade register, the anti-corruption bureau, the economic development board and the notaries.

Sectoral management information systems

The SAFI tax system began in October 2024 on a fourteen-month schedule to national deployment by January 2026. It reached pilot services in early October 2025, with the e-Hetra portal updated to interface with it. In March 2026 it was still being taught to regional and fiscal-centre heads in Antananarivo. A decree of 2 July 2025 makes electronic invoicing mandatory through a central tax platform, starting with large enterprises. The customs code makes electronic filing of the detailed declaration the rule at computerised offices (2023). The TradeNet single window cut average clearance to three days, its operator said in June 2025.

A DHIS2 Tracker pilot registering vaccinated people by name began on 9–10 June 2026 at 20 basic health centres and 54 private sites in Antananarivo-Renivohitra. Basic health centres record cases in outpatient registers and report monthly to the health district. A two-region assessment found discrepancies between registers, reports and DHIS2. SII-CSB, which tracks funding to basic health centres, opened user training at Fianarantsoa on 18 February 2026. Civil servants' files and payroll run through the AUGURE 2 application.

The education ministry compiles its annual school census by hand over about six months, and a World Bank programme is giving principals tablets (2025). The information system was rebuilt under PAPESI, run 2022–2024 by UNESCO and the IIEP. Its evaluation found the programme highly relevant but undersized against weak coordination in the ministry. A permanent identifier follows each pupil from pre-school to lycée, and the MPIANATRA school management platform was launched on 24 October 2026.

On 2 June 2026 the justice ministry announced a digital criminal-records platform. The courts run six case-management applications which do not yet reach every court. Five first-instance courts had been fitted with computers, printers and backup power by December 2023. Police custody is recorded in a register every service holding detainees must keep.

At least six distinct sectoral information systems are in service or in rollout across Madagascar's line ministries and agencies, each with its own sponsoring institution. A press account reported two university hospitals chosen as pilot sites for the hospital digitalisation project (2025).

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In March 2026 the Cour des Comptes found mauvaise gestion financière in the digital ministry's projects across 2022–2025. It examined e-poketra, e-fokontany and Smart Villages in detail. All three drew heavily on the ministry's budget, the Présidence and PRODIGY without reaching their objectives.

PRODIGY closed in June 2026 after six years. It left the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale, the data-protection authority CMIL, the national CIRT and CNECI as standing institutions, with follow-on work under DECIM. UNDP's December 2025 readiness assessment names PRODIGY, Torolalana and Orinasa as the flagship digital-government platforms. The UN's 2024 E-Government Survey puts Madagascar in its middle development group.

On 28 July 2026 the national police opened a free reporting platform for gender-based violence, online harassment and disappearances. It was built in-house, runs on the web and Android in Malagasy and French, and accepts anonymous reports. The higher-education ministry has put its accreditation and equivalence platform into service. A redesign of the e-GP procurement system was tendered in August 2025. Torolalana, the citizen services portal, reaches only the minority with internet access. PRODIGY's count of users of improved digital services is dominated by exam-results consultations.

The tax directorate launched the Torohy platform on 9 October 2026 for reporting corruption in the tax administration. In October 2026 the anti-corruption bureau launched i-Fanagna for remote filing of asset declarations, built with African Development Bank financing. The digital ministry said in October 2026 that it was testing e-Pasipaoro, an online passport application portal. Only 150 of 2,014 contracting entities declared their 2022 contracts on the SIGMP procurement system. The electronic procurement system's first iteration was due to go live in 2026.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

On 3 July 2026 the decentralisation institute INDDL, financed by AFD, equipped the Commune Urbaine d'Antananarivo and 28 peripheral communes under Lalankely III. Computers went to the 28 peripheral communes for the digitalisation of local services. More than 290 units of furniture and technical equipment went to the capital's commune and its arrondissements.

The World Bank-financed MIONJO project launched its District 2.0 arrangement in Anosy and Androy on 1 June 2026, and in Atsimo Andrefana on 4 June. It handed servers to territorial authorities and trained communal officials in digital literacy and the e-Zaha platform. The stated purpose of e-Zaha is better quality, reliability and completeness of commune-level data.

On 19 February 2025 PRODUIR's MATAC/Hanorina phase 2 put computers, printers, scanners and internet boxes into 38 communes of Greater Antananarivo. The equipment served one process, digitalised building-permit authorisation.

The Orinasa online business-creation platform has run at EDBM's Manakara office since July 2025. By December 2025 SIECM civil-registration digitisation had covered a minority of its target communes. SII-CSB user training began at Fianarantsoa on 18 February 2026 for three regions, with a national rollout announced but not dated.

Greater Antananarivo's communes are being equipped for digitalisation through separate donor-financed instruments, each attached to a different administrative function — the World Bank's PRODUIR for building-permit authorisation, AFD's Lalankely III for general office and IT equipment.

Rural digital data capture

In November 2025 DECIM's rural connectivity mapping had to go down to Fokontany, not Commune, level. Cross-matching the Fokontany databases pushed the first draft tender document from end-October 2025 to January 2026. That left the US$185m digital and energy sub-component about a quarter behind (November 2025). Sites are to be picked by a four-criterion matrix: population, public buildings, economic and tourist zones, and meteorological risk.

Most civil registration offices lacked stable internet access in 2023, and an offline birth-registration application was still being designed. Basic health centres record cases in outpatient registers and report monthly to the district. Police stations record everyone in custody in a register the 2008 law against torture makes compulsory. The education ministry compiles its school census by hand and is giving principals tablets (2025).

Article 2 of Décret n°2023-28 allows computer tools in mass land certification but does not require them. The national land programme is digitising cadastral data from orthophoto-based plans, and has no integrated land information system (2026).

Community health volunteers report through a mobile application for community care (2024). In February 2026 the Smart Village programme had put IT equipment into districts, hospitals, schools, gendarmerie posts, communes and farm services in Anosy and Androy.

The 2020-21 drought in the south was the worst of the preceding decade, leaving about 1.5 million people needing assistance. In November 2023 a peer review found the weather service's warnings do not reliably reach the people they are for.

Technology

AI

Magistrates adopted an ethics charter for the judiciary on 19 June 2026, setting out which uses of artificial intelligence are authorised and which are prohibited. It is anchored in the magistrates' code of ethics and is not a statute. Two further proposals from the same seminar were not adopted: a standing platform for magistrates to track AI, and a charter for magistracy students.

A July 2026 civil-society round table found no national artificial-intelligence strategy and no general AI statute. It checked the Plan Stratégique Numérique 2023–2028 and UNDP's December 2025 Digital Readiness Assessment. The 2014 data-protection law bars decisions taken solely on automated profiling.

The customs administration launched a Centre d'Excellence des Données et de l'Intelligence Artificielle in May 2025. The centre automates anomaly detection and refines risk targeting. Its director-general presented results from the Enhanced Risk Assessment targeting system in Washington in 2025. In October 2025 customs deployed a generative-AI agent that analyses invoices to detect manipulation and false invoices. Customs presented an AI chatbot beside its e-Tariff portal in May 2025. The Treasury gave senior staff their first generative-AI training in September 2026. The justice sector is piloting AI in legal research and document management.

Antananarivo hosted an Indian Ocean Artificial Intelligence Summit on 28–29 May 2026. The July round table called for a sovereign national AI policy built on data control, infrastructure security and frugal Edge AI models. Madagascar took part in founding the World AI Cooperation Organization at Shanghai in July 2026, and announced its accession.

ICT Industry

The 2026 supplementary finance law raised the post and telecommunications branch's 2026 growth forecast to +12.2%, from +9.5% in the initial law. It is the largest upward revision named for any tertiary branch. The same law cut the overall growth forecast from +4.8% to +3.8%, citing drought, cyclone damage and the unrest of September–October 2025. The government credits outsourcing, wider network coverage and the DECIM and PRODIGY projects, as the bill did on 29 May 2026.

ASAN'AI opened applications in January 2026 for a free 15-month programme. It places trainees with four outsourcing employers: Intelcia, Konecta, Connecteo and Concentrix. On 24 September 2026 its second cohort certified 99 of 111 trainees after seven weeks with ArkeUp Academy.

About a quarter of firms have a website, below the African average (December 2025). Online purchasing by adults is rare. Firms report frequent and lengthy power cuts (2022). The business and labour environment scores 38.3 out of 100, 41st of 54 African states (2023).

In July 2026 Stellarix, a Malagasy cybersecurity firm, was certified to three ISO standards. It runs a security operations centre based in and staffed from Madagascar. The sector is put at around 3% of GDP (2026). The national digital strategy aims to raise that share by 2028.

Innovation ecosystem

UniPod Madagascar was inaugurated on 28 May 2026 at the CIDST site in Tsimbazaza, Antananarivo. It was presented at launch as the country's first national platform for collaborative design, digital fabrication and technology transfer. It puts five units on one site: rapid prototyping, robotics and artificial intelligence, agrifood, greentech, and a technology-transfer office. It sits inside the higher-education ministry's Direction de la Recherche et de l'Innovation. It is delivered with the industrialisation ministry and UNDP's pan-African Timbuktoo initiative. It follows a cooperation agreement the digital ministry signed with four partner ministries on 26 January 2026.

MIARY DIGITAL sits under the World Bank-financed Projet Pôles Intégrés de Croissance (2026). It is overseen jointly by the economy and finance, industrialisation and trade, and digital development ministries. It closed a second cohort at Diego-Suarez on 8 January 2026, after more than nine months of incubation. It disbursed grants of US$15,000 each to startups drawn from eight regions.

At least three hubs are active in the AfriLabs network: Habaka, Zafy Tody and Koon-Space (2025). Orange opened the country's second Orange Digital Center at the Université d'Antsiranana on 18 June 2026. It carries the network's four free components: École du Code, FabLab Solidaire, Orange Fab and Orange Ventures. Orange says thousands of young people have passed through its Malagasy programmes since 2021. The startup base stays concentrated in Antananarivo, with little venture funding tracked (2025). Madagascar ranked 110th on WIPO's Global Innovation Index in 2024.

Capacity

Literacy

MICS-7 recorded a net adjusted primary attendance rate of 75 per cent and a primary completion rate of 47 per cent (2025).

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index, Madagascar's Education Enrolment score of 42.4 out of 100 placed it 21st of 54 African states (2023). Education Quality, at 32.5, ranked 37th, and Education Completion, at 31.3, ranked 29th. Education rose 6.5 points over 2014-2023 to 49.4. Human Resources in Education stands at 93.5, ninth of 54 and among Madagascar's ten best indicator scores.

Solar United Madagascar's Digital Learning Programme grew from two pilot schools in April 2024 to 15 rural public primary schools across four regions. It reached over 2,500 students by September 2025. Teachers get individual tablets linked to a Raspberry Pi intranet hub holding offline Ministry of Education textbooks and the Rachel and Kiwix libraries. The programme adds teacher development and certification-exam preparation. It runs on the same solar equipment that supplies after-dark study lighting. The state's basic digital-literacy work is one strand of the World Bank DECIM project's digital-skills window (November 2025). No amount attached to that strand has been found.

Training and skills

In December 2025, 87 per cent of those surveyed for the Digital Readiness Assessment named a skills deficit as the principal obstacle to digital progress. In September 2025 the technical and vocational education ministry adopted a digitalisation strategy carrying a national trainee target.

The OIF-backed D-CLIC programme began delivery on 19 January 2026, offering free digital-skills training to 1,000 young people by mid-2026. ASAN'AI opened applications on 23 January 2026 for a free 15-month digital and outsourcing programme for 1,300 young people. DECIM's US$15m digital-skills window allocates US$0.3m to training 5,700 people in advanced digital skills (November 2025). It gives US$2.4m to the research and education network iRENALA (November 2025). Under the same 2025 plan, US$3m with SAYNA and US$7.5m of renovation go to turning the distance-learning institution CNTEMAD into a private virtual university.

The two cohorts launched in January 2026 come to 2,300 training places between them, under 6 per cent of the national target of 40,000 digital-skills trainees by 2028.

In March 2025 the evaluation of PAPESI recorded that it trained 115 education ministry agents in planning methods and on the rebuilt SIGE. It found notable improvement at individual level and limited effects at organisational and institutional level. A national strategy was drafted to extend the training to remaining staff. Meteorological staff are trained through the University of Antananarivo, the WMO regional centres it hosts, ENEAM and ESPA (November 2023). In June 2026, 85 magistrates and 55 institutional representatives attended the UNESCO seminar that adopted the judiciary's AI ethics charter.

On 10 February 2023 the education ministry, UNICEF and Airtel signed a five-year agreement giving 14,000 students in 15 public lower-secondary schools free access to a digital-learning platform. Orange's 2025-2026 écoles numériques rollout launched at CEG Nanisana, a school of 2,700 students, in May 2026. It brings tablets, computers, projectors and teacher training.

Research institutions

US$2.4m of the World Bank DECIM project's digital-skills window goes to iRENALA, the national research and education network (November 2025). The École Nationale d'Informatique at Fianarantsoa added an artificial intelligence programme in 2023. ISPM runs engineering tracks in ICT, AI and cybersecurity, and INSI is a dedicated IT institute.

UniPod Madagascar was inaugurated on 28 May 2026 at the CIDST site in Tsimbazaza, Antananarivo. It sits inside the higher-education ministry's Direction de la Recherche et de l'Innovation. It is delivered with the industry ministry and UNDP's pan-African Timbuktoo initiative.

The 2023 hydromet peer review found no research on the social and economic benefit of the Direction Générale de la Météorologie's services. A baseline evaluation of Solar United Madagascar's Digital Learning Programme began in September 2025, run with the education ministry's Evaluation Unit. An endline was planned for June 2026 to measure gains in reading, writing and numeracy.

Inclusion

Access to services

In July 2026 the national police opened an online platform for reporting gender-based violence and disappearances. A report can be filed anonymously and tracked by reference number. More than thirteen thousand survivors of gender-based violence received medical, psychosocial and legal care in 2025.

Madagascar scored 24.9 out of 100 for Equal Access to Public Services in 2023, 43rd of 54 African states. MICS-7 found 42% of the population with an improved water source, 17% using improved sanitation and 40% practising open defecation in 2025. 42% of women aged 20–24 had married before 18, a share INSTAT says has risen since 2018.

80% of children were registered at birth in 2025. About 6 million adults, some 40% of adults, hold no birth certificate (2026). The registration drive works offline first, with field teams and fokontany chiefs confirming identities against a biometric database (July 2026). The National Assembly has opened a derogatory registration route for adults with no certificate. In the Atsimondrano pilot district a UNDP-backed campaign took enrolment to women, young people and people with disabilities (2026).

Loi n° 97-044 of 1998 guarantees persons with disabilities equal rights in health, education, employment and social integration. A 2019–2022 UN joint programme built disability screening into cash transfers. Madagascar acceded to the 1951 Refugee Convention in 1967 but not to the 1967 Protocol or the 1969 OAU Convention (2025). Décret n° 2015-957 requires a standing consultation body at every decentralised collectivity.

Buying online remains marginal (December 2025). In November 2023 a peer review found weather warnings do not reliably reach the people they are for.

Digital divides

Nine applications to the US$24m affordable-devices window of Madagascar's World Bank-financed DECIM programme had been approved and were in contractualisation by November 2025. Only the Orange and Airtel contracts were unsigned (November 2025). A first call for funds of US$2.5m was made to the project unit on 7 November 2025, and start-up advances were due to disburse at the end of that month. A student laptop pilot was at study stage at the same date. It opened in September 2026, when the postal microfinance institution Paositra Finances began selling laptops on 12-month credit to 1,400 master's students at the universities of Antananarivo and Fianarantsoa. Part of the DECIM envelope made it possible.

Around four in five Malagasy did not use the internet in 2024, far below mobile-phone penetration of 44.4%. Mobile internet costs several times the UN affordability benchmark. In December 2025 the state and the operators announced an agreement to bring tariffs down gradually. The state offered excise-duty relief against reinvestment in network quality. The Ibrahim Index records that internet and computer access has barely moved over the decade while mobile telephony has advanced far faster.

The statistics institute's 2021-2022 household survey found 7.1% of the population with internet access. The share was 2.7% in rural areas against 20.4% in the capital's urban area (2021-2022). Rural electricity access runs far below urban. The regulator ARTEC published the country's first region-level 4G coverage figures in September 2025, covering the four regions of the deep south and none of the rest. The e-Facturation decree phases electronic invoicing in with large enterprises first. It acknowledges that many small and medium firms have neither the computer tools nor a stable connection to comply, and promises training, not exemption. In payments, registered mobile money accounts outnumber active ones several times over. Launching the 2025–2026 school digital-kit deployment in May 2026, the president of Orange Solidarité Madagascar named the gap on the record, framing the equipment as a response to it, not a pedagogical upgrade.

Data

National statistics

Statistical capacity rose 20.1 points over 2014-2023 to 42.9 out of 100 in the Ibrahim Index, Madagascar's fifth most improved indicator. It still ranks 34th of 54 African states. The World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators for 2023 put data products in the 70-89% band and data use in the 50-69% band. Data sources and data infrastructure both sit in the 20-49% band (2023).

In 2023 there had been no economic or business census in twenty years, and no agricultural or business survey in ten. Loi n° 2018-004 repealed the 1967 law and set up the national statistical system, the Conseil National de la Statistique and INSTAT's role.

MICS-7, the first such survey since 2018, ran fieldwork from late December 2024 to May 2025 across about 20,000 households. It reported in February 2026. INSTAT said in February 2026 that it informs about 43% of Madagascar's SDG indicators. The round measures 43 indicators across 11 SDGs. The health ministry's 26th statistical yearbook, posted in February 2026, is compiled from facilities' monthly reports. In February 2026 INSTAT published the Livret Genre, its first gender-statistics compendium.

The fourth population and housing census is planned for 2028, its project document presented in May 2026. No budget, timetable or settled strategy is attached to it.

In March 2026 an IMF mission welcomed the government-finance-statistics decree and recommended a GFS committee. The Treasury says coverage extends only to entities it tracks itself, and granularity is too thin for a complete balance sheet.

Every major statistical undertaking on the record between August 2025 and June 2026 — the microdata platform, MICS-7, the census project document and the finance-statistics reform — carried external technical or financial support.

Open data

As at 27 August 2026 Madagascar had no enacted access-to-information law. Draft loi n° 017/2025 was still unpassed, after attempts running back to 2006. The bill reached committee on 8 May 2026, with deputies aiming to adopt it in plenary on 18 May. It was then left off the extraordinary session opened on 25 August 2026. No record of the May vote has been found.

Décret n° 2025-1526 of 3 December 2025 laid an 88-article text before Parliament. It makes accessibility, systematic disclosure and free access principles. Its article 4.10 requires progressive digitalisation of public services.

Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory scores zero out of ten in five categories. They are digital and ICT economic statistics, energy, pollution, crime, and health facilities. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index put disclosure of public records at 27.9 out of 100 and accessibility at 29.1. Two PRODIGY safeguard documents released in April 2026 cannot be downloaded.

Since August 2025 INSTAT's public microdata platform, financed under the World Bank's STATCAP-II, has opened data from the 2018 census and later household surveys. The Cour de cassation's free online case-law database, launched in May 2025, held 1,003 decisions in October 2026. On 29 October 2026 a committee approved 595 more judgments from 2019-2022 for it.

Across the 23 categories the 2024 open data inventory scores, Madagascar averages 3.0 out of 10 and no category exceeds 5.0, the best being education outcomes. The census cycle runs at ten years, the third census having been taken in 2018 and the fourth planned for 2028, so the microdata opened in August 2025 will be a decade old before it is superseded. The economy and finance ministry plans wider publication of budget information after an international assessment scored the country below its threshold, and the Cour des comptes will resume publishing the budget settlement document in 2027.

Geospatial data

In 2023 a World Bank assessment recorded ten major droughts between 1981 and 2020-21, affecting from 14,000 to 1.3 million people. The table was compiled from disaster-event records, not a monitoring product. The assessment scored drought monitoring, early warning and impact assessment as Low. Cimopolée, a real-time cyclone-tracking platform in the national disaster-risk system, became operational in September 2025.

In 2023 UNICEF published monthly satellite-based drought alerts for the south, recording no national drought monitoring system. A 2025 UNDP assessment lists real-time drought monitoring among the data gaps. In February 2026 UNDP combined satellite images of cyclone flooding with socioeconomic data to target recovery. External researchers built the first country-wide rice-paddy maps, for 2017 to 2025, from Landsat and Sentinel-2. The EU fisheries agreement ties surveillance in Madagascar's waters to a satellite-tracking regime. The land programme is digitising cadastral data from orthophotos.

In May 2026 the meteorological service signed a memorandum with the China Meteorological Administration on radar and satellite capacity, cyclone forecasting and early warning. Madagascar has joined the UN Early Warnings for All initiative. A 2023 peer review judged the service's warnings, hydrology and dissemination only basic. The service's March 2024 observing plan found 4 of the 15 surface stations the global observing network requires compliant, all four run by ASECNA. By June 2026 a US$4.9 million SOFF investment under UNDP had installed a WIS 2.0 data-exchange system, with the station purchase order due in August.

SpaceX filed with the US regulator on 13 May 2026 for Starlink Direct-to-Cell coverage using Airtel Madagascar's spectrum. The first trial ran at Sadabe on 11 July 2026, with commercial launch awaiting ARTEC's authorisation. In June 2026 Airtel and Eutelsat extended a low-earth-orbit rural broadband partnership on OneWeb.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

No United States government digital programme or agreement with Madagascar had been found as at September 2026. No hyperscaler investment had been found either.

China activities

The Council of Ministers agreed on 4 August 2026 that Madagascar would join an international network against online fraud initiated by China. It commits the ministries of foreign affairs, justice, public security and digital development, with the gendarmerie. Madagascar took part in founding the World AI Cooperation Organization at Shanghai on 16–17 July 2026. It was acceding to the organisation as at August 2026.

The Direction Générale de la Météorologie and the China Meteorological Administration signed a memorandum in Shanghai on 18 May 2026. It covers early warning, tropical-cyclone forecasting, multi-hazard warning, and radar and satellite capacity-building.

China Eximbank signed a government concessional loan of some CNY 338 million with Madagascar on 19 February 2020. It funds a Huawei 4G eLTE fibre network across public safety and government agencies in Antananarivo. It also covers data management centres, video-conferencing and surveillance cameras. The loan runs 30 years with five years' grace at 1.5 percent interest, and the Senate approved it on 5 June 2020.

Chinese money is nevertheless a small share of what has been committed to Madagascar's digital sector: the Eximbank loan, at about US$55 million, is about an eighth of the roughly US$442 million on record, against about four fifths from the World Bank (November 2026).

EU activities

Negotiations on the enhanced EU-ESA Economic Partnership Agreement concluded on 10 June 2026, with Madagascar one of four ESA parties. It carries a dedicated Digital Trade chapter and a Protocol on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Customs Matters. The digital chapter bars customs duties on electronic transmissions and strengthens online consumer protection. The customs protocol carries personal-data-protection provisions and an enabling clause for automatic exchange of customs information. Entry into force needs Council authorisation, European Parliament consent and ratification by Madagascar. No signature or ratification date had been stated as at June 2026.

The European Union is the largest trading partner of the four ESA states, with EU-ESA4 trade in goods and services worth €9.7bn in 2024. The agreement will protect 135 EU geographical indications in Madagascar, Mauritius and Seychelles after a transition period.

In April 2025 the European Investment Bank lent AXIAN Telecom money for 4G and 5G rollout under Global Gateway. The national data policy is being drafted inside the GIZ-financed African Union Data Governance project (2025). Team Europe held a data-policy training in Antananarivo in September 2025. The European Commission funded the UNESCO seminar at which magistrates adopted an AI ethics charter in June 2026. Agence française de développement money paid for equipment handed to 29 Greater Antananarivo communes on 3 July 2026. The fisheries partnership agreement in force since December 2023 ties vessel surveillance to a satellite-tracking regime specified by the EU.

Gulf/UAE activities

President Michael Randrianirina proposed in December 2025 a joint Madagascar–Saudi investment body to coordinate and finance projects including digital governance. He said the country aims to launch a secure national digital platform for public administration and security, drawing on Saudi experience. No agreement under the proposal had been found as at September 2026.

Abu Dhabi Ports and the Economic Development Board of Madagascar signed a memorandum on 30 May 2024 to explore collaboration that includes the digitisation of logistics services. No project or amount under it has been found.

India activities

No Indian government or Indian-financed digital initiative in Madagascar had been recorded as at September 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In May 2025 Madagascar contracted France's IN Groupe for about €8.5 million to supply the identity database management system, with Laxton supplying the enrolment kits. The system is to sit in the primary state data centre at Tsimbazaza, with a secondary site at Iavoloha (April 2026). No statement on who holds the source code and operating skills had been found as at September 2026. Nor had one on how the state could exit the contract.

In September 2026 the social-protection fund FID was tendering the lease of two cloud servers under World Bank financing. No rule fixing where state data is held had been found as at September 2026. The data, portability and exit terms of the Huawei-built government network financed by China Eximbank in 2020 had not been published as at September 2026, nor had those of the IN Groupe contract.

The first national data policy is to follow a roadmap for domesticating the African Union Data Policy Framework (2025). In September 2026 the digital minister told UNESCO's AI-ethics forum that digital sovereignty means international cooperation without excessive dependence, not isolation. He pressed for Malagasy to be built into AI tools.
