Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Madagascar's digital build is one large programme with a thin layer of law around it. Biometric enrolment under PRODIGY passed six million adults in its first phase against a two-million target, a law of 28 May 2026 allows births to be registered en masse inside that operation, and about 10 million civil records have been digitised.

The instruments that would govern that data do not exist. The 2015 data-protection law has never had its supervisory commission constituted, the electronic-signature law has no implementing decree, and the cybercrime bill adopted by the Council of Ministers on 8 July 2026 is not enacted.

Two things ran the other way. The same Council of Ministers ordered providers to block pornographic sites with no timetable, mechanism, regulator or appeal route, and the dominant mobile money platform replaced its core system in May 2026 and broke for two months, accounted for by the operator alone.

The data-protection law dates from 9 January 2015 and its supervisory commission has never been constituted, a finding an assessment mission put on the record in February 2025. The 2015 electronic-signature law has no implementing decree; a draft was validated at a ministry workshop in September 2025, with a framework for interoperability, and is not adopted.

The Council of Ministers adopted a bill overhauling the 2014 law on 8 July 2026, defining security incidents and critical infrastructure organisations, revising electronic evidence and connection-data retention, and requiring operators to cooperate with justice. It is not enacted.

The same Council of Ministers ordered internet service providers to block pornographic sites on public-order grounds — a cabinet decision, not a law, with no timetable, technical mechanism, named regulator or appeal route. It follows a May 2026 ministerial warning and a June 2026 announcement of a dedicated social-media law covering harassment, defamation and disinformation, of which no draft is held.

Administrative reform runs through a queue rather than a decree: the Technical Assistance Facility for the Refondation, launched on 20 July 2026, runs to 2030 with UNDP support and takes proposals from administrations through a digital platform.

Open data is institution-specific rather than a coordinated national policy. The Assemblée nationale's own portal, captured 9 August 2026, serves five parliamentary datasets through a read-only, rate-limited REST API under an explicit CC-BY-compatible reuse licence. A second, opendata.mg, is reachable but names no issuing institution, dataset or licence; the finance ministry's own portal does not resolve. No civil-society or parliamentary account of digital governance is on file at all. The texts of Madagascar's bilateral health agreements with the United States, and of the data-sharing agreements attached to them, are withheld, and in litigation since 2 April 2026.

Terrestrial capacity grew privately: Orange inaugurated a 558 km Antananarivo-Mahajanga backbone in December 2025 as third fibre operator, and AXIAN holds US$82m of its own money plus a US$100m European Investment Bank loan, US$40m of it for Madagascar, with no coverage figure published since. International capacity is contracted rather than in service: the DARE1 extension is ready for service in 2028.

The satellite layer moves faster. SpaceX ran Madagascar's first Direct-to-Cell trial on 11 July 2026, with commercial launch pending the regulator, and Airtel deepened a low-earth-orbit rural partnership with Eutelsat OneWeb. Against that, the arrangement to connect ten enclaved zones announced in March 2025 has produced no connected site on record. Yas ran a 5G-Advanced trial with Huawei on 23 July 2026; no commercial 5G service is on record.

Public connectivity money runs through one project whose supervision file is the wiki's most exact record. DECIM's envelope is US$375m, of which US$135m for digital, against the US$400m the Board approved in March 2023 after a 2024 restructuring cancelled part of the financing. Its rural-connectivity tender document slipped from end-October 2025 to January 2026, the mapping having had to descend to fokontany rather than commune level; 664,000 devices had been distributed by the June 2026 review.

Hosting is being built for the identity system rather than for a market: tenders for the main data centre and the identity-card personalisation centre opened in December 2025, and the June 2026 review confirmed two renewable-powered centres with no commissioning date. A national cybersecurity strategy was launched in December 2025 with a three-month delivery target; no strategy document is held.

Identity is the whole story. The first adult phase ran April to June 2026 and passed six million people against a two-million target, run through 2,500 kits across 1,729 centres after the proof of concept was validated in March 2026, the January pilot having enrolled about 130,000 through regime change, civil unrest and a cyclone. Counts are reported on different scopes and no cost figure is published.

What makes it more than a headcount is the law behind it: a law of 28 May 2026 permits births to be registered en masse inside the enrolment operation, bypassing a costly judicial route, with more than 19,000 fokontany chiefs confirming undocumented identities against a de-duplicated database, on an offline-first model aimed at the most excluded. Behind it, about 10 million civil records have been digitised.

The register is load-bearing beyond service delivery. The electoral commission opened a full reconstitution of the roll on 28 July 2026, running four months, with registration gated on presentation of a national identity card — which makes identity coverage the constraint on electoral participation and on the right to contest a result.

Interoperability is real and narrow: Rahaka is operational at the investment board, the social-security fund, the statistics institute, the justice ministry and the tax directorate, on X-Road, with an online one-stop window announced. Two citizen-facing services were built outside that programme: the police reporting platform, which takes online harassment, non-consensual image sharing, sextortion and AI-falsified content as reportable categories, and the higher-education accreditation platform, which publishes its own queue: 546 applications as at 10 July 2026, about 250 expected at the September session.

Payments are mobile-money-led and the dominant platform is unstable. MVola replaced its core platform in May 2026, and the outages and balance-display faults users reported in May and June are attributed by the operator to that migration; no regulator is named, no incident report published and no compensation mentioned. The e-Ariary pilot has a vendor and no reported live pilot, and Airtel Money and Baobab Bank opened wallet-to-account transfers in July 2026.

Statistical microdata sits behind a gate rather than in the open: INSTAT's microdata platform, captured 9 August 2026, advertises ERISC household surveys for 2020 to 2022, an ERISC enterprise survey and the RGPH 3 census, behind a login and registration form, with no dataset documentation, sample size, licence or reuse terms established beyond the landing page.

Applied AI is in service in two places and governed in neither. Customs launched a data and AI centre in May 2025 around an intelligent targeting system and a data warehouse, and was the IMF's African pilot for AI in customs; separately, AI indexing digitised about 10 million civil records. No national AI strategy or governing instrument is held.

What is announced is partnership rather than capability: AXIAN was named a founding partner of an African-language AI initiative in July 2026 with no Malagasy-language model released, and a Madagascar-Egypt partnership on AI in public administration is reported without a signature date. On the sector itself the wiki holds one dated index placing Madagascar behind regional comparators and no national measurement. No India-Madagascar digital cooperation instrument is held.

Affordability is managed by agreement, not regulation. Operators agreed in December 2025 to cut mobile internet prices gradually, with excise-duty relief conditioned on reinvestment in quality, resolving a standoff with the state. No price schedule or reduction figure has been published since, so the effect cannot be stated.

Device access and skills run through one donor facility. The Fonds DECIM approved all nine applications from its April 2025 call, with two operator contracts unsigned, and the accompanying skills sub-component covers a virtual university and training for 5,700 people.

The state recruits for capability it says it lacks: the digital ministry received 259 applications for ten established posts in artificial intelligence, data management, cybersecurity, telecommunications and digital law by 1 August 2026, naming its own gaps through a recruitment notice, with no appointment announced. An initiative to bring high-speed internet and AI to universities was announced in January 2026 with no campus connected on record.
