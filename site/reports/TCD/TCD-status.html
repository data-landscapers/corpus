This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Tchad Connexion 2030, published in 2025, is a US$1.5 billion plan to digitise and interconnect all public services. It sits alongside the Plan Stratégique de Développement du Numérique et des Postes 2020-2030. No dedicated digital government institution exists to run it (2025). No machinery coordinates or audits government technology across ministries, and nothing about that function is transparent to the public. The standing sector instrument remains the Strategic Plan 2020-2030 for the postal, electronic communication and digital economy sector, published by the communications ministry. It covers broadband, universal access, digital identification, digital payments and e-commerce. Drafting of a National Digital Transformation Strategy formally began on 4 November 2026, with UNDP funding an international consultancy. In November 2026 the minister told the ITU conference that national strategies for artificial intelligence and the digitalisation of administration were being developed. The ministry and the United Nations regional commission validated the national data governance strategy at a workshop in N'Djamena, on seven pillars (2026).

The "zéro cash, zéro papier" mandate sits under the Public Finance Reform Strategy 2022-2027, justified by revenue security, spending control and traceability. The 2027 orientations restate it with a new start date, and with no cost, no phasing and no named implementing agency (August 2026). The authorities present the national biometric registry as an instrument of Tchad Connexion 2030 (March 2026). In December 2025 the then-minister for digital economy framed digital regulation as sovereignty: "in the digital age, a country that does not control its data does not control its destiny".

Chad scores 34.8 out of 100 for overall governance in the 2024 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, 47th of 54 African states (2023). That is 14.5 points below the African average, and Chad scores above that average on only 15 of the index's 96 indicators. Within its best-scoring economic sub-category, Effective Administration, the professionalism and reach of the bureaucracy, fell 3.5 points to 30.8, 47th of 54.

Legislation and regulation

Ordonnance n°011/PR/2025 of 5 August 2025 amends article 18 of the 2014 law on the regulation of electronic communications and postal activities. The National Assembly ratified it on 22 October 2025 by 105 votes to 31, with 2 abstentions. The Senate followed on 23 December 2025 by 65 votes of 67, ratifying the ordinance's amendment of article 18 of Loi n°013/PR/2014. It lets the regulator ARCEP serve an operator with a mise en demeure of up to 30 days. The grounds are breach of national law or of the conditions of its authorisation, or anticompetitive conduct.

Law No. 009/PR/2015 on cybersecurity and the fight against cybercrime is the single text carrying the legal basis for cybersecurity and data protection. It also covers digital identification, digital payments, digital signatures and e-commerce. Alongside it stands Law No. 014/PR/2014, which covers broadband, quality of service, digital identification and digital payments. The 2015 package of five laws gives a broad digital-economy base. There is no right to information law, and neither supporting access-to-records measure is in place (2025). In its 2023 return to UNESCO the government named Ordinance 16/PR/2018 and Law 10-017 on the press regime as its legal guarantee of access to information.

Of thirty instruments surveyed across identity, cybersecurity, fintech, e-government, artificial intelligence and connectivity, twenty-one have no Chadian counterpart — among them a digital identity law, an interoperability framework, a national cybersecurity strategy, critical-infrastructure protection, cloud and open data policy, an AI strategy, a startup act, a regulatory sandbox and spectrum policy.

On 27 April 2026 a 34-member committee with a 45-day deadline was inaugurated to overhaul the digital, telecom and postal legal framework. In November 2026 the minister told the ITU conference that the sector's laws were being revised and a Digital Code developed. A draft labour code adopted by the Council of Ministers on 3 September 2026 would bring digital platform workers under labour law. They would be presumed salaried. On 29 October 2026 the Council of Ministers examined a draft decree on electronic communication and digitised procedure in the civil, commercial, social and administrative courts.

Data protection

On 10 April 2026 the National Assembly adopted, by 138 votes to 1, a law on biometric data in judicial and judicial-police procedures. It governs the collection, processing, retention, use and destruction of biometric data exclusively within those procedures. The text concerns police and prosecutors, and is not a general protection for personal data.

The general regime is Act No. 007/PR/2015 of 10 February 2015, implemented by Decree No. 075/PR/2019. The Act governs the collection, processing, storage and transmission of personal data, including the biometric and identity data processed under the national ID system. It sits with ANSICE, created under Act No. 006/PR/2015 and operational since 2020. ANSICE has powers to investigate complaints, authorise cross-border transfers and sanction violations. On 13 March 2026 the Senate ratified an ordinance widening its mandate from cybersecurity to personal-data protection, electronic-transaction security, cybercrime, protection of the national information space and the regulation of video-surveillance and drones. The World Bank found the framework in place not yet fully applied (2023). The government was not yet able to manage security breaches in key public systems or to provide digital trust services including authentication and certification.

Breaches of the 2015 Act carry one to five years' imprisonment and fines of XAF 1-10 million. No statutory duty obliges any data controller to notify users, regulators or the authority of a breach or unauthorised access. The general consumer protection law, No. 005/PR/2015, carries no payment-specific rules on error resolution, chargebacks or e-money disputes. SIM registration against a national ID card or passport is compulsory. Operators must maintain customer identification databases and give the government access to them. An arrêté of August 2025 requires every Starlink user, whether individual, company or public institution, to be formally identified with ARCEP or a licensed operator. Users must supply identity, address, legal references and the serial numbers of modems, routers and antennas. In April 2025 the regulator convened the identity agency, security services and operators over SIMs still sold pre-activated without the national identification number check the rules require.

Regional collaboration

Payment institutions are licensed and supervised under the CEMAC framework regulation of 21 December 2018. It replaced 2011 rules confining mobile operators to issuing electronic money. The rules governing Chad's mobile money are written mostly regionally. A licence under the 2018 regulation carries bank-grade obligations: FCFA 500 million of fully paid-up capital and reinforced solvency norms. It also requires periodic cybersecurity audits, AML/CFT processes and absolute ring-fencing of client balances. The one national rule sits in fiscal law. The 2024 finance law gives every SIM holder identified by national identification number the right to an automatically created mobile-money account from 1 January 2024. The 2024 law obliges businesses to display merchant accounts and halves the tax on electronic transfers to 0.1 per cent.

CEMAC's Additional Act No. 01/13 of 25 June 2013 abolished visas for all CEMAC nationals. Chadians travel within the six-country zone on national identity documents. Member states must issue these as biometric documents to ICAO standards. No formal digital identity interoperability treaty stands behind it. Identity crosses the border more easily than data does.

International capacity reaches Chad through a single route to Cameroon. ARCEP suspended the route's underpinning memorandum over clauses judged contrary to national digital sovereignty. The estate barely crosses the border at all. The Trans-Saharan backbone that would open a second path, via Niger to the Mediterranean, was reported 60% complete in February 2026. Where the state does reach outward it follows the corridor. The e-BNFT transport platform was launched on 3 August 2026 in Douala. It registers Chad's transport operators where the trade route puts them, rather than on Chad's own territory. The Central African internet governance forum closed at N'Djamena on 15 October 2026 with a declaration. It called for harmonised data-protection, cybercrime and electronic-communications law and cheaper transit. Its priorities include a regional anti-cyberattack platform by August 2027. The government committed to support the forum's three-year action plan. At the ITU conference in Doha on 10 November 2026 the telecoms minister asked ADETIC and ARCEP to prepare a study mission to Rwanda.

On 13 March 2024 the transitional council adopted the bill ratifying the African Telecommunications Union's Constitution and Convention. In July 2025 Chad's and Cameroon's regulators set 11 August 2025 for bilateral free roaming under the CEMAC roaming regulation. No launch has been found.

Standards

In 2025 Chad had no government interoperability framework or data-exchange arrangement. None of the operational standards on data quality, service availability or legacy-system retirement was in place or published. There was no electronic government procurement system, and no government cloud platform or policy on where government data may be hosted. On 5 August 2026 the national standards agency adopted ten international standards as draft national norms. ISO lists the agency as a correspondent member observing 7 technical committees (October 2026).

Chad is one of the six CEMAC states covered by the GIMAC interoperable QR code standard, launched on 29 July 2026. The UMAC ministerial committee had validated it in April 2026. In June 2026 ARCEP inaugurated real-time monitoring platforms covering traffic, spectrum, quality of service and VSAT. On 5 February 2026 ADETIC, ANSICE and TECHSO-GROUP agreed to audit and certify the National Data Centre against critical-infrastructure security standards.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

In August 2026 the media regulator HAMA suspended examination of applications to create online newspapers until further notice. It had denounced a proliferation of pages on social networks passing themselves off as online media and carrying false or defamatory content. The freeze carries no end date, no published criteria for resumption and no stated treatment of applications already filed. The sector association AMET welcomed the action against fake pages while drawing the press-freedom line the communiqué did not.

In 2025 there was no government e-participation platform and none of the five further participation mechanisms. Participation is Chad's second worst-scoring IIAG sub-category at 15.4 out of 100, 48th of 54. It is also its steepest decline, 13.1 points lost over 2014-2023, driven by Civil Society Space falling 24.8 points to 11.6. Deliberative and Participatory Governance, which tracks whether the state consults before deciding, rose 5.5 points to 30.1. The one standing multi-stakeholder mechanism is the national internet governance forum, created in 2014 and carrying no binding consultation mandate.

In the October 2025 telecom ratification debate, deputies pressed the minister on why the market would not support a new internet operator. They asked on what basis Starlink's concession had been granted without a public call for candidates. In April 2026 deputies warned that inappropriate use of biometric data could harm citizens' integrity and privacy, and the law passed regardless. The Economic, Social, Cultural and Environmental Council gave its 6 August 2026 session to the digital economy and artificial intelligence. It questioned the former digital economy minister on data protection, cybersecurity and AI governance. The Council adopted its recommendations on 19 August, calling for a national digitalisation policy of wider scope. It also called for a durable financing mechanism for the sector, adequate power supply and specialised digital training. Under the 2014 telecommunications law, standing to trigger enforcement against an operator extends beyond the regulator to professional bodies, user associations and affected persons. ARCEP may publish the notice and its lifting.

Finance

Financial sustainability

On 19 May 2026 the telecommunications minister told a project review that repayment of the modernisation programme's financing had begun. The Huawei-built national data centre, finished and equipped, still awaited certification.

The digital lines of the 2026 budget come to XAF 18.3 billion proposed, 17.6% of it the state's own, against 18.3% of XAF 20.1 billion appropriated in 2025 (FY2026).

The 2026 budget puts XAF 9.08 billion of loan money on the ICT modernisation project (FY2026). It puts XAF 2.77 billion of grant on the digital transformation programme (FY2026), against XAF 11.06 billion of grant on that programme in 2025 (FY2025). The fourth-quarter execution report records XAF 13.98 billion of the digital ministry's XAF 14.31 billion of 2025 grants as mobilised (2025). The World Bank's digital transformation project closes on 30 April 2029. Operations and maintenance costs and further bandwidth purchases then fall to the government.

The 2026 budget gives XAF 400 million to the cybersecurity agency ANSICE (FY2026). In 2026 it gives XAF 600 million to computerising the finance ministry and XAF 569.5 million to digitising military pensions management. The finance ministry's computerisation had XAF 2.02 billion in 2025 (FY2025). The African Development Fund's administration modernisation programme, signed in August 2026, carries a UA 9.52 million grant. The state's share is a UA 1.015 million contribution in kind (2026), and the source does not split out the programme's digital share.

Under the 2014 electronic communications law, operators fund ARCEP with 3.5% of their annual turnover, beside spectrum and numbering fees. Operators also finance the universal service fund FSUCE, which ADETIC manages. By 2023 the fund had paid for no more than a pilot of three rural telecentres. The identity card has been a paid service again since 3 August 2023.

MoUs and other agreements

In April 2025 a memorandum underpinned the N'Djamena–Mberé fibre link to Cameroon. The regulator ARCEP suspended it over clauses judged contrary to national digital sovereignty. In February 2026 the government was pressing Sotel Tchad and Airtel to fast-track a revised text.

In May 2026 ANATS and the International Organization for Migration signed a memorandum on secure identity for vulnerable populations and migrants. It builds on the SIGPTS identity system. In January 2026 ARCEP signed a space cooperation memorandum with Azerbaijan's Azercosmos. It covers satellite connectivity and remote sensing for areas fibre does not reach. An Azerbaijani ICT delegation visited ADETIC in February 2026 to discuss data centres and digital infrastructure.

In March 2026 the International Finance Corporation announced a partnership with the Chadian fintech Cashi. It aims to build an interoperable payments platform connecting banks and mobile money operators. A government mission to Washington from 23 to 31 May 2026 met the State and Commerce Departments. It also met Starlink, InterSystems, Cybastion, Vertiv and 19Labs on connectivity, cybersecurity and digital health.

On 26 June 2019 Chad and China signed a preferential loan framework agreement for the ICT Modernization Project. The Chinese Ambassador, Li Jinjin, and the economy minister, Issa Dubrani, signed it. The concessional loan agreement was signed the same day.

New investments

Chinese state finance accounts for just over half of the money committed to Chad's digital estate — about USD 215 million of the USD 417 million on record (November 2026), against USD 149 million from the World Bank across four projects and USD 40 million from the African Development Bank across two. Twenty-seven commitments make up that record, running from 2015 to 2029. Connectivity takes the bulk of it; most of the money is concessional, but the largest single line is debt.

On 26 June 2019 China Eximbank signed a government concessional loan for the ICT Modernization Project. The 2019 loan is for RMB 1.316 billion, CFA 111.4 billion or about USD 189 million at signature. Parliament approved it unanimously on 30 April 2020. On its 2019 terms it runs 20 years at 2 per cent with a five-year grace period, collateralised against a minimum cash balance in escrow. A separate RMB 170 million Chinese grant followed in 2019, and both finance one construction contract with Huawei. The project was originally to complete on 31 October 2021.

The World Bank's Chad Digital Transformation Project is an SDR 69.5 million IDA grant, about USD 92 million, running 2024 to 2029. It covers infrastructure, digital government and financial services. On 16 September 2026 the telecommunications minister told the Bank's representative of shortcomings in the project, which is to build 500 sites. Two earlier operations have closed: a USD 12 million statistical development project (2017 to 2022) and a USD 35 million revenue-administration digitalisation project (2018 to 2023).

On 20 August 2026 Chad signed an African Development Fund grant of UA 9.52 million for the first phase of PAACAMA. PAACAMA is its 2026-2030 programme to modernise public administration and improve the business climate, and the source does not split its digital share. On 20 August 2026 Chad also signed two Chinese grant agreements totalling RMB 300 million, about USD 44 million. They name digital technologies among their targets, beside infrastructure, social development and skills. The ITU's Central African ICT benchmarking project, which named Chad among eight countries, closed in 2023.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In February 2026 the government was pressing Sotel Tchad and Airtel to fast-track a revised agreement on the N'Djamena–Mberé link to Cameroon. The regulator ARCEP had suspended the underpinning memorandum over clauses it judged contrary to national digital sovereignty. That link is the landlocked country's only entry point for international submarine-cable capacity. Chad's section of the Trans-Saharan backbone toward Niger, its second corridor, was reported 60% complete in February 2026.

13.2% of the population used the internet in October 2025, on 2.79 million users, the same rate as a year earlier. ITU put unique mobile-phone penetration at 44.3% in 2024. Only 8.6% of adults owned a smartphone in 2024. About 60% of the population lay under a 4G network in 2023. What capacity exists is thin, at roughly 21 kbit/s of international bandwidth per user in 2022. Chad's IIAG Internet & Computers score of 5.5 out of 100 was the fourth worst of that index's 96 indicators in 2023.

The state's answer is PMICE, the connectivity modernisation programme launched in July 2020 with Huawei as contractor and financed by a 111 billion FCFA China Eximbank loan. Operators were ordered under a seven-day ultimatum in September 2025 to connect to the national fibre network. The first phase, covering the national data centre, a 1,200 km backbone and the state operator's mobile sites, was provisionally accepted at Sarh on 20 December 2025. Starlink runs on a multiservice ISP licence granted on application without a public tender. In June 2026 the government rejected fuel supply as a justification for the deteriorating quality of Airtel's and Moov's mobile services. Around 500 localities remain cut off from the network or without basic digital services, the outstanding challenge named for the World Bank-financed PATN programme in June 2026.

In November 2026 the minister told the ITU conference that 4,400 km of a targeted 14,000 km of optical fibre was built, 2,700 km under way and 6,900 km unfinanced. He put mobile subscriptions at 15 million and internet subscriptions at 5.6 million at end-2025.

Data Storage

On 5 February 2026 ADETIC, the cybersecurity agency ANSICE and the firm TECHSO-GROUP agreed to audit and certify the National Data Centre before it opens. The certification is against critical-infrastructure security standards. It also covers 100 micro data centres to be deployed across government institutions and linked to the centre. The centre is a four-storey building in N'Djamena, the first phase of PMICE. On 20 December 2025 the ministry declared the €175.1m first phase complete and provisionally accepted. A 2019 China Eximbank concessional loan of about 111.4 billion FCFA, repayable over 20 years at 2%, finances it. Huawei built it, with GOLF/Gulf Consulting supervising the works.

In 2016 Flexenclosure built Tigo Chad a 374 m², 400 kW modular colocation centre in N'Djamena. No higher-tier commercial site has been found. In 2023 no hyperscale or regional cloud provider ran an availability zone near enough to serve the country. Chadian users relied on distant regions, at latencies above 100ms.

In 2025 the government had no cloud platform, no policy on where its data may be hosted, and nothing published about its hosting. No localisation rule obliges data to be held in the country.

Energy

In June 2026 the government rejected fuel supply as a justification for poor network quality. It told Moov Africa Chad to solve its fuel supply or move its transmission sites to solar or wind power as it saw fit.

About 13.4% of Chad's population had access to electricity in 2024. Access reached 51% of the urban population in 2024. Under 1% of the rural population had access in 2023. Access to Energy is among Chad's ten worst-scoring IIAG indicators at 8.4 out of 100, 50th of 54 in 2023. The wider Infrastructure sub-category, which bundles transport, energy and communications, is one of the country's three worst at 17.5 out of 100. In 2023, 96% of firms experienced electrical outages. Generation is about 5% renewable, the rest oil-fired thermal, on 2021 data. The government's own energy compact of December 2024 records 487 MW installed against 235 MW available, and a generation mix 90 per cent thermal. After a fire at the Farcha plant in late July 2026, load-shedding in parts of the capital ran for close to a month.

The World Bank-financed Energy Access Scale-Up Project, approved in 2022 at about US$28.5 million, is rolling out mini-grids and standalone solar in secondary cities and rural areas. Chad has an approved national electrification plan that explicitly provides for decentralised mini-grid and off-grid solutions for productive uses and community facilities. It scores 67 of 100 on RISE's off-grid framework and 44 on its minigrids framework. The water and energy minister travelled to China in January 2026 to advance solar mini-grids for five cities, working with the World Bank and a Chinese supplier. In January 2026 electricity was the first of the three constraints named against the government's own cashless public finance mandate, ahead of uneven internet coverage and digital literacy.

Technical Capacity

In 2025 Chad's biometric identity platform ran on technology supplied by the French vendor Idemia. In 2023 the private technology base was embryonic, with very limited start-up activity and only two small tech hubs. Tertiary ICT teaching is confined to a few computer science programmes at the principal public university. A national higher school of ICT, created in 2015, graduated 121 students in its fifth cohort in December 2025.

In 2025 there was no dedicated digital government institution and no machinery coordinating or auditing government technology across ministries. There was no digital skills programme for public servants. There was neither a civil service HR management information system nor a payroll system. The effectiveness of the administration itself fell over 2014-2023.

Cybersecurity

An ordinance ratified in March 2026 created a legal class of entities of vital digital importance. It expanded the cybersecurity agency's regulatory, investigative and enforcement powers. ANSICE's mandate now reaches electronic-transaction security, cybercrime, the national information space, video-surveillance and drones. The statutory base remains Law No. 009/PR/2015 on cybersecurity and the fight against cybercrime. The same law covers digital signatures, digital identification and payments.

The ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024 places Chad in tier 4, "Evolving", the second-lowest of five tiers. In January 2025 ANSICE held a videoconference with the United States CISA on threat monitoring, engineer capacity and critical-infrastructure protection. In February 2026 it agreed with ADETIC and TECHSO-GROUP to audit and certify the National Data Centre. No statutory duty obliges any data controller to notify breaches or unauthorised access.

On 20 July 2026 ADETIC, the agency that builds the state's networks, equipped the national police for cybercrime work. On 3 August 2026 the media regulator HAMA said offences on digital platforms belong exclusively to the judicial authorities. In June 2026 ARCEP inaugurated real-time monitoring platforms covering traffic, spectrum, quality of service and VSAT. By a circular reported on 25 September 2026, the women and early-childhood ministry banned sending official documents over messaging applications. A breach is a disciplinary fault.

DPI

Data Exchange

On 1 April 2026 the tax and treasury directorates signed a protocol for automated real-time exchange of data on assessments, collections, arrears and payments. It sits inside a digitalisation of the public finance chain through SIGFiP, e-Tax and electronic invoicing. In March 2026 the finance ministry and ANATS signed a protocol for real-time consultation of the national biometric population register. It was the first connection of its kind to the register. In May 2026 Airtel's SIM registration data was interconnected with the same register.

In 2025 Chad had no government interoperability framework, and none of the operational standards that go with one was in place. The 2015 package of digital-economy laws establishes no authority for cross-government data sharing. In 2024 back-office systems had been built in silos, and no transactional digital service faced an end user. In 2023 they ran on no common technical or security standards, which prevented them exchanging data. In 2025 the tax administration's system exchanged data with no other government system. No health information system was integrated into a government-wide exchange (2025).

The 2027 budget orientations, published in August 2026, would interconnect the tax, customs and Treasury systems with budget, accounting, cadastre, land and identity platforms. No ministry is to put in place a system incompatible with those already running.

In May 2026 TCHADIX, the national internet exchange point run by ADETIC, marked its first year of operation. It keeps national traffic off international routes.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Only three per cent of Chadians over twelve held a national identity card in 2023, when the World Bank appraised the identity system. The system is SIGPTS, introduced in 2020 and run by the National Agency for Secure Documents (ANATS). Its biometric population register issues the national identification number beside the national ID card and the passport (2023). Idemia supplies the biometric technology (2025).

Enrolment begins at around age twelve (2023), and the refugee programme beside it at ten (2025). The card was free from November 2022 and became payable again from 3 August 2023, after more than 735,264 applications in the free period. By 1 May 2025 a refugee pilot had issued more than 4,000 refugee identification numbers in N'Djamena, against 7,000 targeted for the pilot area. Refugee documents had not been accepted for bank accounts or SIM registration.

In 2023 the card was the recognised proof of identity for government services and banking checks, but not the sole mandatory credential. The 2023 finance law requires the number to open a bank account, register with social protection or subscribe to utilities. Biometric enrolment of state agents against the number began in March 2023 to find fictitious civil servants. No figure on either has been published. In May 2025 the regulator found the number used for 21.77% of Airtel's identified subscribers and 43.96% of Moov Africa's, with 41,253 and 144,057 lines not identified at all.

The government intends the register to become the identification layer that public and private bodies connect to (March 2026). In May 2026 ANATS and Airtel Chad signed a partnership for mass enrolment and the securing of population data.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Chad now has three licensed payment institutions: Airtel Money, licensed in 2023, the domestic fintech Konoom in 2025 and Moov Money Tchad in 2026. With them, oversight of the mass-market mobile-money rails migrates from the telecoms regulator to the regional banking supervisor COBAC. The COBAC licence carries bank-grade capital, solvency, cybersecurity-audit and client-money obligations.

Active mobile money use runs at about 13% of the population (2025), against 55% in Cameroon. BEAC recorded FCFA 526.7 billion of mobile transactions in Chad at end-2024. Bank rails score 9.0 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index's access to banking measure, 43rd of 54 (2023). The wallets carry transfers, merchant payments and bills — electricity, water, school fees (2024).

Chad has no domestic instant payment system and participates in GIMACPAY, the CEMAC-wide platform launched commercially in July 2020. The regional central bank BEAC owns about 99.2% of GIMACPAY and also settles it (2025). Transactions inside the zone are treated identically whether domestic or cross-border, and settle through BEAC's SYGMA system once a day (2025). Chad is covered by the CEMAC interoperable QR code standard adopted in April 2026 and routed through GIMACPAY. The regional central bank has acceded to the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, with member-state banks to connect by the end of 2026.

A 2025 circular bars revenue agencies from collecting state revenue in cash. Taxpayers have been able to declare and pay online through the e-Tax platform since January 2023. The Ministry of Finance received 3,000 electronic payment terminals on 24 July 2026. A press account of September 2026 finds the terminals deployed in the revenue offices. It finds their use held back by power cuts, thin provincial connectivity and agents' limited skills. In payments going out, 0.1% of adults receive government payments in digital form, against 176,798 active mobile money accounts in 2021 (2023).

Registries

Chad ranks seventh in Africa on the Ibrahim Index's civil registration measure, at 75.0 out of 100 and up 12.5 points over 2014-2023. The measure scores the legal and institutional framework, not what it produces. In 2019 birth registration reached 25.5% of children under five, 50% in urban areas against 21.1% in rural ones. Death and marriage registration were almost non-existent.

In 2025 registry offices, hospitals, vaccination centres, municipalities, mobile units, refugee camps and schools transmitted records by SMS to ANATS. ANATS validates them and sends them back for issuance. Campaigns have registered over 120,000 children through hospital-based registry offices and over 180,000 pupils through schools since 2023 (2025). A national operation with UNICEF launched on 24 July 2026 runs a first six-month phase across twelve provinces. It counts children household by household and registers pupils at the start of the school year.

The Unified Social Registry held 684,500 households in September 2025. It does not use the national biometric identifier as its key, which limits matching (2025). The biometric voter register, 7,388,348 voters as of 2021 and updated across seven southern provinces in 2023, is compiled independently of the national population register. The courts are not connected to the register either. A workshop on 25 February 2026 was convened to lay the groundwork for an interconnection between the Tribunaux de Grande Instance and the national biometric population register. It also covered access to the late-registration judgments the courts issue. There is no national digital cadastre, only pilots (2023). Land titles began moving onto the E-Conservation system from 2023 while recording stayed largely on paper. Company registration is handled manually (2026). On 18 September 2026 the investment agency signed a convention to take company-creation fees by mobile money. The agency describes an online platform for company creation as set up. The IMF assesses the taxpayer register as incomplete and not yet reliable (2024). The newest is e-BNFT, which took enrolment of road-freight operators online on 3 August 2026.

Sectoral management information systems

In 2022 the health management information system ran on DHIS2, with almost all health districts reporting into it. In August 2024 an audit found on site that health centres send monthly activity reports that a district officer keys into the platform. The first health statistics yearbook built on DHIS2, for 2023, records report completeness of 92% at health centres and 68% at hospitals. Timeliness in 2023 was 41% and 20%. The digital health technical committee met on 9 November 2026 under a recent ministerial arrêté widening its remit to electronic patient records and telemedicine.

The education management information system has collected data from schools since 2009. By 2025 its functions had been decentralised to 16 of 23 provinces, across a system of 12,863 primary schools. On 30 October 2026 the education minister ordered the Mirador staff platform operational by end-November 2026. The order makes a biometric staff card a condition of every personnel act.

An e-Justice platform was launched with United Nations development support in October 2025. In mid-2025 the EU-funded, INTERPOL-implemented police information system was still renovating its data collection centre and rolling out fingerprint identification. The project was extended to December 2025.

Tax administration sits on SIGTAS, launched in 2015, and the E.Tax platform of the Direction Générale des Impôts, covering personal and business taxes (2026). Finance Law 2026 makes e-Tax use exclusive and mandatory and requires electronic invoicing. It provides for automated data interconnections between the tax administration and specified institutions. In 2025 the tax administration's custom-built software exchanged data with nothing else. A commercial financial management system is in use for treasury execution, at central government level only, alongside a commercial public debt management system (2025). Chad has no civil service human resource management system and no payroll system (2025).

Half of the ten core back-office government systems tracked are in use — financial management, tax, customs, social insurance and debt management — and half are absent. Of the four whose software type is recorded, three are commercial off-the-shelf products and only the tax system is custom-built.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In 2025 Chad had no national government service portal, and none of the six capabilities that would go with one. These include any means for citizens to take part in designing the services. The country scored 0.1785 on the UN's E-Government Development Index in 2024, 189th of 193. Nor is there any e-participation channel or central feedback and grievance platform (2025), or any electronic government procurement system (2025). In its place runs a platform, launched in March 2023, that registers public contracts and collects their registration duties online from approval to treasury payment.

The National Employment Promotion Office runs a job-seeker site linked to no other administrative database (2025). Businesses and taxpayers file and pay through the e-Tax platform. The 2026 finance law makes its use exclusive and mandatory. Litigants and lawyers in the capital can use the e-Justice pilot. Notarial acts moved onto e-Notaire, launched by the finance ministry in December 2024. No usage figure has been published. In April 2026 academy rectors presented OPTIMA, a locally built public-sector HR application, as having enrolled state agents in several ministries. None of these platforms shares a front door or an identity check. As at 2023 there was no gateway through which a citizen could authenticate with a digital ID, and no government-to-person or government-to-business transactional service.

In 2025 Chad had no dedicated digital government institution and nothing coordinating or auditing government technology across ministries. In October 2026 the National Police said the e-Visa platform takes more than 100 applications a day, against fewer than 10 at its launch in December 2024. The platform has been mandatory for travellers who need a visa since 11 May 2026. The 2027 budget orientations would take business creation online end to end, from filing and fees to identifiers and the delivery of documents. A platform the government calls its sovereign data platform is in use for tracking delivery of the president's programme. On 7 August 2026 the Prime Minister received a two-year report drawn from it.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In 2023 less than half the population had access to broadband connectivity, and outside N'Djamena and the secondary cities there was no grid electrification at all. In 2025 the financial management system covering treasury execution ran at central government level only. Budget orientations published in August 2026 propose generalising SIGFiP to local authorities, public establishments, projects and missions abroad, for real-time monitoring of budget execution. No rollout date is attached.

In 2025 education management information functions had been decentralised to two-thirds of the provinces, with the remainder to follow. That concerns compiling and using data at province level, not what the schools themselves record. On 23 September 2026 N'Djamena city hall and ANATS signed a memorandum launching a project to digitise the commune's civil registers. The project, supported by the Organisation Internationale de la Francophonie, is to connect the commune's systems securely to ANATS's population and secure-documents system. Interoperability work between the three began in April 2026. On 15 September 2026 the Abéché town hall, the provincial telecommunications delegation and the ICT agency met on a partnership to digitise municipal services. No service or budget has been published. On 21 April 2026 N'Djamena's commune launched a digital taxpayer census with digitised revenue collection at its central market.

On 7-8 August 2026 officials at Sarh were handed new monthly paper forms for collecting and transmitting civil-status data, five years after the TASDJIL application was introduced. In August 2026 registration coverage differed sharply between neighbouring southern provinces under the same statute, the same fee waiver and the same national campaign. The July 2026 registration campaign associates administrative and traditional authorities with the enrolment to vouch for its reliability. ANATS teams run the enrolment itself.

Rural digital data capture

In 2021 rural primary schools returned enrolment and attendance on paper questionnaires, carried up through three tiers of inspectorate. In 2022 rural clinics recorded data on paper registers, with the monthly report entered at the health district. In 2021 the refugee agency ran a separate education data system for the camps, on different tools from the ministry's. In December 2025 the government was relying on talks with technology providers to narrow the rural digital divide.

In 2025 civil registry offices, hospitals, municipalities, refugee camps and schools passed registration data to ANATS by SMS. ANATS validates it and returns it for issuance. The child registration operation of July 2026 equips teams recruited in the communities with smartphones for household visits. In 2024 a tablet-based consultation record piloted in southern Chad was being extended into southern health districts. In 2025 solar-powered telemedicine kiosks reached remote areas.

In 2023 rural electricity access was close to nil. 4G coverage stopped well short of the population in 2023, and Lac province spent months without internet after October 2024. Rural Economy Support rose 23.7 points over 2014-2023 to 53.4 out of 100. Rural Land & Water Access fell 11.4 points to 40.6.

Technology

AI

A presidential decree of 21 October 2025 created a directorate-general for artificial intelligence inside the digital ministry. The ANSICE reform approved in March 2026 gave the agency provisions on social media, digital services and AI systems. In 2024 no AI or advanced analytics was in use in any government data-exchange system. Basic interoperability between ministries had not been achieved. The Economic, Social, Cultural and Environmental Council took up the digital economy and AI on 6 August 2026. It adopted its recommendations on 19 August 2026. Two health pilots are on record: tuberculosis screening of 7,116 people, and vaccination-planning analytics launched on 2 April 2026.

Chad has no artificial-intelligence strategy and no artificial-intelligence law.

A pre-validation workshop for the National Artificial Intelligence Strategy opened in N'Djamena on 8 October 2026. The UN Economic Commission for Africa led the drafting, with priorities for 2027–2035 in agriculture, public administration, security, education and health. The draft is to go to government by the end of 2026.

ICT Industry

In 2023 Chad's business and labour environment scored 31.9 out of 100 in the Ibrahim Index, 47th of 54. Its business and competition regulations component fell 13.3 points over 2014-2023 to 43.3. In 2025 there was no startup or innovation law, and no government financing or SME support for technology firms. In 2025 trackers recorded a few dozen companies, in fintech, e-commerce and digital services. Chad ran no business or establishment survey in the ten years to 2023.

The state's own systems are bought in rather than built.

Innovation ecosystem

Chad is not among the 133 economies ranked in the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024. AfriLabs lists two N'Djamena hubs: Wenaklabs, founded in 2014, and Chad Innovation, running since 2018 and affiliated to AfriLabs. Wenaklabs hosts the Francophonie's D-CLIC programme, launched in April 2026 as Makers Tchad. In August 2025 the University of N'Djamena opened its first incubator, calling for a cohort of 25 founders. In May 2026 the employment office's PROJIACE programme selected 25 of more than 600 projects for incubation and acceleration.

In 2025 Telemedan, founded by Abakar Mahamat, delivered healthcare to remote areas through solar-powered kiosks. The Ministry of Health and the World Bank back it. Konoom, an N'Djamena payments startup launched in 2025, holds a COBAC licence for its wallet. In 2025 a minority of tracked companies had raised venture or private-equity money.

An arrêté of 1 September 2025 created a technology innovation centre, publicly launched on 30 October. It covers a digital library, start-up incubation, research laboratories, digital-skills training and administration digitalisation. A youth entrepreneurship fund created by Loi n. 005/PR/MFB/2020 was launched in July 2020 at 30 billion FCFA. At its 2020 launch it was to lend through banks under a 70 per cent state guarantee at about 6.25 per cent, with ICT among eligible sectors. Neither has published a tenant list, a cohort size or a disbursement total. The national development plan Tchad Connexion 2030, signed in May 2025, commits to drafting a start-up act and targets 600 incubated start-ups by 2030. No draft act has been found. In 2025 the government had no public-sector innovation programme or strategy.

Capacity

Literacy

Education enrolment scored 21.2 out of 100 in 2023, 48th of 54 African countries and 1.2 points below its level a decade earlier. Completion scored 14.4 and equality in education 22.3. Education is one of Chad's more improved sub-categories over 2014-2023, and still ranked 49th of 54 in 2023.

In December 2025 internet access was uneven between urban and rural areas, and technical skills were named a central driver of the transition. In 2025 no government digital skills programme reached into schools or the wider population. The education management information system has collected school data since 2009. Its annual yearbook does not reliably reach school principals and local inspectors. In 2024 a large share of pupils attended school with no document attesting their legal existence.

In July 2026 the payment-terminal programme under Tchad Connexion 2030 was to be followed by digital and financial literacy work, public digital centres and rural connectivity. On 19 September 2026 the NGO House of Africa launched a campaign on digital citizenship and internet risks for young people in Abéché.

Training and skills

In 2025 Chad had no digital skills programme for its public servants: no strategy, no programme, and no reach into schools or the wider citizenry. What training exists is run and paid for by outside bodies.

In April 2026 the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie launched its D-CLIC programme in Chad as "Makers Tchad — Jeunesse et innovation pour l'emploi", hosted at the N'Djamena innovation hub Wenaklabs. It teaches fabrication and connected-device skills, including CNC machining and the internet of things. In the east, UNHCR inaugurated a digital learning centre at the Abéché one-stop shop in February 2025, funded by Italy, for refugee and host-community young people. Both of the country's identifiable technology hubs sit in the capital. Provision outside N'Djamena depends on whichever agency chooses to place a centre there.

At least three separate donor-funded programmes carry the country's digital skills training — the OIF's Makers Tchad, the PDMFI financial-inclusion training and UNHCR's Abéché learning centre — and the two that publish participant numbers come to roughly 270 trainees (2026).

In July 2026 the ministry of higher education, research and vocational training trained its agents on the StatEduc application. The training covered questionnaire configuration, deployment of data-entry masks, consistency checking and the interpretation of indicators. It was framed as planning infrastructure for the higher-education and vocational sector. The national administration school hosted the opening content-design workshop of a five-country project to move civil-service training online in March 2025. In July 2025 the ICT agency signed a framework accord with an engineering institute in Ouagadougou to train officials in artificial intelligence and emerging technologies. Neither reports a cohort. The World Bank's digital transformation project, running to 2029, carries capacity building alongside its infrastructure and government systems components. The national higher school of information and communication technologies graduated a fifth cohort of 121 in December 2025. It received 45 computers from the ICT agency in July 2026.

Research institutions

In 2026 the Université de N'Djamena was the only institution with computer science degree programmes, and its research output was minimal. The national ICT school, a public higher-education and research establishment, graduated its fifth cohort in December 2025. An order of 3 August 2026 created an interdisciplinary AI research laboratory inside the national development research centre. Its mandate is to draw up a national AI research plan. In September 2026 the telecommunications minister asked the World Bank to equip the ICT school with modern AI laboratories.

Inclusion

Access to services

Lac province was cut off from the internet from 28 October 2024 for more than three months, a shutdown attributed to security grounds. In February 2025 Internet Society Chad protested that it isolated thousands of people from communication, education and the economy. A resident's open letter of September 2026 describes the province's internet as still heavily restricted nearly two years on. No restoration is on record. In 2025 Chad had no national government service portal, and no tax, social protection or jobs portal either. Obtaining a service means presenting yourself at an office. In 2023 government payments almost never reached people in digital form.

In 2024, 829,000 pupils were attending school with no paper attesting their legal existence. The obstacles are distance from civil-status centres, cost, and simple lack of information. In February 2026 the court fee for the jugement supplétif that substitutes for a missing birth record was suspended. In July 2026 a registration operation launched with UNICEF began working through schools and household visits. In 2024 the national identity card was a paid service. Biometric enrolment does not begin until around age twelve, so a registered child holds no credential.

By May 2025 a World Bank-funded pilot with CNARR, UNHCR and Idemia had registered refugees on SIGPTS and issued thousands of cards. The biometric IDs replace CNARR and UNHCR credentials that local authorities, banks and service providers often did not accept. In March 2026 ANATS took delivery of biometric enrolment equipment from UNHCR for the same push.

Equal Access to Public Services, which measures whether provision reaches all social groups and regions evenly, rose 13.1 points over 2014-2023 to 25.5 out of 100. That placed Chad 42nd of 54 in 2023.

Digital divides

In 2023 mains electricity reached almost none of the rural population. In December 2025 internet access showed sharp disparities between urban areas and rural regions. Narrowing that divide was still a matter of talks with a vendor under Tchad Connexion 2030. In 2019 birth registration was markedly more complete in towns than in the countryside.

In 2024 smartphone ownership among Chadian adults was among the lowest in sub-Saharan Africa. In October 2025 active SIM connections ran at several times the share of people using the internet. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index's measure of internet use, computer access and affordability put Chad near the bottom of the continent. The same profile shows education participation and outcomes among Africa's weakest.

GSMA's 2025 report carries a 29% mobile internet gender gap for sub-Saharan Africa and no country estimate for Chad.

In 2025 refugee-hosting areas in eastern Chad had very little 4G coverage, and handset cost and literacy limited mobile use further. The third population census enumerates herders and nomads through a dedicated national coordination role. On 29 September 2026 the ICT agency inaugurated a community multimedia centre at Moundou under the universal service programme. No count of other centres or of users has been published.

Data

National statistics

A decree of 20 July 2026 extended the main enumeration of Chad's third population and housing census to 6 August. An earlier decree had fixed the window in May. The stated reason was the need to reorganise teams and sweep the national territory. The census has a dedicated national coordination for enumerating herders and nomads.

In 2023 Chad's weakest pillars in the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators were data sources and data infrastructure, both at 20-49%. Data use and data products scored 70-89% in 2023. In the ten years to 2023 Chad ran household, agricultural and health surveys, but no labour force or business survey. Over twenty years it ran a population census but no agricultural or business census.

Capacity of the Statistical System rose 14.0 points over 2014-2023 to 42.7 out of 100, 35th of 54. In 2024 INSEED disseminated survey and census data through its own portal. Administrative data held by sector ministries remained poorly harmonised and outside any shared government exchange. On 22 September 2026 the health ministry opened validation of the 2025 health statistics yearbook, built on the national DHIS2 base. The higher-education ministry runs its own StatEduc questionnaires, with no published route into national statistics.

In 2024, 48.33 per cent of newborns were registered within the three-month legal deadline. About 2.9 million children under five were undeclared in 2024. The coverage rate still rests on a 2019 household survey. The 2025 DataReportal edition put internet adoption at 13.2%, with users up 4.3% and the rate unchanged. The World Bank operation funding INSEED's collection and dissemination closed in 2022.

Open data

Disclosure of Public Records is Chad's seventh most improved Ibrahim Index indicator, up 17.4 points over 2014-2023. It stood at 35.6 out of 100 in 2023, 20th of 54. Accessibility of Public Records, whether a citizen can obtain those records in practice, rose 1.9 points over 2014-2023 to 16.5, 42nd of 54.

Chad has no government open data portal, and none of the supporting measures such as catalogue coverage and update frequency are recorded (2025). A national data governance strategy was validated on 7 October 2026. No public dashboard, data-sharing agreement or annual report covers the operation of government data systems. The back-office systems that do exist, SIGFiP, the eTax application and ASYCUDA World for customs, are internal to individual ministries. There is no right to information law by which anyone could compel disclosure.

The statistics institute's site lists five diffusion channels. They are an Open Data Portal, a national data summary page, a national statistical data archive, an SDG goal tracker and an indicator tracker for the national development plan. The archive's access policy requires that microdata not be copied or transmitted to anyone without prior written agreement. It requires that a prescribed source line be cited, and that two copies of any report produced be supplied to the institute. The material is published and it is not open. The customs directorate's public data page posts trade, revenue and activity reports, with 2025 export and import analyses for download.

Chad's strongest categories in the 2024 Open Data Inventory are agriculture and health facilities at 7 out of 10. Government finance and price statistics score 6.5, and education facilities and poverty 6. Population and vital statistics score 5, with education outcomes, gender, reproductive health and crime statistics all at 4.5. Economic statistics are weaker still: international trade and labour at 3.5, balance of payments and national accounts at 4, money and banking at 5. Three of the 23 categories assessed score zero: energy, pollution, and the digital and communications category itself. The sector this report describes publishes no open national data about itself.

Geospatial data

In 2025 the national meteorology agency issued 248 alert bulletins, tracking convective storms by satellite nowcasting with European partners. It has shared 24 stations' data internationally every hour since October 2024. A SOFF grant of US$6.98 million, effective 19 November 2024 and run by the World Food Programme, is to bring the agency's observing network to the global basic standard. By June 2026 four of six sites for new automatic weather stations had been chosen. In January 2026 ARCEP signed a space cooperation memorandum with Azerbaijan's Azercosmos, covering remote sensing for areas fibre does not reach.

Starlink's operating licence was approved in November 2024 and confirmed by the telecommunications ministry in March 2025. The service went commercially live on 4 July 2025 with a published tariff grid. On 22 October 2025 the minister told the National Assembly it runs on a multiservice ISP licence, granted on application without a public tender.

In 2023 donor-supported pilots with Kadaster International, Esri and Trimble had tested fit-for-purpose cadastral mapping in selected areas. No operational national digital cadastre followed.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

From 23 to 31 May 2026 a Chadian government mission in Washington met the State and Commerce Departments. It also met Starlink, InterSystems, Cybastion, Vertiv and 19Labs on connectivity, cybersecurity and digital health.

Starlink was licensed to operate in November 2024, and went commercially live on 4 July 2025.

In January 2025 ANSICE and the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency held a videoconference on threat monitoring and critical-infrastructure protection. It followed Chad's participation in the 2024 Counter-Ransomware Initiative summit.

China activities

In January 2025 China's ambassador, Wang Xining, asked how Chad would take ownership of the National Data Centre without Chinese technicians. On 20 December 2025 the ministry declared the programme's first phase, including the data centre, complete and provisionally accepted. In February 2026 the centre's security audit and certification were still to be carried out before launch.

In January 2025 Huawei Technologies was the contractor finalising the National Data Centre. Huawei is also the construction contractor for the wider programme: a south-to-east fibre backbone and a loop around N'Djamena serving mobile sites. A China Eximbank concessional loan and a separate RMB 170 million grant of 2019 both finance that one contract. The loan is collateralised against a minimum cash balance held in an escrow account. The framework agreement was signed on 26 June 2019 and ratified on 20 August 2019.

Chinese state finance accounts for the larger part of all digital-sector money on record for Chad, ahead of the World Bank and the African Development Bank combined.

In December 2025 the government was stepping up the rollout of Tchad Connexion 2030 in talks with Huawei. On 17 August 2026 the Chinese ambassador announced 5,000 scholarships in technology and artificial intelligence.

EU activities

In mid-2025 the EU-funded, INTERPOL-implemented Chad Police Information System was still renovating its data collection centre and rolling out fingerprint identification. The project was extended to December 2025.

By May 2025 a World Bank-funded pilot with CNARR, UNHCR and the French firm Idemia had issued refugees biometric IDs in the thousands. In March 2026 Chad was still issuing refugee cards on Idemia technology, and ANATS had received biometric enrolment equipment from UNHCR.

On 14 May 2026 Chad launched a digital Journal Officiel, giving free online access to laws, decrees and procurement notices back to 1958. The government's general secretariat carries it, with UN technical support and EU funding. In February 2026 the Cour suprême received computers, scanners and server-room equipment through a European-funded parliamentary and electoral support project. In December 2020 the European Investment Bank financed three feasibility studies on broadband, telecommunications and rural connectivity. No findings from them have been found.

In February 2025 UNHCR inaugurated an Italian-funded digital learning centre at Abéché for refugee and host-community young people.

Gulf/UAE activities

In October 2023 Chad and the United Arab Emirates signed a memorandum on cybersecurity, cybercrime and international cyber cooperation in Abu Dhabi. No other Gulf instrument with Chad whose subject is digital has been found.

At the Emirates-Chad investment forum of 10 and 11 November 2025, Chad announced 20.5 billion dollars secured against its five-year plan. The November 2025 total was 4.1 billion from forty private companies and 16.4 billion in donor pledges. By September 2026 US$790.5 million of the pledges, 3.9 per cent, had been signed across seventeen companies. In January 2026 the President promulgated the law ratifying an Emirates-Chad investment protection agreement. No digital share of the pledges has been published. No Gulf state, fund, operator or vendor was found holding a position in Chad's digital infrastructure as at September 2026.

India activities

In February 2025 Chad and India discussed digital cooperation, with training in artificial intelligence among the areas Chad sought. The talks also covered support for ENASTIC, the national ICT school, and a postal bank. India held no operating, financing or vendor position in Chad's digital estate as at August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In July 2026 the government was exploring a partnership with the American firm Cybastion to audit, certify and secure the national data centre. In May 2026 ANSICE had already audited the security of the installed equipment.

In 2024 ministries were turning to private hosting. The World Bank project finances national data hosting, migration and disaster recovery, including private cloud hosting for selected ministries. In August 2026 the telecommunications minister announced two data centres commissioned in N'Djamena and a third to be built for the finance ministry. Under the 2015 law ANSICE authorises cross-border transfers of personal data. No instrument fixing where state data is held, or under whose law it is processed, was found in force as at September 2026.

A May 2026 agreement gives Moov Africa secure access to the national identification number database. The World Bank project relies on international contractors, with an emphasis on transferring skills to local teams. No record was found, as at September 2026, of whether the state holds the source code and skills to run the SIGPTS identity platform. The data jurisdiction and exit terms of the Huawei data-centre contract and of ANATS's operator agreements had not been published as at September 2026.

At the first UN global dialogue on AI governance in July 2026, the minister set out AI used in an ethical, frugal and sovereign way. In May 2026 Chad's self-assessment on the African Union's AUDP-CAT tool put its data governance maturity at 30.27%. On 7 October 2026 a workshop convened by the UN Economic Commission for Africa validated the national data governance strategy.
