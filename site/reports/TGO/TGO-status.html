This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Togo Digital 2025, adopted in 2022, covers artificial intelligence, broadband, cybersecurity, data centres, data protection, digital identification, payments and e-commerce. A 2025–2030 successor plan was still being drafted in May 2025. Its stated priorities are public services, digital entrepreneurship, access for marginalised groups, cybersecurity and digital skills. The World Bank's 2025 index records the strategy as due for updating, with its successor only planned or in draft. No adopted successor has been found as at November 2026.

A programme to digitise the administration took the foreign affairs ministry through a four-week sprint in March 2026, after energy and tourism. A National Cybersecurity Strategy runs from 2024 to 2028. On 6 November 2026 the Council of Ministers put forward for adoption a 2026–2029 postal-sector policy. Its orientations include the sector's digital transformation, digital financial inclusion and e-commerce. The same index found no cloud policy governing where government data is hosted, and no government cloud (2025).

Effective Administration rose 21.2 points over 2014–2023 to 63.3 of 100.0, 15th of 54 African states. Public Administration reached 59.1 of 100.0 in 2023, 17th of 54. The finance ministry's revenue strategy for 2027 to 2031, published in September 2026, puts CFA 5,995m of its CFA 10,500m cost into digital transformation. That share includes electronic invoicing and AI-assisted risk analysis.

Legislation and regulation

In December 2025 the National Assembly replaced Togo's 2018 anti-money-laundering law. The new law extends customer due diligence to virtual asset service providers. It transposes updated FATF recommendations and a 2023 WAEMU directive.

The orientation law on the information society and the law on electronic transactions date from 2017. The cybersecurity and cybercrime law dates from December 2018, and the personal data protection law from 29 October 2019. The law on biometric identification of September 2020 created the identity agency ANID and the unique identification number. Civil status still runs on a 2009 statute.

Public-treasury electronic money rests on a 2002 WAEMU regulation and a 2015 BCEAO instruction. Applications go to the BCEAO's national directorate, which has three months to rule. BCEAO Instruction 001-01-2024 requires every payment fintech in the union to hold a licence or cease trading. About 20 payment institutions had been licensed union-wide by September 2025, and the deadline had been put back three times.

On 30 July 2026 the Council of Ministers amended the decree setting the fees owed by electronic-communications operators. On 14 August 2026 the National Assembly adopted at first reading a bill for accession to the Hague Apostille Convention. On 21 August 2026 the Senate adopted a reform of the commercial courts that provides for an electronic platform and online hearings. In August 2026 the territorial administration ministry opened a review of the 2009 civil registration law. In September 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a bill to ratify the convention on the union's Banking Commission. The communication ministry said the 2020 Press and Communication Code would be revised to cover content creators and influencers.

In January 2025 the trade ministry consulted businesses, regulators and consumer groups, stating that the rules specific to e-commerce remained limited. No draft e-commerce text has been found as at November 2026.

Data protection

Togo's data protection authority began operating in March 2025 and opened with a public awareness campaign from 1 April. It opened more than five years after the statute that created it, and four after the decree of 9 December 2020 that constituted it. Its entire staff went to an induction workshop at Kpalimé from 30 July to 1 August 2026. In July 2025 the authority opened the declaration and authorisation of video-surveillance cameras under the 2019 law.

The 97-article law gazetted on 29 October 2019 creates the authority directly, in article 55. It runs a tiered set of prior formalities: declaration with a one-month receipt, exemption, prior authorisation, or a reasoned opinion. Article 8 makes prior authorisation mandatory for processing biometric data and for the interconnection of files. It also covers use of a national identification number or any other general-purpose identifier. The state's own biometric identification programme and its unique identification number do both. Article 9 requires a reasoned opinion before the State may process data touching state security, criminal enforcement, the census, sensitive categories, or payroll and tax.

The 2019 law gave processing on behalf of the State two years to comply, and everything else one year. Both periods expired before the regulator opened its doors. Cross-border transfer needs an adequate level of protection in the destination state plus prior notification to the authority. The law provides an authorisation route for inadequate destinations. There is no requirement that data be stored in Togo.

In June 2026 the authority gave attestations to a first cohort of 32 corporate data protection correspondents after a three-day training. The correspondents were drawn from banking, insurance, telecoms, health and local government. It trained around 3,000 young Togolese in digital hygiene with ANCy in November 2025. It convened ministry chiefs of staff and secretaries-general in Lomé on 15 September 2026 on their obligations under the 2019 law. At the first national data-protection day, on 29 October 2026, it said guides for the media, health and civil registration were in validation.

Regional collaboration

On 29 June 2021 the National Assembly voted the bill authorising Togo to ratify the African Union's Malabo Convention on cyber security and personal data protection. The government presented it as access to a harmonised regional framework and to cooperation with other states.

On 4 October 2024 ECOWAS ministers adopted the Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024–2029 and a directive on confidence-building measures in the cyber and ICT space. They directed member states to have the ECOWAS Roaming Regulation implemented by all mobile operators by the end of 2025. No published text of the directive has been found. The ECOWAS budget for 2026–2028 carries no separate digital or ICT appropriation. Its only ICT-named line, Youth Entrepreneurship in ICT, shows nil actual spending from 2024 and nil in the 2026 to 2028 estimates.

In March 2024 Togo sat in the WURI regional identity programme alongside Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire and Benin. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger left ECOWAS and WURI in 2024 and are building national biometric ID on self-hosted infrastructure. In October 2025 Senegal and Togo agreed to explore interconnecting their national identity databases. Since 10 March 2025 a pilot has interconnected the Togolese and Malian customs systems through Burkina Faso on the Lomé-Bamako corridor. In October 2025 a Senegalese delegation studied Togo's e-ID, with ANID presenting its architecture and its use of MOSIP. Mutual recognition of credentials across the bloc remained a strategy validated in March 2026.

On 30 September 2025 the BCEAO's PI-SPI instant payment platform launched across the eight WAEMU states. The BCEAO runs the core WAEMU payment systems. The Togolese and Malian data-protection authorities signed a cooperation agreement on 23 September 2026. The Togolese and Beninese authorities held a joint working session at Lomé on 2 and 3 October 2026. In March 2026 Togo was one of four pilot countries in the West African Health Organisation's first phase of automated sharing of routine health data.

Standards

On 30 July 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a decree on national conformity assessment of local and imported products before they reach the market.

The World Bank's 2025 index records a government interoperability framework as partially used across ministries. The state's enterprise-architecture guide defines that framework over four levels. Its version page records version 1.0, in draft, with the review date blank and an empty revision table. No decree or order giving the guide legal force has been found. The digital agency's catalogue of ICT standards for government systems was validated at its fourth version in July 2023. It sets standards in nine families, from security and interoperability to identity, accessibility and electronic signature. No act adopting it has been found.

Identity runs on MOSIP, the open-source platform adopted under a 2021 memorandum. Technical specifications for foundational identity systems are being harmonised across the region under WURI. The BCEAO publishes the scheme rules of its instant payment platform in a public FAQ. On 18 November 2025 it opened a public developer sandbox with technical documentation and integration guides.

In August 2026, 46 national standards drawn up by the Agence togolaise de normalisation were disseminated at Lomé and Kara. They cover agri-food products. The agency is a full member of the International Organization for Standardization.

In September 2026 the telecoms regulator was drafting a rule requiring at least 95 per cent nationwide compliance with quality-of-service standards, with penalties. External audits of payment and financial institutions are required under BCEAO rules and the OHADA accounting framework. Togo has no independent public audit oversight authority, and audit findings are not published.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Freedom of Association & Assembly stood at 12.5 of 100.0 in 2023, the second lowest-scoring of the 96 indicators on which Togo is measured. Civil Society Space fell 17.4 points to 44.4, placing it among the ten most deteriorated.

Media Freedom fell 5.2 points to 55.5 of 100.0 in 2023. Digital Freedom fell 6.8 points to 51.3, 24th of 54. The CIVICUS Monitor rates Togo's civic space Repressed, at 29 of 100, the rating it has held since 2020 (September 2026). When protests began on 26 June 2025, Facebook, Telegram and Signal were blocked on the three main networks and YouTube and DuckDuckGo disrupted. Network measurements showed every service restored by 25 September 2025. A March 2025 review by the Media Foundation for West Africa found the penal code, the 2018 cybercrime law and the 2019 national security law often used to silence journalists. On 9 November 2026 the media regulator called on content creators, bloggers and influencers to register their identity, residence and contacts in its database.

As at 2025 there is no e-participation platform and none of the five further participation mechanisms tracked. What exists is sectoral. The telecoms regulator takes complaints by a call centre, mail, e-mail, messaging apps, a web form, chat and callback. The national service portal carries a procedure letting any person over 18, resident or in the diaspora, suggest improvements to security services, administrative procedures and civil protection.

ISOC Togo runs the national Internet Governance Forum, convened for Lomé on 19 June 2026. Its theme was governing in the era of AI, big data and geopolitics. It runs alongside a national Internet governance school, TogoSIG, and a Youth IGF track.

The 53-article statute of 30 March 2016 sets a 30-day response period, 15 days for researchers and journalists. It vests oversight in the Médiateur de la République. It is implemented by a decree of 10 August 2017 running to 26 articles. Accountability & Transparency is Togo's lowest-scoring governance sub-category, at 36.0 of 100.0 in 2023. It ranks 30th of 54 and rose just 0.9 points over the decade.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2025 budget carried XOF 8.47 billion of loan money for the WURI biometric identification project and XOF 7.79 billion for the statistics harmonisation project. The 2024 budget carried XOF 1.81 billion and XOF 1.0 billion for the two projects.

The digital lines of the 2025 budget came to XOF 53.0 billion appropriated, 57.1% of it on the state's own financing codes, against 80.2% of XOF 22.6 billion in 2024 (FY2025).

The 2026 budget appropriates XOF 12.04 billion to the WURI project, XOF 5.0 billion to ProDigiT, XOF 6.21 billion to digitalising town halls and XOF 3.13 billion to the digital acceleration project. The 2026–2028 budget programme prints these four as the digital ministry's externally financed investment. The 2026 general budget prints no financing source against its other digital lines. Its 2026 special allocation accounts carry XOF 1.37 billion for the social registry and XOF 6.85 billion for the administration modernisation programme.

The 2026 budget opens a XOF 46 million chapter for hosting the DHIS2 health information system. It gives the state data-centre company SIN XOF 2.0 billion, against XOF 1.5 billion in 2025.

The 2018 cybersecurity law creates a Digital Sovereignty Fund to finance the national cybersecurity strategies, leaving its operation to a decree. A 2023 decree splits spectrum royalties equally between the state budget and the regulator. The 2023 decree also sends 30% of regulatory fines to the universal service fund.

In May 2026 Poland's development bank BGK signed about EUR 24 million for Cyber Defense Africa, the state's joint venture with Asseco Data Systems. It is the first tranche of a 12-year programme of more than EUR 80 million, under an EU EFSD+ guarantee (May 2026).

MoUs and other agreements

On 16 July 2026 Togo and Luxembourg signed three accords in Lomé carrying EUR 78.55 million. Part of the money is for digital transformation of the administration and for cybersecurity through the TogoCyber+ programme. The accords sit under an Indicative Cooperation Programme for 2026–2031 with a digital transformation component.

In June 2024 Agence Togo Digital took licences to three units of Kazakhstan's eGov stack from the state IT operator NITEC. They are an interoperability and services layer, a single web platform for public agencies, and an analytics tool. The licences followed a presidential visit to Kazakhstan and a ministry memorandum at Digital Almaty earlier that year. Kazakhstan reported USD 1.58 million of aid committed in 2024 to transfer e-government components to Togo.

On 10 December 2025 the data protection authority and the broadcast regulator signed a cooperation accord. Under it the regulator opens its control and monitoring room to the data protection authority. Since 19 October 2026 a data-protection team has worked inside the regulator's monitoring service.

New investments

On 16 July 2026 Togo signed CFA 257.4 billion of financing with the World Bank Group, about USD 429 million at that date. Its one digital line is USD 20 million more for the WURI identity project (July 2026).

The World Bank approved USD 100 million for the Togo Digital Acceleration Project in December 2024, running to 2030. It covers broadband, public services and skills, and USD 14.0 million had been disbursed at July 2026. In 2024 the IFC arranged a senior loan of up to EUR 50 million for Togocom, with co-financing taking the package above EUR 120 million. The money is for 4G and mobile money.

Germany's BMZ commits EUR 37.1 million over 2022–2028. KfW e-governance grants of EUR 20 million and EUR 15 million run fibre to town halls, hospitals and schools to 2028. The EU's ProDigiT puts EUR 4 million into digital skills to 2030.

Germany's EUR 9.5 million public financial management project ended on 30 June 2026. Togo is outside the World Bank's regional digital integration programme for West Africa. The Millennium Challenge Corporation terminated its Togo threshold programme on 18 December 2025. USD 11.1 million of the USD 20.5 million ICT project had been committed at 30 June 2025.

Phase 2 of Togo's DHIS2 education system was financed at 71,305,244 FCFA over 2023–2024. The IDA/GPE basic-education project carried 8.139 billion FCFA, and the June 2025 sector review cost 119,493,000 FCFA to organise.

That analytical layer costs under one per cent of the sector money beside it, and less than convening the review.

Togo's digital estate has drawn 39 external commitments, worth about US$365 million on windows running from 2016 to 2034 (November 2026), regional operations excluded. One, the Millennium Challenge Corporation's 2019 ICT grant, was cancelled in 2025. World Bank Group entities are the largest financiers by a distance, German public finance next (2016-2026). Connectivity and public administration take about seven tenths of the money; skills training takes about one per cent (2016-2026).

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Togo's mobile communications score rose 35.6 points over 2014-2023 to 71.1 of 100. It was the second most improved of the 96 indicators in the Ibrahim Index. Its internet and computers score reached only 35.8 in 2023, 23rd of 54. Unique mobile subscriber penetration stood at about 78.2% of the population (2025). About 37.6% of Togolese used the internet in 2023. Price is part of the distance between those two numbers. An entry-level 1GB mobile broadband basket cost around 15.1% of monthly GNI per capita in 2020. It was among the five most expensive in Africa.

Government specification pages, with prices valid to February 2023, put the incumbent's backbone at about 1,500 km north-south at 24 fibres a link. It is doubled at Lomé and on two northern axes and built to reach every prefecture. The second operator's backbone runs about 800 km at 96 fibres. The government network runs about 300 km, linking over 600 public buildings in Lomé. Its core was lifted from 10 to 100 Gbit/s. Lomé is one of eight landing points on the West Africa submarine cable built by Maroc Telecom, in service since 2021-22. Equiano was connected at Lomé in 2022, with services from 2023. It was the one modern cable still serving the region when four others failed in March 2024. A terrestrial route from Lagos to Accra through Togo and Benin was commissioned in May 2025. The Togolese joint venture of the state infrastructure company and a pan-African operator commissioned it, with partners. It is an inland path for the next subsea failure.

The index's public perception of infrastructure indicator stands at 55.2 of 100 in 2023, 12th of 54. The measured infrastructure sub-category scores 41.3, 22nd. The regulator's first national monitoring campaign of 2026 found neither mobile operator meeting its quality standards. Overall compliance in that 2026 campaign was 75.15 and 54.07 per cent. In September 2026 it tendered a public map of home-fibre networks to compare operators' coverage and speeds by locality.

Data Storage

Togo has no government cloud or shared hosting platform, and no cloud policy governing where government data is hosted (2025).

Lomé Data Centre has been operational since June 2021 and is certified Tier III+. It is run by the state-owned Société d'Infrastructures Numériques. In September 2025 its operator published more than 1 MW of critical power, a 99.982 per cent availability commitment and a power usage effectiveness of 1.4. It states direct access to submarine routes and a local exchange point, and no occupancy, rack count or delivered load. On 26 May 2026 a protocol with ST Digital committed to a private-cloud service catalogue hosted in Togo. No hyperscale cloud provider has a presence in the country.

Law No. 2019-014 requires an adequate level of protection, or the authority's authorisation, for transfers of personal data abroad. It does not require data to be stored in Togo. The e-ID records sit in an encrypted central database hosted on private cloud infrastructure provided by the IDEMIA/Atos consortium.

Energy

On 26 June 2026 the energy minister told the National Assembly that peak demand of about 360 MW was being met from about 180 MW of domestic capacity. Imports from Ghana and Nigeria had fallen from 110 and 115 MW to 20 and 70 MW.

In 2023, 86 per cent of Togolese firms experienced power outages, at an average of 2.7 a month. Access to energy scored 32.8 of 100 in 2023, 30th of 54, after an 8.1-point gain over the decade.

In December 2025 the energy ministry put national electricity access at 75%, up from 70% in 2024, with rural access below 35%. The September 2025 national energy compact targets universal access by 2030. In 2023 access was 95.0% in towns and 30.5% in rural areas.

In 2025 the state's off-grid effort ran through the CIZO pay-as-you-go solar programme, with tax exemptions and subsidies for rural households. The rural electrification agency AT2ER builds mini-grids financed by the African Development Bank, the EU-Africa Infrastructure Trust Fund, the Sustainable Energy Fund for Africa and the government. Renewables supplied around a quarter of electricity output in 2021. In 2025 the domestic tariff rose to 93 CFA francs per kWh between 31 and 120 kWh, to support the utility's finances.

On 24 June 2026 the World Bank approved USD 160 million for electricity access in Togo and The Gambia, with no split between the two stated. In September 2026 the energy ministry tendered 5,200 smart-metering devices for the state utility. Official figures put the utility's technical and commercial losses at nearly 16 per cent (September 2026).

Technical Capacity

On 12 June 2026, 80 digitalisation officers from 27 ministries were certified after three sessions from September 2025. The state's digitalisation framework sets out who holds the skill in five tiers, from the ministry to private integrators. It names giving itself legal force as a step still outstanding.

Germany's digital transformation programme, designed in 2022 and running to 2028, names weak public-private cooperation and patchy technical expertise as obstacles. The national e-ID database was built, and is hosted, by the IDEMIA/Atos consortium.

In 2023 the education ministry recruited a StatEduc consultant and an information specialist to move its databases from MS Access to PostgreSQL. The same plan moved the school census to online entry. Training for inspectors on the education data system reached few staff, was never refreshed, and devices are short.

Effective administration is among the ten most improved of Togo's IIAG indicators over 2014–2023. Togo Developers and Python Togo, the two main open-source groups on GitHub, counted a few dozen members between them in 2026.

Cybersecurity

On the ITU's index of national cybersecurity commitments, Togo was placed in the second tier in September 2024. It was one of four African states scoring between 85 and 95 of 100.

The regime rests on Law No. 2018-026 of 7 December 2018, which creates the national cybersecurity agency at article 6. The law defines the cybercrime offences and procedure and provides for a digital sovereignty fund. It refers implementing decrees to its article 48. It was amended by Law No. 2022-009 of 24 June 2022. A July 2019 decree sets how the agency designates operators of essential services. An August 2022 decree governs the qualification of trust providers and security products. The estate is governed by ANCy, the CERT.tg incident-response centre and a National Cybersecurity Strategy running 2024-2028. The strategy's published text sets four pillars under a strategic committee chaired by the Prime Minister. Luxembourg's July 2026 cooperation agreements fund the security of the administration's digital infrastructure through a TogoCyber+ programme.

Under BCEAO Instruction n°001-03-2021, payment service providers and financial market infrastructure managers must notify the central bank of a service-interrupting incident on a fixed clock. Togo's own data protection law imposes no equivalent breach notification. An ECOWAS directive on confidence-building measures in the cyber and ICT space instructed member states to implement it on publication. As at August 2026 the text has still not been published, so nothing has been triggered.

Cyber Defense Africa, the joint venture the state formed with Asseco Data Systems in 2019, received about EUR 24 million from Poland's BGK in May 2026. Its operations centre handled 63,763 incidents in 2025, about 35 per cent of the 181,088 in 2024, on the agency's own reports. Fourteen essential-service operators were audited for compliance under the 2019 decree. The cybersecurity agency issued a call for expressions of interest on 27 August 2026 to build a national pool of cybersecurity professionals.

DPI

Data Exchange

In October 2026 Togo and the Sahel Confederation states set 31 January 2027 for a technical unit to supervise interconnected customs data flows. A pilot has linked the Togolese and Malian customs systems on the Lomé-Bamako corridor since March 2025.

The World Bank's social assistance programme targets formal interoperability protocols between government systems for December 2028 (September 2024). The enterprise-architecture guide defines a common interoperability framework over four levels. Its version page records version 1.0, in draft, with an empty revision log.

An order of 10 September 2024 sets the route for a public body to obtain social-registry data. The body signs a convention with the identity agency and obtains the data-protection authority's authorisation. Each request is logged, and the agency reports quarterly. In April 2025 the government said a federated inter-administration network, RSIA, had been piloted since late 2024 with 15 institutions connected. Each institution keeps its own data and exposes services through a local security server. No later stage of RSIA has been found.

In 2025 customs, tax administration and social insurance exchanged data with other government systems; the civil service HR system exchanged none. Since January 2019 the business one-stop shop's database has been linked to the commerce register, so registering a company also registers it for tax. Banks have been connected to the revenue office's tele-payment platform since 2016–2018.

In 2023 the tax identification number was a separate credential from the biometric NIU, with no automated link. Identity records cannot be checked against the civil registry to confirm a death. Linking the e-ID with civil status, the identity card, passports and nationality remains at the design stage. The electronic visa launched in June 2022 connects to neither passport issuance nor immigration control.

Digital Identity and CRVS

When enrolment closed in the Centrale region on 26 September 2026, more than six million people had been registered and about 5.9 million identity cards produced. On the agency's figures, nearly 80 per cent of those cards had been distributed by September 2026. Kara is the last region to cover, from October to December 2026. By 21 October 2026, 4.72 million of the 5.9 million cards had been handed to their holders.

Law No. 2020-009 of 10 September 2020 creates ANID, the NIU and the e-ID, and was amended in June 2022. It makes the number legal proof of identity before the public administration. Registration is not compulsory for individuals, but public bodies must record and use the NIU when they identify someone. Enrolment captures a facial photograph, ten fingerprints and both irises. Children under five are registered on demographic data alone, against a parent's number. Every resident is eligible, refugees and asylum seekers included. The number and the card are both free. On the agency's own account, the paper card reaches only a small minority of Togolese.

A decree of 26 December 2024, gazetted 23 April 2025, requires telecom operators to collect the number for every subscriber, national or resident. Operators must accept the identity agency's card where no other document exists, retain the data and report to the regulator. The decree obliges operators to collect the number; it does not oblige them to query the agency to verify it. The NIU coexists with the legacy national identity card and has not replaced it (2025). The tax number is obtained separately, and civil registration is not linked to the NIU.

The system runs on the open-source MOSIP platform, adopted under a 2021 memorandum. It is built and hosted by the IDEMIA/Atos consortium on private cloud infrastructure. It is financed largely from outside, with about $72 million from the World Bank's regional WURI programme (2024). That financing was topped up again in July 2026. Oversight runs through the ordinary courts and the data protection authority, with no mechanism specific to identity.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Togo's pension fund made its first disbursements by bank transfer and mobile money in July 2026 and gave beneficiaries until 31 August 2026 to supply payment details or have payment interrupted. The Caisse de Retraites du Togo ended cash payment at its counters from July 2026, paying pensions through Mixx by Yas, Flooz or bank transfer.

The BCEAO's instant payment platform PI-SPI went live across the eight WAEMU states on 30 September 2025. Mixx by Yas became the first mobile wallet on the platform on 14 April 2026. By the end of October 2026 the platform had 3.9 million users and 17 connected providers in Togo, and had carried 163,000 transactions worth nearly CFA 15 billion. Since 2 November 2026 interoperable electronic-money transfers between individuals in the union must go through it. The BCEAO set 30 September 2026 as the connection deadline for banks, electronic-money issuers and payment institutions, and 30 June 2027 for microfinance institutions.

Togo held 12.55 million electronic-money accounts at the end of 2024, 6.07 million of them active. They were served by more than 81,000 service points. Active accounts grew 76.87% over 2024, the fastest rate in the union. The market is a duopoly, Mixx on 58% against Moov's Flooz on 42% (July 2026). Penetration stood at 57.2%, with CFA 1,398 billion transacted in the third quarter of 2025. Access to banking services remains among Togo's ten worst-scoring governance indicators, at 22.1 of 100 in 2023.

Novissi paid about 920,000 beneficiaries, 63% of them women, directly into mobile money accounts (2023). It lifted national mobile money penetration by around seven percentage points. The audit court found that Novissi's 2020 transfers and running costs totalled CFA 13.17 billion, and that the digital economy minister produced no consolidated accounts of them (January 2023). The Treasury plans prepaid cards issued only to people the state pays or who owe it money (July 2026). The revenue office launched e-TPU on 17 September 2026. On the platform, small and micro enterprises, artisans and traders declare and pay the single business tax online.

Registries

Togo reports that more than 99% of its land titles have been digitised and that land procedures can be completed online (2022). Only about 46,183 titles have ever been granted since colonial times, with customary tenure covering most of the country (2023). The digitised land books were delivered in December 2025, and the land directorate stopped issuing manual titles. A national land agency created in September 2025 received its first director general in October 2026. On 6 November 2026 the Council of Ministers examined at first reading a bill amending the 2018 land code to digitise land management and create a national land observatory.

A decree of 24 April 2023 sets up the social registry, held electronically. Each entry carries a person's identity number and a household number. The identity agency manages, maintains and verifies it. Administrations must base aid programmes on it and keep electronic beneficiary registers, and it is audited each year against the targeting rules. Coverage was effectively zero as at September 2024, against a target of half the population registered by December 2028. It remains partially deployed (2025). Novissi was targeted through the voter register and telephone records for want of a social registry. The voter register covered 93% of the population in 2020, and more than 4.2 million voters were registered for the April 2024 elections.

The 2009 civil status law requires an act for every birth, marriage and death of anyone resident in Togo, of whatever nationality. Civil registration is one of the country's best-scoring governance indicators, at 75.0 of 100 in 2023 and seventh of 54 African states. The 2017 national survey found 17.1% of children unregistered and a birth certificate for only 40.9% of under-fives. The registers remain on paper: what went online in December 2025 was the request for copies of acts, in five pilot communes. Identity records cannot be cross-checked against the civil registry to confirm a death.

The commerce register was reissued online in 2019 as a fully digital register open to the public. The one-stop shop issues a single card carrying commerce, tax and social security numbers together.

Sectoral management information systems

Returns from Togo's roughly 16,000 pre-primary, primary and secondary schools are filled in on paper (2022). They are digitised only once they reach the district inspectorates, which key them into StatEduc. DHIS2 sits above that stack as the analytical layer; it does not replace it as the collection layer. In health, DHIS2 has been the health ministry's information system since 2016. It is the national repository, owned and maintained by the ministry. In 2025, 88% of districts submitted more than 90% of their monthly reports, and the data-quality score rose to 82. The evaluation of the 2018-2022 health information plan found a patient-record system for regional and university hospitals 20% built (March 2023). The health ministry made a national digital health strategy its first information-system priority for 2024-2025 (November 2023); none has been found since.

Financial management, tax administration, customs, human resources, social insurance and pensions, debt management and electronic procurement are all recorded as in use (2025). Payroll is the one core system absent. The financial management system reaches central government only. The finance ministry ran a web-based budget system, SOLAF, in parallel with the older integrated system through 2024. It trained 236 regional authorising officers in it, with a decision on a full switch due at the year's end. No decision has been found. In November 2026 the finance ministry called for a firm to diagnose its budget and accounting information system.

The revenue authority's nationwide tax census enumerated 65,958 economic units to March 2022; only 7.7% of those answering held a valid tax identification number. The West Africa Police Information System has operated since 2022 from a central unit in Lomé rather than at rural facilities. The criminal procedure code requires officers to draft and sign procès-verbaux and to keep physical custody registers on site, so case data starts on paper as a matter of law. The commercial court's SYSCOM platform takes electronic filing, notifies and enrols appeals, and transfers appealed files to the court of appeal. Fees for about forty services of the Lomé high court have been payable online since 2 July 2026.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Togo's public service portal listed 155 administrative procedures in October 2026, on the national digital observatory's count. The Togo Digital Agency runs it, and citizens open accounts with national identity documents. A companion app, Gouv Check, verifies the QR codes on the documents it issues. A new version went live on 3 September 2026, keeping existing logins and past requests. The World Bank's 2025 index records the portal as transactional, with citizens involved in its design.

Since 15 December 2025 citizens of five pilot communes can request civil-status records online and track them. The energy ministry added 29 procedures at the same time. The foreign ministry put ten procedures on the portal from 1 October 2026, and the communication ministry put ten services of the state broadcasters online.

Since 15 October 2019 large and medium enterprises have had to file and pay taxes online. The revenue office's exemptions platform became mandatory on 1 January 2026. An electronic visa has been available since June 2022. In July 2026 the volunteering agency launched a mobile app for its four programmes.

In 2025 government procurement published tender notices and awards online. In October 2026 the procurement regulator opened recruitment of four consultants to set up electronic procurement.

In 2025 there was no central feedback or grievance platform for public services, and no e-participation platform. The social protection portal was still being implemented. In January 2025 the security ministry opened an online platform for reporting abuses by the security forces.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

Since December 2025 citizens of Agoè-Nyivé 1, Golfe 1, Avé 2, Zio 2 and Lacs 1 can request civil-status documents online and track them. Those five communes received computer equipment in April 2026. No other commune offering an online service has been found.

KfW is financing a EUR 20 million grant programme over 2024–2028 to connect town halls, hospitals and schools to the fibre network. It also strengthens the capacity and equipment of municipal administrations. A second phase commits EUR 15 million over 2025–2028 to municipal services, transparency and further connections. Germany's public financial management programme ended on 30 June 2026. Its successor works on communal and regional financial governance in the Savanes and Kara regions.

Since 2019 Togo has run a DHIS2-based education information system nationally, built with GPE KIX and Norad support. Its dashboards cover enrolment, teacher-pupil ratios and exam results for district and school staff. It works best at regional level, where staff use it to decide where to send teachers and build schools. Validated data reaches it weeks after collection, too late for in-year decisions at district or school level.

In March 2025 Doufelgou 1 and Sotouboua 2 validated taxpayer files of 597 and 991 taxpayers built with the SIG Recettes application. Bassar 1 validated a census of 1,358 taxpayers the same month. On 11 August 2026 Golfe 2, in Greater Lomé, launched a georeferenced census of households, businesses and taxpayers. Togo has no operating national addressing system, and address databases exist only in Grand Lomé.

Rural digital data capture

Biometric enrolment opened in Maritime region Zone 1 in November 2025, ran as mass enrolment in Zio to February 2026, and moved to the Plateaux-Ouest prefectures in May 2026. The agriculture ministry is digitising producer registries for five value chains, with technicians capturing data on smartphones. Since 2024 community health workers in the Maritime Region have reported through an electronic community health information system. In October 2026 the agriculture ministry held a national consultation, with World Bank support, on digital public infrastructure for agriculture.

In 2022 returns from about 16,000 schools were filled in on paper and digitised only at the district inspectorates. The criminal procedure code requires gendarmerie officers to keep physical custody registers on site.

Rural Economy is one of Togo's three best-scoring IIAG sub-categories, at 63.6 of 100.0 in 2023, 25th of 54. Rural Land & Water Access rose 22.9 points over 2014–2023 to 62.9, and Rural Economy Support 20.9 points to 66.5. Rural Market Access fell 17.3 points to 63.0 by 2023.

Most of the rural population had no electricity connection in 2023. The land title register has never covered more than a small minority of holdings. A July 2026 amendment to spectrum royalties was framed as drawing satellite and machine-to-machine operators to Togo, to extend rural connectivity.

Technology

AI

Voix du Togo was launched in October 2026 as a three-year programme collecting voice and text in Ewe, Kabiye and Moba first, to train models for the national languages. It is part of the open-source national programme for the country's 50 languages run by the Togo AI Lab.

The Togo AI Lab is a ministry initiative run with the University of California Berkeley's Center for Effective Global Action, the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation, IDF and Germany's GIZ ProDigiT programme. It delivers the language project with the pan-African data-science platform Zindi and the Togolese startup Umbaji. The programme sets a per-language target of 50 hours of validated audio recordings and 6,000 translated sentence pairs (July 2026). These are to be gathered through four Zindi competitions carrying about $40,000 in prizes.

The national Internet Governance Forum was convened for Lomé in June 2026 around governing in the era of AI, big data and geopolitics. Digital minister Cina Lawson calls national-language models "essential public infrastructure". AI is one of the domains named in the Togo Digital 2025 strategy, adopted in 2022 for a horizon to 2025. Advanced degrees in the field are under development at the École Polytechnique de Lomé (2026).

A six-pillar national strategy was presented in draft in December 2025, with no adoption on record. The economy ministry launched an AI-based market-inspection platform in March 2026. Since July 2026 a UNDP-donated GPU server at Lomé Data Centre, run by the Togo AI Lab, has powered an assistant for the public-procurement control directorate. The World Bank's 2025 index records no artificial intelligence in citizen-facing public services. The country's one deployed model predates the lab: machine learning over mobile-phone metadata and satellite data to decide who received Novissi cash payments (2023). The local-language AI pilot gathers voice and text through paid public contributions, with UNDP support. The Ayre Index scored Togo 42.6 of 100 in November 2026, below its readiness threshold of 53 and 37th of 56 Commonwealth states.

ICT Industry

In June 2026 Agence Togo Digital said that the great majority of Togolese firms are micro, small or medium, and only a small minority of them use digital tools at all. That month the agency launched a digital-maturity toolbox for small firms. It offers a diagnostic, a catalogue of solutions, certified advisors and a resource library. It is funded through GIZ's ProDigiT programme, with German, Luxembourgish and EU backing. The Togo Digital 2025 strategy set a target of 3,000 firms digitised, and that strategy's horizon has now passed.

The digital economy was about 4% of GDP in 2022, against a target of 10%, with a new strategy in preparation for 2025–2030. Telecommunications, computer and information services earned a trade surplus of CFA 11,956 million in 2024, almost all of it from telecommunications. Computer services earned CFA 19 million abroad in 2024 against CFA 8,586 million spent. The startups that founded the industry body TogoTech are Togolese-owned and consumer-facing, and employ a few hundred people between them at most. Gozem's venture raises are the sector's largest by a distance.

In 2024 the investment agency API-ZF digitised twelve company-establishment procedures through its single window. A small-firm programme paid for by German cooperation runs alongside it. Ecobank, headquartered in Lomé, built its instant cross-border service on the rails of the Singaporean fintech Thunes (October 2025). The hosting market is the subject of a dedicated data-centre market briefing (July 2025). Most firms reported power outages in the World Bank's 2023 enterprise survey.

Innovation ecosystem

In November 2025 the BCEAO opened a public developer sandbox for instant payments. Semoa won the most complete non-bank payments licence in January 2026, and Gozem Money entered service in October 2025.

Togo ranked 117th of 133 economies in the WIPO Global Innovation Index 2024, with a score of 15.6. It was third among low-income economies, behind Rwanda and Madagascar.

In 2025 trackers counted a few hundred registered startups, few of which had raised outside money. Gozem raised a $30 million Series B in February 2025. Development finance institutions still did most of the investing. In October 2025 about 15 firms, Gozem, Semoa, Édolé, MiaPay and Clinicaa among them, launched the industry body TogoTech in Lomé.

On 11 January 2023 the Council of Ministers adopted a draft innovation law, with start-up labels and tax and customs incentives. In October 2025 the digital minister said the Startup Act was still being adopted. It provides labels, a support fund for labelled firms, regulatory sandboxes and a national start-up register. In May 2026 an opposition deputy wrote that the National Assembly was about to adopt it.

In 2025 Djanta Tech Hub in Lomé, funded by the World Bank, ran pre-incubation and incubation cohorts with CcHub. It is planned as the first of more than 100 hubs. The World Bank's 2025 index records a strategy and a running programme for public sector innovation.

Capacity

Literacy

In June 2026 a programme to train 20,000 civil servants in basic digital skills was announced, with no start date. The data protection authority opened with a public awareness campaign in April 2025. In November 2025 it ran basic digital hygiene training for young Togolese with the cybersecurity agency.

Education completion rose 22.0 points over 2014–2023 to 58.6 of 100.0, 16th of 54 African countries. The wider education score rose 10.1 points to 54.8, 24th of 54.

Equality in education scored 63.9 of 100.0 in 2023, 12th of 54, and fell 2.6 points over the decade. The teaching workforce scored 50.3, 35th of 54, after a 22.8-point rise. Enrolment and education quality both scored 48.7.

In January 2021, 18% of Togolese owned a computer and 32% had a phone that could reach the internet. In the same survey, 18% often got their information over the internet (January 2021).

Training and skills

At inspection level, the training for Togo's education data system was delivered years earlier and never refreshed (May 2026). It reached only a few staff at each level, and devices are in short supply.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs trained 280 agents in cybersecurity and digital tools as part of a digitisation sprint (March 2026). The cybersecurity agency began certified training for 250 IT staff in 2025. The data protection authority has trained a first cohort of 32 corporate data protection correspondents (June 2026). By September 2026 the scheme had been extended to 33 ministries and public institutions, briefed on naming certified correspondents. Across the public service there is a digital skills programme covering advanced digital skills and data literacy, with the training free to take (2025). Eighty digitalisation officers from 27 ministries, selected from 1,442 candidates, were certified at Lomé on 12 June 2026. Their three sessions from September 2025 covered project and product management, leadership and artificial-intelligence tools.

IAI-Togo has taught computing in Lomé since 2002 as part of a pan-African higher-education network. Higher-education graduates fell from 13,925 in 2021 to 11,769 in 2023. The Ecole Polytechnique de Lomé is building advanced degrees in engineering mathematics, computer science, artificial intelligence and data science (2026). The university joined the Campus 42 peer-learning network on 25 and 26 April 2026. It teaches programming and artificial intelligence with no instructors or lectures. It is open to people without conventional academic backgrounds. A first artificial-intelligence summer school trained 100 students, researchers and young professionals from 24 to 29 August 2026.

The World Bank's Togo Digital Acceleration Project funds digital skills training among its four components through to 2030 (2024). The European Union's ProDigiT runs to 2030 (2024). The data-protection authority and the Universite de Kara signed a convention for a professional master's in digital law, cybersecurity and data protection (2026). The Francophonie's D-CLIC programme trained 244 young people and women in web and mobile development and digital marketing (May 2025). In June 2025 the government launched free online programming and AI courses in English with an AI tutor, targeting 15,000 students by the end of that year.

Research institutions

In July 2026 the Togo AI Lab was running an open-source national programme to build AI models for the country's 50 languages. Éwé, Mina, Kabyè, Tem, Moba and Bassar are among them. The lab is a ministry initiative run with Berkeley's Center for Effective Global Action, the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation, IDF and GIZ. It delivers the language project with the data-science platform Zindi and the Togolese startup Umbaji, gathering data through prize competitions. HISP West and Central Africa built the education information system with Norwegian and Global Partnership for Education funding.

Togo ranked 117th of 133 economies in the 2024 Global Innovation Index, with a score of 15.6.

IAI-Togo has taught computing in Lomé since 2002. The Ecole Polytechnique de Lomé is developing advanced degrees in AI and data science with a French partner university (2026).

Inclusion

Access to services

Togo ranked 9th of 54 African states on the IIAG's measure of how evenly public services reach different groups, at 59.2 of 100.0 in 2023. Access to banking services is among the country's ten worst-scoring indicators on the same index. At the end of 2024, 6.07 million electronic-money accounts were active. Togolese report that identity documents are not easy to obtain, a perception that has slipped since 2014.

Under the 2020 identity law, enrolment and the credential are free, where the old paper card carried a fee. Anyone staying in the country is eligible, refugees and asylum seekers included. Children can be registered from birth on a parent's number. Registration is not compulsory, but administrations must record and use the number.

Between 2023 and 2025 the national refugee body recorded 41,773 Burkinabé granted refugee status. It produced and distributed 10,972 refugee identity cards in the Savanes between 22 May and 16 July 2025. Refugees are documented on a system separate from the national identity register. On 4 August 2026 the African Development Bank approved a US$1 million grant to register and document 5,000 new arrivals. The region then hosted more than 55,000 refugees and close to 16,000 internally displaced people. In 2023 the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child raised a residual statelessness risk for children of refugee parents.

In December 2025 online civil-status requests opened in five pilot communes. In 2025 tax registration, filing and payment, and job applications, ran through portals in use. The social protection portal was not yet in use, and there was no central grievance channel. Abuses by the security forces can be reported online since January 2025, and the health insurer opened an online complaints platform on 27 March 2026.

Digital divides

In August 2026 Togo switched pension payments to bank transfer and mobile money, and named no route for a beneficiary with no bank account, no handset or no mobile coverage. The two support channels offered, a freephone number and a messaging app, both need a connection. Payment is interrupted for those who do not supply their details in time.

In 2023 electricity access split sharply between town and country, and internet use remained a minority activity. The IIAG scores Togo far lower on internet and computers than on mobile communications (2023). When Afrobarometer last asked, in January 2021, computer ownership and internet-capable handsets were both minority holdings. Mobile data cost about 9.5% of average monthly income in 2026, and more than a fifth of the income of the poorest 40%. Only 44.9% of young people used the internet (2026).

On the IIAG's 2023 data, the evenness of educational provision is one of the few education measures to have gone backwards over the decade. The education MIS reports enrolment, teacher–learner ratios and exam results by gender and by disability status. No such breakdown has been found for most other services. Biometric enrolment for the e-ID has run region by region (2025). Kara, the last region, is to be covered from October to December 2026. Where a person lives therefore still bears on whether they have been enrolled.

Data

National statistics

Field preparation for a third harmonised household living-conditions survey opened on 9 February 2026, and the survey runs in the field to December 2026. A fourth demographic and health survey launched on 13 March 2026 over about 10,000 households, the first since 2013–2014. Its results are expected in 2027.

Togo's Capacity of the Statistical System score reached 64.2 of 100.0 in 2023, up 13.4 points over the decade and ninth of 54 African states.

In 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators put Togo above 89% on data use and at 70–89% on data products. In 2023 it scored 50–69% on services and infrastructure and 20–49% on data sources, its weakest pillar. Over the decade to 2023 Togo fielded three or more household surveys, two health surveys, one agricultural and one labour force survey, and no business survey. Its latest population and business censuses fall within ten years, its latest agricultural census within twenty.

The second national statistics development strategy was adopted on 27 August 2020 for 2020 to 2024. In July 2026 the statistics council adopted strategic orientations for the statistical system. No successor strategy has been found as at November 2026.

In April 2024 an independent panel found the education sector's data function not yet fully accessible and operational. In December 2022 the tax authority's census found the great majority of economic units outside the tax register.

Open data

In December 2024 the Togo Digital Agency launched an open data platform, opendata.gouv.tg, and a geoportal, geodata.gouv.tg. They centralise data from 23 ministries and over 550 administrative units. In April 2026 the statistics institute trained focal points to move the portal from version 1.0 to 2.0, with SDMX modelling. The portal's terms place published data under an open licence unless stated otherwise, and name no specific licence (November 2026). The World Bank's 2025 review found the portal updated weekly or daily, with basic datasets entered mostly by hand. No national open data policy has been found as at November 2026.

The Open Data Inventory's 2024 scores put balance of payments and money and banking at 7 of 10, prices at 6.5, and government finance and national accounts at 5.5. Trade scored 3.5 and labour 4.5. Education outcomes scored 7, and health outcomes, food security and crime 4 each. Pollution scored 3, the lowest of any category.

The 2016 access-to-information law and its 2017 decree are in force, with oversight by the Médiateur. The 2025 review records a data governance function led by an autonomous institution. In 2023 disclosure of public records scored 24.2 of 100.0, 39th of 54, and their accessibility 28.5, 30th of 54. Both are among Togo's ten worst-scoring IIAG indicators.

In 2025 tender notices and awarded contracts were published online, and the commercial register has been open to the public since its 2019 online release.

Geospatial data

The Novissi emergency transfer programme identified most of its recipients by applying machine learning to mobile phone metadata and satellite data (2023). The government's data lab, run with UC Berkeley, applies satellite-imagery models for ministries, agriculture among them (2025). Under the food-system resilience programme, the meteorological agency was equipped with a satellite image reception antenna and new agrometeorological stations. Agro-hydro-climatic forecasts are pushed to producers by SMS and voice call. On 7 and 8 April 2026, index-insurance claims resting on satellite analysis and climate data were paid to producers in three agricultural zones. The government's data laboratory tracks agricultural plots from imagery and trains models forecasting maize and sorghum production. No Togolese satellite has been found. Togo's infrastructure single window runs on CENTIPOS, a national network of 25 GNSS correction stations giving centimetre-precision positioning. The network also serves the cadastre, precision agriculture and environmental mapping. The Directorate of Cadastre and Land Conservation is modernising the cadastre through a georeferencing programme for land titles (2025). It is adding a digital cadastral layer alongside the digitised titles. The digital minister announced an earth-observation cooperation under which Kyrgyzstan is to transmit satellite data for agriculture and train local staff (2026). A US$25 million Green Climate Fund grant is to set up a climate information and early-warning system (2025). It is the country's first single-country project with the fund. No system built under it has been found.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In July 2026 the Togo AI Lab, a ministry initiative, was run with the University of California Berkeley's Center for Effective Global Action and the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation. IDF and Germany's GIZ ProDigiT programme are also partners. The lab delivers its national-language AI project with the pan-African data-science platform Zindi and the Togolese startup Umbaji. By October 2026 the laboratory was working with more than ten public agencies on over 70 projects. Google.org committed USD 2 million in December 2025 to scale the Togo Data Lab, run by the digital ministry with the Berkeley centre. The Millennium Challenge Corporation terminated its Togo threshold programme, with its USD 20.5 million ICT project, on 18 December 2025. Google's Equiano subsea cable reached Lomé in March 2022. It is operated in Togo by a joint venture of the state infrastructure company and CSquared. No US hyperscaler data centre or cloud region in Togo had been found as at November 2026.

China activities

On 16 October 2026 Togo signed a memorandum of understanding with Huawei in Shenzhen covering energy, smart transport and smart customs systems. It provides for technology transfer and the training of local staff, and states no value. It follows a strategic partnership protocol concluded with Huawei in September 2024, in the margins of the China-Africa forum. The trade minister signed it for Togo, to strengthen cooperation in information and communication technology. Its text has not been found, and no amount, scope or timetable has been stated. No Chinese-financed digital infrastructure project in Togo has been found. Togolese officials toured China's G60 science and technology corridor on 31 July 2026, during a seminar run by the Chinese commerce ministry. Thirteen accords were signed in Beijing on 15 October 2026. They include a commitment to deepen cooperation on digital commerce and supply chains. They also include a customs Authorised Economic Operator protocol between the revenue office and China's customs administration, and partnerships between China Media Group and the state broadcaster. None of their texts has been found. Ecobank, headquartered in Lomé, signed a memorandum with China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System on 10 October 2026. It aims to speed payments between its 34 African markets and China.

EU activities

Germany's EUR 9.5 million public financial management programme, run by GIZ, ended on 30 June 2026. Its successor works on communal and regional financial governance in the Savanes and Kara regions. It does not carry forward the central-government digital tools, and no body or budget has been named to sustain them.

The EU's ProDigiT commits EUR 4 million over 2024–2030 to digital skills. It is delivered by GIZ with German, Luxembourgish and EU backing. In June 2026 it funded Agence Togo Digital's digital-maturity toolbox for small firms. It is also among the backers of the Togo AI Lab.

On 16 July 2026 Luxembourg signed three cooperation agreements in Lomé, part of which fund digital transformation and cybersecurity through TogoCyber+. Poland's BGK financing for Cyber Defense Africa, signed in May 2026, carries an EU EFSD+ guarantee. The agriculture ministry is digitising producer registries with EU and GIZ support under the ProComp programme. The EU-Africa Infrastructure Trust Fund co-finances the rural electrification agency's mini-grids.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf-state investment in Togo's digital infrastructure was on the public record as at August 2026.

India activities

No Indian digital cooperation programme in Togo was on the public record as at August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In January 2026 the head of the data-protection authority said its final objective is to require operators to store Togolese personal data in Togo. The authority already receives requests to authorise transfers of personal data abroad. No instrument fixing where state data is held or under whose law it is processed was in force as at September 2026.

In 2021 management of the state-owned Lomé Data Centre was given to Africa Data Centres for five years. The contract obliged it to transfer skills to Togolese staff. In December 2022 the Cabinet authorised its cession to a private operator. In July 2023 the minister said it was still looking for an investor-operator. In April 2026 the health insurer INAM's new system was hosted on the social security fund's own data centre, which the two present as keeping the data sovereign.

The national identity database is hosted on private cloud infrastructure provided by the IDEMIA/Atos consortium that built it. Its location and governing jurisdiction had not been published as at September 2026. The data terms of the IDEMIA/Atos contract, the NITEC licences and the SIN protocol with ST Digital had not been published either.

UNDP's September 2026 review lists Togo among six African countries where it deployed country-hosted GPU compute. In December 2025 Google.org gave US$2 million to the Togo Data Lab, run by the digital ministry with Berkeley's CEGA, to train 27 data specialists.
