Botswana: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-24

Edition of 2026-08-24  ·  106 systems and instruments tracked, 11 of them not held
↓ PDF
Compiled 2026-08-24 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 106 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 45 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-24, 18 did not, 32 carry no stated baseline, and 11 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 172 sources for this place in the window — 38 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 134 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The window's real movement is legislative. The Digital Services Act went from passed-but-not-in-force to in force on 21 November 2025; the Cybersecurity Act 2025 went from a Bill to made and gazetted, and is still not in force; and the Remote Service VAT Act took effect on 1 June 2026 at 14% on non-resident digital services. The data-protection commission moved from three contract officers with no premises to a commissioner in post enforcing against a government directorate.

Money moved the other way. Three digital development lines were cut for FY2026/27 — the communications ministry ICT project from P369,079,545 to P251,000,000, research and development from P477,403,718 to P213,700,000, and basic education computerisation from P270,000,000 to P253,000,000 — against FY2025/26 outturns of 38.7%, 9.0% and zero respectively.

Two operating positions worsened: identity-card production and the listed operator's profit, down 45% to P146.2m.

Data-centre capacity is where the year's building actually happened. The state facility went from built but neither certified nor launched to in operation, Tier III certified and carrier-neutral, with critical government services migrated onto it though no confirmation is on file that the mid-2026 initial phase completed. The commercial facility went from a first module under commissioning with generators en route and no revenue, to fully commissioned at proof-of-concept revenue, its on-site gas generation now supplemented by solar.

Two cross-border items advanced from nothing to paper. A memorandum covering cross-border fibre interconnection, capacity exchange and IP transit was signed with no build, cost or date; a solar data-centre campus at Palapye has had nothing on file since October 2025. The one physical delivery is the first phase of a regional fibre corridor, live at 800Gbps, whose live section is inside Zimbabwe.

Two measures did not move at all, and both matter. International internet traffic stands where it did in February 2025 at just over 100Gbps, the only national capacity figure the base holds; and the P100m gateway upgrade completed in April 2025 has had no successor investment.

Identity moved backwards on two measures and sideways on a third. Card replacement went from operating without reported delay to delays running since the start of 2026, with no backlog figure or restoration date. The electronic identification system went from prioritised for FY2025/26 as a public-private partnership and not progressed, to rescoped, with a biometric identity card itemised at P31,700,000 in FY2026/27. Behind both, the public key infrastructure entered the record for the first time as funded, begun, incomplete and non-operational.

Service delivery advanced on count and not on use: e-services went from 33 to 52, with no usage figure at either end, while the payroll portal was taken offline by the data-protection regulator — the only Botswana government system the base records as suspended that way.

Two things acquired a price for the first time. The voter register went from no priced line in any held document to P50,000,000 appropriated for FY2026/27 within the commission's P336,870,640 development budget, and a cross-border instant payment corridor went live inbound from South Africa and Zimbabwe.

Four positions held exactly still: the e-procurement system's beginning-of-2027 target, the deeds registry's substantially manual processing, the biometric grant cards at more than 75,000 beneficiaries, and BOTSAT-1's commercialisation, still stated as the next phase with no product, customer or revenue.

Three statutes changed state in the window. The Digital Services Act came into force with a compliance clock to November 2027; the Cybersecurity Act 2025 was assented and gazetted and has not commenced, the stated April 2026 date having passed; and the Electronic Payment Services (Amendment) Regulations 2026 came into operation, extending scope to money or value transfer services and making unlicensed operation punishable by P1,000,000 and five years. The Remote Service VAT Act moved from announced to in effect against a stated collection target of about P450m a year.

The institutions those statutes create did not follow. The National Cybersecurity Authority went from not existing to created in law with a board, a chief executive and a licensing power — and no organisation or vote in the FY2026/27 estimates; the Digital Services Authority is where it was, announced and contested.

The exception is the data-protection commission, which went from three contract officers with no premises, published address, website or deputy commissioner to a commissioner in post enforcing against non-bank financial institutions and then against a government directorate. Regionally, roaming went from Botswana-Namibia reduced rates only to six SADC states, with cuts of between 10% and 98.6%.

Nothing in Botswana's AI position moved, because there is nothing to move: no national policy, strategy or regulatory instrument is held at either end of the window. What entered the record is one commitment — the Judiciary's resolution to train judges in AI — and one external measure of adoption.

The sector's measurable positions both regressed or stalled. The listed operator's profit before tax fell 45% to P146.2m on revenue down 1.6% to P1.47bn, on company-reported figures. Research activity is unchanged for a second year: 45 projects carried forward, five completed and none added.

The one clear advance is diplomatic: Botswana went from not a signatory to the 68th signatory of the Artemis Accords.

The budget is where this window's clearest deltas sit, and four of five run down. Against FY2025/26 appropriations, FY2026/27 gives the communications ministry ICT project P251,000,000 rather than P369,079,545 — a third consecutive cut from P736.6m in FY2024/25 — research and development P213,700,000 rather than P477,403,718, and basic education computerisation P253,000,000 rather than P270,000,000 on a prior-year outturn of P0.
