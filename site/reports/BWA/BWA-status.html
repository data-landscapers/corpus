Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

The data-protection regulator became an enforcing body in this window. It suspended a government payroll portal by enforcement notice on 17 July 2026, the first published enforcement against a government body, having a year earlier run on three contract officers with no premises, address or website. It still has no budget organisation or line of its own.

The identity layer is the weakest part of the stack. The national public key infrastructure has P423m secured, control transferred to the intelligence directorate, no working system, no timeline and no accountable owner, which leaves Botswana with no certificate-backed identity verification and no recognised electronic-signature layer; identity-card production is degraded by too few staff and ageing equipment with frequent breakdowns.

The budget is the clearest part. Digital development lines were cut for FY2026/27 against FY2025/26 outturns already low — the communications ministry ICT project from P369,079,545 to P251,000,000, on a P142,712,599 outturn at 38.7% — and no Auditor General figure exists for any year. Eleven of the rows below are Not held.

The Digital Services Act came into force on 21 November 2025 with a compliance clock to November 2027; the Cybersecurity Act 2025, assented and gazetted in November, is not in force, a stated April 2026 commencement having passed with no notice. The authority it creates has a board, a chief executive, licensing power over cybersecurity providers and power to approve software before market placement — and no organisation or vote in the FY2026/27 estimates. A Digital Services Authority announced in the FY2026/27 ministry plan has no establishing instrument and was contested as duplicative in August 2025.

The Data Protection Act has been in force unchanged since 14 January 2025; enforcement moved — action against non-bank financial institutions in February 2026 and a government directorate in July.

The Remote Service VAT Act took effect on 1 June 2026, charging 14% on non-resident digital and remote services against a stated collection target of about P450m a year. Roaming cuts of between 10% and 98.6% now cover six SADC states. The ministry announced an ICT memorandum with Namibia for signature on 6 August 2026, adding digital transformation, emerging technologies and cybersecurity to roaming and cable-transit cooperation; no term or funding was published. No IPv6 transition plan and no open-data portal or policy are held.

The appropriations are the strongest evidence in this report and they read as retrenchment. For FY2026/27 the communications ministry ICT project falls to P251,000,000 from P369,079,545, a third consecutive cut from P736.6m in FY2024/25; basic education computerisation falls to P253,000,000 from P270,000,000, on a FY2025/26 outturn of P0; and the research and development project falls to P213,700,000 from P477,403,718, on a 9.0% outturn. The largest single recurrent digital line is essentially flat at P587,310,850, and the only line fully spent in FY2025/26 was higher education computerisation, which rises to P16,000,000. No audited outturn exists for any of them.

One procurement runs against that retrenchment: a P654.4m learning-technology contract awarded by direct procurement on 25 June 2026, its financial evaluation received, adjudicated and approved the same day, out of a vote that spent nothing the previous year.

Botswana's network position is stated by ministers and unverified elsewhere. The country has about 16,000 km of fibre laid, on a ministerial statement with no published register behind it, and the only national capacity figure in the base is just over 100Gbps of international traffic in February 2025, eighteen months old. Internet penetration is 2.09m users and 81.4% at end-2025 on a reference study, against a regulator figure of about 73% three weeks earlier; the two are unreconciled.

Rural reach is described without measurement: a satellite programme has 61 villages connected with 105 areas in process, and the Village Connectivity Project is running with no site count, target, budget or completion date.

Data-centre capacity is the one area with hard specification, and it is thin. The state's Digital Delta facility is Tier III certified and carrier-neutral; the commercial Kala centre is fully commissioned at proof-of-concept revenue, designed for up to 1MW, its growth gated on an upstream gas-gathering extension rather than on demand. A Palapye campus with 250MW of solar and 100-400MW of storage is a memorandum with no cost or timeline, and nothing on file since October 2025.

Security is legislated and unmanned: the 2018 cybercrime Act remains operative, its repeal enacted but not commenced, and no cybersecurity strategy document, date or version is on file.

Identity is where the base records the most damage. The public key infrastructure is funded, begun, incomplete and non-operational, the public accounts committee being told P423m is secured and control has passed to the intelligence directorate, with no working system and no accountable owner. The electronic identification system was rescoped from a public-private partnership to integrate digital identity, a national wallet and e-government services, with a biometric identity card itemised at P31,700,000 in FY2026/27. Card production is running with delays since the start of 2026, no backlog figure and no restoration date, while the amendment Act making a passport or driving licence valid during replacement is in force with service providers refusing even the replacement receipt.

Payments are legislated ahead of infrastructure. The Electronic Payment Services (Amendment) Regulations 2026 are in operation, extending scope to money or value transfer services, reaching beneficial owners and 10% shareholders on fit-and-proper, and making unlicensed operation punishable by P1,000,000 and five years. Against that regime, the national payment switch is funded in plan only, with no build, procurement, operator or date, and the Citizen Wallet has had no build, pilot, budget line or date since September 2025. One rail is live: an instant cross-border corridor inbound from South Africa and Zimbabwe, with no volume figure held.

Service delivery is counted in services rather than in use. The government platform records e-services increased from 33 to 52, with no usage figure at either end, and the payroll portal is offline with no reopening date. The deeds registry is substantially manual on a workload of 9,000 to 30,000 transactions a year since the 2017-18 land Acts, with no digitisation programme or budget line, in a country where about 70% of land is tribal land. Nothing is held on district or council digital systems.

Botswana holds no national AI policy, strategy or regulatory instrument at any date. The only state-side commitment in the base is the Chief Justice's statement that the Judiciary had resolved to train judges in AI, with no programme, budget or date attached. Adoption is measured only from outside, at 0 to 0.40 chatbot visits per user per month on a 0 to 2.6 scale.

The sector's largest listed operator is contracting: profit before tax fell 45% to P146.2m on revenue down 1.6% to P1.47bn for the year ended 31 March 2026, on company-reported figures. Research activity is flat: 45 projects carried forward, P9.5m spent in FY2025/26, five completed and none added.

External technology relationships are narrow. Botswana acceded to the Artemis Accords on 25 June 2026 as the 68th signatory; a vendor ICT academy operates across five tertiary institutions with no student numbers held; and the only Gulf item is a heads of agreement behind the Kala data centre whose counterparty is not named in the operator's most recent quarterly account. Nothing is held on Indian cooperation.

Eleven rows carry no reliable statement, and the pattern is that Botswana's instruments are named in speeches and absent from documents. Four are missing instruments: no cybersecurity strategy, named as the coming authority's implementing mandate; no AI policy or strategy at any date; no IPv6 transition plan; and no open-data portal or policy, the only open-data item in the base being a hackathon.

Three are missing measures of the state's own delivery: no official internet penetration figure, no digital-literacy rate, and no universal-fund annual allocation or school target. One is a whole layer of government: nothing on district or council digital systems.

The remaining three bear directly on the finance section above: no audited outturn for any digital budget line in any year, no named European Union instrument behind the assistance the minister described, and no India cooperation of any kind. Each row is in gaps.csv with what would settle it; none has yet been probed on a date.
