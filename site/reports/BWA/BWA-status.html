This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In the Ibrahim Index for 2023 Botswana scored 65.8 out of 100 for overall governance, fifth of 54 African states. It fell 3.6 points over 2014-2023 while the African average gained 1.0. Across the index's 96 indicators the country improved on 32 and declined on 55.

In September 2025 BOCRA published a Universal Access and Service Fund Framework keeping the fund under its governance, with oversight by an independent board of trustees. The fund is financed chiefly through a 1% levy on licensed operators above a turnover threshold (2025). It was established as a trust in April 2014 under the Communications Regulatory Authority Act. The ministry will consult stakeholders during 2026/27 on a revision of the 2007 national ICT policy.

The Universal Access and Service Fund ran for more than eleven years before it was given a published governance and financing framework.

A five-day data governance engagement from 7 to 11 September 2026 worked to translate the national data policy's principles into a national data strategy. It had continental and European support, and no draft or adoption date has been published.

Cabinet adopted SmartBots, the overarching digital transformation strategy, in February 2020, and it was implemented from October that year. In November 2020 the President told Parliament it was in the implementation stage. A World Bank diagnostic published in 2022 found SmartBots presented on a website as an action plan, with no strategy document put to the public. In September 2026 President Duma Boko told the UN Economic Commission for Africa that SmartBots would be formally launched in November 2026. It is meant to carry the country to a knowledge-based, high-income economy by 2036, and no budget or target for it has been published.

In August 2026 Botswana was still drafting a national AI policy with UNESCO's support, the policy having reached an advanced stage by December 2025. At the end of October 2026 the president of the Botswana Information Technology Society told a regional panel that the policy had been submitted to Parliament. No parliamentary record of the policy has been found.

Legislation and regulation

The Digital Services Bill and the Cybersecurity Bill were presented under a certificate of urgency on 14 August 2025, and Parliament passed both. The Digital Services Act, No. 22 of 2025 establishes a Digital Services Authority. It requires public bodies to keep an Enterprise Data Inventory and to publish information on data-asset use at least once a year. It also requires the ministry responsible for national registration to assign unique identifiers and to provide an online platform to verify them.

The Value Added Tax (Remote Services) Regulations, Statutory Instrument 74 of 2026, were made on 29 May 2026. They set registration, return, remittance and invoicing rules for suppliers of remote and digital services. The Value Added Tax Act, 2026, published on 1 July 2026, re-enacts the VAT code with rules for remote-services suppliers.

Statutory Instruments 149 and 150 of 2025, published on 4 December 2025, brought the Persons With Disability Act, 2024 partly into force. They define who qualifies as a person with a disability and commence Parts I to VI and IX and section 72. The Refugees (Recognition and Management) Act commenced on 1 November 2025. The Access to Information Act has still to be brought into force.

The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, 2014 gives electronic signatures legal effect and provides for electronic commerce. The National Registration (Amendment) Act 2025 lets a passport or driving licence stand as identification while a lost, damaged or expired Omang is replaced. In January 2026 the Chief Justice committed to training judges to handle AI-driven cases and deepfake evidence.

Most of the digital-governance statute book has been written since 2024, and it outweighs everything enacted between the mid-1990s and 2016.

Data protection

On 17 July 2026 the Information and Data Protection Commission ordered the immediate suspension of the government E-Payslip portal. It issued a determination and an enforcement notice against the Directorate of Public Service Management. The directorate accepted data-controller accountability and said the portal would stay offline until legal, technical and security requirements were met. It has published a privacy notice and completed a data-protection impact assessment for the portal. In February 2026 the Commission's new head had vowed a firmer stand against non-bank financial institutions that fail to comply with the Act.

The Data Protection Act No. 18 of 2024 was published on 29 October 2024 and came into force on 14 January 2025. It repealed the Data Protection Act No. 32 of 2018. The new Act binds the state and controllers established outside Botswana, which the 2018 text did not reach. It imposes duties covering children's data, impact assessments, breach notification, records of processing and cross-border transfers. A controller must notify the Commission of a breach without delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours. Section 74 subjects every transfer abroad to the Act's adequacy, safeguard and derogation rules. It also requires a copy of the transferred data to remain in Botswana for the period of processing.

In November 2025 the minister responsible for communications said he intends to amend the Act to require every business operating in Botswana to store its data in the country. M42's August 2026 announcement of a ten-year health partnership with Botswana states no data-residency condition, governance arrangement or ownership terms for national genomic data. In the Ibrahim Index for 2023 Botswana scored 84.6 out of 100 for digital freedom, fifth of 54 African states and essentially unchanged over the decade.

Regional collaboration

In July 2026 a SADC central bank subcommittee meeting at Ezulwini approved regional frameworks on cybersecurity, climate-related financial risk and cross-border crisis management. Botswana led one of the working groups that produced them. Under the committee's 2026-2030 strategy the subcommittee plans a regional financial stability index and crisis simulations to test cross-border resolution.

In August 2026 Botswana's data-governance experts took part in a UNESCO, Smart Africa and Zimbabwe workshop in Harare. It worked through UNESCO's data-governance toolkit and national self-assessments towards a roadmap for regional harmonisation.

The SADC Digital Transformation Strategy and Action Plan stands as a draft dated 12 October 2022. It is aligned to RISDP 2020-30 and the African Union's digital strategy to 2030. In April 2026 Southern African communications regulators adopted the Lilongwe Declaration on Collaborative Regulation at a CRASA-hosted summit. The declaration is voluntary and non-binding by its own terms. Regional anti-money-laundering follow-up reporting found in February 2026 that Botswana has not conducted a national assessment of the risks from virtual assets and emerging financial technologies.

South Africa named Kazungula and Mamuno among the one-stop border posts to accelerate under its 2026 SADC chairship. It pledged to measure progress partly in reduced border-crossing times. Namibia's MTC and BoFiNet completed a first direct fibre interconnection by August 2026. The two governments, their operators and their regulators signed an ICT-cooperation memorandum. In March 2026 a bank corridor began carrying SADC instant payments into Botswana.

Standards

BOCRA's Quality of Service and Quality of Experience Guidelines have been in effect since 1 April 2026. They set an enforceable service-quality regime for mobile operators, broadband providers including satellite services, and network facilities providers. In November 2026 Basebi Mosinyi of Botswana was elected to one of Africa's three seats on the ITU Radio Regulations Board for 2027-2030.

Sector guidelines issued in October 2019 require every regulated communications operator to develop and file business continuity and disaster recovery plans. The Standards Act established the Botswana Bureau of Standards as the national standards body. The bureau is a full member of ISO, an affiliate-plus member of the IEC and a member of the African standards organisation. The Digital Services Act, 2025 requires enterprise data inventories, information resource management and information security measures inside government.

In July 2026 the national public key infrastructure was incomplete and non-operational. The country has no domestic root of trust for certificate-backed identity verification. In May 2026 BOCRA hosted an IPv6 workshop with AFRINIC, ICT Dynamix and Mascom to train internet service provider and government engineers.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Public perception of freedom of speech in Botswana dropped 24.5 points over 2014-2023, to 58.4 out of 100 (2023). It was one of the country's ten most deteriorated governance indicators. Over the same decade media freedom rose 3.5 points to 60.9. Freedom of expression and belief held at 84.3 (2023).

The Constitution (Amendment) Bill No. 14 of 2025 was gazetted on 4 July 2025. The civil-society umbrella BOCONGO formally rejected it and demanded a people-driven Constitutional Review Act instead. Government has since consulted on a proposed Constitutional Court, casting trade unions as strategic partners. The minister addressed an interfaith consultative workshop on it on 29 January 2026.

The Local Government Act, 2012 establishes district, urban, village and ward Development Committees. The National Planning Act, 2024 places a statutory duty of liaison with civil society on the National Planning Commission. Parliament runs Botswana Speaks, with its uSpeak and Speak4Yourself feedback channels. The Budget Pitso consults stakeholders on the national budget before it goes to Parliament.

None of the country's documented channels for citizen participation publishes a figure for its reach or its uptake.

No body was identified as accountable for the stalled national public key infrastructure at a Public Accounts Committee hearing (July 2026). The responsible ministry's permanent secretary pointed the committee on to the Directorate of Intelligence and Security. Lawmakers' demands to know how the investment would be recovered, and who would be held responsible, went unanswered. The value of data is being argued in the national press through a "data colonialism" lens. Absa Botswana's managing director used the Directorate of Public Service Management's 2026 leadership conference to argue that government should simplify processes before digitising them. The same address urged government to stop asking citizens for information it already holds.

In October 2024 a charge against a publisher over a Facebook post was withdrawn and his challenge to section 59(1) of the Penal Code was referred to the High Court. The 2025 LONDA digital rights index scored Botswana 35 of 60, up from 28, and recorded no internet shutdowns.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Every digital project in the development half of Botswana's 2026/27 estimates is financed from the Domestic Development Fund, the volume's only external money sitting on HIV, livestock and water lines. The 2026/27 recurrent estimates do not print whose money pays for their digital lines (FY2026/27).

The digital development projects on record come to about P1.75 billion proposed for 2026/27, against about P1.55 billion appropriated for 2025/26 and P2.32 billion for 2024/25.

The communications ministry's own ICT project was reduced on revision from P736.6 million to P266.6 million in 2024/25. The ministry's Development Fund spending for that year came to P514.1 million. The Department of Shared Digital Services spent P487.1 million of the P587.3 million voted for 2024/25. It is proposed at P587.3 million again for 2026/27.

Under the Communications Regulatory Authority Act of 2012 the Universal Access and Service Fund draws on a levy of up to 1% of designated operators' gross turnover. It also takes the regulator's year-end surplus. In July 2026 it paid for a P1.45 million rural school ICT laboratory handed over at Makakung. In August 2026 BoFiNet, the state fibre wholesaler, reported P376 million of revenue and said it operates without government subvention.

MoUs and other agreements

During the President's state visit to China, Botswana signed a cooperation agreement on digital transformation with Huawei on 13 October 2026, with no sum stated. Orange Botswana and Huawei Technologies Botswana signed a memorandum on networks, AI, cloud and data infrastructure, reported in November 2026, with no project or value stated. In November 2026 BoFiNet and the Education Infrastructure and Management Company signed a memorandum on school fibre, campus networks, digital classrooms and local hosting. No school count, budget or timetable was stated.

In August 2026 Botswana signed an ICT-cooperation memorandum with Namibia at Kasane. It commits both communications ministries, their operators and their regulators to cross-border connectivity. In February 2026 MTC Namibia and BoFiNet had agreed fibre interconnection, IP transit and wholesale capacity exchange through the Buitepos-Ngoma corridor. The Ministry of Health's ten-year partnership with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services was signed by 22 August 2026. It is a pharmaceutical and medical-product supply agreement first, with digital health, genomics and precision medicine as avenues it opens.

On 17 December 2025 the government and India's KP Group signed a memorandum of understanding to develop renewable generation, storage and transmission. The Indian side put the investment to its stock exchange at INR 360 billion, for around 5GW of capacity (December 2025).

In all, 36 external commitments to Botswana's digital estate are on record, starting between 2015 and 2026 with windows running to 2030, and worth about USD 31 million together (November 2026). Three are active, one approved and one completed, and 31 carry no recorded status; the KP Group memorandum is an energy agreement and is not among them.

New investments

The World Bank's Renewable Energy Support and Access project, P181221, made its first disbursement on 23 December 2025. The project is worth about USD 88 million (2025). It disbursed three weeks after taking effect and some six months after signature on 3 June 2025. In January 2026 the World Bank approved a health emergency project for Botswana with a USD 13.1 million IBRD sub-component for information systems and digitalisation.

The European Union runs a EUR 1.5 million grant for digital transformation in Botswana from 2022 to 2030. EUR 1.4 million of it is a Cooperation Facility for the capacity of government institutions, through dialogue, expertise exchange and civil society engagement (2022). The source does not say how much of the grant is digital.

Eighteen financiers appear in the record of Botswana's digital estate (November 2026). All give through grants except the World Bank, which lends, and Orange Botswana, which invests its own money. Counted by number, Korean public bodies outside KOICA lead with six small grants, ahead of USAID with five.

The largest single commitment is the World Bank's USD 13 million health information-systems loan of 2026, followed by Orange Botswana's USD 5 million Tier III data centre (November 2026).

On 31 October 2025 Finance Minister Ndaba Gaolathe sought P8.83 billion for the Public Investment Programme under NDP 12. P2.07 billion of it would finish existing projects and P6.76 billion would fund new ones under the Botswana Economic Transformation Programme. Revenue collection takes the largest single institutional share. The national payment switch sits in a P486 million ICT allocation with an accounting and budgeting system upgrade and a fintech sandbox.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

In February 2026 the regulator approved Botswana Telecommunications Corporation's revised mobile data tariffs with immediate effect. The new one-day bundles start at P2 for 100MB. On 22 September 2025 the regulator announced the mobile operators' reductions under its own costing and pricing study. Orange Botswana's cuts followed the study's conclusion in May 2025.

4G covered 98.2% of the population in March 2024. Mobile subscriptions stood at 4,370,350 in September 2025, 6% up on the year. In 2024 Statistics Botswana put internet use at 57.5% of individuals and household internet access at 68.9%. It found 84.2% of people aged five and over using a mobile telephone in 2024.

In 2021 Botswana bought its international capacity through South African and Namibian transit routes, supplied by BoFiNet and private carriers. In September 2026 BoFiNet raised its international capacity through the Ramatlabama and Pioneer Gate crossings from 300 Gbit/s to 1 Tbit/s, under a long-term lease. The first direct fibre link between Namibia's MTC and BoFiNet, Gobabis–Buitepos–Charles Hill, was completed by August 2026. The Botswana Internet Exchange in Gaborone, established in 2005, is defunct. In October 2026 BoFiNet announced an internet exchange point at its data centre, with no member count given. BoFiNet's P43 million local-access programme, completed in August 2026, connected 191 customers in Moshupa, Ramotswa/Taung and Thamaga.

That programme worked out at roughly P225,000 for every customer connected.

In October 2026 the universal access fund had deployed 12 mobile broadband and voice sites with Orange Botswana along the 1,900km Trans-Kalahari Highway, for P34.5 million. Closing the remaining 4G gaps on the corridor is costed at about N$52 million across 13 sites (August 2026).

In August 2026 BTC targeted a commercial 5G launch in Gaborone by October 2026, behind Mascom and Orange, both live since 2022. No BTC 5G launch had been found as at November 2026. On 20 November 2026 BTC and Starlink announced a direct-to-mobile satellite service for standard 4G smartphones outside terrestrial coverage. Its commercial launch is set for early 2027, with no pricing announced.

Data Storage

Mascom has turned its Tier III-certified data centre from an internal facility into a commercial one (July 2026). It sells colocation, cloud, infrastructure-as-a-service and disaster recovery to outside customers. Orange Botswana opened a US$5.2 million data centre at the Botswana Digital and Innovation Hub in Gaborone in late 2023. The building had been in use since December 2019. It holds Tier III certification for design and facility, on its consultant's account.

BoFiNet's Digital Delta facility was Tier III design-certified by Uptime Institute in 2020. It took Tier III certification of constructed facility in October 2025. It was launched in Gaborone in November 2025 as a carrier-neutral site built by main contractor Zhong Gan. Critical public services had been migrated onto it by May 2026. The migration began in November 2025 on P100 million from the universal access fund. Government then intends a second accredited facility outside Gaborone for redundancy. Agencies bought recovery hosting from BoFiNet before it opened: the skills council extended its disaster-recovery hosting contract there in July 2025.

In the quarter to 30 June 2026 the Kala Data Centre completed its first full quarter of continuous operation, on revenues the company describes as proof-of-concept scale. It pairs gas-to-power generation at Tlou Energy's Lesedi project with UAE-based Kala Data FZCO. It is designed for up to 1 MW. It ran materially below that design in the year to June 2026 for lack of gas from the one connected well. A further campus near Palapye, to be solar-powered, rests on a memorandum of understanding signed by October 2025.

Botswana's operating data centre estate now runs on three distinct models: the state-owned Digital Delta, the commercial Mascom and Orange facilities, and the gas-anchored Kala.

In May 2026 Liquid Intelligent Technologies brought its cloud and cybersecurity portfolio into the market. There is no in-country AWS, Azure or Google region; the nearest availability zones are in South Africa. In March 2024 the communications minister told Parliament that a sovereign government cloud to be called GovCloud would be implemented; no later account of it has been found.

Energy

The power utility has asked the energy regulator for a 46% average tariff increase for 2026/27, which would take the domestic rate from P0.90 to P1.57 per kWh, citing unreliable local generation and heavy reliance on imported power (December 2025).

Coal accounted for over 97% of electricity generation in 2021. Access reached 76.6% of the population in 2024/25. Urban areas including Gaborone and Jwaneng were above 90%, while rural access has barely moved in a decade (2024/25). In 2023 firms recorded an average of 2.4 outages a month, with 64.2% affected. On the Ibrahim Index, Access to Energy rose 9.5 points over the decade to 2023, to 69.3 out of 100 and 10th of 54 African states.

In 2025 the National Energy Compact recorded no clear regulatory framework for mini-grids and no clarity on licensing or tariff setting. It recorded no defined grid-arrival policy, and a programme of four pilot projects for six villages still under procurement. It committed to enforceable minimum technical standards for mini-grids and solar home systems by December 2025.

The World Bank's renewable energy support and access project made its first disbursement in December 2025. Its implementation runs through the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Minerals and Energy, the Botswana Power Corporation and the energy regulator (February 2026). A December 2025 memorandum with India's KP Group envisages large-scale renewable generation, storage and transmission, with no financing agreement behind it. In the quarter to 30 June 2026 Tlou Energy was assessing a solar development alongside the Kala Data Centre.

Technical Capacity

In 2023 Botswana ranked 2nd of 54 African states for Effective Administration, at 77.7 out of 100. It had lost 9.1 points over the decade. Its Public Administration score fell over the same period.

A 2018 World Bank diagnostic found maintenance and technical support for the national identity system contracted largely to foreign vendors. It acknowledged vendor lock-in, deepened by scarce domestic ICT skills. Tertiary provision rests on a few institutions, chiefly the University of Botswana's computer science department and BIUST's computing and telecommunications degrees (2024). In 2025 a tracker of Botswana-based GitHub contributors with 50 or more followers listed only eight people.

In April 2025 BoFiNet completed network and international-gateway upgrades worth over P100 million through citizen-owned contractors. It framed the arrangement as skills transfer. In May 2026 the regulator hosted an IPv6 workshop with AFRINIC. The European Union's digital transformation grant for 2022–2030 is aimed at the capacity of government institutions, through dialogue and expertise exchange.

Cybersecurity

Botswana's national public key infrastructure is incomplete and non-operational with no timeline for resolution (July 2026). It has not delivered the identity verification and legally recognised electronic signatures it was designed to provide. More than P400 million was secured for the build before control of it was transferred from the communications ministry to the Directorate of Intelligence and Security. The transfer was made on grounds of sensitivity. The permanent secretary referred the Public Accounts Committee onward to that agency, which left no body identifiable as accountable for the project.

The Cybersecurity Act 2025 was drafted to implement the national cybersecurity strategy. The minister placed the Act's enactment in November 2025 within the strategy's implementation, and the authority's establishment in 2026/27. The Act requires owners of critical information infrastructure to report incidents within eight hours; the financial sector is among the critical sectors. It establishes a National Cybersecurity Authority tasked with protecting critical information infrastructure. Botswana sat in Tier 3, 'Establishing', for scores of 55 to 85, in the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024.

Senior police officers moved in February 2026 to strengthen enforcement of the Cybercrime Act against social-media misuse, citing a growing number of online-abuse cases. Police recorded 399 cyber threat cases in 2025, up 25 per cent from 320. Air Botswana activated incident response after a suspected ransomware attack on 3 November 2026 gave unauthorised access to systems holding employee information. The regulator disclosed a spike in botnet infections that had compromised several critical systems in 2024. No regular security audit, penetration test or vulnerability assessment of the identity system has been identified.

The national incident response team has run inside the communications regulator since 2020, funded from its own revenue. It is the primary contact for cybersecurity incidents for state bodies and the private sector (2025). The 2026 National Cyber Drill flagged AI, IoT and quantum computing as forward threats. A two-day crisis simulation was run with the United Kingdom government under its Africa Cyber Programme (April 2025). On 21 November 2026 the regulator said it is developing Child Online Protection Guidelines with children and other stakeholders.

DPI

Data Exchange

In March 2026 Phase I of the Labour Market Information System was fully operational. It had completed links to the National Identification System, the companies register and the 1Gov SMS Gateway. In March 2026 the Health Information Exchange's client registry and facilities registry were reported ready for deployment. The client registry draws a unique patient identifier from the Omang or passport.

An EU-funded Expertise France project with Estonia's ESTDEV runs from August 2024 to August 2027. It is working on the National Interoperability Framework and its standards, on core registries and on proof-of-concept solutions. The programme document set an interoperability framework for 2023 and core-registry compliance by 2027, with no structured implementation milestones published (2024).

A 2018 World Bank diagnostic found the National Identification System interfaced with births and deaths registration, the electoral register, the social benefits register, payroll and the transport registry. Section 20 of the National Registration Act confines that sharing to yes/no queries under departmental memoranda. Full data transfer is prohibited without ministerial authorisation.

In September 2026 the bankers' association launched a national electronic know-your-customer project letting banks verify customers against the national citizen database. No go-live date, data-protection arrangement or statutory basis has been found for it. The Digital Services Act, 2025 establishes a Digital Services Authority and places data inventory, information resource management and information security duties on government bodies.

In 2024 Botswana had no well-defined location addressing system. The addressing component of the LAPCAS land programme, run with Sweden's mapping authority, has never been implemented. Responsibility for addresses and road names rests with local government.

Digital Identity and CRVS

On 24 October 2026 the Minister of Labour and Home Affairs said nearly half of the 45,000 cards needed to clear the Omang backlog had been produced. He said the normal 14-day wait should return within two to three weeks. On 28 July 2026 the ministry had told Parliament that staff shortages and ageing, breakdown-prone production equipment had delayed Omang replacement since the start of the year. It answered with extended working hours, redeployment and supplier engagement.

Omang coverage exceeded 90% of the eligible adult population in November 2022. The unique number assigned at birth becomes the Omang number at 16, and death registrations update the population register automatically (2023). A 2018 diagnostic described the system as centralised and biometric, with fingerprint de-duplication since 1988. Its national repository connects in real time to district registration stations and hospital registration centres. Applications are made at registration offices, and cards are produced centrally in fourteen working days (2025).

The 2026/27 budget gives P105 million to Omang card production and registration system maintenance, and P42 million to computerisation projects including the Biometric Omang system. A Digital ID project to integrate digital identities, a national e-wallet and e-government services has replaced the earlier Electronic Identification System plan. The February 2026 budget accelerates the National Digital Identification System. In July 2026 the national public key infrastructure beneath it was incomplete and non-operational.

SIM-card registration was mandatory for all users in March 2024. The Omang is issued only to citizens. In 2025 the law made no general provision for refugees, asylum-seekers or stateless persons to open bank or digital payment accounts.

In August 2026 the permanent secretary for labour and home affairs said the Omang continues to be hacked. The ministry aims to introduce a computer-generated card with distinct features.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Substituted regulation 22 of Botswana's 2026 electronic payment rules requires an e-money issuer to ring-fence the interest on its settlement account. It may be withdrawn only to pay interest on customers' balances and for account maintenance. The previous rules had required that interest to go to account maintenance and the promotion of financial inclusion. The Bank may now direct how it is distributed, against a monthly return.

Statutory Instrument No. 46 of 2026, published on 2 April 2026, brings money or value transfer services inside the licensing perimeter. The Bank gains investigation powers over suspected unlicensed operation, and fit-and-proper testing extends to beneficial owners and 10 per cent shareholders (April 2026). Operating without a licence carries P1,000,000 and/or five years' imprisonment.

The amended regulations had been in force for three and a half months before the Bank of Botswana announced their implementation.

From 1 February 2026 a fourth daily settlement window runs across the interbank settlement and clearing systems. Every commercial bank must credit beneficiary accounts within 60 minutes of cleared settlement. The added 5pm window processed P66.92 billion of transactions from February to October 2026, the President told Parliament. The Botswana Interbank Settlement System settled P1.56 trillion in 2025, down 0.64%. In July 2025 there was still no national retail payments switch, and the National Payments Switch Act to create one had been drafted, with no timeline for tabling. By February 2026 market mapping for the switch was complete, and a second phase was under way through a special purpose vehicle set up by the Bank.

68.5% of adults reported making or receiving a digital payment in 2025, and mobile money carried P33.5 billion between March 2023 and March 2024. In May 2025 the SmartSwitch biometric card carried monthly food-grant allowances for more than 75,000 beneficiaries, usable at over 1,200 local shops. In 2022, 72 per cent of government grants were paid in cash and 20 per cent into bank accounts. In March 2026 First Capital Bank Botswana integrated the SADC TCIB platform for real-time inbound payments from South Africa and Zimbabwe.

Registries

The 2026/27 budget starts the first phase of a four-year programme to upgrade the Land Information System and Integrated GIS (February 2026). The programme is aimed explicitly at fragmented land data and weak inter-agency coordination. Digital public services for property transfer are almost entirely absent (2025).

The Companies and Intellectual Property Authority's Online Business Registration System has been fully online nationwide since 2019. Name reservation and incorporation are completed online, and routine approvals are typically issued within 24 hours. About P15 million went on a system the press called defective, and the same supplier was hired again for its upgrade. The upgraded system relaunched in March 2025 to disclose companies' beneficial owners.

Birth registration completeness stood above 92% in both 2022 and 2023. District and sub-district registry offices are equipped and register vital events directly into the national Births and Deaths Registration System (2023). The registration department describes the system as partially automated and real-time, with paper notification forms and printed certificates still part of the process. On 24 September 2026 the registration ministry launched a Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Strategic Implementation Plan for 2026-2031. Its central output is a digital registration system interoperable with other government systems, alongside a review of the law on the privacy and security of registration data. Botswana's civil registration score fell 12.5 points over 2014-2023 to 75.0 out of 100, seventh of 54 African states (2023).

In August 2026 the government told Parliament that its proposed electoral reforms include one-time voter registration and the Omang as the primary form of identification. They also give political parties access to the verified electronic voters' roll, and a technical team is to evaluate them before they are presented to Parliament. In October 2024 the Botswana Congress Party had gone to the High Court seeking to compel the Independent Electoral Commission to give it a digital voters roll. The court held that publishing the roll online is the commission's discretion, and 1,038,275 voters were registered for the October 2024 election.

Sectoral management information systems

The Ministry of Health signed a ten-year partnership with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services on or before 22 August 2026. Its stated substance is pharmaceutical and medical-product supply, with digital health and genomics as avenues it opens. The counterparty, the UAE artificial-intelligence and genomics company M42, presents the same agreement as its first entry into Africa. The announcement states no data-residency condition, no governance arrangement and no ownership terms for the national genomic data the partnership would generate.

Electronic health records were not interoperable within or between the public and private sectors in 2024. The standards and interoperability framework the National eHealth Strategy identified as necessary remained under development (2024). A 2024 study of health workers found a prevalent reliance on paper-based records. It found more than 18 systems for data collection and reporting that had proved hard to integrate. A centralised electronic medical record had been implemented in 22 health facilities in Ngami by March 2026. The remaining districts are scheduled for the 2026/27 financial year.

In February 2026 the revenue service was modernising Lekgetho Live, its tax administration system, which went live in May 2020. The work gives real-time visibility of taxpayer accounts and automated risk scoring. The revenue service takes P4.13 billion under the NDP 12 investment programme, the largest single institutional share, for electronic VAT invoicing, digital taxation of online services and border infrastructure (October 2025).

That is roughly eight and a half times the entire ICT envelope in the same programme, the envelope carrying the national payment switch.

The examinations council marked 32 subjects electronically in 2024, feeding its Malepa processing system. It deployed an in-house mark-capture application in 2024/25. The education ministry's EMIS unit runs the annual school census, and published the 2022 census results in June 2024. An EMIS system was being procured in 2020, and no account of its delivery has been found. In March 2026 the justice ministry told Parliament the courts' Case Management System was not operational because of system errors.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

On 17 July 2026 the Information and Data Protection Commission ordered the immediate suspension of the government E-Payslip portal. The directorate running it said the portal would stay offline until legal, technical and security requirements were met, giving no reopening date.

The 1Gov1Citizen portal carried 52 public services fully online in February 2026, with a further 179 in final integration testing. Government service portals authenticate citizens on the Omang number (2024). In March 2026, 194 e-services built in 2025 were being withheld because of technical problems with the government accounting and budgeting system. A redesigned payment platform was under procurement. At the first national procurement forum in March 2026 the government committed to launch an integrated electronic procurement platform by 2027.

The UN E-Government Development Index put Botswana at 0.6118, 112th in the world, in 2024. Its Ibrahim Index public administration score fell 8.9 points over 2014-2023 to 70.3 out of 100, still fifth of 54 African states. An Auditor General assessment reported in 2025 found project-management and oversight failures in the country's P500 million e-government programme. It found the programme delivering very few benefits at huge cost.

In May 2025 President Boko visited e-Estonia during a state visit, to examine how the Estonian e-governance model improved service delivery and transparency. In May 2026 Rwanda's Irembo discussed digital governance, sovereign infrastructure and citizen-centred delivery at a Rwanda-Botswana business forum.

On 29 September 2026, after a systems outage at the Department of Road Transport and Safety, the transport ministry extended expired permits by hand under an official stamp. Vehicles were cleared on manual receipts until the systems were restored, with no restoration date given.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In September 2026 the communications minister told Kanye District Council that the Digital Services Act requires all services to be offered online by November 2027. He said council services such as refuse collection and hawkers' licence applications should be offered online. He asked the council to budget for applications on the government cloud.

In 2024 street naming and addressing sat with local government, and Botswana had no well-defined location addressing system. The national addressing component of the LAPCAS land programme was never implemented.

The Local Government Act, 2012 gives citizens a statutory channel into local development planning through district, urban, village and ward Development Committees. The Digital Services Act, 2025 sets digital-service duties for every public body.

Gaborone City Council, a commercial bank and a local proptech firm launched the PayRates platform for paying property rates online, reported 14 June 2024.

Rural digital data capture

A 2024 study found health workers in Botswana's clinics relying predominantly on paper-based records. Primary schools complete the annual school census on questionnaires sent to them each year (2018). The returns are aggregated centrally by the ministry's information-system unit.

In May 2025 the President told senior police officers they spent 60 percent of their time on paperwork. He pressed for technology to free them for field work. The conference resolved to prioritise technology for predictive policing. The Universal Access and Service Fund and the SmartBots programme target broadband for more than 500 villages by 2030 (August 2026). District and sub-district offices register births and deaths directly into the national system (2023). They reach remote areas through health-facility desks and mobile outreach.

In 2025 rural electricity access remained far behind the towns. 4G covered nearly all the population in March 2024. In 2023 the Ibrahim Index gave Botswana no score for rural economy or any of its four indicators. It is the only one of the index's sixteen sub-categories left blank for the country.

Technology

AI

At the end of October 2026 the president of the Botswana Information Technology Society said the national AI policy had been submitted to Parliament. No parliamentary record of it has been found. The policy had reached an advanced stage by December 2025, and in August 2026 UNESCO was still working with Botswana and six other southern African states on drafting national AI policies. A UNESCO readiness assessment examined the country's legal, social, gender, economic and infrastructural readiness in 2025. On 3 November 2025 a minister told the UNESCO General Conference that Botswana had validated the AI Readiness Assessment Methodology. A 2023 civil-society digital-rights review had found AI regulation still evolving.

On 13 October 2026 Botswana signed a digital transformation agreement with Huawei that covers AI-powered public security for the police. The joint statement issued with China the same day commits Botswana to consider joining the World AI Cooperation Organization. In June 2025 the United States donated AI tuberculosis-detection software to the Ministry of Health. On 15 September 2026 OpenEvidence and Penn Medicine said an AI clinical decision-support platform would be extended to Botswana, with no timetable, site count or cost.

In August 2026 the Chinese ambassador published a signed newspaper article offering AI cooperation for economic diversification, government administration and talent training. It named no counterparty, sum or instrument, and ITWeb Africa reported the same offer. In April 2026 the communications minister made working visits to China and Austria seeking partnerships in AI, computing and data centres. No agreement or funding was reported from them.

In July 2026 the communications minister warned that AI chatbots had given harmful advice to children, including encouraging suicide. He called for stronger governance and developer accountability. In January 2026 the Chief Justice committed to training judges for AI-driven cases and deepfake evidence. The 2026 National Cyber Drill flagged AI among its forward threats.

ICT Industry

In July 2026 Mascom opened its Tier III data centre to outside customers. In October 2025 the state wholesaler BoFiNet was inviting businesses to sign up to its Block 8 Digital Delta facility. It launched the site in November 2025 as a Tier III certified carrier-neutral facility. In May 2026 Liquid Intelligent Technologies, part of Cassava, extended its cloud and cybersecurity portfolio into the country.

In August 2026 BTC, the state-owned incumbent, targeted October 2026 for a 5G launch, behind Mascom and Orange, both live since 2022. BoFiNet's revenue fell in the year to 31 March 2025, and the company changed chief executive in March 2026. Its international gateway upgrade, completed in April 2025, was routed through citizen-owned contractors and framed as skills transfer.

In 2023 economic diversification was Botswana's worst-ranked measure in the Ibrahim Index's Foundations for Economic Opportunity category, at 47.0 out of 100. That placed it 42nd of 54 African states, down 0.8 points over 2014-2023. In 2023 recurrent monthly power interruptions affected most firms.

Innovation ecosystem

As at 2025, the government-backed Botswana Innovation Hub ran a science and technology park in Gaborone, listed in the pan-African AfriLabs network. Beside it, the Institute of Entrepreneurial Development's Business Incubator runs Venture Creation Labs, including a women's co-working and incubation space. The hub's science and technology park was 43% occupied in March 2024.

The Botswana Tech Fund launched in April 2026, anchored by Pula Investments and advised by Launch Africa Ventures. It works with the Botswana Innovation Hub on deal sourcing and portfolio support, and its first phase is about a tenth of its total target. In October 2026 it named its first accelerator cohort of seven startups, three of them from Botswana. The Bank of Botswana began taking applications to its Regulatory Sandbox on 4 March 2026. It invited live testing of digital banking, inclusive payment and interoperability propositions.

Botswana ranked 87th of 139 economies in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2025. It was fourth in Sub-Saharan Africa and 27th of 36 upper-middle-income economies.

Capacity

Literacy

Some 59.2% of individuals, 1,071,314 people, had basic ICT or e-skills in 2024, against 41.4% a decade earlier, on Statistics Botswana's own household measure.

Education Completion scored 96.1 out of 100 in 2023, third of 54 African states. Education Quality rose 14.4 points over 2014-2023 to 73.2. Education Enrolment, the weakest of the education measures at 50.5 and fifteenth of 54, fell 3.4 points across the same decade. Nothing at all is reported for Human Resources in Education (2023).

In November 2023 a non-governmental organisation launched the regulator-funded eBotho Digital Literacy Roadshow. It ran four months of workshops at public libraries, with librarians trained as trainers. Financial-literacy training is bundled into the Absa and Yellow Light Taxi Association scheme alongside its payment terminals and credit offer (August 2026).

Training and skills

Statistics Botswana and the Botswana Public Service College signed a memorandum of understanding on 6 October 2026 to design and deliver statistical-literacy training for public servants. The college had already trained 25 Statistics Botswana employees in records management. The Ministry of Child Welfare and Basic Education awarded a P654.4 million contract for a STEAM e-learning, coding and robotics programme to Emeritus Training Academy Botswana. The award was made by direct procurement and approved on 25 June 2026.

In May 2026 BOCRA ran an IPv6 workshop with AFRINIC and ICT Dynamix. In June 2026 the ITU Development Bureau brought the regulators of Botswana, Malawi, Zambia and Zimbabwe together in Botswana for foundation GIS and data-management training. That training ran under its Africa Broadband Maps project. The regulators' training in 2026 was run with these outside partners.

The University of Botswana's Computer Science department was established in 1992 and enrols around 300 undergraduates and 20 postgraduates (2024). BIUST offers BSc degrees in Computer Science and Software Engineering and a BEng in Computer and Telecommunications Engineering (2024). Science, technology, engineering and mathematics took 30.1% of tertiary enrolment in 2024, down from 32.1% in 2023.

In January 2026 the Chief Justice committed to training judges for AI-driven cases and deepfake evidence. In August 2026 China's ambassador offered artificial-intelligence cooperation including talent training. The offer named no project, counterparty or sum.

Research institutions

In August 2026 the SADC Executive Secretary told the Council of Ministers in Durban that no member state meets the regional research-and-development target of 1% of GDP. None, Botswana included, meets the African Union's 1.5% target either (2026).

Postgraduate computing research runs through the University of Botswana's Computer Science department, whose programmes include an MSc and a PhD (2024). Botswana placed in the middle of WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2025.

Inclusion

Access to services

In 2023 Botswana's Equal Access to Public Services scored 79.5 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index, third of 54 African states. It had fallen 6.9 points over the decade. The wider Inclusion and Equality score fell 4.5 points to 64.8, eighth of 54. Citizens' sense of how easily an identity document can be obtained fell further over the same years. In July 2026 an MP told Parliament that residents presenting an Omang renewal receipt with their old card had been refused service.

Statutory Instruments 149 and 150 of 2025, gazetted on 4 December 2025, partly brought the Persons With Disability Act, 2024 into force. The Department of Gender Affairs serves as secretariat to the National Gender Commission. A joint government and United Nations programme on gender-based violence prevention and response was launched in June 2026. In March 2026 the 2026/27 budget estimates announced One-Stop Service Centres bringing medical, legal, counselling and psychosocial support for survivors into one place.

On World Refugee Day 2026 Botswana handed citizenship documents to 64 refugees. The handover met a Global Refugee Forum commitment covering protracted cases, unaccompanied minors and people at risk of statelessness. In 2025 the law made no general provision for refugees and stateless people to open bank or digital payment accounts.

In March 2026 flooding across central and north-western Botswana displaced families, closed a college and cut access routes. It prompted an IFRC emergency response operation. In August 2026 taxi operators the banks had not served could take card payments through an Absa partnership launched in Gaborone. The banking history built through it is the qualifying condition for the bank's business credit.

Digital divides

In 2026 cellular connections in Botswana comfortably exceeded the population while a substantial minority of adults held no bank account. Internet and computer access made the sharpest ten-year improvement of any Ibrahim Index indicator for Botswana to 2023. In September 2025 mobile tariffs were cut across the market on the regulator's costing study. BTC's bundles were restructured on the same basis in February 2026.

Protection against Discrimination rose 19.4 points over 2014-2023 to 19.4 out of 100, from a base of zero. At 21st of 54 African states it remains among Botswana's ten worst-scoring governance indicators (2023). In 2025 electricity reached rural communities far less than Gaborone or Jwaneng, a gap that had barely narrowed in a decade.

In 2024 basic ICT and e-skills reached a majority of individuals, up sharply on a decade earlier. In February 2025 primary schools such as Struizendam were gaining computer laboratories and tablets through the communications regulator's corporate social investment. A digital infrastructure gap placed Botswana among the continent's lower adopters of artificial intelligence in 2024.

Data

National statistics

On 6 October 2026 Statistics Botswana and the Botswana Public Service College signed a memorandum of understanding to run a statistical literacy programme for public servants across the National Statistical System. The memorandum also provides for common professional standards for statistical staff in the public service. In August 2026 the second Botswana Strategy for the Development of Statistics, covering 2026-2030 and aligned with National Development Plan 12, was launched. In November 2026 Statistics Botswana launched its own Corporate Strategic Plan 2026-2030. The plan covers data governance, modernised information systems and the adoption of AI and machine learning.

In 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators recorded three or more agricultural surveys in the past ten years, but only one household survey and one health survey. They recorded no labour force survey and no business survey. In March 2026 Statistics Botswana released preliminary labour-force results from the Botswana Multi Topic Household Survey, conducted from November 2024 to November 2025. They put unemployment at 21.0%, against 17.6% in 2015/16. The census programme was complete in 2023, with a population census, an agricultural census and a business census all held within the same ten years.

The country's Capacity of the Statistical System score rose 12.6 points over 2014-2023 to 67.8 out of 100. That placed it eighth of 54 African states, and made it one of the country's ten most improved governance indicators. The Statistics Act, 2009, in force since 1 December 2010, established the statistics office as a body corporate. In 2022/23 Statistics Botswana began revising the statistical legal framework to domesticate the African Charter on Statistics, which Botswana ratified in January 2021.

Statistics Botswana released 72 of the 102 outputs it planned for 2024/25. Most of the missing outputs were social indicators held back by late administrative data from ministries. Its development budget was P110.7 million, against P206.1 million sought.

As at January 2025 the National Statistical System was moving from paper-based to electronic collection and building interoperability across government. The work runs under the 'Power of Data' plan, which the country joined in September 2023.

Open data

The Access to Information Act (No. 16 of 2024) was enacted in 2024. It had not been brought into force in August 2026, its commencement still on notice.

In 2023 Disclosure of Public Records scored 34.3 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index, 24th of 54 African states. It had fallen 6.7 points over 2014-2023 and ranks among the country's ten worst-scoring indicators. Accessibility of Public Records stood at 45.0, 13th of 54 and down 2.4 points.

In 2024 Open Data Watch's Open Data Inventory scored the coverage and openness of official economic statistics between 4.5 and 7.5 out of 10. Trade scored highest at 7.5 and labour statistics lowest at 4.5. Social statistics ran from 3.5 to 6.5, with population and health-facility data highest and crime and food security lowest. Environmental statistics ran from 4.0 to 5.5.

The Digital Services Act, 2025 requires public bodies to keep an enterprise data inventory and to publish on their use of data assets at least once a year. The Digital Services Authority is to maintain a platform for sharing public data assets. In 2025 a general government open data portal was in place, refreshed annually with basic datasets. In October 2024 an opposition party went to the High Court to compel the electoral commission to hand over a digital voters roll. The rulebooks of the recognised clearance and settlement systems are open to inspection at the operator's head office in business hours.

Geospatial data

No use of satellite or Earth observation data by government had been recorded in Botswana as at September 2026.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In May 2026 signed commentary in Mmegi argued that the WTO framework cannot stop profits from data generated in the Global South concentrating in a few multinationals in the Global North.

By October 2025 US data centre operator ChillMine and Netherlands-based AAAS Energy had signed a memorandum for a solar-powered data centre campus near Palapye. The campus is intended to serve global hyperscalers and energy-intensive AI computing for southern Africa. No hyperscaler is a party to it.

China activities

On 13 October 2026, during President Duma Boko's state visit, the two states raised relations to a comprehensive strategic partnership. Their joint statement affirms digital sovereignty and each state's right to choose its own path on artificial intelligence. It takes Botswana into China's digital economy and green development trade-cooperation initiative, and commits it to consider joining the World AI Cooperation Organization. The same day Botswana signed a cooperation agreement on digital transformation with Huawei at the company's Beijing research institute, with no sum stated. It covers cloud computing to host government data, smart education, telemedicine and AI-powered public security for the police.

In August 2026 the Chinese embassy in Gaborone offered AI support, naming no Botswanan counterparty, sum or date. Ambassador Fan Yong set out the offer in a signed article in a Botswana newspaper on 20 August 2026, republished by FOCAC four days later. It proposed AI cooperation for economic diversification, government administration and talent training. It also proposed sharing Chinese e-government, digital-city and big-data public-health practice. It framed the offer through Chinese-led AI governance vehicles and against a "small yard, high fence" approach.

On 17 November 2026 Stanbic Bank Botswana became the first bank in the country to offer direct access to China's renminbi cross-border payment system. Between 9 and 19 April 2026 the communications minister made working visits to China and Austria seeking partnerships in AI, computing and data centres. No agreement, funding envelope or counterparty commitment was reported from either leg.

EU activities

Since August 2024 an EU-funded Expertise France project with Estonia's development agency ESTDEV has worked on the National Interoperability Framework, core registries and proof-of-concept solutions. The programme's Action Document set an interoperability framework target for 2023 and core-registry compliance by 2027. No implementation milestones between those two points had been published as at 2024.

The EU runs a EUR 1.5 million grant, about USD 1.6 million, for digital transformation in Botswana over 2022–2030. EUR 1.4 million of it is a Cooperation Facility for the capacity of government institutions (2022). Its stated means are dialogue, expertise exchange and civil society engagement. The source does not say how much of the grant is digital.

On 21 May 2025 President Duma Gideon Boko visited e-Estonia during a state visit. He met President Alar Karis, Estonian digital experts, technology firms and academic institutions. The visit examined how Estonia's e-governance model has changed public service delivery, transparency and citizen empowerment. The Palapye data centre campus memorandum of October 2025 was signed on the energy side by Netherlands-based AAAS Energy.

Gulf/UAE activities

M42 announced a ten-year health partnership with Botswana in August 2026. The announcement states no data-residency condition, governance arrangement or ownership terms for the national genomic data it would generate.

Botswana's Ministry of Health signed with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services on or before 22 August 2026. The ministry gives pharmaceutical and medical-product supply as its substance, with digital health, genomics and precision medicine as avenues it opens. On 26 August 2026 M42, the UAE artificial-intelligence and genomics health company, announced the partnership as its first entry into Africa.

In August 2026 M42 ran ADHDS, the operator of Abu Dhabi's Malaffi health-information exchange. Kareem Shahin, chief executive of M42's Digital Health Solutions platform, attended the signing. The health ministry named him as the chief executive representing ADHDS.

In September 2025 the Kala Data Centre paired gas-to-power generation at ASX- and BSE-listed Tlou Energy's Lesedi project in Botswana with UAE-based Kala Data FZCO.

India activities

India's KP Group is not among the 18 financiers on the record of Botswana's digital estate, because its memorandum is an energy agreement (November 2026).

On 17 December 2025 the Government of Botswana and India's KP Group announced a memorandum on large-scale renewable generation, storage and transmission capacity. The Indian side put the investment to its stock exchange at INR 360 billion (December 2025).

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 the communications minister told Kanye District Council that the whole government system is being migrated to a secure government cloud. That cloud sits at the Digital Delta Data Centre. At the facility's launch in November 2025 the government presented it as giving the country full sovereign control over its national data. In October 2026 Botswana signed a cooperation agreement with Huawei whose scope includes cloud computing to host government data. A joint statement with China the same day affirms digital sovereignty.

Section 74 of the Data Protection Act 2024 requires a copy of personal data transferred abroad to remain in Botswana for the period of processing. In November 2025 the minister said he will amend the Act to compel every business operating in Botswana to store its data in the country.

A 2018 World Bank diagnostic found maintenance and technical support for the national identity system contracted largely to foreign vendors, with vendor lock-in acknowledged. In December 2024 a government team working with Oracle's experts restored the government accounting and budgeting system. In July 2026 the national public key infrastructure sat with the intelligence service, incomplete and not operating. Nothing had been found as at September 2026 on who holds the identity system's source code and operating skills. Nor had anything been found on whether the state can exit its support contracts.

The terms on which the Ministry of Health's ten-year partnership with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services governs data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at September 2026.

In August 2026 Botswana's data-governance experts joined the UNESCO and Smart Africa workshop in Harare towards harmonised data rules across southern Africa.
