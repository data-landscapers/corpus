Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Cape Verde's digital state is delivering systems while its published budget for them falls away. The unified public-services portal Gov.cv went live on 24 February 2026, consolidating services that on the Prime Minister's own account had not communicated with each other (launch account), and the state began producing identity cards, passports and residence permits on national territory on 18 May 2026 (producer's account). Against that, the digital ministry's appropriation fell 87.5% across two budgets, from CVE 1,390,965,632 for FY2024 to CVE 174,200,533 for FY2026, with capital at a printed zero for a second year (budget law).

The gap between the two is filled outside the vote. At 31 March 2026 the ministry's reprogrammed budget stood at CVE 1,614,624,282, 9.3 times the figure Parliament passed (quarterly account). The published appropriation and the money actually being spent have almost no relationship to each other, and no audit instrument covers either: the Court of Auditors' published opinion series ends at FY2023. The plan of record has also lapsed, the digital governance action plan having run to end-2025 with no successor held (plan presentation).

The regulator's own market series stops before the window opens. At 30 June 2025 Cape Verde held 484,929 internet subscriptions, 94 accesses per 100 inhabitants and 592,769 active SIM cards, with the incumbent on 72.3% of the internet market and the satellite entrant on 0.2% nine months after starting (regulator indicators). No quarter after that is held, so the National 5G Strategy's target of 90% internet penetration by 2026 cannot be tested against any measurement.

That strategy was approved by resolution on 20 February 2026, but no spectrum assignment, licence award or network build is on file (strategy presentation). The state separately approved a guarantee of up to EUR 37 million to the incumbent operator for inter-island cable renewal and international connectivity; the competitor objected publicly, demanding neutrality and separation of strategic infrastructure management from commercial service provision (resolution account).

Cybersecurity is the section's clearest movement and its clearest measure of prior neglect. A national security operations centre and response team began operating inside the state network on 30 October 2025, and the FY2026 budget then inscribed the first cybersecurity budget unit in the Cape Verdean budget at CVE 20,000,000 — 0.02% of state expenditure, five years after the decree that created the response team (budget law). No incident count, staffing figure or service catalogue has been published since.

Cape Verde's public-service layer consolidated in the window. Gov.cv opened on 24 February 2026 as a single web and app gateway covering finance, civil registration, identity, tax, higher education, transport and social services, with departmental portals to migrate into it; no count of migrated services has been published (launch account). The criminal-process component of the justice information system was handed from the state IT agency to the justice ministry on 17 March 2026, in operation in every comarca in the country (handover account).

Identity is where the state moved furthest. Production of the identity card, electronic passport and residence permit had been contracted to Portugal, with over CVE 176 million ratified after the services were supplied; since 18 May 2026 the documents are produced domestically (producer's account). No enrolment count, activation count or coverage rate is held for either the card or its mobile credential, from any date.

What is missing is the layer beneath. A diploma on interoperability standards for public-administration systems and a diploma regulating data governance were both named as 2024 legislative deliverables and neither is adopted two years past that year (committee statement) — while interoperability is asserted as a working property of the new portal, the company registry and the civil-registration to identity linkage. An open-data portal announced in the same statement is likewise not held, and the household income and expenditure survey fielded in December 2022 has published no definitive results.

The digital governance action plan ran 2021 to 2025; no successor plan, extension or evaluation is held, so the 2026 statements about a digital nation run without a current published action plan (plan presentation). The Minister of Economy, Trade, Industry and Digital Transition convened a strategic consultation in Praia on 6 August 2026 to feed a national diagnosis and the priorities of a future digital transformation agenda (ministry statement). No draft, title or adoption date is held.

A first dedicated regulatory framework for satellite communications is under consultation. ARME opened submissions to 31 August 2026 on licensing, spectrum, frequency coordination, user protection, network security and emergency communications for satellite operators, a direct regulatory response to Starlink's December 2024 entry to the archipelago and distinct from the general authorisation regime under which Starlink was licensed; no draft text or adoption date is published.

Data protection rests on a 2001 statute closely mirroring the European model, last amended in 2021 to require designation of data-protection officers — an obligation the supervisory authority's president said in February 2025 several institutions had not met (interview). On the face of its own site the authority has published no genuine activity report since 2016, and its FY2025 appropriation of CVE 36,313,115 — roughly a dozen maximum penalties — sits inside the National Assembly's private budget rather than any ministerial vote. Its FY2026 appropriation is unestablished: the budget volume contains no occurrence of the authority in 266 pages.

The electronic procurement platform was re-let at CVE 70,000,000 over four years on terms of perpetual licensing, deployment on national infrastructure and full control of source code, and made mandatory for 2026 (clarification note).

The Prime Minister told Parliament on 31 July 2026 that the statistics institute produced falsified data and ordered an independent evaluation; the institute's board rejected the allegations on 3 August and offered to host any external evaluation (both accounts). The two accounts have not been reconciled.

Cape Verde's artificial intelligence and data strategies were both at kick-off inside the window. Development of a National Artificial Intelligence Strategy and a Data Strategy for Public Administration was launched at a meeting on 28 April 2026 attended by senior officials, external consultants and European Union representatives, with the stated goal of moving government off siloed systems; no draft, adoption date or lead institution is published (kick-off account).

The technology park is the sector's one substantial physical asset. Both campuses were inaugurated in May 2025 as a EUR 51.85 million project carrying EUR 45.5 million of African Development Bank financing, hosting 23 companies from seven countries and 311 professionals at full occupancy of 52 offices (financier's release). No occupancy, tenancy or employment figure dated inside the window is held, so the park's current position rests on a fifteen-month-old measurement. Its second phase went to open international tender on 29 July 2026, with the contract value unpublished.

Several stated instruments cannot be inspected. A startup law with tax benefits and a digital-nomad visa are stated to be in force on a minister's summary in trade press, with no instrument number, adoption date or take-up figure held for either. The government's target of raising the digital economy's share of gross domestic product to 25% is stated against a current share given as about 7% in one account and about 5% in another, neither sourced to national accounts.

Two fee-financed ceilings sit inside the digital-transformation budget without matching expenditure records. The universal service fund has carried CVE 122,073,633 unchanged to the escudo across FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, financed by operator contributions and the radio-spectrum fee and appearing in no organic, functional or economic classification (budget law); the civil-identification earmark has likewise carried CVE 306,516,802 unchanged across the same three budgets. No expenditure, project or beneficiary record exists for the universal service fund from any date.

Execution tells a starker story than appropriation. The digital ministry executed 33.0% of its reprogrammed budget in FY2024 and 37.9% in FY2025 against state-wide rates near 80%, and 10.1% at the first quarter of 2026 against 18.6% state-wide (quarterly account); its FY2024 investment line executed a printed zero. The state IT agency's own forecast service revenue of CVE 1,094,297,000 for 2026 is 6.3 times the ministry's entire vote, and no agency accounts have been published since the FY2024 volume.

The absences here are administrative rather than topical. The most consequential is that no audit instrument covers FY2024 or FY2025: the Court of Auditors' published opinion series ends at the FY2023 account, so the finest published record of Cape Verdean digital spending is the executive's own quarterly provisional account (quarterly account). The rectificative budget approved on 31 July 2026 had not been gazetted at 4 August, and its organic table is what would settle whether the reprogrammed digital budget is ratified or reversed.

Three subjects hold nothing at all for Cape Verde: technical capacity, satellite and Earth observation, and rural or inter-island last-mile service delivery.

The regulator is the other structural silence: no budget or activity report for it is held for any year.
