Of 62 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 33 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-10, 17 did not, 3 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 47 sources for this place in the window — 15 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-02), 32 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Niger acquired an identity architecture and a fibre backbone in twelve months, and did not acquire the institutions meant to secure them. Four instruments of April and May 2026 created three registers and an agency, enrolment for the identity card began in April and for the passport in July, and 1,031 km of fibre and a Tier III data centre were provisionally accepted in November 2025.

Over the same period the public key infrastructure, cybersecurity centre and forensics laboratory blueprinted in July 2025 did not move, the national cybersecurity strategy stayed in elaboration, the national digital-development policy stayed unadopted, and the data-protection authority published nothing about what it does.

The window's physical delivery is one event. Provisional acceptance of 1,031 km of Trans-Sahara fibre on five cross-border axes and of a Tier III data centre at Niamey came in November 2025, two months after the 30 September 2025 delivery date the minister had given. A national internet exchange point opened in August 2025.

Against that, three institutions enumerated by the same critical-infrastructure committee in July 2025 — a national public key infrastructure, a cybersecurity centre with a security operations centre and a CERT, and a digital forensics laboratory — are in exactly the position they were in at the window's start: planned, with nothing on record built. The national cybersecurity strategy is still in elaboration a year after the symposium that opened it.

Satellite access changed shape rather than scale: Airtel Niger commercialised a telco-integrated service in January 2026, moving the customer relationship to the operator, while direct retail continued unchanged.

The regulator gained instruments and used them. A continuous monitoring platform was inaugurated in January 2026, and by July it had produced the first published verdict on network quality: deficient at all four operators, with a verification campaign to 28 August 2026.

Identity is where almost all the movement sits, and it happened in five months. Nothing existed at the window's start; by its end the AES biometric identity card was in mass enrolment across Niamey's 20 commissariats, mandatory at 15 and over and capturing fingerprint, face, voice and iris, and the AES passport had opened enrolment in five ICAO-specification categories. Behind them, the ordonnance and décrets of April and May 2026 created a civil register, a nationality register, a population register and the agency to run it, and the World Bank programme that had been running alongside the sovereignty track was restructured to carry them.

The declaration rate the whole design rests on did not move: 66% of births nationally. The one regional figure published in the window runs the other way — Diffa reports timely declaration up from 56% in 2022 to 88.04% in 2025 and 173,109 certificates issued over three years, on trained village chiefs, mobile teams and four computerised communes.

Administrative systems advanced onto state platforms. The integrated human resources and payroll system produced its first figures — 16,500 files digitised and 23,000 updated — against about 200,000 public agents, and student grants are to follow onto the Treasury's application. Health acquired a validated master plan with 27 projects where it had none, and the tax administration recovered its systems and convened on what comes next.

Payments interoperated for the first time: bank-to-wallet transfers in June 2026, and kiosks taking the identity-card fee at enrolment sites from April — the same operator at both ends.

Nothing in the legal frame changed. Loi n°2022-59 and the High Authority under it stood at the start of the window and stand at the end, with no caseload, decision or sanction figure published in between — across the twelve months in which the state enrolled its population's biometrics. The national digital-development policy remains drafted and unadopted.

What moved is enforcement. Unauthorised resale of satellite connectivity was met with a formal notice in September 2025 under Loi 2018-45, and network quality with mises en demeure on all four operators in June 2026. Both rest on a standard the regulator does not publish.

Externally, two bilateral relationships were opened where none was held: telecommunications cooperation with Algeria in March 2026 and an identity-system mission to Burkina Faso in July 2026. The EU country programme had already ended before the window opened.
