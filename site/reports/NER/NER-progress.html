This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A national digital-development policy for 2026-2035 went to national validation in March 2026 on three strategic axes, and has not been formally adopted.
Full recordA redrafting committee was installed in July 2025 with one month to update the draft (committee).

The validation workshop of March 2026 covered governance and security of the digital sector, infrastructure and services, and skills and innovation, targeting 2035 and aiming to position the country as a regional fibre-interconnection hub (validation, interview).

Full recordThe ministry confirmed in May 2026 that the 2021-2030 national digital policy remains the operative reference framework and that the new household survey of access to information technology will be used to update it.

The successor draft was handed to the minister on 13 October 2025 after roughly two months of drafting, with implementation to follow only after adoption, and went to a national validation workshop in March 2026 on three strategic axes.

The successor has not been formally adopted, so the base holds two instruments — one operative and dated 2021, one drafted and unadopted.

Full recordSeveral fibre sections of the trans-Saharan backbone were provisionally received on 14 November 2025, presented as securing international internet access and interconnecting Niger with Algeria, Chad, Nigeria and Benin.

The steering committee session of 13 March 2026 then adopted a 2026-2027 work programme and budget of over 30 billion FCFA, of which 16 billion is bank credit, 12.76 billion grant and 2.17 billion national counterpart.

Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A feasibility study for the national data centre was completed with tender documents drafted, following a procurement notice in February 2026.
Full recordThe information society agency issued a tender on 4 February 2026 under the Villages Intelligents project, its first lot dedicated to data centre and network infrastructure, with bid opening set for 25 March 2026.

The feasibility study reported in June 2026 covers sizing, detailed design, site selection to Tier III+ standard, tender-document drafting and a cloud and storage business plan, concluding that the government has what it needs to launch the tender.

There is no published cloud or data-storage strategy instrument: what the base holds is a facility programme and a business plan, not a policy on where public data is held or under what conditions.

Nothing in the window publishes the framework's content, a technical specification, a compliance duty or a roadmap for applying it, and no register of systems assessed against it exists in the base.

Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations The data protection authority was abolished in June 2026, removing the body that held the declaration duty for artificial-intelligence operators.
Full recordThe government abolished the data protection authority by decree and transferred its prerogatives to the ministries of justice and of the interior as part of a wider administrative reorganisation; no primary decree text was located.

The provisions themselves stand. The consolidated text of the 2022 data protection law defines artificial intelligence at article 57, requires developers, manufacturers and service providers operating from Niger to declare to the authority, and restricts fully automated decisions and profiling at article 52.

What has gone is the body those duties were owed to. No transitional arrangement naming which ministry now receives a declaration is on file, and the law names neither deepfakes nor algorithmic disinformation.

Full recordThe arrêté places commercial Wi-Fi zone provision under a declaration regime with the regulator, caps each provider at five points of sale, bans signal amplifiers and masts, sets an annual fee schedule by geographic zone and gives three months to comply.

The framing statute is the 2019 law on electronic transactions, which defines the rights and obligations governing electronic transactions in Niger; its full text is held only as an official landing page, the attachment having resisted capture.

No consumer-protection rules specific to online sale, and no volume figures for electronic commerce, are on file.
