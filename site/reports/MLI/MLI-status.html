Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Mali's digital programme is a sovereignty programme, and the objects it has built are those that keep data and identity inside the state or inside the three-country Sahel confederation. A Tier III National Data Centre opened in Bamako in January 2026; the passport is issued to AES specification by a Chinese supplier that replaced a French one; the state took majority control of SOTELMA in June 2026.

The regulatory layer is older than the build. The data-protection authority has enforced under a 2013 statute since before this record opens, and opened its second 2026 session with more than 1,000 files. In the same period the cybercrime pole sentenced a journalist to 12 months with six suspended for saying in public that cybercrime law was being used against journalists.

What is thin is money reaching users. The universal access fund cut its 2026 budget by 32% in July after spending 8% of it in six months, and digital payment at civil registry centres raised about FCFA 200 million across 23 months.

A national AI strategy, a data-management law and a national public key infrastructure exist only as recommendations made at the end of July 2026.

The Tier III National Data Centre, inaugurated on 31 January 2026 with a stated availability of 99.982%, is the centrepiece, and no rack, floor or power capacity figure has been published for it. The institutional cyber stack was assembled around it in the same twelve months: a National Cybersecurity Strategy 2026-2030 adopted in December 2025, ANSSI created in June 2026, and the cybercrime pole's criminal chamber hearing its first four cases in May 2026.

Access is carried by two operators. Orange Mali is building 300 4G towers, half of them rural, and fibre to about 300,000 households under a EUR 80m IFC-led loan, with diesel-to-solar site conversion in scope; Moov Africa Malitel doubled fibre speeds to up to 100 Mbps across six cities in June 2026 and announced ADSL retirement for end-2026. Satellite 3G and 4G reaches about 360,000 remote residents.

International routing improved without any Malian asset changing hands: CSquared added 2Africa West capacity to the backbone that reaches inland to Mali, giving an independent subsea route behind the Abidjan, Accra and Lagos landing concentration.

Two positions are recommendations rather than systems: a law making SMTD-SA the sole national operator of transport infrastructure, and universal connectivity by 2030. A named-analyst investigation carried by Maliweb reports AFRIBONE as the sole surviving independent provider, buying wholesale IP transit at near-retail prices. Starlink kit imports remain restricted: after an October 2024 lifting, AMRTP restated the ban on unauthorised equipment in March 2026 and announced seizure and dismantling operations in June 2026.

Identity runs through the confederation. The E-AES passport is issued in five categories to ICAO specification, supplied by Emptech since October 2025; the CIB-AES card was piloted in Bamako in June 2026 for Burkinabe residents, on specifications the three AES security ministers validated in November 2024. Domestically the NINA number authenticates service applications, while birth registration stands at 89.5% across 747 functional centres and death registration at about 20%.

The base layer is older than any of that. A draft ordinance adopted on 24 July 2026 formalises the cahier de recensement, the hand-kept village register from which family record books are issued, replacing a 2006 statute — so civil registration still begins on paper.

Service platforms are numerous and mostly unmeasured. Online criminal-record and nationality certificates went live in February 2026 with an authenticity check against forgery; Sigui-Dolo handles driving licences and vehicle registration and is linked to TresorPay and the customs system; OpenClinic and SIGIP remain in build; a national digital health portal launched in July 2026, and one Bamako hospital runs an end-to-end circuit on a unique patient identifier.

Payments have two state flows and one market. Pension payment has a completed bank-channel pilot; digital payment at civil registry centres raised about FCFA 200 million between July 2024 and May 2026, and every collectivite has been ordered to report within 30 days on why. In the market a second telco-led prepaid card launched in April 2026. Against that, the BCEAO records Mali's mobile-money distributor numbers down 46.34% in 2024, the steepest fall in the Union, and its share of Union transaction value at 11.7%, down from 12.1%.

Government's own systems are being wired together: 13 services on the AGETIC intranet and 13,441 domain names under .ml, an integrated human-resources system in build, and a monitoring system that published a completion rate of 43.43% for the second half of 2025. A national public key infrastructure is recommended and not built. INSTAT runs its own microdata catalogue — a NADA archive of 107 studies led by the 2022 census (August 2026).

The authority here is older than the systems it regulates. Loi n2013-015 has been in force since May 2013 and the APDP enforces under it: about 2,000 declaratory files reviewed in 2025, with one 5m FCFA fine and more than 100 formal notices, a regional antenna at Segou, and more than 1,000 files before a ten-day session opened on 3 August 2026. No figures for sanctions issued or files cleared are published, so the docket is a workload count.

Platform regulation took the opposite route. On 29 July 2026 the justice minister instructed prosecutors to act against content networks using penal and cybercrime provisions already on the books — no new statute, no notification to platforms, and enforcement duties pushed onto parents, landlords and hotel keepers. Five days later the cybercrime pole sentenced Chahana Takiou, publication director of 22 septembre, to 12 months with six suspended for arguing that journalists should be tried under press-offence law rather than cybercrime law. Neither the judgment nor the charging instrument is held.

Three instruments exist only as recommendations from the Semaine du Numerique of 1 August 2026: a data-management law, a startups law and a Conseil national du Numerique. Externally, Mali was re-elected to the African Telecommunications Union board for 2027-2031 and spoke for the three AES states at the UEMOA digital economy salon. The European Commission states the EUR 151m committed for 2021-2024 has no 2025-2027 successor, funding running instead through a shared central-Sahel allocation with no digital activity named. An academic evaluation finds no single access-to-information law, the right split across Loi 98-012, Loi 2013-015 and Loi 2017-062.

Mali has no national AI strategy. One was recommended on 1 August 2026, and the only AI-governance instrument the country has joined is the Francophone West African guidelines adopted with five other states in July 2026. The Centre d'intelligence artificielle et de robotique has one demonstration on record, of a locally built intrusion-detection technology; the other AI system on the ledger is an operator's customer assistant launched in June 2026.

The sector's largest movement was ownership rather than technology. The state raised its stake in SOTELMA from 49% to 56% for FCFA 160bn in June 2026, cutting Maroc Telecom to a minority 17 years after privatisation, and the passport supplier changed from Idemia of France to Emptech of China in October 2025, which officials described as regained sovereignty and local data management. No United States or India bilateral digital instrument is held. UAE operator e& committed $6bn to the ITU's Partner2Connect coalition across 16 markets including Mali and Niger, in March 2024, undivided, with no Mali-specific figure.

Research institutions

System or instrument Status ↗ source Milestone
Higher-education and research digital capacity In development Universite Numerique du Mali strategic framework and action plan validated 2024-05-07

AGEFAU, the universal access fund, signed two four-year conventions with the education and employment ministries, committing it to connect every secondary, technical and vocational school by end-2027. The fund sits under direct Primature tutelle, an arrangement a named-analyst investigation dates to 2012 and contrasts with the regulator-attached funds of neighbouring states.

Skills programmes are being opened faster than they are being measured. Recruitment for the first cohort of a 15,000-youth programme with the Association Kabakoo opened on 1 August 2026; an operator reports 350 graduates placed; and eight teacher-training institutes received FCFA 88,740,927 of Japan-funded equipment with no maintenance, connectivity or software provision stated. The IFC-led loan carries a 70%-by-2032 women's digital-training target with no interim figure. A 2009 ministry policy document recorded no long-term national research strategic plan, against a 1:100 teacher-student ratio at Bamako. UNESCO backed validation of a strategic framework for the planned Université Numérique du Mali in May 2024, citing 132,186 students across 16 institutions and an 81:1 ratio against its own 25:1 norm; no creation decree has followed.

The universal access fund is where Mali's inclusion money sits and where the record is sharpest. AGEFAU cut its 2026 budget by 32%, from FCFA 41,151,471,000 to FCFA 27,886,688,000, on 23 July 2026, after spending 8% of the annual budget in the first half against receipts at 56% of forecast. In the same week it signed two four-year conventions with the education and employment ministries, committing cumulative education spend under the conventions to above FCFA 17bn.
