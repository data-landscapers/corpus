Mali: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-24

Edition of 2026-08-24  ·  84 systems and instruments tracked, 4 of them not held
↓ PDF
Compiled 2026-08-24 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 84 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 56 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-24, 16 did not, 8 carry no stated baseline, and 4 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 74 sources for this place in the window — 15 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-02), 59 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Twelve months built Mali's sovereign infrastructure and its cyber institutions, and left its financing and its rights position where they were or worse. A Tier III National Data Centre opened, a cybersecurity strategy was adopted, ANSSI was created, a cybercrime criminal chamber began hearing cases, the passport supplier changed from France to China, and the state retook majority control of SOTELMA.

Over the same period the universal access fund cut its 2026 budget by 32%, the mobile-money distributor network fell 46.34%, and a journalist was sentenced before the cybercrime pole. The institutions were built faster than anything they were meant to deliver to users.

The whole cyber institutional stack was created inside the window: the National Cybersecurity Strategy 2026-2030 in December 2025, the criminal chamber of the cybercrime pole in May 2026, and ANSSI in June 2026 to implement the first. So was the Tier III National Data Centre, inaugurated on 31 January 2026. None of the four has published a budget, a staffing figure or a capacity figure.

Access moved on operator investment rather than public programme: an EUR 80m IFC-led loan to Orange Mali in November 2025 for 300 4G towers and fibre to about 300,000 households, Moov's fibre speeds doubled in June 2026, and ADSL retirement announced in July 2026. Route diversity improved through a third party's subsea capacity rather than a Malian asset.

The positions that did not move are the ones users would notice. Satellite coverage of remote regions carries the same figure it did a year ago. A universal connectivity target of 2030 and a law consolidating transport infrastructure in SMTD-SA were both recommended in the window's last two weeks and neither has a text. Whether Starlink kits may be imported has been unestablished throughout.

Identity moved twice, in the same direction. The AES common passport of January 2025 became the E-AES passport in five ICAO-specification categories, with Emptech of China replacing Idemia of France as supplier in October 2025, and the CIB-AES card was piloted in Bamako in June 2026 for another confederation state's residents. Underneath it, the village register from which family record books are issued was given a new legal form in July 2026 and remains a hand-kept ledger. Birth registration at 89.5% and death registration at about 20% are the same figures the base held a year ago.

Service platforms multiplied: online criminal-record and nationality certificates in February 2026, Sigui-Dolo, OpenClinic and SIGIP presented in September 2025, a national health portal in July 2026, and a unique patient identifier running in one hospital. Almost none of them publishes a usage figure. The two that do publish numbers are unflattering: government's own monitoring system reported 43.43% of planned activities completed for the second half of 2025, and digital payment at civil registry centres raised about FCFA 200 million across 23 months.

The market moved backwards where it counts for users. A second telco-led prepaid card launched in April 2026, but the BCEAO records distributor numbers down 46.34% in 2024, the steepest fall in the Union, and Mali's share of Union transaction value down from 12.1% to 11.7% — the cash-in and cash-out counter contracting while two state flows are pushed onto digital rails.

The data-protection authority advanced on its own account: from about 2,000 declaratory files and a 5m FCFA fine in 2025 to a ten-day session on more than 1,000 files opened on 3 August 2026, with a Segou antenna opened in December 2025. The mandatory lease-declaration decree it was reviewing in October 2025 has not been adopted.

The speech position regressed. At the start of the window nothing was on file; at the end, a ministerial instruction of 29 July 2026 reads existing cybercrime provisions onto platform content without new legislation, and the cybercrime pole has convicted a publication director of undermining the credit of the state.

Four instruments entered the record as recommendations in the window's final week and none as law: a data-management law, a startups law, a Conseil national du Numerique and an AES protocol on AI governance. Regionally Mali was re-elected to the ATU board for 2027-2031 and carried the AES brief at the UEMOA salon that adopted the Declaration de Ouagadougou.
