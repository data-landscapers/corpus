This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

NITDA signed the National Cloud Computing Guideline, the National Cloud Technical Guideline and the National Digital Infrastructure Assurance Framework on 4 August 2026. They were signed with the state-owned carrier Galaxy Backbone, and set data-classification and provider-certification requirements. The technical guideline, issued as version 1.0, becomes mandatory on 1 January 2027 under sections 6 and 32 of the NITDA Act 2007. It binds federal institutions, state governments, cloud providers, data-centre operators and government contractors, and is reviewed every three years. The computing guideline sets Cloud First for federal institutions from the same date. The assurance framework, at version 2.0, sets four certification classes for infrastructure providers and service integrators. Nigeria's national telecommunications policy dates from 2000, and the Nigerian Communications Commission is rewriting it for cloud, artificial intelligence and data centres.

In July 2026 the communications minister paused enforcement of new internet-platform rules until a unified digital-economy framework is in place. He set up a joint NCC, NITDA and NDPC technical committee to resolve their overlapping mandates. In August 2026 the digital-lending dispute rested on a ministerial direction, with operators asking both regulators for coordination protocols.

A digital public infrastructure framework issued in March 2025 sets a target of 75 per cent of public services digitised by 2027. A Presidential Committee on DPI Implementation, chaired by the President, was inaugurated in May 2025 to coordinate identity, payments and data exchange. The Government Service Portal, launched in July 2026, sits inside the e-Government Master Plan 2.0. In June 2026 the Central Bank replaced its five-year planning horizon with the three-year Payments System Vision 2028. In February 2026 none of its fintech roadmap was in force, including a Single Regulatory Window and a Responsible AI in Finance Hub. The overarching document remains the ministry's 2023 Strategic Blueprint. In November 2026 NITDA launched a public dashboard tracking its 2024-2027 Strategic Roadmap and Action Plan. Nigeria ranks 33rd of 54 African states on the Mo Ibrahim Foundation's governance index, down 1.4 points over the decade to 2023.

Legislation and regulation

The National Identity Management Commission Act 2026 was signed into law on 26 June 2026 and repealed the 2007 Act. It makes the National Identification Number the country's foundational credential under a "one person, one identity" policy. Its gazetted text had not been published at the end of July 2026. The Cybercrimes Act 2015 remains the principal cybercrime law, as amended in 2024. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 is the general law on personal data. The Evidence (Amendment) Act 2023 makes electronic records and signatures admissible in court.

The Investments and Securities Act 2025 brought crypto assets under securities law. An executive order signed in July 2026 created a Virtual Asset Council chaired by the Central Bank of Nigeria. It creates no new regulator and transfers no powers. A Revenue Service circular of 31 July 2026 sets a 1.5% stamp duty on virtual-asset trades, collected by exchanges and remitted in the token traded.

The National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill 2024, which would make NITDA a super-regulator, reached public hearing in November 2025. In October 2026 the chair of the House committee on ICT and cybersecurity put its enactment, with the cybercrime and cybersecurity bills, between January and June 2027. Four artificial-intelligence bills passed second reading on 3 December 2024, and no AI statute has followed. SB.648 would oblige large platforms to keep offices in Nigeria; it had a Senate hearing on 23 July 2026. The Fintech Regulatory Commission Bill, heard on 2 March 2026, is still pending. A House bill of late October 2026 would require payment data generated in Nigeria to be stored and processed in the country.

In July 2026 the Federal High Court upheld the FCCPC's digital-lending regulations but held airtime lending to be the NCC's alone. The appeal was pending in August 2026. In October 2026 a regulator survey found two of 19 licensees fully ready for the Internet Code of Practice 2026. The health ministry said in October 2026 that it is drafting rules for AI in healthcare.

Data protection

On 29 July 2026 the FCT High Court gave judgment against Stanbic IBTC for marketing to two former customers after they withdrew consent. It awarded ₦15,000,000 in damages, the first such award by a Nigerian court for a data-protection breach. A Lagos High Court judge held on 25 September 2026 that Meta's use of Nigerian users' data for behavioural advertising is unlawful. The judge awarded the naira equivalent of US$100,000, and Meta filed a notice of appeal on 30 September. On 14 September 2026 the High Court of Lagos State held that one user's upload of contacts does not give the consent of the people in that list. Digital-rights lawyers sued the electoral commission in July 2026 for having no published privacy policy.

In July 2026 the Federal High Court upheld the data protection commission's power to designate Data Controllers and Processors of Major Importance. The commission then issued a registration directive reaching PoS agents, banks, telecoms operators, health providers and platforms. Its rules sit in the General Application and Implementation Directive of March 2025. Transfers abroad require adequate protection, not localisation, and the United States is not a recognised destination.

Compliance notices went to 1,369 organisations in August 2025, and to more than 1,000 educational institutions in February 2026. The largest penalty remains ₦766,242,500, imposed on MultiChoice Nigeria in July 2025. On 3 November 2025 the Federal High Court in Abuja adopted a settlement with Meta, and the commission's orders against Meta, including a US$32.8m fine, were set aside.

A circular of 27 July 2026 ordered every ministry and agency to appoint a data protection officer and file audit returns. Three days after the commission announced it, no copy was on the Secretary to the Government's portal. In July 2026 Cross River State approved a facial-recognition and licence-plate network for its security adviser's office. The approval names no lawful basis, retention period or oversight arrangement.

Regional collaboration

Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger severed ties with ECOWAS in 2024 and left the WURI regional identity programme. Their Alliance of Sahel States has issued a common biometric passport since January 2025. Burkina Faso has issued alliance-aligned national identity cards since November 2025, and Niger since March 2026.

ECOWAS ministers adopted the Digital Sector Development Strategy 2024-2029 in Cotonou on 4 October 2024. The same meeting adopted a directive on cyber confidence-building measures, and required every mobile operator to apply the ECOWAS Roaming Regulation in full by the end of 2025. In January 2026 ECOWAS adopted a digital roadmap in Lagos for a Regional Digital Single Market of more than 400 million citizens, built on fintech, identity and broadband. The Community budget for 2026-2028 carries no separate digital appropriation, and its only ICT-named line shows nil from 2024 to 2028. The World Bank's US$137m second West Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, of March 2026, covers Benin, Liberia and Sierra Leone.

Nigeria issued the first ECOWAS biometric national identity cards in Abuja on 28 November 2025. In May 2026 the AfCFTA Secretariat chose Nigeria, Kenya and Morocco as the first pilots for shared cross-border rails for identity, payments and data exchange. Nigeria joined the Global Cross-Border Privacy Rules Forum as an associate member in April 2025. In April 2026 the West Africa Telecommunications Regulators Assembly finalised harmonisation reports on 5G, subsea-cable resilience, cybersecurity and satellite regulation. Nigeria's regional integration score rose 16.5 points over the decade to 2023, placing it 20th of 54 African states. It was the sixth-largest gain among Nigeria's 96 indicators in the Ibrahim Index.

Standards

NITDA approved a National Software Quality Assurance Framework in July 2026. Under it, every government software project must be independently tested and certified before go-live, as a condition of NITDA IT Project Clearance. Systems are tiered by risk, with Class A covering core banking, national identity infrastructure and power-grid control. The framework reaches full operation in Q2 2027, and in July 2026 no Licensed Software Testing Organisation existed to do the testing.

The Cyber Resilience Framework for the Nigeria Communication Sector took effect on 23 February 2026 and binds every licensee. Incidents must be detected within four hours and confirmed within twenty-four, and full compliance falls due on 23 February 2027. An NCC guidance note of 7 August 2026 requires every licensed operator to budget for cybersecurity and appoint a chief information security officer.

From 10 October 2026 the regulator warned owners of phones without Nigerian type approval that they could be cut off after 30 days on a network. Its Device Management System checks each handset's IMEI against a central register. A Device Checker launched on 7 October 2026 lets buyers verify a handset by its IMEI, over *315# or online. On 29 October 2026 the regulator added an app for manufacturers, importers and dealers to scan IMEIs into the platform.

The e-Government Interoperability Framework dates from 2019. The DPI Technical Standard makes the National Identification Number the primary identifier across government systems. NITDA's draft DPI technical standards for identity, payments and data exchange went out for consultation in April 2025. From October 2025 the Central Bank required all PoS terminals to be geo-tagged and the industry to move to ISO 20022 messaging. The Standards Organisation of Nigeria is the country's ISO member body, sitting on 109 ISO technical committees in 2026. Its certification arm was accredited in January 2024. In August 2026 tertiary institutions were drafting their own AI guidelines without a national framework. The courts are digitising without a national judicial technology strategy (August 2026).

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Omoyele Sowore, a presidential candidate, is being prosecuted at the Federal High Court in Abuja under section 24 of the Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act 2024. The charge concerns social-media posts of 25 August 2025, and he pleaded not guilty on 2 December 2025. In August 2026 the case stood adjourned to 28 September, with the Department of State Services applying to foreclose his defence. The service had demanded that X and Meta remove the posts; both were co-defendants until the amended charge. In February 2026 section 24 was still being used against journalists. Nigeria's digital freedom score in the Ibrahim Index fell 14.6 points over the decade to 2023, leaving it 26th of 54 African states. Its media freedom score, 32nd, fell 4.8 points, and formal freedom of expression held flat.

The Senate held its public hearing on SB.648, the platform-offices bill, on 23 July 2026. Its sponsor opened a public consultation on Facebook on 27 July 2026, four days after the hearing. On 18 July 2026 civil-society groups warned that the bill's enforcement powers could be used to block platforms. A youth-led coalition backed it at the hearing without contesting any provision.

The World Bank's GovTech Maturity Index for 2025 records no government platform for citizen participation in policy or decision-making. It returns "No" on the indicator and all six sub-indicators, while rating right-to-information legislation in force. Over the decade to 2023 the accessibility of public records was among Nigeria's strongest placings in the Ibrahim Index and one of its largest gains. What the state discloses of its own accord barely moved. In August 2026 a civil-society framework for National Assembly hearings by radio, SMS, USSD and digital platforms was still a draft. In October 2026 the Nigeria AI Governance Forum, convened by the Global Network for Cybersolution, inaugurated a 15-member council to give policy advice on AI.

Finance

Financial sustainability

Nigeria's own money carries 66.6 per cent of the digital lines in the 2026 federal appropriation, ₦362.0 billion of ₦543.3 billion, up from 39.3 per cent of ₦1,027.4 billion in 2025; the 2024 share was 46.6 per cent.

The 2025 Appropriation Act carried ₦400.6 billion of tied multilateral or bilateral loan for phase II of the national ICT backbone. The 2026 appropriation carries ₦30.0 billion for it. The 2026 appropriation also carries ₦89.3 billion, ₦36.0 billion and ₦26.0 billion of African Development Bank, Islamic Development Bank and French Development Agency loans for a digital and creative enterprises programme. That programme does not split out its digital share. The identity commission's own 2026 vote is ₦37.1 billion. In October 2026 the World Bank proposed extending the ID4D credit behind enrolment by 12 months, to 31 December 2027. Its December 2024 restructuring had set the close at 31 December 2026. The extension is to build the successor identity system, whose contract was signed on 15 September 2026. No outturn against the digital appropriations had been published by September 2026.

Galaxy Backbone, owned by the Federal Government and Jigawa State to serve federal agencies, earned ₦9.41 billion in 2024. That was 11 per cent less than in 2023, and it lost ₦214 million after tax. In September 2025 its 1Gov cloud was extended across ministries to cut their operating costs. In August 2026 a Joint Technical Committee chaired by the Budget Office's Director-General was set up to bring government cloud spending under the sovereign cloud initiative. NITDA's review of more than ₦4.2 trillion of federal IT project submissions for 2023-2026 had saved over ₦400 billion.

In August 2026 the Federal Executive Council approved the Smart National Transport Databank as a 20-year design-build-finance-operate-transfer concession to Asia-Arab Investment Nigeria Limited. It covers at least 250 roadside gantries, traffic management centres in every state and a main data centre. How the concessionaire is paid, and what the state owes under it, had not been published by September 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

The health memorandum the United States and Nigeria signed in December 2025 says it is not an international agreement, and that its support depends on the availability of funds. It names an American cloud supplier for the national insurance claims database as an example, not a concluded contract.

In January 2025 the United States and Nigeria signed a US$2 million grant toward the 90,000 km fibre build. In February 2025 the Nigerian Communications Commission signed a grant agreement with Swedfund for crowdsourced network-performance data. Nigeria and Finland signed a memorandum on digitalisation and innovation in March 2026. It covers cybersecurity, digital governance and infrastructure, and Finnish finance for Project BRIDGE through Finnvera and Finnfund is still prospective. In June 2025 Nigeria's space agency signed a memorandum with China's Galaxy Space for direct-to-device satellite connectivity, with technology transfer and training. In February 2026 Nigeria signed a subsea fibre agreement with Equatorial Guinea. In October 2026 the Central Bank signed a memorandum with Singapore's Global Finance & Technology Network on financial innovation, with no funding attached.

In April 2026 the government and the police signed a memorandum to map police stations and connect them to the national backbone. In August 2026 the agricultural development fund and the statistics office agreed to harmonise agricultural statistics. On 15 September 2026 NITDA and the national chambers of commerce signed a memorandum on digital adoption by small businesses. NITDA's 2025 scheme to place Nigerian technology professionals in remote work abroad rests on a partnership, not a contract. In February 2025 the president opened a five-area artificial intelligence alliance with Google in Paris. In July 2026 Tether agreed to supply stablecoin liquidity to business customers of the Nigerian exchange Busha.

New investments

The US$500 million World Bank credit behind Project BRIDGE had disbursed nothing by 24 July 2026. It was approved in October 2025 and became effective on 27 April 2026, against a 2030 closing date. In April 2026 it anchored a US$800 million sovereign package with US$200 million from the African Development Bank and US$100 million from the EBRD. The AfDB approved its share in April 2026.

Behind that sits about US$4.5 billion of external and private commitment to Nigeria across 349 recorded deals between 2015 and 2026, from 81 distinct financiers, the World Bank the largest by value and the African Development Bank second. Grants are the commonest instrument across the 2015-2026 record, at 281 of the 349 commitments but only about US$258 million, followed by equity at 34. The two largest single sums on the 2015-2026 record are a US$500 million World Bank concessional credit and MTN's US$290 million 5G spectrum licence. Over the same 2015-2026 record connectivity absorbs the most money at about US$2.2 billion, ahead of payments and data storage at about US$0.5 billion each, while training and skills is the most numerous subsector at 94 commitments. Of the 349 commitments, 89 go to private-sector recipients and 27 to the public sector.

The US$430 million ID4D package approved in February 2020 combines IDA, French Development Agency and European Investment Bank money. By October 2026 about US$234 million, or 54.4%, had been disbursed, and a 12-month extension to 31 December 2027 was proposed. In February 2026 JICA signed a ¥3.142 billion grant seeding a US$50 million start-up fund with the sovereign wealth fund. The European Union committed €45 million in December 2025. The IFC lent US$150 million to Airtel Africa in July 2026. On 27 July 2026 Dimension Data closed a ₦4.05 billion first tranche of a ₦20 billion bond, the first domestic bond for a Nigerian fibre build.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

By 24 July 2026 Project BRIDGE, Nigeria's flagship fibre programme, had laid none of its 90,000 km, raised none of its US$1,100m private-capital target and signed none of its 25 planned client contracts. The World Bank's appraisal of September 2025 put terrestrial fibre at 30,000 km, against the 120,000 km the national blueprint requires. The project company, Bridge Open Access, was announced on 10 August 2026. In August 2026 private investors were to hold 51% to 75% of this wholesale open-access company. The 2025 appraisal sets US$500m of IDA credit against US$1,100m of commercial finance, and rates fiduciary risk High. On 14 October 2026 the government, the European Union and the EBRD signed a €14m grant for network design and project preparation.

The regulator counted 44,678 fibre cuts between January and September 2026, 9,283 of them from vandalism. In August 2026 it set up a standing committee with the works ministry and the National Security Adviser's office to protect fibre as critical infrastructure. No terms of reference, budget or enforcement power had been published for it by November 2026.

Nigeria had 197.42 million telecommunications subscriptions in August 2026, with 4G at 54.77% and 5G at 4.92% of mobile subscriptions. Broadband penetration was 58.22% in August 2026, against the 70% the national broadband plan set for December 2025. In December 2024 4G reached 84.60% of the population and 5G 13.28%. The regulator's 2030 targets are at least 96% for 4G and 50% for 5G. Nigeria's mobile communications score made the largest ten-year gain of its 96 Ibrahim Index indicators to 2023, to 14th in Africa. Internet use, affordability and computer access ranked only 31st. The first of a planned 3,700 government towers for unserved communities was commissioned in Abia State on 16 October 2026.

Fixed broadband median download speed reached 39.93 Mbps in July 2026, against 22.15 Mbps in January 2025. Peak traffic at Nigeria's internet exchange grew from 4 Gbps in 2015 to more than 2 Tbps by March 2026. MTN Nigeria reported profit after tax of ₦707.54bn for the half year to 30 June 2026.

Data Storage

A Central Bank circular of June 2026 orders banks and payment providers to hold payments data in Nigeria by 2027. In September 2026 the bank said guidance on what counts as payment data and on cross-border processing was still being prepared, and told institutions not to wait for it. A NITDA survey presented in October 2026 found 22 of 44 financial institutions had not begun. In 2026 Amazon Web Services' only African region was in Cape Town, with Nigeria served from there and from edge infrastructure. Its local presence is a Lagos Local Zone, in place since 2023. Hyperscaler region investment has gone to Kenya and South Africa.

In July 2026 around 26 facilities, 18 of them commercial, carried 50 to 56 MW of live capacity. That made Nigeria Africa's second-largest market after South Africa, with almost all capacity in Lagos. An independent research house counted about 28 MW across some 15 live facilities in January 2026. MTN opened a 4.5 MW Tier III centre in July 2025, and Digital Realty a 2 MW site at Lekki in August 2025. Equinix announced a US$22m Lagos centre in November 2025. Kasi Cloud's US$250m Lekki campus, announced in February 2026, is backed by the sovereign wealth fund. In October 2026 AFRIDATA signed a US$1.87m grant to study two data centres in Lagos and Delta.

NITDA's National Cloud Policy of October 2025 sets residency rules for sensitive sovereign data. The National Digital Cloud Policy of 17 August 2026 sorts government and regulated data into four levels. National-security data stays on infrastructure in Nigeria, and financial, health, biometric and identity data is stored there at rest. Commercial data has no general localisation duty, and the sovereignty provisions await presidential approval. Subscriber data and government sovereign data were already required to sit in Nigeria in September 2025. The NCC's August 2026 guidance adds two years of in-country retention for call logs. In August 2026 NITDA named cable cuts and outages at foreign providers as the risk the sovereign cloud answers. The case rests on the March 2024 damage to four West African cables.

Energy

In July 2026 the telecoms regulator agreed with the Rural Electrification Agency to power base stations and fibre repeaters from off-grid renewable installations. The agreement also places telecoms equipment on the agency's energy assets. The agency already runs mini-grids under an Off-Grid Electrification Strategy and regulations the electricity regulator made in 2016. In May 2026 the grid had never reliably carried more than 6 GW for about 220 million people. Data-centre operators had an average of four hours of grid power a day. In 2023, 61.2% of the population had access to electricity, and rural access was far below urban. Nigeria's access-to-energy score in the Ibrahim Index rose 13.6 points over 2014-2023. It was one of the ten largest gains among the country's 96 indicators, and left it 26th of 54 African states.

A 2019 survey of Nigerian technology companies recorded more than 30 power outages a month. A 2025 study named unreliable electricity and connectivity as barriers to electronic health records in Nigerian hospitals. In May 2026 diesel power cost several times grid power in South Africa and Kenya. In July 2026 data-centre operators named power reliability, beside skills, as what holds back live capacity.

Technical Capacity

The World Bank's appraisal of 2025 flagged weak capacity at the ministry implementing Nigeria's flagship fibre programme. It rated the operation's environmental and social risk Substantial and required an environmental and social expert in place throughout. In August 2026 the telecoms regulator said a digital industrial park and learning centre it financed and built at Enugu were never activated. Nigeria's public administration score in the Ibrahim Index fell 6.6 points over 2014-2023, to 37th of 54 African states. The state's effectiveness at delivery rose 9.2 points over the same decade, to 30th.

Nigeria had more than 1.1 million GitHub developer accounts in 2024, up 28% in a year and the largest developer community in Africa. In July 2025 the identity commission moved the national identity register onto a platform it built and manages itself, replacing a vendor. In 2025 Japan funded a start-up hub in Abuja being built for NITDA. In July 2026 data-centre operators named skilled engineers, beside power, as what the estate lacks.

Cybersecurity

Since 23 February 2026 every Nigerian telecoms licensee has been bound by a Cyber Resilience Framework for the communications sector, with full compliance due on 23 February 2027. A breach of sensitive personal information is a Critical incident, to be notified to the regulator's response team within four hours of detection. That is a shorter clock than the Data Protection Act's, and runs to a different body. Its information-sharing centre circulates threat intelligence among providers and national bodies, and reporting runs to regulators. Nothing in it requires an incident to be named publicly.

The framework splits sector cybersecurity across four existing bodies and creates two more. The four are the NCC's response team, ngCERT, the National Cybersecurity Coordination Centre and the data protection commission. The governing strategy dates from 2021. In July 2026 the root certification authority role moved from NITDA to the identity commission. The ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024 places Nigeria in its third tier, with legal measures its strongest pillar at 19.52 out of 20. In September 2026 ngCERT recorded a significant rise in ransomware, phishing and business email compromise across sectors.

INTERPOL named the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre the best cybercrime unit in Africa for 2024. In November 2025 the securities regulator, central bank and financial-crimes commission agreed to track and freeze illicit digital wallets jointly. Since March 2026 financial institutions must run automated anti-money-laundering monitoring. A 2024 order designates the bank verification and national identity databases as critical national information infrastructure. In May 2026 the data protection commission put attacks at more than 4,000 a week and cybercrime losses at about ₦12bn for 2024. Operators recorded 19,384 fibre cuts and 3,241 equipment thefts between January and August 2025. In February 2025 Nigeria had no established domestic cyber-insurance market.

DPI

Data Exchange

On 30 July 2026 the Government Service Portal went into service integrated with the Nigerian Digital Exchange. Federal agencies now read a citizen's details from one another instead of collecting them again. The exchange was announced in August 2025, built with the European Union, Finland and Estonia. It is modelled on Estonia's X-Road. In June 2026 eight federal registers, among them identity, banking, tax, immigration and voter, were still siloed.

NITDA took the Nigeria Government Enterprise Architecture over from Korea's KOICA in March 2026. It is hosted by the state carrier Galaxy Backbone and piloted at NIMC, the Customs Service, the Immigration Service and NITDA. In October 2026 the interior ministry introduced a system to share data among the immigration, correctional and fire services.

Since 1 January 2026 a company's Corporate Affairs Commission number has served as its tax identifier. Since 27 March 2026 the National Single Window has taken a single customs submission in place of duplicate paperwork across agencies. Export processing on it is due to open on 14 December 2026. Under an April 2026 arrangement between the central bank and the telecoms regulator, banks check a telecom feed for SIM swaps before clearing a transaction. Open banking, on tied consent, a central registry and standard interfaces, was approved for an August 2025 launch. The national education information system went live nationwide on 1 July 2026, covering 124,548 schools and more than 40 million learners.

Digital Identity and CRVS

142 million people had registered for a National Identification Number by September 2026, on the identity commission's own count. That was up from 123.9 million enrolments in October 2025. The World Bank put numbers issued at about 121 million at 30 September 2026. Its identity project has targeted 180 million since December 2024. In October 2026 the Bank proposed extending the project to December 2027 to deploy a new open-source identity system, whose contract was signed on 15 September 2026. Men made up 56.25% of the register and women 43.75% in October 2025.

The identity statute passed in 2026 makes the commission Nigeria's root certification authority for electronic signatures and digital trust services. In July 2026 the commission reactivated an inspectorate against illegal enrolment centres, identity theft and online fraud. In October 2026 a technology outlet reported a presidential directive ordering every federal agency to use the commission's authentication service as its default identity check.

In March 2026 an identity number was required for healthcare, education, banking, employment, telecoms and welfare. Registering a newborn requires at least one parent to hold a valid number. Satellite internet users were required to submit a number and a headshot by December 2025. In March 2026 corrections to a record ran through a device-locked smartphone portal, while 72% of adults had no smartphone. Nigerians' rating of how easy it is to obtain an identity document fell steeply over the decade to 2023, to 34th of 54 African states.

Nigeria began issuing the ECOWAS biometric identity card on 28 November 2025. It is valid for travel across the bloc without a passport. In July 2026 Nigeria completed integration with the ICAO Public Key Directory, so its passports can be verified at any member state's border. In October 2026 Katsina State and the commission agreed a working group to draft a memorandum on joining the state's identity system to the national one within eight weeks. In 2026 the supervising minister said a national cooperative identity reform had begun at no cost to government.

Digital Payments and Fintech

In June 2026 the IMF found that Nigerians' shift into dollar-linked stablecoins is weakening demand for the naira and blunting monetary policy. Nigeria received US$59bn of crypto-asset inflows between July 2023 and June 2024. It has taken around 60% of sub-Saharan Africa's stablecoin inflows since 2019. On 5 November 2026 the central bank issued regulations licensing stablecoin issuers and banning algorithmic stablecoins. Issuers need paid-up capital of N10bn for naira tokens and N15bn for foreign-currency tokens, and the start date is left to a later instrument.

By September 2025 the national safety-net programme had paid N330 billion to 8.5 million of 15 million targeted households. It verified each by identity number and paid into bank accounts or mobile wallets. A N50,000 emergency relief grant began paying into mobile wallets in October 2026, to households drawn from identity records and the social register.

The instant payment scheme has run since 2011 and handled around 11 billion transactions in 2024. Its board is a central bank deputy governor and the chief executives of the deposit-taking banks. Electronic transactions were worth ₦1.2 quadrillion in 2025, against ₦395 trillion in 2022. The first transaction on a National Payment Stack built to ISO 20022 settled on 7 November 2025. By late September 2026 the stack had passed 100 million transactions, on its operator's count. In July 2026 the eNaira was being redesigned toward a wholesale model. Nigeria ranks 12th of 54 African states for access to banking services, up 2.3 points over 2014-2023. In October 2026 Onafriq wound down its Baxi agent-banking network after five years, citing inflation, costs and the growth of instant account transfers.

MTN Nigeria's fintech revenue fell 72.4% to ₦12.99 billion in the second quarter of 2026, after it suspended airtime and data credit. In the first half of 2026 its mobile money revenue grew about 132% and active wallets rose to 5.0 million. By August 2026 the credit service had resumed, with lending at about a quarter of its first-quarter rate.

Registries

By May 2026 just over 12.3 million of the more than 77 million people on the National Social Register had been linked to a National Identification Number. Over 11.5 million of these were validated by May 2026. In 2026 the register covered more than 19 million households in all 774 local government areas. In 2025 an independent analysis put its reach at 69.94 million people, 52.6% of the multidimensionally poor.

The electronic civil registration and vital statistics platform went live in all 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory on 1 July 2026. In July 2026 it ran through 4,011 registration centres, against a target of about 8,000. The population commission has asked states to fund their own rollouts, and Akwa Ibom was the first to begin. Nigeria's civil registration score in the Ibrahim Index fell 12.5 points over 2014-2023, to 31st of 54 African states.

In 2025 less than 5% of land was formally titled. In March 2025 the government planned to digitise land records nationwide. In February 2025 only 9% of registered companies were in the tax system. From January 2026 corporate registration numbers serve as tax identifiers.

On 1 October 2026 the postal service launched a national digital postcode, giving each addressable building an 11-character code. A day later its application was reported to show homeowners' names, and to allow search by name. The postmaster-general replied that field teams had recorded addresses as residents describe them, owners' names included. In November 2026 the postal service said the postcode database is hosted in Nigeria on Galaxy Backbone, with Amazon Web Services serving only the public website.

In October 2026 the electoral commission put the voters' register at more than 103 million, after a biometric clean-up. On 30 October the commission said some registration officers had entered AI-generated photographs and other irregular records. In November 2026 it said it had removed more than 1.6 million invalid records after biometric screening, and had found 54 staff in 10 states implicated. It plans to certify a cleaned register on 15 December 2026.

Sectoral management information systems

The human-resource modules of the federal payroll and personnel system went live on 27 July 2026, serving over 508 federal agencies and more than 600,000 civil servants. Staff can now manage their records, download payslips and apply for leave online. The same system remitted ₦1.96 trillion for those agencies in 2024.

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech index records live systems for all nine core functions it assesses. Every one whose software type is recorded runs on commercial off-the-shelf products. The financial management system reaches central and local government, but tracks performance only for selected programmes. Nigeria's tax and revenue mobilisation and its budgetary and financial management both fell over 2014-2023 in the Ibrahim Index, to 20th and 24th of 54.

Tax runs on TaxPro Max, and businesses above ₦5bn turnover have had to integrate their invoicing since 1 August 2025. In January 2025 customs ran B'Odogwu, a web-based system benchmarked on ASYCUDA. In August 2025 it was due to be joined to the National Single Window. Electronic filing at the Supreme Court has been compulsory since 1 July 2026, on a case management system launched that month. By May 2025, 100 of Lagos State's 326 primary healthcare facilities used electronic medical records. Edo State's schools reporting system covered 51 schools in 2025. In October 2026 Kwara State approved electronic medical records across its hospitals, with training from November.

In July 2026 school admission in Edo State was entirely manual, and its birth-registration database was connected to no educational platform. In October 2025 Nigeria joined the Digital Public Goods Alliance and planned open-source deployments of OpenCRVS, DHIS2 and OpenMRS. The health ministry's bulletin gave a national facility reporting rate of 91.5% for June 2025.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

On 30 July 2026 the Government Service Portal was soft-launched as a single sign-on gateway to federal services. It was built by Galaxy Backbone with Korea's KOICA under the e-Government Master Plan 2.0, after pilots with a few agencies since 2025. In May 2026 the government launched GovGuide, a multilingual assistant built on Meta's Llama model, to guide citizens through federal services.

The World Bank's 2025 GovTech index rates the national service portal transactional, so services can be completed through it. Businesses and individuals can register, file and pay taxes online, and electronic procurement publishes tenders and awards. A national contractor database, deployed from 2010, was joined in 2018 to the companies registry, revenue service and pension commission. On 8 October 2026 the procurement bureau said its new electronic system would end physical submission of bids. The index records no government online employment portal. Over the decade to 2023 the accessibility of public records was among Nigeria's largest gains in the Ibrahim Index. Equal access to public services barely moved.

In September 2025 full rollout began of a 1Gov cloud run by Galaxy Backbone, supplying storage, government mail and electronic signature across ministries. By December 2025, 31 ministries and departments were declared fully digital, with 100,828 official mail accounts. In October 2026 the electoral commission said its online nomination portal had processed more than 15,000 nominations for the 2027 elections.

In 2025 Lagos launched an AI chatbot on WhatsApp and a self-reporting evidence portal for survivors of domestic and sexual violence. In September 2026 Ondo State launched an AI platform for civil-service payroll, retirement and pensions.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

Only two of Nigeria's 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory reached high digital maturity in the Nigeria Governors' Forum's July 2025 assessment. Most sat at a moderate stage. In July 2026 the National Population Commission asked states to pay for their own rollout of electronic civil registration. In August 2026 the communications regulator leased its unused Enugu digital industrial park to the state for 15 years.

Enugu State is funding a solar-powered, connected school in each of its 260 wards, allocating ₦266.7bn in 2025 and ₦198bn in 2026. All 260 were promised for January 2026; in May 2026 many were still incomplete and unequipped. A school at Mbu was demolished in July 2025 after failing integrity tests. A ₦11.4bn contract for 22 schools, ₦5.7bn of it paid upfront, was terminated, and ₦1.23bn had been recovered by May 2026.

In September 2026 Lagos said it had laid about 3,000km of a planned 6,000km state fibre network between 2019 and 2025. In November 2026 Osun agreed a UK-funded state digital reform project, with no amount stated. In September 2026 Ondo launched Ondo Pay, a civil-service payroll platform using bank-verification data to remove duplicate records. In October 2026 Adamawa began building a state system to hold one personnel and payroll record across its civil service. Anambra's SmartGov suite has checked local government payroll against the identity and bank-verification registers since June 2025. In October 2026 Anambra made its website a single gateway to state services, budgets and complaints. In June 2025 Lagos began fixing QR-coded address plates linked to property data, piloted in Eti-Osa. Cross River's state-wide surveillance network, approved in July 2026, names no lawful basis, retention period or oversight. In 2025 the federal financial management system reached local as well as central government.

Rural digital data capture

Rural Nigeria's median mobile download speed was 16.4 Mbps in March 2026, up 47.5% on September 2025, on a crowdsourced test base of 543,123. In October 2025 the communications regulator said only 23% of rural communities had internet access. In October 2026 the government set out a blended funding model for community-owned rural networks, with the universal service fund paying for towers and equipment.

A 2025 study found electronic health records held back by unreliable power and internet, with rural grid access at about 41%. In March 2026 rural primary schools marked attendance on paper forms digitised further up the chain. In June 2026 field registrars in hard-to-reach areas were reverting to paper when the network failed. In May 2026 registrars were recording young children's births on handheld devices under the national civil registration rollout. By 2025 a donor-supported Edo State programme tracked attendance in real time at a few schools, some rural. Nigeria's rural policy framework ranks 18th of 54 in the Ibrahim Index, its best placing there. Its support to the rural economy rose 13.0 points over the decade to 2023.

In January 2026 Niger State had 21 identity enrolment centres for about 7.5 million people, a round trip costing around ₦3,000. Without a number a woman cannot reach maternal care funded by the Basic Health Care Provision Fund. In January 2026 rural women's phone ownership stood at 42%, against 78% in urban areas. In May 2026 poor rural broadband limited the reach of GovGuide, the AI assistant aimed at rural and low-literacy users.

A police data centre opened at Force headquarters in May 2021, while divisional stations still took statements by hand. The European Union and INTERPOL handed the platform to the federal government in April 2025, with eight agencies connected.

Technology

AI

In June 2025, 87.5% of the fintechs the Central Bank of Nigeria surveyed were using AI for fraud detection. In June 2026 the Bank launched its own machine-learning system for real-time fraud detection.

NITDA published a draft National Artificial Intelligence Strategy in 2024. In March 2026 the minister declared it final, to go to the National Assembly for approval. AI bills that passed second reading in December 2024 had gone no further by January 2026. In 2026 Nigeria ranked 38th of 135 countries and first in Africa on the Global Index on Responsible AI. In October 2026 NITDA warned the public not to enter identity numbers, bank details or medical results into public AI tools.

GovGuide, launched in May 2026, answers government-service questions in English, Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba on Meta's Llama model. N-ATLAS, an open-source model for Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian English, was released in September 2025 by Awarri with NITDA. Customs has used machine-learning fraud detection and AI vehicle valuation since at least 2022. In April 2026 it began training on AI-based revenue reconciliation. In September 2026 the electoral commission said it runs AI checks on results records before final validation, each subject to human audit. In October 2026 an opposition movement asked it to drop the checks until its governance framework is published.

In June 2026 the government launched the Nigeria AI Scaling Hub, offering shared computing hosted by Galaxy Backbone with Gates Foundation support. In October 2026 the communications ministry proposed pooling GPU capacity with Rwanda, Senegal and Kenya.

In March 2026 researchers found little evidence that AI surveillance reduced crime, and minimal use of camera footage in prosecutions. Cross River's July 2026 specification puts facial recognition and number-plate reading in a command centre under the State Security Adviser. In March 2026 the data protection commission took deepfakes to an international working group of more than 60 regulators.

ICT Industry

Paystack bought Ladder Microfinance Bank in January 2026 and restructured under The Stack Group. The group is jointly owned by Paystack's chief executive, Stripe and employees. Flutterwave took a microfinance banking licence in April 2026 after acquiring Mono. The tier-one banks keep their payments infrastructure inside their own groups: GTCO's HabariPay, Access Holdings' Hydrogen and Stanbic IBTC's Zest. By August 2026 the Central Bank had begun regulating fintech ownership as well as fintech conduct.

The federal payroll and personnel system has been migrated to SoftSuite, built by the Nigerian firm Soft Alliance (July 2026). The Head of the Civil Service places the move under the Nigeria First Policy, as cutting dependence on foreign software licences. Nigeria's core federal systems run on commercial off-the-shelf software. No global hyperscaler operates a full cloud region in the country, and their African regions are in Kenya and South Africa. Terra Industries holds a joint venture with the Defence Industries Corporation of Nigeria to localise production of drones, robotics and cybersecurity capability (2026). Nigeria holds Africa's largest developer community.

Nigeria ranked 49th of 54 African states for economic diversification on the Ibrahim Index in 2023, down over the preceding decade. On WTO figures, exports of digitally delivered services reached US$1.55bn in 2024, against imports of US$105.34bn over 2005-2024.

A study of 21 Nigerian filmmakers found that unequal access to subscription generative-AI tools reinforces a split in Nollywood (August 2026). High-budget productions aim at cinema and global streaming, and low-budget productions at domestic streaming and YouTube. The study found models trained predominantly on Global North data producing cultural misplacement and stereotyping. It found the tools substituting informal pre-production labour, not creative roles, eroding the relational economies that have sustained Nollywood's workforce.

OPay filed a registration statement in the United States on 9 October 2026 for a New York listing. The filing shows a South African banking group's unit buying up to US$200m of shares in a private placement alongside the offering, and 50.1 million monthly active users at end-July 2026.

Innovation ecosystem

In August 2026 the Nigerian Communications Commission said a digital industrial park and learning centre it financed in Enugu was never activated. It was meant for outsourcing, start-up incubation and skills training, and has passed to the Enugu State Government on a lease. In October 2026 the foundation stone of a JICA-funded start-up hub was laid in Wuye, Abuja. In September 2026 a US$12m Korean-funded entrepreneurship centre was under construction in Abuja with UNDP.

A 2026 national assessment by the Innovation Support Network found over 190 active hubs across all six geopolitical zones, concentrated in Lagos and Abuja. Nigerian start-ups raised just over US$400 million in 2024. Nigeria ranks 113th of 133 economies on WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024.

The Nigeria Startup Act 2022 provides for start-up labelling, regulatory sandboxes and tech-hub support. Its labelling committee was inaugurated in July 2024, and the consultative forum that nominates to its council in April 2025. A National Blockchain Policy adopted in 2023 provides for emerging-technology sandboxes. In June 2025 Itana, in the Lekki Free Zone, offered remote incorporation as a digital free zone for $2,000, and $1,150 a year to renew. The World Bank's 2025 index credits Nigeria with a public-sector innovation institution or an innovation strategy, but not both.

In May 2026 Galaxy Backbone and NITDA's innovation office began supplying discounted sovereign cloud to start-ups in the iHatch programme. Huawei, in Nigeria since 1999, opened an innovation centre in Lagos in May 2025. In 2019 Nigerian technology firms named electricity supply among their most severe constraints.

Capacity

Literacy

In 2021 about 9% of Nigerians had used a computer in the previous three months. In 2021 computer use reached only 33% of tertiary-educated women and 43% of tertiary-educated men. Use divided sharply by sex, and again between town and country. No later national measure of digital skill has been found by November 2026.

In October 2026 NITDA launched a national digital literacy dashboard recording 2,769,512 reported training achievements. Its targets are 70% digital literacy by 2027 and 95% by 2030. The agency's head said the figures count training, not proficiency. By February 2026, 54,377 civil servants had enrolled in the Digital Literacy for All course, and over 40% had completed it.

In May 2026 the government launched an AI assistant answering government-service questions in Nigerian languages, aimed at rural and low-literacy users. In July 2026 it began training teachers in federal schools to use AI. A 2025 study found low digital literacy among health workers held back electronic health records. In May 2026 pupils in Enugu State were resuming in smart schools still incomplete and unequipped.

Nigeria's school enrolment scores 38.7 of 100 in the Ibrahim Index for 2023, 29th of 54 African countries. Completion scores 69.6, placing it 10th.

Training and skills

Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Education signed terms of reference in July 2026 for an AI Teacher Capacity Development Programme covering about 11,700 teachers in the federal unity colleges. It followed a pilot in six colleges, one per geopolitical zone, and no cost, timetable or evaluation design has been published. Teacher supply and training scores 83.0 of 100 in the Ibrahim Index, up 8.8 points over the decade to 2023.

NITDA's 3 Million Technical Talent programme, launched in 2023, registers participants through the National Identification Number. In 2025 Nigeria had a strategy and a free programme for public-service digital skills, covering basic skills and data literacy. In October 2026 up to 50,000 fellows of the EU-funded Nigeria Jubilee Fellows Programme were given free access to Cisco's Networking Academy. In October 2026 the regulator's Digital Bridge Institute was designated an ITU Digital Transformation Centre.

The data protection commission opened a Virtual Privacy Academy in May 2025. It runs a certification partnership with Mastercard aimed at the informal sector. In November 2026 it said more than 10,000 data protection officers had been certified. Its two-year programme is financed by Meta under a 2025 court-approved settlement.

NITDA commissioned a National Cybersecurity Centre at Bayero University, Kano in May 2025. In August 2026 drone and robotics laboratories run by a defence contractor were being placed in Miva Open University's curriculum. Nothing has been published on research ownership or the vetting of dual-use content. In August 2026 tertiary institutions were drafting their own AI rules without a national framework.

Research institutions

N-ATLAS, an open-source model for Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian-accented English, was released in September 2025. In 2025 Awarri Technologies fine-tuned it on more than 400 million tokens with NITDA and NCAIR, on Meta's Llama-3 8B. In July 2025 Google funded African-language research at continental scale through a hub for African languages.

In May 2025 NITDA commissioned a National Cybersecurity Centre at Bayero University, Kano, for cyber education, research and talent development.

Inclusion

Access to services

In 2025 surveys by two organisations of the blind found most Nigerian mobile banking apps, USSD channels and ATMs unusable for blind and low-vision customers. Some were made to sign indemnity forms to obtain an ATM card. The Discrimination Against Persons with Disabilities (Prohibition) Act 2018 covers banks. Since July 2026 accessibility has been a testable requirement for citizen-facing government platforms. None of eight public websites audited in May 2026 had baseline accessibility features. They included the admissions board, the identity commission, the revenue service and the disability commission. In August 2026 an estimated 35 million Nigerians had disabilities.

In October 2026 the communications regulator launched zero-rated educational data, giving eligible learners up to 100MB a day on approved platforms. Every eligibility option in its June 2026 consultation required users to register. In September 2026 Oyo became the 26th state to adopt the UNICEF-backed Nigeria Learning Passport. Since about 14 September 2026 access to TikTok over mobile data has been disrupted in Kaduna, Kebbi, Kano and Sokoto.

In March 2026 an identity number was required for healthcare, education, banking, employment, telecoms and welfare. In May 2026 the identity commission counted over 206,000 refugees and displaced people and 32,000 inmates enrolled. In May 2026 the social register's linkage to identity numbers was unfinished. Over the decade to 2023 Nigeria ranked 21st of 54 African states for equal access to public services.

The 2026 access-to-finance survey put 79% of adults using a financial product and 73% formally included, above the national target of 70%. In September 2026, 53% of the poorest fifth were still excluded. Retail equity trading rose 138.76% in January to May 2026 on the same months of 2025. In April 2025 most bank account-holders had no access to credit. In July 2026 Rank turned the rotating ajo and esusu savings circle into a licensed product with guaranteed payouts.

Digital divides

In March 2026 corrections to a National Identification Number record ran through a device-locked smartphone portal, shutting out the 72% of adults with no smartphone. Agents charged ₦12,000 to ₦62,000 for the workaround, against an official fee of ₦2,000.

In 2021 about 12% of Nigerian men and 6% of women had used a computer in the previous three months. In 2021 the figure was 1.7% for rural women and 4% for rural men, against 12% for urban women and 21% for urban men. In 2024 GSMA found 52% of adult men used mobile internet, against 33% of women. The gender gap in smartphone ownership was 38% in 2024. In January 2026 men also outnumbered women on the identity register.

Nigeria ranks 41st of 54 African states for equal socioeconomic opportunity in the Ibrahim Index, up 1.5 points over the decade to 2023. In October 2025, 23% of rural communities had internet access, against 57% of urban communities. In March 2026 the Universal Service Provision Fund counted 87 clusters of unserved areas holding about 23 million people. In May 2026 state readiness for digital public infrastructure was uneven. A national strategy for community networks runs to March 2027.

About 109 million Nigerians were online at the end of 2025, 45.5% of the population. The regulator's population-coverage series for 2024 runs far above that for every generation but 5G. The Ibrahim Index rates Nigeria's internet use, affordability and device ownership far below its mobile network in 2023.

Data

National statistics

The World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators put Nigeria's data sources pillar in the 20-49% band for 2023. It was the lowest of its five pillars. In 2023 data use and data products scored 70-89%, and data services and infrastructure 50-69%. No business or establishment survey was conducted in the ten years to 2023. Agricultural, health and household surveys ran three or more times in that decade, and the labour force survey twice. No economic census fell in the preceding twenty years. An agricultural and a population census did fall in that period.

In August 2026 Nigeria still relied on its 2006 census, with at least N219.17bn appropriated from 2021 to 2026 for a count postponed in 2023. In July 2026 the National Population Commission said the President will set the new date, which cannot fall in an election year. The commission launched a National Geospatial Data Repository from its enumeration-area demarcation in November 2023. In July 2026 about 57% of births and under 20% of deaths were registered, against roughly 5 million births a year.

Nigeria's Revenue Service collected ₦27.1 trillion in the first seven months of 2026, 66.57% of its ₦40.71tn target. The National Bureau of Statistics put information and communication at 11.31% of real GDP in the first quarter of 2026, up from 10.59% a year earlier. In September 2026 the agriculture ministry said it had set up a federal department of agricultural data to run a national digital farmers register. In November 2026 the central bank was moving its fiscal survey of the states and 774 local governments from twice-yearly paper returns to quarterly digital submissions.

The communications regulator's December 2025 urban-rural network analysis used a different tile definition from its March 2026 edition, so the two are not one series. The December edition gave an urban median download of 20.5 Mbps against a rural 11.0 Mbps.

Open data

Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory scores Nigeria's economic and financial data highest, at 7 out of 10 for balance of payments, government finance, trade, and money and banking. National accounts score 6.5. Environmental data scores lowest, with agriculture and land use at 3.5 and the built environment at 5. Among social statistics, poverty scores 6.5 and education outcomes 6, against 4 for food security and 4.5 for education and health facilities.

The Freedom of Information Act 2011 gives a statutory right of access to records held by public institutions. The World Bank's 2025 GovTech assessment rates that right effective. Its electronic procurement system publishes tender notices and awarded contracts. Accessibility of public records ranks 6th of 54 in Africa in the Ibrahim Index, up 14.5 points over the decade to 2023. Proactive disclosure ranks 23rd, up 3.5 points.

The gazetted text of the NIMC Act 2026 had not been published by 29 July 2026, six weeks after assent. In August 2026 the circular said to order ministries to comply with the data protection law could not be found on the Secretary to the Government's portal. The portal did not answer a written request to explain why.

In March 2026 the US State Department put a subset of the health memoranda online, then removed public access. Appendix 5 of the United States-Nigeria memorandum, its Data Sharing Agreement, has not been published. Nor have the remaining memoranda or the related agreements.

Geospatial data

The space agency joined the CropWatch cloud system for crop condition, yield and drought warnings in 2021, and it feeds a quarterly bulletin. In April 2026 the agency issued customised bimonthly bulletins to ministries and agencies. Its West African CropWatch centre was launched in Abuja in July 2024. In June 2025 satellite imagery, telecoms data and AI were used to find urban slum households for the national social register.

In November 2025 the agriculture ministry signed a memorandum with the space agency on satellite tracing of farm plots. Its November 2025 white paper says the EU deforestation regulation puts over USD 1bn a year of export earnings at risk. In April 2026 the space agency launched an Earth Observation-based Agricultural Innovations Project with the science and agriculture ministries. In September 2026 Federal Executive Council approval was stated for six new satellites, four of them for Earth observation. No manufacturer, cost or launch date was given.

By the fourth quarter of 2024 Starlink was Nigeria's second-largest internet provider, with about 65,600 subscribers. In September 2025 it paused new orders in parts of Lagos and Abuja because of congestion. In January 2026 the communications regulator granted Amazon's Project Kuiper a landing permit. Satellite subscribers had to attach an identity number and a headshot to their accounts by December 2025.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

Nigeria's specimen sharing agreement with the United States has been in force since 18 January 2026. Under Article 4(a), Nigerian participation in any multilateral access-and-benefit-sharing arrangement, including surveillance and laboratory networks, shall not prejudice compliance with it. Article 4(b) restates the parent memorandum's term that failure on these commitments could bring changes to planned US assistance. Failure could also bring discontinuation of the memorandum or termination of the agreement. In return Nigeria gets acknowledgement of the Federal Government and its institutions in publications and reports, and a stated intent to share associated findings. It also gets priority access to any countermeasure developed primarily from Nigerian material, subject to the availability of funds and applicable law.

Public Citizen v. United States Department of State was filed in the District of Columbia on 2 April 2026, and Nigeria is one of its sixteen countries. The Nigeria Request, submitted on 22 December 2025, seeks the health memorandum, its appendices, the data sharing agreement, the specimen agreement and the financing agreements. It also seeks budget tables and implementation plans. The appendix that would show which Nigerian health systems the United States may reach is not public.

On 10 July 2026 the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission opened a probe into Meta, Alphabet, X and generative-AI platforms on a federal directive. Nigeria's lever on the platforms has been competition law, not data law. The competition tribunal upheld the commission's US$220m penalty against Meta in April 2025. The penalty was still contested in July 2026. The Data Protection Commission's own two-year privacy programme is financed out of Meta settlement money. No global hyperscaler runs a full cloud region in Nigeria. Amazon serves the country from Cape Town and from edge infrastructure. The Data Protection Commission does not treat the United States as an adequate destination for personal data. American money in the network build is a feasibility grant for the fibre backbone.

China activities

In July 2026 Cross River State specified a state-wide smart surveillance system after technical engagements with Huawei, including an inspection of its facilities in China. Huawei's local partner Dubass Synergy Multi-Services Limited is to implement it. In March 2026 Nigeria had Africa's largest AI surveillance estate.

In October 2025 MTN Nigeria and Huawei lit the country's first 400G/800G hybrid optical network, in Lagos. In June 2026 21st Century Technologies partnered China Mobile International to build local cloud and AI capacity. In March 2025 Benue State's plan to train its civil servants in digital tools built on a skills partnership with Huawei. Huawei, in Nigeria since 1999, opened an innovation centre in Lagos in May 2025. The communications minister called it "a catalyst for Nigeria's tech sovereignty".

On 28 April 2026 China handed over the ECOWAS Headquarters Building it had built in Abuja to the ECOWAS Commission. In August 2026 Nigeria's Permanent Representative to the UN said in Beijing that Nigeria seeks Chinese geophysical and nautical data, technology transfer and investment for its marine economy, not financial aid.

EU activities

In 2020 the European Investment Bank committed a US$215 million framework loan for Nigeria's eID platform and biometric identity for residents and Nigerians abroad. In 2022 Agence Française de Développement held a US$100 million sovereign concessional loan to give every person in Nigeria a single official identifier. Both sit alongside World Bank money.

In 2026 the EBRD committed a sovereign loan of up to US$100 million for the government's stake in the vehicle building about 90,000 km of open-access fibre. In 2021 the EIB lent €100 million to expand 4G capacity and lift coverage in the Lagos region from 90% to 98% of the population. The European Union committed €45 million in December 2025 for connectivity, subsea capacity, 5G and last-mile access. In March 2026 a Global Gateway package co-financed the same fibre rollout with the EBRD, and the data exchange work. On 14 October 2026 the EU and the EBRD signed a €14m grant for the fibre network's design and preparation.

In 2025 Nigeria and Team Europe were working jointly on data exchange. The Nigeria Data Exchange was built with the European Union, Finland and Estonia. Finland signed a memorandum on digitalisation in March 2026, covering cybersecurity, digital governance and infrastructure. In January 2026 Denmark's cBrain, with the Nigerian provider Publica AI, was deploying its case-management platform across federal ministries. In February 2025 the communications regulator and Swedfund signed a grant for crowdsourced network measurement. In October 2026 the Dutch government granted €450,000 for a project on the use of the Cybercrimes Act, legal aid and digital-rights litigation.

Gulf/UAE activities

In August 2026 Enugu State said it was working with teams from Qatar to turn the digital industrial park it leased from the communications regulator into an AI certification centre. It also planned with them to convert the Digital Bridge Institute in the city into an AI institute. No federal Gulf agreement on digital infrastructure has been found by November 2026.

In 2023 Abu Dhabi's Mubadala led a US$76 million financing round for Moove, which lends vehicles to ride-hailing drivers. It was Mubadala's first investment in an African-founded start-up.

India activities

India approved a US$100 million line of credit to Nigeria for a National Rural Broadband Network on 18 June 2019. The Federal Ministry of Communications manages its implementation. No other Indian-financed digital programme had been found running in Nigeria by August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 Edo State replaced a foreign-owned e-governance platform with one of its own, serving all 128 state ministries, departments and agencies. It had paid for the old platform's development and then paid licence fees to keep using it. In July 2025 the identity commission moved verification onto a platform it built and manages itself. In July 2026 federal payroll and personnel records moved to software built by a Nigerian firm, on the stated ground of data sovereignty. The contract for the next identity system, built on the open-source MOSIP platform, was signed on 15 September 2026. On 14 October 2026 the identity commission said its database is hosted entirely in Nigeria, not in the cloud, and run by its own staff.

In August 2026 more than 85 per cent of Nigerian workloads ran on public clouds, and 22 per cent of the 1,000 most-visited websites were hosted locally. In 2025 government hosting sat both inside and outside the country. Payments data must be held in Nigeria from 1 January 2027. In October 2026 a Luminate-commissioned study put the annual cost of reliance on foreign cloud services at about US$850 million.

The state's own host, Galaxy Backbone, is owned by the Federal Government and Jigawa State. In September 2026 it held a technical partnership with Huawei. Its terms on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published by November 2026.

In August 2026 NITDA's director-general said sovereignty has to cover connectivity, compute, data centres, cloud and AI models. The government's citizen-service assistant and the language model N-ATLAS both run on Meta's open-source Llama. In May 2026 Nigeria was chosen as one of three first pilots for the AfCFTA's shared identity, payment and data-exchange rails. In September 2026 Chad and Nigeria signed a joint communiqué on digital cooperation.
