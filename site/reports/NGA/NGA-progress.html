Of 265 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 152 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-25, 54 did not, 49 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 688 sources for this place in the window — 93 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 595 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

Nearly four times as many sources sit in the later half of this window as in the earlier, so arrival counts measure coverage as much as construction.

No system climbed a maturity band, and every system that changed band went backwards. Satellite broadband regressed, its largest provider pausing new orders over congestion and then brought inside a biometric verification regime; the retail central bank digital currency is under review and being redesigned toward a wholesale model; and enforcement of the new internet-platform rules was paused pending a unified framework. One programme stalled — 260 state schools whose January 2026 completion date passed with many incomplete.

Everything else arrived or held. Nigeria's digital state was substantially built before the window opened.

The same year produced a data-localisation directive, a virtual-assets executive order, a crypto tax circular, an identity statute, an open-banking launch and a three-year payments vision.

One position worsened and the rest of the section arrived.

Internet-platform regulation regressed: enforcement of new rules was paused in July pending a unified national digital-economy policy framework. The same month's competition directive put the same class of company under investigation for abuse of dominance.

Data-protection enforcement held its band and changed character within it: already active at the window's start, having lifted compliance from about 4% to 55%+ by March 2025, what the year added was judicial confirmation. The Federal High Court upheld the Commission's registration power in July, and days later a private claimant won the first damages award under the Act without needing the Commission at all. Neither judgment is held, and one has no established delivery date.

What arrived: an executive order that creates a coordination council rather than a regulator, and a tax framework that collects duty in the token. The two rows that held position throughout are the statute itself and the 2021 draft data and cloud policy — five years a draft, while the localisation question it was written to answer was settled instead by a central bank circular.

Satellite broadband regressed. The largest provider had become Nigeria's second-largest ISP on Q4 2024 figures; by September 2025 it had paused new orders over congestion, and by December more than 66,000 of its users were required to submit an identity number and headshot under a regime built for SIM cards. What arrived: a rebased optical backbone, a landing permit for a second satellite constellation, a hyperscale campus at Lekki, four sovereign-cloud initiatives, a cyber resilience framework with four-hour incident reporting, and the removal of the 5% telecom excise duty. The national fibre programme was in development on 2025-08-01 and is in development now. The data-centre estate held its position while the localisation deadline that will test it was set. And the grid behind both has no dated position at either end that the base can state, and the localisation deadline falls on 2027-01-01.

Almost nothing in this section changed band.

The identity register held the same position throughout: implemented at the window's start and implemented now, with enrolment rising from 123.9m to 136m against a 180m target. What did enter the record is the statutory layer around it — the NIMC Act 2026, the root certification authority transfer, ICAO directory integration and the ward-level enrolment drive.

The national data exchange was announced in August 2025 and only began moving personal data in production in July 2026, behind the Government Service Portal; the lawful basis for that operation is not on record at either end.

The payments instruments issued in the window were open banking, a national payment stack, automated anti-money-laundering standards, market-concentration caps and the localisation directive among them. The rails they govern held their positions throughout.

The one regression is the retail central bank digital currency, Africa's first, now under review and being redesigned toward a wholesale model with the Bank stating it is not abandoned.

The section's one movement is a delivery failure. A state programme to build 260 schools, one in every ward, powered by solar and connected before instruction was layered on, promised full opening of all 260 in January 2026. By May 2026 many were incomplete and unequipped with pupils already resuming. The stated sequencing was power first, then connectivity, then teaching; the schedule was not met.

Everything else here either arrived or held. Arrivals were a zero-rated education consultation, an AI-literacy programme entering schools through teachers and a ministerial declaration on meaningful connectivity; the skills and inclusion programmes all held.

The measurements are baselines rather than movements: rural access at 23% against 57% urban, women at 38.8% of enrolled identity holders, and correction of register errors routed through a smartphone portal in a country where about 72% of adults have no smartphone. None is re-measured inside the window.
