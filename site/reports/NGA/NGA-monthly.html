National Strategy on Public Service Reforms

President Tinubu Approves National Strategy on Public Service Reforms — ThisDay, 1 October 2026

President Bola Tinubu has approved a renewed National Strategy on Public Service Reforms, the director general of Nigeria's Bureau of Public Service Reforms, Dasuki Arabi, said in Abuja on 30 September 2026. The three-yearly revision will add government policies on AI and blockchain in the public service. No approval date or text was given.

The Nigerian Communications Commission launched an Internet Governance Working Group at its first Internet Governance Symposium, held in Lagos on 15 October 2026. The commission called for wider cooperation on artificial intelligence, online fraud, privacy and child protection, and cited its Internet Code of Practice 2026 and a cyber resilience framework for the sector.

Legislation and regulation

CBN payments data-localisation directive

Split-cloud architecture puts Nigerian banks under new pressure — BusinessDay (Nigeria), 4 October 2026

Nigerian banks and large fintechs face the central bank's 1 January 2027 deadline for storing payment transaction data in the country. Cloud providers say some may keep applications on foreign clouds and host the regulated data locally, an arrangement the central bank has not ruled on.

Nigerian Fintech Regulatory Commission Bill (HB.2389)

One Regulator or Many? Inside the Bill That Could Rewrite How Nigeria Licenses Its 430 Fintechs — BusinessTech Nigeria, 9 October 2026

BusinessTech Nigeria explains the Nigerian Fintech Regulatory Commission Bill 2025 (HB.2389), which would create a single regulator to license and supervise fintech services now split between the central bank, the SEC and other agencies. The bill passed second reading in the House of Representatives on 28 October 2025 and had a public hearing on 2 March 2026; it remains pending.

Lagos State innovation bill

Lagos Backs AI Builders Over AI Buyers at the Nigeria Innovation Summit 11.0 — TechTrends Africa, 10 October 2026

Lagos State's special adviser on technology, Ganiyu Oseni, delivered Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu's address to the 11th Nigeria Innovation Summit in Lagos on 6 October 2026, saying Nigeria should build AI rather than depend on computing done in Europe. The address cited US$3.9 billion of venture capital in five years, more than one million connections on the state's fibre network and a planned innovation bill.

Internet Code of Practice 2026

NCC Hosts Stakeholders To Chart Course For Future Of Internet, Launches Working Group — Independent Newspaper Nigeria, 15 October 2026

Nigeria's communications regulator launched an Internet Governance Working Group at its symposium in Ikeja on 15 October 2026. It said it will publish implementation guidelines and FAQs for the Internet Code of Practice and run workshops for licensees not yet ready to comply.

NCC survey finds gaps in licensees’ readiness for Internet code — Vanguard, 15 October 2026

An NCC survey found only two of 19 licensees fully ready to implement Nigeria's Internet Code of Practice 2026, with nine partly ready and five not prepared. Licensees named the cybersecurity and AI provisions as the hardest and asked for guidance and reporting templates.

Sector-specific regulations for AI in healthcare

FG moves to regulate AI use in healthcare, protect patients’ data — Nigerian Tribune, 21 October 2026

Nigeria's Minister of State for Health, Iziaq Adekunle Salako, told the AI in Health Summit 2026 in Abuja that the government is drafting sector-specific rules for AI in healthcare, building on the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 to cover health data, secondary use of clinical information, anonymisation and consent. He pointed to the National Digital Health Architecture endorsed by all 36 states and the FCT, a planned Health Information Exchange, and weak infrastructure, power and broadband as obstacles.

Draft executive bill on cybercrime and electronic evidence

NCC, NITDA, AGF, National Assembly demand stronger cybersecurity measures against AI-driven threats — Daily Post Nigeria, 27 October 2026

Nigeria's Attorney-General said the Federal Ministry of Justice has advanced a draft executive bill to strengthen the cybercrime legal framework. It covers emerging technologies and the preservation and exchange of electronic evidence. NITDA said it is reviewing cybercrime legislation with the Office of the National Security Adviser and the National Assembly.

National Payment Data Protection and Sovereignty Commission Bill

Banks, Fintechs Face N1bn Fine for Exporting Payment Data Under New Bill — Business Post Nigeria, 2 November 2026

A bill before Nigeria's House of Representatives, sponsored by Lanre Okunlola, would set up a National Payment Data Protection and Sovereignty Commission. Institutions moving payment data abroad would face a minimum fine of N1 billion. Individuals would face 10 years in prison and a minimum fine of N500 million.

National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill

Nigeria is rewriting its cyber laws, but can it secure its systems against hackers? — TechCabal, 6 November 2026

TechCabal reported on 6 November 2026 that Nigeria is reviewing its cybersecurity laws. It is considering a national cybersecurity architecture with baseline standards. It says the Cybercrimes Act of 2015 was amended in February 2024 and the 2021 national strategy is due for review. A House committee chair gave January to June 2027 as the expected enactment window for the bills.

Data protection

Lagos High Court judgment on Meta's behavioural advertising

Meta appeals Nigerian ruling restricting Facebook, Instagram Ad tracking — BusinessDay NG, 1 October 2026

Meta filed a notice of appeal on 30 September against a Lagos High Court judgment of 25 September that held its processing of Nigerian users' data for behavioural advertising unlawful. The court ordered it to stop, comply with the data protection Act within eight weeks and pay the naira equivalent of US$100,000 in damages.

NDPC and National Orientation Agency data-protection awareness MoU

Nigeria’s NDPC and NOA Sign MoU to Expand Data Protection Awareness — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026

Nigeria's data protection commission and the National Orientation Agency signed a memorandum on joint public awareness work. It covers training for the agency's staff, coordinated campaigns across traditional and digital media, and guidance on applying the 2023 data-protection Act to the agency's own mandate.

Registration of data controllers and processors of major importance

Federal High Court Upholds Mandatory Data Protection Registration for PoS Operators — Andersen Nigeria, 9 October 2026

Nigeria's Federal High Court in Lagos ruled on 17 July 2026 that the Nigeria Data Protection Commission acted within its powers in designating point-of-sale operators as data controllers of major importance. Such operators must therefore register with the commission and pay its fees. A PoS agent had argued the requirement breached his constitutional right to privacy.

America First Global Health Strategy MOU

Nigeria-US health deal under scrutiny — Trust Radio, 12 October 2026

Nigeria's five-year health memorandum with the United States, signed on 19 December 2025 and reported as worth about $5bn, came under scrutiny after Ghana's president said Ghana rejected a similar deal over data concerns. The deal requires Nigeria to share health data and specimens; Atiku Abubakar's campaign demanded the full text, doctors faulted a clause letting the US keep using shared data after termination, and the health ministry did not respond.

Nigeria's communications minister Bosun Tijani, speaking through NCAIR's director at BUILD 2026 in Abuja, proposed pooling GPU capacity with Rwanda, Senegal and Kenya so researchers could combine compute to train large models. Standards

Device Management System and Type Approval enforcement

Nigeria’s Phone Crackdown: Protecting Networks or Pricing Out Everyday Users? — Tech Africa News, 16 October 2026

From 10 October 2026, Nigeria's communications regulator began texting owners of phones not verified under type approval that network access could be blocked after 30 days of active use. The checks run on the NCC's Device Management System against each phone's IMEI, and grey-market and overseas-bought phones are among those flagged. A registration route and official fees had not been clearly published by 16 October.

Device Checker for type-approved handsets

NCC device verification questions Nigeria's phone imports — TechCabal, 20 October 2026

Nigeria's Communications Commission launched a Device Checker on 7 October 2026 that lets buyers dial *315# to see whether a phone is type approved, using its IMEI. An article on 20 October 2026 notes that 99% of smartphones are imported and about 63 million devices are sold each year, and questions how well the NCC can enforce compliance. Buyers of flagged phones have no standard refund route.

Device Checker: Nigeria introduces app to simplify phone verification — TechCabal, 29 October 2026

Nigeria's communications regulator, the NCC, announced a Device Checker app on 29 October 2026. Authorised manufacturers, importers, distributors and dealers use it to scan handset IMEIs and upload device data. The NCC is also licensing phone dealers in Kano State.

Why unverified phones could affect consumers, networks — Vanguard, 5 November 2026

Nigeria's Communications Commission urged buyers to check a phone on its Device Checker platform before paying, Vanguard reported on 5 November 2026. The NCC says about 231 million devices already on Nigerian networks were verified automatically before the framework took effect in October 2026. Zonal Controller Tunji Jimoh said the platform reads no personal data. The technical presentation, however, described linking a device's IMEI to subscriber identification.

NITDA Moves to Boost Digital Public Services as MDAs Begin IPv6 Migration Training — Techeconomy, 20 October 2026

Nigeria's NITDA began its first IPv6 migration readiness training for federal ministries, departments and agencies, with AFRINIC and the Nigeria IPv6 Council, as reported on 20 October 2026. Internet Exchange Point of Nigeria traffic

IXPN at 20: How Nigeria’s Internet Exchange Point Has Transformed Local Internet Connectivity — TechTV Network, 3 October 2026

The Internet Exchange Point of Nigeria, founded in 2006, is marking its 20th anniversary with a month-long programme; it says more than 130 networks use it, with points of presence in Lagos data centres, Abuja and Enugu. The programme includes a visit scheme for about 100 students, an IPv6 training programme with AFRINIC and the Nigeria IPv6 Council, and an awards dinner in Lagos on 30 October 2026.

Nigeria targets 90% local Internet traffic, regional hub power — The Guardian Nigeria, 4 November 2026

The Internet Exchange Point of Nigeria (IXPN) marked its 20th year with a goal of 90% local traffic by 2046. Chief executive Muhammed Rudman said up to 70% of traffic is now local, against 1% in 2006. The exchange has over 130 connected networks and plans 1.6T capacity trials in 2033-2036.

Nigeria’s internet capacity expands as data centre links hit 1Tbps - Businessday NG — BusinessDay Nigeria, 8 November 2026

The link between Digital Realty's Medallion data centre and Equinix in Lagos has reached 1 terabit per second. Medallion's link to Rack Centre runs at 500 gigabits per second. Muhammed Rudman, head of the Internet Exchange Point of Nigeria, gave the figures at its 20th anniversary event. BusinessDay reported them on 8 November 2026.

Nigeria's communications regulator issued the communique of its Digital Connectivity Investment Forum, held in Abuja on 29 and 30 September with Swedfund and Ookla. State right-of-way charges for fibre

Financial institutions' readiness for payments data localisation

A NITDA survey of 44 financial institutions found 22 had not begun meeting Nigeria's payment data localisation rules. The deadline is 1 January 2027. The survey was presented at a NITDA-CBN dialogue in Lagos. On their own answers, 59% of firms were on track and 34% at risk. Most asked for a definition of payment transaction data.

CBN warns banks on cybersecurity risks as cloud dependence grows — BusinessDay Nigeria, 28 October 2026

The Central Bank of Nigeria issued regulations for non-security virtual assets on 5 November 2026. The Central Bank of Nigeria released regulations on non-security virtual assets on the evening of 5 November 2026. Issuers of foreign-currency stablecoins must set up a licensed Nigerian entity with at least ₦15 billion in paid-up capital, and issuers of naira-backed stablecoins need ₦10 billion. Algorithmic stablecoins are banned, and existing operators have 180 days from the effective date to apply for a licence. See the Nigeria regulations entry for the full account.

Nigeria's Central Bank wants every crypto P2P payment linked to a bank transaction — TechCabal, 6 November 2026

The Central Bank of Nigeria's regulations of 5 November 2026 require peer-to-peer crypto platforms to route naira payments through banks or approved channels. Platforms must also verify traders' identities. A platform may not release a token until the name and Bank Verification Number on the paying account match its verified customer. Platforms must keep records linking each payment to its token transfer. Travel Rule information is required for transfers of $1,000 or more. See the Nigeria regulations entry for the full account.

Nigeria's crypto firms face $744,000 capital rule as CBN, SEC divide oversight — TechCabal, 6 November 2026

Under the Central Bank of Nigeria's regulations released on 5 November 2026, the bank oversees stablecoins used for payments, transfers and settlement. The Securities and Exchange Commission keeps trading and securities. A firm licensed by the Commission needs a separate central bank authorisation. It also needs at least ₦1 billion in additional capital for payment services, and the same capital cannot count twice. The Commission has raised the minimum capital for digital asset exchanges from ₦500 million to ₦2 billion. June 2027 is the deadline for existing operators. See the Nigeria regulations entry for the full account.
