Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Djibouti's digital position rests on transit. Twelve submarine cable systems land there, six connected to Italy, and what is built on that base is the part of the record with delivery behind it. Identity is the other: Mobile ID launched on 9 February 2026 over a biometric card issued nationwide since 2025.

The legal layer was built in a single push and is largely untested. The Digital Code was adopted on 30 June 2025 with a 156-article data-protection regime, and the Startup Act signed on 1 December 2025. No implementing decree under either is held, the data-protection commission has no record of being constituted, and the police wear body cameras as standard issue with no published instrument governing the footage.

The largest commitment on the ledger is not built: a memorandum of 19 July 2026 for a US$1.35bn AI compute centre and special economic zone, with nothing disbursed and no site on file.

The Digital Code of 30 June 2025 folds data protection, cybersecurity and electronic commerce into eight volumes and about 800 articles. Its 156-article privacy regime provides for 72-hour breach notification, mandatory data-protection officers, a public register and tiered prior authorisation over biometric processing.

None of that is evidenced in operation. The Commission nationale de protection des données it establishes has no record on file of its constitution, membership, register or decisions, and no implementing decree under the Code is held. The prior-authorisation tier has one concrete test available: the Police Nationale and road-safety officers carry body-worn cameras routinely in central Djibouti, recording triggered manually or automatically by intervention type or detected threat level, and no retention period, access rule, data controller or authorising instrument is published as at 4 August 2026. That account is the state daily's.

The other statutes are recent and equally undecreed. The Startup Act was signed on 1 December 2025, with every operative mechanism — the label, the portal, the fund of funds, the sandbox, the visa — deferred to decrees that are not held; the PND-ADEEG for 2025–2030 was adopted by decree on 30 June 2026 under Vision Djibouti 2035. Djibouti signed the United Nations Convention against Cybercrime in October 2025 and has filed no ratification instrument since.

Open data is strong on legal publication and thin elsewhere: no dataset inventory or update policy is held for either the Journal officiel or the IMF e-GDDS page. No civil-society, parliamentary or press-freedom account of digital governance in Djibouti is held at all, which is why every statement above rests on official and trade sources.

Five commitments to Djibouti's cable, financial-governance and higher-education systems are held from external financiers, each committed rather than disbursed and none with an implementation report on file: US$38,830,000 from the Islamic Development Bank for the DARE Regional Submarine Cable Project, 2019, US$20,000,000 from the World Bank for the Djibouti Economic and Financial Governance Project, 2025, and US$11,422,000 from the Agence Française de Développement for higher-education digitalisation, 2020. A Chinese grant for the Urban Security Monitoring System is recorded at US$23,046,763, against a record whose own title states US$20m; the two have not been reconciled. The Startup Act's fund of funds is provided for in articles 25-28, the management company still to be tendered, no capital committed.

No domestic figure had been on file against the 2026 loi de finances, which names no ICT ministry line, ANSIE allocation or e-government envelope. The Council of Ministers' approval of the ANSIE 2026 budget arrêté, 30 December 2025, puts the first domestic figure on record: 396,120,056 FDJ, down 160.6m FDJ from 556.7m FDJ in 2025, of which 108.1m FDJ is investment — application firewalls, an archiving system, datacentre colocation, and support renewals for datacentre equipment and X-Road — the balance largely staff costs. The Council of Ministers records the reduction against the creation of the Autorité Nationale de la Cybersécurité, which took over part of ANSIE's prior mandate. The arrêté itself is not published; this record is the strongest evidence reached.

Cybersecurity

International capacity is the country's strongest position and its most concentrated dependency: Med-Or, an Italian foundation, puts 12 cable systems landing there, six connected to Italy, carrying about 18% of international submarine cable data. Two extensions are contracted rather than in service: the Horizon Fiber corridor to Addis Ababa and Port Sudan, about 2,800 km on a binding agreement of 4 February 2026, and the southern extension of DARE1, for service in 2028.

The market behind the cables has one licensed operator: Djibouti Telecom, a statutory monopoly under loi 13/1998 and loi 80/2004. No authorisation for satellite broadband entrants is held: its chief executive stated in April 2025 that there is no clear framework for such players, and no licensing decision has followed.

Domestic reach runs through the World Bank-financed Djibouti Digital Foundation and EARDIP, which reported fibre at Ali-Sabieh regional hospital in June 2025 and Damerjog in October 2025. No national coverage figure is published.

Hosting is thin. Wingu's TO7 facility opened on 25 November 2024; the PAIX and Fonds Souverain joint venture of May 2024 has no groundbreaking or revised date on file, and no operator discloses rack count, megawatts or utilisation for any facility.

Cybersecurity has institutions ahead of evidence. The Autorité Nationale de Cybersécurité was created by Loi n° 195/AN/25 of 15 December 2025, replacing a pre-statutory label, and its DJ-CERT team took part in the IGAD exercise of June 2026, where the state daily reports it placed first in both scenarios; the organiser published no result.

The biometric identity card has been issued nationwide since 2025, enrolling fingerprints, iris, photograph and electronic signature against a national identification number, with mobile kits for the elderly, hospitalised and remote. Mobile ID launched on 9 February 2026 on top of it, biometric data held only at the Interior Ministry. Interoperability with driving-licence and health records, and verification for banks and social protection, are in preparation with no date.

D-Money has run since June 2020, with an online gateway from 10 December 2023; the Smart Wallet with Visa and Tech5 remains a memorandum of 16 September 2025 with nothing in service, its 60% cash-reduction target the parties' own.

In trade and company formation, Djibouti Port Community Systems is the mandatory national platform for import, export and transit data and customs runs on ASYCUDAWorld, which the 2026 budget law constituted as the compulsory e-Guichet single window, interconnected with the treasury, alongside orders for a central-administration data-exchange platform and a prices database — none of them dated or funded in the text. A platform for online company creation opened in January 2026; the broader G2B platform is still in design.

The state's own records are digitised on a state-funded programme running since 2023, which reports over 200,000 secured digital files for the Presidency and a UNDP accord of 2 August 2026. Delivery is asserted by the parties, with no retention schedule or access rule stated. SNDS-3, the statistics strategy for 2025–2029, was validated on 29 April 2025 with no adoption instrument held.

Djibouti has an applied AI system in service before it has a strategy or an instrument for it. The MAZU weather early-warning system, supplied by the China Meteorological Administration, was upgraded to version 2.0 in July 2026, sharpening forecast resolution to 3 km from 9 km. A first national AI strategy has been in preparation since 27 January 2026 with UNESCO and UN ESCWA; no strategy document is held, and no AI law, decree or procurement rule is on file.

The compute the strategy would govern is being contracted in parallel. The signing minister named a cross-border data corridor between Shanghai and Djibouti as a success indicator of the LinkWise memorandum, alongside full Djiboutian jurisdiction over data generated on the territory; the corridor, the capital, the chips and the operating company are all foreign, and the memorandum text is not held.

The domestic sector is small and newly organised: the ministry launched the LIMO platform in July 2026 with no user figures published, and a federation of digital enterprises was reported in creation in December 2025 without a published constitution or membership. The Startup Act's sandbox and startup visa exist in the statute and nowhere else. No India–Djibouti digital agreement and no Gulf-state investment in digital infrastructure is held.

Connectivity reaches most of the population. The gaps cluster in the distance between an instrument existing and one operating. Four concern machinery the Digital Code and the Startup Act created — the data-protection commission, the implementing decrees, the rule governing body-camera footage, and any instrument covering artificial intelligence. Two of the four, the commission and the decrees, were probed on 9 August 2026 and returned nothing. The domestic-budget gap that stood alongside them was settled outside the probe cycle: the Council of Ministers' record of the ANSIE 2026 budget arrêté, approved 30 December 2025, put a figure on file, and the row is no longer carried here. The other eight were not probed.
