Djibouti: monthly update, September – October 2026

Edition of 2026-10-01  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Developments summarised from sources published between the beginning of last month and today.
Summary of the month · Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics

Summary of the month

The first national strategy was presented on 3 September for 2026 to 2030, alongside a readiness assessment scoring the country about 24.5 out of 100 — the first such measurement the repository holds.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

The interoperability framework acquired a statement of intent for the first time since it was published in 2022. The Council of Ministers' 2026 work programme of 1 September names paperless services and interoperability among the state-modernisation priorities. Nothing in it evidences the framework in use, and no system, timetable or budget is attached — so what has changed is that the government has said the word, not that anything is interoperable.

Regional collaboration

In September the Digital Cooperation Organization's secretary-general and the digital-economy minister discussed cooperation on artificial intelligence, digital skills and capacity building; no memorandum, programme or budget is stated, and the repository holds no accession date for Djibouti's membership.

Finance

New investments

The investment on offer was estimated rather than committed. A country private sector diagnostic identified at least US$600 million of private investment and about 12,000 jobs available over five years across off-grid solar energy, data centres and tourism, with the policy actions that would bring them about. Nothing in it is a transaction.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

On 7 September the Djiboutian, Ethiopian and Sudanese operators presented the Horizon terrestrial fibre corridor to Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, other carriers and equipment makers at a pre-commercialisation meeting in Addis Ababa, seeking partners, customers and technical feedback before deployment. The account gives no cost, financing or ready-for-service date, and no attendee commitment.

DPI

Data Exchange

Customs has begun preparing to put trade procedures on one platform. The administration set out an electronic single-window project at a preparatory workshop on 1 September, saying it goes beyond strengthening the declaration system customs currently uses and would shorten processing times, increase transparency and make operations more predictable for traders, working with the commerce ministry, the continental free trade secretariat and a Korean agency that has done the same in three other African countries. No design, budget, procurement or timetable is published, and no baseline clearance time exists to measure the shortening against.

Technology

AI

The national strategy in preparation since January was presented. The ministry convened cross-ministerial focal points on 15 August to prepare the country's first national artificial-intelligence forum, which opened at the Palais du Peuple on the morning of 2 September under the President's patronage. On 3 September the strategy's main lines were presented for 2026 to 2030 on six axes - data and AI governance, cybersecurity, training, research, innovation and inclusion. Its text is not published.

The readiness assessment presented with it, run on UNESCO's methodology, rates the country emerging, at about 24.5 out of 100 on AI readiness against an ICT development index of 61.6, and finds no law dealing specifically with artificial intelligence. The report itself is not held.

The forum also carried a first national AI prototyping competition, putting young Djiboutians' projects in front of it. No entrant count, prize or follow-on support is published.

Capacity

Training and skills

Two cohorts finished. Ten children aged 9 to 16 completed seven weeks of coding, robotics and artificial intelligence at the national technology and innovation centre on 5 September, and Djibouti Telecom certified 35 of its engineers in artificial intelligence on 17 September. Neither account gives a cost.

Inclusion

Access to services

The corridor programme put training against formalisation rather than connectivity: forty women traders took export-readiness training at Bishoftu on 30 and 31 July 2026 under a EUR 32m European corridor programme, covering business formalisation, product standards, export documentation and customs procedure. The training was delivered on the Ethiopian side of the corridor and no Djiboutian cohort, customs-system component or digital element is separately costed.

Data

Use of satellite data

The month's movement was political rather than technical. The head of state took part on 10 September in the second day of the first International Space Summit at the Grand Palais in Paris, convened at the French president's invitation and gathering delegations from more than 120 countries, arguing for a space ambition serving development. State media used the occasion to set out the programme's own record since 2020: first specialists trained, two nanosatellites designed and put into orbit, ground capacity developed and cooperation established with major French space institutions. No satellite name, launch date, ground-station location, budget or data-use record accompanies the account, and the summit produced no commitment the repository can read.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

The communications minister received the United States ambassador on 31 August to discuss American investment in new technologies and telecommunications, and training for media professionals. It was a courtesy call: no instrument, sum or timetable followed. On 7 September Google, Meta, Microsoft and Amazon were among the prospective partners and customers shown the Horizon terrestrial fibre corridor in Addis Ababa, and none of them is reported to have committed to it.

China activities

The month's only movement here is a conversation. Djibouti discussed the development of its digital infrastructure with Huawei, against its position on the telecommunications routes between Africa, Asia and the Middle East and its stated ambition to be a regional digital hub. Nothing held records an agreement, a project or a value behind it.
