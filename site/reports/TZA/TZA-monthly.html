Legislation and regulation

Accreditation of content creators doing journalistic work

Content creators in Tanzania face media rules as role in news grows — The Citizen, 17 September 2026

Tanzania's journalists accreditation board said content creators doing journalistic work fall under the Media Services Act, so that none may report news or conduct interviews without accreditation, which requires at least a diploma. The board also said a creator doing journalism must register their platform with the communications regulator as a media outlet.

Digital service tax on foreign digital service providers

The Citizen Budget 2026/27 — Ministry of Finance, United Republic of Tanzania, 22 September 2026

Tanzania's finance ministry published its Citizen Budget for 2026/27, a 34-page plain-language account of spending of TZS 54.50 trillion, revenue sources, deficit and financing. Its revenue measures include raising the digital service tax on foreign digital service providers from 2% to 3%. The document carries no issue date.

Business Licensing (Amendment) Regulations 2026

Digital Payments Now a Business Licensing Requirement — FB Attorneys, 9 October 2026

Law firm FB Attorneys notes that Tanzania's Business Licensing (Amendment) Regulations, 2026, published as Government Notice 158N on 30 June and in force from 1 July 2026, require applicants for new or renewed business licences to show they can take digital payments, such as Lipa Namba or TANQR. Businesses in minerals, livestock, agricultural produce, timber and fisheries must also show a bank account.
