Compiled 2026-08-19 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 128 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 59 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-19, 19 did not, 37 carry no stated baseline, and 13 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 163 sources for this place in the window — 34 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-02), 129 in the later (2026-03 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The largest measured movements of the year were in payments. Interoperable instant transactions went from 453m worth TZS 29.82tn in 2024 to 651m worth TZS 54.95tn in 2025, active mobile-money accounts from 63.2m to 75.8m, and a mandatory electronic-payments order that did not exist at the start of the window came into force on 1 July 2026.

The identity register moved on counts rather than on coverage: 25.8m registered and 21,514,741 numbers issued in April 2025 became 26.4m registered, 22.3m issued and 20.6m cards distributed by December, and the share of eligible adults went from 81.9% to about 81% against a growing denominator.

Two positions worsened on their own measures: smartphone users fell from 29.8m to 28.5m while penetration rose, and startups raising disclosed funding went from six raising US$41,250,000 in 2024 to three raising US$3,706,063 in 2025.

The backbone advanced and its uptake did not. Laid distance went from 13,820 km of a 16,280 km planned total across 109 of 139 districts at April 2025 to 15,167 km across 122 of 139 districts at December 2025, and the figure the ministry was given for one region was 942.5 km built against 65 citizens connected. Alongside it, 758 towers and about 1,100 km of fibre were transferred to government in April 2026, having been under construction since May 2023.

Commercial capacity moved further. 5G facilities went from 1,255 to 1,804 on 10,084 towers, and population coverage from 30.13% to 32.83%. The state operator's fibre-to-the-home target slipped from one million customers by 2027 to one million by 2028, with rollout begun across seven areas. Zanzibar's broadband partnership went from an unpriced memorandum to a twenty-year agreement worth TZS 300bn targeting about 100,000 households, with nothing yet on record being built.

Three positions did not move at all: the Mbezi data centre, unchanged since March 2025; a cyber-skills partnership with South Korea, with no trainee numbers reported in twelve months; and the national cybersecurity forum, which has recorded no fifth edition.

Identity integration is the strongest movement in this section: institutions connected to the register's verification database went from 128 to more than 138 across banking, insurance and social security. The build behind it moved from a first phase already delivered to a second phase tendered for iris and facial biometrics, a headquarters and offices in 31 districts on an external loan of about US$70m. In payments, the core mobile-money platform was replaced with one specified at more than 1,000 transactions a second and a 99.999% availability target, and the rails acquired both a mandate and a price ceiling inside the last five weeks of the window.

Two exchange-layer positions are unchanged and one is unmeasured. Jamii X-Change is still under construction with no go-live date; the health management information system's most recent held state is from October 2023; and the government service bus gained external validation without any published count of institutions that joined by its July 2025 deadline.

Data-protection enforcement moved from dormant to active without producing an outcome. At the start of the window the registration deadline had expired and commission activity was confined to training and awareness; by the end there was a fresh deadline of 8 April 2026, nationwide inspections announced from the following day and a seven-day ultimatum to part-registered bodies. No count of registered controllers and no inspection outcome, fine or decision is held. The Act itself is unchanged, with no amendment or new regulations in the window.

Access to the network was restricted and the record of it is entirely third-party. No restriction was in force at 1 August 2025; a monitoring organisation records eight shutdowns across 2025, including a five-day nationwide blackout at the October election, with no restoration date, no gazetted instrument and no regulator order held for any of them.

The National Data Management Framework did not move: stakeholder drafting was under way for a January 2026 target and the base holds no record of completion or slippage past it.

The one priced arrival is a US$25m credit facility, extendable to US$50m, for remittance and stablecoin corridors. The largest commitment in the base, the US$150m Digital Tanzania Project, did not move and is scheduled to close in 2026 with no closure recorded.
