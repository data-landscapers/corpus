This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-04.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy The ENTD.GW strategy was launched in January 2025 with ten pillars and 55 interventions to 2030, and Council of Ministers approval is still pending.
Full record2025-01-29 — the National Digital Transformation Strategy 2025-2030 was launched by the prime minister and the minister responsible for digital transformation, with UNDP support: ten pillars, six objectives, 55 interventions and a roadmap to 2030.

2025-02-04 — a UNU-EGOV and UNDP follow-up meeting recorded that Council of Ministers approval of the strategy's decree was still pending, which is where the base leaves it: nothing in the twelve months to 2026-08-26 records the decree approved, gazetted or brought into force.

The strategy is the instrument the World Bank-financed WARDIP programme and the UNDP Mais Digit@l grant both implement, so the financing behind it is moving while the legal instrument is not.

Data protection Data protection authority A National Cybersecurity and Data Protection Agency was committed to in January 2025; nothing on the base confirms it exists.
Full record2025-01-22 — the government presented the results of a WARDIP cybersecurity feasibility study and committed to creating a National Cybersecurity and Data Protection Agency, framed by the minister responsible as central to data protection and digital sovereignty.

The commitment predates the window and no source since confirms the agency established, staffed or operational. The country therefore has no supervisory authority on the record, and the draft data governance and protection decree approved in July 2025 is not confirmed in force either, so neither the body nor the instrument it would enforce is in place.
 No change
Data protection National data protection readiness Readiness rests on a draft policy decree and an agency still only committed to, with no enacted statute behind either.
Full recordThe base holds no data protection statute for Guinea-Bissau. What it holds instead is a policy decree approved in draft on 10 July 2025 and not confirmed in force, and an agency committed to in January 2025 and not confirmed established.

Neither moved in the twelve months to 2026-08-26. No registration or notification regime, no data subject rights case, no enforcement action and no cross-border transfer rule appears on the ledger.

The biometric identity network, the civil registry digitisation and the electoral register update reported elsewhere in this report are all proceeding without any of it, which is what the absence costs.
 No change
Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration Guinea-Bissau joined AUDA-NEPAD's programme for African developing island economies in July 2026, whose roadmap to 2031 includes digital transformation.
Full record2026-07-24 — at the ministerial segment in Abidjan, AUDA-NEPAD launched a Programme for African Developing Island Economies covering Guinea-Bissau among seven states, with ministers adopting a communique committing to a 2026-2031 roadmap on six pillars including digital transformation.

It is a continental commitment rather than a national action, and no country-level implementation under it is reported. The country's substantive regional work sits elsewhere on this ledger — the ECOWAS biometric credential and the World Bank's West Africa regional digital programme — and neither is an AUDA-NEPAD instrument.

No ECOWAS or UEMOA policy instrument adopted domestically appears in the window.

Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure The ECOWAS biometric identity card reached its first reported field issuance, 262 cards to women cross-border traders in Sao Domingos.
Full record2026-03-16 — the National Borders Commission issued 262 ECOWAS biometric identity cards to women cross-border traders in Sao Domingos, Cacheu, under a Guinea-Bissau-Senegal mobility project backed by ECOWAS and financed by GIZ. It is the first field distribution the base records.

The standing position is that Guinea-Bissau is one of six ECOWAS states to have fully operationalised the credential, so what moved in the window is use rather than adoption.

The only other shared regional asset on the ledger is the ACE submarine cable, reported under international bandwidth. No regional data centre, internet exchange point or interconnection arrangement appears.

Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy The transitional parliament passed a law on the dissemination of false information online, and the government first invoked it in July 2026.
Full record2026-05-23 — the transitional parliament passed unanimously, with amendments, a Digital Integrity law on the dissemination of false information on the internet, presented by the government spokesman as aimed at those who spread hate and act against the State. The publication date, the entry-into-force date and the enforcement mechanism — including how it would reach citizens abroad — are not stated in the record.

2026-07-21 — the Ministry of Communication invoked the law for the first time the base records, in denying a claim that TikTok was to be shut down.

It is the only instrument on this ledger addressing online speech, and nothing records a court, a regulator or a civil-society challenge testing it. No consultation on the bill appears either.

New investments Mobilisation of non-state finance A MIGA guarantee covers Sonatel's equity investment in Orange Bissau at US$25.4m, and no new private commitment appears in the window.
Full recordThe standing position is a US$25,435,357 MIGA guarantee to Sonatel SA covering its equity investment in Orange Bissau S.A., approved for the 2025 financial year as part of a combined Orange Guinea and Guinea-Bissau programme, with nothing disbursed as captured.

Nothing in the twelve months to 2026-08-26 records a disbursement, a new guarantee or a second private transaction. The one other private commitment on the ledger is the operator-backed training programme reported under digital literacy, which states no financial value.

The fibre backbone the country's connectivity depends on is to be built by a private wholesale operator under exclusive licence, and that operator has not been selected — so the largest private commitment in prospect is not yet a transaction.
 No change
New investments Development-partner project financing A World Bank WARDIP grant and a UNDP digital transformation programme are the standing development-partner financing, neither reporting new movement in the window.
Full recordTwo commitments stand. The World Bank's Guinea-Bissau allocation under the regional WARDIP programme is US$60.00m in IDA grant plus US$0.50m of government counterpart, launched in country in April 2025 and carried in the Bank's own project record. UNDP's Mais Digit@l programme committed US$3,360,500 to implement the national digital transformation strategy, of which US$566,152 is recorded disbursed.

No fresher disbursement figure, extension or successor appears in the twelve months to 2026-08-26, and no third development partner appears on the ledger. What this money moved is reported elsewhere: the backbone design and its safeguards study, and the interoperability regulation.

Connectivity Internet usage Internet use was last measured at 32.5% penetration, 723,000 users, with two-thirds of the population offline, before the window opened.
Full record2025-03-03 — the last measurement the base holds puts internet users at 723,000 and penetration at 32.5%, leaving 67.5% of the population offline.

The figure predates the twelve-month window and nothing fresher was published into it: no regulator return, no operator disclosure and no household survey covering internet use appears for Guinea-Bissau. The census reported under Censuses and surveys is the one national data collection inside the window, and its preliminary release carries no connectivity variable.

So the direction of internet use over the window is unmeasured, at the point where the national backbone is still in design and satellite is the only service to have entered the market.

Connectivity Mobile affordability No evidence
Connectivity National fibre backbone The WARDIP-GB backbone reached its environmental and social safeguards study in May 2026, on a 2,877 km, 56-node design not yet built.
Full recordThe country has no national fibre backbone. The design for one specifies 2,877 km over 56 nodes in four phases, targeting coverage of more than 99% of the population and operation by a private wholesale operator under exclusive licence; that specification is itself undated and carried at year precision.

2026-05-21 — the environmental and social impact study was published, which is the movement in the window: the project passes from design toward implementation procurement.

The starting position was thinner. In August 2025 the country was seeking deployment experience from Chad with no fixed design published, after the programme's launch in April 2025. No construction contract, no selected wholesale operator and no built phase appears on the ledger.
 Advanced
Connectivity International internet bandwidth The ACE submarine cable remains the country's only international fibre link, with a second, ECOWAS-led cable approved at feasibility stage in October 2025.
Full recordInternational capacity rests on a single submarine cable. The ACE landing is the country's first and only international fibre-optic connection, live since March 2023, and it is held only as background in a secondary record: no primary on the landing itself is on the base.

2025-10-22 - member-state experts with their technical and financial partners reviewed and approved the feasibility study for a second submarine cable serving Cabo Verde, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea-Bissau, at a workshop the ECOWAS Project Preparation and Development Unit convened in Monrovia.

That cable is at feasibility and nothing further: no route, cost, landing site or construction date is published. Nor does any capacity, price or utilisation figure for the existing link appear on the ledger, so the base can state what carries the traffic and not how much it carries.

The national backbone that would distribute that capacity inland is still in design.

Connectivity Satellite broadband licensing and availability Starlink has been commercially live since June 2025 under a provisional authorisation from the regulator, and nothing moved in the window.
Full record2025-06-19 — Starlink entered service, Guinea-Bissau's seventh West African and twentieth continental market, following a provisional authorisation from ARN-TIC in April 2025. Both events fall before the twelve-month window.

Nothing since records a definitive licence, a second satellite operator, a subscriber figure or a tariff, so what the base holds is the fact of availability and nothing about its reach. An initial provisional licence referred to in the launch record is not itself held.

Satellite is the only service reaching what the absent national backbone does not, which is what makes the missing uptake figure matter.

Cybersecurity National cybersecurity readiness A national cybersecurity strategy sits in a draft decree and the agency to run it is still only committed to.

Digital Identity and CRVS Robustness of system A third production centre in Biombo took the national network to 18, and the ECOWAS credential reached first field issuance.
Full record2026-02-11 — the contractor Semlex opened a production centre at Safim in Biombo, its third in that region and rated at 100 cards a day, bringing the national network to 18 biometric identity production centres, with further centres planned for Sao Domingos and Bolama.

2026-03-16 — the ECOWAS biometric card was issued in the field for the first time the base records, 262 cards to women cross-border traders in Sao Domingos.

Beneath both is a standing position of a functional biometric national identity card system, with Guinea-Bissau one of six ECOWAS states to have fully operationalised the regional credential. What the ledger does not hold is an enrolment total, a coverage rate, an authentication service or any legal instrument governing the system: the movement is in production capacity, not in what the credential does.

Digital Payments and Fintech Population uptake Active electronic-money accounts grew 33.28% in 2024, the union's second-fastest rate, on a regional report published in January 2026.
Full record2026-01-01 — the regional central bank's annual report on digital financial services records Guinea-Bissau's active electronic-money accounts up 33.28% in 2024, the union's second-highest growth rate after Togo, on a 1.94% share of accounts opened union-wide and about 1% of regional transaction value, served by two electronic-money issuing initiatives of the 69 operating across the union.

The data year is 2024 and the publication falls inside the window, so what the window holds is the arrival of the figure rather than the growth itself. These are shares within a regional report and not a national release: the base holds no account total, no transaction count and no payments statistic published in Guinea-Bissau.

No named mobile-money service, no interoperable switch and no consumer-protection rule for payments appears anywhere on this ledger.

Registries Civil register Birth registration stands at about 46% of under-fives, with SIREC digitising births, deaths and marriages and no fresher figure since.
Full recordThe Ministry of Justice's civil registration office describes SIREC digitising births, deaths and marriages with UNDP-trained officers, roughly three months into a Bissau-first rollout, against a target of at least 90% registration, with registration of under-fives at about 46% from an undated 23.4% baseline. A parallel account covers the digitisation of civil identification, registry and notary services.

Behind them, a UNDP project co-financed by Switzerland built a digitisation centre and software to recover the paper civil-registry books, held at year precision.

All of it predates the twelve-month window. No dated confirmation of regional extension, no fresher registration rate and no death or marriage registration figure appears in it.

Registries Electoral register The updated electoral register was handed to the elections commission on 18 August 2026 recording 966,152 voters, unchanged in size from the last update.
Full record2026-08-18 — the electoral register was handed to the elections commission in both physical and electronic form ahead of the 30 August referendum, recording 966,152 registered voters.

2026-08-17 — the operation behind it issued 25,146 replacement voter cards over thirty days from 13 July, worked by 51 brigades covering every region.

The electorate total is unchanged from the previous update, so the operation replaced cards rather than added registrants. No audit, no de-duplication statement and no link between the register and the biometric identity system is published. It is the only register on this ledger that moved in the window.

Literacy Digital literacy (general population) A government and operator programme stood up its coordination committee in May 2026, to train 1,000 young people a year for four years.

National statistics Censuses and surveys The fourth census released preliminary results in July 2026 putting the population at 2,191,202, in the country's first fully digital count.
Full record2026-07-23 — the national statistics institute released preliminary results of the fourth General Population and Housing Census: a population of 2,191,202, 50.7% women and 45.7% urban, across 304,939 households and 559,303 dwellings, against 1,449,230 at the 2009 count.

It is the country's first fully digital census, collected by computer-assisted personal interview and leaving a digital cartography and technology base for future operations, with UNFPA as lead technical agency and World Bank support for the statistical capacity behind it.

Full tabulations are pending. One record's headline population differs from its own body and from the other two, and the base resolves it against them. No statistics law, no national strategy for the development of statistics and no other survey appears on this ledger.
