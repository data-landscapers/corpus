Strategies, plans and policies

National artificial intelligence strategy

Uganda Targets AI Sovereignty, Minister Says — Nile Post, 10 September 2026

Uganda is developing an artificial intelligence strategy aimed at building national capacity to develop, govern and benefit from the technology rather than depend on foreign systems, its science and technology minister said. He named data, computing infrastructure, energy, models, applications and skills as the foundations that need investment.

Domestic taxes compliance risk management strategy

URA unveils data driven tax compliance strategy for FY2026/27 — Eagle Online, 14 September 2026

The Uganda Revenue Authority began implementing its taxpayer compliance risk management strategy and compliance improvement plan for 2026/27. It will use taxpayer records, transaction data and sector trends to target evasion, inaccurate declarations, non-registration and late filing, and prioritises compliance with the EFRIS e-invoicing system and digital tax stamps.

National Communication Policy, 2026

Government Launches National Communication Policy 2026 — Ministry of ICT and National Guidance (Uganda), 13 October 2026

Uganda's Minister of ICT and National Guidance, Justine Kasule Lumumba, launched the National Communication Policy, 2026 in Kampala on 13 October 2026. It sets common communication standards for ministries, agencies and local governments, covering crisis communication, access to public information and citizen feedback, and requires each to adopt and budget for an aligned communication strategy.

National Data Strategy

Uganda launches National Data Strategy — New Vision, 20 October 2026

New Vision's account of the National Data Strategy's launch in Kampala on 20 October 2026, giving the spend by area: UGX 684.9 billion for data infrastructure and 553 billion for data governance. The strategy itself is summarised under the ministry's document.

Uganda National Data Strategy 2026/27–2030/31 — Ministry of ICT and National Guidance, Uganda, 20 October 2026

Uganda's Ministry of ICT and National Guidance published the National Data Strategy 2026/27–2030/31, costed at UGX 1,662.6 billion over five years, mostly from government budgets with UGX 333.6 billion expected from selling data services. It rests on data governance, data infrastructure and use of data, overseen by a steering committee chaired by the finance ministry and run by a National Data Office in the ICT ministry.

UBOS rejects new national data strategy, labels ICT Ministry launch 'illegal' and 'redundant' — The Independent (Uganda), 21 October 2026

The Uganda Bureau of Statistics publicly rejected the ICT ministry's National Data Strategy on 21 October 2026, a day after its launch, calling it illegal, irregular and redundant. Its executive director said the strategy breaches the UBOS Act, which makes the Bureau the coordinator of the National Statistical System, and that existing statistics plans already cover its ground.

Legislation and regulation

UCC order suspending public internet

Uganda Communications Commission explains internet shutdown — New Vision, 7 September 2026

Uganda’s communications regulator published the text of its suspension directive: public internet, new SIM sales and outbound data roaming stopped, mobile VPNs disabled, a whitelisted exclusion list from referral hospitals to railway signalling, and restoration only on written notice.

Digital lending platform regulation

Uganda's central bank to probe claims mobile money users are paying off strangers' loans — Kikubolane, 15 September 2026

Bank of Uganda governor Michael Atingi-Ego told a parliamentary committee on 14 September 2026 that the bank will investigate claims that mobile money operators it supervises, MTN and Airtel, helped digital lenders recover unpaid loans from the wallets of people listed as guarantors without consent. Standalone digital lenders fall under the finance ministry, not the central bank.

MTN, Airtel deny deducting customers' money without consent — PML Daily, 16 September 2026

MTN Uganda and Airtel Uganda told Parliament's public enterprises committee, reported on 16 September 2026, that they do not deduct customers' money without consent or require loan guarantors, and that they only provide platforms for licensed lenders. They asked the Uganda Communications Commission to block unlicensed lending apps. The committee has summoned 58 online lending companies over recoveries from people named as guarantors without consent.

Restriction of repeat online offenders

Uganda proposes stricter measures against serial cyber offenders — CAJ News Africa, 15 September 2026

The communications regulator said it is working with parliament on a framework allowing targeted denial of service to repeat abusers of cyberspace, its director likening the measure to withdrawing a driving licence. No bill, offence definition or appeal route is published.

UCC enforcement against unlicensed public Wi-Fi

UCC Reviews Crackdown on Unauthorised Wi-Fi Operators After Complaints — Nile Post, 12 October 2026

Uganda's communications regulator said it is reviewing its enforcement against unauthorised Wi-Fi and hotspot resellers after complaints from operators. A public notice was promised within the week.

Press Release on Illegal Wi-Fi Hotspots — Uganda Communications Commission, 13 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission said on 13 October 2026 that about 4,000 unlicensed operators resell public Wi-Fi at UGX 1,000 per GB against UGX 2,000 from licensed providers, costing about UGX 148 billion a year in tax. The Uganda Revenue Authority will block equipment imports by unlicensed operators, and a nationwide exercise to take down installations and seize routers is imminent.

UCC Tightens Licensing Rules for Wi-Fi Hotspot Operators — Nile Post, 20 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission said on 20 October 2026 that commercial Wi-Fi hotspot resellers must be licensed and must stop reselling home internet packages. Its seven-day deadline for unlicensed operators ended on 19 October 2026. It will open a registration system by service category and set up a joint committee with government, security agencies and seven hotspot operators.

UCC Moves to Tighten Monitoring of Internet Data Resellers — Nile Post, 22 October 2026

The Uganda Communications Commission agreed with mobile operators and licensed internet providers in ISPAU to tighten tracking of data consumption and billing, aimed at unlicensed resellers of bulk connectivity over public Wi-Fi. Eligible resellers are to be brought into licensing through onboarding guidelines, and illegal access points and routers removed; no date or design for the monitoring was given.

E-commerce trust mark and online consumer redress

MINISTRY OF TRADE TO INTRODUCE DIGITAL MARK FOR ONLINE CONSUMER PROTECTION — Uganda Media Centre, 15 October 2026

Uganda's trade minister Sanjay Tanna told a press conference on 15 October 2026 that his ministry will introduce a digital mark for online sellers to protect consumers, expected within a few months. The ministry is also building a database of sellers.

Uganda Champions AU single digital market plan to boost cross-border trade — Daily Monitor, 16 October 2026

Ahead of a technology and intellectual property symposium on 22-23 October, Uganda's trade minister set out plans to back the AU's single digital market, including a trust mark for vetted online sellers and rules giving online buyers redress, to be rolled out in the EAC first. A Jumia-led e-commerce industry association and a call for a Consumer Protection Act were also presented.

Govt welcomes Starlink internet service provider — New Vision, 2 September 2026

Starlink began commercial service in Uganda, ending the regulatory process that followed a memorandum and a five-year operating licence signed with the communications regulator in May 2026. The licence requires a national gateway and physical point of presence, an operational office, and registration of every device activated in the country.

Starlink goes live in Uganda; price comparisons against MTN, Airtel — Pulse Uganda, 2 September 2026

Prices published with the Ugandan launch put a residential 100 Mbps package at Shs203,704 a month against an estimated Shs2.3 million to start, plus a Shs437,036 regulatory charge — against fibre offers from the two mobile incumbents advertised at Shs110,000 and Shs99,000 for the same speed.

Uganda-Tanzania fibre route on the transmission network

UETCL in Spotlight Over Uganda-Tanzania Fibre Connection — Nile Post, 4 September 2026

Uganda's parliamentary committee on state enterprises questioned the Uganda Electricity Transmission Company (UETCL) over the cancellation of open bidding for a partner to commercialise its fibre network. The energy ministry directed the termination in 2026 and Samanga Utilities was then considered for a Uganda–Tanzania fibre link, following a presidential directive of August 2025 seeking cheaper internet routes than those through Kenya.

Paratus Goma-Mombasa route

Paratus expands East Africa connectivity ahead of ITW Africa — TechTrendsKE, 9 September 2026 DRCRwandaKenya

Paratus said its 2,000km Goma-to-Mombasa terrestrial fibre route is live and carrying traffic, announced at the ITW and Datacloud Africa conference in Nairobi. The route links eastern DRC to the Kenyan coast through Kigali, Kampala and Nairobi, with access to subsea capacity landing at Mombasa. Starlink services have launched in Uganda alongside it, and the company is introducing a Layer 2 connectivity service over low-earth-orbit satellite for the East African market.

Airtel satellite-to-mobile service with Starlink

Airtel Uganda Rolls Out Starlink Satellite-to-Mobile Service — Tech Africa News, 24 September 2026

Airtel Uganda launched Starlink satellite-to-mobile service, giving compatible smartphones SMS and light data such as WhatsApp outside terrestrial coverage, free for a 30-day trial and then through data bundles. Uganda is Airtel Africa's second commercial market for the service after the DRC in August.

Uganda's President Yoweri Museveni ordered the cancellation of the government's agreement with Rowad Capital Commercial to revive Uganda Telecom at a State House meeting on 6 October 2026, according to unnamed meeting sources. UGPass

NITA-U moves to replace paper-based land verification with digital checks — New Vision, 17 September 2026

Uganda's IT authority is building a digital signature and document-authentication service on the national authentication platform, binding a signature cryptographically to the holder's national identification number so land sale agreements, transfer forms and consent letters can be signed digitally. The integration platform is to carry real-time verification to the registration authority, so a lands office checks the register rather than a photocopied card; forged signatures in land transactions are the stated first target.

National identification number and the taxpayer number

What 'NIN Will Replace TIN in All Transactions' Actually Means — Business Times Uganda, 1 September 2026

Uganda's cabinet approved phasing out the standalone taxpayer identification number for individuals, replacing it with the national identification number already printed on the identity card. Nothing states a commencement date or a transition arrangement for the roughly nine to ten million Ugandans not yet on the register.

Minister Musasizi Explains Why NIN Is to Be Used as TIN as ICPAU Commits to Enforce AI Adoption in Accountancy Sector — Mulengera News, 3 September 2026

Uganda's finance minister, Henry Musasizi, said on 3 September 2026 that Cabinet's decision of 31 August does not replace the tax identification number with the national identification number, but requires the two to be interoperable. He said linking them would show who of taxable age and income is missing from the tax register.

URA Explains Use of NINs to Replace TINs — Nilepost, 7 September 2026

Uganda's revenue authority says the move from taxpayer numbers to national identification numbers will not automatically make every citizen a taxpayer, the purpose being a single more secure identifier for government services and more accurate taxpayer records. It follows the finance minister's statement four days earlier that the two numbers must be interoperable rather than one replacing the other.

Transitioning from TIN to NIN will create jobs and open up formal markets — New Vision, 16 September 2026

Uganda's revenue authority said the taxpayer identification number will be phased out for individuals in favour of the national identification number, with companies using their registration number instead. It said the old system reached only just over 5 million taxpayers and that the change will remove duplicate and ghost records.

Government launches integration of National ID, tax identification systems — NTV Uganda, 30 September 2026

Uganda's internal affairs minister launched the integration of the national identification and tax identification systems at the country's first Identity Expo and Symposium in Kampala.

Linking NINs to Tax Records Will Tackle Identity Theft, Says URA — Nile Post, 30 September 2026

The Uganda Revenue Authority is moving to the National Identification Number as individuals' tax identifier, first mapping existing tax numbers to NINs, then taking fingerprint consent at URA service points. Basic biodata such as name, date of birth and photograph is drawn from the national identity authority's servers. Companies receive an entity number from the business registry, URSB.

More than 3mn tax records await NIN match before login switch — Uganda Business News, 17 October 2026

Uganda Revenue Authority had linked 2.13 million of its 5.41 million individual taxpayer records to National Identification Numbers by 30 September 2026, leaving about 3.27 million unmatched, the finance ministry told Parliament. Taxpayers with linked records are due to log in with their NIN from 1 January 2027, and no end date has been set for TIN logins.

MPs Verify, Confirm Digital Number Plate System is Working During Naguru Command Centre Visit — Nile Post, 3 October 2026

Uganda's parliamentary Committee on Physical Infrastructure watched police track four Parliament vehicles live through the Digital Number Plate System at the Naguru command centre. How ignored warnings, missing feasibility studies plagued Uganda’s digital plates project — Daily Monitor (Uganda), 13 October 2026

Audit Russian digital number plate firm – MPs ask Auditor General — Watchdog Uganda, 16 October 2026

Refugee internet use by gender

Closing the digital divide for refugee women means understanding their full experience of connectivity — GSMA Mobile for Humanitarian Innovation (Zainab Moallin), 10 September 2026 EthiopiaTanzaniaMauritaniaGlobal

The GSMA published research on 10 September 2026 on refugee women's mobile use, drawing on fieldwork with Venezuelan refugees in Colombia and in Mauritania's Mbera camp. It reports that refugee women in Ethiopia are twice as likely to have SIMs registered in someone else's name, that in Uganda about a quarter use the internet against half of men, and that in Tanzania identity rules push women to register SIMs through intermediaries.

Digital inclusion for refugee women must go beyond connectivity: GSMA — Biometric Update, 14 September 2026 EthiopiaTanzaniaGlobal

GSMA research finds identity rules for SIM registration push refugee women in Ethiopia and Tanzania to register lines in others' names, limiting control of mobile money and communication. In Uganda about 25% of refugee women use the internet against 50% of men.
