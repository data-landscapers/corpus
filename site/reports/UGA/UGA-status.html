Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

The national identity system is the deepest layer of Ugandan digital public infrastructure and the one with the clearest measurement. Migration of 28,571,893 legacy records completed in February 2026, and the registrar's own review sets renewals at 14.3m of a 15.8m target, 90.5%, against first-time enrolment of 6.4m of 17.2m, 37.3%. Of 10,152,559 cards printed, 2,581,457 have been collected. The system is efficient for people already on the register.

Data protection moved from a paper position to a demonstrated one. The protection office's five corrective orders against a foreign controller were confirmed complied with on 17 July 2026, inside the statutory timelines and without an appeal. Orders against a second foreign controller issued a year earlier have no compliance record at all, and the office has issued nothing on the personal-data processing that ran through the January 2026 election.

Payments carry the largest measured volumes in the base: UGX 326tn of e-money in FY2024/25, agent banking up 76.1%, and debit-card use down 5.1% with internet-banking users down 6.6%. Growth is in agents and handsets while both screen-based channels contract.

The compiled finance layer holds no domestic state budget for Uganda at all, so every state financing figure in this report is an announcement, a levy remittance or a donor commitment rather than an appropriation. Sixteen of the rows below are Not held.

Two new terrestrial routes entered service — a Goma-to-Mombasa route through Kampala in February 2026 and a Nairobi–Kampala route in June 2026 with 1 Tbps activated and headroom to 30 Tbps — while the National Backbone Infrastructure's most recent published utilisation figure remains the under 30% of full capacity Parliament recorded in December 2023 when approving its fifth phase. Parliament attributed part of that to the absence of grid power at sites the fibre reaches, and nothing later in the base measures either.

The five-year satellite operating licence signed in May 2026 requires a national gateway, a physical point of presence, a staffed local office and registration of every in-country device, and follows a period in which terminals were disabled on regulatory direction. A January 2026 ruling that access would run wholesale through licensed local operators and the May direct licence are not reconciled.

Social media and over-the-top messaging remain blocked with no lifting on record, having been retained after general internet was restored in January 2026.

An updated national information security framework was issued in July 2026 citing a 60% year-on-year rise in attacks, but the framework document is not held, and the most recent police series remains 446 recorded cybercrime cases for 2024 with UGX 72.1bn lost. Uganda has no national computer emergency response team on the record.

The regional communications regulators' organisation puts Uganda's mobile data price at approximately USD 1.40 per gigabyte, attributing it to a 40% tax burden on smartphones, in a dataset covering 2024–2025; the figure is printed in USD with no exchange-rate or conversion basis stated.

The government service bus is baselined at 37% of agencies integrated, against a 73% target for FY2029/30, and the only other figure the base holds for it — more than 100 agencies and private entities — rests on a 2024 case study using a different denominator. The two are not reconciled. The national authentication platform has no user or transaction figure at any date. A hundred further public entities were added to the electronic government procurement system on 3 August 2026, taking it from 36 to 136.

Three registry changes were made in 2026: a centralised digital register for companies and non-governmental organisations, registration of resident foreign nationals of whom about 1.95m of the roughly 2m in scope are refugees or asylum-seekers, and a birth-registration drive with a published fee schedule. No birth-registration coverage figure has been published since 2016. The registration authority states it is operating at 70% of its approved workforce, with 25 districts served by a single member of staff.

From 1 January 2027 over-the-counter withdrawals are capped at UGX 50m a day for individuals and UGX 500m a day for businesses, with interbank cheque limits halved, in a measure whose stated purpose is a cash-lite economy. A stablecoin settlement corridor across four East African markets, a tokenised-asset exchange under acquisition, and insurance sold over a mobile-money channel. The national payment switch and the real-time settlement replacement are both recorded as in progress with no confirmed go-live.

United Bank for Africa's Uganda subsidiary went live on the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System on 3 August 2026, announced by the operator with no corridor list, volumes or tariff; no held source puts the Bank of Uganda on the system as a settlement participant.

A comparative account of wallet-based micro-investing across the region names Okusevinga, a 2026 Ugandan USSD pilot opening money-market and bond funds to investments from UGX 10,000, aimed at drawing cash and informal savings-group money into regulated instruments; no provider, launch date or user figure is published.

The statistics bureau broke ground on a Statistics House office block at Entebbe on 7 August 2026, to house a data centre, a data-science hub and a statistics-innovation hub alongside training and census-coordination space; the government states the block is fully financed but has published no contract value, budget line or completion date.

On 17 March 2026 the Constitutional Court nullified sections 11, 23 and 26 to 29 of the Computer Misuse Act, together with the Penal Code's criminal-libel provisions, on quorum grounds and for vagueness. Those sections were the state's principal tools for prosecuting online speech.

The data-protection office ran a full cycle to confirmed compliance against a foreign platform while the January 2026 electoral processing has produced no decision, complaint entry or public statement on file. The directives behind the pre-election shutdown were ordered tabled to Parliament on 10 March 2026 and the base holds no record that they were.

The pre-election shutdown cut connectivity from 18:00 local on 13 January 2026, with access returning at 23:00 local on 17 January; the communications commission announced restoration "as of midnight" on 18 January, with social media and over-the-top applications expressly still restricted, and platform blocking ran until its public notice of full restoration on 26 January 2026, thirteen days after the order. A joint civil-society submission to Uganda's fourth-cycle Universal Periodic Review, filed in July 2026, seeks statutory independence and fining powers for the data-protection office.

The health memorandum of 10 December 2025 provides for a Data Sharing Agreement not on record as executed, capped at seven years from execution, requiring prior written authorisation with the Government of Uganda as data owner, and binding data activities to the Data Protection and Privacy Act Cap 97 and the National Data Governance Framework.

The five-year agency strategy targets active e-government use rising from 9.2% to 40% and IT-law compliance from 67% to 81% by FY2029/30. The government's own review found gaps and inconsistencies in the data agencies submit through the national monitoring system, stating no error rate and no remediation date.

The procurement portal publishes 26 of 148 required data elements, 17.6%, and 4.2% of required project-level disclosure paths. No Ugandan open-government-data policy or portal is held at all.

A bill capping foreign funding at 20,000 currency points, about UGX 400m a year, above which ministerial approval is required was before joint parliamentary committees in April 2026 and was described as recently enacted on 2 August 2026; no assent date, gazette notice or text is held, so the base cannot say whether it is law.

Uganda has no adopted artificial-intelligence instrument. The strategy was described as being in its final stages in April 2025 and was still in drafting sixteen months later, with no text or gazette record; on 4 August 2026 the ICT ministry told the parliamentary committee it is due by the end of the financial year. A secretariat operates inside the ICT ministry with no budget line or mandate document on file. On 2 August 2026 the minister announced a separate policy to regulate artificial intelligence and social media; no other record of it is held.

A national AI research cloud opened at Makerere in March 2026, framed as replacing foreign cloud on data-sovereignty grounds and published with no capacity, cost or user figure. Sunflower 14B and 32B, released open source in October 2025 by a not-for-profit with Makerere University, cover 31 Ugandan languages, with their developer claiming state of the art on translation in 24 of them.

Four startups raised a disclosed US$5.5m in 2025 against two raising US$2.4m in 2024, on a tracker counting disclosed rounds only. No sector output, employment or contribution-to-GDP figure is held.

The bilateral health memorandum signed at Kampala on 10 December 2025 plans United States support of US$1,719,960,000 against Uganda's US$577,001,796, including US$113,102,289 for data systems. The memorandum itself only provides for a future Data Sharing Agreement (§2.5.2); ProPublica reported on 23 July 2026 that it reviewed that agreement directly and that it grants the United States direct, real-time login access to nine of Uganda's health data systems for seven years — a term matching the memorandum's own §2.5.2 cap. The agreement's text is unpublished and not held, and neither Uganda's Ministry of Health, its Personal Data Protection Office nor the US Embassy answered ProPublica's questions (reconciled 2026-08-10).
