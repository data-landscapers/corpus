Mauritius: progress report, 2025-08-01 to 2026-08-09

Edition of 2026-08-10  ·  67 systems and instruments tracked, 9 of them not held

Compiled 2026-08-10 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 67 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 39 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-09, 14 did not, 5 carry no stated baseline, and 9 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 57 sources for this place in the window — 13 in the earlier half (2025-09 to 2026-03), 44 in the later (2026-04 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The comparison below rests mostly on its later half: of 52 sources for Mauritius in this window, 12 fall before March 2026 and 40 after, so this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label.

What it does establish is a year in which policy and law moved a great deal and delivery figures did not move at all. A national data strategy, a national AI strategy, a fintech strategy, a critical-infrastructure cyber regime, regulations on data protection officers and a 119-page omnibus amending 58 Acts all arrived inside it. Over the same twelve months the identity wallet published no user figure, the payment switch published no volume, and the interoperability framework remained the one written in 2002.

The one measured movement is somebody else's: a bank's retail payment figures. The one position that did not move and should have is the state's own interception conduct, unreformed at both ends of the window.

Cybersecurity

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-09 Movement
Critical Information Infrastructure regime Did not exist; cybercrime was punishable but no framework existed to designate and regulate operators of critical systems (2025-08) In force across financial and banking, public service, information technology and broadcasting, energy and water, and transport, with non-compliance carrying up to 25 years' imprisonment and fines up to Rs 2m (2026-06) Advanced

Cyber Threat Intelligence Sharing Platform Did not exist The central bank would be empowered to establish it and to require financial institutions, and other institutions it specifies, to participate on terms it determines; the bill was introduced and not enacted, and no platform, terms or participant list exists (2026-07) Advanced
Postal sector threat sharing Did not exist The national postal operator is being wired into the Universal Postal Union's global postal information-sharing centre, and the ICT minister urged adoption of ISO/IEC 27001 as the standard; no membership date or commitment published (2026-07) Advanced

Hosting gained an anchor rather than capacity. The estate was ten facilities at the start of the window and is ten at the end; what changed is that the Rose-Belle facility was certified Tier IV in December 2025 and took on e-health, the government portal, the identity wallet and government artificial-intelligence workloads, giving the ageing central government centre a disaster-recovery pair it did not have.

Connectivity moved in commitments, not in cable. LION and LION2 carried most international traffic at both ends of the period and SAFE still retires around 2027. Against that the year produced a prime-ministerial commitment to the America-India Connect system with a non-disclosure agreement signed and terms unfinished, and two operator-named landings with no contract. Nothing new is in service.

Cybersecurity is where the year's clearest advance sits. At the start of the period the country could punish cybercrime and had no framework to designate and regulate operators of critical systems; the designation regulations came into force on 1 June 2026 with penalties up to 25 years and Rs 2m, and the July omnibus would add a compulsory central-bank threat-sharing platform. Both are twelve-month-old positions that did not exist a year ago.

Satellite is the row that stayed still: the authorisation regime was in force at both ends and no service has been authorised under it.

The foundational layers did not move, and the absence of movement is an absence of published figures rather than of activity. The identity wallet carried no user figure at the start of the period and carries none at the end; the payment switch published no national volume at either end; the interoperability framework is still the 2002 one and the health strategy still the 2015 one.

What did move is governance around them. The fintech committee went from not existing to statutory form from 1 September 2026 in a single month, on the back of a strategy published in June. A digital travel authorisation, a statutory electronic licensing channel for clinical trials and wider statutory data-sharing by the statistics office all appeared inside the window and none is enacted.

The welfare system began its digitisation after thirteen years: 10.5m documents are being scanned into a platform serving about 800,000 payments a month, with integration stated at about eighteen months from May 2026.

The only measured movement in this section belongs to a commercial bank: cash fell from 30% to 27% of consumer spend and QR payments rose 67% across 2025. Cross-border, the period added two announcements and no established corridor — a Seychelles link announced without corroboration, and regional settlement participation reported by another country's central bank.

Open data

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-09 Movement
Freedom of Information Bill Did not exist Announced as in preparation; no draft, text or timetable has been published in the six months since (2026-08) Advanced

This is the section that moved most, and the movement is asymmetric. The data-protection regime advanced on three fronts inside the window: enforcement volume became public at about 3,000 actions a year, European adequacy became a stated pursuit, and regulations on data protection officers were promulgated for entry into force in January 2027. A national data strategy that did not exist a year ago was launched in January 2026.

The Act those instruments hang off did not move. The 2017 statute is in force at both ends, amendments to it were committed in July with no text and no date, and it is not among the 58 Acts the year's omnibus amends — so the period's expanded inter-agency data sharing arrives against an unchanged safeguards Act.

Neither did the state's own conduct. The interception apparatus described in Parliament as running since 2017 without judicial oversight is at the same position at both ends of the window: no reform instrument, no inquiry outcome, no judicial-authorisation requirement, and leaked recordings still testing the framework.

Two commitments were made and not delivered inside the window: a Freedom of Information Bill announced in January and Electronic Transactions Act amendments referred to in July. And one debate opened that had no earlier position at all: consultations on verifying social-media identity, on 2,300 online-harm cases in the first half of 2026.

US / hyperscaler activities

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-09 Movement
Amazon Web Services memorandum Did not exist Signed with soft scope covering skills, institutional enablement and a scale pathway for local innovators, with no disclosed value or binding deliverable; the training is to run initially in premises rented for the purpose, with permanent infrastructure only a possibility (2026-08) Advanced
Hyperscaler infrastructure presence Baseline not held Google already operates a ground station at Baie-du-Jacotet serviced by the state operator, so the cable commitment would deepen a relationship rather than open one; what is contracted with the hyperscalers so far is skills and confidentiality, not capacity (2026-08) Baseline not held

A year ago Mauritius had no artificial-intelligence position on the record. It now has a strategy with guidelines that govern by impact rather than geography, a legal frame in the form of an incentive scheme and an immigration category, a live model registry, an enterprise platform whose school pilot has already been narrowed, and a certification work-stream it co-chairs. That is a complete policy stack built in twelve months.

What it does not have, at either end of the period, is a measure. The compute the stack assumes is an operator claim, and the study that would establish how many Mauritian firms use artificial intelligence was commissioned in July 2026 — by a chamber of commerce, after the strategy and after the budget. A domestic critique published in March argued the sequencing was the wrong way round.

The hyperscaler relationship deepened without becoming capacity: an Amazon memorandum with no disclosed value, and the establishment that Google's ground station at Baie-du-Jacotet already existed.

Training and skills

System or instrument At 2025-08-01 At 2026-08-09 Movement
National AI training programme Did not exist A target of 50,000 people trained in one year — 25,000 professionals, 12,000 ninth-grade pupils, 8,000 teachers and 5,000 civil servants — on a Rs 25m platform; no completion or enrolment figure has been published (2026-06) Advanced

Skills went from no stated commitment to the most specific one on this record. The 2026-2027 budget funds a 50,000-trainee target in a single year, a Rs 25m platform, an investment tax credit and a special economic zone with preferential data-centre power. Nothing has yet been published against any of those numbers, and no measure exists of the skills base the target starts from.

Sub-national inclusion acquired its first named commitment: a ministerial mission to Rodrigues in May 2026 set a cybersecurity working framework, a youth project and integration of the national sign-on through post office agents.

External financing did not move. The largest digital-sector commitment on the record is still a 2018 buyer's credit with no repayment statement, and the country's growth in this area is as a conduit for other people's payments rather than a recipient of investment.
