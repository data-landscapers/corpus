This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Mauritius's Ibrahim Index governance score was 72.8 out of 100 in 2023, second of 54 African states and down 4.0 points since 2014. It has lost the first place it once held. The score declined on 55 of the index's 96 indicators and improved on 32. Public administration fell 6.1 points to 72.2 in 2023.

Digital Mauritius 2030, adopted in December 2018, is the long-horizon plan. It was preceded by the Digital Government Transformation Strategy 2018-2022. The Blueprint for Mauritius 2025-2029, released in May 2025, names data governance as an enabler and AI as a pillar. It commits the state to a unified government portal with single sign-on, a government super app, an integrated digital immigration system and a hub-and-spoke data-exchange platform. A National Data Strategy 2025-2029 was launched on 28 January 2026. At the launch the Data Protection Commissioner announced that a Freedom of Information Bill was being prepared. A National Fintech Strategy 2026-2030 followed in June 2026.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a digital transformation strategy whose implementation is institutionalised, not ad hoc. It also recorded a data-governance function with an approved policy, housed inside another institution, and a dedicated institution responsible for digital government. An inter-ministerial committee to drive the digital strategy was set up in August 2025. In August 2026 Parliament was told of a Project Monitoring and Implementation Unit under the Prime Minister's Office. The unit has no decision-making power and can only coordinate, monitor and report bottlenecks. In April 2025 the ministry named its own weaknesses: a slipping e-government ranking, personal data dispersed across government holdings, and a legal framework lagging behind practice.

Legislation and regulation

On 24 July 2026 Prime Minister and Finance Minister Navin Ramgoolam signed the Economic and Financial Measures (Miscellaneous Provisions) Bill (No. XIII of 2026). Its clauses 2 to 59 amend 57 enactments and repeal one. It accompanies a 128-page Finance Bill executing the 2026-2027 budget. The bill inserts an AI City Scheme into the Economic Development Board Act. From 1 September 2026 a new Part IIIA of the Financial Services Act puts the National Fintech Governance Committee on a statutory footing. A new section 34A of the Virtual Asset and Initial Token Offering Services Act prohibits unlicensed solicitation of investors by any medium. It presumes any publicly accessible communication to target Mauritius. Commencement is staggered across eight fixed dates from June 2025 to January 2027.

The July 2026 bill does not amend the Data Protection Act 2017 and carries no cross-reference to it. The Finance Bill strengthens data exchange between administrations, which will need solid personal-data safeguards.

The Information and Communication Technologies Act 2001 is the sector statute. Under it the ICT Authority licenses internet service providers. The Cybersecurity and Cybercrime Act 2021 repealed the Computer Misuse and Cybercrime Act 2003. Identity law sits in the National Identity Card Act, amended by successive Finance Acts and supplemented in 2024 by three sets of regulations. They include the Mobile ID Regulations. No dedicated data-exchange statute has been found. Inter-agency sharing rests on the Data Protection Act and the Electronic Transactions Act 2000. An amending Act to the Electronic Transactions Act came into operation on 25 September 2026. A law firm reads it as recognising electronic transferable records and contracts formed by automated message systems.

In 2026 the ICT ministry held a working session with Meta on faster content removal, Kreol moderation and proposed amendments to the Cybersecurity and Cybercrime Act. No framework with Meta has been signed. In November 2026 the ICT ministry said draft amendments to that Act had gone to the State Law Office. They cover deepfakes, doxxing and fake profiles, and would create a cybercrime agency. No text has been published.

Data protection

The Data Protection Act 2017 has been in force since 15 January 2018, aligned with the EU General Data Protection Regulation, including on biometric data and data subject rights. It requires controllers to notify the Commissioner of a personal data breach within 72 hours where feasible. Affected individuals must be told where the risk is high. Regulations made on 17 June 2026 set the designation, qualifications, independence and reporting duties of data protection officers. They enter into force on 1 January 2027. Guidance issued in September 2026 requires every management company to register as controller and processor. Each must appoint its own data protection officer. In October 2026 the ICT minister said about 23,000 of an estimated 60,000 entities required to register had done so. On 29 October 2026 the ministry and the Data Protection Office launched a guide on data protection for the financial sector.

In May 2026 the Data Protection Office was headed by Commissioner Drudeisha Madhub. She is also President of the Francophone Association of Data Protection Authorities. The office investigates complaints, issues enforcement notices and prosecutes. Appeal lies to the ICT Appeal Tribunal, and the office reports annually to the National Assembly. It cannot impose administrative fines. Its thousands of actions a year are almost all resolved amicably.

Central storage of biometric data was held unconstitutional in 2015. In 2021 the UN Human Rights Committee found that storing biometric data on identity cards violated the right to privacy. Since 19 February 2024 the National Identity Card Act has required an independent body to be appointed to carry out periodic security audits of the identity system. Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam has confirmed that illegal wiretapping took place. The interception apparatus operated without judicial oversight. The Counter Terrorism Unit and the National Security Services accessed it. In April 2026 a case of data surfacing on the dark web was cited in the domestic argument over where data should sit.

Regional collaboration

The Bank of Mauritius is the settlement bank for COMESA's Regional Payment and Settlement System (October 2026). Mauritius participates in the Smart Africa Alliance. Its first payments corridor abroad links the national switch to India's Unified Payments Interface. It was agreed in February 2024 as the country's first bilateral cross-border payments arrangement. Mauritius is among four countries considering onboarding their currencies to the SADC real-time gross settlement system, potentially by the end of 2027 (July 2026). COMESA partners accept the identity card for visa-free travel on a bilateral basis. Mauritius waives visas for citizens of all COMESA states. In April 2021 the data protection commissioner stated that Mauritius ratified the Malabo Convention in March 2018.

METISS, a 3,200 km cable linking Madagascar, Mauritius and Réunion to South Africa, was operational by March 2021. Six operators built it under a construction agreement signed in December 2017 under the Indian Ocean Commission's auspices.

AFRINIC, the continental internet registry incorporated in Mauritius, is run by a court-appointed receiver, who served a legal notice on the Registrar of Companies on 29 October 2026. The notice demands the removal of Cloud Innovation, the registry's litigant, from its register of members. Cloud Innovation rejected the demand on 1 November 2026, citing a June 2025 court order to include it as a member.

Standards

Cabinet took note on 28 August 2026 of a Digital Service Framework setting mandatory principles and standards for every government digital service. It covers services developed, procured or maintained, and was published by the Government Information Service. How compliance is checked is not stated.

Mauritius has had a mandatory e-Government Interoperability Framework since 2002. The World Bank records the framework as extensively used, not nominal (2025). Ministry-level chief information officers work alongside a government-wide CIO function with a central advisory and research role (2025). Open source is partially adopted, in several sectors but not government-wide (2025). Mauritius was a participating member of the ISO/IEC committee that writes information-security, cybersecurity and privacy standards (November 2023).

The Mauritius Standards Bureau's national ISO/IEC 27001 certification scheme listed eleven certified bodies at 30 June 2026. The holders include CERT-MU, the revenue authority's server room, the Central Information Systems Division, the Police IT Unit and the Emtel and Mauritius Telecom data centres. On 25 January 2025 the bureau adopted four ISO/IEC artificial-intelligence standards as Mauritian Standards, among them ISO/IEC 42001 on AI management systems. Sector regulators set further standards one regime at a time. The National Fintech Governance Committee created by the July 2026 bill may approve sector-wide standards and frameworks, but is expressly not a body corporate.

Mauritius Telecom is a founding contributor and co-chair of TM Forum's MODaaS collaboration, announced on 20 July 2026. The work is to define controls and conformance criteria for a model-as-a-service certification framework. The chamber of commerce study on enterprise AI adoption carries AI governance as a declared strand.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

Digital freedom scored 51.2 out of 100 in 2023, 25th of 54 African states and down 21.7 points since 2014. It is the lowest rank Mauritius holds on any digital measure. Rights fell 18.1 points over 2014-2023 to 64.7, the largest ten-year fall of any sub-category for Mauritius. Media freedom fell 10.7 points to 68.7 (2023).

On 1 November 2024 the communications regulator directed internet providers to suspend Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and X until after the election. It cited national security after leaked recordings of wiretapped conversations. The order was rescinded a day later. In September 2025 a journalist was questioned and his electronic devices seized over an alleged breach of the ICT Act.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded an e-participation platform for public consultation on government decisions and a citizen feedback mechanism for government services. After Cabinet on 5 June 2026 the technology ministry announced consultations toward a mechanism to verify the identity of social-media users. It is aimed at disinformation, impersonation, defamatory content, scams and child sexual abuse material. The ministry acknowledged the tension with privacy and free expression, the ease of circumvention by VPN and the need to negotiate with the platforms.

A Mauritian analysis published in 2026 argues that contract enforcement is a serious constraint on growth. It holds that justice reform may do more for the economy in the long run than a new AI strategy or another digital platform. In April 2026 practitioners argued that data is a strategic asset and data sovereignty a strategic question. They pressed for critical state and enterprise data to be hosted within or close to the territory, and for Mauritius to become a regional cloud-and-cybersecurity hub.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The Accountant-General's accounts for 2024/25 record Rs 52.5 million spent of Rs 260 million voted for hospital e-Health. They record Rs 43.3 million spent of Rs 210 million for the e-Licensing project, and nothing of Rs 50 million for the e-Passport project (2024/25). The Safe City project spent Rs 876.4 million of Rs 950 million (2024/25).

The digital lines of the 2026-27 estimates come to Rs 3.51 billion appropriated, up from Rs 3.20 billion in 2025-26 (2026/27).

The Public Sector Investment Programme for 2026-27 to 2030-31 directs its external grants and loans to water, port, airport, road, health and national-security projects. It labels one of its 33 e-government initiatives, an electronic catch-reporting system, as EU-funded. The 2026-27 estimates print no financing source against their digital lines.

The 2026-27 estimates carry Rs 131.9 million for running the Government Online Centre, Rs 76.1 million for its data centre and Rs 40.5 million for its Tier IV data centre. They carry Rs 125 million for the national identity card unit and Rs 1.1 billion for the Safe City project (2026/27). Cabinet put Rs 85 million a year behind free internet for households on the Social Register when the measure went live in February 2026.

Mauritius Telecom's Tier IV centre at Rose-Belle is to host the e-Health system, the new government portal and Mobile ID (December 2025). It is also to supply the backup the Government Online Centre lacks. The commercial terms of that hosting had not been published as at September 2026.

MoUs and other agreements

Mauritius Telecom signed a memorandum of understanding with Amazon Web Services on 16 July 2026. It is aimed at skills, at helping businesses and public institutions turn ideas into solutions, and at a pathway to scale for local innovators. No value or binding deliverable has been disclosed. On the same day Mauritius Telecom announced a partnership with SBM Bank to create a shared entrepreneur platform. No terms, value or delivery date have been published for it.

New investments

On 1 April 2018 Mauritius Telecom, the minority state-owned operator, signed a preferential buyer's credit worth USD 73,687,000 with the Export-Import Bank of China. The credit part-financed the Safe City Project, and the Government of Mauritius issued a sovereign guarantee for it. It was extended on concessional terms, with a long maturity and a grace period before repayment. USD 22,349,375 of it was outstanding at 30 June 2018, the first year-end the loan appears in. No larger external commitment to the digital estate has been found.

Kuunda, a Mauritius-based fintech lending short-term to small businesses in six countries, raised a USD 7.5 million pre-Series A round in October 2025. Its borrowers are in Tanzania, Uganda, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique and Pakistan. Since leaving the FATF grey list, Mauritius has been used by fintechs as a treasury and billing gateway into fragmented African markets.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Internet and computer access scored 88.7 out of 100 in 2023, second of 54 African states and up 33.3 points since 2014. It is the most improved of the 96 measures in Mauritius's Ibrahim Index profile. Mauritians' own assessment of their infrastructure fell 25.6 points to 74.4 over the same decade. Household internet access reached 85.8% in 2024, up from 72.6% in 2020 and 29.0% in 2010. 79.5% of the population used the internet in 2023. Mobile connections stood at around 161% of the population in early 2024. Fibre-to-the-home access cost 1.8% of GNI per capita in 2022.

The island lands the SAFE, LION, LION2 and METISS submarine cables (2025). Mauritius Telecom's T3 cable, 3,200 km to South Africa, reached its landing station at Baie Jacotet in March 2023. SAFE is due to retire around 2027, and a replacement, T4, is planned (July 2025). Mauritius Telecom's 2026-2029 strategy adds new submarine cables and a 5G-Advanced rollout. In July 2026 the government announced that Mauritius would join the America-India Connect subsea cable programme. The Mauritius Internet Exchange Point is hosted at the Rogers Capital data centre in Ébène (July 2025). The electricity utility's subsidiary runs a wholesale fibre backbone of over 300 km on the grid's overhead ground wire. In March 2019 it selected a vendor to deploy an enhanced backbone across 40 points of presence.

In August 2026 the regulator said low-orbit broadband may be sold only through a licensed Mauritian provider with an earth-station licence for each terminal. OneWeb operates through its local partner; Starlink has met the regulator and holds no licence. Its head put the mobile sites held up by permits at 30 for Emtel and 15 for Mauritius Telecom. In October 2026 the regulator opened a call for applications for a mobile number portability licence, with no launch date given.

The dedicated broadband policy is the National Broadband Policy 2012-2020, whose window closed in 2020. Shipping and postal capacity fell 17.0 points over the decade to 45.1 out of 100 (2023).

Data Storage

Mauritius Telecom's Rose-Belle data centre is certified Tier IV and is to host strategic state systems (December 2025). They include the e-Health system, the new government portal, Mobile ID, the penalty-points system and government AI equipment. Government data systems have been hosted at the Government Online Centre in Ébène since 2005. That site has grown to about 80 racks and lacked a fully operational backup. In 2025 networking equipment for the Centre's Tier IV disaster-recovery site at Rose-Belle was bought for Rs 27.55 million.

Ten data centres were operating across Port Louis, Réduit, Arsenal, Quatre Bornes and Rose-Belle in July 2025. Their operators include Aphelion, Rogers Capital, Harel Mallac Technologies at Tier III, Emtel at Arsenal and BIRGER's Candos recovery facility. Bhumishq at Réduit is carrier-neutral and designed to Tier IV. The island has no hyperscale cloud region of its own, so the global platforms serve it from distant regions.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a government private cloud on one shared platform, delivered to public bodies as a service. Its stated purpose is to keep government data inside the country (2025). The 2018 long-horizon strategy had provided for resilient public-service data centres. In April 2026 Cabinet explored a national sovereign cloud for government on VMware Cloud Foundation technology. In July 2026 the state operator was expanding its Tier IV data centre with GPU clusters and a sovereign cloud. A domestic critic argued in March 2026 that an infrastructure-first approach risks obsolescence on delivery, and that AI services should be built now on hybrid cloud.

Budget 2026-2027 designates 83 arpents at Côte-d'Or as a special economic zone for AI and digital activity. It offers preferential electricity tariffs for data centres, 30-year renewable leases and 100% foreign ownership (June 2026). The Bank of Mauritius's 2022 cloud guideline lets financial institutions host material cloud services offshore only on conditions. They must know the city and country of hosting, and host only where data protection law is equivalent to Mauritius's. They must ensure the host country's authorities have no access to the data.

Energy

41.6% of the 353 formal-sector firms surveyed for the World Bank's 2023 enterprise survey reported at least one power outage in the previous fiscal year. Outages most often came at roughly monthly frequency. Access itself is effectively complete, with 99.7% of the population connected to the grid (2023). Mauritius scores 99.3 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index's energy measure (2023). Renewables supplied about 24% of electricity (2023). They grow under the Renewable Energy Roadmap 2030, supported by net-billing and subsidised household solar schemes (2024). Energy and water are among the five sectors in the first phase of critical information infrastructure designation (June 2026). This brings the utilities' own control systems inside the cyber regime. The electricity board's tariff 255 charges data centres of Tier III and above Rs 2.97 per kWh. The investment board's portal stated that rate in October 2026.

Technical Capacity

In August 2026 the government told Parliament it was consulting to recruit engineering and project-management professionals, possibly one from abroad, for a new unit on delays in major public projects. A Thales-led consortium with the local firm Harel Mallac Technologies built the current national identity system (2024). Day-to-day operation sits with the National Identity Card Unit in the Prime Minister's Office. The government's own HR management system still carries manual processes and paperwork (2025). The Central Information Systems Division's application development unit builds and maintains small and medium systems for ministries on the government cloud. They include the e-Payroll system, in use since October 2020.

Cybersecurity

Critical Information Infrastructure Designation Regulations have been in force since 1 June 2026. Before them the state could prosecute cybercriminals but had no framework for designating operators of critical systems. The first phase covers financial services and banking, the public service, ICT and broadcasting, energy and water, and transport. Operators have twelve months to comply. The National Cybersecurity Committee designates them in consultation with the sector regulators. An owner who fails to comply faces a fine of up to Rs 100,000 and up to five years' imprisonment.

The parent statute is the Cybersecurity and Cybercrime Act 2021, and the standing policy the National Cyber Security Strategy 2023-2026. The Director of Audit found one of its 20 projects complete at October 2025, and the National Cybersecurity Committee not yet operational at the end of that year. In October 2026 the Committee was convened and the ICT ministry took about 70% of government websites offline against the PHP2Shell flaw. 85 to 90% were back by 24 October 2026. Mauritius sits in the top tier of the ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, for scores of at least 95 out of 100.

A Bill dated 24 July 2026 would empower the Bank of Mauritius to establish a Cyber Threat Intelligence Sharing Platform for financial institutions. Participation would be compulsory, and could extend to "such other institutions as the Bank may specify". The Bank invited bids for the platform on 20 October 2026, closing 20 November. Banks have had to undergo external audit and penetration testing of critical systems since the central bank's 2023 cyber guideline. The results stay supervisory, and no independent audit of MauCAS is published.

Reported security incidents rose from 5,221 in 2024 to 6,073 in 2025. Online harassment cases rose from 1,324 to 1,636 and cybercrime cases from 491 to 702. The police cybercrime unit handled 930 cases from January 2023 to January 2026. They led to 130 arrests, seven court referrals and no conviction. The 2026-2027 budget funds a Cyber Forensic laboratory at Rs 13 million and an information security management system across the public administration.

DPI

Data Exchange

InfoHighway, the national data-exchange platform, has been live since August 2016. In 2025 it connected 33 government publisher organisations through 192 system connections supporting more than 600 e-services. It is a publisher-subscriber model that stores no data of its own and fetches records live from source databases. It runs on the government intranet from the Government Online Centre, under a High-Level Management Team chaired by the technology ministry. A connection is made only after a signed agreement between the agencies concerned and central approval (2024). A live public factsheet on the homepage reports publisher agencies, connections, e-services and query volumes (2026).

The July 2026 Finance Bill widens data sharing between administrations, which will need solid personal-data safeguards. No dedicated data-exchange statute has been found. The sharing rests on the Data Protection Act 2017 and the Electronic Transactions Act 2000. It also rests on a mandatory e-Government Interoperability Framework in place since 2002, which the World Bank records as extensively used (2025).

The Civil Status Division publishes citizen records, so other agencies verify users' details against civil registration at source (2023). The Central Population Database reaches the revenue authority, the Economic Development Board and the National Transport Authority (2024). The Corporate and Business Registration Department is a listed publisher, so business registration data can be queried in real time (2025). For the Negative Income Tax, the revenue authority draws civil status records to identify eligible low-income employees and pay the benefit automatically (2025). Customs, tax, debt management, financial management, human resources and social insurance exchange data through a Government Service Bus and point-to-point interfaces (2025).

Digital Identity and CRVS

World Bank data show 99.1% of Mauritians aged 15 and over held a national identity card or equivalent document in 2021. Section 4(1)(a) of the National Identity Card Act requires every citizen to apply within six months of turning 18; the Mobile ID is optional. A unique National Identity Number is generated when a birth is registered and deactivated when the death is registered (2019). From 19 October 2026 civil-status certificates are printed on security paper with serial numbers generated by the civil status database.

MauPass, live since December 2020, registers citizens by card number and validates them against the Central Population Database (2024). Under section 7(2), the card or the Mobile ID is sufficient wherever a person must prove identity. The card is required for the Basic Retirement Pension, Widow's Pension, Social Aid and other benefits, and identifies beneficiaries in the Social Register (2024). Operators verify the photograph against the card at SIM registration (2024). Memoranda signed on 15 April 2022 provide for a Central KYC system reachable through InfoHighway. The central bank passes information from the Central Population Database to participating institutions. A digital driving licence was soft-launched on the government app on 29 September 2026, with legal effect from 1 January 2027. Access needs a fully activated MauPass account.

The third-generation card, MNIC 3.0, was introduced in February 2024. A Mobile ID wallet built with Thales technology followed in September 2024, delivered through the MWA app. By 30 June 2025, 138,280 MNIC 3.0 cards had been issued and 9,417 citizens had enrolled on the Mobile ID, with the earlier card being phased out. Holders can view their own record, change a residential address and declare a lost card (2024). Fingerprints and a facial image are captured at enrolment, but fingerprints are stored only on the card, following a 2015 Supreme Court ruling (2024). Ease of obtaining an identity document ranks 1st of 54 African states at 84.6 out of 100, down 15.1 points since 2014 (2023).

Digital Payments and Fintech

MauCAS, the Mauritius Central Automated Switch, has run an instant payment system nationwide since August 2019 (2024). It offers 24/7 account-to-account and QR payments across banks and payment service providers. Person-to-person transfers are its most used transaction type, followed by merchant QR payments and payments to government. The Bank of Mauritius owns and runs it as system operator, scheme manager and overseer. Instant payments rose to 19.4 million worth Rs63.0bn in the year to June 2025, from 9.9 million worth Rs33.2bn a year earlier. The rise followed the removal of person-to-person charges and a rise in the transaction limit to Rs250,000. Eleven banks and four non-banks take part. Government-to-person and business-to-business payments are not yet enabled and sit on the roadmap.

Around 80% of adults made or received a digital payment in the previous year (2021). Access to banking services scores 63.5 out of 100, first of 54 African states, up 14.6 points over 2014-2023. QR payments at the Mauritius Commercial Bank rose 67% year on year in 2025. The Bank of Mauritius began a retail digital currency pilot with one bank in December 2023, and in July 2025 its governor said a digital rupee had been tested in a pilot phase in 2024.

MauCAS is linked to India's Unified Payments Interface network-to-network, with settlement in Indian rupees (2024). It is the country's first bilateral cross-border payments arrangement. The Angolan kwanza became the second settlement currency of the SADC real-time gross settlement system on 27 July 2026, the first addition since the rail launched rand-only in 2013. Mauritius is among four countries considering onboarding theirs, potentially by the end of 2027 (July 2026). The central bank behind each settlement currency supplies liquidity in it when the system runs short.

A National Fintech Strategy 2026-2030 was published on 25 June 2026, developed with UN ECA support. A new Part IIIA of the Financial Services Act commences on 1 September 2026. It puts the National Fintech Governance Committee on a statutory footing, chaired by the Minister. The Committee seats the central bank Governor, the Financial Services Commission chief executive, four ministries, the Economic Development Board and four private-sector members.

Registries

The cadastral database built in 2006-2011 under the Land Administration, Valuation and Information Management System covers the whole island in digital aerial imagery. It assigns a mandatory Parcel Identification Number to every land deed. Over 547,000 parcels are captured, making Mauritius one of three African countries whose land registry covers more than half of private land (2023). The Registrar General's e-Registry offers online registration, payment and search of registry documents (2024).

Under sections 8A to 8C of the Civil Status Act 1981, the Central Population Database is compiled directly from births, deaths, marriages and changes of citizenship. Section 8B confines it to citizens, so resident non-citizens are not recorded. Civil registration scores 75.0 out of 100 in 2023, 7th of 54 African states, unchanged since 2014.

The Corporate and Business Registration Department runs the business register online, with incorporation, registration and public search (2026). An anti-money-laundering amending Act of July 2024 requires the Registrar of Companies to keep a beneficial ownership register open to the competent authorities. The revenue authority identifies individuals by identity card number and businesses by Business Registration Number or Tax Account Number (2025).

The July 2026 omnibus bill creates a national land transport database under a new Traffic Management and Road Safety Unit, with online registration of vehicle transfers. It also establishes a beneficial-ownership register of partnerships and creates clinical-trial disease registries and research databases under a new Public Clinical Trial Unit.

The November 2024 electoral roll held 1,002,857 registered voters against a voting-age population of 1,064,944. The Electoral Commissioner publishes a continuously updated Register of Electors by constituency, ward, polling station, sex, age and citizenship (2024). A March 2026 audit found the Social Register held 25,350 individuals in December 2023, against about 101,900 people in relative poverty. It runs on Oracle software nearing obsolescence, and its replacement has been delayed.

Sectoral management information systems

The health ministry's electronic medical record was in pilot at some thirty public facilities in the East and Centre in October 2026. More than 150,000 patients were registered, and Phase 1 acceptance is planned by December 2026. Testing began at the Sir Anerood Jugnauth Hospital on 23 August 2025. The project sits under a Roadmap for Digital Health Transformation prepared with UNDP support (2025). A Digital Health Architecture Blueprint launched with UNDP on 3 March 2025 is to guide a National Patient Database.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a financial management information system on commercial software across central and sub-national government. The treasury system is extensively used by all ministries, departments and agencies. Government payments settle through real-time gross settlement. Tax administration runs on commercial software connected through the Government Service Bus. Customs runs on commercial software, and payroll on custom software. Social insurance and pensions run on custom software with records fully digitised. Programme performance indicators cover only some programmes.

Electronic procurement covers online tendering and contract management (2025). Only 116 of 205 public bodies used the e-Procurement System in 2023-24. An assessment published in June 2024 found its use highly selective, with a small share of procurement value passing through it.

In 2022 UNESCO described OpenEMIS as the national education management information system. Administrators at national, zonal and school level entered student and staff profiles, attendance, enrolment and performance directly. The education ministry's 2025-2026 annual report still lists a centralised, full-fledged system as work to do. It approached UNESCO and the Community Systems Foundation and sought UNDP support. It ties implementation to new frameworks, regulatory approvals and funding. DHIS2 was in use at regional and national level, with extension to district and primary care still planned (2022).

The 2025-2026 budget allocates Rs 43 million to replace the Crime Occurrence Tracking System with a new police case management system. It adds biometric capability to the Criminal Attribute Database, with tendering from the end of August 2025. The force's 2021 policing plan put use of the tracking system up from 30% to over 80% of divisions in 2020. A 2023 press account dates the replacement of handwritten occurrence books by computer entries to 2015.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Online applications for the State Age Pension opened on 7 October 2026. A pension estimate app was launched alongside them on the KOREK! super-app, built by the social security, ICT and finance ministries. Close to 500,000 people had registered on the KOREK! app by October 2026, 250,000 of them with identity verification, on the ICT minister's count. Cabinet recorded that online payment of fines for non-penalty offences was to go live through the Supreme Court website on 30 October 2026. It also recorded that development of a Unified Government Web Portal is ongoing.

Mauritius scores 42.2 out of 100 for ease of resolving a commercial dispute in the World Bank's Business Ready 2025 assessment, against Singapore's 73.3. Singapore is the comparator Mauritius sets for itself. It scores 49.0 against 66.0 on the quality of dispute-resolution regulation and 46.4 against 62.8 on the public services supporting it. Statistics Mauritius reported more than 70,000 cases pending before the courts at the end of 2025, over 13,000 of them civil. In February 2024 remote hearings were limited to judge-in-chambers matters. In October 2026 electronic filing ran at the Supreme Court's commercial division, with a pilot for judge-in-chambers cases, and the other courts had no central records system.

A central e-services portal at govmu.org carries ministry services and electronic payments to government (2025). The national online service portal is transactional at level 3 or 4, and citizens complete transactions on it (2025). An online tax portal covers taxpayer registration, filing, payment and further services (2025). The revenue authority runs individual income tax e-filing and a File and Pay service (2025). An online social protection portal covers registration, benefit information and payments (2025). A separate public employment job portal handles recruitment, selection and online applications (2025). Mauritius scored 0.7506 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, in the very high band and among the leading African states. A dedicated GovTech institution responsible for digital government is established (2025).

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

The Rodrigues Regional Assembly launched a digital roadmap of its own, the Rod-Digital Vision, on 6 May 2026. It rests on four pillars: trust, innovation, sustainability and resilience. Later that month a ministerial mission to Port Mathurin announced a working framework for the island, notably for cybersecurity. It provides for a team based in Mauritius, an intended cybersecurity and AI representative on Rodrigues, and monthly meetings.

Since April 2016 the District Council of Grand Port has taken trade-fee payments through the local authorities' portal, la.govmu.org. Registration for the e-payment facility is made in person with identity and business registration documents.

Rural digital data capture

In October 2026 the health ministry's e-Health record was in pilot at some thirty public health facilities of the East and Centre. It is to be extended to other facilities and regions after Phase 1, whose acceptance is planned by December 2026. No count has been found of the area health centres, community health centres or mediclinics it reaches.

The electricity grid covers effectively the whole population (2023), and household internet access has climbed steeply since 2010. In 2012 births, deaths and marriages were registered through offices on the main island, Rodrigues and Agalega, only some of them computerised. Paper entries passed through a computerised office before the document came back to the applicant.

School-level administrators enter education data directly into OpenEMIS (2022). Police stations, rural ones included, run the crime occurrence tracking system introduced in 2011, with terminals reaching the criminal records database. In May 2026 Mauritius Post agents on Rodrigues were enlisted to promote the KOREK! super-app, and MauPass integration was reviewed with them.

The 2024 Ibrahim Index carries no score for Mauritius in its rural economy sub-category or any of its four indicators. It is the only one of the index's sixteen sub-categories on which Mauritius cannot be scored.

Technology

AI

The Mauritius AI Registry at airegistry.mu went live on 9 July 2026. It lists Mauritius-relevant AI models, agents and skills without hosting them, and its software stack is open-sourced. Each entry carries provider identity, local relevance and, where applicable, institutional validation. Mauritius Telecom, the state operator, runs it, along with mytGPT and its mytGPT Education service (July 2026).

In December 2025 Cabinet adopted the DIVA virtual assistant as the official chatbot for all ministries and departments. It is to be upgraded into a family of AI agents for citizens and businesses. Cabinet placed an Artificial Intelligence Unit under the ICT ministry to steer the 2025–2029 digital blueprint. In July 2025 the ICT minister announced round-the-clock AI-assisted online patrols against fake profiles and false news.

The FAIR Guidelines were issued with the AI for Mauritius strategy in April 2026. They are vendor-neutral and border-agnostic, require bias audits in fintech and gaming, and place local-representative duties on foreign AI providers serving Mauritius. They are not binding, and sit on a stated path toward legal force. In October 2026 Cabinet recorded that World Bank engagements had identified assistance for the formulation of an AI Act. No draft or timetable has been published. The one older rule is sectoral: the Financial Services Commission's 2021 licence and rules for investment advice delivered through AI-enabled algorithms. The state has held an AI strategy since 2018.

In late July 2026 the chamber of commerce and industry bodies commissioned a study of enterprise AI adoption, for which no measured baseline exists. AI is named 35 times in the 2026-2027 budget speech (June 2026).

ICT Industry

The July 2026 omnibus bill inserts an AI City Scheme into the Economic Development Board Act, to promote investment in AI, digital sectors and advanced computing. Certificate holders are entitled to prescribed fiscal incentives, duty exemptions and expedited permits. The bill adds an AI Founder category, at USD 1 million invested, to the Act's permit criteria (2026). Budget 2026-2027 adds an investment tax credit of 15% a year over three years, 45% in all, for manufacturers investing in AI solutions.

Mauritius scored 92.6 out of 100 on business and competition regulations in 2023, first of 54 African states and up 5.3 points over the decade. The World Bank's 2025 assessment records that Mauritius supports GovTech start-ups and has public-private arrangements for delivering government services online. It names no scheme, contract or firm. The Safe City project, the largest technology procurement found, began as an unsolicited bid by Huawei in 2015.

Innovation ecosystem

In 2024 TechCabal described the startup sector as small, with the large rounds attributed to the island reflecting its role as a holding domicile for pan-African operators. Mauritius ranked 55th in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024.

The World Bank's 2025 assessment records a public sector innovation strategy, a programme to deliver it, and an innovation function covering digital skills and public service innovation. It names none of the three. Private incubation runs through a small set of active hubs, among them Turbine, a member of the pan-African AfriLabs network (2020). By November 2026 Turbine said it had helped create 35 start-ups since 2016. In September 2024 the central bank launched Innov8, a fintech hub with a regulatory sandbox. The 143-acre Cote d'Or Technopole was inaugurated in October 2023, and no occupancy figure has been found. The AI City Scheme's AI Founder permit sets an investment threshold for founders brought in from abroad.

Capacity

Literacy

Mauritius scored 85.4 out of 100 on education in 2023, first of 54 countries and 4.3 points lower than in 2014. Human resources for education scored 99.9 and enrolment 79.8, both the highest on the continent (2023). Completion scored 97.0, second of 54. Education quality also ranked second, at 83.9, but fell 4.9 points over the decade.

In May 2026 digital awareness work aimed at young people on Rodrigues was made part of the island's integration into the national AI and cybersecurity strategy.

Training and skills

Budget 2026-2027 commits Mauritius to training 50,000 people in artificial intelligence within a year (June 2026). The target is 25,000 professionals, 12,000 Grade-9 pupils, 8,000 teachers and 5,000 civil servants. The budget funds a National AI Learning Platform at Rs 25 million and scales MyT-GPT Education into secondary schools (June 2026). In October 2026 Cabinet recorded that the training programme was being developed. No enrolment figure has been published.

Mauritius Telecom launched a mytGPT Education pilot in January 2026, tested in ten schools. After resistance from teachers and parents, the operator narrowed it from a student-facing tutor to a teacher coach (July 2026). It now aims at a personal AI assistant for every secondary school teacher by 2027.

In 2023 the higher education commission counted 4,556 tertiary students in ICT, 9.1 per cent of enrolment.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded a digital skills strategy and programme for the public service, covering basic digital skills and data literacy, partly on a fee-paying basis. A UNESCO-supported programme for education ministry staff trained administrators to enter data directly into the national education management system (2022). On 6 November 2026 the incumbent operator and Amazon Web Services launched the myt Learning Hub, a free portal of 16 courses in AI, cloud, cybersecurity and data. It serves a government target of training 50,000 people in digital and AI skills within a year.

Research institutions

The National Research and Innovation Institute Act 2026 replaces the Mauritius Research and Innovation Council with an applied-research institute. Its mission-oriented research is to cover digital transformation, alongside climate resilience, food security and public health. The institute is to run a National Research Information System that maps projects and tracks outputs. No commencement date for the Act had been found as at November 2026. The higher education regulator amended its guidelines on the use of AI in higher education in May 2026.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services scored 84.2 out of 100 in 2023, second of 54 African states and down 15.7 points since 2014. The Inclusion and Equality sub-category fell 6.0 points to 71.0.

Almost every adult citizen held a foundational identity document in 2021, and the card is compulsory from the age of 18. The National Identity Card Act restricts the card to citizens. Section 8B of the Civil Status Act confines the Central Population Database to citizens too (2022). The card is the mandatory document for the Basic Retirement Pension, Widow's Pension, Social Aid and other social security benefits (2024). First issue of the card is free of charge (2019). UNHCR, which has no office in the country, registered 2024's asylum-seekers remotely. It rates the legal framework not yet aligned with the 1951 Refugee Convention.

Statistics Mauritius put the fibre-to-the-home tariff at 1.8% of GNI per capita in 2022, down from 2.2% in 2021. Mauritius has the highest score in Africa on access to banking services (2023). The Blueprint for Mauritius 2025-2029 adopts a mobile-first, no-one-left-behind framing and promises a Government Super App. When State Age Pension applications moved online on 7 October 2026, help was offered at 38 Social Security Offices to applicants with limited digital literacy or access. In October 2026 Cabinet noted laptops and assistive equipment for blind and visually impaired secondary pupils, with the Mauritius Telecom Foundation. No count, cost or delivery date has been published.

Digital divides

The 2024 Open Data Inventory scores Mauritius's social statistics lowest: food security 3 out of 10, poverty and health outcomes 3.5, crime 4. Gender statistics and education facilities score 4.5.

Electricity reaches 99.7% of the population (2023). Household internet access reached 85.8% in 2024, which leaves about one household in seven offline. Individual use was lower, at 79.5% of the population in 2023.

The MARS cable to Rodrigues, in service since 2019 under a 20-year US$44.8m contract, had 19.9 of its 100 Gbps sold at 30 June 2025. The island had no ICT strategic plan, and the company formed to operate the cable was still not running. In October 2026 Cabinet noted a Digital Promotion Agency branch on Rodrigues, after an August session onboarded some 500 citizens onto MauPass and the KOREK! app.

Data

National statistics

Statistical capacity scored 78.1 out of 100 in 2023, third in Africa and down 11.4 points since 2014. The World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators score data use above 89% and data sources only 50-69% (2023). Services, products and infrastructure score between the two, at 70-89% (2023).

In March 2026 Mauritius adopted the enhanced tier of the IMF's data dissemination standard, the first African country to join it. It had graduated to the standard itself in February 2012. The central bank launched a central statistical database on 3 September 2026, built with the IMF and the African Development Bank. No series list, update cadence or licence is stated for it. Statistics Mauritius trained users on the second phase of its MauStats platform, built with UNDP, from 19 to 28 October 2026. The phase adds 14 statistical modules, among them prices, wage rates and tourism, and data exchange through APIs. The Finance Act 2025, assented on 8 August 2025, made the statistics office, the central bank and the health ministry a National Statistical System whose members must share statistical data, and barred its use for enforcement or surveillance.

A population census, an agricultural census and a business census have all been run within the past ten years (2023). The 2022 census enumerated 1,235,260 residents, against 1,237,090 in 2011. Over that decade there were three or more household and labour force surveys and one health survey, but no agricultural or business survey.

Vital statistics are extracted from the Civil Status Division's computerised system, and put the population at 1,240,316 at 1 July 2026. External trade figures are compiled from customs declarations on the revenue authority's database. Crime, justice and security statistics for 2024 draw on police, prosecution, court, prison and probation records. The Bank of Mauritius reports MauCAS transaction volumes and values at least annually. The July 2026 Finance Bill widens what Statistics Mauritius passes to the Ministry of Finance and shortens the deadlines for the public accounts.

Open data

Disclosure of public records rose 25.1 points over 2014-2023 to 70.6 out of 100, second in Africa. Accessibility of those records fell 12.4 points to 45.5, twelfth in Africa.

A National Open Data Policy was adopted in 2017. The portal carries basic information and datasets refreshed weekly or daily, but publication to it is largely manual (2025). Economic and environmental categories score between 5 and 7 out of 10 in the 2024 Open Data Inventory. Built environment and population score highest at 7, and banking lowest at 5.

In every area of the World Bank's 2025 GovTech assessment, Mauritius reports its supporting documentation as internal and unpublished. The areas run from cloud and interoperability to data protection, right to information and start-up support.

The Electoral Commissioner keeps a continuously updated Register of Electors by constituency, ward, polling station, sex, age and citizenship (2024). A Freedom of Information Bill was announced as in preparation on 28 January 2026, at the launch of the National Data Strategy.

Geospatial data

The cadastral database of the Land Administration, Valuation and Information Management System was built between 2006 and 2011. It covers the whole island in digital aerial imagery. Every land deed carries a mandatory Parcel Identification Number.

On 23 September 2026 the meteorological service validated a five-year plan for weather, climate and early-warning services. Its weather radar, built with Japanese grant aid to monitor cyclones, was evaluated after completion in 2022.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

Mauritius Telecom signed a memorandum of understanding with Amazon Web Services on 16 July 2026. It is aimed at skills, at helping businesses and public institutions turn ideas into solutions, and at a pathway to scale for local innovators. No value or binding deliverable has been disclosed. The operator and the cloud company held an executive workshop on cloud, data and AI for more than 125 delegates of state-owned enterprises (2026).

In February 2026 Mauritius Telecom said it had met NVIDIA's senior leadership while seeking accelerators. It returned to the same supplier in July. On 16 July 2026 the Prime Minister announced that Mauritius will join the America-India Connect subsea cable programme. It presents the island as a digital gateway on the Asia-Africa corridor.

China activities

Huawei made an unsolicited bid for the Safe City project to the government in 2015. The commercial contract was signed in December 2017, ahead of the loan agreement that funded it, and Huawei is the contractor. The financing was a preferential buyer's credit from the Export-Import Bank of China, carrying a sovereign guarantee.

The project equips the police with 4,000 surveillance cameras on main roads, motorways and walkways, and 4,500 smartphones (2018). It includes a radio trunking network, a central watch and management system and an integrated emergency response layer. Cabinet noted on 16 October 2026 that 162 Safe City cameras on 75 sites had been damaged in riots that began on 12 October. No repair cost or timetable has been published.

EU activities

Mauritius is aligning its data-protection law fully with the EU GDPR (2026). It is pursuing the European Union's adequacy assessment during 2026. The 2026-27 investment programme labels one e-government initiative, an electronic catch-reporting system, as EU-funded.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf state, operator or investor was found holding a position in Mauritius's digital infrastructure as at August 2026. None was found in its platforms or its financing.

India activities

The UPI-RuPay arrangement agreed in February 2024 is Mauritius's first bilateral cross-border payments agreement. Mauritians can pay directly on India's payment rails. MauCAS, the domestic rail, is joined to the Unified Payments Interface network-to-network. QR payments settle in Indian rupees through a designated settlement bank.

Prime Minister Ramgoolam and Prime Minister Modi held discussions at the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi in February 2026. Mauritius's move to join the America-India Connect subsea cable programme followed them. It was announced on 16 July 2026. State Informatics Limited, the state IT company, signed a memorandum with Tata Consultancy Services on 15 October 2026. It covers developing digital solutions, transferring skills and modernising public- and private-sector services. No sum or timetable is stated. The Prime Minister told the National Assembly on 28 April 2026 that India would help build a command-and-control centre and forensic capacity for the police cybercrime unit. On 6 November 2026 Cabinet agreed a cybercrime memorandum with India's home affairs ministry and approved an Indian-funded cell-broadcast early warning system.

Digital sovereignty

In September 2025 the technology ministry told would-be suppliers of a government sovereign cloud that all data, metadata and encryption keys must stay in Mauritius. Backups and disaster recovery are included, under exclusive Mauritian jurisdiction. The operator must be locally incorporated and controlled, and the Government Online Centre is the designated hosting site. The ministry described wrapped hyperscaler models with local key ownership alongside fully sovereign stacks. It left key custody, portability and exit terms to the procurement itself.

The central bank owns and operates the MauCAS switch (2024). InfoHighway runs on the government intranet from the Government Online Centre. The National Identity Card Unit operates the identity system a Thales-led consortium built. Whether the state holds the identity system's source code, or can change its supplier, was not on record as at September 2026.

In August 2026 the parties to Mauritius's entry into the America-India Connect cable had signed a non-disclosure agreement, while negotiations with Google continued. The terms of Mauritius Telecom's July 2026 memorandum with Amazon Web Services on data jurisdiction, portability and exit had not been published as at September 2026.

In February 2026 Mauritius Telecom asked NVIDIA for a few hundred B300 GPUs and was quoted about a year's lead time. At the SADC summit in August 2026 the prime minister announced a carrier-neutral green data centre and a "Digital Embassy Village".
