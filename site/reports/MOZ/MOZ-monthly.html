Strategies, plans and policies

Digital Transformation Operational Plan 2026-2029

Transformação Digital em Moçambique: ATDI e instituições alinham prioridades para modernizar o Estado — MZNews, 15 September 2026

The digital transformation agency met in Maputo to set the basis of an operational plan for 2026 to 2029, its stated aim to replace isolated systems with a common architecture. A sovereign cloud and data centre and a national interoperability platform are among the priorities.

Governo Garante “Soberania do Estado” Sobre Dados Públicos — Diário Económico, 21 September 2026

A second account of the Mozambique digital transformation meeting, summarised under the AIM report. It leads on the state administration minister, Inocêncio Impissa, saying that data produced by public bodies must belong to the state. The plan has not yet been costed.

Governo quer acelerar transformação digital dos serviços públicos até 2029 — Agência de Informação de Moçambique (AIM), 21 September 2026

Mozambique is drafting a three-year operational plan to speed up the digitisation of public services to 2029. At a meeting of the technical commission on digital transformation in Maputo on 21 September 2026, the head of the digital transformation agency said the Citizen Portal already offered about 26 services and had over 3,000 users before its official launch. An interoperability portal, a payments platform for state services, a government cloud and a government data centre are to follow. The commission expects to harmonise and cost the plan within two to three months, with funding spread across many donor projects.

Strategic Plan for the Information Society 2019-2028 (Resolution 52/2019)

Digital Inclusion in Africa: Mapping the Ecosystem Gap — Paradigm Initiative, 22 September 2026 EthiopiaAlgeriaNamibiaCôte d'IvoireDRCAfrica

Paradigm Initiative published a baseline study on 22 September 2026 of digital inclusion in Ethiopia, Algeria, Namibia, Côte d'Ivoire, Mozambique and DR Congo. It compares the six countries' policy commitments, funding, governance and private-sector involvement, and notes that access remains uneven for young people, women and rural populations.

Postal Sector Transformation Strategy and implementation roadmap

Mozambique's regulator INCM has called for expressions of interest (ref. INCM/SC/95/2026) for a six-month study of the postal market and a transformation strategy and roadmap, with particular attention to the designated operator CORREIOS. The scope includes digital transformation, postal financial services and e-commerce.

Internet for All strategy

Only 15% of Mozambicans can afford mobile internet access — Club of Mozambique, 7 October 2026

A proposed Internet for All strategy for Mozambique, to be implemented by the communications regulator with targets to 2030, was presented in Maputo. A partner organisation said only 15% of the population can afford to keep a mobile data package, against about 5.6 million mobile internet subscribers in some 36 million people.

Public Finance Management Strategy (EGFP) 2026-2035

Mozambique Plans to Integrate AI into Public Finance Management Systems — Consulting.co.mz, 12 October 2026

Mozambique's government approved a Public Finance Management Strategy for 2026–2035 that includes a digital transformation plan for 2026–2030. It names AI, machine learning, data mining and the Internet of Things for treasury, debt and state-asset management, and puts all major processes on the e-SISTAFE system by 2035. The approval date is not stated.

Legislation and regulation

Communications consumer protection and dispute regulations

Relatório de Defesa do Consumidor dos Serviços de Comunicações 2025 — Autoridade Reguladora das Comunicações (INCM), Moçambique, 9 September 2026

Mozambique's communications regulator, INCM, reported 391,723 telecoms complaints in 2025, up about 44% from 271,993 in 2024, with 95.2% of closed complaints resolved; postal complaints fell from 276 to 258. Vodacom logged 115,100 complaints and Movitel 87,428. INCM named Tmcel, Teledata, TMT and StarTimes for closer follow-up, and two postal operators filed no returns.

Mozambique telecoms complaints surge 44% — ITWeb Africa, 10 September 2026

Mozambique's communications regulator recorded 391,723 consumer complaints in 2025, up 44 per cent on 2024, with 372,846 of the telecommunications complaints resolved and an average satisfaction index of 82 per cent. It attributes much of the rise to better recording rather than worse service, one operator's logged complaints having gone from 213 to 24,036 on a new incident-tracking system.

Telecommunications Traffic Control Regulations (Decree 48/2025)

Mozambique's Internet Shutdown Case: African Courts Draw a Line on Executive Power — Collaboration on International ICT Policy for East and Southern Africa (CIPESA), 10 September 2026 Africa

Mozambique's Constitutional Council ruled in July that a government must first have lawful authority to order an internet shutdown before it can justify one. An advocacy organisation reads the ruling as requiring proof both that the power was lawfully created and that it was exercised by a legally authorised body, and sets it against a wider continental pattern of shutdowns.

Postal Security Regulation

Governo aprova novo regulamento para reforcar a Seguranca Postal e proteger o Comercio Electronico — MZNews, 16 September 2026

Mozambique's Council of Ministers approved a Postal Security Regulation on 15 September 2026, setting controls against theft, fraud and cyber threats to the postal network as e-commerce parcels increase.

Law regulating artificial intelligence in education

Governo prepara lei para regular IA na educação — Integrity Magazine, 17 September 2026

Mozambique's education ministry says its law regulating the use of artificial intelligence in education is almost ready and will reach the Council of Ministers before the end of 2026, covering every institution from primary schooling to higher education with all of them consulted. The text is still being drafted and is not published.

Governo prepara lei para regular uso da Inteligência Artificial na Educação — MZNews, 23 October 2026

Mozambique's education and culture minister Edson Macuácua said on 22 October 2026 that the government is preparing a law to regulate artificial intelligence in education, centred on ethical and responsible use, fundamental rights and copyright. He spoke at the launch of MAZA IA, a learning tool built by MOZEDUCA with Vodacom Moçambique, and called for national solutions, wider access to digital platforms for poorer students and investment in teacher training.

Decree on electronic transactions and e-commerce

Mozambique: Government plans to track and monitor businesses on digital platforms — Club of Mozambique, 20 October 2026

Mozambique's government will track and monitor businesses operating on digital platforms, including online credit providers, under its recently approved decree on electronic transactions and e-commerce. The Council of Ministers' spokesperson said so on 20 October 2026. The Digital Transformation Agency is among the bodies that will supervise operators.

Data protection

Registration and licensing regime for digital platforms and intermediary service providers

FACIM 2026: INTIC Acelera Regulamentacao Para Proteger Cidadaos no Espaco Digital — Diario Economico, 1 September 2026

Mozambique's ICT institute INTIC told a panel at the 61st FACIM fair in Maputo that digital platforms and intermediaries, including foreign operators serving Mozambicans, must register and declare where their data are stored. Its legal head Edson Psico said enforcing the ICT legal framework needs money, technology, staff and inspection capacity.

FACIM 2026: INTIC Accelerates ICT Regulation to Protect Citizens Online — 360 Mozambique, 2 September 2026

Officials of Mozambique's National Institute of Information and Communication Technologies (INTIC) said at a panel during the 61st Maputo International Trade Fair that applying the country's ICT laws needs money, technology, staff and cooperation across sectors. Legal head Edson Psico named enforcement of data-protection rules, incident response and supervisory capacity as the main challenges, and said Mozambique depends on external technology.

Personal Data Protection Law (draft)

Proteccao de dados nao pode travar liberdade de imprensa, alerta MISA — Ikweli, 3 September 2026

Press-freedom group MISA Mozambique said at a round table in Maputo that the forthcoming personal data protection law must not restrict freedom of expression, the press or access to information. The head of the national ICT institute INTIC, Lourino Chemane, said passing the law would only be a start, and institutions, enforcement and public awareness would need strengthening.

Mozambique | Parliament convenes extraordinary and ordinary sessions for October — Club of Mozambique, 2 October 2026

Mozambique's parliamentary Standing Commission on 1 October 2026 placed a Personal Data Protection draft law among 35 initial agenda items for the Assembly of the Republic's fourth ordinary session, opening on 28 October. The agenda also includes a framework law on public administration and revisions to the customs and tax procedure codes.

Mozambique's ICT institute INTIC signed a memorandum with Kamaleon, a rural mobile-multimedia provider, on 21 September to run digital-literacy, cybersecurity and data-protection campaigns in rural communities. Mozambique's ICT institute INTIC, UNDP and the National Elections Commission are training 26 institutional trainers on AI in election administration at Macaneta on 19-23 October 2026. Media Barometer

Mozambique: New barometer to monitor media, combat disinformation and fake news — Club of Mozambique, 5 September 2026

Mozambique is to introduce a Media Barometer to measure audience trust and track disinformation and deepfakes, announced on 4 September 2026 at the Government Information Office's coordinating council in Maputo. The Higher School of Journalism designed it. A 2025 survey counted 1,207 active media outlets, including 275 radio stations and 147 television channels.

Fifth General Population and Housing Census

INE enfrenta défice de 45 milhões de dólares para financiar o Recenseamento Geral — Rádio Moçambique, 13 October 2026

Mozambique's national statistics institute, INE, has about US$60m of the US$110m the next general census needs, US$45m of it from the government and the rest from partners, leaving a gap of about US$45m, its spokesman Pedro Duce said on releasing the results of the pilot census in Magude district. INE is still raising funds for a census next year.
