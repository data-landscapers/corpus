Compiled 2026-08-19 from the Data Landscapers source base. Sections follow the status report. Each opens with a movement ledger comparing the position at the start and end of the period, which runs to the date of issue rather than to the last month's close.

Of 152 systems and instruments on this place's ledger, 77 changed position between 2025-08-01 and 2026-08-19, 27 did not, 29 carry no stated baseline, and 19 are Not held at both ends.

Shape check, run before the comparison: 169 sources for this place in the window — 29 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 140 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The earlier half of the window is thin: this is a shorter comparison wearing a longer label, and the movement below rests mostly on the later half.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The twelve months to 6 August 2026 moved Mozambique's legal framework for cyber security from nothing on record to two statutes in force, and left its data protection framework where it was. The Cyber Security and Cybercrime Laws were promulgated on 13 June 2026 (promulgation account); the personal data protection law was in preparation at the start of the period and is in preparation at the end, with a Cabinet submission planned for June 2026 that is not on record (ministerial account).

Three systems entered service that did not exist at the start of the window: the instant payment system, the breach reporting platform and the public device-identity lookup. One instrument was made and then partly struck down inside the same window — the Telecommunications Traffic Control Regulations, published on 16 December 2025 and declared unconstitutional in part on 29 July 2026 (judgment).

Twenty-four rows carry no stated baseline. That is a fact about the record rather than about the country, and it concentrates in the subjects where Mozambique's base thickened sharply after February 2026.

Payments moved further than any other system. At the start of the window there was no instant payment system; by 30 July 2026 one was carrying more than 11,000 transfers a day (communique account), and the 2008 payments regime had been replaced by a new statute in force from 2 July (enactment account). A notice of 2 June made connection to the single payments network a condition of offering electronic payment products and required any outage of more than ten minutes to be reported within 60 minutes (notice account).

Identity production rose against its own prior year: 856,101 cards on 813,307 applications in the first half of 2026, against 730,527 on 677,305 a year earlier (directorate account). Free issuance widened from the identity card alone to three documents and was extended to 2027.

The interoperability platform is the position that did not move visibly in either direction: it is described as under implementation and the base holds no operator, go-live date or list of connected agencies at either end of the window.

The clearest movement is the one that went backwards. The Telecommunications Traffic Control Regulations were approved in October 2025, published on 16 December 2025 and in force from that date; on 29 July 2026 the Constitutional Council voided the provisions permitting suspension or blocking of services, compelled access to call and internet-protocol detail records, binding technical norms and intervention in operator networks without consent (judgment). The government stated on 4 August that it will seek the same end by another route (government response).

Against that, the cyber statutes advanced from draft bills approved by government in September 2025 to promulgation on 13 June 2026, and the licensing regime under the Electronic Transactions Law advanced from a regulation in force with no licences issued to 19 licences issued of about 170 companies under evaluation (licensing account).

The data protection law is the row marked Stalled: a draft in preparation with no tabling date at the start of the window, and a draft in preparation with no tabling date at the end, against a stated intention to reach Cabinet by June 2026 (ministerial account).
