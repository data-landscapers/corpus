Status report

This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

The Information Society Policy, approved by the Council of Ministers in March 2018, revoked the informatics policy of 2000. It carries broadband and universal access. A civil-society review of September 2026 treats the Strategic Plan for the Information Society 2019–2028 as the operative national strategy. It finds the plan's execution hampered by limited consolidated public reporting and dependence on donor cycles. Drafting of a National Digital Transformation Strategy began in February 2026. A draft of 22 August 2026 still left its Council of Ministers approval date blank. The ITU's project timeline had set that approval for June 2026.

A National Cybersecurity Policy and Strategy was gazetted in 2021 and is being revised into a 2026–2030 strategy. The National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2025–2031 launched in August 2025. The Intellectual Property Policy and Strategy 2026–2035 was approved on 30 June 2026. It orders a study of AI's effect on the patent framework. In October 2026 the government was reported to have approved a Public Finance Management Strategy for 2026–2035, with a digital transformation plan for public finances over 2026–2030. By 2035 all major public financial management processes are to run through e-SISTAFE.

In November 2025 INTIC circulated its second zero draft of a National Data Governance Policy. It would create a National Data Governance Council able to compel testimony and documents, run unannounced inspections and seize evidence.

President Chapo ordered an overhaul of digital government at the country's first national digital-transformation conference in February 2026. In August 2026 the government said infrastructure investment is the private sector's responsibility and its own role is policy and regulation.

On 21 September 2026 the Digital Transformation Operational Plan 2026–2029 was under appraisal at the technical commission's second session. It is to be harmonised and costed within two to three months. It names an interoperability portal, a payments platform for state services, a government cloud and a government data centre. In October 2026 a proposed Internet for All strategy with targets to 2030 was presented in Maputo. Only 15% of the population was said there to afford mobile data in 2026.

Legislation and regulation

On 29 July 2026 Mozambique's Constitutional Council struck down the government's telecommunications blocking power. It held that the executive had legislated in a field reserved to the Assembly of the Republic. Decree 48/2025 of 16 December had approved the traffic-control regulation. The communications minister accepted the ruling on 3 August 2026. The next day the Council of Ministers spokesman said the executive would pursue the same end by a route that does not contradict the Council. No bill, instrument or timetable for it has been found.

The Cyber Security Law (13/2026) and the Cybercrime Law (14/2026) were published on 1 July 2026. They came into force on 29 September 2026. The 2026 cyber law finances a Cyber Security Fund from 1 per cent of the prior year's gross revenue of licensed cybersecurity firms. The National Payments System Law followed as Lei n.º 15/2026 of 2 July 2026, replacing a regime in place since 2008. Decrees 71/2025 and 72/2025 of 31 December 2025 brought data centres and cloud services under licence.

The Press and Media Law, Law 3/2026, was gazetted on 26 May 2026. Law 3/2026 brings online-only publications inside the media framework and caps foreign capital in a media organisation at 35 per cent. The Council of Ministers approved a Postal Security Regulation on 15 September 2026. In October 2026 it approved a decree on electronic transactions and e-commerce. Under it the government says it will track and supervise businesses run on digital platforms. In September 2026 the minister said a law on artificial intelligence in education would reach the Council of Ministers before the end of the year. A Labour Law revision to cover work done through digital platforms is being drafted.

Decree 13/2023 requires operators to validate every subscriber and device against the regulator's registration database and a risk centre before activating it. No digital-identity-specific statute has been found. Decree 67/2017 frames data sharing between public bodies and between public and private ones, and no comprehensive cross-government data-sharing law exists. The regulation setting the interoperability framework for digital government systems is in its final phase of revision.

Data protection

In January 2026 the World Bank cut the digital acceleration project's cybersecurity and data protection subcomponent from US$18 million to US$11 million. The original sum had assumed six years of support to set up the Data Protection Office, which the remaining project period no longer allows. The Data Protection Law was still pending and the office not yet established. In March 2026 the project's indicator for data-protection regulations adopted stood at 0 against a target of 2.

In September 2025 a draft law went to public consultation. Parliament's programme for the session running from 28 October to 22 December 2026 schedules it for plenary debate on 6 November. On 3 September 2026 a media institute told a Maputo round table that the law must not become a barrier to press freedom and the right to information. In the same week INTIC set out a registration and licensing regime for digital platforms and intermediaries, foreign operators included. Applicants are to declare where their data are stored.

In 2025 a UN inception report found protection scattered across article 71 of the Constitution, the Electronic Transactions Law of 2017, article 189 of the Law on Credit Institutions and article 8 of the Labour Law. The Electronic Transactions Law requires notification to INTIC before personal data is processed, and consent before it is transferred. Decree 73/2025, in force since 1 March 2026, gives postal and telecoms consumers rights of access, objection, rectification and erasure over the data operators hold. INTIC holds some data-protection competencies without being an authority, and the draft law would make it the national one. By February 2026 it had launched a pilot platform for reporting personal-data breaches.

On 29 July 2026 the Constitutional Council found that the blocking decree restricted freedom of expression and information and the privacy of communications. Decree 48/2025 had let INCM, by court order, instruct partial or total blocking of telecommunications. The Council held that the Telecommunications Act authorises no structural and permanent surveillance. The review was requested by the Provedor de Justiça, prompted by the Centre for Democracy and Human Rights.

Regional collaboration

Mozambique's ATDI and Ethiopia's FaydaVerse Digital Solutions Enterprise signed a memorandum of understanding in Addis Ababa on 27 April 2026. It covers digital identity and public infrastructure, open standards, interoperability and capacity-building. In October 2026 a 14-member mission led by the digital agency went to Ethiopia for training on a national digital identity platform. In February 2026 the president named Kenya and Rwanda as the models for the digital government overhaul he ordered. Mozambique has ratified the African Union's Malabo Convention on cyber security and personal data protection. Its only regional economic community membership is SADC. It scored 57.6 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index's Regional Integration measure in 2023, 14th of 54 African states.

In August 2026 the customs administrations of Malawi, Mozambique, South Africa, Zambia and Zimbabwe adopted a roadmap for customs-to-customs data exchange in Pretoria. It is phased from priority corridors.

On 9 October 2026 the ICT institute was reported to have joined TriDigital, a common framework for assessing the digital maturity of public institutions. It covers Angola, Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique and São Tomé and Príncipe and runs to January 2028. On 12 October 2026 the ICT institute was elected to the management committee of the African Network of Cybersecurity Authorities for Southern Africa. In October 2026 it also joined the SADC Regional Working Group on Data Protection. The SADC ICT ministers had urged states on 26 June 2026 to appoint representatives.

Standards

In 2025 the World Bank found a government interoperability framework only partially used across government. The regulation that would set it is in its final phase of revision. Decreto n.º 82/2020 vests management of the .mz domain and its official subdomains in INTIC. It requires registrant data published through WHOIS to be accurate, complete and current. It gives registrants rights of access, updating and rectification. The government operates no open-source software programme (2025). The national standards institute is an ISO correspondent member, not a full member body.

The communications regulator recorded 391,723 consumer complaints in 2025, up 44% on 2024. It resolved 95.2% of the telecommunications complaints in 2025. The regulator attributed much of the rise to better recording. On 5 September 2026 the prime minister directed that INTIC's digital certification system be extended across all state sectors.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The Centre for Democracy and Human Rights petitioned the Provedor de Justiça on 29 January 2026 against the government's internet-blocking decree. The Ombudsman put the question to the Constitutional Council on 17 February, and judgment came at the end of July 2026. In 2025 the World Bank found no online citizen participation or e-consultation platform and no online feedback or grievance redress mechanism. In September 2025 the Presidency opened the national and diaspora consultation phase of an Inclusive National Dialogue. Its technical commission includes civil-society members, and ten working groups draw partly on citizens who answered a public call. In March 2026 the social security institute ran a nationwide consultation on revising the compulsory social security regulation. No submission count or published outcome has been found for either.

Mozambique has a right to information law in force, on the World Bank's 2025 assessment.

Over 2014–2023 Freedom of Association and Assembly fell 12.5 points to 37.5 out of 100. Civil Society Space fell 6.4 points to 55.1, and Deliberative and Participatory Governance fell 5.5 points to 52.9. Freedom of Expression and Belief fell 19.4 points to 62.3, 25th of 54 African states. Media Freedom stands at 50.1, 34th of 54 (2023). The communications regulator suspended two radio stations in Nampula city, Vida and Encontro, for 48 hours in February 2025.

In March 2026 the communications minister publicly rejected the suggestion that the telecommunications regulations were an attempt to restrict internet use.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The final 2026 budget maps give the Ministry of Communications and Digital Transformation MZN 2.64 billion of externally financed investment and nothing from internal funds (FY2026).

The domestic-state share of the digital lines of the 2026 budget is 40.0% at the appropriated stage, down from 62.3% in 2025 (FY2026).

The 2025 budget carried MZN 1.83 billion from internal funds for producing passports and other identity and travel documents (FY2025). The 2025 budget also carried MZN 133.8 million for identity cards and MZN 55.0 million for leased circuits serving the identification and migration services. The 2026 operating line of CEDSIF, the finance ministry's systems centre, is MZN 657.8 million, 89.2% of it salaries (FY2026).

The General State Account for 2025 records the MZN 500 million domestic public-services modernisation project reduced to nothing in-year and unspent (FY2025). In 2025 the revitalisation of the government data centre was executed at MZN 25.5 million of MZN 30 million voted. In 2025 the externally financed strengthening of INTIC's certification and PKI services was executed at MZN 5.1 million against a revised MZN 3.03 billion.

The World Bank's EDGE grant closes on 28 February 2027. The Digital Acceleration Project closes on 31 July 2028. As agreed with the government in January 2026, it keeps operating outside the national e-SISTAFE financial management system on parallel arrangements.

Under Decree 11/2025 the universal-access fund, a separate patrimony run by the communications regulator, takes 1% of licensed operators' prior-year gross revenue. A licensing regulation in force since August 2026 lets the regulator convert operators' fines into universal-service obligations. In September 2026 the digital transformation commission expected to cost the 2026–2029 operational plan within two to three months. Its financing is spread across many separately funded projects.

MoUs and other agreements

In December 2025 the United States set out about US$83 million for health data systems over 2026–2030. The money falls under the America First Global Health Strategy, in a memorandum that declares itself not an international agreement. It is subject to the availability of funds. The United States may revisit its funding levels if no mutually acceptable technology roadmap is agreed.

ATDI and Ethiopia's FaydaVerse Digital Solutions Enterprise signed a memorandum in Addis Ababa on digital identity and public infrastructure in April 2026, with no sum attached. In August 2025 the communications ministry signed a memorandum with the European business association EuroCam. It sets up a joint working group to identify e-government and digital-inclusion projects and mobilise public-private partnerships. In July 2026 the Ministry of Economy was exploring a partnership with SpaceX on connectivity and digital infrastructure, with nothing signed. In September 2026 the finance ministry and Brazil's public services management ministry signed a memorandum on exchanging experience and training staff in procurement, payroll management and digital transformation. No value or deliverable has been published for it.

New investments

By 31 March 2026 the World Bank's Digital Acceleration Project had disbursed 41.67% of a revised US$201.31 million envelope. Implementation progress was upgraded from moderately unsatisfactory to moderately satisfactory. A restructuring in January 2026 raised its rural connectivity allocation from US$42.5 million to US$55 million. The same January 2026 restructuring cut cybersecurity and data protection from US$18 million to US$11 million, and digital inclusion from US$40 million to US$30 million.

Mozambique's digital sector has drawn 155 external commitments, announced between 2015 and 2026 and running to 2033 (November 2026). The 153 with a recorded value come to about US$1.3 billion as at November 2026, most of it lending, not grant aid, and most of it for connectivity rather than digital government, identity or health systems. The World Bank carries just under half on six operations, China Eximbank the next largest share on two loans to the state operator. More than a fifth of the total is on commitments not confirmed as moving.

The World Bank's Sustainable Energy and Broadband Access in Rural Mozambique Project runs to US$343 million over 2021–2027. Its Digital Governance and Economy Project was approved in 2021 as an IDA grant of SDR 105 million, US$150 million equivalent, running to 2027. The Digital Acceleration Project runs 2022–2028 on an IDA credit of SDR 148.8 million, US$200 million equivalent at appraisal (June 2022).

China Eximbank lent to the state operator Tmcel for a first phase of national mobile network modernisation in 2020. A further US$200 million facility for a second phase was reported secured in 2025.

The European Union funds VaMOZ Digital!, a EUR 22.5 million grant over 2022–2029. The ITU carries that programme's regulatory component on CHF 2,244,820 over 2023–2027. In June 2026 the EU signed a further EUR 28 million for digital transformation.

Canada's two UNICEF-channelled phases for civil registration and vital statistics, CAD 19.5 million from 2017 and CAD 6.5 million from 2021, both closed in 2023.

ICT equipment valued at more than US$300,000, financed by KOICA and delivered through UNDP, was handed to the disaster risk management institute on 9 September 2026.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Mobile service reached all but six of Mozambique's 453 administrative posts in 2024, down from eleven a year earlier. On the regulator's finer unit, 1,013 of 1,222 localities had service in 2024. About 84 per cent of the population was within reach of a 4G signal in 2024.

20.5 per cent of individuals used the internet in 2024. INCM counted 17.1 million mobile voice subscriptions in 2024. A gigabyte of mobile data cost under a dollar in 2023. The IIAG's Internet & Computers indicator put Mozambique at 18.8 out of 100 in 2023, 45th of 54 African states and unmoved over the decade. Mobile Communications rose 12.8 points over the same decade to 62.5.

INCM awarded 5G and 4G licences to all three operators on 23 June 2026. The licences carry a June 2027 deadline for service in every provincial capital. The regulator is building a tariff management system, to operate from November 2026, after complaints that data bundles run out before their stated expiry. In October 2026 the regulator validated the national Internet para Todos strategy, which targets 95% broadband coverage and at least 60% of schools connected by 2030. The communications minister put connected schools at 418 in October 2026, with a target of 1,000 by 2029.

In June 2025 the World Bank's digital acceleration project carried a US$50 million rural tower investment due for completion in April 2027. It also carried US$11.5 million for MoRENet bandwidth to universities and technical institutions in June 2025. Infrastructure financed by the universal-access fund is State property held by the operating carrier in usufruct and subject to mandatory roaming. MOZIX, the only internet exchange point, runs in Maputo with around 19 peering members and peak traffic above 150 Gbps (2025).

In September 2026 the finance and communications ministries invited expressions of interest from strategic partners for Tmcel, including a possible partial sale of the state's shares. Tmcel counted 1.79 million active mobile customers at the end of 2025 on 1,303 sites, 724 of them carrying 4G. Of the 4G sites, 447 were in the south against 120 in the north.

Data Storage

Mozambique operates no government cloud, and has no government cloud policy in place (2025). The state's own data centre at Maluana is technically capable but under-used. The regulator's own site carries the case that without national digitisation and systems-integration strategies it has not drawn government institutions to put their data in it (July 2025).

Maputo held at least seven commercial facilities in 2025. They are a carrier-neutral Digital Realty site, NTT, SEACOM, PCCW Global and Teledata, alongside Vodacom's Tier III building. Vodacom opened a further data centre in March 2025, and BCX launched Alibaba Cloud's Africa local public cloud from the city in 2024. Universidade Eduardo Mondlane inaugurated a data centre on 3 August 2026, with roughly fourteen times the storage of the server room it replaces. It is meant to help secure the .mz domain and the MozIX exchange.

The rules on where that data may sit were enacted at the end of 2025, subject to a 90-day vacatio legis, with provisions on data centres located abroad. Decree 72/2025 allows classified information only on private clouds with the regulator's express authorisation, which its transfer abroad also needs. The decree keeps financial-sector data off the lowest of three provider tiers. Decree 71/2025 requires operators of essential services, from public administration and health to energy and finance, to use primary data centres on national territory. Secondary centres abroad need the regulator's authorisation, and centres serving essential services must sit in the advanced or standard resilience category. The financial sector has its own rule. Banco de Moçambique's Aviso 6/GBM/2026 was gazetted on 31 August 2026, in force 60 days later, and revokes the 2003 notice. It requires every bank and financial company to keep its primary data centre and principal information systems in Mozambique. Secondary centres abroad need the central bank's authorisation.

In July 2026 the communications minister conceded that national compute, data centres included, cannot support more advanced AI models. The government's position is that building it, including the proposed regional AI data centre, is the private sector's responsibility (August 2026).

Energy

Mozambique's off-grid electrification framework scored highly on the World Bank's RISE indicators in 2021. The rural energy fund FUNAE ran around 76 mini-grids totalling about 6MW.

36 per cent of Mozambique's population had access to electricity in 2023. Access to energy scored 17.2 out of 100 on the IIAG in 2023, 42nd of 54 African states, up 4.6 points since 2014. Urban access stood at 76 per cent against 15 per cent in rural areas in 2022. Hydropower generates 83 per cent of electricity and gas 15 per cent (2024). Drought, cyclone damage and infrastructure vandalism strained the state utility EDM's ability to maintain supply through 2025.

The university data centre opened in August 2026 has integrated solar generation alongside grid and generator supply. In August 2026 the government named strengthening energy infrastructure among the five priorities for its artificial-intelligence ambition.

Technical Capacity

INTIC, which holds Mozambique's cybersecurity and data-governance mandate, was appropriated MZN 73,636,370 for 2026. Of that, MZN 57,064,340 is personnel and MZN 16,423,800 goods and services, with no line in the investment tables.

Public Administration is Mozambique's best-ranked IIAG sub-category, at 62.6 out of 100 and 12th of 54 African states in 2023. Effective Administration, which measures the professionalism and functioning of the civil service, sits at 45.3 and 35th of 54. Budgetary & Financial Management fell 20.8 points over the decade to 61.1, the third most deteriorated of the country's 96 indicators.

In July 2026 fourteen Mozambican specialists held Open CSIRT Foundation SIM3 certification, the instrument by which a response team's maturity is assessed. INTIC technicians and the wider professional base have had donor-funded cybersecurity training. In 2025 the World Bank recorded a digital skills strategy and a civil-servant training programme pitched at basic skills.

Since 2018 the national identity and travel-document system has run under an eight-year public-private concession, with the supplier keeping a permanent Maputo office. In January 2026 the government was tendering for outside services to strengthen civil registration and identification.

Cybersecurity

Mozambique ranked first among Portuguese-speaking African states on the ITU's 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index, improving on its 2020 result. In January 2026 the World Bank cut the digital acceleration project's cybersecurity and data protection allocation from US$18 million to US$11 million.

Law 13/2026 formally creates the national cyber incident response team and seats its operation inside INTIC. The Cyber Security Law and the Cybercrime Law came into force on 29 September 2026, with implementing regulations due within 180 days of publication. In March 2026 the project's CSIRT-improvement target had been met. The minister said on 7 October 2026 that a national network of response teams has teams in government, the cyber defence unit, the research network, the regulator, the telecoms sector and four provinces.

Notice 2/GBM/2026 requires every financial institution to report any payment outage lasting more than ten minutes to the Banco de Moçambique within sixty minutes. Regulations on cloud computing and radio centres approved alongside the two cyber statutes are mandatory for operators of critical infrastructure (July 2026). Across the estate there is no general mandatory data breach notification requirement (2024). INTIC had launched a pilot platform for reporting personal-data breaches by February 2026.

In October 2026 the communications minister put cyber incidents recorded in 2025 at about 39,600. He said a 2026–2030 cybersecurity strategy now being prepared would be the main instrument for implementing the Cybersecurity Act. No independent security audit, penetration test or vulnerability assessment of the national identity system has been published (2023).

DPI

Data Exchange

The World Bank and African Development Bank's data-governance diagnostic of December 2025 found data management practices across the public sector fragmented. A government interoperability framework exists but is only partially used across government (2025). In 2026 the regulation that would put that framework on a statutory footing was still in the final phase of revision.

Of fifteen government functions counted in 2025, only civil registration was even partially connected to a national data exchange. Health, passports, digital identity, payments, planning and statistics and revenue were under development; agriculture, justice, employment, driver licensing, social protection, education and business registration had got no further than planned; and the electoral register was connected to nothing at all.

An X-Road interoperability platform was under implementation in May 2026. The digital agency said in September 2026 that it expects the platform in the fourth quarter of the year. The road transport institute, the civil identification directorate and the justice ministry were being integrated into it in June 2026. The mobile digital identity and electronic signature infrastructure tendered in May 2026 is specified to run over that exchange and to connect to the DNIC population register. The Unique Citizen Identification Number is assigned automatically only to children born since 2017. An interoperability diagnostic of the social protection, civil registration and tax systems therefore proposed a composite identifier drawing on the identity card, citizen and taxpayer numbers (2021).

Since July 2024 e-SIRCEV has exchanged birth data in real time with the hospital data management module, launched in Inhambane province. Facilities using the e-SIRCEV birth module there were to grow from five to nineteen. A disease-specific laboratory platform carries HIV viral-load results electronically to health facilities instead of on paper (2025). A national master facility list built on DHIS2 has been public with an open API since August 2021, giving each health facility one identifier. e-INAS, the social protection beneficiary system, was linked only to the payment providers M-Pesa, eMola and Paycode (2021). The digital transformation agency trained 25 technicians from nine public bodies on the X-Road data-exchange layer, led by Estonian experts (2026).

Digital Identity and CRVS

DNIC produced 856,101 national identity cards in the first half of 2026, 297,470 of them issued free of charge under the World Bank-funded EDGE project. Free first issuance has been in force nationwide since 13 November 2023. In July 2026 it was extended to 2027 and widened to the birth certificate and the cédula pessoal. In March 2026 the civil identification database held 11,038,940 people, against a World Bank project target of 17 million for December 2026.

Donor money carried better than a third of the country's identity-card output in the first half of 2026: the free EDGE-funded cards were 34.7% of all cards produced.

Roughly a third of children under five had their births registered in 2023. The statistics institute counted 8.8% of births in 2025 as registered, against 8.9% in 2024. A free birth-registration campaign runs from 23 March to 31 December 2026, aimed at around seven million unregistered people. Since March 2019 the e-SIRCEV electronic register has run in 121 of Mozambique's 164 civil registries, assigning the Unique Citizen Identification Number at birth (2021). In March 2026 e-SIRCEV held 4,574,257 people, against a project target of 13 million. The justice sector is installing 100 more civil-registration posts in health units with maternity wards, beside about 250 already operating. A campaign launched at Nacala in March 2025 registered births and issued identity cards on the spot, aiming at 200,000 people over two months.

In 2021 the Bilhete de Identidade was the primary legal proof of identity, needed to vote, open a bank account, take formal employment and reach education and health services. In 2023 it was a physical biometric credential with no digital authentication: no online biometric matching, no PIN or one-time password, no QR verification. In May 2026 the digital agency ATDI tendered for a national mobile digital identity and electronic signature infrastructure. The specification asks for a system device-agnostic across SIM, eSIM and Java Card. By September 2026 a consultancy shortlist had gone to the World Bank.

Muhlbauer has held an eight-year concession since 2018 to build, distribute and support the identity and travel-document system. It keeps a 60-person office in Maputo and has trained around 800 government staff (2024). The World Bank's US$150 million EDGE grant, appraised in 2021, finances wider legal identity coverage and a unique identification number.

Digital Payments and Fintech

METIX launched in March 2026 with interbank person-to-person transfers free for individuals and access over USSD. It is operated by SIMO. The central bank governor put its volume at about 12,000 transactions a day in October 2026, from about 3,000 at launch. Participation is mandatory for mobile wallet operators. It was established as a 24/7 retail instant payments mechanism by Notice No. 1/GBM/2026. The market underneath it holds 15 commercial banks, 12 microbanks, three e-money institutions and more than 25 million mobile wallet accounts open in early 2026.

Electronic money accounts reached 130.9 per 100 adults in the second quarter of 2026, against 33.8 for traditional bank accounts. The e-money figure was 144.7 per 100 men against 118.5 per 100 women. 46% of adults held an active mobile money account in 2024.

The Banco de Moçambique holds the majority of SIMO's shares, appoints its board chairperson, acts as settlement agent and approves its fees (2025). Only banks sit on the board, and the scheme rules are available to participants only.

Mozambique has no legal framework giving refugees, asylum seekers or migrants a right to open digital payment accounts (2023). Standard know-your-customer checks require a national identity document or a passport. The central bank accepts social protection beneficiary cards, issued by INAS and not by the identity system, as an alternative identity document for opening a payment account. Government-to-person disbursement over the switch is a planned next phase, not active at national scale (2025). The social action institute had paid over 200,000 of about 800,000 post-emergency beneficiaries through mobile wallets by March 2023. WHO has paid more than US$14.4 million to nearly 246,000 health workers and volunteers by mobile money since 2024. Formal remittances from South Africa fell 36% between 2022 and 2024, a shift back towards informal channels. The central bank governor was reported saying the metical could be used for regional payments through the settlement system from January 2027. The central bank licensed a payments aggregator as a payment service provider, among the first such licences.

Registries

17.2 million voters were registered for the 2024 general elections, 333,839 of them in the diaspora. The elections commission's technical secretariat STAE produces the register afresh for each election. In August 2026 the commission was studying Angola's permanent voter registration as an alternative to Mozambique's pre-election model. More than 80% of land held under good-faith or customary occupation is not formally registered (2021). The World Bank land project closed in March 2024 with 1.28 million land-use rights recorded in the SiGIT land information system, against a target of 2 million. A 2024 survey found roughly half of manufacturing micro, small and medium enterprises had no state registration of any kind. Only around 11% held a taxpayer number in 2024.

A 2024 decree-law made beneficial-owner declarations registrable acts. The business register takes online submissions from legal entities through the registries service's user portal. Civil registration runs on e-SIRCEV, operated by the National Directorate of Registries and Notaries. The Revenue Authority's taxpayer register is keyed to the NUIT and covers personal and business taxes alike. Social protection beneficiaries sit in INAS's own registration system and the e-INAS platform (2021). A national social registry was still to be established in 2025. A national postal addressing code was piloted in Manhiça in 2020, with six cities planned over the following five years.

In August 2026 validation of a beneficiary and humanitarian assistance management system was completed, with national launch stated for October 2026. The platform is backed by the World Food Programme and built to interoperate with the social-support database, to curb duplicate and fictitious registrations. The disaster management agency INGD announced a biometric registry of aid recipients, piloted in Beira, to stop duplicate assistance. No data-protection basis, retention rule or enrolment target has been published for it.

In 2018 interoperability between the land information system, municipal cadastres and the Justice Ministry's property registry was still a project goal. A NUIT is compulsory before business licensing, which links the tax and business registers by procedure.

Sectoral management information systems

In 2025 the World Bank recorded custom-built financial management, payroll, tax administration and human resources systems, with customs on commercial off-the-shelf software. The treasury system settles through real-time gross settlement but is only partially used by ministries, departments and agencies.

Customs has cleared through the Janela Única Electrónica single window since 2012, which had cut clearance steps from 32 to 8 by 2017. Decree 37/2023 made electronic lodging of declarations mandatory. On 18 November 2025 the Council of Ministers authorised negotiation of a new single-window concession by direct award with Escopil Internacional and SGS. Mozambique ranks first of 54 African states on tax and revenue mobilisation (2023).

In 2025 StatEduc, the education management information system, still relied on physical questionnaires and aggregated records, with a fully digital entry platform only planned. Milange district in Zambézia has taken its education data system down to school level since mid-2024. The education ministry's technology plan, last updated in 2019, sets out student records keyed to the Unique Citizen Identification Number.

In 2016–17 primary health care facilities recorded and reported on paper registers and forms, with 52 monthly reporting forms mandated. A memorandum signed with the United States in December 2025 commits to a national health data exchange and a health data warehouse. It provides for a US-built patient tracking system to pass to Mozambican management by June 2026. In July 2024 that system's facility databases were being merged district by district onto a cloud server in each province.

In July 2024 the courts' electronic case system ran in only 21 pilot courts, with Law 8/2024 on electronic case processing still unregulated. Two climate-informed malaria forecasting pilots on DHIS2 stalled when the climate data feed stopped (2025). A KOICA-funded digital road traffic-offence monitoring centre began operating in Greater Maputo in December 2025. In August 2025 the road transport institute signed a memorandum with CEDSIF, the finance ministry's systems centre, to host and modernise its driver-licensing and vehicle systems. In August 2024 the President laid the first stone for a building in Maputo to house the interior ministry's public security management information system. It is financed with US$11m from Korea's export credit bank and was due in December 2025.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Since 3 August 2026 the Tax Authority has been issuing tax clearance certificates requested online through the taxpayer portal, against a stated ten working days. In 2025 the national public service portal was informational only, so a citizen could read about a service but not transact through it. In March 2026 the World Bank counted 66,155 people using digitally enabled government services, against a project target of 2 million. In September 2026 the Portal do Cidadão offered about 26 services to more than 3,000 users, ahead of its official launch. Passports and driving licences go first in the inventory of public services drawn up for digitisation.

In 2025 the World Bank found an online tax portal supporting registration, filing and payment. E-procurement is in use and publishes both tender notices and awarded contracts. An electronic procurement system to run the cycle from planning to payment was launched in April 2025, for phased rollout. Its platform supplier was chosen by international tender in February 2026. A public job portal and an online social protection portal are in use.

Decree No. 40/2026 routes notification of low-risk activities preferably through the e-BAU platform, treating paper as exceptional. From 8 November 2026 industrial licence applications move onto e-BAU, with licences issued electronically under a QR code. An application is deemed approved where the authority misses its deadline. Since 1 September 2026 applications for special transit licences for oversize and overweight vehicles run end to end online. An integrated centre issuing identity cards, passports, DIRE residence documents and driving licences opened in Maputo on 14 October 2026.

In 2023 no central government portal authenticated users with the national identity card: the e-visa platform and the SIGAV passport-appointment system used ordinary login credentials. In January 2026 the ICT institute launched a platform for citizens to report personal-data breaches and misuse. In November 2025 the finance ministry and prosecutor-general agreed a confidential national line for reporting corruption in public procurement. The municipal development programme's grievance portal states on its page that it is offline and read-only.

In October 2026 the digital transformation agency held its first digital service delivery design lab with the World Bank, bringing public bodies together to pick priority services and map user journeys.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

As at September 2020 Maputo ran property tax on SISPGIA and economic-activity fees on SISTAE. Revenue collection and management ran on SIRR and SIGERE, the budget on M17 and solid-waste management on SGRSU. The regulator INCM piloted a national postal addressing code in Manhiça in 2020, with six cities planned over five years. No provincial or district administration had been found connected to a national data exchange as at August 2026. No sub-national service portal had been found in operation.

Rural digital data capture

As at 2024 the revised Civil Registration Code had established the electronic civil registration system e-SiRCEV as integrated with the national identity and health systems. The education data system still ran on physical questionnaires in 2025. A pilot begun in 2024 lets hospital staff send a birth straight into the civil registry, starting in five districts of Inhambane. One district of Zambézia has taken its education data system down to school level since mid-2024.

INCM put a 30-site rural connectivity project out to tender in August 2025. By October 2025 it had awarded Vodacom a 60-station build across nine provinces. A separate World Bank operation runs last-mile rural broadband on the same infrastructure as rural electrification. Rural electricity access stood at a small fraction of the urban rate in 2022. The agriculture ministry said in December 2025 that it was designing a single portal for producer and plot registration, certification and traceability. Most rural land was unregistered in 2021.

Technology

AI

A National Commission for Artificial Intelligence, created by a decree in force since April 2026, began advising government in May 2026. INTIC put a first National Artificial Intelligence Strategy out for public consultation between 4 May and 4 June 2026. The text must pass a multisectoral working group convened by the communications minister, then the commission, then the Council of Ministers. The draft, covering 2026–2031, rests on five principles: ethics and human rights, transparency and accountability, technological security, digital inclusion, and data sovereignty. It cites UNESCO's ethics recommendation, the OECD AI Principles and the EU AI Act among its reference instruments. The work is backed by UNESCO, the ITU, the EU, the African Union and the World Bank.

In December 2025 the World Bank recorded no AI law, and found preparing one not a priority given more pressing gaps in the legal framework for data. On 22 October 2026 the education minister said a law on AI in education would centre on ethical and responsible use, fundamental rights and copyright. He spoke at the launch of MAZA IA, an AI learning tool built by MOZEDUCA with Vodacom. The Africa AI Governance Index ranked Mozambique 34th of 54 in October 2026, with 0.96 out of 4.

Since November 2025 the central bank has answered the public through an AI chatbot around the clock. In October 2026 the governor said the bank was implementing an internal Artificial Intelligence Policy for responsible, secure and transparent use. In September 2026 the centre running the state's financial systems invited expressions of interest for an AI consultancy. A malaria forecasting platform sits installed but not yet in use in the production health information system (2026). In September 2026 Mozambique announced it would extend PAAD, an AI-driven immunisation analytics platform built by Zenysis with the health ministry, from 15 districts to more than 80.

An artificial-intelligence association launched in Maputo on 26 August 2026, presented by INTIC as a platform joining government, academia, the private sector and civil society. In November 2024 researchers published the first manually translated Portuguese-Emakhuwa news corpus. The communications minister conceded in late July 2026 that national compute cannot support more advanced models. In early August he told an investors' forum that Mozambique should become Southern Africa's AI hub.

ICT Industry

In 2025 the startups a commercial tracker lists for Mozambique ran to single figures. The overwhelming majority of enterprises, and the great bulk of the labour force, operate informally. Mozambique scored 13.8 out of 100 for secure employment on the Ibrahim Index in 2023, 49th of 54 African states and down 2.9 points over the decade.

Tmcel, the state operator, ended 2025 with 1.79 million mobile customers and fixed lines down 20 per cent to 15,537. The government opened the selection of a strategic investor for part of its shareholding.

MSource laid the first stone of a US$3 million phone and laptop assembly plant on 22 December 2025, with an initial capacity of 80,000 units a month. In 2024 telecommunications, computer and information services ran a deficit of US$118.4 million, on credits of US$9.1 million.

Innovation ecosystem

Dealroom recorded no disclosed venture capital investment in Mozambique over the trailing twelve months (2025). In 2025 it tracked nine startups with a combined enterprise value of USD 20 million, ranking the country 36th among African markets. Mozambique placed 128th on WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024, in the bottom quartile of the economies ranked.

Three hubs and incubators are listed in AfriLabs' member directory (2025): Iniciativa para Democracia e Cidadania, ideiaLab Mozambique and Ideario Hub. The universal-access fund's 2025 regulation reserves 3 per cent of its spending for innovation. The data centre inaugurated at Universidade Eduardo Mondlane in August 2026 is meant to open its capacity to start-ups and small technology firms.

Capacity

Literacy

Human Resources in Education scored 87.9 out of 100 on the Ibrahim Index in 2023, 21st of 54 African states and up 6.7 points over the decade. Education Quality fell 3.2 points over the same period to 28.0, 41st of 54. The Education sub-category as a whole rose 5.6 points to 52.0, 27th of 54 (2023).

Gross tertiary enrolment is 7.3%, and science and engineering graduates are 9.6% of all graduates, ranking Mozambique 111th of 133 economies (2024).

The 5G licences awarded in June 2026 oblige operators to connect schools and community institutions, with equipment, installation and support included. The education ministry's 2019 technology plan sets out interoperability between its own systems and the rest of government.

On 21 September 2026 INTIC and a rural mobile-multimedia firm agreed to run awareness campaigns, workshops and community forums on digital culture, cybersecurity and data protection. They are to run in Portuguese and where possible in local languages. No budget, reach target or start date was stated. 7,033 civil servants had been trained in digital skills by November 2025, on the government's count.

Training and skills

By July 2026 the World Bank-funded PADIM project had run 14 training actions for 30 INTIC technicians and trained 2,032 professionals in cybersecurity. Mozambican specialists hold SIM3 certifications individually, the instrument by which a response team's readiness is assessed.

In 2025 Mozambique had both a digital skills strategy and a digital skills training programme for civil servants, pitched at basic digital skills. In April 2026 the public administration training institutes put three free online courses for public servants live, basic computer skills among them. There were 56 higher education institutions at the end of 2023, Eduardo Mondlane University the oldest and largest of them. The ITU is running the regulatory and policy component of the EU-funded VaMoz Digital! programme over 2023–2027, which covers a digital skills curriculum.

In 2025 a public job portal was in use for registration, vacancy search and applications, including public-sector recruitment. MozDevz, a Mozambican community, trains young people in ICT and STEM.

In August 2026 Escola Superior de Jornalismo and Universidade Politécnica were revising curricula, training staff and drafting responsible-use rules for AI, without banning the technology. In October 2026 the ICT institute, UNDP and the electoral commission trained 26 institutional trainers in artificial intelligence for election administration. The commission's 2027 plan carries staff training in the use of AI for electoral communication.

Research institutions

Universidade Eduardo Mondlane's data centre, inaugurated in August 2026, is tied to the university's ICT master plan for 2026–2029. Its intended uses include private-cloud services and access for students, researchers, start-ups and small technology firms.

In June 2025 the Digital Acceleration Project carried an allocation for MoRENet backbone and last-mile bandwidth for universities and TVET institutions. In January 2026 the World Bank named MoRENet expansion among eighteen contracts it called transformational. The higher-education sector it would connect counted 56 institutions at the end of 2023, with Eduardo Mondlane the oldest and largest.

Inclusion

Access to services

The PSSB, Mozambique's flagship social transfer programme, reached 709,028 beneficiaries by the end of 2024 against a target of 1,199,573. That is 59 per cent of the 2024 goal, on a Centre for Public Integrity assessment.

First-time identity cards have been free of charge nationwide since November 2023. The waiver has since been extended to 2027 and widened from the identity card to the birth certificate and the cédula pessoal. In October 2026, at the new integrated Centro Pronto in Maputo, a private service fee plus VAT lifted an adult identity-card renewal from MZN 160 to MZN 1,058. The central bank allows social-protection beneficiary cards to stand as an alternative identity document for opening a payment account (2023).

A toll-free line for complaints about public administration opened in June 2021. The water regulator takes complaints and appeals online through its RECO platform (June 2026). The central bank received 1,164 complaints against financial institutions in 2025, down 35.7%, and sanctioned nine institutions. It received 487 in the first half of 2026, 44 of them against the two mobile-money issuers.

Financial access points rose 47.0 per cent between December 2024 and May 2026, driven by a 54.0 per cent increase in e-money agents. Article 23 of the 2025 universal-access fund regulation makes a tariff 20 per cent below the normal one mandatory on universal-access services. The June 2026 5G licences oblige operators to connect at least 3,000 schools and 1,000 community institutions including health centres, with equipment, installation and support included.

Digital divides

Mozambique's June 2026 5G licences oblige operators to supply 710,000 subsidised smartphones to low-income users, small firms and students on instalment terms. The handsets are 420,000 4G and 290,000 5G. Internet access stood below a quarter of the population when the Internet for All project launched in December 2024. Only a minority of the population held a smartphone in 2021. Internet access, cost and computer availability together form one of Mozambique's ten worst-scoring governance indicators (2023).

In 2018 GSMA found around 9 per cent of women in Mozambique using mobile internet, against 22 per cent of men. The mobile phone ownership gap was about 24 per cent in 2018, then the third largest among the African markets it surveyed.

Article 14 of the 2025 universal-access fund regulation reserves up to 14 per cent of FSAU spending for digital inclusion aimed at vulnerable groups. The 2025 regulation's principal category takes 55 per cent. In January 2026 the World Bank cut the digital acceleration project's digital inclusion allocation from US$40 million to US$30 million. Twenty-six regions of the country had no bank branch (2025). Rural households had a small fraction of urban electricity access in 2022.

Data

National statistics

Mozambique's next census is set for 1 to 15 August 2027. The digital system meant to run it failed in testing in August 2026. By October 2026 the statistics institute had secured about US$60m of the US$110m it needs.

In 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators recorded no labour force survey in the previous ten years. The same indicators record three or more health surveys, two household surveys and two agricultural surveys over those years. A population census and a business census fall inside the window, though the agricultural census does not. Mozambique scores 70–89 per cent on data use and data products, 50–69 per cent on data services and infrastructure, and 20–49 per cent on data sources (2023).

The Ibrahim Index rated the Capacity of the Statistical System at 53.0 out of 100 in 2023, 23rd of 54 African states and up 8.7 points over the decade. INCM's 2024 communications regulation report carries per-operator coverage gaps and provincial subscriber breakdowns.

In 2025 the World Bank found government data management practices fragmented. Arrangements for governing that data were only planned or in progress, with the function sitting inside another institution (2025). Its December 2025 diagnostic recommends an inventory of the data held across government, with INAGE to lead a common data exchange infrastructure. Around 80% of the labour force and around 90% of enterprises operate informally (2024).

Open data

Mozambique had no open government data portal in 2025. Law No. 34/2014 grants a right to information and gives the authorities a maximum of twenty-one days to answer.

Accessibility of Public Records stood at 20.6 out of 100 in 2023, 38th of 54 African states and down 2.7 points over the decade. Disclosure of Public Records, which measures what the state publishes of its own accord, is 30.7, 33rd of 54.

Across the 23 data categories Open Data Watch assessed for Mozambique in 2024, the average score is 4.5 out of 10, and 14 of the 23 score 5.0 or below.

In 2024 Open Data Watch scored education outcomes 7 out of 10 and balance of payments 6.5. Poverty scored 3.0, and national accounts, agriculture and crime 3.5 each. The Banco de Moçambique publishes national payment system statistics on a fixed schedule. The e-procurement system publishes tender notices and awarded contracts (2025).

In 2025 the rules of the national instant payment scheme were available only to its participants. In April 2026 a Washington court was asked to compel the State Department to release the data sharing agreement attached to Mozambique's health memorandum with the United States. A national data governance policy reached a second zero draft in November 2025.

Geospatial data

In July 2026 a programme financed by the African Development Bank and Korea certified 30 specialists from the meteorology, disaster management, cartography and remote-sensing, roads and water bodies. It left nine LiDAR-equipped drone systems, simulators, unmanned surface vessels and data platforms. The cohort was deployed half-trained into the January 2026 Marracuene floods, which the district recorded with no loss of life. The bank's account names no custodian institution, running cost or arrangement for sharing the mapping output.

WMO credits the meteorology institute's early warning system with holding Cyclone Freddy's 2023 toll under 200. The system runs on satellite, radar and surface data relayed by SMS and community radio. A US$7.89 million Systematic Observations Financing Facility investment through WFP, effective April 2024, is to upgrade the national weather observing network. By June 2026 no station had yet been installed or improved.

In February 2026 INCM deployed Starlink terminals donated by SpaceX at accommodation and coordination centres in flood-affected areas. The state operator began proof-of-concept testing of Angola's ANGOSAT-2 satellite for telecommunications services in August 2026, under a bilateral memorandum signed that June.

In September 2025 a World Bank-commissioned strategy set out a five-year plan to embed satellite crop monitoring in the agriculture ministry. Neither the ministry nor the statistics institute held a formal remote-sensing mandate in 2026. No national space or geospatial data policy had been adopted as at August 2026.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

The health memorandum the United States and Mozambique signed on 15 December 2025 penalises failure on a Data Sharing Agreement executed the same day. It attaches neither that agreement nor the Specimen Sharing Agreement beside it. Its text carries no personal-data, data-ownership, hosting or residency clause anywhere.

In March 2026 the State Department put a subset of the health memoranda on its website, then removed public access. It has published none of the related agreements, including Mozambique's. It refused a public-interest fee waiver and had reached no final determination by 2 April 2026, when Public Citizen sued in the District of Columbia. Mozambique is one of sixteen countries named in that case, whose freedom-of-information request expressly seeks the data sharing agreement.

The memorandum is the instrument behind the planned United States funding of Mozambique's health data systems through to 2030. The money is meant to build a national health data exchange and data warehouse. It is also meant to build the SIS-RME electronic medical record, which is to absorb the US-built SESP patient tracking system. SESP was due to pass to Mozambican management by 20 June 2026.

The Ministry of Economy's approach to SpaceX on connectivity and digital infrastructure was still exploratory as at July 2026, with nothing signed. In 2024 USAID donated computers to the Attorney General's Office for its digitalisation. On 29 October 2026 the US Embassy ran a one-day course in AI, data policy and cloud computing for civil servants with Google.

China activities

Mozambique became a founding signatory of the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization in Shanghai on 16 July 2026, one of 29 founding states and one of ten African ones. At the meeting that followed, Xi Jinping pledged 5,000 AI training places over five years and AI meteorological systems for around 30 countries. In April 2026 the digital transformation agency signed a memorandum with Huawei on public-sector AI, Portuguese-language models, data centres and cloud. It also covers training about 5,000 technicians over three years. No value or data terms have been published for it.

China Eximbank financed a concessional loan to Tmcel for the first phase of modernising and expanding the national mobile network in 2020. Tmcel has since secured a second Eximbank facility for a further phase, reported as being on "very good terms" (2025). Alibaba Cloud's Africa local public cloud service runs from a Maputo data centre under BCX (2024), with no state agreement behind it.

China Eximbank is the second-largest external financier of Mozambique's recorded digital commitments, behind the World Bank, and both of its loans go to the same borrower, Tmcel.

EU activities

The European Union funds VaMOZ Digital!, a EUR 22.5 million grant (about US$24 million) running from 2022 to 2029. In June 2026 it signed a further digital transformation allocation under Global Gateway, with a stated focus on women's participation in the digital economy.

In May 2026 the European Union was among the backers of the national AI strategy's development, with UNESCO, the ITU, the African Union and the World Bank. The draft strategy takes the EU AI Act as one of its explicit reference instruments. In July 2026 Vodacom's group chief executive met President Chapo in Maputo to discuss connectivity, fibre expansion, AI adoption and digital inclusion. No amount, agreement or timeline was announced.

The European Union holds three of the recorded external digital commitments, but a smaller recorded value than either the World Bank or China Eximbank.

Gulf/UAE activities

No Gulf party has been found financing, building or running Mozambique's digital systems as at August 2026.

India activities

An Indian government brief of April 2025 lists an information technology park among the projects delivered in Mozambique under Indian concessional lines of credit. It gives no location, value or completion date for the park. In October 2026 CSM Tech, a GovTech firm based in Bhubaneswar, was reported to hold a US$174,000 contract from TradeMark Africa to build a transport and trade observatory for the Beira Corridor. The platform is to bring together port, road, rail and customs data.

Digital sovereignty

In September 2026 ATDI set a government data centre and a sovereign cloud among the bases of its 2026–2029 operational plan, to secure government information. On 21 September 2026 the state administration minister told the digital-transformation commission that data produced by the public administration must belong to the State.

Decree 71/2025, the data-centre regulation, carries provisions on data centres located abroad and on storing information classified as a state secret. Banco de Moçambique's Aviso 6/GBM/2026, gazetted on 31 August 2026, requires every bank and financial company to keep its primary data centre and principal systems in Mozambique. Globally operated systems are exempt on notice. INTIC's November 2025 zero draft of a data governance policy would classify government data in four tiers, up to secret or classified.

In July 2026 CEDSIF, the finance ministry's systems centre, tendered for backup power at its secondary data centre for critical financial systems. The e-government institute INAGE built CloudGov, a file-storage and sharing system for public institutions, in 2020. The identity and travel-document system runs under an eight-year concession awarded to Muhlbauer in 2018. Whether the state holds its source code, or can exit the concession, had not been found as at September 2026. The data jurisdiction, portability and exit terms of that concession had not been published as at September 2026. Nor had those of the December 2025 data-sharing agreement with the United States.

In February 2026 INTIC signed a memorandum with Brazil's National Institute of Information Technology on accreditation, audit and training for operating a Mozambican root certification authority.
