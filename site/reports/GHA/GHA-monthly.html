Centralised technical review of public-sector AI procurement

Ghana tightens scrutiny of public sector AI buys — ITWeb Africa, 2 October 2026

Ghana's communications minister announced that every AI system bought by a public institution will go through a centralised technical review. The national information technology agency will scope deployments against interoperability, data-protection, cybersecurity and local skills-transfer requirements, alongside procurement law.

Digital Health Policy and Strategy 2023-2027

WHO and UNDP support Ghana to integrate artificial intelligence into digital health strategy — WHO Regional Office for Africa, 12 October 2026

Ghana's Ministry of Health and Ghana Health Service, supported by WHO and UNDP, are reviewing the Digital Health Policy and Strategy 2023–2027 to incorporate AI, reported on 12 October 2026. The programme focuses on AI-enabled disease surveillance combining routine health data with climate data for malaria, cholera and meningitis. No start date or cost is given.

Ghana's Biometric SIM Plan Fixes the Right Gap, but the Data Safeguards Must Be Written Into the Rules — People of Internet, 18 October 2026

In an unsigned analysis, the outlet People of Internet argues that Ghana's plan to add biometric checks to SIM registration addresses a real gap but needs data safeguards written into the rules. Ghana's Data Protection Commission said it had registered 2,575 data controllers in 2026, more than in any previous year, and was adding spot checks, inspections and complaint investigations.

Ghana's President John Mahama said the United States has asked to reopen talks on the roughly US$109m five-year health compact that Ghana's Cabinet rejected, with a view to amending or removing the disputed clauses. UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents

Ghana to host UN convention signing on digital cargo documents on October 26 — MyJoyOnline, 16 October 2026

Ghana will host the signing ceremony for the UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra on 26 October 2026. The Ghana Shippers' Authority says the convention gives electronic bills of lading and similar documents the legal standing of paper ones, but that the benefits depend on ratification and readiness.

26 countries sign Accra Convention to boost trade facilitation, digitalisation — MyJoyOnline, 28 October 2026

Twenty-six states signed the UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra on 26 October 2026, with Ghana first. Known as the Accra Convention, it gives electronic cargo documents the legal effect of paper. Road, rail and air documents can serve as documents of title. Ghana's trade minister said it will ratify; the Convention needs ten ratifications.
