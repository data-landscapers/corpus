This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In July 2026 the second National Public Sector Reform Strategy went through a stakeholder validation workshop in Accra, and still needs sign-off from the Chief of Staff, Cabinet and Parliament. It aims to digitise all public services and bring key service providers onto one platform with the National Identification Authority. It runs to seven pillars, 27 programmes and 135 activities over five years. Officials at the workshop cited delayed fund releases in the first phase, fragmented coordination, weak performance management and gaps in digital service delivery.

The World Bank's 2025 assessment found that Ghana has a current digital transformation strategy with a named lead, while the whole-of-government machinery behind it is only planned or in draft. The National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025–2035 was published in December 2025. Its third pillar is digital infrastructure and inclusion, and its fourth is data access and governance. It was launched publicly in April 2026. In July the government announced a ten-year implementation plan it is developing with United Nations partners.

Older instruments include a National Data Strategy validated in July 2024 and the Digital Economy Policy and Strategy. The second version of the interoperability framework and enterprise architecture was issued in 2022 as a draft for stakeholder review. The 2024 National Cyber Security Policy and Strategy also covers data centres and cloud services.

In October 2026 the communications minister announced a centralised technical review of every AI system bought by a public institution. No directive or start date has been published. The same month he said his ministry is reviewing the policy, institutional and regulatory frameworks of the digital sector.

The 2025 assessment found that the documents behind the digital transformation strategy, the data-governance arrangements and the public-service digital skills programme are not published. It found no open source policy for government software. The oldest open-data instrument found is the sectoral GHEITI policy of December 2016, which sets no publication timetable and names no open licence.

Legislation and regulation

The National Identity Register (Amendment) Regulations 2026 were gazetted on 27 March 2026 and commenced on 9 June. The National Identification Authority publicised them on 16 July. The transactions that require biometric verification are set by Regulation 7 of the 2012 regulations, which L.I. 2523 leaves in place. The identity stack is Act 707 of 2006, Act 750 of 2008, Act 950 of 2017 and L.I. 2111 as amended by L.I. 2356 (2018). An organisation in breach faces 500 to 2,000 penalty units.

The Electronic Communications Act 2008 (Act 775) covers telecommunications, spectrum, quality of service and universal access. The National Information Technology Agency Act 2008 (Act 771) reaches from data centres to digital signatures and e-waste. The Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843) establishes the Data Protection Commission. The Cybersecurity Act 2020 (Act 1038) is drafted to be read with eleven named enactments. It carries production orders, interception powers and a data-retention duty on service providers. On 7 September 2026 the communications minister said Parliament had passed the legislative instrument for biometric SIM verification.

The Data Harmonisation Bill 2025 went to public consultation in November 2025 and has not passed. No statute yet governs data exchange across government. The Virtual Asset Service Providers Act 2025 (Act 1154) takes a risk-based, technology-neutral approach, with capital-adequacy, custody and anti-money-laundering requirements.

In July 2026 the Bank of Ghana set out that digital credit is licensed under the Non-Bank Financial Institutions Act 2008 (Act 774), on paid-up capital of GHS 2,000,000. The regularisation deadline passed on 30 June 2026. The Bank named twenty unlicensed lending applications on 3 August 2026 and a further twenty on 8 September. It named a third twenty on 6 October. On 20 July it said a register of licensed providers would follow "in due course".

Data protection

On 12 October 2026 the Data Protection Commission referred a microfinance company that had not renewed its registration to the police criminal investigations department for possible prosecution. The same day it directed an Accra hospital to report to the police over an expired registration certificate. It had begun spot checks on organisations processing personal data without registering on 5 October. It registered 2,575 data controllers in 2026, more than in any earlier year. On 20 October it directed redaction of published records naming 1,964 Ghanaians evacuated from South Africa, and opened a regulatory review.

In 2026 the Commission received no treasury money, its whole GHS 9,720,400 budget coming from internally generated funds. It has a staff of 39. The cyber regulator on the same programme is funded from the Consolidated Fund.

The Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843) establishes the Commission, requires data controllers to register and grants rights of access, correction and erasure. Breach notification is mandatory under Act 843, under Act 1038 within 24 hours, and for financial institutions to the Bank of Ghana. A draft Data Protection Bill 2025 would add a 72-hour breach deadline and cross-border transfer rules for sensitive data. It had not been enacted by October 2026. The National AI Strategy recommends turning the Commission into an authority with offices in all sixteen regions.

A Governing Board chaired by Teki Akuetteh was sworn in at the end of July 2025. The World Bank found in 2025 that neither the authority nor the wider regime publishes reports on its oversight activity. The Commission's first-half 2026 account gives growth of 51.5% in regulatory activity and 98.4% in newly registered data controllers, with no base figures. Agencies drawing on the identity register must be approved user agencies, and the law gives no operational detail on their safeguards, fees or accountability (2025). A 2024 review found enforcement of cross-border transfer restrictions needs strengthening.

Regional collaboration

Ghana's central bank is testing cross-border payment links with two African partners, its governor said on 13 October 2026. It is testing interoperability between central bank digital currencies, stablecoins, bank accounts and mobile wallets in the Ghana–Rwanda and Ghana–Nigeria corridors. The Pan-African Payment and Settlement System has been extended to mobile wallets in the Ghana–Nigeria corridor. Ghana has twenty institutions live on that system (June 2026). It has a fintech passporting arrangement with Rwanda (February 2026). The governor said more than ten countries are being engaged to join it.

Smart Africa's cross-border digital identity pilot with Rwanda and Benin completed its first phase (October 2026). It connected the three national systems and verified identities end to end on synthetic data. The Ghana Card already serves as a travel document for Ghanaians entering from ECOWAS states. In March 2026 the ECOWAS–World Bank identity programme validated a cross-border interoperability strategy. Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger left ECOWAS in 2024 and launched a common biometric passport in January 2025.

A pilot data exchange with Côte d'Ivoire's customs was launched in Accra in July 2017. ECOWAS sent a technical mission in July 2025 to resolve interconnection problems still standing. A pilot link with Togo's customs opened at Noépé–Akanu on 1 August 2023.

Ghana ratified the African Union's Malabo Convention in 2019. On 26 October 2026 26 states, Ghana first, signed the United Nations Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra. It gives electronic cargo documents the legal effect of paper, and enters into force on the tenth ratification.

ECOWAS ministers adopted a Digital Sector Development Strategy for 2024–2029 at Cotonou on 4 October 2024. The Community budget for 2026–2028 carries no separate digital appropriation, and its only ICT-named programme line has a nil allocation. On 1 October 2026 the Bank of Ghana hosted the Liberian central bank's governor to study how its interbank payment company was set up.

Standards

By October 2026 Ghana's national identity system was connected to Smart Africa's cross-border data exchange, alongside Benin's and Rwanda's. On 20 October the three completed the pilot's first phase, verifying identities end to end on synthetic data. An operational pilot for border checks comes next, subject to national approvals. A technical mission in Kigali in April 2026 worked through trust chains, API contracts and incident response.

The e-Government Interoperability Framework launched in 2009 stalled on funding shortfalls and unclear mandates, leaving government data fragmented across agencies. Version 2.0 of the framework was issued in 2022 as a draft for stakeholder review. In 2025 a framework and a Government Service Bus were in operation but only partly used across agencies, and legacy systems had not been moved onto the exchange platform.

The World Bank found in 2025 that public procurement runs on an electronic system for tendering and contract management, used to enforce procurement standards. The financial management system applies one budget and accounting classification at central and sub-national level. Ghana has no open source policy for government software.

Act 771 of 2008 gives the National Information Technology Agency functions over digital signatures. The Cyber Security Authority accredits practitioners and certifies products. No one may provide a cybersecurity service without its two-year licence.

The 5G licence terms of July 2026 set cell-edge download speeds, verified by drive testing and site audits. In April 2026 the West African telecommunications regulators' association finalised technical reports on 5G, subsea-cable resilience, cybersecurity and consumer protection. In October 2026 the central bank governor said the bank will review how banks have implemented its revised Cyber and Information Security Directive. The bank has also directed banks to give their fraud functions direct access to the chief executive (2026).

Public debate and participation in policymaking

On 5 August 2026 the Right to Information Commission fined 254 ministries, departments, agencies and assemblies GH₵20,000 each. They had missed the statutory deadline to file their 2025 annual reports under the Right to Information Act 2019. In 2025 the World Bank found the law in force with a working mechanism for requesting public records. The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores both accessibility of public records and their disclosure at around half marks (2023).

In 2025 Ghana had no national online consultation or e-participation channel. It had no feedback mechanism through which service complaints reach government and are answered. The government reports that citizens are consulted in the design of the national service portal. In July 2026 the communications ministry took industry comment on a draft digital-economy fund bill at a stakeholder session. The communications regulator must hold public hearings on first-instance licence applications and publish licence terms.

In March 2026 the head of the IMANI think tank said the One Million Coders programme lacks a strategy for jobs. Civil society has pressed publicly for community-network licensing reform. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index digital freedom scores 86.0 and ranks 4th of 54, down 7.7 points over the decade to 2023. Media freedom scores 69.7 and ranks 7th, down 11.6 points.

In September 2026 a senior nursing officer was charged with abetting the publication of false news under the Criminal Offences Act and remanded without bail. The charge concerned videos from a UK-based TikTok account. The Presidency asked two media bodies to draft a national roadmap on harmful online content, including alternatives to arrest. On a court order the state can have service providers block, filter and take down content, with a penalty of 100 penalty units for each day of non-compliance.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The Ghana Card fee schedule in force since 2 February 2026, approved under L.I. 2512, charges GH¢30 for first registration from age 25 and GH¢200 for a replacement. The identity authority's 2026 appropriation of GHS 430.5 million is GHS 321.1 million from the treasury and GHS 109.4 million of retained fees. GHS 100 million of those fees come from the Ghana Card project.

Margins ID Group's costs were to be recovered from the card project's proceeds over a 15-year term (2019). In September 2025 the authority's acting head said the government owed the partner about US$64–68 million. A Government Support Agreement obliges the state to top up any monthly shortfall. He said SIM registration had not brought in the verification income expected.

In the 2026 estimates the telecoms regulator's GHS 483.8 million is all its own income, as is GHS 10.5 million of the IT agency's GHS 22.9 million. Passport fees fund GHS 128.8 million of the GHS 148.8 million passport programme. The universal-service fund's retained income is budgeted at GHS 198.8 million for 2026, against GHS 2.0 million for 2025.

In July 2026 a draft bill would turn that fund into a wider Digital Economy and Innovation Development Fund. In May 2026 a draft NITA bill carried a 1% levy on ICT services. The Cybersecurity Fund was still to be made operational before the end of 2026.

The 2026 estimates carry GHS 138 million of donor funding on the ministry's ICT infrastructure and regulation sub-programme, against GHS 81.5 million in 2025. The refresh of the government data centres in Accra and Kumasi is procured under an IDA credit (June 2026). In July 2026 Parliament approved three digital tax-administration agreements. The Minority objected that the VAT platform's foreign operator could take up to 3% of the revenue collected.

MoUs and other agreements

In December 2025 Ghana signed a US$1bn strategic investment agreement with the United Arab Emirates for an innovation and artificial-intelligence hub. In September 2025 the identification authority signed an exclusive two-year memorandum with the US firm Trust Stamp. Its aim is a revenue-sharing contract for tokenised identity services. In July 2026 the Youth Employment Agency signed a memorandum with Ghana Digital Centres on Microsoft-certified training in cloud, AI, cybersecurity and data analytics. In August 2026 the communications ministry announced a partnership with Digital Policy Alert and the St. Gallen Endowment on digital governance and digital trade. On 14 August 2026 Ghana renewed its country programme memorandum with TradeMark Africa, which covers digital customs systems at the Akanu–Noépé border post.

New investments

In 2026 MTN began a three-year programme putting US$1.1bn behind its Ghanaian infrastructure, where its previous commitment spread US$1bn over five years. The programme is announced and not contracted. MTN Ghana's capital spending fell sharply in the first half of 2026.

Around twenty external financing commitments to Ghana's digital sector are on the record, with start years from 2015 to 2026 and windows running to 2030, together worth roughly US$2.7 billion attributable to Ghana. That is money committed and signed rather than money disbursed, and it mixes operator capital expenditure, equity, grants and sovereign lending. Connectivity alone takes nearly three-quarters of it and training about a tenth, while digital identity draws a single small commitment.

China Eximbank signed a loan in December 2019 for national-security communications and one in May 2020 for rural telephony. In October 2020 it signed two more for distance learning, at five tertiary institutions and at the Institute of Local Government Studies. Parliament approved a US$100 million China Development Bank loan in November 2020 for an Accra traffic management project, with Huawei as contractor.

The World Bank's US$200 million Ghana Digital Acceleration Project runs 2022-2027 across broadband, digital financial services, digital public services and skills. In March 2026 the World Bank recorded US$18.3 million disbursed, rated progress towards the project's objectives moderately unsatisfactory, and said implementation was constrained by commitment authorisations and budget ceilings. A June 2026 report said no disbursement had been made since September 2024. Germany is funding a EUR 12.5 million programme on digital transformation for inclusive entrepreneurship over 2023-2026.

The European Union's 2021-2027 programme for Ghana sets no digital priority area and makes no digital allocation. It treats digital as a cross-cutting theme across green growth, sustainable cities and governance. Its indicative EUR 203 million covered 2021-2024. A 2024 amending decision allocated EUR 136 million for 2025-2027, again with no digital priority area. The ministry's account of the National AI Strategy launch in April 2026 gives no money figure.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

On 11 September 2026 both 700 MHz 5G lots went to MTN Ghana, the sole qualified applicant, for US$100.9m. Telecel Ghana took the three 2.3 GHz lots it applied for. On 2 October MTN Ghana announced the award of all three 3 GHz lots for US$101.1m, on 15-year licences. The regulator had put eleven lots on the market on 16 July 2026 at a total reserve of US$230m. MTN Ghana, the one operator designated as holding significant market power, paid a 40% premium on its reserve. On 15 July the regulator had deleted the exclusivity clause from NGIC's wholesale 5G licence. Licensees must reach 70% population coverage by 6 March 2027, partly from existing networks.

In 2025 the GSMA reported 3G and 4G population coverage both above 99%. Just under 70% of Ghanaians used the internet in 2023. About two-thirds of the population were unique mobile subscribers (2024). Only 48% of MTN's subscribers were on 4G at 30 June 2026. In October 2026 the minister put the year's programme at 800 new cell sites and 2,000 upgrades across the two largest operators.

In the third quarter of 2024 fixed broadband fell 6.83% to 216,143 subscriptions. In June 2026 MTN cut a 100 Mbps fibre package from GH¢987 to GH¢299. In August 2026 only 4 of 39 networks peered at all three exchange points, and Accra-IX passed 200 Gbps. Six submarine cables land at Accra, 2Africa among them since February 2024. A government-owned backbone of about 1,000 km runs from the Burkina Faso border to Accra, privately operated since 2021. An open-access land route to Lagos through Togo and Benin was commissioned in May 2025.

Under the Electronic Communications Act 2008, international capacity must be offered to all providers on a cost-based, non-discriminatory basis. 5G licensees owe 1% of net revenue to the universal-access fund. There is no licence class for community networks. In June 2025 36 of 78 authorised internet providers were not operating.

Data Storage

In October 2025 Digital Realty opened Ghana's largest data centre in Accra. ACR2 is carrier-neutral, with 1.7 MW of critical IT load and 500 racks, and is connected to the 2Africa submarine cable. Equinix's US$320m acquisition of MainOne, completed on 5 April 2022, brought its Accra data centre and landing station under US ownership. Accra also hosts PAIX, Africa Data Centres' ACC1, Etix and an Uptime Tier IV facility run by Onix.

In 2024 eight to ten commercial facilities carried about 4.8 MW of live IT load, with more under construction than in service. Africa Data Centres has announced a build at the Trade Fair site in central Accra. In 2025 AWS, Azure and Google Cloud served Ghana from South Africa, at latencies above 100 milliseconds. Power is costly, and only about half of generating capacity reaches the end user (2024).

In June 2024 the communications ministry said it was working to have government data held abroad hosted in Ghana. In 2025 agencies drew on a single government cloud on government-owned infrastructure. No policy governs where government's own data may be hosted. The National Data Centre was being modernised for AI services in July 2026. On 15 October 2026 the minister said US$250 million is earmarked for the first phase of a national AI compute centre. The centre is in procurement, with phase one due within 24 months.

In 2026 the minister restated a policy that sensitive financial data must be stored in Ghana. A health AI policy in development holds that patient data should not be stored abroad without safeguards. The draft data protection bill would add localisation and cross-border transfer rules. The National AI Strategy 2025–2035 sets a compute target of 10²¹ FLOPS by 2027, rising to 10²⁵ by 2035. On 9 October 2026 the science minister said the atomic energy commission's proposed AI data centre must operate under human oversight.

Energy

In 2024 solar supplied 0.11% of Ghana's total energy supply and hydro 6.2%, while oil and natural gas together supplied nearly two-thirds.

89.5% of the population had electricity in 2023. Access was 97.5% in urban areas and 77.8% in rural ones that year. In August 2026 the government launched a GHS598m project to connect 206 communities in the Volta Region within six months. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index Ghana's access-to-energy score of 58.3 places it 14th of 54, up 9.5 points over 2014–2023.

The national grid failed nationwide on 29 July and again on 20 August 2026. The second failure was a fault on the Akosombo–Volta transmission line. In the World Bank's 2023 enterprise survey firms averaged 2.9 power outages a month, and 73.8% reported outages. In 2026 one operator stated that its Accra data centre runs on a 640 kW solar farm by day and the grid by night, with three 456 kW backup generators.

On the utilities regulator's schedule effective 1 April 2026 a lifeline household using up to 30 kWh a month pays GHp 86.90 per kWh. Use of 31–300 kWh is charged at GHp 196.88, and use above 300 kWh at GHp 260.15.

In 2024 five pilot mini-grids served about 10,000 people on Lake Volta islands. The off-grid policy requires every mini-grid to be state-owned. The Scaling-Up Renewable Energy Programme, launched in May 2025, plans mini-grids, solar home systems and net-metered rooftop installations.

Technical Capacity

In July 2026 officials named delayed fund releases, fragmented coordination, weak performance management and gaps in digital service delivery as persisting challenges in public-sector reform. In December 2021 the European Union had assessed the National Information Technology Agency as lacking the digital-government planning skills and the funding needed for the platforms the government envisages.

A 2025 survey of Ghana's tech sector found it concentrated in Greater Accra. A large share of those working in it belonged to no tech community. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index Ghana ranks in the bottom half of Africa on the human resources behind its education system (2023). Under the Cybersecurity Act 2020 the Cyber Security Authority accredits cybersecurity professionals and certifies products.

Ghana sits in the 'High' group of the UN's 2024 E-Government Development Index. In 2025 the World Bank found the public-service HR system custom-built and in use, but still running manual, paper-based processes. The public-service digital skills programme teaches basic digital skills and data literacy from freely available material. Its programme document has not been published.

Cybersecurity

The Cyber Security Authority recorded GH¢209.7 million of cybercrime losses on 4,938 incidents between January and September 2026, against GH¢22.5 million on 4,604 incidents in the whole of 2025. Ghana sits in the top tier of the ITU's 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index. In 2025 fraud cases reported by banks and payment providers rose 48% to 24,778, almost all of the rise electronic fraud at payment providers. On 1 October 2026 the central bank said it is developing a fraud intelligence hub for financial institutions.

In August 2026 the Authority fined the Office of the Registrar of Companies GH¢240,000 and an unlicensed service provider GH¢120,000. On 18 August it fined a Big Four firm's Ghana practice GH¢360,000 for providing regulated services without a licence. The same day the two declared the issues resolved in a joint statement. The registry called its sanction premature and asked the Attorney-General's Office to intervene.

The Cybersecurity Act 2020 (Act 1038) makes the Authority the regulator that licenses providers, accredits professionals and certifies products. Owners of critical information infrastructure must report an incident within 24 hours and submit audit reports. A 2021 notice designated ICT, banking, energy and health providers as critical information infrastructure. In October 2026 the minister said 13 sectors are designated and about 200 institutions identified as owners. A National Cyber Security Policy and Strategy was adopted in 2024.

In October 2021 the national response team's constituency was eight sectoral teams, omitting energy, health and transport. The Act makes each sector pay for its own team. Its eighteen-member Joint Cybersecurity Committee is drawn mostly from intelligence, defence and law-enforcement bodies. The 2026 estimates make the Authority 86.0% treasury-funded, GHS 21,801,749 of a GHS 25,346,198 appropriation.

In October 2026 the Authority said Ghana led an operation that arrested eight suspects and rescued 120 suspected trafficking victims at cybercrime centres in Greater Accra. On 22 October it and the technology agency ordered every government institution to patch two critical PHP vulnerabilities within seven days.

DPI

Data Exchange

The Data Harmonisation Bill 2025, which would create a National Data Exchange Platform, went to public consultation in November 2025 and has not passed. A separate Data Exchange Act was still only proposed in July 2026. On 15 October 2026 the communications minister said his ministry is developing a national data exchange hub to connect 48 government data registries. On 21 October he said the registries will keep their own databases, and developers will query them through one backbone.

In 2025 the World Bank found a Government Service Bus and an interoperability framework that agencies use only in part. Ghana's customs, tax, financial management, pension and public investment systems exchange data with other government systems. Most of their documentation is internal and unpublished. The eGovernment Interoperability Framework of 2009 stalled on funding shortfalls and unclear mandates. Procurement for an exchange was re-issued in 2019 and again in 2022 without a clear outcome.

In 2025 the Ghana Card had largely replaced the health insurance card and the taxpayer number. Full integration with hospital records had not been achieved. A newborn's identity number comes through a point-to-point link between the Births and Deaths Registry and the National Identification Authority (2025). In 2024 holders still carried voter, health insurance, passport and driving-licence credentials alongside the card. The national household registry presents itself as the hub for social protection data, keyed on its own identifiers.

By October 2026 Ghana's identity system was connected to Smart Africa's cross-border data exchange, alongside Benin's and Rwanda's. The pilot lets the three verify each other's identity cards for travel. Its first phase was completed on synthetic data on 20 October 2026.

Digital Identity and CRVS

The National Identity Register (Amendment) Regulations 2026 came into force on 9 June 2026. They bar organisations from photocopying, scanning or keeping copies of the Ghana Card, and require biometric verification against its chip or the register. Banks, telecoms operators, hospitals, insurers and government agencies have until 2 November 2026 to integrate. The sanctions end in withdrawal of an operating licence.

The system rests on Act 707 of 2006, Act 750 of 2008, Act 950 of 2017 and the 2012 regulations as amended. The register is a biometric database that matches every new registration's fingerprints. It held more than 19.5 million enrolments in May 2026. The card is required for banking, passports, driving licences and land registration. Since 2 February 2026 first registration has cost GH¢30 from age 25, and a replacement GH¢200. A paid 24-hour centre opened in Accra in August 2026.

The card number has been the taxpayer identifier since April 2021. The Bank of Ghana requires the card for transactions at licensed financial institutions. It has been an ICAO-compliant travel document within ECOWAS since March 2022. On 8 April 2026 the Authority said the card "has not been activated for financial transaction purposes". In September its head said an electronic wallet on the card was in its final stages, with no date given.

The Births and Deaths Registry registered 677,140 births in 2022, 92.7 per cent coverage. In March 2024 a system was launched to give babies born in health facilities an identity number at birth registration. A 2025–2030 plan to digitise civil registration was validated in July 2025. The identity register was built under a 15-year partnership, with the Authority's share of the contract USD 124 million (2019).

In 2026 the Authority began school-based registration of children aged 6 to 14. On 9 October 2026 it said registration of children aged 0 to 5 would begin the following week. From 19 October 2026 Ghanaians in 11 countries can replace their cards online.

Digital Payments and Fintech

In August 2026 mobile money carried 1,046 million transactions worth GH¢518.8 billion. The interoperable service handled 35.7 million transactions worth GH¢6.5 billion in the same month, about 1.3 per cent of the total. Registered accounts reached 85.8 million, of which 26.4 million were active. In 2024 75.7 per cent of adults owned a mobile money account.

GHIPSS, a Bank of Ghana subsidiary, runs instant pay, mobile money interoperability, e-zwich, the national switch, the clearing house and the GhQR standard. In 2025 282.95 million of its 290.31 million interoperable transactions were person to person. Instant pay carries a GHS 50,000 cap on each transaction (2023). Revenue authority offices have taken no cash since 1 July 2021. In August 2025 the LEAP cash grant began piloting mobile money payments through GhanaPay in 50 districts. Its 99th cycle paid GH¢139.3 million to 350,580 households in December 2025.

The Payment Systems and Services Act 2019 (Act 987) requires every provider to run a dispute mechanism, with unresolved complaints adjudicated by the Bank of Ghana within 20 days. The Bank publishes an annual payment systems oversight report. After the regularisation deadline of 30 June 2026 it named 20 unlicensed loan apps on 3 August, 20 more on 8 September and 20 more on 6 October. It told banks and payment providers not to process for them.

In June 2026 twenty Ghanaian institutions, nineteen of them banks, were live on the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System. In October the governor said the system had been extended to mobile wallets in the Ghana–Nigeria corridor. MTN completed the separation of its mobile money business in the first quarter of 2026. The electronic transfer levy had been abolished by November 2025.

Registries

In June 2026 a report found manual processes still running alongside the Lands Commission's digital system, with its gains largely in Accra. No signing of a USD 85 million digitisation deal announced in 2023 had been confirmed. The Commission's online portal handles application tracking, payments and receipts (2025).

Under the Companies Act 2019 the Office of the Registrar of Companies was separated from the Registrar-General's Department. It runs a national e-portal for name search, reservation and registration. In 2024 its register connected by API to the revenue authority, the National Identification Authority and Ghana Post. Its beneficial ownership register held 175,984 declarations on 30 September 2025, up from 24,256 in 2021. An end-to-end digital registration platform was in pilot in mid-2026.

GhanaPostGPS has covered the country on a five-metre grid since 2017, but few Ghanaians use their digital address (2024). A digital address has been mandatory on business registration since March 2019.

The Electoral Commission's certified 2024 register held 18,774,159 voters, nearly all with biometric data. In 2023 Parliament rejected the Commission's plan to make the Ghana Card the sole proof of identity for registration. No technical link joins the two databases.

In September 2026 the national household registry profiled 11.24 million people in 2,906,288 households. It is the single targeting database behind LEAP, school feeding and health insurance. In August 2026 the driver and vehicle licensing authority began piloting electronic number plates ahead of a 2027 rollout.

Sectoral management information systems

In October 2025 the Health Minister disclosed a failed health-data contract on which the vendor had been paid about $77 million. About 15 million patient records had to be rebuilt. The ministry ordered a nationwide rollout of a replacement platform. In Greater Accra primary care the district reporting system and the patient-record system run side by side without exchanging data (2026). Rural primary care still records on paper registers (2024). The education management information system has been digitised since 2022, with head teachers entering the data.

In 2025 the World Bank found an integrated financial management system covering treasury and budget preparation across central and local government. The treasury single account is widely used by ministries and agencies. The system does not track spending to programme level. The HR and pension systems use the national identity number as their identifier.

The revenue authority's customs valuation risk system was fully deployed from March 2026. By July it had analysed about 366,000 import declarations, a quarter of them referred for officer review. A new integrated tax administration system entered pilot on 1 April 2026 at one service centre for selected large taxpayers. In October 2026 the authority began collecting VAT at the point of payment when a Ghanaian card or wallet pays a foreign digital-services supplier.

The court automation system launched in 2019 has stalled on unreliable connectivity, inadequate funding and manual processes (2025). Police stations send suspects' fingerprints to CID headquarters on paper forms, and just over 3,700 records had been captured in the automated system. In January 2026 the national labour market information system was still being developed ahead of public testing.

The West African Police Information System had equipped 35 police units by August 2019. A first terminal at Tamale was reported fully operational in October 2023. A legal-aid organisation recorded 2,475 cases on the police case tracking system in Volta from December 2021, with patchy use.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In May 2026 a redesign of Ghana.gov began, taking GOV.UK and Rwanda's Irembo as models. The platform is being expanded to carry about 16,000 services, integrated with the national identity credential. The work is funded by the $200 million World Bank Ghana Digital Acceleration Project. From 14 August 2026 tax payments through the Ghana.gov payment platform failed intermittently. The online passport application portal went down on 25 August.

In 2025 the World Bank found the portal transactional end to end for some services, company formation among them. Sectoral portals cover tax, social protection and public jobs. In 2025 citizens still carried several identity cards for services the Ghana Card could perform. From 15 September 2026 the accountant-general suspended the salaries of government employees whose identity details did not match the payroll.

The revenue authority's taxpayers' portal takes payment by mobile money, card, USSD or bank, and is mandatory for businesses above GHS 5 million in annual revenue (2025). Procurement is mandatory on the Ghana Electronic Procurement System by direction of the Finance Minister. The system is integrated with the national payment system. The Interior Ministry launched an online services portal in December 2025. On 13 August 2026 the government signed a contract to digitise 3.5 million public records.

In 2025 one institution held both the coordination and the audit mandate for digital transformation. Digital transformation was not run as one government across ministries. In 2024 Ghana scored about 0.63 on the UN E-Government Development Index, the highest in West Africa. In December 2021 the European Union found trust in government e-services low, and most Ghanaians unaware the services existed.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In 2025 the World Bank found that Ghana's integrated financial management system covers treasury operations and budget preparation across central and local government. The second National Public Sector Reform Strategy spans the Local Government ministry, among others (July 2026). In July 2026 officers from regional councils and municipal assemblies were trained on district revenue software. The training ran under the Ghana Secondary Cities Support Programme. In September 2026 the Office of the Head of the Local Government Service signed a memorandum with a Ghanaian networks company to build a digital backbone for all 261 assemblies. No value or timetable was stated. The same month the local government ministry said assembly meetings and town halls would be broadcast live nationwide, with no start date.

In 2024 a review found that open government planning reaches municipal assemblies, but participation is partial and initiatives are fragmented across agencies. In November 2020 Parliament approved a US$100 million China Development Bank loan for a traffic management project for the Accra metropolitan area. It is the largest sub-national digital investment found.

Rural digital data capture

In June 2025 the regulator recorded the one authorised provider in Ghana's rural internet licence class as not operational. In March 2026 GIFEC called its Rural Telephony Project the fund's top priority. The project is financed by a China Eximbank buyer's credit signed in May 2020. In October 2026 the communications minister said the government will commission an independent audit of the 2,016 sites built under the contract. Days later he gave the contractors, who he said had been paid in full, 30 days to finish all outstanding sites. In July 2026 the regulator set the dominant operator's rural coverage at 40% of the rural population by December 2029, rising to 80% by December 2035.

A 2025 study found that no health facility had moved fully to electronic capture, and Savannah Region facilities given tablets in 2023 kept reporting from paper registers. In September 2025 the health insurance authority reviewed the expansion of its telehealth project. Police stations send fingerprints and crime details to CID headquarters on paper forms. The civil registry's 2025–2030 plan lists mobile registration in remote areas among the things to build. A 2025 study of headteachers in Wenchi Municipality found their use of ICT for administration constrained by electricity, connectivity and hardware.

In 2025 the GSMA reported near-universal 3G and 4G coverage. Rural electricity access is well behind urban (2023). Rural phones were still mostly basic handsets (2021). In the 2024 Ibrahim Index rural market access scores 81.8, sixth of 54, up 27.6 points over the decade. In February 2026 the agriculture ministry signed for a private operator to build and run a national farmer registration and subsidy platform.

Technology

AI

In October 2026 the communications minister said every ministry now has an AI focal person, after an AI boot camp for Cabinet ministers. In May 2026 the disaster management organisation took over a multi-hazard chatbot that answers the public in several languages. The finance ministry adopted a legal AI tool across its legal directorate (August 2026). Automated traffic enforcement, with fines paid through Ghana.gov, was set to start on 1 October 2026. A commentator reports the start put back to 1 November.

The revenue authority's AI trade valuation system was fully deployed at Customs from March 2026, and by July had analysed about 366,000 import declarations. It raised assessed customs collections by 17.5% over the values importers declared (July 2026). In September the authority's head put customs collections at up to US$450m a month, against about US$350m before. In July 2026 the health service launched PROMPTS, an AI-triaged SMS line for pregnant women and new mothers.

In October 2026 the minister said the government is building Ghana Chat, a national language model for public institutions. The National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025–2035 was published in December 2025 and rests on eight pillars. It was launched in Accra on 24 April 2026. It proposes a National AI Fund of GHS 5bn over 2025–2030, scaling to GHS 15bn over 2030–2035. It also proposes a Responsible AI Authority within its first year.

On 8 October 2026 the Bank of Ghana said it is developing a directive on AI in the financial sector. In August 2026 the Ministry of Health set out the National AI Health Policy it is developing. Ghana has no AI statute and handles AI harms under the Cybersecurity Act; in May 2026 police arrested eleven people over a deepfake video of the President. The draft Emerging Technologies Bill would create an agency to enforce AI principles through existing regulators. In October 2026 it was still a draft, when a governance index placed Ghana eighth of 54.

ICT Industry

A satellite account of Ghana's ICT sector put it at 3.1% of GDP in 2010, falling each year to 2.2% in 2014. Over the same years the sector's gross value added grew from GHS 1,411.2m to GHS 2,489.3m. Telecommunications carried more of that value added than any other ICT activity. In February 2026 MTN Ghana was elevated to a major subsidiary of MTN Group. In the six months to 30 June 2026 data made up 58.7% of its service revenue.

In 2024 the Ghana Hubs Network counted over 103 technology and innovation hubs across the 16 regions. Ghanaian startups raised around USD 52m in equity in 2025, or about USD 90m including debt, down on the year. In 2025 company formation could be completed online end to end. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index Ghana's economic diversification score fell 7.4 points over 2014–2023 to 54.7, 37th of 54. In 2023 firms reported power outages most months.

In January 2026 Margins ID Group, which produces the Ghana Card, won the contract to build and operate The Gambia's national identity system. CSquared has built metro fibre across the four largest cities (2025).

Innovation ecosystem

Ghana ranks 101st of 139 economies in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2025. The World Bank's 2025 assessment found a startup policy, public financing for startups, and public-private arrangements for online service delivery. It also found a public sector innovation strategy and a body whose remit covers public service innovation. Government software has no open source policy. In July 2026 the Accra startup KutanaPay, founded in 2023, ran an escrow platform for cross-border trade that releases funds only when both parties confirm delivery.

Capacity

Literacy

The 2021 census found 80.4% of people aged six and over had used an ICT device in the previous three months, and 68.6% had used the internet. Personal computer ownership was marginal. A 2026 study found teachers and nurses using markedly more applications than adolescents. By mid-2024 One Teacher, One Laptop had distributed 280,000 laptops, without matching investment in school Wi-Fi.

On 20 July 2026 the Education Minister announced a revised kindergarten-to-JHS curriculum adding coding, artificial intelligence, electronics and TVET. It still goes to the President, then Cabinet, then Parliament. By July 2026 a communications ministry programme with UNESCO had trained 240 civil servants as AI trainers in their own institutions. In September 2025 the Data Protection Commission began a privacy awareness campaign across all 16 regions. In August 2026 the central bank and the securities regulator took a virtual-asset literacy initiative to schools, marketplaces and churches in Ashanti.

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index equality in education scores 46.6, 30th of 54, up 0.3 points over 2014–2023. The education sub-category gained 9.4 points over the same decade to 60.5, 16th of 54. Human resources in education scores 72.5, up 14.0 points, and ranks 33rd.

Training and skills

In September 2026 the Ghana TVET Service signed a memorandum with UNESCO, KPMG and Microsoft to train at least 100,000 TVET facilitators in AI and digital skills. On 2 August 2026 the One Million Coders programme held 141,954 registered accounts, 27,782 admitted learners and 5,812 course completions. In September 12 universities signed to carry its courses in first-year degrees. In October the ministry said it would retarget the programme at third- and final-year students. At one university 195 of 1,447 enrolled students had completed the course. In July 2026 the Youth Employment Agency and Ghana Digital Centres agreed to train 2,000 young people in cloud, AI, cybersecurity and data analytics.

In October 2026 the University of Ghana secured more than 96,000 Gemini for Education licences for staff and students. The Kumasi science and technology university took 95,000 Google licences. In August 2026 a Czech-funded project began training over 100 community journalists from about 60 media houses on cybercrime. In 2025 the World Bank found a public-service digital skills programme whose governing document is not published.

Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology has one of the strongest domestic computing programmes (2025). On 30 October 2020 China Eximbank signed a loan connecting five tertiary institutions' satellite campuses to fibre. In July 2023 a civil-society survey found 107 of 138 sampled senior high schools without working government Wi-Fi. In June 2024 an investigative outlet found 48 of 50 schools it visited offline while the provider was paid. The education ministry called the report inaccurate, citing a GHS 84m contract for 1,013 sites paid pro rata. In October 2026 the Korean ambassador said the Korean aid agency is launching a US$28m digital basic education programme for 2026 to 2032.

Research institutions

The Ghana National Research Fund, established under Act 1056 and launched in June 2026, carries GH¢100 million for 2026. It funds competitive grants, doctoral and postdoctoral programmes and priority research. In September 2026 China granted US$30 million towards a University of Science and Technology at Damongo, whose fields include artificial intelligence. Korea's KOICA holds a US$9 million grant to the University of Ghana for 2022-2027, to build an ICT innovation centre and promote ICT start-ups. The grant also funds Asian studies. The University of Ghana law school has run an Africa Centre of Excellence on Data Governance and Digital Rights since 2024. Google announced an artificial-intelligence research centre in Accra in 2018.

Inclusion

Access to services

In the 2024 Ibrahim Index equal access to public services scores 58.0, 10th of 54, a change of -0.1 points over 2014-2023. The wider inclusion and equality sub-category rose 5.2 points to 69.3, 3rd of 54. Sections 23 and 24 of the Electronic Communications Act 2008 define universal service to include free emergency calls and provision for persons with disability.

The Ghana Card is required for banking, passports, driving licences and land registration (2025). Since 2 February 2026 first registration has cost GH¢30 from age 25. A 24-hour centre in Accra has charged for its services since 1 August 2026. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index the ease of obtaining an identity document is among Ghana's ten most deteriorated measures of the decade. In the 2023 voter registration most new registrants proved their identity through two guarantors.

In July 2026 the Bank of Ghana undertook to publish its register of licensed digital lenders "in due course", without a date. It has published lists of unlicensed loan apps instead, twenty in August, twenty in September and twenty in October.

The Bank's directive of January 2025 requires banks and payment providers to make their digital channels accessible. In December 2025 the communications minister pledged to bring government digital platforms up to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. People with disabilities hold mobile money accounts at markedly lower rates, women with disabilities least of all. In 2025 the World Bank found no channel through which a complaint about a service reaches government and is answered.

Digital divides

Between 2018 and 2022 Ghana's gender gap in phone ownership narrowed from 13 percentage points to 7, while the gap in smartphone ownership widened from 6 points to 17. In 2022 63% of men and 46% of women owned a smartphone. In September 2026 the communications minister put national smartphone penetration at 71.8% in June 2026.

In 2019 smartphone ownership was 58.4% urban and 28.1% rural, while basic-phone ownership was 61.3% rural and 38.7% urban. The same regulator survey found 16.8% of households with internet access at home, and 7.9% of people aged five and over owning a computer. No later round of the survey has been found.

A July 2026 review found that one of the eighteen priority areas of the ICT for Accelerated Development policy addresses gender, and the 2020 Digital Financial Services Policy carries no gender-disaggregated indicators. Its fieldwork found women's handsets were most often gifts from male partners, chosen by the giver.

The Electronic Communications Act 2008 sets no rate, base or floor for the levy that finances the universal access fund. In June 2025 the regulator recorded the one provider in the rural internet licence class as not operational. In 2023 rural electricity access still trailed urban by a wide margin.

Data

National statistics

In 2023 the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators put Ghana in the 70-89% band on data use and data products, and in the 50-69% band on data services, sources and infrastructure. The 2024 Ibrahim Index scores the capacity of Ghana's statistical system at 57.4, 18th of 54, up 9.2 points over the decade to 2023.

The 2021 Population and Housing Census, Ghana's first fully digital one, counted 30,832,019 people. Its general report was completed in February 2022, with a volume on ICT. In 2023 the population, agriculture and business censuses had all been run within ten years. The survey programme had three or more household, health and agriculture rounds, against two business and two labour surveys. In October 2026 the statistical service committed to early preparation of the next census, digital-first with geospatial methods.

In March 2026 the statistical service signed data-sharing memoranda with 25 ministries, departments and agencies. The education information system collects data on 104 of its 124 indicators, mostly inputs such as enrolment (2024). The financial management system monitors programme and SDG performance only in part (2025). The Births and Deaths Registry put birth registration coverage at 92.7 per cent in 2022.

The communications regulator's bulletin for the third quarter of 2024 appeared around May 2025, about eight months after the period it covers. In 2017 Ghana was one of two African states known to have compiled an ICT satellite account. That account measured ICT value added above the published GDP figure for the whole Information and Communication sector. A US$1 million Hewlett Foundation grant ran from 2021 to 2025 to strengthen SDG monitoring.

Open data

The Ghana Open Data Initiative began in 2012 under the National Information Technology Agency, and a 2018–2019 revamp produced a draft National Data Sharing policy. In 2024 the portal hosted 315 data catalogues from 22 ministries and agencies. In 2025 the World Bank found it carried basic datasets, updated mostly by hand. In Open Data Watch's 2024 inventory most of Ghana's economic statistics score 6.0 to 7.0 out of ten. Health, energy, agriculture and education facilities score between 2.5 and 4.0. In September 2026 a university laboratory and the environmental agency tested a national air-quality data hub.

The GHEITI multi-stakeholder group adopted an open-data policy in December 2016. It sets no publication timetable and names no open licence. In 2025 the World Bank found a data governance function housed inside another institution, with its strategy still in preparation. It found that the digital transformation strategy, the data governance arrangements and the public investment management system are not published.

In 2025 the World Bank found a right-to-information law in force with a working request mechanism. In the 2024 Ibrahim Index accessibility of public records scores 49.8, eighth of 54, a shift of 0.1 points over the decade to 2023. Disclosure rose 11.4 points to 50.0, ninth of 54. In August 2026 the Right to Information Commission fined 254 public institutions for missing their reporting deadline.

Use of satellite data

In August 2026 a Ghanaian geospatial firm said it runs an agricultural monitoring system on satellite imagery, and a national forest monitoring system with an AI deforestation tracker. No national space or geospatial data policy had been adopted as at September 2026. In March 2026 the space science institute and a Belgian research centre were building earth-observation maize yield estimation under the European Space Agency's EO Africa programme. In 2024 the meteorological agency was testing district maize yield forecasts with a US university. Its researchers run EUMETSAT's nowcasting software in-house (2022). In March 2024 the agriculture ministry said it would watch farms in its input programme by satellite twice daily. No account of that monitoring in operation has been found.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In late 2025 Digital Realty opened ACR2 in Accra, Ghana's largest data centre. In 2022 Equinix took over the MainOne landing station with its acquisition of MainOne. The US Development Finance Corporation has signalled intent to help finance Africa Data Centres' planned facility at the Trade Fair site in Accra.

Google announced an artificial-intelligence research centre in Accra in 2018, and has since opened an AI Community Centre in the city. In October 2021 the national computer emergency response team named Microsoft and the Ghana–US SGI cooperation among its affiliations.

USAID's US$9 million Evaluate for Health task order ran from 2014 to 2020 on health data and the health management information system. The Hewlett Foundation, a private US funder, committed US$1 million over 2021-2025 to strengthen SDG monitoring. On 27 July 2026 the US Embassy, AmCham Ghana and American Tower Corporation attended the ministry's stakeholder session on the draft Digital Economy Fund bill.

China activities

In August 2026 Stanbic Bank Ghana became the first bank in the country to offer clients direct access to China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System. Payments can now settle in renminbi from cedi accounts without a correspondent bank. In June 2026 the People's Bank of China authorised Standard Bank Group and ICBC jointly as the Renminbi Clearing Bank of Africa. Ghana is one of six African markets in Standard Bank's rollout (July 2026).

China Eximbank signed loans with Ghana for national-security communications in December 2019 and rural telephony in May 2020. In October 2020 it signed two for distance learning, at five tertiary institutions and at the Institute of Local Government Studies. Phase 2 of the ALPHA security network rests on a buyer's credit against a Huawei and China Machinery Engineering Corporation contract. The Rural Telephony and Digital Inclusion Project rests on a Sinosure-insured buyer's credit against a Huawei and CNTIC contract.

The December 2022 sovereign default suspended service on Ghana's Eurobonds, commercial loans and most bilateral loans. On 28 April 2026 China handed over the ECOWAS Commission headquarters in Abuja, a China-aid building.

EU activities

The European Union's 2021-2027 programme for Ghana sets no digital priority area and makes no digital allocation. It treats digital as a cross-cutting theme across green growth, sustainable cities and governance. A 2024 amending decision allocated EUR 136 million for 2025-2027, again with no digital priority area.

In May 2026 Czechia donated forensic hardware and software worth almost GH¢450,000 to Ghana's Police Cybercrime Unit. Its Transformation Cooperation Programme funds the National Community Media Cyber Capability Building Project (2026). Germany's development ministry funds a 2023-2026 programme on digital transformation for inclusive entrepreneurship. It earlier funded a project with the University of Cape Coast on practice-oriented ICT qualifications.

Ghana's National Data Strategy, validated in July 2024, was developed with Smart Africa, the European Union and GIZ. In October 2021 the national response team named the Council of Europe's GLACY+ programme among its affiliations. In 2015 Denmark's development finance institution made a further equity investment in Intelligent Card Production Systems in Accra, the plant that produced the Ghana Card.

Gulf/UAE activities

Ghana and the United Arab Emirates signed a US$1bn strategic investment agreement in December 2025 for an innovation and artificial-intelligence hub at Ningo–Prampram. Dubai's ports and free-zone corporation is to finance and oversee the build, on land Ghana provides. About US$400m is allotted to AI computing, among it US$180m for a compute hub to be built by G42 and US$100m for an AI-based digital identity system. A further US$150m is for 5G in three cities and US$120m for a hyperscale data centre. Construction was scheduled to begin in 2026. No start of construction or disbursement has been found as at October 2026.

India activities

In October 2025 the health minister said the cloud infrastructure of the LHIMS patient-record system was built in India. No Indian state-financed digital infrastructure, financing commitment or bilateral technology agreement with Ghana has been found as at October 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In April 2026 Ghana rejected a US$109m health aid proposal because it required access to health data and pathogens for US companies. The Data Protection Commission's head said the proposed agreement reached metadata, dashboards, reporting tools, data models and data dictionaries. In September 2026 the President confirmed in New York that Cabinet had rejected the text. In October he said the United States had asked to discuss amending the clauses Ghana objected to, and that Ghana would meet it.

In October 2025 the health minister said the LHIMS patient-record system's software was not owned by the state. A platform under state control would replace it.

In June 2026 bid documents showed NITA's data centres in Accra and Kumasi about 90% full and being refreshed, with knowledge transfer to NITA scored. The customs management system runs in Ghana Link's privately operated Tier IV data centre in Kumasi (February 2026). No instrument fixing where state data is held had been found in force as at October 2026.

In January 2026 the ministry began taking the .gh root out of the private custody it has been in since 1996. The redelegation agreement is due before the end of 2026. No terms on data jurisdiction or exit have been published for the Ghana Card partnership or for Ghana Link's hosting of customs.

In September 2026 the minister said Cabinet had approved US$250 million for a National AI and Computing Centre to give researchers sovereign access to high-performance computing.
