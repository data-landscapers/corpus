This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

In December 2025 the President ordered the full digitisation of public institutions by decree, with every institution to adopt digital systems by January 2026. In 2025 the World Bank found no national digital transformation strategy, no lead institution or coordinating machinery for one, and no published strategy documents. The same assessment found no audit or oversight function covering government digital programmes.

In June 2024 World Bank supervision reporting recorded a Digital Development Road Map and Master Plan still with its consulting firm. Government Enterprise Architecture work was bundled with a needs assessment for connecting ministries to the internet. A National Spectrum Management and 5G Strategy began at an inception workshop in July 2026, run with an external expert team. In June 2026 the regulator approved a telecom tariff exchange-rate adjustment. Its statement describes the adjustment as phased without numbering the phases, and sets no end point.

Addis Ababa Othow was sworn in as governor of the Bank of South Sudan on 31 July 2026, two days after Johnny Ohisa was dismissed with no reason stated. Othow had headed the bank before, from June to November 2025, and Ohisa had been reappointed for a third term earlier in 2026. The instant payment system partnership of February 2025 was announced under Ohisa. Othow was governor when the interbank payment system launched in October 2025.

In October 2019 the Ministry of Finance and Planning approved single-sourcing of the e-Government concession for Crawford Capital, without competitive tender, noting "the National Security concern attached to the project". The UN Commission found the Revenue Authority complicit in the revenue arrangement, in what it called clear violation of articles 9 and 10 of the UN Convention against Corruption. In May 2026 the Revenue Authority and the ICT ministry called the engagement lawful, transparent and endorsed by the Council of Ministers. A consultancy to develop the Government Enterprise Architecture was out to procurement under the regional digital integration project (August 2026). No whole-of-government interoperability framework had been adopted by then.

Legislation and regulation

President Salva Kiir signed the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Act 2026 into law on 18 February 2026. It replaced the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Provisional Order 2021. Three months earlier the Revitalized Transitional National Legislative Assembly had passed the bill, with amendments, on 25 November 2025. The enacted text was withheld from journalists at the signing.

No data-protection statute is in force (December 2025), and a bill promised to the legislature is not law. The consumer protection regime that does exist does not address payment services. The central bank has instead extended data-protection, record-keeping and complaint duties to mobile-money operators by regulatory notice in September 2025. Mobile money's standing rests on the central bank's affirmation of it as legal tender under the 2017 Electronic Money Regulation (July 2025). That regulation's 2025 amendment, reported in effect on 1 September 2026, sets three identification tiers with daily limits of SSP 1.625m to 13m. It obliges every digital payment platform to connect to the national payment system through a licensed switch. By May 2026 the President had signed the legal framework establishing a national ICT Authority. The Authority is to oversee digital regulation, data protection, communication services and emerging-technology coordination.

The identity statutes are the Nationality Act 2011, the Civil Registry Act 2018 and civil registry regulations adopted in December 2024. Raising ordinary passport validity from five years to ten requires amending the passport act (July 2026). It also needs the approval of both the Council of Ministers and the Transitional National Legislative Assembly. None of this has been done, and ordinary validity remains five years. South Sudan acceded to the 1954 and 1961 Statelessness Conventions in November 2024.

The National Communication Authority ordered internet service providers to block all social media on 22 January 2025 amid unrest. The ICT Authority is being set up as a state entity under the ministry while legislation is awaited (August 2026). The National Payment System Bill was validated at a five-day workshop opened on 10 August 2026.

Data protection

South Sudan has no data protection law and no data protection authority (2025). It therefore has no monitoring of compliance and no published enforcement record. Experts completed the drafting of the country's first Data Protection Policy Bill in October 2025. Koneta Initiative and Paradigm Initiative facilitated the process with the National Communication Authority and civil-society organisations. The bill would create a Data Protection Commission. The Minister of ICT said in December 2025 that a first Data Protection Bill would be introduced in 2026. On 8 September 2026 the ministry said the bill was still in preparation, and the deputy minister urged the legislature to speed its passage.

In the meantime protection rests on Article 22 of the 2011 Transitional Constitution, unevenly enforced. It also rests on a Consumer Protection Act 2011 that establishes a Consumer Protection Council with court and complaint enforcement mechanisms. That Act does not address payment services. No data protection authority has been appointed and no law provides for one (2022). For the individual there is no right in law to see, correct or delete the personal data held about you (2022). The country imposes no data localisation requirements and no restrictions on foreign access to data held in it (2025).

Amnesty International documented in 2021 that the National Security Service conducts abusive surveillance without legal safeguards, creating a climate of fear. The single-sourcing of the e-Government concession followed written advice from the then Director-General of the National Security Service's Internal Security Bureau. He proposed that the contractor work in partnership with the Bureau's ICT Department, because the project aimed to create huge databases and integrate services accessible online.

East African Community rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection were approved at official level on 24 July 2026. They must still pass the Community's decision-making organs before any partner state adopts them.

Regional collaboration

Visa fees revised in July 2026 charge citizens of Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to enter. Under the same schedule Tanzanian and Egyptian citizens enter visa-free, and South African citizens for stays under 30 days. The portal collecting the fees runs on a platform built for the Government of Kenya.

In 2025 the country's instant payment system connected to no cross-border scheme and did not take part in the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System. Connection to the East African Payment System was an ambition, not a work stream. At the SSIPS launch in October 2025, East African Community and African Development Bank representatives presented the system as regional alignment. In July 2026 regional bodies agreed a peer review mechanism for macroeconomic surveillance and frameworks for the EAC Five-Year Development Strategy 2026/27–2030/31. These apply to South Sudan as a partner state.

On 24 July 2026 officials approved East African Community rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection. The rules must still pass the Community's decision-making organs before any partner state adopts them. An East African Community passport has been announced, and needs legislation not yet tabled (July 2026). The World Bank's Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project supports its borrowers through shared digital infrastructure, harmonised regulation and digital public goods.

A 15-year licence effective 16 October 2025 lets MTN's Bayobab build and operate cross-border fibre on the East 2 West corridor. The revenue authority signed a third customs cooperation memorandum with Uganda's, covering digital interoperability and transit, reported 16 September 2026. A workshop on domesticating the African Union Data Policy Framework was convened in Juba for 8 to 10 September 2026. On 7 September 2026 the health ministers of South Sudan and Uganda signed a cross-border health security memorandum. It provides for real-time sharing of epidemiological information.

Standards

The interbank payment system launched in October 2025 runs on the ISO 20022 messaging standard. In 2025 the World Bank found no government interoperability framework or data-exchange platform. It found no data-quality or service-level standards for exchange between government systems, and no programme to retire legacy systems onto a common standard. The financial management system supports no standardised financial classification, so it cannot track spending to programme performance or SDG reporting. South Sudan has no government open source software policy and no recorded adoption of open source software in the public sector (2025).

In May 2026 the government listed enforcement of technology standards among the roles of a planned ICT Authority. No standards work by the Authority had been found as at August 2026. The East African Community's Secure Data Sharing Framework was endorsed at a validation workshop on 25 June 2026, a stage before adoption by the partner states. The electronic government procurement system is in use and publishes both tender notices and awarded contracts (2025).

In September 2026 the National Communication Authority took part in the third African preparatory meeting for the 2027 World Radiocommunication Conference. Non-geostationary orbit and direct-to-device satellite systems were among the items. No national position or delegation mandate has been published.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The full text of the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Act 2026 was withheld from journalists at its signing in February 2026. The ICT ministry has publicly defended the Act against criticism. In January 2025 the regulator ordered providers to block all social media during unrest.

In 2025 South Sudan had no e-participation or public consultation platform, and no citizen feedback mechanism for complaints about government services. Zain South Sudan's June 2026 press release calls the tariff measure a phased Telecommunications Tariff Exchange Rate Adjustment and numbers no phases. When the second adjustment took effect at midnight on 26 July 2026, none of the three operators published the revised rates. The regulator's approval set no end point for the programme.

Freedom of expression and belief fell 16.7 points over 2014-2023 to 15.6 of 100, 51st of 54 African states. It is one of South Sudan's ten most deteriorated governance measures. Media freedom scored 30.7 of 100 in 2023, 49th of 54 and down 5.1 points over the decade. The country has never held a national election, and nationwide voter registration was still being set up in mid-2026.

At a Juba panel on 2 September 2026, officials of the national human rights commission rated the country weak on transparency and very weak on accountability. On 31 August 2026 the information technology minister denied two documents alleging a US$15m influence payment by the firms contracted for e-government.

Finance

Financial sustainability

In December 2025 South Sudan's Auditor-General found no measures in place to sustain the USD 14.8 million IFMIS expansion beyond the World Bank grant paying for it. The finance ministry promised an IFMIS and HRMIS sustainability strategy. The 2025/26 draft budget book gives the finance ministry a new ICT directorate, proposed at SSP 1.56 billion. It gives the Ministry of Information a new IT services directorate at SSP 237.6 million for 2025/26. Neither carries anything in the 2024/25 column.

In that book every agency with a digital line names Consolidated Funds as its only source. The book carries no donor-financed digital project. The immigration and passports directorate, now an agency of its own, is proposed at SSP 63.8 billion for 2025/26, against SSP 9.6 billion in 2024/25. That line also carries border control.

The digital lines of the 2025/26 draft budget come to about SSP 69.4 billion proposed, against about SSP 11.6 billion appropriated for 2024/25, nine-tenths of the 2025/26 figure being the immigration and passports directorate.

The finance ministry's 2024/25 budget performance report gives spending by agency, not by the directorates carrying the digital lines.

In October 2025 Ensoft signed a six-year build-operate-transfer agreement to run a revenue-assurance system for the Revenue Authority and the National Communication Authority. Under the 2025 agreement it keeps 3.56% of all revenue collected through the system, with no fixed contract value, and passes the system to the government at the end. Since April 2026 every licence, spectrum and regulatory fee owed to the National Communication Authority has had to be paid through the national e-Service platform.

The National Communication Act 2012 establishes a Universal Service and Access Fund. The fund's budget chapter names Consolidated Funds as its only source, with SSP 536.4 million appropriated for 2024/25, SSP 510 million of it repairs and maintenance. SSP 785.9 million is proposed for 2025/26.

Repeated payment disputes with the identity system's private vendor, and an August 2024 breakdown, interrupted issuance of passports and nationality certificates (2026). In May 2026 the Interior ministry supplied five new production machines for passports, identity cards and nationality certificates.

MoUs and other agreements

In May 2026 South Sudan's ICT ministry confirmed a collaboration with Crawford Capital Ltd for e-services research and technical support. The ministry presented it as following established procedures and Council of Ministers resolutions. No value was stated.

New investments

The World Bank's US$162 million Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project, 2023 to 2028, is the largest commitment found for South Sudan's digital estate. South Sudan borrows under it alongside Somalia, with IGAD and the East African Community borrowing regionally. The figure is the envelope across all four. It finances the national spectrum management and 5G strategy launched in July 2026, led by an external consultant team.

In 2024 Finnfund lent US$5 million in mezzanine finance to CREI for 413 solar-hybrid energy systems at MTN South Sudan's telecom sites. A second US$5 million followed, signed in November 2025 and backed by the EU's Africa Connected programme. In August 2026 Germany's development ministry committed EUR 5 million to the East African Community for a regional AI research and training network covering South Sudan.

In June 2025 the government approved a design budget for the second phase of the national fibre-optic programme.

The record holds 26 non-state commitments naming South Sudan, about US$28.6 million in all (as at November 2026). Two Finnfund investments in solar-hybrid power for telecom sites, in 2024 and 2025, make up US$10 million of it, and three US grants for the national health information system about US$7 million. The World Bank's US$162 million regional integration envelope for 2023 to 2028, shared with the other borrowers, is larger than all of them, and no Chinese digital finance is on the record.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Until June 2026 South Sudan's telecom operators had to bill at an official rate of 4,526 SSP to the dollar while the market ran near 27,000. In June 2026 the National Communication Authority approved a phased adjustment of tariff exchange rates, with no published end point. A second adjustment took effect on 26 July 2026, and none of the three operators published the revised rates. Three operators raised voice and data prices again on 17 August 2026, and the legislature moved to summon the minister. MTN had cut tariffs by a quarter in December 2025, from some of the most expensive mobile data in Africa (November 2025).

Active mobile data subscriptions reached 3.60 million in 2025, more than double the 1.77 million of the year before. Mobile connections rose to 4.47 million in 2025 on the regulator's count. Around a quarter of the population was a unique mobile subscriber (2024), and internet use ran at 12 to 16 per cent over 2024-2025. 3G or 4G covered 15% of the population, with 2G voice networks predominating (2024). There were 7 active mobile broadband subscriptions per 100 people, against an African average of 44.8 (2024). The country has no internet exchange point.

In February 2020 Liquid Telecom completed a link connecting Juba directly to its submarine landing station at Mombasa. Bayobab holds a 15-year licence effective 16 October 2025 to build national and cross-border fibre on the East 2 West corridor. In October 2026 Bayobab said it had finished laying 2,038 km of fibre on the corridor's Kenyan and South Sudanese segments, with activation still to come. International bids were invited on 10 September 2026 for about 397 km of fibre from Juba to Nadapal on the Kenyan border, closing on 26 October 2026. Kenya had built its side of the link, about 730 km from Eldoret to Nadapal, by May 2025.

On the 2024 Ibrahim Index, infrastructure scored 6.3 of 100 in 2023, last of 54 African states. Internet and computers scored 3.0 and mobile communications 20.1 (2023).

Data Storage

The World Bank's implementation report of 30 June 2026 for the regional digital project lists no data-centre component among the five it funds. In April 2026 the ICT minister said construction of a national data centre was under way and about half complete.

South Sudan's guidelines on government data hosting, including cloud adoption, were still unpublished at 16 June 2026. They were unchanged from their November 2022 baseline, against a target date of April 2028. The country imposes no data localisation requirement and no restriction on foreign access to data held in it (2025).

In 2025 commercial hosting inside the country was absent, and cloud services reached users from distant regions. In 2023 the back-end systems of the state's e-service platform interoperated poorly. No centralised data management system existed, which made hosting data on that platform difficult. A nationally owned operator runs a Tier 2 data centre at Hai Malakal in Juba, commissioned in 2019.

Energy

By 2026 490 of MTN South Sudan's 499 telecom sites had been fitted or renovated with solar-hybrid systems under a ten-year energy-as-a-service contract with CREI. Finnfund part-financed the work. The operator said more than 500 mobile sites had been modernised with solar-hybrid power by September 2026.

About 5.4% of the population had access to electricity in 2023. Access ran at roughly 30% in urban areas and under 2% in rural areas (2023). Generation was around 571 GWh in 2023, some 96% of it thermal diesel and under 4% solar photovoltaic. Supply to the few connected to micro and mini-grids was unreliable, at very high tariffs (2023). Access to energy scored 1.9 of 100 in 2023, last of 54 African states, up 1.8 points over the decade.

In 2023 off-grid solar products carried a 30% import duty. The supply side was short of distribution channels, quality-verified products and working capital. At the China-Africa summit in September 2025, electricity supply was named the major impediment to South Sudan's digital infrastructure build-out. In rural and remote areas, absent electricity and radio communications, poor roads and minimal logistics constrain policing itself. Community and customary mechanisms stand in for statutory policing there.

In one week of August 2026 two connections were completed: more than 400 customers at Nimule, and a solar plant for the Aweil reference laboratory. Transmission works to bring Ugandan hydropower to Juba through Nimule were reported advancing in August 2026.

Technical Capacity

In March 2026 the Revenue Authority named a shortage of specialised personnel, alongside the ICT-infrastructure deficit, as what is holding back tax digitisation. It is working with the National Communication Authority on connectivity.

The spectrum and 5G strategy process begun in July 2026 is financed by the World Bank's regional digital project and led by an external consultant team. The national identity system runs on foreign vendors and external operational support. In 2022 the government named limited institutional and personnel capacity, weak coordination among agencies and unharmonised procedures as the main obstacles to its civil registration and identity work. In 2025 there was no digital skills strategy and no digital skills programme for public servants.

In May 2026 the government announced a planned ICT Authority, under the ICT ministry, to manage national digital infrastructure, enforce technology standards and lead public-sector digital transformation. Effective administration and statistical capacity both sat near the bottom of the African field in 2023. Both fell over the preceding decade.

Cybersecurity

The Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Act 2026 was signed on 18 February 2026. It requires operators of critical national infrastructure, banking and financial services named explicitly, to report any cybersecurity incident or data breach to the national authority within 24 hours of detection. They must also submit regular audit reports, and administrative penalties enforce both duties. The Assembly passed the bill with amendments on 25 November 2025. The President signed it into effect three months later.

Alongside hacking, espionage, cyberterrorism and economic sabotage, the 2026 Act criminalises a set of speech and identity offences. They are fake websites and accounts, false information, online impersonation, hate speech and gender-based harassment. Enforcement is split. The Ministry of Justice interprets and enforces the law (February 2026). The ICT ministry coordinates with the National Communication Authority and law enforcement on capacity-building and public awareness. The National Security Service was documented conducting abusive surveillance without legal safeguards (2021). No data protection authority exists that could review a government system.

The National Communication Authority launched SS-CIRT, a computer incident response team, on 16 October 2024. A regional World Bank project still recorded no established national team in June 2026. The authority and the Bank of South Sudan are standing up a Digital Financial Services Security Lab with the ITU (March 2026). Its team was given nine days of hands-on training to security-test mobile payment applications. On 4 November 2026 the two signed an updated memorandum on joint supervision, enforcement and the cybersecurity of digital financial services, replacing their 2018 agreement. Readiness measured before any of this scored 34.38 on the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index 2024, in the index's 'evolving' tier.

DPI

Data Exchange

The interbank settlement system went live in October 2025. It runs on an ATS+ architecture pairing real-time gross settlement with an automated clearing house on the ISO 20022 messaging standard, banks onboarded in phases. The instant payment system scoped in February 2025 is designed to sit on top of those rails, aligned with efforts to integrate clearing and settlement.

In 2025 the World Bank recorded no government interoperability framework and no data-exchange platform, and no data-quality or service-level standards for exchange between government systems. In June 2024 the government enterprise architecture was still to be done, bundled with a needs assessment for connecting ministries to the internet and a government VPN. The work sits in the World Bank-financed regional digital integration project, which runs to 2028. In 2023 the e-service platform's back-end systems interoperated poorly, and the country had no centralised data management system. The ICT minister's 2023 policy paper listed rules for data sharing among frameworks still to be established.

In 2024 the civil register was not integrated with other systems. The electoral register is compiled with no technology at all. In 2025 the business register exchanged no company information with other public agencies. The tax register does reach another: registering on eTax checks the national ID personal number against government records before a taxpayer number is issued. Health runs DHIS2 in parallel between the Ministry of Health, WHO and UNICEF. Education runs three separate platforms, and social protection a donor platform managed by UNOPS. In February 2024 two EU-funded cash consortia piloted a shared data-stewardship model for deduplicating beneficiaries and managing referrals.

The East African Community's Secure Data Sharing Framework, which covers all partner states, reached validation in Dar es Salaam on 25 June 2026. A foreign-registered firm collects a US-dollar e-Permit charge on imports before any tax assessment is issued. Collection continued after the firm was sanctioned on 12 May 2026.

Digital Identity and CRVS

Since January 2026 the personal identity card, the Butaka Shakhsia, has been required for banking, land transactions and court processes for adults aged 18 and over. In 2026 about 90% of the population held no form of identity, an estimated 10% a nationality certificate and under 1% a national identity card. Women held 37% of nationality certificates in 2026. The card carries a photo, a thumbprint image, a 2D barcode of biographic data and an ICAO-compliant machine-readable zone. In January 2026 the country still had no national digital identity system, working from fragmented biometric databases.

The directorate held 380,000 personal ID cards and roughly 35,000 nationality certificates when it resumed issuance in January 2026, after a shortage in every document category. Deliveries in June and July 2026 left it with 37,500 regular passport booklets and close to 200,000 nationality cards, enough to December 2026. At the handover of 9 August 2026 the outgoing immigration director put stock at about 32,000 passport booklets and 172,000 national identity cards. Greater Yei began distributing more than 4,000 cards in July 2026. School-based national identity registration was launched in August 2026.

From the start of 2024 a UNHCR and European Union partnership funded more than 17,400 cards in the Central Equatoria return counties of Morobo, Lainya, Yei and Kajo-Keji. Under the 2024/25 schedule a new nationality certificate costs SSP 15,000, and a replacement at least SSP 30,000. In July 2026 a national identity card cost more than SSP 50,000 and a passport US$100.

Between January and June 2024 the Commission for Refugee Affairs issued 14,653 refugee cards under the Refugee Act 2012, the national ID being reserved for nationals. The nationality certificate is available at any age, the identity card only to adults. In the African Union's 2016 monitoring round the civil register scored under 35% for digitisation. South Sudan was then the one responding country with no law providing for birth and death registration. The migration agency biometrically registered about 160,000 displaced people between April and June 2026.

Digital Payments and Fintech

South Sudan's first electronic interbank payment system went live on 10 October 2025. It moves money between banks in real time, and is expected to support mobile money integration later. A central bank circular made connection compulsory for every licensed bank by 31 January 2026; thirteen were on it by November 2025. In 2025 the central bank's national payment system vision and strategic framework was still a project's terms of reference.

In February 2025 the Bank of South Sudan and AfricaNenda announced a National Instant Payment System partnership under governor Johnny Ohisa Damian. It scoped a roadmap, capacity-building and bank-telco interoperability, following a 2023 proof of concept.

In July 2025 the Bank of South Sudan affirmed mobile money as legal tender under the 2017 Electronic Money Regulation, making refusal to accept it an offence. In 2021 around 6% of adults held an account at a bank or mobile money provider, and mobile money penetration was below 1%. More than 90% of the economy was cash-based in July 2025. The central bank's Strategic Plan 2023-27 targets 30% mobile money use among adults by 2027. On 8 October 2026 the trade minister met one operator's management on reopening its suspended mobile money transactions.

A November 2020 contract entitles Crawford Capital, as the Revenue Authority's e-Tax collector, to 2% of all digitally assessed taxation. Commercial banks received 0.5% for collecting and transporting cash revenue in 2025. Its payments subsidiary levies a further US$20.16 on a US$100 visa. From late 2023 banks were told to deposit non-oil revenue into accounts the company controlled, not the Revenue Authority accounts the law requires. Since January 2026 passport and national identity fees have had to be paid digitally. On 29 August 2026 the economic reform committee ordered banks to enforce cash payment of tax, reversing the cashless measure. In August 2025 the finance ministry convened accounting officers to pay every civil servant's salary into a bank account, with no date set for it to become mandatory. About six payment cycles of the government cash-transfer programme were pending in August 2026.

Registries

In April 2025 the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security and FAO launched a Farmers' E-Registry and a Co-operatives E-Registry. They carry farmer and cooperative records and an e-voucher module for inputs, with roll-out under way across all ten states and three administrative areas. The nationwide civil registration exercise launched in 2019 is event-based, and there is no operational population register.

A birth-notification system run with the Ministry of Health, the civil register's only digital element, has not worked since 2018. The registry works mainly in towns, and birth registration completeness is among the lowest in Africa.

An online Business Registration Service has run at the Ministry of Justice since 2023. In 2025 the World Bank found name verification, registration and certificate issuance digitised, but not beneficial ownership updates or online fee payment. It scored zero for exchange of company information among public agencies, and found no link with the land registry. The Revenue Authority's platform issues a taxpayer number only after the Ministry of Justice validates a business.

In mid-2025 the Ministry of Lands, which had only recently taken over registration from the judiciary, was still designing a digital registry to replace manual paper records. The draft national land policy describes largely manual registries in Juba, Wau and Renk alone, with no national cadastre. Central Equatoria State launched a digital land registry for Juba in August 2023; no record of it operating since has been found. In June 2026 Juba County said it would digitalise its land registration in one system for application, registration and transfer. A geographic addressing system for Juba, built from an aerial survey, was announced in November 2024; no survey has been found since.

The elections commission set 22 December 2026 as the election date, with voter registration still pending at 21 August 2026. On 2 September 2026 the commission's chairperson ruled out biometric registration, choosing paper forms to be transferred to computers later.

Sectoral management information systems

South Sudan had no human resource management system for the public service and no government payroll system as at 2025. It is running a US$15m World Bank-backed biometric registration of civil servants to purge ghost workers, the data held on a Ministry of Finance server (January 2026). It runs in three phases: national institutions, then the states, then the organised forces, the last launched in June 2026.

Filing through the eTax platform has been mandatory for all taxpayers since 1 July 2021. Around 62,000 individual taxpayers and around 21,000 organisational taxpayers were registered as at 2026. eTax and eCustoms remain separate systems, and the Revenue Authority's September 2025 management review called for their integration. eCustoms, built by the Revenue Authority and Crawford Capital, was introduced at Juba and Nimule in March 2022. No customs data was exchanged with other government systems as at 2025.

A 2024/25 maturity assessment rates the health information system at 40.5% overall. Facilities sent 73% of expected monthly reports into DHIS2 in 2025, against 62% in 2020, and 24% of districts reached 90%. Three education systems, the ministry's own, a donor-funded attendance monitor and a refugee EMIS, use inconsistent indicator definitions and are linked to nothing.

A programme-specific biometric beneficiary system has run under World Bank-financed safety nets since 2014, and authenticated 100% of programme payments by 2024. There is no unified national social registry. It runs its own biometric registration and deduplication, not the national ID. A social insurance system is in use but does not interoperate with other government systems and does not use the national identification number. The social insurance fund opened contributor registration on 1 October 2026 and began talks with a bank on linking its digital platform. An education-in-emergencies information system was launched on the DHIS2 platform, with 12 county education departments equipped (August 2026).

A UN agency reports 10,836 cases recorded in 2021 through a case monitoring system for the judiciary, the justice ministry and the national police in Juba.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Crawford Capital Ltd, through its payments subsidiary Capital Pay Ltd, has been the exclusive provider of e-Government Services since a 16 November 2019 contract with the ICT ministry. Its reach runs through the assessment and collection of taxes, customs duties and other non-oil revenues. The contract gives the contractor 75% of the eServices profit share against the ministry's 25% (2025). That share was revised upward from the 65% the initial contract indicated (2025). The e-visa portal runs on the 'e-Citizen' platform Webmasters Kenya built for the Government of Kenya. The UN Commission found Crawford neither built nor significantly customised it.

Under the visa fees revised on 20 July 2026, applicants apply on the portal, pay online and download the approved document before travel. The stated processing time is 72 hours. The eServices platform gives citizens and businesses a single sign-on account for business registration, trade licensing, and revenue and customs services. Users pay by mobile money, card or bank transfer. In November 2026 its catalogue listed 14 ministries and agencies and 47 named online services, 22 of them at the communication authority. The procurement authority declared its tender portal complete in February 2025, for bid invitations, awards and supplier registration.

In 2025 an integrated financial management information system was in use on commercial off-the-shelf software, covering treasury and budget preparation. A treasury single account was still under implementation. The finance ministry signed in February 2025 to extend the system, a cloud-hosted FreeBalance suite, to most ministries, departments and agencies. The extension adds biometric payroll verification, under the World Bank's public financial management project. South Sudan ranked second from last of the 193 countries on the UN E-Government Development Index, with a score of 0.1191 (December 2025).

The revenue authority launched an electronic tax audit module on 21 September 2026, after manual audits were prohibited by directive.

Of the 32 headline GovTech indicators the World Bank assessed for South Sudan in its 2025 update, seven record a system or law in place, one records implementation in progress, and 24 record the item as absent.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

South Sudan's integrated financial management information system covers central government only and does not extend to sub-national government (2025). States and counties sit outside the one system that would carry public money through a digital record. Digital systems are concentrated in Juba and operate at national level only, with rural counties effectively excluded (2025).

County health departments key paper returns from the clinics below them into the national health platform (2025). Where the local tier touches the digital state, it does so as a transcription point, not a user. The registers a local administration would draw on, the civil registry among them, reach the towns and stop (2024). In 2023 rural populations were scored at the lowest assessed level for representation and participation, where they had been for the decade before.

The capital's council ordered an end to manual payments in October 2022 and told businesses to register on a vendor-built revenue system. The state government launched a digital revenue system and e-services for the council in September 2026. On 30 October 2026 the mayor said all council revenue had moved to digital collection.

Rural digital data capture

South Sudan's rural economy scored 10.7 of 100 in 2023, 45th of 54 African states and down 8.9 points across the preceding decade. It was the steepest fall of any of the sixteen sub-categories the Ibrahim Index measures. Rural land and water access dropped to 13.0 of 100, and support to the rural economy to 19.3 (2023).

In 2025 South Sudan's digital systems were concentrated in Juba and operated at national level only. Border regions and rural counties were effectively excluded, and network coverage outside the capital was intermittent. Rural electricity access ran far below the urban rate in 2023. Off-grid supply carried a 30% import duty and reached rural households through two purchase channels only (2023).

Facilities sent 73% of expected monthly reports into DHIS2 in 2025, against 62% in 2020. Rural health facilities keep handwritten registers, carried to county offices for transcription into DHIS2 (2025). Rural primary schools keep handwritten classroom registers aggregated by hand for state and national reporting (2026). Policing beyond the towns works without electricity or radio communications, on poor roads and minimal logistics.

In April 2025 the agriculture ministry and FAO launched farmers' and co-operatives e-registries, with roll-out under way beyond Juba. In July 2026 national identity cards began to be collected in the Greater Yei region. The civil registry still operates mainly in towns (2024). The regulator has named rural connectivity among its priorities for 2026.

Technology

AI

In August 2026 Germany's development ministry funded an East African Community network for artificial-intelligence research and training covering the partner states. It carries an EAC artificial-intelligence strategy for 2025-2028 and digital skills training. Its AI4EAC Innovation Challenge drew 3,900 students from 100 universities across the region in its 2025 pilot.

In 2025 South Sudan's citizen-facing government services carried no artificial intelligence or chatbot. In December 2023 a UNDP programme installed two portable AI-assisted X-ray units for tuberculosis detection at Torit and Rumbek. At the Global AI Summit in Kigali in April 2025, the ICT undersecretary named infrastructure, a quality data centre and skilled people as the three prerequisites for AI. The government presented the fibre-optic build as the first of them. In June 2025 the Go Girl ICT Initiative was building a Juba Arabic dataset, YoGage AI, to train systems to detect hate speech. By July 2025 Nuer, Dinka and Acholi had been added to Google Translate from volunteer-built sentence data, with 10 to 20 more of the country's languages planned.

In March 2026 a regional science and technology conference explored an artificial-intelligence policy roadmap for the eight partner states. As at July 2026 member states had not taken up the EAC central bank governors' call to harmonise regulation of artificial intelligence and digital finance.

ICT Industry

MTN's Bayobab holds a licence effective October 2025 to build and operate national and cross-border fibre, IP transit and leased circuits. As at March 2026 it was building the national backbone with NCT South Sudan. MTN itself holds a renewed mobile operator licence granted in April 2025. It brought eSIM service to the market in January 2025. The mining and petroleum licence cadastre was a vendor system.

Business and competition regulation scored 16.3 of 100 in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states and down over the preceding decade. Economic diversification ranked at the same place in the same measure. Technology startups are very few. ICT service exports were US$35.6 million in 2023 on the IMF balance-of-payments series. In 2025 company formation and business registration were listed among the services on the national government portal.

Innovation ecosystem

South Sudan is not among the 133 economies covered by WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2024, its data coverage being insufficient for inclusion. In 2025 there was no startup or innovation law, and no state programme of startup financing or small business support for the digital sector. There was no public-sector innovation programme, laboratory or fund, nor any stated mechanism for public-private collaboration on digital public services.

The University of Juba's UNIPOD, a US$1.2 million innovation facility, opened in 2023 with UNDP support. In 2023 at least three AfriLabs member hubs, Konetahub, Ataka Hub and Junub Open Space, were operating alongside Scenius Hub in Juba. Juba has an active Google Developer Group, profiled in 2021. In August 2026 MTN South Sudan opened the Aweil ICT Hub in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State for digital-skills training, scholarships and innovation. No capacity, budget, term or intake figure was published. As at 2025 technology startups were very few and venture capital activity was not visible.

Capacity

Literacy

Equality in education scored 3.8 of 100 in 2023, last of all 54 African states, and education quality 1.9 of 100, 51st of 54. Education as a whole scored 10.6 of 100, 53rd of 54. Its strongest component, enrolment, stood at 25.9 and 45th of 54, after a rise of 3.8 points over the decade.

In 2026 rural primary schools captured attendance, enrolment and administrative data in handwritten classroom registers, aggregated by hand for state and national reporting. In 2025 the ministry's own education data system, a donor-funded attendance platform and a separate system for refugees used indicator definitions that do not match. None was linked to the others or to other government systems.

In 2024 UNESCO set up the country's first media and information literacy centre at Juba Day Secondary School, with computers and internet access. In October 2026 the head teacher put those who had used it at about 500. Renovation began in August 2026 on three government facilities designated as digital skills centres, under a programme to reach 3,000 civil servants.

Training and skills

In 2025 South Sudan had no digital skills strategy, no digital skills programme for public servants, and no digital skills programme reaching citizens or schools.

In 2025 at least three institutions offered computer science and information technology degrees. They are the University of Juba's school, established in 2009, Ayii University and Starford International University. Juba's Google Developer Group, profiled by Google in 2021, is one of the few organised technical communities.

In August 2026 Germany's development ministry funded an East African Community AI research and training network, with digital skills work for young people across partner states including South Sudan.

In 2025 there was no state-run employment or jobs-matching platform. In August 2026 1,225 entrepreneurs graduated from a mobile-money operator's training programme. An ICT hub opened at Rumbek in Lakes State on 8 September 2026.

Research institutions

The University of Juba's UNIPOD, a US$1.2 million innovation facility, was launched in 2023 with UNDP support.

In 2025 at least three institutions taught computer science and information technology, the oldest the University of Juba's own school, established in 2009. A regional artificial intelligence innovation challenge open to the eight East African Community partner states drew 3,900 students from 100 universities in its 2025 pilot. A second edition was launched in August 2026.

Inclusion

Access to services

Equal access to public services scored 8.2 of 100 in 2023, last of 54 African states, though up 4.6 points over the decade. Since January 2026 the Directorate of Civil Registry has required the personal identity card, the Butaka Shakhsia, for banking, land transactions and court processes. A minority of the population held one as at 2022.

In 2024 the civil registry operated mainly in towns and was not integrated, leaving most rural citizens unreached. Both the passport and the identity card sit behind fees, as does a nationality certificate. In 2022 an application required two photographs, a blood test and a credible witness. Refugees hold cards issued by the Commission for Refugee Affairs under the Refugee Act 2012, the national card being reserved for nationals. Documents for returnees, displaced people and those at risk of statelessness come through donor-supported projects and issuance campaigns in the Central Equatoria return counties.

In 2025 a national portal offered services that can be completed online, not only described. Account holding was the lowest recorded among Global Findex economies in 2021. The economy was overwhelmingly cash and outside the banking system as at July 2025. The national instant payment system was still a roadmap in February 2025.

Phase one of the tariff exchange-rate adjustment took effect on 26 June 2026, adding about 70 SSP a minute to a 181 SSP baseline for a one-minute call. MTN South Sudan, Zain South Sudan and Digitel implemented a second adjustment at midnight on 26 July 2026, and none of the three published the revised rates. Both followed MTN's December 2025 tariff cut. In August 2026 1,400 teachers were trained in sign language, Braille and inclusive pedagogy.

Digital divides

Equal socioeconomic opportunity rose 12.9 points over the decade to 2023, to 16.8 of 100, 50th of 54 African states. It is one of South Sudan's ten most improved governance measures. The country sits at or near the foot of the same table on mobile communications and on internet and computers.

An estimated 1.90 million people were online at the start of 2025, 15.7 per cent of a population of 12.1 million. By 2026 mobile coverage reached most of the population. In November 2025 a gigabyte of mobile data cost about US$23.70, among the most expensive on the continent.

In 2023 electricity reached a minority of urban households and almost none of the rural population. In 2025 digital systems were concentrated in Juba, leaving border regions and rural counties effectively excluded and coverage outside the capital intermittent. In the same year facility-level electronic health records were concentrated away from rural primary care.

For 2026 the National Communication Authority set rural connectivity, child online protection and regulatory reform through ITU and East African Community cooperation as its priorities. Access was still heavily concentrated in Juba. Education quality and equality in education both sit at the bottom of the African table (2023).

Data

National statistics

A second national strategy for the development of statistics had been drafted by June 2026, its endorsement delayed by political instability at the finance ministry. Surveys still drew on a sampling frame from 2008 (June 2026).

The 2025 Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey, run by the statistics bureau with UNICEF across more than 10,000 households, reported in April 2026. It was the first nationally representative survey of women and children since independence. It found 42% of children attending primary school and 19% of children stunted (2025). The president and the peace-agreement parties postponed the population census until after the December 2026 elections (December 2025). The bureau's chair warned that constituency boundaries lack the data a census would supply. The bureau projects the population at about 16.36 million by the end of 2026 and 19.99 million by 2030 (July 2026).

The capacity of the statistical system scored 21.4 out of 100 in 2023, 51st of 54 African states and down 10.4 points over the decade. South Sudan scored under 20 per cent on both data services and data sources in 2023, against 50-69 per cent on data products. Before the 2025 survey, one household survey had been run in ten years, and no agricultural, business, health or labour force survey (2023). The bureau pre-tested the first nationally representative labour force and migration survey, on tablets, in Juba from December 2025 to January 2026. A population census has been held within the last twenty years; an agricultural census and a business census have not (2023).

Rural health facilities keep handwritten registers, carried to county offices for transcription into the national DHIS2 platform (2025). Primary schools aggregate handwritten classroom registers by hand for state and national reporting (2025). Civil registration scored under 35% for digitisation in the African Union's 2016 monitoring round. The government's financial management system cannot track spending to programme performance (2025).

The National Bureau of Statistics runs a Data Portal with an API and a National Data Archive holding census and survey microdata (2026). With IGAD it agreed data-sharing protocols among ministries for migration and displacement statistics during 2025.

Open data

In the 2024 Open Data Inventory, South Sudan's statistics on ICT and digital, government finance, trade and labour all scored zero out of ten. The same zero applied to education facilities and outcomes, health facilities, crime and justice, food security, agriculture, energy, pollution and land use. A zero means a user cannot obtain the data in open, usable form from official sites, not that no part of government holds it.

Thirteen of the twenty-three categories assessed for South Sudan in 2024 score zero out of ten, and only one exceeds five.

That one is money and banking, at 6.0 out of 10 in 2024. Poverty and income followed at 5.0, and population, national accounts and price statistics at 4.5.

In 2025 there was no national open data portal. There was no national data governance body, no data strategy and nothing published about how government data is governed (2025). A right to information law is on the statute book, but there is no online route to request records and no online publication of them. Disclosure of public records scored 13.0 out of 100 in 2023, 46th of 54, and accessibility of public records 7.8, 49th of 54.

The full text of the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Act was withheld from journalists at its signing in February 2026. Nothing is published on how government systems share data among themselves (2025).

Geospatial data

The World Bank's Joint Food Security Monitor raises monthly alerts for South Sudan's 78 second-level administrative areas (July 2026). It draws on vegetation-index and rainfall drought indicators, read with food prices, fuel prices and exchange rates.

South Sudan Meteorological Services is installing 27 more automatic weather stations with FAO support (February 2026). That brings the network to 40 stations sharing data through the WMO information system. The Systematic Observations Financing Facility approved a US$2.08 million grant for the observing network on 18 February 2026. It pays for stations, maintenance, data systems and staff training. By 30 June 2026, with FAO implementing, 13 of the 16 automatic stations in the facility's compliance plan had been installed. The Nile Basin Initiative handed seven hydrometeorological stations worth US$500,000 to the water ministry in March 2024. No national space or geospatial data policy had been adopted as at August 2026.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

No American public financing, hyperscaler infrastructure or digital agreement had been found in South Sudan by August 2026.

China activities

Exploratory talks were held with Huawei in Hangzhou, reported 31 August 2026, with no agreement signed. At the China-Africa Cooperation Summit in September 2025, Huawei signalled interest in South Sudan's digital architecture, including the national data hub and smart-city plans. It had largely left the country after 2014. It pressed Chinese and African partners for more digital-infrastructure financing.

From June 2016 to November 2018 Chinese government grants funded the first phase of an Educational Technical Cooperation Project with the Government of South Sudan. It delivered course design, printed textbooks, training in China for South Sudanese teachers and the construction of training centres. An ICT teacher training centre equipped under it was handed over in Juba in July 2017. A month of ICT training was run in February 2018, and the ICT infrastructure and training component was completed in September 2018. A second phase was agreed by exchange of letters on 18 June 2020.

EU activities

In 2024 Finnfund lent US$5 million in mezzanine finance to Communication and Renewable Energy Infrastructure (CREI). The loan financed 413 solar-hybrid energy systems for MTN South Sudan's telecom sites. The loan covered their installation, operation and maintenance. In November 2025 Finnfund signed a follow-on investment in the company. It was backed by the EU's Africa Connected programme, through the European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus.

A UNHCR and European Union partnership paid for more than 17,400 national identity cards issued in 2024.

In August 2026 Germany's development ministry BMZ committed EUR 5 million to the East African Community for a regional AI research and training network across partner states including South Sudan. It funds an EAC AI strategy for 2025-2028 and digital skills for 50,000 young people, with no country allocation attached.

Gulf/UAE activities

The legislature ratified the 2022 general cooperation agreement with Saudi Arabia on 19 August 2026. It had rejected the agreement in May.

India activities

No Indian financing, infrastructure contract, agreement or training programme had been found in South Sudan by August 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In 2026 the World Bank reported that South Sudan's identity and passport system had been formally transferred to government ownership. A German vendor built it under a build-operate-transfer arrangement. It still depends on that vendor for software licensing, maintenance, consumables and technical support. The cost of five new production machines delivered in May 2026 is confidential under the supply agreement. Nothing had been found by September 2026 on whether the state holds the system's source code and operating skills, or can exit its support contract.

In January 2026 the National Communication Authority set up an oversight committee with MGI Communications. The Swiss company manages South Sudan's international gateway services and critical data-centre infrastructure. The committee is to strengthen institutional control over the gateway and the national data centre, in line with sovereignty and security priorities. The terms governing data jurisdiction, portability and exit under the MGI arrangement had not been published as at September 2026. The Revenue Authority's revenue-assurance system is hosted and overseen by the National Communication Authority, operated by Ensoft and due to pass to the government after six years (October 2025). At the December 2025 audit, the finance ministry's IFMIS micro data centre and its fully paid backup site had not been shown to be functional. No evidence was given that the technical staff had been trained to operate it.

In October 2024 the ICT ministry presented the planned national data centre as giving the government full control of its digital data. No instrument fixing where state data is held, or under whose law it is processed, had been found in force as at September 2026.

In April 2026 South Sudan and the other seven EAC partner states resolved to build AI systems trained on East African data. The systems are to be stored on regional infrastructure and governed by East Africans. They backed the resolution with a regional AI fund. In September 2026 a three-day workshop in Juba worked on a roadmap for domesticating the AU Data Policy Framework. The AU Commission, AUDA-NEPAD, GIZ and KICTANet hosted it with the ICT ministry.
