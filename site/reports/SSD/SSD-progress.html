South Sudan: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-06  ·  139 systems and instruments tracked

Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy A presidential decree ordering full digitisation set a January 2026 adoption deadline, and the deadline passed without a compliance report.
Full recordThe decree granted an electronic services council full authority over the digitisation of public institutions (decree).

The deadline passed and nothing on file records compliance, an extension or a report against it. The country is placed 192nd of 193 on the international electronic government index for 2024.

A decree with a deadline and no compliance mechanism is the shape of much of this ledger, and the reason is recorded separately: there is no operating gazette in which an instrument can be published or checked.

Full recordThe policy covers telecommunications, postal and information technology, and is corroborated as current by the universal service fund's own pages and by a 2022 country diagnostic (policy).

The nearest thing to a newer directive is a regulator's circular of April 2026 requiring its licensees to integrate with a single national service platform (circular).

No review, successor or delivery report against the 2012 policy appears anywhere in the base.

Full recordThe strategy is donor-financed through the regional digital integration project and consultant-led (strategy).

No draft, spectrum award or timetable follows it. It is the only forward-looking connectivity instrument in this unit that is not a fibre route, and it is being written by external consultants inside a project the same lender finances.

Full recordThey are a results indicator of the regional digital integration project and the lender records them as unpublished (guidelines).

That absence bears directly on the national data centre and the international gateway, both operated by the same private company, and on the question of where government data currently sits — which nothing published answers.

Full recordSpeaking on a data sovereignty panel in November 2025, the authority's Assistant Director for Cybersecurity said countries including South Sudan can store data abroad without compromising sovereignty, through mechanisms such as data embassies (position).

That is a searched absence rather than an unexamined one: no localisation mandate exists and none is being pursued. The only instrument in prospect is a data protection framework still in drafting.

Full recordThe ICT ministry launched a consultancy tender under the regional digital integration project to design and develop the strategy, with bids closing on 30 June 2026 (tender).

No award, draft or adoption date follows it. Two of the three instruments a data-governance regime needs - the strategy and the enterprise architecture - are at tender stage at once, and the third, a data protection statute, is recorded separately as not held.

Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy An open data portal runs at the statistics bureau on public, registered and restricted tiers; no whole-of-government policy exists.
Full recordThe bureau's resources page describes an operational portal with publicly accessible datasets, visualisation, multi-format download and interface access, on three data-access tiers (portal).

It is scoped to statistical data. No open data policy binding ministries generally, no licence statement and no dataset inventory outside the bureau's own catalogue appears in the base.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation No data-protection statute or commission exists, only a constitutional privacy right, and the minister states a bill will come after a policy bill drafted in October 2025.
Full recordWhat stands is the Transitional Constitution's privacy article and nothing else: no statute and no commission as at August 2026 (absence).

The minister stated in December 2025 that a bill will come in 2026, and a policy bill was drafted in October 2025 by a civil-society partnership (statement, feature, drafting).

Meanwhile a biometric payroll register, a humanitarian biometric registration and a planned universal digital identity are all being built with no statute governing any of them.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation A Cyber Crimes and Computer Abuse Act was signed in February 2026, criminalising false information, online impersonation and hate speech alongside hacking, and the text is withheld.
Full recordThe bill passed in November 2025 and was signed on 18 February 2026, with the ministry setting out its operation the following day (signature, passage, operation, effect).

The speech offences and the withheld text are the objections on record. The country has no data-protection statute, no privacy commission and no incident response team, so the first computer-crime statute is also the first digital statute of any kind.

Its text cannot be read because there is no operating gazette, which is recorded separately.

Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The Nationality Act 2011 is the operative statute, creating the register of nationals and the certificate issued from it.
Full recordThe Act establishes the Directorate of Nationality, Passports and Immigration and requires it to maintain a Register of South Sudanese Nationals; the Nationality Certificate is the credential it creates (Act).

No statute governs the national identity card, biometric capture or identity verification as such. The card programme recorded separately runs under an administrative mandate, not a digital identity law, and the base holds no bill that would supply one.

Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation A National Payment System Bill went to a five-day validation workshop in August 2026, with no text, introduction date or parliamentary timetable held.
Full recordThe workshop opened on 10 August 2026 (workshop, transition, bill).

The separately recorded national instant payment system has been planned since early 2025 and has not moved, so the statute and the rail are both at the stage of intention.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability An ICT Authority is being established while awaiting legislation, and a May 2026 account states the President already signed its legal framework; the two are unreconciled.
Full recordBoth accounts trace to one speaker. In May 2026 the minister said the law is out already and signed by the President; in August 2026 he was reported standing the body up while parliamentary legislation is awaited (establishment, framework).

No bill, act, presidential order or gazette entry can be found. The justice ministry's law collection stops at 2017 and omits the cybercrime Act too, the Office of the President publishes no decrees, and the Assembly's bills register stops in 2023 — so the registers are not current enough for their silence to settle anything.

The division of competence with the existing communications regulator is unstated. The qualified status is the resting state.
 Mixedan ICT Authority reported both as legally established and as awaiting legislation, from the same speaker
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No artificial intelligence statute: the Cybercrimes and Computer Misuse Act 2026 is the closest instrument, enacted citing AI-enabled abuse.
Full recordThe legislature passed the bill on 25 November 2025, with the ICT minister giving artificial intelligence being used to discredit and abuse people as a reason for it (passage), and the President signed it into law on 18 February 2026 (Act).

The Act is technology-neutral - hacking, identity theft, cyberstalking, cyberbullying, false or misleading information - with no artificial-intelligence or deepfake provision of its own (explainer).

A general cybercrime law invoked against a specific harm is not regulation of that harm.

Full recordThe lender records it as non-existent (strategy).

Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The National Bureau of Statistics Act 2024 superseded the 2011 Act, though its text is not published.
Full recordThe bureau's own legal-framework page identifies the 2024 Act, assented in November 2024, as its principal legislation, superseding the 2011 Southern Sudan Centre for Census, Statistics and Evaluation Act (framework).

The Director General briefed the Vice President's office in September 2025 with the Act as one of two documents discussed alongside a census development tool (briefing).

The Act's full text is not reachable through the bureau's pages or the justice ministry's laws index, which still lists only the 2011 Act. A statute in force that a citizen cannot read is the standing condition of much of this base.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation A technical committee was given fourteen days in January 2026 to launch an Official Gazette and clear a publication backlog of every law since 2011, and no edition is on file.
Full recordThe committee was formed by the President on 12 January 2026 (committee).

A protocol issued the week before classifies all documents bearing the President's signature as privileged executive communication that cannot be photographed, scanned or shared online.

Those two facts together are the single most consequential entry in this ledger. A country with no operating gazette and a rule against reproducing signed instruments cannot have its laws read, which is why the ICT Authority's status is unresolvable and why the cybercrime Act's text is withheld.

Data protection Data protection authority Not held, searched 2026-09-06 - the communications regulator carries information protection and data privacy among its functions in the absence of an authority.
Full recordThe authority's own overview describes its statutory mandate under the National Communication Act 2012 as including information protection and data privacy, alongside its telecommunications, broadcasting and postal regulatory role (overview).

A civil-society organisation convened a two-day workshop in May 2026 with the regulator, members of parliament and the UN mission's legal unit to review and validate version 1.0 of the Data Protection Bill 2025 (workshop).

A sector regulator holding a privacy function inside a telecoms mandate is not a data protection authority, and the argument that a bill alone would not create one is made separately in the research recorded elsewhere.

Data protection National data protection readiness A Data Protection Bill reached version 1.0 and a validation workshop; no statute and no authority exists.
Full recordCivil society convened the regulator, members of parliament and the UN mission's legal unit over two days in May 2026 to review and validate the bill (workshop).

Against that, the regulator's own overview shows the privacy function still sitting inside a telecoms mandate (overview), and a research organisation has argued that what the country needs is a genuinely independent and resourced authority rather than a bill alone (analysis).

Readiness here means one drafted bill and one borrowed function.

Standards National quality standards The standards bureau is developing a metrology framework to verify mobile data consumption independently.
Full recordThe bureau announced at World Metrology Day in May 2026 that it is building a regulatory and metrology framework to measure and verify mobile data consumption, coordinating with the communications regulator before engaging operators (framework).

Its constituting statute gives it a council, standard-setting powers and conformity assessment and quality-mark functions (Act); the communications regulator's incident response team adopted a National Cyber Incident Management Framework in November 2025 on central coordination, mandatory reporting and national visibility (incident framework).

Independent verification of what an operator bills for would be the first consumer-facing quality standard in this base. It is at planning stage.

Standards Adoption of international standards The regulator committed to the Common Alerting Protocol in May 2026, under a regulation that already lets it recognise foreign approvals.
Full recordThe 2016 type approval regulation, made under the National Communications Act 2012, establishes type approval and acceptance and expressly permits the regulator to recognise foreign national regulatory authorities' standards and equipment approvals (regulation).

As the country's focal point for the international telecommunications union's early warning pillar, the regulator held a national workshop and committed to adopting the Common Alerting Protocol for standardised multi-channel alerts, backed by a planned US$2.8 million emergency operations centre investment (commitment).

Recognising others' approvals is cheaper than writing standards, and it is what this base shows the country doing.

Data Exchange Interoperability of education systems The education data landscape is largely manual and fragmented with limited interoperability; a costed reform plan was produced in March 2026.

Full recordThe regional digital integration project, at US$67 million, is the active programme addressing connectivity and the digital divide through broadband fibre deployment (mission).
