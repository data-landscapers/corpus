Shape check, run before the comparison: 89 sources for this place in the window — 29 in the earlier half (2025-08 to 2026-01), 60 in the later (2026-02 to 2026-08). The two halves are comparable, so the comparison is made over the whole window.

Movement values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a measured position worsened. Closed — the programme ended. No change — the position at both ends is the same. Baseline not held — the base carries no position at the start of the period, so no movement can be stated. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, slipped.

Summary of the period

The payments layer gained wholesale plumbing and no retail rail. SSIPS went live on 10 October 2025 with 13 banks, combining real-time gross settlement and automated clearing; the retail instant system announced with AfricaNenda in February 2025 passed its December 2025 rollout target with no go-live documented. Over the same period cash was removed as a payment channel for passports and then for tax at revenue offices.

Identity moved in supply rather than in system. Passport issuance recovered from a shortage across every category to about three days' processing and five production machines were delivered, while birth registration stood at about 10% at both ends of the period.

The governance layer arrived in the wrong order. A cybercrime statute entered force in February 2026 while the data-protection bill went from no draft to a drafted policy bill and no further. One position worsened: the cost of mobile data was raised twice with no rate published.

Four instruments moved and each moved less far than its announcement suggests. The Cyber Crimes and Computer Abuse Act went from not existing to in force in February 2026, with its full text withheld at signing. The data-protection bill went from no draft to a drafted policy bill and is not before the Assembly, so the country ended the period as it began it, with constitutional privacy and no statute.

The full-digitisation decree entered force in December 2025 with a January 2026 deadline for all public institutions to adopt digital systems; the deadline passed inside the period and no compliance report has been published. The ICT Authority went from not existing to being established while legislation is awaited, on an account that does not sit with the May 2026 report that its legal framework was signed.

Both backbone programmes advanced on paper and neither delivered. The state route moved from a US$9m design budget in June 2025 to a 2,400 km route announced in October 2025 with no construction date; the private route went from no licensed backbone operator to a 15-year licence effective 16 October 2025 and a build in execution from early 2026.

The national data centre is the one row where the record moved backwards. It was reported as nearing completion in September 2025 and described as halfway complete in April 2026. Both are summit statements by or about the government and the two have not been reconciled, so no progress figure for the build can be stated for either date.

Institutional control tightened around infrastructure the state does not operate. The regulator constituted an oversight committee with MGI Communications in January 2026 covering the international gateway and the data centre, both of which the same Swiss company holds at both ends of the period. Payment-security capacity was added through a Digital Financial Services Security Lab with the ITU, and spectrum strategy development was launched in July 2026 with nothing published.

Rural coverage is the one infrastructure row that stalled outright: in Kauto, an extension expected from February 2026 was not delivered and the area ended the period as it began it, with one site. Energy capacity did advance, privately: Finnfund's follow-on took its total CREI investment to US$10m, with 490 of 499 MTN sites renovated.

Identity gained documents and machines, not a system. Passport issuance moved from a severe shortage across every category to processing at about three days with stock stated to December 2026, five production machines were delivered in May 2026, and national identity card distribution reached Greater Yei in July 2026. Against that, birth registration stood at about 10% at both ends and Nationality Certificate coverage at roughly the same share of the population. Two systems that would change those numbers — a unified digital identity and a centralised civil register — moved from not existing to announced, with nothing named behind them.

The one biometric register that operates is a payroll. It went from no record before January 2026 to a US$15m programme in its third phase, covering the organised forces, with data on a Ministry of Finance server and no data-protection authority over it at either end of the period.

Payments moved at the wholesale end only. SSIPS entered service in October 2025, replacing a paper-based manual interbank process, and its settlement component was reported in use by May 2026 without figures. The retail rail stalled. Mobile money — the layer citizens actually transact on — was in force as legal tender at both ends, with no conduct supervision on record at either.

Revenue systems accumulated. An e-Services Platform, electronic cargo tracking, an e-visa portal with published fees and a six-year revenue-assurance agreement at 3.56% to the vendor all entered the record where none stood before. The e-Government concession they mostly run through did not move, though its position is now contested at both ends.

Use rose and affordability worsened. The regulator reported internet users moving from 1.3 million to 1.9 million and penetration from 12% to 15.7%, the only national measurement in the period. Against that, mobile data was about US$23.70 per gigabyte in November 2025; MTN then cut tariffs by 25%, and two exchange-rate adjustments raised charges twice more with no revised rate published by anyone. The period therefore ends with no published price for mobile data.

Skills provision produced one costed plan and no delivery: Central Equatoria's three centres at a stated US$2,806,100, with no financier or start date named.

All twelve were unheld at both ends of the period, so none registers as movement. The two that matter most are the ones the period's own instruments should have produced: a data-protection statute, promised for 2026 alongside a cybercrime act that entered force, and a domestic budget line for the ministries and authorities carrying every programme above. Each was probed on 9 August 2026 and none returned a document.
