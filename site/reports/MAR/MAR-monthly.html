Morocco: monthly update, September – October 2026

Edition of 2026-10-01  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
Developments summarised from sources published between the beginning of last month and today.
Summary of the month · Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion

Summary of the month

The digital ministry presented its AI roadmap to 2030 as moving into implementation on 8 September, with targets of MAD 100bn in value added, 50,000 jobs and 200,000 certified talents, relaunched the national Idarati portal on 18 September with access to 874 online services, and on 30 September announced the first two Darija language tools of its partnership with Mistral. For the 23 September legislative elections, candidacy declarations had to be filed through the elections portal.

The third national disability survey found only 20 per cent of people with disabilities reporting internet access, and the Casablanca prosecutor general opened an investigation on 14 September into a party leader's claim that his party's phones were monitored.

Governance

Data protection

The Casablanca prosecutor general opened a judicial investigation on 14 September into a party leader's claim that his party's phones were monitored, citing the conduct of the elections. No finding is held.

Regional collaboration

The UN Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia and the minister for digital transition signed a memorandum in Rabat establishing a framework for regional cooperation on modernising public administration and advancing digital governance reform. No programme, value or deliverable is attached to it.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

A party's election manifesto proposes that administrative silence count as approval, that prior authorisations be reduced, and that artificial intelligence be used in the governance of public policy. It binds nobody unless the party forms a government, and no bill, scope, exempted-decision list or timetable exists.

The spyware allegations were argued rather than adjudicated. Two Moroccan commentaries on 8 September set out the domestic case: an academic and politician rejected as overstated the July 2026 consortium findings tying Moroccan bodies to the compromise of the Spanish prime minister’s phone, and a separate press analysis asked whether the Spanish courts will reopen a related file — neither the findings nor any court filing is held here, and the repository establishes nothing about the underlying allegation either way.

Finance

MoUs and other agreements

The digital transition ministry and a critical digital-infrastructure supplier signed a memorandum in Rabat on developing digital infrastructure, cloud computing and artificial intelligence. No value, deliverable or date is published, so what is held is an intention.

New investments

The African Development Bank and the insurance supervisor ACAPS launched a US$680,000 InsurTech programme in September, US$510,000 of it an AfDB grant, to run until early 2027 and extend digital insurance to the under-insured.

ICT Infrastructure

Data Storage

On 8 September the Dakhla green data-centre campus was put at 100 hectares and a potential 500MW, part sovereign and part open to private investment, in a portfolio the ministry presented as taking national data-centre capacity towards one gigawatt. No cost, operator, financing close or build date is held.

DPI

Other GovTech and e-Gov

The digital transition ministry relaunched the national Idarati portal on 18 September as a single access point to 874 online services and 2,533 published administrative acts, with a marketplace of AI tools attached. No user count is given.

The interior ministry made the elections portal the compulsory route for candidacy declarations to the 23 September legislative elections, closing it at noon on 8 September. No filing count, or fallback for candidates without access, is stated.

Technology

AI

The AI roadmap was presented as moving to delivery on 8 September. The ministry set targets of MAD 100bn in value added, 50,000 jobs and 200,000 certified talents by 2030, showed a sovereign AI marketplace, an assistant on the administrative-procedures portal and a national e-wallet proof of concept, and the four JAZARI institutes held general assemblies and moved to operations. The roadmap document is still not published.

On 30 September the digital ministry and Mistral announced a Darija dialect classifier and a Darija speech-recognition model as the first open-source tools of their partnership, saying further generative-AI and public-service models are in development. The release does not say when the tools become available, and no terms, cost or data-hosting arrangement of the partnership is published.

The national rail operator tendered a MAD 1.08 million study to design a national video-surveillance system with AI analytics and central supervision by 2030, reported on 10 September; no overall budget or deployment timetable is announced.

Innovation ecosystem

The state put money behind the startup strategy. A decree of 3 August authorised Tamwilcom to invest up to MAD 347m over three years through the Fonds Catalytique Startups in funds backing Moroccan and foreign startups, with nine fund managers pre-selected; no first commitment is published.

Capacity

Training and skills

The Génération AIoT programme opened seven cohorts in Casablanca, Fès and Oujda, aiming at 1,200 trained people a year from February 2027. Launched in February by the digital transition ministry with a foundation and a technology firm, it had drawn 2,118 applications and gathered 95 participants. No completion or placement figure is published.

The administration school and the digital development agency agreed on 22 September to train civil servants in digital transformation, AI and data management; no number of officials or start date was announced.

Research institutions

Two published outputs show what the university departments are working on, neither of them adopted by anyone. Researchers at two universities built an electronic voting model combining biometric verification of the voter, blockchain and the ability to keep working where connectivity is weak or absent, tested in a field trial with 300 participants and published on 4 September in a peer-reviewed cybersecurity journal. It is a research platform, not an electoral system the authorities have taken up. Separately a scientific review available from 1 September records an expansion of Moroccan research applying artificial intelligence to drought, water scarcity and rising temperatures, across water, agriculture, energy, cities and natural hazards. Neither account carries a paper count, a funding figure or any record of operational adoption.

Inclusion

Access to services

Preliminary results of the third national disability survey put prevalence at 8.7 per cent of the population, up from 6.8 per cent in 2014, across 20,412 households, with the employment rate among people with disabilities at 12.7 per cent - 23 per cent of men and 4 per cent of women - and only 20 per cent reporting internet access despite most owning mobile phones. Officials caution that the 1.9-point rise partly reflects broader detection rather than an equivalent rise in disability. The repository holds no accessibility standard, audit or enforcement mechanism to read the access figure against.

Digital divides

Residents of thirteen douars in the Ben Cherro area of Beni Mellal province say phone service cuts from about 8pm until the following morning, with internet throughput degraded over the same hours, and that the consequences are sharpest in emergencies, reported on 17 September. The account is a local association figure's through a news outlet; no operator statement, regulator measurement or restoration commitment stands beside it.
