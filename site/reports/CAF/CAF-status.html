Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

The Central African Republic's digital state exists mostly as law and appropriation rather than as systems. A personal data protection law has been in force since January 2024 with no implementing decree and the authority it creates is not established, eighteen months past its statutory deadline; a cybersecurity law of January 2024 created a national agency that appears nowhere in the 2024, 2025 or 2026 estimates.

The appropriations show the scale: the 2026 programme budget carries FCFA 4,000,000 for network and information-system security and FCFA 2,000,000 for digital trust, FCFA 6,000,000 for the year for the whole country, against FCFA 1,600,000,000 transferred from the same ministry to the regulator.

Connectivity limits everything else. Internet use is about 670,000 users, 12.0%, at October 2025, and satellite service live since March 2026 costs about 136% of monthly income per head. Fourteen of the rows below are Not held.

The backbone has not moved in three years. 935 km of terrestrial and sub-fluvial fibre has been in service on the Bangui-Douala corridor since February 2023, with no new kilometrage or route since, while the 2026 estimates give FCFA 2,000,000 to connect prefecture capitals to Bangui over it. Against that the minister has stated a twelve-thousand-kilometre fibre commitment, roughly ten times what is built, with no contract, financing or timetable.

Mobile service is licensed at all three operators and thin on the ground. The third launched fourth-generation service in Bangui in May 2026; a licence is not coverage, and 4G population coverage was measured at 0.3% in 2023. Where one operator is the only network, its failure removes everything at once: at one northern town subscribers reported no calls, no internet and no mobile money for close to a month, some officials unpaid because salary transfers run over mobile services.

Satellite access arrived and was at once conditioned: service has been live since 16 March 2026 with 450 government kits, and in April the ministry deactivated roaming on kits acquired abroad, on traceability and national-security grounds.

Nothing is built for hosting: the national Tier III data centre remains unbuilt, five threads and three sponsors circling it, a 2026 pitch repeating a 2022 target. The only cybersecurity capability delivered rather than announced is a certification platform in a funder's 2023 delivered scope, with no operator or issuance record. Electricity access, the constraint every item here meets, was 17% in 2023.

Identity reaches citizens, and it is priced informally. The card's legal tariff of FCFA 4,500 was restored by interministerial order in June 2025 after five years of charging above it; at one prefecture police are reported to serve one religious group first and to demand FCFA 100,000 to 200,000 from another.

The one identity system with a hard number is electoral: a definitive list of 2,398,158 registered voters — 1,641,083 from 2020 plus 757,075 registered in 2025 — across 3,910 voting centres. A national digital identity system was announced in November 2025 at US$13m, unifying existing biometric and civil-registration databases, on a 2027 to 2032 window, procurement still ahead.

Government platforms exist and publish no use: a unified public-services portal launched in December 2025 with no usage figure; a ministry-wide planning platform digitised one whole ministry at a cost stated nowhere; and the online tax platform has published no filing figure since its March 2025 launch. The only state system with a delivered result is a payroll clean-up removing more than 2,000 ghost workers. The state electricity utility's bills became payable over one operator's mobile-money service on 2026-08-04, with no tariff, transaction fee or volume disclosed.

Below that the layer is missing: no interoperability or data-exchange framework is held, the minister's own January 2026 statement being that state information systems remain heterogeneous; no health, education or agriculture system is held as a system, only appropriations of FCFA 6,000,000 for education and FCFA 2,000,000 for agriculture; and nothing on local-government systems, in a year when municipal and regional elections were held for the first time in about 36 years.

Two statutes are in force and neither has produced an institution. The data protection law of January 2024 has no implementing decree and its authority, which would wield a sanction ceiling of 5% of pre-tax turnover, is not established. The cybersecurity and cybercrime law of January 2024, carrying six to ten year sentences and fines of FCFA 5m to 10m, is unchanged in text and was named by a commentator in July 2026 as the instrument now used against online content; its own text is not held. No national cybersecurity strategy, response team or operations centre exists: the only evidence is a 2022 appraisal recording all three absent and a score of 3.6 on a cybersecurity index against 29.5 for the region.

A new public procurement code was adopted in December 2025, replacing a 2008 law, with a FCFA 10,000,000 publicity threshold and a 5% preference margin for national and regional firms; promulgation is not recorded and the enacted text is not held.

The universal service fund is a rule without an outcome. Provided for by a 2018 law and financed by a 2% levy on operators' previous-year turnover, it is still not operational, with effective implementation targeted for 2026, and has never published an amount collected or a balance in the seven years since the levy took effect.

No national AI policy, strategy, decree or bill appears anywhere in the base, and no AI system is recorded as deployed in state service delivery; the one AI-in-government item held is an analytical finding that a presidential land certificate was likely machine-generated. What has moved is a request: the communications council asked the regulator for tighter subscriber identification and for cooperation with international platforms on closing accounts, acknowledging that its competence covers media professionals. No instrument was adopted.

The most consequential technology record here is cryptocurrency. Bitcoin's legal-tender status was revoked in March 2023; a state-sponsored token ended with 10% of its 210-million target sold and under EUR 2m raised; and a presidential meme coin passed US$900m market capitalisation over its launch weekend, then lost more than 75% of its value and remains the payment rail for tokenised land concessions, 122 plots sold for about US$38,308 by November 2025. No evidence is held that its revenues were declared as public income.

The sector cannot be sized: no revenue, employment or operator disclosure is held, the regulator publishes nothing, and no startup funding figure exists.

The 2026 state budget sets resources at FCFA 368bn against expenditure of FCFA 396.35bn, a deficit of FCFA 28bn, own resources up 11.18% and public investment down 2.06%. Inside it, the digital lines are small and their purposes often unstated. Two private-invoice transfer lines in the digital ministry's vote carry FCFA 1,000,000,000 and FCFA 500,000,000 for 2026, the first doubled in a year, with no purpose stated in any year; together they are 21 times the ministry's entire investment title. The digital development agency's transfer is FCFA 300,000,000 for urban optical loops plus FCFA 118,000,000 to clear six months of unpaid 2024 salaries. The state also budgets FCFA 250,000,000 of telephone arrears to its own incumbent for a third year while negotiating that operator's privatisation.

The largest 2026 digital line outside the ministry's vote is revenue collection: FCFA 420,000,000 for customs system deployment.

None of these figures is enacted: every figure held is proposed or revised, all three enacted finance laws being image-only scans, and no outturn is held at any grain because the quarterly execution series is published entirely as page images whose digits do not extract.

Fourteen instrument rows carry no reliable statement, and that proportion is itself the finding. Four are systems no document establishes: a cybersecurity strategy, response team and operations centre; a government interoperability layer; sector management information systems; and local-government digital systems for the councils elected in December 2025.

Four are capability absences: no technical capacity or workforce record at any date, no digital literacy programme, no digital skills programme, no research capacity. Three are technology absences: no AI policy, no AI system deployed in state service delivery, no India-linked activity.

The last three are why this report's own figures cannot be tested: no independent measurement of internet penetration, every figure tracing to one international estimate redistributed by three intermediaries; no enacted appropriation for any year; and no budget outturn at any grain. For the last two the documents exist as page images, so availability is not the constraint and extraction is. Each row is in gaps.csv with what would settle it; none has yet been probed.
