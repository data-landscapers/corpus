This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

By 31 August 2026 the higher education ministry had validated a science, technology and innovation policy for 2027 to 2035. An evaluation of the 2015-2024 policy found that none of its objectives were met. The budget speech delivered to the National Assembly on 5 December 2025 said the Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy had begun formulating a National AI and Emerging Technologies Policy. In April 2026 the ministry reported that policy still under development and not adopted. The Government Cloud Policy that accompanies the Government Cloud Strategy 2023-2027 awaits sign-off, with its issue and effective dates left blank.

In May 2025 the Digital Government Policy and Roadmap was validated under World Bank WARDIP financing. It plans a Government Service Bus and an e-government interoperability framework. A National Data Policy covering data governance and data sovereignty was validated in July 2025. No adoption of either policy has been found.

The National Digital Economy Masterplan 2024-2034 is the overarching strategy for the digital agenda. It attaches a target year and an estimated cost to each project it plans. The older ICT4D Policy Statement 2018-2028 frames eight pillars, from human capital and e-government to broadband, universal access and cyber-security capability.

The National Broadband Policy 2020-2024 has run its term. The strategy behind it costed nineteen action plans at D65,000,000 in 2020. No successor broadband instrument has been found. The country's first unified National Land Policy 2026-2035 was published on 12 August 2026, after a presidential launch on 22 December 2025.

On the Ibrahim Index, The Gambia scored 54.7 out of 100 for overall governance in 2023, 20th of 54 African states and above the continental average. It gained 7.2 points over the decade and lost 0.1 over the most recent five years.

Legislation and regulation

The Gambia's first comprehensive data-protection statute is law: the National Assembly passed the Personal Data Protection and Privacy Bill 2025 by October 2025. The enacted Act treats biometric data as sensitive and gives data subjects rights of access, rectification and erasure. The legislature's own bill tracker recorded the bill at "Assented" as at 15 July 2026.

Telecommunications is still governed by the Information and Communications Act 2009. Its Chapter II Part XIII carries the older provisions on processing personal data, privacy of communications and interception. The Communication Bill 2025 would modernise that Act and reach cybercrime, data protection and legal recognition of electronic signatures, contracts and transactions. It was moved for second reading on 3 March 2026 and referred to committee, members having raised its silence on surveillance powers. As at July 2026 it was still before parliamentary committees.

PURA put draft Broadcasting and Online Content Regulations 2026 out for consultation in April 2026 alongside six other drafts. The drafts are made by the minister under section 226(1) of the 2009 Act, so the country's online-content rules face no second reading, committee stage or vote. Draft enforcement regulations would build a single compliance, penalty and appeal code across every regulated sector. Draft unified fees regulations would build a single fee code. Draft complaints and consumer-protection regulations would give a consumer right to complain, with timelines, hearings and appeals.

In March 2025 the National Assembly passed a criminal offences bill replacing colonial-era law and removing the offence of criminal defamation. The assent copy published by the justice ministry keeps offences of inciting to sedition and of false publication and broadcasting; no assent date has been found.

A Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Bill received its first reading on 5 March 2026. A State Intelligence Service Bill had been validated at a workshop by 31 July 2026. The Statistics Act 2005 remains the constitutive statistics law. No general artificial-intelligence legislation was in force or before the legislature as at June 2026.

Data protection

The Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act 2025 establishes an independent oversight Commission. The bill laid before the National Assembly made the Information Commission, created in 2021 for access to information, that authority. It gave the Commission powers to investigate complaints, enter and search premises, impose fines and issue enforcement notices. Controllers must notify the Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, and notify affected individuals where the risk is high. Concealment carries criminal penalties. Individuals gained rights to be informed, to access, rectify and erase their data and to object. The bill set a one-month deadline for answering an access request. The Commission became a full voting member of the Network of African Data Protection Authorities on 10 June 2026.

In 2010 ECOWAS Supplementary Act A/SA.1/01/10 required each member state to establish a legal framework and an independent supervisory authority. Its Article 36 permits transfers to non-member countries only where protection is adequate, and only on prior notification of that authority. The Gambia signed the Malabo Convention on 2 December 2022 and committed to ratify it so that it could be domesticated.

The Government Cloud Policy of 2023 sets government data residency, awaits sign-off and covers the government sector only. No general legal localisation rule has been found. At the Communication Bill's second reading on 3 March 2026, members raised its silence on surveillance powers. The National Intelligence Agency has been renamed the State Intelligence Service, and its bill had been validated, not enacted, by 31 July 2026. The WAPIS-DACORE police data centre was inaugurated on 14 October 2021 with ECOWAS, INTERPOL and the EU.

On the Ibrahim Index, protection against discrimination scored 0.0 out of 100 in 2023, the country's lowest indicator of any kind. It fell 8.3 points over the decade.

Regional collaboration

On 31 August 2026 Omar Alieu Touray handed the ECOWAS Commission presidency to General Birame Diop of Senegal, ending The Gambia's four-year tenure. Ghana's Amin Amidu Sulemani took the infrastructure, energy and digitalisation portfolio at the same handover.

PURA and Senegal's ARTP signed a memorandum in December 2025 to implement the ECOWAS Community Roaming Regulation. It caps voice, SMS and data charges, grants thirty consecutive free incoming-call days and sets up joint anti-fraud monitoring, with services from 1 March 2026. The regulator names equivalent agreements with Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Sierra Leone and Liberia. ECOWAS ministers at Cotonou had required full implementation by all mobile operators by the end of 2025. A Lomé experts' meeting in August 2025 tracked progress through bilateral tariff agreements.

A Senegal-Gambia memorandum signed in June 2026 moved into implementation around connectivity, cybersecurity, skills and digital transformation. The two regulators are separately coordinating frequencies and quality-of-service work.

The Gambia signed the Malabo Convention at Addis Ababa on 2 December 2022, one of fifteen signatory states, and undertook to ratify it. In April 2024 the communications ministry told a United Nations commission that the government had ratified it in March 2024. No instrument of ratification or deposit has been found. The country was welcomed into the Smart Africa Alliance in March 2026 as one of six recently joined member states.

A draft ECOWAS directive on electronic communications, begun in 2019, was still short of adoption at a September 2025 validation workshop. The ECOWAS Community budget for 2026-2028 carries no digital appropriation, and its one ICT-named line is at nil. The Gambia scored 60.1 out of 100 on regional integration in 2023, 10th of 54 and up 17.4 points over the decade.

Standards

As at September 2026 the Bureau held ISO Correspondent membership, with places on ISO technical committees, and was a member of the International Electrotechnical Commission. It has been the WTO Technical Barriers to Trade enquiry point since 2014. ISO's secretary-general visited on 29-30 June 2026 and pressed the country to contribute to international standards, not only adopt them. He asked for Gambian experts on technical committees.

The national standards catalogue of 2022 contains almost no ICT-specific standards. Its electrotechnical section holds adopted IEC entries only, and many entries are marked draft or withdrawn. The Gambia Standards Bureau was established by the TGSB Act 2010. It publishes the Gambian Standards series by ICS class, across management, food, building, metrology, halal, petroleum, packaging, electrotechnical, transport, and water and sanitation.

The National Land Policy 2026-2035 commits the government to develop geo-information data standards and a nationwide unique property identifier for a computerised Land Information System. The National Digital Addressing Policy gives the Bureau the technical standards and GICTA the national addressing databases and portals. GICTA's published account of its statutory functions claims enforcement of standards for government hardware, software and databases. It also claims custody of a national data bank and coordination of government e-signature infrastructure. As at April 2024, ECOWAS and the EU D4D Hub had formulated guidelines for certified electronic signatures while the roadmap was still being defined.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

On 18 July 2026 Gambia Participates convened members of the Human Rights and Constitutional Matters and the Education and ICT committees with the Gambia Press Union and the Media Council. The meeting concerned the Communication Bill, then before committees. The union set out the restrictive media laws it wants repealed. The organisers noted that lawmakers could not publicly discuss a bill before committees, but could hear stakeholders' concerns.

PURA opened a public consultation on seven draft instruments by notice dated 1 April 2026, with comments due from 1 to 10 April. Part V of the draft Broadcasting and Online Content Regulations provides for registering designated online content providers and journalists, with a register of journalists. It sets a separate registration regime for "Social Media Users with Significant Public Reach". The draft puts content-monitoring and editorial duties on intermediaries, reaches foreign platforms and establishes a Content Complaints Committee. It also sets rules for political and electoral coverage and for live broadcasting. It would be made by ministerial regulation under the 2009 Act, with no parliamentary stage at which it is debated.

The Gambia ranked first of all 54 African states on public perception of freedom of speech in 2023, at 90.3 out of 100. Digital freedom scored 84.6, 5th and up 44.6 points over the decade, and media freedom 66.8, 10th.

The August 2023 stakeholder engagement plan for the World Bank-financed WARDIP sets the programme's consultation, disclosure and grievance arrangements. The Ministry of Information runs a national misinformation and disinformation response centre, Factguard. It takes public submissions of messages, photos, videos and links by free hotline, and trained human analysts check them. Each determination is published with its sources and reasoning on a public audit trail.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The state's own money carries 41.0 per cent of the digital lines in The Gambia's approved 2026 budget, GMD 661.9 million of GMD 1,612.4 million, against 20.8 per cent of GMD 2,457.6 million in 2025. The treasury-funded lines of the Public Administration Modernization Project come to GMD 253.6 million in 2026, against GMD 77.2 million in 2025.

The World Bank's regional digital integration grant closes on 29 December 2028. It carried GMD 1,945.7 million of IDA money in the digital ministry's 2025 budget. The 2026 budget carries GMD 516.7 million under it, beside a new GMD 433.9 million IDA line for regional statistics harmonisation.

The 2026 budget puts GMD 70.4 million of domestic money into application software and licences for the government's financial systems. The same 2026 budget puts GMD 20.0 million into developing the integrated financial management system, and GMD 7.2 million into data storage facilities and fees for the modernisation project.

In December 2025 the communications minister told lawmakers that the private partner taking on a state operator's network would build, operate and transfer it without any government guarantee and under a performance bond. He did not give the number of years.

MoUs and other agreements

On 15 April 2026 the Gambia Revenue Authority, the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority, SICPA and Vista Bank signed a memorandum to work toward a revenue assurance system covering the mobile operators. In January 2025 the government signed with the Kalp Foundation to build a blockchain-based digital public infrastructure platform branded "Gambia One". India's foreign ministry records a memorandum on digital public infrastructure cooperation signed in October 2024. It also records the e-VBAB scholarship arrangement, in place since March 2020, under which Gambian students study online.

In July 2026 the Accountant General's Department signed with SUTURA on digital revenue collection. In December 2025 the Ministry of Higher Education, Research, Science and Technology signed with the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences toward a satellite centre in the country. PURA signed with Senegal's ARTP toward implementing the ECOWAS community roaming regulation.

New investments

In July 2026 YCELL agreed to take an 80 per cent stake in GAMCEL, with a network modernisation programme attached. SYSROAD signed a public-private partnership with GAMTEL in December 2025 to modernise the national broadband network. That network was built on the 2017 China Eximbank concessional loan for the Gambia National Broadband Network. The government signed a contract with Margins ID Systems in January 2026 for national identity cards on a build, co-operate and transfer model. The smaller commitments include the Gates Foundation's now-closed payments pilot and an African Development Bank grant for unique bank identification across the West African Monetary Zone. They also include the EU-funded DHIS2 platform tracking the national development plan.

The World Bank's IDA grant of SDR 38.1 million was signed on 14 December 2023 and closes on 29 December 2028. In 2023 that was US$50.0 million equivalent, under the Western Africa Regional Digital Integration Program. It funds telecommunications regulatory capacity and submarine infrastructure for the first phase of the Amilcar Cabral cable. The country is not among the beneficiaries of that programme's second phase, a US$137 million operation announced in March 2026 for Benin, Liberia and Sierra Leone. The World Bank approved US$160 million in IDA financing on 24 June 2026 for an electricity access project covering Togo and The Gambia jointly.

Eleven distinct commitments are on record as at September 2026, announced between September 2016 and August 2026, eight of them country-specific and three regional operations naming The Gambia as a beneficiary. The eight country commitments come to about US$219 million as at September 2026: four grants, two concessional loans, one equity stake and one public-private partnership, from seven financiers, the World Bank most often with two. Connectivity draws four of the eight, ahead of government and e-government systems with two. Most of the money is not yet moving: the largest three, led by YCELL's GAMCEL programme, are recorded as approved rather than disbursing, and the African Development Bank's DIGEST operation remains at pipeline stage. The weight of it sits with the two state-owned operators.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

On 4 September 2026 the mobile network moved to nine-digit numbers. Existing numbers took a two-digit operator prefix: 87 for Africell, 83 for QCell and 86 for Comium. Gamcel keeps its seven-digit numbers in this phase. The country's internet went down nationwide on 4 June 2026, after more than two weeks of electricity outages.

Internet use reached 49.5% of Gambians in 2024, against 45.9% a year earlier. About two-thirds lived under a 4G signal that year. Active mobile subscriptions reached 3,484,313 in 2024, against 3,159,599 in 2023, a count of SIMs, not of people. A gigabyte of mobile data cost USD 1.80 a month in 2021, 1.5% of monthly income per head. Police arrested 19 protesters outside the regulator on 22 August 2025 over its D50-per-gigabyte minimum data tariff. The regulator set mobile data at D75 per gigabyte from 1 January 2026, ending the floor.

Public perception of infrastructure scored 32.4 out of 100 in 2023, down 34.5 points since 2014. Infrastructure is the worst-scoring governance sub-category, at 33.7 and 33rd of 54 African states. Internet and computers scores 26.4 and ranks 35th, up 19.7 points over the decade.

The Broadband Strategy 2020-2024 set targets of 5 Mbps for every subscriber, broadband coverage above 90% and 4G coverage above 85% by 2024. GAMTEL signed a US$50 million partnership with SYSROAD SA HOLDING on 24 December 2025 to modernise the national broadband network. Starlink paid its licence fee in December 2024 and was still without a licence more than a year later. The minister declined to sign a VSAT licence for a company that is not a VSAT operator.

In September 2026 the incumbent's international capacity stood at 25 Gbps over four ACE gateways, with a 28 Gbps backup link through a Senegalese operator. A second submarine cable was still at bid evaluation in July 2026. The Serekunda Internet Exchange Point was founded in July 2013 by fourteen members, including Africell, Gamtel, QCell and PURA. It publishes no current capacity or participant figure. The government 4G LTE network serving offices and officials grew from four base stations to eight in 2022.

Data Storage

QGroup House on Kairaba Avenue, presented as the country's first data centre, was inaugurated on 29 April 2026. Its size and capacity have not been disclosed. President Barrow inaugurated a National Data Centre at Abuko on 30 June 2026, with the launch of the National Identity Management System. The supplier Margins ID Group developed the Abuko facility, and also supplied the financing and long-term operational support.

In 2023 the government ran on a Tier-2 data centre managed by the state operator Gamtel, with a Tier-4 facility under consideration. The Government Cloud Strategy 2023-2027 records that power is not sufficient and reliable for the existing data centres, naming outages as risks of data loss and downtime. It records Gamtel buying generators as backup, and power and cooling costing comparatively more in The Gambia. The financial management system already ran from primary and disaster-recovery data centres in 2017. In October 2026 Gamtel rented site, tower and rack space at 43 sites to ISPs and small businesses.

The Government Cloud Policy of 2023 requires data classified as Classified to be stored and processed in-country, on a government private cloud or on-premise. The policy awaits sign-off, its issue and effective dates left blank, so the rule is not in force. Outside government there is no general data localisation mandate. The data protection bill permitted cross-border transfers of personal data where the receiving country's law or binding safeguards ensure appropriate protection.

Energy

In April 2026 NAWEC admitted a crisis and signalled long-term load shedding. Power cuts had run for nearly two weeks by early June 2026, some communities going more than 24 hours without electricity. NAWEC put the crisis down to maintenance challenges and equipment rehabilitation. In June 2026 it restored key generators and advanced a 50 MW solar project. The nationwide internet outage of 4 June 2026 followed more than two weeks of electricity outages.

The National Energy Compact puts national electricity access at 73.7% in 2024, against a target of 100% by 2030. Access stood at 85.3% in urban areas and 35.3% in rural areas in 2023. 645 communities remained unelectrified in 2025, and the compact puts public and private funding needs to 2030 at US$552 million. It makes on-grid connection the main path and confines mini-grids to areas where extension is unfeasible. A tariff-equalisation policy requires subsidy for mini-grid operators. Inaugurations of completed access projects in 719 communities were scheduled between 7 and 15 February 2026, under the GERMP, GEAP and ECOREAP programmes.

In 2026 a World Bank public finance review put the average tariff at around USD 0.21 per kilowatt-hour, among the highest globally. It attributes this to the sector's weak finances and system losses, and to reliance on heavy fuel oil. Renewables supply about 13% of the mix once imported regional hydropower is counted, against a 2030 target of 30%. Access to energy scored 31.9 out of 100 in 2023, 31st of 54 African states, up 5.4 points over the decade.

Technical Capacity

A two-week certified course in results-based management and ICT data analytics opened in July 2026, its participants expected to build and maintain dashboards. The WARDIP financing agreement of December 2023 requires a steering committee, a technical committee and an implementation unit inside the communications ministry. The incident-response team's 2026 staff training was delivered by the ITU and financed through the same programme.

Google Developer Group Banjul lists fewer than a thousand members (2026).

The Civil Service Reform Programme 2018-2027 requires capacity building, its predecessor's having not taken place. Three accredited institutions offer ICT and computer science programmes. The E-Government Strategy 2021-2024 left the ICT Agency an interoperability framework of policies, standards and guidelines to apply across government. At a June 2025 forum GICTA set out to bring ICT professionals across ministries and agencies under its oversight, as its 2019 Act provides.

Effective administration and statistical capacity both rose sharply over 2014-2023 and now rank inside the continent's top twenty. Most sub-categories deteriorated in the governance category housing infrastructure, public administration and the rural economy. The statistical system's own review found more than a third of its members unaware that the Statistics Act existed.

The pensions directorate's software was developed by the deputy accountant general's team and validated internally in June 2024. In March 2025 the public service ministry and the accountant general's department were building a human-resources and payroll data warehouse with dashboards.

Cybersecurity

The Gambia's binding computer-misuse and cybercrime law is still Chapter III Part III of the Information and Communications Act 2009, assented in May 2009 and never displaced by a dedicated statute. A Cybercrime Bill introduced in March 2024 had not been enacted as at June 2026. In May 2026 the press union named it among pending laws that threaten media freedom. The Communication Bill 2025 would reach cybercrime, data protection and the legal recognition of electronic signatures and contracts. It stood referred to committee after the motion for its second reading on 3 March 2026.

The National Cybersecurity Strategy covered the period 2020 to 2024. A revised National Cybersecurity Policy for 2022 to 2026 was issued in September 2022. Cyber-security capability is one of the eight pillars of the ICT for Development Policy Statement 2018-2028. The ITU's Global Cybersecurity Index 2024 places The Gambia in its 'establishing' tier, for scores of 55 to 85 out of 100. The index measures declared instruments, not tested defences.

The national computer security incident response team, gmCSIRT, had its staff trained in May 2026 on the incident-response and threat-intelligence toolchain they are expected to operate. The training named RTIR, T-Pot, MISP and Taranis NG. It was financed through the World Bank's WARDIP programme and delivered by the ITU. Its service profile of 22 April 2026 places it under the regulator's oversight at the Abuko earth station, serving government, critical infrastructure, operators and the public.

Cybersecurity is one of the four priorities under which the Senegal-Gambia telecommunications memorandum moved into implementation in 2026. It also sits within the declared threat frame of the State Intelligence Service, the renamed National Intelligence Agency, alongside espionage, disinformation and transnational organised crime.

DPI

Data Exchange

On 16–17 July 2026 the Gambia–Senegal Monitoring Committee, meeting at Saly, agreed to exchange customs data by API once the GAINDE system is installed at Karang, Keur Ayib and Selety within three months. It also agreed to deploy SIGMAT for real-time transit tracking and to reactivate the EnCen intelligence-sharing platform. A 2025 World Bank assessment found the regulator's SIM registration database still planned, with no electronic identity authentication. The 2026 budget speech names integration and interoperability in the payments space as a priority for the year.

The World Bank's supervision of the RISE project records a January 2024 baseline of no interoperability across the Gambia Social Registry, the education management information systems, civil registration and health insurance. The value was still 0 at 25 November 2025, with one instance being verified. The target is at least three systems interoperable by October 2028, carrying a US$500,000 performance-based allocation.

In October 2024 data exchange was classified as planned or piloted, with no national exchange in operation. The communications ministry's E-Government Strategy 2021–2024, dated October 2020, calls for an e-Government Interoperability Framework of policies, standards and guidelines for the ICT Agency to apply. The strategy's window closed in 2024, and no such framework has been found.

In May 2026 The Gambia validated a National Enterprise Architecture framework under the GovStack initiative with ITU support, presented as a blueprint for interoperable, citizen-centric services. The Digital Government Policy and Roadmap validated in May 2025 plans a Government Service Bus. The Government Open Data Strategy 2024–2027 proposes a secure data-exchange pilot of the X-Road kind. None of them names an exchange in service.

Digital Identity and CRVS

President Barrow launched the National Identity Management System on 29–30 June 2026, a single National Identity Register with biometric enrolment and chip-based cards. He received the first GAM ID card at the launch. Phase one of enrolment, for citizens aged 18 and over, began on 4 August 2026 at seven Greater Banjul centres. A published schedule took issuance upcountry in three tranches between 7 and 21 September 2026. The card it replaces had reached about 60% of adults (2024).

In 2024 the card then in issue supported on-card and offline authentication only, with no authentication services across government. Bank know-your-customer checks relied on paper identity documents. Manual passports remained in circulation with duplicates issued, with no biometric deduplication against the identity database.

In January 2026 the government signed a build, co-operate and transfer contract with Margins ID Systems Applications. The 2026 contract runs eight years, with the supplier bearing the build cost and taking 70% of revenue. Valid existing cards are replaced free, and the new card is valid for ten years. The card costs 450 dalasi, the same for first issuance, renewal, replacement and vulnerable groups. The legal framework for identity carries gaps in inclusion, data protection, cybersecurity and cross-border recognition (2025).

On 22 March 2024 the health and interior ministries signed a memorandum to generate one national identity number across the identity system and the electronic civil register. Children are assigned a national identification number at birth through the electronic civil registration system. The credential is a dual-interface biometric ECOWAS card compliant with ECOWAS and ICAO standards. The Gambia is not among the states financed by the World Bank's WURI identification programme.

On 6 October 2026 an immigration officer testified that the department no longer has access to data generated under the former biometric identity system. On 13 October the department denied this, saying the servers used under the Semlex contract remain in The Gambia and the data is accessible. In November 2022 the National Audit Office raised 34 high and 2 medium findings on the Securiport immigration-security contract at Banjul airport.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Fintech cash-in rose 4.4% to D28.2 billion and cash-out 5.3% to D32.1 billion between the first and second quarters of 2026. The Central Bank's August 2026 Monetary Policy Committee release carries transaction values alone, with no registered-user or active-user figure. Three months earlier the Committee itself had called for further analysis of why registrations had fallen.

Five Central Bank publications between December 2025 and August 2026 give four different readings of the same registered fintech-user series, two of them issued on the same day and contradicting each other, leaving the March 2026 count unresolved.

BANTABA 2.0 went live on 15 December 2025, a Mojaloop-based instant payment system operated by Gamswitch. It clears and settles across banks, mobile money operators, microfinance institutions and fintechs. It was built under the Gambia Interoperability Switch Programme, led by the Central Bank and the communications ministry. The operator describes what is live as a first phase.

As at April 2026 six commercial banks were live on the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, and no Gambian mobile-money operator or wallet was on the list. In June 2026 one operator connected its Gambian and Sierra Leonean wallets for instant transfers. Since January 2026 the Nafa cash transfer has paid bi-monthly grants to more than 15,000 rural households through Wave. In March 2026 Yonna Wallet became the first Gambian fintech authorised to collect payments for all government services through the Accountant General's Department.

Digital retail payments run under the Central Bank's 2011 mobile money regulation. Its section 8.1 obliges licensed providers to use systems capable of domestic and international interoperability; it does not oblige them to interoperate. Access to banking services scored 15.4 out of 100 in 2023, one of the country's ten worst governance indicators, up 1.2 points over the decade.

Registries

In November 2025 a National Assembly committee found that a UNDP-funded digital workflow system for land administration had gone unused, and that the deeds registry under the Lands (Registration of Deeds) Act 1980 remains manual. It is a deeds record, not a cadastre or parcel index. The National Land Policy 2026–2035 mandates a digitalised National Land Administration System, a centralised Land Information System and digitised land registration. In September 2026 the lands minister told the National Assembly that contracts for World Bank-supported land digitalisation had been awarded. The only cadastral mapping found is a single-area pilot at Kotu Stream, tendered under the WACA coastal project.

The electronic Civil Registration and Vital Statistics system, launched in August 2022, replaced paper birth registration and issues digital birth certificates carrying a national identification number. Cumulative registrations passed 1.4 million by June 2026, against 1.17 million in February 2023. Marriage registration and divorce certificates are handled separately, by application form, at the Ministry of Justice's marriage registry (2024).

As at November 2025 fifteen programmes drew on the Gambia Social Registry to select beneficiaries, against a closing target of eight, with coverage steady at 43 districts. It held around 203,000 households, roughly 70% of the population, in 2024. It is not linked to the foundational identity system, and the Social Security and Housing Finance Corporation keeps its own seven-digit identifier.

Kanifing Municipality's digital address database, built between July 2021 and February 2022 over 36,000 georeferenced properties, serves its waste management and property-tax collection. Business registration runs online through the Single Window Registry. The Independent Electoral Commission keeps a continuously updated nationwide biometric voter register. In 2024 it was not linked to the foundational identity system.

In August 2026 opposition parties set the 212,095 supplementary registrations the commission announced against 179,445 records on the data it gave them. In October the largest opposition party put its dataset at 183,163 records and asked for figures before and after duplicates were removed. On 22 October 2026 the commission's chairman said parties will receive the final register only after the revising courts finish and magistrates sign it.

Sectoral management information systems

As at June 2026 the Integrated Tax Administration System had reached taxpayer sensitisation but not rollout, so e-registration, e-filing and e-payment were not live. The contract was first awarded to SOGEMA Technologies of Canada, which went bankrupt. An Arabsoft and Tunisie Micro-Informatique consortium was engaged at USD 7,270,192.20 in October 2025. The 24-month build is designed to interoperate with the Central Bank, the Accountant General's Department, the Companies Registry, the national identity system and ASYCUDA World.

In March 2026 the customs stack comprised ASYCUDA World, a National Single Window, automated weighbridges, OCR cameras at all entry and exit points and electronically verifiable excise stamps. A Digital Tax Invoicing System was launched in July 2025.

The Ministry of Basic and Secondary Education has run a DHIS2-based education management information system since 2018. Its SEMIS App was scaled nationally by 2021, capturing learner data on offline-capable devices. The Health Management Information System identifies patients by the national identification number generated at civil registration. The two ministries signed a memorandum in July 2026 to link their systems, with the integration not yet operational. The national health policy of 2024 described the health information system as paper at facilities and electronic at the seven regional directorates and the ministry. The main referral hospital launched a pilot electronic medical record developed by the medical research unit (2023).

In March 2026 the Inspector General of Police confirmed that the force still documents its work on paper. Its arrest and detention procedure prescribes paper instruments alone. Computer-aided transcription runs in six courts with electronic filing, fee payment and case assignment. In January 2026 the Chief Justice said suspended donor funding had stalled its expansion. UNDP tendered in March 2026 for an integrated digital case management system for the justice and interior ministries and the judiciary.

In 2026 a Labour Market and Migration Information System linked to the job centres was not yet fully operational. A DHIS2-based platform tracking the national development plan across ministries was built with European Union funding of EUR 493,346, covering 237 indicators across the plan's seven pillars.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In September 2026 the Liberia Revenue Authority agreed to help the Gambia Revenue Authority build a revenue collection dashboard. It is to send the developer of its own dashboard to Banjul for an initial three weeks. The procurement authority trained a second cohort of its cadre from 8 to 10 October 2026 on the 2022 Act, the 2024 Regulations and the rollout of the Electronic Government Procurement system. The new instruments are mandatory from January 2027. A World Bank fiscal-management project had dropped its acquisition of the system for delays in the technical prerequisites, before closing in June 2024.

The government's account of 2025 non-tax revenue, D4.05 billion and 66% of target, named the absence of digital payment systems among the reasons. Work is under way on a GovPay platform to bring public-sector payments across ministries onto one monitored channel.

The Gambia scored 0.2552 on the UN E-Government Development Index in 2024, ranking 181st. Gambians rated obtaining an identity document at 38.4 out of 100 in 2023, 21st of 54 African states and up 7.8 points over the decade. In September 2026 a MyGov platform carrying identity-card, passport and immigration workflows was built but not launched, held up by unresolved contracts over passport issuance. Rollout is targeted before the end of 2026.

In August 2026 the government suspended the up-country round of its civil servant and pensioner verification exercise over technical challenges. It confirmed that the Greater Banjul round would start on 20 August. A National Digital Addressing Steering Committee was launched in October 2025. In October 2026 the government began issuing a biometric driving licence valid for three years, replacing annual renewal. The finance ministry introduced a digital duty waiver application system, committing to a decision within 72 hours where requirements are met, as reported on 30 September 2026.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In August 2026 the Accountant General's Department publicly rejected Kanifing mayor Talib Bensouda's account of the council's use of the Integrated Financial Management Information System. It offered ledgers and expenditure reports it had generated for the council as evidence that the system is in use there.

The finance ministry's 2024 progress report put the system in all area councils, with one council fully on it and most on unreliable links. Its 2025 performance report recorded untrained users at Basse and bandwidth shortages at Brikama. The April 2026 end evaluation found it fully used only in Kerewan, with the councils' revenue system still unlinked.

The National Digital Addressing Policy (2023) makes local governments the custodians of digital addresses and of their own address databases. It requires each to operate a local digital address portal carrying region-wide spatial data. The local databases are to be shared with a national database held by the Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy.

In June 2026 Kanifing had tied digital address codes, built on Google Plus Codes and displayed on compound gates, to property coordinates and its property-rates register. Its rates manager said collectors now use tablets that navigate to a property by its address, not the owner's name. The council expects the codes to improve emergency response and rates collection, and is adding street numbering.

The Greater Banjul Area Sustainable Urban Development Programme 2020-40 has an African Development Bank Transition Support Facility grant. The grant covers the Greater Banjul Digital Master Plan.

Rural digital data capture

In 2025 Smart Paper Technology had maternal, child and immunisation data entered on structured paper forms, then scanned and synchronised with DHIS2. A TB e-Tracker pilot at eleven facilities, rural ones among them, moved case data from paper registers onto tablets (2025). The Ministry of Health replaced paper civil registration with an electronic system and extended satellite internet to health facilities, rural ones included. The 2026 budget speech ties that connectivity to birth registration (December 2025).

The National Digital Economy Masterplan 2024-2034 extends the fibre backbone outward from the Greater Banjul Area. It calls for projects to connect remote and underserved areas and makes a secondary submarine cable a priority. The rural economy scored 57.8 out of 100 in 2023, 29th of 54 African states. Access to markets fell 13.7 points over the decade to 63.0. Rural electricity access remains far below the urban rate (2023).

The education ministry's DHIS2-based management information system, run since 2018, reports teacher attendance by structured SMS where there is no connectivity. The SEMIS school system captures learner data on offline-capable devices (2025). In 2025 few schools had internet for teaching or computers to teach on, and many lower basic schools had no electricity.

In March 2026 the Inspector General of Police said the force still documents its work on paper. Its arrest and detention manual prescribes paper instruments alone: the station diary, the pocket diary and the Diary of Action.

Three digital policing programmes are on record — a 2021 West African Police Information System commitment, a 2026 automated fingerprint identification milestone and a 2025 case-management result — and none puts digital capture at a rural police post.

Technology

AI

On 27 April 2026 the National Misinformation and Disinformation Response Centre was launched with ECOWAS funding, built by the Gambian firm Dundal Systems. The state broadcaster reported it as West Africa's first AI centre against fake news. The centre's own site describes review by human analysts. On 13–15 October 2026 UNDP and the Independent Electoral Commission launched e-Monitor Plus, an AI-assisted platform with human verification. It monitors online misinformation, disinformation and hate speech ahead of the December presidential election.

In October 2026 the Africa AI Governance Index placed The Gambia 37th of 54, at 0.91 out of 4 and in its Nascent tier. The index measures formal governance instruments, not capability. The digital economy masterplan records that The Gambia has never had an AI policy and strategy. It calls for a real-time data analytics system for government, still to be built. In April 2026 the communications ministry told a UN commission that the national AI policy and strategy was still under development. No general AI legislation was in force or proposed as at June 2026, and no dedicated rules covered hallucination or deepfakes.

In May 2026 a case study reported that the revenue authority chooses property owners for rental-tax audit from valuations built by a vendor's machine-learning model. The model draws on pooled tax, utility, municipal and telecom records, and the vendor owns the outputs. The Gambia Revenue Authority's 2025-2029 strategic plan sets project IE06 to pilot big data, cloud computing, AI, machine learning, IoT and APIs in 2026-2027 at D10.4 million. The cost column is headed "project cost (not funded)".

In June 2026 Gambia Participates' founder reported AI-generated images being used to build false narratives during the supplementary voter registration. The same donor-funded programme trained journalists and fact-checkers in Kololi to use AI to debunk them. An open-source Mandinka-English translation model was released in April 2026. A memorandum signed in May 2026 between a Singapore AI firm and a Banjul innovation hub sets out to build Mandinka, Wolof and Fula datasets.

ICT Industry

In July 2026 GAMTEL and YCELL signed a share investment and network modernisation agreement. Under the July 2026 agreement the state is divesting 80 per cent of GAMCEL, its mobile operator, to YCell. The agreement covers nationwide 4G and 5G radio access, core network, OSS/BSS and billing systems, hybrid power at tower sites and fraud management. GAMTEL separately entered a public-private partnership with SYSROAD SA HOLDING to expand core network capacity (December 2025).

Gambian ICT gross value added fell from around 3.5% of GDP in 2023 to around 2.4% in 2024. Sector employment was 2,922 in 2024.

The country's first data centre, QGroup House, was inaugurated in April 2026 by a domestic operator whose partnership with Huawei runs back 17 years. A draft e-commerce policy and strategy reached a national validation workshop on 24 September 2025 under World Bank WARDIP support.

Innovation ecosystem

AfriLabs lists technology hubs including The Disruptive Lab, Gambia Tech and Startup Incubator Gambia (2026). India handed over a Banjul vocational training and incubation centre in 2017, which teaches ICT among its disciplines. Tracxn lists 185 Gambian startups, seventeen of them funded and most in banking and fintech (2026). It records no material venture funding round for the country.

The Gambia is in none of the 133 economies profiled in the Global Innovation Index 2024, nor in the 2019-2023 editions.

The December 2025 budget speech named a Digital Startups Policy, a National Data Policy and a Digital Government Policy and Roadmap among the instruments government was strengthening. In January 2025 the government signed with the Kalp Foundation to build a blockchain-based platform branded "Gambia One". No build, pilot or funding under it has been found.

Capacity

Literacy

In 2025 only 13.2% of Gambian schools had internet access for teaching, 15.6% had computers for pedagogical use and 57% of lower basic schools had electricity. The Education Sector Policy 2016–2030 commits the school system to teaching programming, database design, website administration and network-systems administration. It also commits to integrating ICT into teaching and learning generally.

The 2016 policy carries no count of schools with working ICT laboratories, no connectivity figure and no count of ICT-qualified teachers. School ICT access is one strand of a whole-sector policy, with no dedicated chapter or standalone school connectivity programme.

Education was one of The Gambia's three best-scoring governance sub-categories in 2023, at 61.8 out of 100 and 11th of 54 African states. Education quality scored 57.4, enrolment 48.5 and completion 58.3, the last two up around ten points over the decade.

In November 2025 Africa Missions Global launched an artificial-intelligence education app for Gambian schools, with the basic education ministry as partner. In December 2025 the government signed a framework agreement with Smart Africa for a digital academy training policymakers, civil servants, young people and women. The Central Bank's National Financial Inclusion Strategy carries a financial literacy and consumer protection working group. It includes a gender-sensitive consumer protection framework aimed at women and youth.

Training and skills

By May 2026 nearly 300 law-enforcement officers had been trained on the automated fingerprint identification system through a pool of ten national trainers. Twenty-five planners from more than ten government institutions were trained on demographic modules, software and databases in August 2026. More than twenty health staff were trained on event-based surveillance signal detection and reporting over two days in December 2025. India has awarded 302 scholarships to Gambian students for online undergraduate, postgraduate and diploma study since March 2020. On 29 September 2026 the employment ministry was reported to have signed memoranda with three training institutions. They cover three years of intermediate and advanced digital skills training.

The Civil Service Reform Programme 2018–2027 requires capacity building, its predecessor's having not taken place. It provides for ICT training across ministries, a human-resource management information system, e-government and the closure of civil-service capacity gaps. The Civil Service University's ICT department, running since the late 1990s, lists a national diploma in computer science, Cisco network-certification and cyber-security courses.

In March 2026 the National Accreditation and Quality Assurance Authority named eleven institutions accredited to offer degrees. They include the University of The Gambia and the Civil Service University. It warns that government does not recognise unaccredited degrees. The University of The Gambia's School of Information, Technology and Communications offers BSc degrees in Computer Science, Information Systems and Telecommunications. It also runs six-month diplomas in networking and cloud, web development, data science, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and advanced web applications. Human resources in education scored 88.0 out of 100 in 2023, 20th of 54 African states.

None of the degree-granting institutions with dedicated ICT provision publishes enrolment or graduation figures for those programmes, and none of the foreign channels supplying Gambians with digital and technical places publishes completion or placement data.

Research institutions

On 10 December 2025 the Ministry of Higher Education, Research, Science and Technology signed a memorandum with the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences at Mbour to establish an AIMS satellite centre in The Gambia. Its roadmap leads toward an autonomous centre of excellence. It gives Gambian students access to AIMS postgraduate programmes in mathematical sciences, data science and artificial intelligence in Senegal, Rwanda and South Africa. It also covers training for lecturers and teachers, collaborative research and shared access to datasets.

The Gambia Bureau of Statistics' trend volume on the labour market covers 2023 to 2026. It was financed by the World Bank through the HISWACA project, with ILO technical support. Domestic computing research rests on the University of The Gambia's ICT school and its three undergraduate degree programmes.

Inclusion

Access to services

In April 2026 draft regulations giving consumers a right to complain, with timelines, escalation, hearings, appeals and protection against retaliation, went out for comment and are not in force. PURA's toll-free 148 call centre, with a documented handling and escalation flow, is the complaint route for regulated utilities. The state utility's app takes complaints and photographed fault reports alongside bill payment (April 2026). The Ombudsman opened complaints to online filing in February 2015.

Inclusion and equality was The Gambia's only top-ten governance sub-category in 2023, at 62.8 out of 100 and 10th of 54 African states. It gained 14.5 points over the decade, and equal access to public services scored 55.6, 13th of 54. Access to banking is among the country's ten worst-scoring indicators.

The Persons with Disabilities Act 2021 addresses information and communications in general terms and sets no digital or web accessibility standard. A framework for monitoring the Act, accessibility included, was validated in February 2026. On 11 October 2025 Ecobank Gambia handed a refurbished ICT laboratory of fifteen machines running the NVDA screen reader to the Gambia Organization of the Visually Impaired.

The Nafa cash transfer programme moved into 10 new districts in November 2025, with nationwide expansion planned for 2026. It pays rural households through a mobile money platform, selected from the national social registry.

The National Migration Policy 2020-2030 is the standing framework for refugees, statelessness, non-discrimination, identity documents, border management and migration data. The Refugee Act 2008 entitles refugees to a refugee identity card and a Convention travel document. UNHCR recorded 4,214 people of concern at 31 May 2026.

The Universal Access and Service Policy 2020 provides for a Universal Access and Service Fund and Board. It gives priority to rural communities, women, youth, persons with disabilities, schools and health facilities, and commits to universal broadband coverage within three years.

Digital divides

On 7 October 2026 Smart Africa opened a workshop to pilot gender impact assessment tools for digital policy, with Eswatini and The Gambia as pilot countries. In October 2025 the communications ministry launched a project with the Digital Cooperation Organization to give women-owned small businesses digital skills and business capacity.

The National Gender Policy 2025-2034 names the digital gender divide as a problem but puts no figure on it. GSMA's Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025 does not survey the country. No published measure of the Gambian digital gender gap has been found. The policy, dated June 2024 and funded by the African Development Bank, succeeds the Gender and Women Empowerment Policy 2010-2020. Its situational analysis covers economic development, gender-based violence, education, health, justice and governance.

On 1 October 2025 the World Bank released Gambian microdata from the Global Findex Database 2025, drawn from 2024 fieldwork. It adds national readings of mobile ownership, internet use, digital safety and account access. The Bank also publishes a Gambia Gender Data Landscape, setting gender-equity indicators against regional and income-group peers.

The national digital economy masterplan of 2024 records internet use in urban areas at almost twice the rural rate. The same shape shows in the gap between urban and rural electricity access and in the small share of schools with internet for teaching.

Data

National statistics

Statistical capacity scored 60.1 out of 100 in 2023, 14th of 54 African states, after a 33.8-point rise over the decade. It was the seventh most improved of the country's 96 indicators. On the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators the country scores above 89% on data use but 20-49% on data sources (2023). Household, health and labour force surveys have been fielded in the last ten years, but no agricultural survey and no business survey.

In July 2026 the Gambia Bureau of Statistics published the fifth round of the Labour Force Survey. That round puts the working-age population at 1,434,115, employment at 733,364 and unemployment at 6.2% (March 2026). Labour underutilisation stood at 23.6% on LU3 and 32.1% on LU4 in March 2026. In May 2026 the bureau published the National Food Security Survey Report 2026, covering all eight Local Government Areas. The 2024 population and housing census, the country's first fully digital one, counted 2,422,712 people in its preliminary results (May-June 2024).

NSDS III, published in September 2023, concedes weaknesses in coordination, in modernisation and in data sharing and protection. It records that 35.3% of members of the national statistical system were unaware the Statistics Act 2005 existed. On 17 November 2025 STATAFRIC's peer review named modernising the statistical legal framework among the areas requiring sustained attention. It credited dissemination through the IMF's enhanced General Data Dissemination System and a National Summary Data Page. The Act sits in the bureau's library as an image-only scan with no text layer.

Communication Statistics 2024, dated June 2025, reports mobile subscribers only, with no internet subscription count or penetration rate. Electricity access is monitored from census figures, pending a Multi-Tier Framework survey planned under the National Energy Compact (2025). The education ministry has published a statistical yearbook every May since 2014, from a school census whose response rate has stayed above 95%. Quarterly trade statistics are compiled from the revenue authority's ASYCUDA World customs records.

Open data

In the 2025 Open Budget Survey The Gambia scored 36 out of 100 on transparency, 24 on public participation and 59 on oversight. It ranked third in sub-Saharan Africa on budget transparency.

The Government Open Data Strategy 2024-2027 states that government has no legal framework for open data, no centralised registry and no data-quality software. It records that the missing framework has left some individuals and organisations reluctant to share data, even within government. Formulated with the e-Governance Academy, it sets its targets against 31 December 2026. The Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy put it in place for 2024 to 2027. A 2018 dissemination policy provides free public-use microdata through an online catalogue after registration, with licensed and enclave tiers. That catalogue held three studies on 2 July 2026.

Across the twenty-three Open Data Inventory categories assessed for The Gambia in 2024 the country averages 4.2 out of 10, with three categories at zero and only seven reaching 5.5 or above.

Telecommunications and digital statistics, energy statistics, and crime and justice statistics each scored zero out of ten in the 2024 assessment. Health facilities scored 3, health outcomes 3.5, population 4 and education facilities 4 (2024). Balance of payments, government finance and price indexes each scored 7, and banking statistics 6.5 (2024).

The Access to Information Act 2021 is the statute under which the Information Commission was established. Disclosure of Public Records scored 27.1 out of 100 in 2023, 36th of 54 African states, and Accessibility of Public Records 29.7. Both are among the country's ten worst-scoring indicators, after a decade of improvement. In August 2026 the Commission was procuring a communication strategy on that Act and the Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act 2025, funded by UNFPA under the Peacebuilding Fund.

Use of satellite data

The 2026 budget speech reports satellite internet installed in health facilities to improve birth registration rates. The national Early Warnings for All roadmap, endorsed in October 2025, sets the country's multi-hazard coverage ambition.

On 30 August 2024 the national meteorological service took delivery of Meteosat Third Generation PUMA and ClimSA satellite reception stations. They allow continued reception of Meteosat Third Generation model data and satellite imagery. The stations were supplied under the EU-funded ClimSA programme, contracted by the African Union to the Swiss firm Tecnavia.

FAO's 2024 monitoring framework for the ROOTS project documents a platform tracking rice, vegetable gardens, mangroves, forestry, agroforestry and infrastructure. It works through a National Land Cover Reference System for agricultural and natural-resource planning. In 2025 FAO presented the Gambia GeoLab, a national geospatial platform on Google Earth Engine built under ROOTS. The Global Center on Adaptation funded a Google Earth Engine platform for weather and climate advisories, with training for 30 participants including meteorology staff.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

In July 2025 the minister told the National Assembly that the United States State Department, embassy and Starlink had pressed persistently for a licence while his ministry reviewed the application. Starlink paid its licence fee in December 2024 and was still without a licence more than a year later.

On 23 July 2024 Starlink's director of global licensing and activation, Ben MacWilliams, met officials in Banjul. This followed a virtual meeting between Foreign Minister Mamadou Tangara and Elon Musk on bridging the digital divide. He also met the leadership of PURA and the minister then responsible for the sector.

China activities

In April 2026 Huawei, QCell's technology partner of 17 years, attended the inauguration of QGroup House. The privately built facility holds the country's first data centre.

A China Eximbank concessional loan signed in December 2017 financed the national broadband network. It was on-lent to the state operator GAMTEL and secured against a cash balance the lender could reach. The borrowing took the government through its ceiling on new non-concessional external debt. GAMTEL fell into arrears on interest, and was then required to keep and replenish a dedicated escrow account to service it.

In October 2023 the embassy in Banjul named the National Broadband Network and 5G networks among the projects Chinese companies undertake in the country. It named no company, contract, value or completion date for any of them.

In August 2026 the Chinese ambassador announced that 25 Gambian students would travel to China in September under Chinese Government and MOFCOM scholarships. He also announced plans for a Luban Workshop at the University of Applied Science, Engineering and Technology.

EU activities

In April 2026 EUR 493,346 of EU funding built a DHIS2-based platform tracking 237 indicators across the seven pillars of the Recovery-Focused National Development Plan. It supports collection, analysis and reporting across ministries, with training for the move to programme-based budgeting.

The EU's grant envelope for The Gambia for 2021–2027 is EUR 193 million, under the Global Gateway strategy. It carries no digital-specific figure. The Commission's statement of the programme names digitalisation in one clause, in business and public administration, with no project or instrument attached (September 2026).

The criminal Automated Fingerprint Identification System went live on 28 April 2026 under the EU-funded, INTERPOL-implemented WAPIS programme. The Criminal Records Office premises were renovated and nearly 300 officers trained around it.

The Multiannual Indicative Programme for 2021–2027 was amended at mid-term review on 31 October 2024, with annual action plans for 2022, 2023 and 2024. Its five headings are democratic transition, sustainable growth and jobs, energy, human development and migration management, and none is digital. EU money also sits under the Meteosat and ClimSA reception stations delivered to the Department of Water Resources. It also funded the peer review of the national statistical system, implemented by Expertise France. The National Digital Addressing Policy was developed with the AU-EU Digital for Development Hub and Estonia's eGovernance Academy.

Gulf/UAE activities

As at September 2026 the Islamic Development Bank counted The Gambia among the beneficiaries of the ECOWAS fibre backbone. It financed the backbone with the African Development Bank and the World Bank. Its account names no date, sum or contractor for the Gambian leg. In January 2026 the Bahraini firm CTM360 signed a memorandum with PURA on the cybersecurity of critical national infrastructure. In September 2026 the higher education minister agreed with the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority to draft a memorandum on AI training.

India activities

In March 2026 the Indian High Commission marked ITEC Day for Gambian officials trained under India's technical cooperation programme. India's brief on relations with The Gambia records a memorandum of understanding on digital public infrastructure cooperation signed on 3 October 2024. It states no funding or milestones for it.

Digital sovereignty

At the June 2026 launch officials said the identity database is hosted in The Gambia, under Gambian law, and owned by the state. The President said the whole system remains Gambian-owned, although Margins ID Group supplied the technology, financing and operational support. All project assets transfer to the government after eight years. In June 2025 the President had promised a unified identity system locally designed, built, operated and managed by government services. Nothing published by September 2026 states who holds the source code and operating skills. Nor does any published text say how the state could exit the contract.

Under a January 2026 memorandum the Bahraini firm CTM360 is to assess the cybersecurity of the regulator and other critical national infrastructure. It is to offer its platform free to all Gambian critical infrastructure operators. Neither its terms nor the Margins contract's terms on data jurisdiction and exit had been published by September 2026.

In August 2026 The Gambia's ambassador in Geneva announced cooperation with China's Guizhou province. It includes a national data laboratory in The Gambia on the Guizhou model.

The National Data Policy validated in July 2025 is aligned with the African Union Data Policy Framework. Stakeholders strengthened its provisions on cross-border data flows.
