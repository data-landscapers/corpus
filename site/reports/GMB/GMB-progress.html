Gambia: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-06  ·  123 systems and instruments tracked
↓ PDF
Governance · Finance · ICT Infrastructure · DPI · Digitalisation · Technology · Capacity · Inclusion · Data · Geopolitics · Where the record is thin

This report asks the same set of questions of every country. The rows below are a fixed frame of indicators, one row each, chosen in advance and covering all thirty-eight subjects the base tracks — so what appears here is decided by the frame and not by whichever records happened to accumulate. Each row says what happened on that indicator during the period, with every claim linked to the source it rests on.

Where a row reads No evidence, the base holds nothing on that indicator. That is a statement about this base, not about the country — it does not mean nothing exists, only that nothing has been collected here yet. The rows that carry evidence say what it is; the rows that do not are left to speak for themselves.

The period is 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

Governance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Strategies, plans and policies Digital transformation strategy The digital economy master plan for 2024 to 2034 is the framing instrument, with no mid-term review or costed implementation plan held.
Full recordA ten-year science, technology and innovation policy for 2027-2035 was validated, after its minister conceded the previous one achieved none of its objectives (validation).

It was adopted for a ten-year period and its position is unchanged (master plan).

No monitoring report, budget allocation against it or delivery milestone appears anywhere in the base, and no domestic budget appropriation for the digital sector is held at any date, so a ten-year plan runs without a measurable financing line behind it.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies ICT strategy The 2018-2028 ICT policy is still inside its stated period and has been overtaken by the 2024-2034 master plan.
Full recordThe eight-pillar policy statement issued in December 2017 was the foundational instrument and runs on paper to 2028.

It was overtaken rather than repealed: the National Digital Economy Master Plan 2024-2034 now carries the sector's direction, leaving two instruments with overlapping periods and no published statement of which governs.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Broadband strategy The 2020-2024 broadband policy and strategy expired at the end of 2024 and no successor was located.
Full recordThe strategy behind the policy was costed action plan by action plan and targeted 5 Mbps for all, 90 per cent coverage and 85 per cent fourth-generation coverage by 2024. The policy itself is held with its capture truncated.

The term has ended and no successor instrument is on the record. What is moving is procurement rather than policy: a US$50m partnership signed in December 2025 is to lift the national backbone from 40 to 800 Gbps.
 Regressed
Strategies, plans and policies Data storage / cloud strategy A cloud strategy runs to 2027; the cloud policy that would give it a data-sovereignty rule is unsigned.
Full recordThe strategy issued in June 2023 finds the Tier-2 government data centre power-constrained and reliant on diesel backup, which is the condition any migration has to work around.

Its companion policy sets a draft data-sovereignty and data-classification rule and has not been signed into effect. Hosting has moved anyway: the national identity data centre inaugurated at Abuko in July 2026 holds the identity system on Gambian soil.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data interoperability framework / roadmap An interoperability framework was proposed in 2020 alongside an ICT agency; the agency exists and the framework does not.
Full recordThe e-government strategy for 2021-2024 proposes both a government interoperability framework and an ICT agency, and records that no interoperability layer existed as at 2020.

The agency was constituted and lists powers over a government data bank, technical standards, electronic signatures and cloud services. None of those powers has produced a published framework, and the register-to-register indicator that would show one working still stands at zero.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies AI strategy The ministry told the United Nations in April 2026 that a national artificial intelligence policy is still in development.
Full recordThe national statement to the science and technology commission is the government's own dated position: the policy is in development, not adopted.

The December 2025 budget speech names an artificial intelligence and emerging technology policy and a digital government roadmap among the 2026 priorities, which puts the drafting inside a stated work programme rather than leaving it as an intention.
 Advanced
Strategies, plans and policies Data localisation policies The only localisation rule is the unsigned cloud policy's draft data-sovereignty and classification provision.
Full recordThe cloud policy sets a draft data-sovereignty rule and a classification scheme for government workloads, and has not been signed. Nothing else in the base states where Gambian government or personal data must be held.

Practice has run ahead of the rule: the identity data centre inaugurated at Abuko in July 2026 hosts the national identity system inside the country, which is localisation by procurement rather than by instrument.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Data governance policy The national data policy has been a published draft since 2025 with no adoption, gazetting or implementing instrument.
Full recordIt was published by the communications ministry as a draft and has not moved (draft policy).

It sits beside an enacted data-protection statute and a validated interoperability architecture, so the policy layer is the one part of the data stack that has stalled rather than the whole of it.
 No change
Strategies, plans and policies Open data policy The 2024-2027 open data strategy records no registry, no legal framework and no portal, with targets set for 2026.
Full recordThe strategy is unusually plain about its own starting point: The Gambia has no data registry, no open-data legal framework and no portal. Its targets fall in 2026.

Nothing in the base records any of the three arriving. The strategy is the instrument this indicator asks for; the portal it provides for is a separate question the ledger carries as its own row.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Data protection legislation The Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act 2025 was enacted after several stalled years, with extraterritorial scope and seven lawful bases.
Full recordIt carries a sensitive-data regime covering genetic, biometric and health data, and rights of access, rectification, erasure and protection from solely automated decisions (review).

Passage was in October 2025 (passage).

No implementing regulation, commencement notice or first enforcement action under it is held, and the commission that would supervise it is not separately constituted on the record.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Cybersecurity legislation The 2009 Act still carries the computer-misuse and cybercrime provisions; its replacement is stuck in committee.
Full recordThe Information and Communications Act, assented on 29 May 2009, holds the computer-misuse, cybercrime and data-processing provisions and remains the operative instrument.

The Communications Bill 2025 that would replace it went to a second reading on 3 March 2026 and was referred to committee, with concerns over surveillance oversight unresolved. The regional convention on cybersecurity was signed in December 2022 and is still unratified.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation covering digital id The digital identity strategy for 2023 to 2028 is in force and now carries a delivered system, with no statute governing it.
Full recordThe strategy was validated at a supported workshop in May 2024 and provided for a system that has since been built (validation, strategy).

What the base does not hold is the legal basis for issuing identity cards outside the country, which the opposition alleged was unlawful in August 2025 for issuance in Mauritania, Congo and Togo; no official response, regulation or registry statement has followed (allegation).
 No change
Legislation and regulation Digital payments legislation The 2011 mobile money regulation remains the rulebook, carrying the interoperability duty the 2025 switch delivers on.
Full recordThe regulation made in October 2011 sets a GMD10m capital floor, a cash-merchant regime and a duty to interoperate. A separate pricing policy effective December 2013 sets cost-recovery tariffs for the settlement system, clearing house, securities settlement and the national switch.

The interoperability duty took fourteen years to deliver: the instant payment switch launched in December 2025 links banks, mobile money and fintechs on a phased rollout.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Legislation enabling data interoperability The 2019 Act gives the ICT agency the powers an exchange would need; none has been exercised in a published instrument.
Full recordThe agency's own statement of its functions under the 2019 Act covers a government data bank, technical standards, electronic signatures and cloud services.

What is missing is any instrument made under them. The Data Protection and Privacy Bill 2024 reached assented stage on the Assembly's own tracker in July 2026, which supplies the safeguard side; the exchange side remains a proposal in the e-government strategy.
 No change
Legislation and regulation AI legislation / regulations No artificial-intelligence instrument exists and the policy is still in drafting as at April 2026.
Full recordThe ministry's statement to the United Nations science and technology commission puts the national policy in development. No bill, regulation or regulator guideline addressing artificial intelligence has been laid.

Where the state has acted on synthetic content it has done so institutionally rather than legally: a misinformation response centre runs a fact-check hotline with a public audit trail, staffed by human analysts.
 No change
Legislation and regulation E-commerce legislation The Communications Bill 2025 passed second reading in March 2026 and covers electronic commerce, signatures and cybercrime in one instrument.
Full recordIt also covers electronic communications and broadcasting, and was referred to National Assembly committees after the second reading on 2 March 2026 (second reading).

The press union is pressing for repeal of the restrictive media laws around it (committee stage).

No enactment date is held, and until it passes the country has no electronic transactions or electronic signature statute at all.
 Advanced
Legislation and regulation Statistics legislation The 2005 Act is unamended, with the African Union peer review urging a modernised law in November 2025.
Full recordThe Statistics Act 2005 is the constitutive statute of the statistics bureau, held here only as an image-only scan with no machine-readable text.

Both reviews of the system call for its revision. The national statistical strategy found more than a third of officials unaware of the Act it says needs revising, and the African Union peer review of November 2025 urged mandate reform and a modernised statistics law. No amending bill has been laid.
 No change
Legislation and regulation Access to information legislation The Act is in force but most public institutions have yet to appoint the information officers it requires, and requests go unanswered.
Full recordTwenty-one-day statutory requests are going unanswered, on the press union's account of 7 August 2026 (union account).

No government response, compliance count or enforcement action is on record, so a statute that exists is being reported as unimplemented by the constituency that uses it, and by nobody else.
 No change
Data protection Data protection authority The Information Commission carries both the access and the data-protection statutes, and is receiving donor support to raise awareness of them.
Full recordThe support is funded under a peacebuilding project to build a communication strategy raising public awareness of the two Acts (support).

No budget, staffing, caseload or enforcement record for the commission is held, and awareness-raising support is not evidence of enforcement capacity. It is one body carrying two statutes, one of them enacted only in 2025.
 Advanced
Data protection National data protection readiness A State Intelligence Service Bill validated in July 2026 is reported to require judicial warrants and oversight for intrusive measures.
Full recordIt would also give the National Assembly review of the Service's budgets and operations; it has not been tabled (validation).

The text is not held and the Bill does not appear in the Assembly's bills library, so the provisions are as reported from a workshop. It is the only instrument in the base that would put a limit on state access to personal data, and it exists as an account of a draft.
 Advanced
Regional collaboration Regional policy collaboration The revenue authority convened the continental tax forum's master class at Banjul in July 2026.
Full recordThe forum's executive secretary argued there that digital systems deliver only alongside governance reform, and pressed for a united African position in the United Nations tax convention negotiations (master class, proceedings).

It is the country's only convening role in the base, and the argument made at it, that digitisation without governance reform does not raise revenue, is the sharpest external check on the tax-digitisation claims recorded elsewhere on this ledger.

The bilateral digital-cooperation memorandum signed at Dakar on 12 June 2026 held its first steering-committee session at Banjul on 29 August 2026, settling four priorities — infrastructure and interoperability, cyber resilience, capacity building and regulatory harmonisation — with rural electronic-government access named among the work (steering committee).

No timetable, budget or named deliverable is attached to any of the four priorities, so what the committee has agreed is a list of headings.
 Advanced
Regional collaboration Regional legal harmonisation The regional cybersecurity and data protection convention was signed in December 2022 and remains unratified.
Full recordThe ministry's own record of signature on 2 December 2022 states that ratification is still owed, and no instrument of ratification has been published in the four years since.

Harmonisation has moved faster in operations than in law: the regulator and its Senegalese counterpart signed a memorandum in December 2025 to implement the regional roaming regulation, with caps and thirty days of free incoming calls from 1 March 2026, and the Information Commission gained full voting membership of the African data protection network in June 2026.
 No change
Regional collaboration Shared regional infrastructure A regulatory cooperation and free-roaming agreement with Senegal was signed in June 2026, adding to a three-country roaming memorandum of 2025.
Full recordThe Senegal agreement covers telecommunications regulatory cooperation and free roaming, with no commencement date or tariff published (agreement).

A roaming memorandum with Liberia and Sierra Leone signed in February 2025 is unchanged, with no tariff, traffic or commencement figure published (memorandum).

Two roaming instruments in eighteen months and no traffic or price figure for either is the position: the agreements are signed and their effect is unmeasured.
 Advanced
Regional collaboration Cross-border data transfers A three-month customs roadmap with Senegal was agreed in July 2026 to exchange customs data by interface across three border posts.
Full recordThe roadmap installs a shared customs system at Karang, Keur Ayib and Selety within three months, deploys transit tracking, exchanges customs data by interface once the system is live, replaces transit agents with a single administrative document and reactivates an intelligence platform (roadmap).

It is the only cross-border data-sharing arrangement in the base, and no data-protection provision, retention rule or custodian is stated for the customs data it would exchange.
 Advanced
Standards National interoperability standards A national enterprise architecture was validated in May 2026 as the interoperability blueprint, and is not adopted, funded or implemented.
Full recordIt was validated under an international open-standards initiative (validation).

A blueprint validated and not adopted is the same position the data policy is in, and both sit behind an identity system and a data centre that are already running, so the standards layer is arriving after the systems it would govern.
 Advanced
Standards National quality standards The standards bureau reports around 500 standards adopted, on a catalogue thin in information technology.
Full recordThe 2022 catalogue sets out the national register by classification, and the technology classes in it are thin. The bureau's director general put adoptions at around 500 standards when the international standards body's secretary general visited in July 2026, alongside membership of the African standards organisation.

No count of information-technology standards specifically is published, and no national conformity-assessment output is on the record.
 No change
Standards Adoption of international standards The bureau holds Correspondent, non-voting status at the international standards bodies and is the trade-barriers enquiry point.
Full recordThe international standards body's own member profile records the bureau as a Correspondent member without a vote at both the standards and electrotechnical organisations, and as the country's technical-barriers enquiry point under the trade agreement.

That is the ceiling on formal adoption: a correspondent member can adopt standards but cannot participate in making them. In telecommunications the adoption that has happened is regional rather than international, through the roaming regulation implementation memorandum of December 2025.
 No change
Public debate and participation in policymaking Non-governmental contribution to national policy The regulator opened eight draft instruments to public comment in April 2026 under published consultation guidelines.
Full recordThe consultation notice and guidelines of 2 April 2026 put eight draft regulatory instruments out for comment at once, among them enforcement regulations and a unified fee code covering every regulated sector.

The practice predates the guidelines. Stakeholders validated ex-ante wholesale and retail telecom pricing tools in November 2025, a draft e-commerce policy was validated with private-sector participants in September 2025, and the national policy handbook mandates participatory, evidence-based policymaking.
 Advanced
Public debate and participation in policymaking Open discussion of government policy Draft broadcasting and online content regulations went out for consultation in April 2026, one of seven instruments released together.
Full recordThe draft is made under section 226(1) of the Information and Communications Act in consultation with the regulator; its fourteen parts run from general obligations through a content complaints committee, licensing and professional standards, classification tiers, content monitoring, local content, public service broadcasting, watershed and children's programming, and enforcement (draft regulations).

The information minister set out its child-protection provisions in August 2026, covering the identity of child victims, parental consent for interviews with children, and content that harms or exploits minors (provisions).

Its execution block is unsigned and undated, and no consultation closing date or adoption date is published. The seven instruments include journalists registration guidelines, which is the part the press constituency is contesting.

Finance

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Domestic budget appropriations and expenditure Sustainable domestic financing of digital transformation Tax collection rose to D25bn in 2025 from D11bn in 2022, which the finance minister attributes to digitalisation of collection.
Full recordThe tax-to-gross-domestic-product ratio rose from 9 per cent in 2022 to 13 per cent in 2025, stated by the minister at the Banjul master class (ratio).

The authority's 2026 target is D27.5bn (target).

The attribution to digitalisation rather than to rate increases is the minister's own and is an attribution, not an evaluation; no counterfactual, rate schedule or compliance analysis is published beside it. No domestic appropriation for the digital sector is held at any date.

Full recordThe state incumbent's own account puts the partnership at US$50m signed on 24 December 2025, with the backbone to rise from 40 to 800 Gbps and no completion date given.

The rest are frameworks without figures: a digital public infrastructure memorandum with India signed in October 2024 with no funding or milestones; a Bahraini cybersecurity memorandum of January 2026 offering a free critical-infrastructure tier with no value or duration; and a mathematics and artificial intelligence memorandum with a regional institute signed on 10 December 2025 with no site or funding yet.

The announced winning bid was D6.7bn and the signed commitment GMD 6.1bn, and the base does not reconcile the two (bid).

Separately a US$50m partnership was signed in January 2026 to take the national backbone from 40 Gbps to 800 Gbps, with no completion date, milestone schedule or local-currency figure published (partnership).

DPI

Topic Indicator Developments Progress
Data Exchange National data exchange system The register-to-register indicator stands at zero with one link under verification, seven years after the design roadmap.
Full recordThe design roadmap for the social registry was written in 2019, before a national identity system existed to link it to.

The implementation report of November 2025 puts interoperability between the social registry, the education system, civil registration and health insurance at zero, with one link being verified. The enterprise architecture that would govern such links was proposed in 2020 and no framework has been published.
 Stalled
Data Exchange Use of digital id in other systems The national identity card went live in July 2026; the civil register link was designed for three years earlier.
Full recordThe project paper of June 2023 sets out interoperability between electronic civil registration and the national identity system alongside a mass-registration drive covering 1.17m people, so the design predates the credential.

The credential itself arrived in July 2026, with the President the first recipient and a national identity data centre inaugurated at Abuko hosting it on Gambian soil. The bidding for the system opened in July 2024 on a design-build-transfer basis. No system is yet recorded consuming the identity for authentication.
 Advanced
Data Exchange Interoperability of health systems Health insurance is one of the four registers whose interoperability indicator stands at zero.
Full recordThe implementation report names health insurance among the systems the social registry is to connect to, and puts the count of working links at zero with one under verification.

What has moved is surveillance capacity rather than data exchange: a workshop in December 2025 trained more than twenty staff on outbreak signal detection and reporting, and an earlier grant funded 140 desktops and civil registration devices — procurement, not systems in production.
 No change
Data Exchange Interoperability of education systems The education tracker carries a learner identifier for 300,000-plus pupils; its link to other registers is still zero.
Full recordThe education ministry has run its system on the health-platform stack since 2018, tracking more than 300,000 students against a learner identifier introduced in 2020, with text-message attendance reporting for offline schools. The country use case records what it replaced: a fragmented, paper-based system.

A learner identifier is the precondition for linking education to civil registration and social protection. That link has not been made: the interoperability indicator across those registers stands at zero.
 Advanced
Data Exchange Interoperability of social protection systems The social registry was closed out highly satisfactory in 2024 and still connects to nothing.
Full recordThe project that built the registry closed in June 2024 rated Highly Satisfactory, with US$29.8m of US$30m disbursed.

Its connections were left to a successor operation, whose November 2025 report puts them at zero with one link under verification. The 2019 design roadmap had anticipated the problem: it was written before a national identity existed to link the registry to.

Digital Identity and CRVS Robustness of system The identity system went live in July 2026 on a data centre inside the country, two years after bidding opened.
Full recordBidding for the national identification documents and digital identity systems opened in July 2024 on a design-build-transfer basis and closed that August.

The first card was issued in July 2026 with a national identity data centre inaugurated at Abuko, hosted on Gambian soil. No enrolment figure, uptime statement or independent assessment of the system has been published.
 Advanced
Digital Identity and CRVS Registration of entire population A national identity system went live in July 2026 and nationwide rollout of ten-year biometric cards began on 7 August at six centres.
Full recordField reporting from biometric identity enrolment centres in the Greater Banjul Area found high demand meeting daily processing caps (reporting). The base holds no enrolment total.

The system issues instant cards from the national data centre, and the cards are produced with a Ghana-based supplier; no enrolment or issuance figure has been published since launch (rollout, launch).

It runs under an eight-year design-build-finance-operate-transfer contract signed in January 2026, with a 70/30 revenue share, ten-year card validity, free replacement of valid cards and a second-phase data centre; no contract value is published in anything held (contract, terms).

The arrangement it replaced was a foreign concession the government had resolved to end, so the country moved from a concession to a build-operate-transfer contract rather than to direct state issuance.
 Advanced
Digital Identity and CRVS National maintenance of id and credentials systems The system was procured design-build-transfer from a foreign supplier, and now runs from a data centre in-country.
