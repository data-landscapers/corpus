This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

On 14 October 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a National Digital Policy, revising the ICT development policy for 2011-2025. It adopted a national cybersecurity strategy at the same sitting and asked for operational action plans to track both. On 3 July 2026 the Prime Minister instructed every ministry to write its own implementation document for the Vision « Burundi émergent en 2040 et développé en 2060 ». Digitalisation and e-government are among its first priority programmes. The standing digital instrument is the Plan Directeur de Digitalisation des Services Publics 2023-2033, adopted by the Council of Ministers on 10 May 2023. It covers broadband, cybersecurity, data centres and cloud, data protection, digital payments, digital signatures, quality of service and universal access. The policy the new one replaces, the Politique Nationale de Développement des TIC, was adopted on 13 July 2011 for the period 2010-2025.

In November 2025 Burundi validated a National Data Governance Strategy with support from the UN Economic Commission for Africa. It rests on seven pillars, including data management, quality and interoperability, and its three-year action plan is still to be produced. The national validation session for an artificial-intelligence strategy opened on 21 April 2026. The Ministry of Finance, Budget and Digital Economy ran it with UNDP. Stakeholders validated the broadband strategy Burundi Large Bande 2025 on 6 December 2017, and it was still awaiting government adoption in early 2018. The National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2015-2020, whose period has ended, prioritised the rural population, women, youth and micro and small entrepreneurs.

In March 2026 PAFEN, the World Bank-backed digital-economy foundations project, went through a formal mid-term review.

Legislation and regulation

On 22 October 2025 the Council of Ministers adopted Burundi's first bill on personal data, recording that Burundian positive law then had no legal regime for processing personal data. The National Assembly adopted it unanimously on 15 January 2026. Loi n°1/03 portant protection des données à caractère personnel was promulgated on 10 March 2026.

Until then the digital statutes were the 2022 cybercrime statute and the 2024 electronic communications and postal code. On 29 May 2026 the telecommunications regulator ARCT published national e-commerce guidelines under that code. On 26 June 2026 it called on e-commerce platforms and merchant sites to make the compulsory prior declaration of conformity.

Payments rest on Loi n°1/07 du 11 mai 2018 portant système national de paiement. Central bank Règlement n°002/2024 of 30 July 2024 sets three classes of payment institution and requires customer funds to be held in trust accounts. The central bank's oversight framework of 20 November 2025 sets the licensing and sanction regime for payment service providers. For public-sector data the closest enabling text is Décret n°100/085 du 25 juillet 2018. It defines administrative data and provides for electronic access, with rules on confidentiality and security. Loi n°1/08 du 20 mai 2021 organises the statistical system. Loi n°1/18 du 24 juillet 2023 amended it, setting out the national statistical authority and the principle of professional independence.

International connectivity is governed by Décret n°100/166 du 26 mai 2015, a single-gateway regime for access to international telecommunications networks. An infrastructure-sharing law was still in draft in August 2026. Two ordinances adopted in 2026 govern quality of service and SIM cards. On 26 August 2026 the communications ministry convened creators, legal experts and jurists to prepare a first bill on social-media influencers and content creators. A press law promulgated in July 2024 replaced prison with fines for press offences including insult, contempt and false news. On 2 October 2026 the regulator put SIM registration under the 2026 ordinance at about 70% complete. The Senate adopted the access to information bill unanimously on 29 September 2026. No record of its promulgation has been found.

Data protection

The data-protection law of 10 March 2026 regulates collection and use by public and private bodies, sets out privacy rights and creates an independent supervisory authority. It attaches criminal penalties. It places the new authority under the Ministry of Digital Economy. It restricts transfers of personal and sensitive data to jurisdictions without adequate protection or approved safeguards. The authority's detailed operating mandate awaits an implementing decree. In April 2026 the authority was not operating and no adequacy list had been published. Penalties are criminal only, with no administrative fines, and there is no general requirement to store data in the country.

Before 2026 confidentiality rested on scattered sectoral provisions: banking secrecy under the 2017 Banking Act, healthcare confidentiality rules, telecommunications privacy regulations and the 2022 cybercrime law. None of them imposed a duty to notify users, regulators or an authority after a breach.

In January 2026 lawmakers tied the reform to the digitisation of the voter register, computerised public administration and preparations for the biometric national identity card. Officials present the law as removing a key obstacle to deploying the biometric card. The interior ministry describes the order of work as legislation on personal data first, then the software platform.

On 29 May 2026 the telecoms regulator ARCT issued injunctions to the mobile operator Lumitel. They require subscription to value-added services to be voluntary and preceded by notice to the subscriber. In July 2026 the consumer association ABUCO reported no measurable improvement in unconsented enrolment. Lumitel says no such service is activated without a subscriber's deliberate action.

Regional collaboration

By July 2026 East African Community rules on cross-border data flows and personal data protection had been approved. They must still pass the bloc's decision-making organs before any partner state adopts them. At the EAC Digital for Development Forum in June 2026 Burundi called for harmonised data-exchange frameworks for cross-border public services. The same week, the regional Secure Data Sharing Framework went to a validation workshop in Dar es Salaam.

At meetings on 25-29 May 2026 the EAC took a regional mobile roaming framework from the agenda into development. EARDIP, which supplies digital infrastructure and regulatory frameworks, supports the regional cross-border payments work. In July 2026 the EAC held its target for a single currency at 2031. The same committee recorded that no partner state has met all four primary macroeconomic convergence criteria. It agreed peer review for macroeconomic surveillance, with operational frameworks for the 2026/27-2030/31 development strategy.

In 2023 Burundi, a member of COMESA, the East African Community and ECCAS, scored 61.8 out of 100 for Regional Integration, seventh of 54 African states. It was the country's best-ranked measure, in a business environment ranked 43rd. From 16 to 21 February 2026 Burundi hosted the Central Africa Internet Governance Forum for the first time, with eleven countries taking part. From 9 to 12 November 2026 Burundi and the COMESA secretariat met in Bujumbura to prepare its connection to the regional electronic certificate of origin platform.

In November 2019 the national research and education network, BERNET, was connected to the regional UbuntuNet network over a 500 Mbps link from Kigali. Its capacity uptake rose from 0.13 to 0.4 Gbps in 2023. In October 2024 Burundi and Zambia signed a memorandum for a cable under Lake Tanganyika to Makamba. In July 2026 Burundi named postal modernisation and internet infrastructure as its priorities with Vietnam. Vietnam asked in return for a wider role for Viettel's local operator Lumitel. On Burundi's side the channel runs through the secretary general of the ruling CNDD-FDD party.

Standards

In October 2026 BBN was the country's ISO member body, participating in 11 technical committees and observing 28. In May 2026 BBN was an observing member of JTC 1, the joint ISO and IEC committee on information technology. As at June 2023 Burundi had taken up 628 of the 690 harmonised EAC standards, 91 per cent and the highest rate of the six partner states. On 2 February 2022 the Council of Ministers adopted a National Quality Policy. It sets the objectives, roadmap and timetable for the national quality-infrastructure system. On 29 August 2025 the standards body BBN signed a protocol with the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Office Congolais de Contrôle. It covers standardisation, metrology, conformity assessment and progressive mutual recognition of certificates.

Sixty-two of the 690 harmonized East African Community standards remained unadopted as at June 2023. Beyond the quality policy, those EAC adoptions and the payment-messaging migration, the country's standards work since 2022 sits at validation, workshop or self-assessment stage rather than in force.

On 5 February 2026 the central bank's Automated Transfer System completed its migration to the ISO 20022 messaging standard. Interbank operations resumed on the new standard the next day, two months before the instant payment system launched. ARCT's e-commerce guidelines of May 2026 name interoperability, transparency, transaction security and online consumer protection as their standards.

On 17 December 2024 SETIC presented the final Concept type des Systèmes d'Information des institutions publiques for validation. It is a cross-government technical standard that permits the adoption of international ICT standards. No act adopting it has been found. A workshop on 7-10 April 2026 worked on common technical standards for exchanging health data, HL7 FHIR among them. On 9 June 2026 the education ministry opened a three-day self-assessment of its management information system against the African Union's continental EMIS norms.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

On 29 September 2026 the Senate unanimously adopted a law on access to public information. It creates a National Commission on Access to Public Information to hear appeals, and a duty to publish certain information within 30 days. Its exceptions cover national security, private life and ongoing judicial investigations, and it sets no statutory ceiling on access fees. The Council of Ministers had adopted the draft on 10 June 2026. No record of its promulgation has been found. In 2023 Burundi was among the weakest states in Africa for the disclosure of public records and for citizens' ability to obtain them. Its media-freedom and free-expression scores each fell by about 12 points over the decade to 2023.

On 27 March 2025 the media regulator suspended the online blogger coalition Yaga Burundi as unregistered. On 14 September 2026 it suspended a Yaga Burundi programme for six months over an interview with a critical opposition politician. The journalist Sandra Muhoza was convicted in December 2024 over comments in a journalists' WhatsApp group. On 13 April 2026 the Ngozi appeal court sentenced her to six months for racial aversion, already served, and she was freed. Burundi fell 17 places to 125th of 180 in the 2025 press freedom index.

The 2014 communal administration law requires plenary sessions of the communal council to be public. The council must hold meetings at least twice a year open to hill councils and local associations. A multi-stakeholder consultation on 18 April 2025 preceded the November validation of the National Data Governance Strategy.

In July 2026 the consumer association ABUCO proposed états généraux de la communication on telecoms. They would bring authorities, operators, regulators and consumer organisations together to find the root causes of service failure. Its president said repeated appeals to the operators by the head of state and ministers had had no observable effect. In October 2026 the presidency adopted the locally built messaging application Gasape Chat as a channel for citizens' grievances.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2026/27 budget law gives BIF 27.1 billion from national resources to a new digitalisation directorate, BIF 17.4 billion of it for digitalising public services (2026/27). It gives BIF 12.0 billion to the state ICT agency SETIC, BIF 10.1 billion of that for equipment (2026/27). SETIC had BIF 1.8 billion a year earlier (2025/26).

National resources carry 28.6% of the digital lines of the 2026/27 budget law at the appropriation stage, BIF 78.1 billion of BIF 273.4 billion, up from 24.1% in 2025/26 and 26.2% in 2024/25; the rest is donor project money drawn through the budget (2026/27).

The donor lines are BIF 105.4 billion for the World Bank-financed digital foundations project and BIF 89.8 billion for the East African Community regional statistics project (2026/27). The digital foundations project runs to 2028. Its state counterpart line was BIF 189.3 million in 2025/26 (2025/26). The finance ministry reports execution of the 2025/26 budget in-year, through its third-quarter return (July 2026).

The universal service fund's revised 2026/27 procurement plan spends BIF 11.4 billion of its own resources on internet transit for public health facilities, secondary schools and community telecentres (2026/27). Its 2025/26 plan carried BIF 4.8 billion for three pilot digital villages (2025/26). The 2025/26 budget law put BIF 2.7 billion into a capital increase in the Burundi Backbone System, and the 2026/27 law the same again.

The public-services digitalisation master plan prices its programme at USD 111.9 million over 2023-2033. It provides for an e-government fund drawn from the national budget and development partners, to be governed by its own text.

MoUs and other agreements

On 6 February 2026 the United States signed a five-year health-sector memorandum with Burundi committing US$156m, health-data management included. Its data-systems section plans US$1,885,264 of United States funding for six named health data systems in 2026. The sum falls each year to US$475,264 in 2030, subject to the availability of funds. The memorandum states that it is not an international agreement and gives rise to no legal rights or obligations.

In February 2025 Burundi opened talks with the International Finance Corporation on public-private partnership routes for its digital transformation agenda. In July 2026 Burundi and Vietnam took up digital cooperation again. Burundi put postal modernisation and internet infrastructure on its side, and Vietnam a wider role for Viettel's local operator Lumitel. In October 2026 the finance minister held talks in Seoul with South Korea's science and ICT ministry on digital identity, data centres, secure hosting of public data and linking government agencies. They explored financing through the Korea International Cooperation Agency and the Economic Development Cooperation Fund. No agreement, amount or timetable has been found. On 3 July 2026 the Chinese company IBI Crossborder announced a comprehensive mining cooperation agreement with the Burundi Mining Authority, presented as a chapter of the Digital Silk Road.

New investments

The World Bank's Digital Foundations Project, known in Burundi as PAFEN, is a USD 50 million IDA commitment running from 2022 to 2028. A further USD 42 million of additional financing, approved in January 2024, put the emphasis on public financial management. Huawei financed a USD 30 million recovery plan for the state operator ONATEL, adopted by the Council of Ministers in November 2017. Its repayment was drawn from the revenues of ONATEL's mobile arm, which proved insufficient (2020).

Burundi's digital estate has drawn 50 external commitments, worth about USD 187 million on windows running from 2015 to 2030 (November 2026). Of these, four are active, one approved, two in the pipeline, three completed and 40 carry no recorded status. Six from the World Bank come to USD 127 million and the Huawei credit to ONATEL to USD 30 million (November 2026). Lumitel's share of the rural 4G contract and United States money for health data systems, still in the pipeline, are about USD 6 million each (November 2026). The other 41 are grants from bilateral, United Nations and foundation donors, together about USD 18 million (November 2026). A German development grant reaches the country only through a regional East African Community programme (October 2026).

Connectivity takes the largest share of the digital money committed to Burundi, ahead of government technology and public financial management, with national statistics third, sectoral management information systems fourth and digital payments fifth.

The money arrives mostly as World Bank financing, with one vendor credit to the state operator, one commitment structured as a public-private partnership, one United States grant still in the pipeline and 41 smaller donor grants.

In March 2026 an integrated public financial management system, SIGFP_BI, was out to tender under the IDA grants that finance PAFEN. The World Bank's USD 150 million Cash for Jobs Project, approved in December 2021, carries a digital-payments component. In August 2026 Germany's BMZ gave EUR 5 million to the East African Community for a regional artificial-intelligence and digital-skills network.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Lumitel, Viettel's Burundian operator, launched 5G on 19 December 2025 and had installed 80 sites across the seven former provinces by February 2026. On the regulator's coverage figures of February 2026, 2G reaches 97% of the population, 3G around half and 4G around a third.

About 11.3% of the population were internet users in January 2024. Penetration was still about 12.5% in 2025. In December 2024 ARCT stated that fibre covers the national territory: 1,750km on the BBS backbone, 350km on ONATEL's Bujumbura network and 3,400km operated by Viettel. In 2019 Burundian ISPs were using about 2.3 Gb of the 7.1 Gb of international bandwidth they had bought.

In 2020 landlocked Burundi reached submarine capacity on the EASSy, SEACOM and TEAMS systems through neighbouring countries' landings. It has regional fibre connection points into Rwanda, the DRC and Tanzania. On 8 May 2024 the Council of Ministers adopted a memorandum on a further fibre link with the DRC. A memorandum signed with Zambia in October 2024 provides for a cable under Lake Tanganyika from Zambia's Northern Province to Makamba. In September 2026 Tanzania's state operator and the Burundi Backbone System agreed to raise capacity on the Tanzanian route from 4Gbps to 10Gbps, citing demand for cross-border digital services. No capacity figure has been published for the Rwandan or Congolese routes. In 2026 the internet exchange point BDIXP had nine members.

In August 2026 ARCT accepted that internet quality is at times disrupted. It named power cuts, generator fuel, unstable supply from the utility REGIDESO, urban bandwidth saturation, ageing equipment and missing masts. In 2025 outages were driven by electricity, fuel and foreign-exchange shortages.

Under PAFEN, Lumitel holds a USD 10 million contract to extend 4G in rural areas, reaching 86 collines (March 2026). In April 2026 the state operator ONATEL won USD 5.9 million to cover 92 more. In October 2025 an ITU-backed national broadband-mapping project was launched.

Data Storage

At its mid-term review in March 2026 the World Bank-financed Digital Foundations project named a national data centre a priority. In May 2022 government hosting rested on a single small network operations centre and data centre run by the state ICT agency SETIC. In 2020 there was no shared government facility: the revenue authority, the central bank and the Presidency each ran their own.

In 2025 Bujumbura held small-scale colocation sites: Lacell SU's Smart Burundi and the Burundi Backbone System. No hyperscale or major regional colocation operator was present, and there were no direct cloud on-ramps to AWS, Google Cloud or Microsoft Azure. Traffic is typically routed via Nairobi. BBS hosts a national data centre that other public agencies use to back up their systems.

In November 2025 the Bank of the Republic of Burundi put maintenance of parts of its separate alternative site at Rumonge out to open international tender, on its own funds. In February 2026 the Agence de Développement du Burundi tendered for racks, voltage stabilisers and projectors for its own facility. In April 2026 PAFEN re-issued a tender for virtualisation and backup equipment and licences for SETIC, which hosts the national digital platforms.

In April 2026 the national AI strategy built its cloud and data-hub pillar on the same SETIC facility. The 2026 data protection law restricts transfers of personal data to jurisdictions without adequate protection or approved safeguards. Its administration waits on an implementing decree.

Energy

On 16 June 2026 Burundi commissioned the Mulembwe hydropower dam, adding 17MW and completing the 49.5MW Jiji-Mulembwe complex. The complex cost USD 320 million, with African Development Bank, World Bank and European Union finance (June 2026). Total installed capacity was 204.882 MW in September 2025. In August 2025 a multi-day blackout hit Bujumbura and several provinces while a new substation was connected to the grid. On 5 November 2026 Econet Wireless Burundi began a USD 6.5 million programme to move its mobile network to solar power, starting with a third of its sites by July 2027.

In September 2025 the national energy compact put electricity access at 25.9 per cent: 8 points from grid connections and 17.9 from decentralised supply. It commits to 70 per cent by 2030 and 300 mini-grids, at a stated financing requirement of USD 3.49 billion. In 2023 access was 65.0 per cent in urban areas and 2.3 per cent in rural areas.

More than two-thirds of the access that exists is off-grid, and reaching the 2030 target means a rise of 44.1 percentage points from the September 2025 baseline — close to tripling access in around five years.

REGIDESO, the national utility, and ABER, the rural electrification agency, share delivery (September 2025). In December 2023 the Senate passed an electricity bill that would open rural mini-grid development to private investors (April 2024). On 17 June 2026 REGIDESO signed the implementation contract for component 1 of ASCENT Burundi, reinforcing the 30kV network nationwide. The Bujumbura urban network, a private distribution pilot and support to the ministry and the regulator AREEN are still to come. In its 2025-26 year ABER electrified ten rural centres, with works continuing at seven more. On 14 October 2026 the Council of Ministers approved a public-private partnership with Weza Power for Burunga and Buhumuza provinces. Weza Power would extend, rehabilitate, maintain and operate the low- and medium-voltage network there, with the exclusive sale of electricity. The contract is still to be signed.

Technical Capacity

In September 2025 the meteorological agency IGEBU blamed a months-long outage of its automatic weather-station network on expired SIM cards, unpaid mobile data plans and lack of basic maintenance.

In May 2022 the communications ministry MINCOTIM, the state ICT agency SETIC and the regulator ARCT were each assessed as needing technical assistance to carry out their mandates. The government network COMGOV then connected around 55 public institutions at 2.5 Mbps. There was no national data-exchange layer joining government systems.

In March 2023 the interior ministry said travel-document production had been entirely national since the Contec Global contract lapsed. A new, cheaper supplier was contracted for production inputs. In December 2023 civil registry agents were to be trained to run new biometric identity equipment as the rollout extended beyond 11 pilot communes. In 2025 the public software developer community was a few low-activity groups in Bujumbura. In February 2026 one domestic producer was shipping an AI product built in the country.

In June 2026 an AfDB-financed project tendered for equipment for the Rusi polytechnic centre and ten rural digital learning centres. PAFEN carries digital-skills training for the civil servants who manage its shared infrastructure. In October 2026 SETIC listed software development for public institutions, a network operations centre, hosting of national platforms and network maintenance among its services. A court of accounts audit debated in January 2024 found the SIGEFI link at the infrastructure ministry not working. Its staff travelled to the finance ministry to use it. In August 2026 the interior ministry named thin infrastructure and technical capacity among the remaining constraints on civil-registry digitisation.

Cybersecurity

On 14 October 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a national cybersecurity strategy. It provides for a legal and institutional framework, protection of critical infrastructure and of public and private systems and data, and a national incident-response team. An ITU project of 2013 to 2022 procured the team's equipment in 2017 and renovated its premises in 2019. It closed with the team not fully operational.

In September 2026 the Bank of the Republic of Burundi confirmed that €814,957.821 of its funds was stolen in June 2024. The attackers had infiltrated the computer system of a partner bank in Poland. The money had not been recovered and no suspect had been publicly identified. The bank said it would reinforce its security and prevention mechanisms. In 2024 Burundi sat in the lowest tier of the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index.

The legal layer is a stand-alone cybercrime statute, Loi n°1/10 of 16 March 2022, recorded as in force on the government legal database. The 2024 electronic communications and postal code, Loi n°1/22 of 22 August 2024, sits beside it, with the personal data protection law, Loi n°1/03, promulgated on 10 March 2026. Before that law, confidentiality rested on scattered sectoral provisions, none of which imposed a duty to notify anyone after a breach.

Cybersecurity is a strand of the World Bank's Digital Foundations project, which runs to 2028. It is named as a central focus of the e-KORI tax system's phased design. In August 2025 a border management system at Melchior Ndadaye international airport read and verified passports with a direct link to Interpol, and its extension to other posts was announced. In July 2026 East African Community central bank governors said cybersecurity risk to the financial system needed stronger common measures.

DPI

Data Exchange

In trade administration Burundian state systems pass data to each other automatically: the revenue authority's web-based Guichet Unique électronique was launched in February 2026. It connects the OBR, the pharmaceuticals and food regulator ABREMA and the ASYCUDA World customs platform. Importers obtain permits and pay fees online while the agencies share the data. Cargo has been tracked electronically from the Burundian border since 2014. On 22 September 2026 the revenue office began work with Tanzania's revenue authority on interfacing the two tracking systems. Goods could then be followed from Dar es Salaam or Mombasa. From 4 August 2026 commercial applicants for a tax number are routed through the Agence de Développement du Burundi's easybusiness.bi portal. The portal passes their data to e-KORI automatically.

A March 2026 mid-term evaluation of the World Bank-financed PAFEN project recorded 61% of the budget engaged. Its priorities were a national data centre, the identity rollout and public finance digitisation. The platform PAFEN is meant to supply remains under development. No operational, API-based exchange connects the identity system to other government systems. Registering for a tax identification number requires a copy of the applicant's national identity card. There is no database-level linkage between the tax register and the identity system. A 2024 tax administration assessment found taxpayer registration not integrated with the filing and payment modules alongside it.

In health, automated workflows on a ministry-hosted server were in place by June 2026. They carried HIV viral-load requests and results between the treatment record and the laboratory system, and indicators into the national reporting platform. A cross-sector link is still a pilot: since October 2023, 52 health facilities in Kirundo province have registered births and issued birth certificates at the point of vaccination. They issue around 600 a month (January 2025).

The enabling instrument is a statistical one: Décret n°100/085 of 25 July 2018 defines administrative data. It brings that data into the national dissemination regime and provides for electronic access to it.

Digital Identity and CRVS

On 6 May 2026 the interior ministry presented the digital civil-registration and biometric identity platform, declaring itself technically ready to produce cards. Production awaits adoption of the law governing their use. In December 2023 the plan was to extend biometric registration from 11 pilot communes to the whole country before the 2025 elections. Thirteen communes were running biometric civil registration by August 2026.

A 2018 World Bank estimate put roughly 27% of the population unregistered. The national identity card is a paper credential, mandatory from age 16, with no unique number and no central electronic database behind it. In May 2024 the electoral commission confirmed that the biometric card would not be ready for the 2025 voter enrolment. It fell back on the existing card, which it called easily falsifiable.

An October 2024 assessment records no digital authentication, no login to government portals and no way for a person to see the data kept on them. It records no know-your-customer validation for banks and no dedicated digital identity regulation, the card's legal basis resting on civil registration law. The government's portal states that further legal texts remain to be adopted to institute the national identification file.

On 18 July 2026 Burundi told the African Registrars General that manual civil registration persists and there is no single national civil-status database. Civil registration is not yet interoperable with the future digital identity system. A joint ministerial order of 10 June 2024, launched nationally that October, brings birth registration into health facilities. A pilot in Kirundo had raised registration there from 8% to 45% between March 2023 and March 2024.

The finance ministry's 2026-27 procurement plan carries a contract to set up a national digital identity system at BIF 8,902,999,980, by open international tender on the state budget. The refugee office ONPRA enrols asylum claimants in the BIMS fingerprint and iris system and issues refugee identity cards.

Digital Payments and Fintech

On 23 April 2026 the central bank launched BurundiPay, the national instant payment system, making banks, microfinance institutions and mobile money operators interoperable. Burundi became the 22nd African state with a live instant payment system. In June 2026 the government named Lumicash, Ecocash, E-noti and Cashtel as the mobile money services it connects. On 4 August 2026, 17 of the 78 financial institutions licensed to take part had put the system into service for customers. They were five commercial banks, ten microfinance institutions and two payment institutions. The system had about 224,000 users and had carried about BIF 4.29 billion in total (August 2026).

The system arrived five months after the central bank's own deadline: full interoperability was promised for November 2025 and delivered in April 2026.

Modernisation of the national switch, BI-Switch, stalled after the central bank nationalised it in 2024. Access to banking services scored 3.1 out of 100 in 2023, 50th of 54 African states and almost unchanged over the decade before.

In April 2025 there was no centralised identity database that financial institutions could use for know-your-customer checks, and no online or e-KYC in the regulatory framework. Bank and mobile money accounts can be opened with the national identity card. In November 2024 the central bank signed a contract for a biometric financial identity, enrolled by each bank and microfinance institution and kept centrally at the bank. It is built not to depend on the civil or population registers, which the bank describes as neither digitised nor biometric. Implementation was scheduled from October 2025.

In August 2026 the central bank had acceded to the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, with four Burundian banks authorised to take part. On 8 October 2026 the East African Legislative Assembly's accounts committee reported parts of the payment-systems integration project unfinished when its grant closed in June. It recommended an audit. The central bank said it still expects to join the East African Payment System by December.

Registries

On 14 July 2026 RPPCUB-2026, a digitised and geo-referenced census and register of urban property owners, was officially launched. Its first phase covers Muha, Mukaza, Ntahangwa, Gitega, Ngozi and Rumonge, on a budget of around BIF 3 billion (July 2026). A joint ministerial ordinance of 6 February 2026 sets the terms for acquiring the secured electronic land title. The 2026/27 budget law extended the application deadline to 31 March 2027. In June 2025 the land titles and cadastre directorate launched an electronic document and records management system. It is integrated with the building permit and property management systems.

By June 2026 the Registre Social Unique under Merankabandi II had enrolled 249,671 households from targeted areas, against a final target of 290,000. The programme covers all 3,044 collines and quartiers.

In June 2026 the development agency extended the deadline for re-registering persons and companies registered before its digital immatriculation platform. Business registration runs through easybusiness.bi, the agency's online single window. Since 4 August 2026 every tax registration request must go through e-KORI. Individuals attend in person for fingerprint and photograph capture before a certificate issues.

In January 2025 rural civil registry offices worked entirely on paper, beside a UNDP-supported commune-level digitisation pilot in Gitega. Births, deaths and marriages are registered and kept locally at communal état civil offices, with no single nationwide repository. In August 2026 direct birth declaration at health facilities, piloted since 2023 and institutionalised in 2024, was live in 123 facilities and had recorded over 52,000 births. The electoral register recorded 6,013,498 voters before the 2025 legislative elections, against a voting-age population of around 7.1 million. The electoral commission planned partial voter registration in October 2026 in more than 4,000 centres. The finance ministry's 2026-27 procurement plan carries a digital cadastre at BIF 2,692,493,969. It includes a secure network linking four land offices to a central data centre.

Sectoral management information systems

In 2019 computerised hospitals re-entered their data into DHIS2 by hand because automatic transfer from the hospital management system did not work. There is no unified digital identifier linking patients across providers. Under its information development plan for 2026-2028 the health ministry is working to make DHIS2, SIDAInfo and OpenClinic interoperable on the HL7 FHIR standard. No public timeline or budget has been confirmed. In May 2026 UNDP deployed the DataToCare laboratory system into nine sites, linking ten diagnostic instruments with automated transmission of results. The Global Fund financed it.

On 19 January 2026 the revenue authority launched the implementation phase of e-KORI, with a 27-month change-management rollout from 25 February. By June 2026 about 13,000 electronic billing machines had been deployed against a target of 40,000. In September 2025 the revenue authority required managers of public funds to obtain an electronic-billing-machine invoice from suppliers before paying them, on pain of a 20% penalty.

In 2024 the education management information system produced detailed annual yearbooks but was not integrated with human resources, infrastructure or examinations records. In 2024 prosecutors failed to open case files or lost them, and magistrates cited heavy backlogs and poor police documentation for missed hearings. By June 2026 the justice programme's digitisation component had deployed scanning kits and an offline platform for digitising pending case files. It covers the Cour suprême, the cours d'appel, and the tribunaux de grande instance and parquets of four provinces. In October 2026 a platform linking the penal chain from the judicial police to the prisons was reported in preparation. The 2026-27 procurement plan carries an international traffic audit platform at BIF 6,691,559,485. The memorandum with the United States commits the government to DHIS2 as its national health data warehouse, with US$188,526 of support costed for 2026. On 22 July 2025 the health ministry validated the priorities of its health informatics plan for 2025 to 2027, the Belgian development agency reported.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

In the 2024 survey, Burundi's level on the UN E-Government Development Index fell from middle to low. That placed it among the lowest-ranked countries in the world. Its public administration score was 47.6 out of 100 in 2023, 32nd of 54 African states.

On 17 February 2026 the revenue authority, the OBR, announced the launch and operationalisation of ABREMA. ABREMA is a fully digital one-stop shop for pharmaceutical imports. It was first launched in 2021, and its exclusive use was required from 18 February 2026. e-KORI was launched on 19 January 2026 for tax filing and the payment of taxes and fees. The OBR also runs the older Umutangakori online filing and payment service. From July 2026 land tax was declared and paid online only on it, by 30 September. On 1 September 2026 the OBR launched a WhatsApp chatbot answering tax and customs questions in French, Kirundi, English and Swahili.

In October 2026 the World Bank-financed digital foundations project invited proposals to build a national portal for online public services. The portal is to carry at least 11 priority services, and bids are due on 4 December 2026. The immigration authority has run an online portal for passports, laissez-passer and visas since October 2022. Building-permit applications have been taken online since 6 May 2024, through a World Bank-financed system. A government employment platform, PAEEJ-JOB, matched jobseekers with employers through a searchable CV database and vacancy listings (2025).

On 23 September 2026 the digital-foundations project issued an international request for proposals for an end-to-end electronic procurement system. The system is to be built over 18 months with the procurement regulator, and the finance minister puts its entry into force from 2027.

Most of the government service platforms on record as operating belong to the revenue authority. The presidency adopted a locally built messaging application as a channel for citizens' grievances, with no terms disclosed and no rule stated on control of the servers (2026).

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In November 2024 the Prime Minister's Office, through the National Land Commission, issued a national technical manual for communal land services and zonal land counters. It governs premises, IT equipment, staffing and the handling of certificates and registers. It provides for tablet-based field collection of land data and for electronic production and archiving of land certificates at commune level.

On 6 May 2026 the interior ministry presented its digital civil-registration platform, naming electricity and internet coverage at registration centres among the challenges. In August 2026 biometric enrolment was running in a first group of communes, and services were being decentralised towards zone level.

On 12 March 2026 the interior ministry presented a DHIS2-based platform for moving commune data to the centre across sectors. It was presented, not deployed, after preparatory work since 2024. Participants named internet availability and technology infrastructure as the constraints on a national rollout.

As at 2020 the multiservice telecentre planned for every commune, the government intranet and e-government services had not been delivered. National broadband infrastructure had by then reached commune level.

A 2025 reform consolidated the 18 provinces into 5. In August 2026 UNDP handed vehicles to provincial governors, and 165 laptops, 165 power supplies, 68 printers and 68 photocopiers to communal administrations.

In August 2024 the interior ministry launched the e-taxe electronic collection of communal revenue in 12 pilot communes with UNDP funding. In February 2026 the interior ministry and the collecting bank CRDB met in Gitega to prepare its national extension. In 2024-25, 48 of Kayanza commune's 54 revenue collectors used point-of-sale machines, with takings transferred electronically to the commune's account (June 2025). On 5 May 2026 Butihinda commune trained its planning, finance and accounting staff to use Interbank Burundi's IBB M+ platform for communal revenue. In October 2026 it began keying its revenue and spending since July 2025 into the AK-SOFT financial-management software. This followed a Prime Minister's Office workshop for communal finance officers, and its staff cited unstable internet.

Rural digital data capture

In 2025 the mass distribution of insecticide-treated bednets registered households electronically at the door. It managed stock by barcode and QR code and tracked distribution in real time.

In December 2023 the annual school census was collected on paper at the school and entered electronically only at central level. In January 2025 civil registry services were being digitised with UNDP support. The aim was that a record could be found by name and acts were no longer at risk of loss.

In June 2026 an African Development Bank-financed project tendered for IT equipment, tablets for data collection among it, for the Rusi polytechnic centre and ten rural digital learning centres. In August 2026 UNICEF sought suppliers of computers and power units for écoles centrales in the former provinces of Kirundo, Muyinga and Gitega.

On 29 April 2026 SETIC signed a US$5,907,000 contract with the state operator ONATEL to cover 92 collines with neither 2G nor 3G, serving 372,035 people. It is the second award under a PAFEN programme tendering 178 collines in three phases, to reach around 786,000 people. Open-access national roaming is a condition (March 2026). The first award went to Lumitel at more than US$10 million for 86 hills (March 2026).

That prices rural coverage at roughly US$64,000 per colline (April 2026) — the country's own smallest administrative division, and the delivery unit the contract itself uses.

Technology

AI

In September 2026 the finance and budget minister presented Burundi's artificial-intelligence ambitions at the Korea-Africa economic cooperation ministerial in Seoul. He sought financing and partnerships to turn the strategy into projects. No commitment, partner or project from that meeting has been found. On 22 April 2026 the Ministry of Finance, Budget and Digital Economy validated the strategy with UNDP support. It was still awaiting cabinet adoption at the end of June 2026. It follows an AI readiness diagnostic completed with UNDP in November 2025. Its six pillars are governance and ethics, sovereign cloud and data hubs, education and research, innovation and incubators, human capital, and pilots in health and agriculture. It is framed around digital sovereignty, its cloud and data-hub pillar built on the SETIC national data centre. It commits to training public officials in artificial intelligence. On the Africa AI Governance Index of October 2026, Burundi ranked 45th of 54 states with 0.70 out of 4, on evidence to 30 June 2026.

Two artificial-intelligence deployments inside Burundian sectoral systems are on record as at September 2026, both in malaria control and both at pilot stage, against the strategy's plan for roughly fifteen health and agriculture pilots.

From October 2024 Enabel and the malaria programme PNILP piloted AI-assisted reading of rapid diagnostic tests. Bluesquare is building a national malaria data warehouse.

In July 2026 UNDP recorded that institutions produce more data than before, but that it remains largely under-used and often unfit for advanced analytics. That month the DAAD alumni association ABADAAD convened a Bujumbura workshop on AI governance and data ethics. Its practitioners said the country holds a great deal of information but very little structured, consented and usable data.

The 2025 pilot of the EAC's regional AI innovation challenge drew 3,891 participants from 57 universities across eight partner states, Burundi among them. The East African Community is working up AI sovereignty as a bloc-wide position.

ICT Industry

On 29 May 2026 the telecoms regulator ARCT issued guidelines setting technical and operational standards for national e-commerce. On 26 June 2026 it called on platforms and merchant sites to make the compulsory prior declaration of conformity. The sector is regulated under the 2024 electronic communications and postal code.

In April 2026 ARCT's observatory counted three mobile operators, Econet Leo, Viettel Burundi and ONATEL, beside the satellite ISPs Starlink and NT Global. In December 2025 Viettel's Lumitel marked ten years in the country, and the group undertook to increase its Burundian investment.

In February 2026 a Burundian producer was shipping a machine-learning application, built in the country, that diagnoses crop diseases for farmers. Two further locally built artificial-intelligence products have been found: the mining-governance and geospatial tool MineGuard, and AfriPrompt.

On 3 July 2026 the Chinese company IBI Crossborder announced a comprehensive mining cooperation agreement with the Burundi Mining Authority. The company describes it in digital terms: digital transformation of the mining value chain, data-driven exploration and a platform connecting Burundi's minerals to markets. A phone assembler at Buringa, TECOO, employed more than 70 workers in December 2025, up from 45 technicians at its start about two years earlier.

Innovation ecosystem

As at 2025 Dealroom tracked around three startups in Burundi and no disclosed venture capital investment. Burundi ranked 127th in WIPO's 2024 Global Innovation Index, in the bottom quartile of ranked economies. As at 2020 the World Bank's digital economy assessment found no venture capital or angel investment available to digital entrepreneurs. It described digital entrepreneurship as embryonic, with a single technology hub in the country.

As at 2025 AfriLabs listed four member organisations: Burundi Business Incubator, Maison de l'Entrepreneur, DICOS Incubator and Impact Hub Bujumbura. It also listed BujaHub, KIT-HUB, Kesho Innovation Center and the Burundi Innovation Hub, an ITU WSIS Prize winner in 2023. BujaHub runs incubation facilities in Gitega, the political capital, as well as Bujumbura. It runs its Moonshot programme with the government, UNDP, the Youth Bank, the Women Investment Bank and the PAEEJ youth employment agency. In April 2023 the startup sector's actors, incubators, access to finance and regulation were mapped for Orange Corners and the Netherlands Enterprise Agency.

On 16 March 2026 the World Bank-financed PRETE-NYUNGANIRA project launched a national business-plan competition for small enterprises, with ICT among its priority sectors. On 24 June 2026 the ICT secretariat SETIC opened the JuneTech digital festival with the KiTHub innovation hub, backed by PAFEN. The national AI strategy's innovation pillar provides for incubators in Bujumbura, Ngozi and Gitega.

Capacity

Literacy

Burundi's Education score rose 5.9 points over the decade to 2023, to 45.3 out of 100 and 37th of 54 African states. Education completion rose 12.6 points to 20.5, and education quality 11.8 points to 44.2. Over the same ten years enrolment fell 1.4 points to 31.0, 41st of 54.

In 2023 Burundi scored 86.3 out of 100 for human resources in education, meaning teacher supply and qualification, 24th of 54 African states. It is among the country's ten best-scoring indicators of any kind.

PAFEN, the digital economy project running from 2022 to 2028, runs a digital access and inclusion component covering awareness, access to devices and "culture numérique". It is aimed at rural communities, women and girls, and vulnerable groups. Its published description sets out no baseline, no measurement instrument and no numeric target.

The EU's 2021-2027 programme for Burundi treats digital literacy as a cross-cutting provision, beside digital tools in education, and not as a priority area of its own.

Training and skills

In March 2024 Burundi had 49 école-centrale computer centres equipped and working, against a national target of 760 school networks. On 11 August 2026 UNICEF tendered for 585 computers and UPS units across 39 écoles centrales in Kirundo, Muyinga and Gitega.

That tender works out at about fifteen machines per school, and even completed in full it would leave the equipped centres at well under a fifth of the 760-centre target.

In 2025 at least three universities offered ICT and computer science degrees: the Université du Burundi's engineering faculty, Bujumbura Christian University and the Université du Lac Tanganyika. No enrolment figures have been published for them. In 2023-24 the Ecole Normale Supérieure enrolled 347 computing students, 29 per cent of them women. From 16 to 30 March 2026 Burundi held its second national coding camp for girls under the African Girls Can Code Initiative. UN Women ran it with three ministries, and Belgium financed it.

In September 2026 PAFEN's coordinator set a target of 5,000 civil servants and 10,000 young people trained in basic digital skills within 12 months. No start date or certification has been stated. The project's shared-infrastructure sub-component includes digital-skills training for the civil servants who use, manage and maintain that infrastructure. From 7 April 2026 the finance ministry held a change-management workshop to prepare the administration for the SIGFP public financial management system. In May 2026 education ministry cadres were trained in database administration in Yaoundé, to modernise the education management information system.

On 15-16 April 2026 Burundi validated its first national strategy on open educational resources at a ministry workshop. It aims to put the country's physical teaching materials on a platform reachable by phone. The communiqué of the second EAC regional education conference, in Nairobi on 25-28 August 2026, tasks the EAC Secretariat with monitoring an accountability framework. The framework sets country-owned targets to 2028, reviewed by regional peer review.

Research institutions

In July 2026 ABADAAD, the association of Burundian former DAAD scholarship holders, held a workshop in Bujumbura on AI governance and data ethics. The association's president is a professor.

The 2025 pilot of the AI4EAC Innovation Challenge drew 3,891 participants from 57 universities across eight East African countries, Burundi among them. A second edition, announced in August 2026, carries USD 50,000 in prize funding. It adds Equity Group Holdings and the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology as partners. In March 2026 the EAC AI Alliance was launched, inviting universities across the bloc, Burundi's among them, to join a regional network on AI in education.

Inclusion

Access to services

In 2025 the G3ict accessibility index found no requirement in any assessed domain that public services, websites or e-government be usable by persons with disabilities. There is no universal service obligation covering disability and no accessible e-government or web-content rule. The government's report to the UN disability committee, covering 2014 to mid-2021 and published in October 2025, gives no data on ICT use by persons with disabilities. Burundi has ratified the UN convention and has a 2018 law on the protection and promotion of their rights.

Since February 2025 access to public healthcare runs on the Carte d'Assistance Maladie, a separate health-insurance card compulsory for adults not otherwise covered. The national identity card is a paper credential bought at the communal office. The register behind it leaves a substantial share of the population unrecorded.

In 2024 the regulator ARCT ran a free short code, 151, and suggestion boxes in operator agencies, for telecoms consumer complaints only. In August 2026 the revenue authority ran its own free helpline on short code 500 for e-KORI users. During 2025 the Ombudsman analysed and dealt with 776 case files, thirty of them carried over from 2024.

The 2014 communal administration law creates consultative development committees at commune, hill and neighbourhood level to advise on development planning. In April 2026 the World Bank opened an online consultation on its 2026-2031 country partnership framework. In August 2026 a community digital hub with shared computers and digital-literacy training opened at Shombo. No user count or funding source for it has been found.

Digital divides

In 2023 Burundi ranked first in Africa for equality of access to public services between women and men. On the same index, equality of socioeconomic opportunity sat near the bottom of the continent and fell sharply over the decade to 2023. ENIF 2024, the national financial inclusion survey run by the central bank and the statistics institute and published in 2025, reports its results by sex.

GDP per capita was USD 199.6 in 2023, 52nd of 54 African states, on a GDP of USD 2.6bn for 13.2 million people. In January 2024, 88.7% of the population were offline. In 2024 the first national survey of ICT access, covering 4,100 households, found computer use below 1%. In 2023 the gap between urban and rural electricity access was close to total. In February 2026, 5G was live ahead of a working 3G layer for much of the country. In May 2026 power cuts and weak connectivity were among the main obstacles to rolling out the biometric identity card nationwide.

In April 2026 Burundi presented its AI strategy with low 4G coverage and weak rural electrification named as persisting challenges. In April 2025 the central bank said it would lean on USSD and offline transactions on its payment switch, given thin smartphone ownership. In December 2024 a survey of 17 centres for people with disabilities found 63% of users with no internet connection, citing cost and network problems. Equality in education improved over the decade to 2023, while enrolment fell over the same period.

Data

National statistics

On the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators for 2023, Burundi's data sources pillar scored under 20%. Data services and statistical infrastructure fell in the 20-49% band, and data use and data products both in the 70-89% band (2023). Burundi had fielded three or more household surveys in the preceding ten years, but no labour force survey and no business survey. It had held no business or establishment census in twenty years. On 15 October 2026 the General Enterprise Census of 2026 was launched to count every formal and informal enterprise and update the national enterprise register. On 6 November 2026 INSBU presented national accounts for 2017-2024, on the 2008 standard, to the statistical council. Mo Ibrahim's index put the capacity of the statistical system at 34.2 out of 100 in 2023, 43rd of 54 African states and 3.7 points down over the decade.

Statistical output rests on administrative records rather than survey rounds: three separately produced yearbooks — INSBU's national annuaire, the education ministry's and the trade, transport, industry and tourism ministry's — were published in the fourteen months to January 2026, all compiled from routine administrative sources.

In December 2024 INSBU published the 86th edition of the Annuaire Statistique du Burundi, drawing 22 chapters from ministry administrative sources. In December 2025 the education ministry issued Tome 2 of its 2024-2025 education yearbook. In January 2026 the trade ministry issued its own 2024 edition, built from licences, permits and traffic records.

Décret N°100/153 of 16 November 2022 replaced ISTEEBU with INSBU as the national statistical authority. INSBU is technical coordinator of the Système Statistique National and permanent technical secretariat of the Comité National de l'Information Statistique. SNDS-III, a third-generation strategy, is in force, and its mid-term evaluation had reported by INSBU's sector group meeting in the second quarter of 2026.

Burundi ran a general census of population, housing, agriculture and livestock in 2024, with preliminary results in 2025. The 2024 Ibrahim index carries no data for 16 of its 96 indicators for Burundi, including all 14 of its Afrobarometer public-perception measures.

Open data

On 10 June 2026 the Council of Ministers adopted a draft law on access to public information. On 29 September 2026 the Senate adopted the bill unanimously. The bill carries exceptions for national security, private life and ongoing judicial investigations. No record of its promulgation has been found. In 2023 both the disclosure of public records and citizens' ability to obtain them ranked among the weakest in Africa.

In the 2024 Open Data Inventory, government finance and price statistics score 7.5 out of 10 and labour 7. Banking scores 6.5, international trade 6, balance of payments 5 and national accounts 4.5. Environmental categories run from agriculture and land use at 7, through resource use at 6.5 and energy and pollution at 6 each, to the built environment at 5.5. Social statistics run from health facilities at 7, through education facilities and food security at 6, to gender at 5 and crime at 4.5. Education and health outcomes, population, poverty and reproductive health score 5.5 each.

Across the 23 data categories assessed for Burundi in the 2024 Open Data Inventory the average score is 5.7 out of 10, and no category reaches 8.

On 8 May 2026 INSBU validated a communication strategy for the national statistical system, aligned to East African Community guidelines. Material on INSBU's open data platform carries a licence giving a worldwide, free and non-exclusive right to use, copy, modify, translate, publish, adapt and distribute it. Derivative products, commercial or otherwise, are allowed, subject to attribution. On 7 November 2025 a first national data governance strategy was validated at a workshop with UN Economic Commission for Africa support. Practitioners describe a country with a great deal of information but very little structured, consented and usable data. They name this as the limit on artificial intelligence. In November 2023 the institute's head said its national data portal was open to all, with some survey indicators not updated yearly. A national summary data page under the IMF's enhanced dissemination system went live in April 2023.

Geospatial data

On 4 September 2025 IGEBU joined the WMO Information System 2.0 and began moving off the legacy Global Telecommunication System. Burundian weather observations entered international exchange for the first time. The link released a backlog of observations archived without international transmission since 2022. IGEBU also holds the national mapping mandate.

In May 2026 IGEBU and the WMO held a launch workshop on impact-based forecasting under the WISER Action First project. IGEBU publishes Meteosat Second Generation products over the Indian Ocean disc, updated automatically. It keeps a public archive of graded regional rain alerts from March 2026. They include a precipitation rate combining geostationary infrared with low-earth-orbit microwave, High Rate SEVIRI 3.9µm imagery, a convection RGB product, natural-colour RGB and a cloud mask.

Since 2009 a partnership with the Japan International Cooperation Agency has been updating the mapping of Bujumbura and Gitega. IGEBU states that Burundian cartography now uses remote sensing, GPS, geographic information systems and drones. One of its four stated purposes is identifying and managing agricultural, urban and protected zones. In February 2026 the Bureau Central du Recensement and IGEBU were cleaning and updating the census cartography database across 3,044 collines and quartiers. The work included geoprocessing vector data and interpreting satellite imagery.

In September 2025 Starlink had been licensed and operating in Burundi since 2024, but its equipment cost more than US$500, out of reach for many.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

On 6 February 2026 the United States signed a five-year health-sector memorandum with Burundi committing US$156m, health-data management included. Its data-systems section names SIDAinfo as the electronic medical record, to reach 460 facilities by 2026 and 572 by 2028. It names DHIS2 as the national health data warehouse. United States funding for data systems is planned at US$1,885,264 in 2026, falling to US$475,264 in 2030. The government commits to pay the software licensing, cloud computing and hardware costs the United States does not.

Under the 2026 memorandum the two governments intend to negotiate a data-sharing arrangement, expected to run for seven years, for accountability to the United States Congress. No such arrangement had been published as at October 2026. No hyperscaler activity in Burundi had been found as at October 2026.

China activities

In July 2026 the Chinese company IBI Crossborder announced a mining cooperation agreement with the Burundi Mining Authority. Its stated objects are digital: digitising the mining value chain, data-driven exploration and a platform connecting the country's minerals to markets. The company presents it as Digital Silk Road work. On 28 May 2026 the Chinese embassy handed ICT and production equipment to the state news agency, Agence Burundaise de Presse.

A Chinese satellite-television project completed in March 2019 equipped 300 Burundian villages with satellite reception. It ran under the "Access to Satellite TV for 10,000 African Villages" programme, financed through the South-South Cooperation Assistance Fund. It delivered a pledge China had made at the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation. Huawei extended a USD 30 million credit for a recovery plan for the state operator ONATEL, adopted by the Council of Ministers in November 2017. It was meant to modernise the equipment of ONATEL's mobile arm.

Of the four Chinese engagements on the record in Burundi's digital and media sector, two are completed transactions — the 2017 vendor credit to ONATEL and the 2019 satellite-television installation — and the other two are an in-kind equipment donation and a framework agreement with no recorded implementation. Two of the four were announced by Chinese state and Chinese corporate publishers; the 2026 equipment handover was reported by a Burundian institution and the ONATEL credit by a Burundian newspaper.

EU activities

The EU's Multiannual Indicative Programme for Burundi 2021-2027 gives digital no priority area of its own. Digital runs across its three priorities as digital literacy, context-adapted digital tools, digital tools in education and a "partnership for digital transformation".

On 24 June 2026 the EU-EAC DEEP programme for the digital economy, e-commerce, e-payments and public e-services was launched. It reaches Burundi as one of eight East African Community partner states, with no Burundi-specific component set out. In August 2026 Germany put money into an EAC-wide artificial intelligence research and training network that reaches Burundi the same way.

On 14 July 2026 the EU, working through UN Women, handed the government three digital platforms for handling gender-based-violence cases. A vehicle and seventeen motorcycles came with them. In 2024 EU money was behind the justice sector's judicial-statistics framework and structured case-file archiving in pilot areas. The EU was among the financiers of the Jiji-Mulembwe hydropower complex, inaugurated in June 2026. In March 2026 Belgium funded the second national coding camp for Burundian girls, run by UN Women with three ministries.

Gulf/UAE activities

In August 2025 the President received a Qatari royal delegation leading the Al Massour Holding group. It announced a decision to invest heavily in sectors including cybersecurity, digital transformation, telecommunications and banking. In November 2026 the finance and digital economy minister asked Saudi Arabia to consider backing the national digital identity card and a national data centre. No pledge was stated. No Gulf or United Arab Emirates memorandum, framework agreement or loan on digital or data had been found as at November 2026.

India activities

On 22 June 2026 Isidora Ntakiyiruta, adviser to the Ministry of Finance, Budget and Economic Planning, met India's representative to Burundi, Upender Singh Rawat. They discussed cooperation on digital government services, digital identity and e-payments, for public-service delivery and financial inclusion. The question is whether Burundi can adapt India's model of digital identity linked to instant payments. Nothing had been agreed or built as at June 2026.

Digital sovereignty

In January 2026 the Merankabandi social-protection programme replaced its management information system with the open-source CORE-MIS, hosted by the government at SETIC. Project and government staff run both production and test environments, and local teams were trained in setup, deployment and maintenance. In March 2023 travel-document production had been entirely national since the Contec Global contract lapsed, with a new supplier contracted only for production inputs.

In May 2021 ministry ICT focal points questioned whether SETIC could be trusted with state data. Ministries were then hosting their websites on foreign servers, although SETIC offered hosting free. The 2026 data-protection law restricts transfers of personal data to jurisdictions without adequate protection or approved safeguards. No instrument fixing where state data is kept, or under whose law it is processed, had been found in force as at September 2026.

In May 2026 the interior ministry presented the biometric identity platform as ready to produce cards. Where the national identity database is hosted, and under whose jurisdiction, had not been published as at September 2026.

In June 2026 Burundi called at the East African Community for harmonised data-exchange frameworks for interoperable cross-border public services.
