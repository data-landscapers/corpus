Status values. Implemented — in operation or in force. Piloting — running with a limited user group or in a controlled environment. In development — build or drafting under way, not yet operating. Planned — announced or provided for, no build or draft on record. Enacted — an instrument passed into law; pair with a qualifying clause for its in-force date. Under review — a law or policy currently being reconsidered. Discontinued — closed or superseded. Not held — the base carries no reliable statement of status; these are the gaps to fill and are listed again at the end.

Summary of position

Zimbabwe has a data protection statute in force and no administrative sanctions under it. The Cyber and Data Protection Act has applied since 2022, with the communications regulator designated as data protection authority (the Act); the regulator stated in May 2026 that administrative sanctions are not yet in place, leaving criminal penalties of up to seven years' imprisonment for the accountable chief executive as the only enforcement route (regulator account). Mandatory compliance inspections begin on 1 September 2026 (notice account).

The National AI Strategy 2026-2030, launched on 13 March 2026, provides for an implementation office, a regulatory committee, a data platform, a fusion centre and a sovereign national cloud (launch account). All five remain proposals, and a media rights organisation records that the strategy has no legal force (organisation analysis).

The identity system that would underpin any of it has not started. A national digital identity was announced in May 2025 for a July 2025 start and no launch, enrolment or coverage figure is on record (ministerial account).

Satellite terminal subscriptions reached 67,057 by the fourth quarter of 2025, up 31.62% on the quarter (regulator report). Two licences were issued to the operator on 6 September 2024, conditional on the regulator clearing every agent and dealer distributing terminals; the widely cited US$575,000 is the trade press's arithmetic on the 2021 fee schedule, not a regulator figure (regulator statement).

The satellite operator was excluded from the regulator's equipped international bandwidth table from the second quarter of 2025 and remained excluded at the fourth, while carrying 35.05% of fixed internet and data traffic (analysis).

Fifth-generation service reaches 18.9% of the population and 0.0% of the rural population (regulator report). The Harare Internet Exchange Point was built on a US$200,000 African Union Commission grant and housed in the state fixed operator's exchange building; when the regulator reported it to the ITU in October 2020 every Zimbabwean access provider was connected and it had two international peers (regulator contribution). The ITU's record of the project to establish a national incident response team, which ran to 28 February 2021, still states that its achievements will be updated on completion (project record). The ICT ministry's own 2025 list of priority projects separately names establishment of a national computer incident response team (ZW-CIRT), for monitoring, detecting and responding to cyber threats, alongside a National Data Centre project; neither carries a completion date or operating status (priority projects).

None of the commercial storage estate is carrier-neutral: an analyst brief for a European Union facility put about six live facilities at roughly 2 MW of live load, on 2024 estimates (market brief). The state fixed operator's own product page states its data centre environment is Tier 3 standard, an undated and uncertified operator claim, vendor- and ICT-provider-neutral, running Microsoft HyperV, VMware, OpenStack and Huawei FusionSphere virtual-machine platforms with active-active redundancy across multiple data centres nationally (TelOne product page). A health financing partner reports 1,044 health facilities solarised across all ten provinces, more than 60% of the total, on 11 megawatts of capacity (case study).

The central bank reported 511.1 million electronic transactions worth ZiG1.37 trillion in the six months to 30 June 2026, and the same conference heard that of hundreds of thousands of cards in the market only about 20,000 are in use (conference account). The central bank's own fourth-quarter payment systems report, held for the first time, puts electronic transactions at ZiG736.03bn in the quarter to 31 December 2025, up 7.78% on the third quarter, with mobile money carrying 86.70% of volume but only 10.51% of value against RTGS's 58.29% of value on 1.20% of volume (RBZ report). The stock exchange and a corporate-capital firm signed a memorandum of understanding in July 2026 to channel growth capital into SMEs through the Zimbabwe Entrepreneurship Exchange (ZEEX), a newly approved venue combining primary-market fundraising, asset tokenisation and secondary-market trading; no capital commitment or listing timetable is stated (MOU account).

The Smart Zimbabwe 2030 master plan, previously known to the base only at second hand, is now held in full: it sets a National Data Centre as a Smart Government outcome and records, as at December 2018, no interoperability between data centres, systems and databases across ministries (master plan). The Office of the President and Cabinet's E-Government Technology Unit issued Version 2.0 of the government-wide ICT specification standards in May 2025, revising the May 2023 original as the reference standard for all public-sector ICT procurement (standards). ZIMRA is readying a second phase of its Tax and Revenue Management System (TaRMS), integrating third-party stakeholders after a first phase said to have improved data integrity, payment accuracy and taxpayer compliance (TaRMS account). Kwekwe City Council's own account of its finance department states billing runs on the Promun (ERP) package across rates, refuse, sewerage and water charges, with an explicit information-technology and e-governance function (council page).

The 2026 monetary policy statement capped cash withdrawal charges at 2% and card terminal charges at 1.5% to a maximum of US$20, and raised transfer limits to ZiG13,000 per transaction with a ZiG50,000 monthly cap (policy account). A named analyst records the governor conceding limited capacity to enforce them (analysis).

Against about 1.5 million undocumented children and passport backlogs recorded at more than 300,000 in 2022, the home affairs minister told the Senate in May 2026 that he was not aware of any backlog (Senate account). Civil registration is computerised in all ten provinces and 63 districts but in more than 20 of 209 sub-offices (registrars' meeting). A mobile exercise issued 3,203,389 registration documents free between February and September 2022 against a target of 2 million (ministerial statement).

The national electronic medical record, Impilo, is implemented at 1,254 of a planned 1,900 health facilities, and a health information exchange to connect laboratory systems, logistics management and DHIS2 to it is under development; both are reported by a health financing partner, not the ministry (case study). The health ministry's own platform now describes Impilo as a national health operating system spanning care-finding, provider verification, medicines and diagnostics directories and virtual care, most of it usable without a citizen account (platform), and its continuous-build Core FHIR Implementation Guide (ZWCore), marked for demonstration purposes only, defines national profiles for patient, encounter and facility resources (FHIR guide). The e-health strategy that named interoperability and patient privacy as preconditions ran to 2017, and no successor is held.

Licences under the 2024 regulations ran from 570 institutions in June 2025 to 721 by October 2025, and trained data protection officers from more than 500 to about 1,200 (regulator account). A notice run in the press on 26 July 2026 sets mandatory risk-based inspections from 1 September, with certification of every officer at US$1,250 plus a US$30 application fee (notice account). Licence fees under the same regulations are tiered by data-subject count — US$50 a year for 50-1,000 subjects, US$300 plus a US$30 application fee for 1,001-100,000, and US$500-2,500 above that — so an organisation in the lowest band faces over US$1,300 of first-year compliance cost; a legal expert calls the registration-and-fee model itself, rather than the underlying data protection, "new and strange" (fee account). Neither notice has been published by the regulator itself.

The operative National ICT Policy 2022-2027, signed by President Mnangagwa and previously known to the base only through later documents that cite it, is now held in full: its legislative programme commits to enacting frameworks for a National Data Centre, cloud computing and big data, and digital financial services, and to legislating autonomous regulatory bodies; the document itself states no adoption or publication date (the policy).

The National AI Strategy 2026-2030 is now held in full. Cabinet approved it on 14 October 2025 (post-Cabinet briefing); it sets out four strategic pillars, five flagship initiatives and three phases to 2030, and carries no publication date, no financing envelope and no costed action plan (strategy). Its proposed act, implementation office, national council, data agency, regulatory committee and sandbox are all still proposals. A media rights organisation reports that the strategy has no legal force, and that the monitoring centre established by the 2021 Act operates under the Office of the President with no judicial oversight (organisation analysis).

An amendment bill to the statute that constitutes the regulator was gazetted on 10 October 2025, is not in force, and sat with the Parliamentary Legal Committee on 2 August 2026; its replacement section 25 requires ministerial directions to the regulator to be prospective and of general application (Bill). A statutory instrument gazetted on 12 June 2026 barred the regulator from licensing any applicant less than 75% owned by Zimbabwean citizens; it is reported repealed in full by an instrument the base does not hold (the instrument).

The justice minister told the Senate on 23 July 2026 that transfer of the voters' roll to the registrar-general has begun, stating no date, transition plan or safeguard (Senate account).

The compute behind the operator artificial-intelligence service launched in April 2026 is hosted at an affiliated group's facility in South Africa, stated in the operator's own launch announcement (announcement). A trade account reports the university high-performance computer quoted to local developers at tens of thousands of United States dollars for limited access in large blocks (account).

A media rights organisation reports that the proposed truth-verification platform names no supervising institution, criteria or safeguards, and that the fusion centre carries no governance structure, no limit on data collection, retention or use, and no route to challenge a threat assessment (organisation analysis).

Harare ended talks on a bilateral health cooperation memorandum in February 2026 over long-term United States access to national health data. The United States embassy stated in August 2026 that the memorandum's conditions were co-investment, financial accountability and the sharing of disease specimens during outbreaks, and that assertions it sought Zimbabweans' personal data are categorically false (embassy statement). Both accounts are supportable on the strategy's own model texts, acquired in August 2026: the model data sharing agreement gives the United States secure, uninterrupted access, expressly including login credentials, to seven national health systems including electronic medical records, for twenty-five years continuing ten years past termination — and in the same clause tells the partner government that to the maximum extent practical it should not provide individual level data or personally identifiable information unless there is no other way, the model access role being a viewer role without access to such information. The template memorandum requires physical specimens and related data, including genetic sequence data, of detected pathogens with epidemic potential to be provided within five days of detection, for twenty-five years. Neither text is the Zimbabwe draft, which was never signed and has not been published (2026-08-09).

Household internet access reached 75.5% in the 2025 survey against 50.1% in 2020, while household computer ownership fell to 11.2% from 14.2%, at 21.2% urban against 4.7% rural (survey account).

The national financial inclusion strategy is now held in full: the Reserve Bank's second-phase strategy (2022-2026) targets a rise in overall usage of formal financial services from 70% to 90%, with a digital financial services target of 77% to 90% on transactional accounts, and carries no costed action plan or funding envelope (strategy). The Research Council of Zimbabwe's 2023 and 2024 annual reports both confirm no gross domestic expenditure on research and development, or R&D-as-share-of-GDP figure, has been published for Zimbabwe (2023 report).

More than 400 health facilities hold satellite kits, none activated because the health ministry states the subscription fees are too high, and the universal services fund covers subscriptions only for digital centres and schools, for one year (National Assembly account).

External financing is old and largely Chinese: two export-credit facilities and two grants for a computing centre, the newest committed in 2019. The one recent commitment, a SEK 36,000,000 youth digital skills grant of 2023, still shows nothing disbursed (grant record).

Domestic appropriation is now on record: the 2026 Appropriation Act votes ZiG258,292,000 to ICT ministry policy and administration, ZiG505,225,000 to ICT development and promotion, ZiG1,470,460,000 to civil registration and ZiG994,652,000 to migration management (the Act). Telecommunications licensees pay 1.5% of monthly gross turnover into the universal services fund (2021 regulations), and the Auditor-General audited that fund for 2023 and 2024 and published no balance, contribution total or disbursement figure (audit report). The grand challenge provided for in the strategy was launched on 4 August 2026 across agriculture, health, education, financial services, mining and public administration (launch account); the regulator reported 738 applications and 68 projects selected for an August boot camp in Nyanga, narrowed to 18 candidates for the next stage, with no award or budget published (Nyanga account).

The second is the regulator's own statistics: the Q1 2026 sector performance report is carried only through a news account of a post-Cabinet briefing, so the 379 base stations, the 19,000 km backbone and the 57.28% traffic rise have no per-operator table behind them. The third is delivery under the identity programme, where no figure for cards issued or persons enrolled is held at any date.
