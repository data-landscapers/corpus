Zimbabwe: monthly update, July 2026

Edition of 2026-08-06  ·  38 systems and instruments tracked, 28 of them not held
↓ Download PDF

Developments recorded from artefacts published between 2026-07-01 and 2026-08-06 — July 2026 carried forward to the date of issue, so the report holds the nightly catch to the day it was cut. Sections follow the status report.

Summary of the month

The ICT minister launched a whole-of-government resource planning system on 29 July 2026 and committed to an artificial-intelligence ethics framework tabled by the end of the first quarter of 2027, mandated open interface standards for public-sector platforms, and a ring-fenced share of the universal services fund for public-good projects, stating no amount (launch account).

Two measured positions were published in the same weeks and point in opposite directions. The 2025 household survey put household internet access at 75.5%, up from 50.1% in 2020, and household computer ownership down to 11.2% from 14.2% (survey account). The central bank reported 511.1 million electronic transactions worth ZiG1.37 trillion in the half year, against a researcher's finding that only about 20,000 cards are in active use (conference account).

The health minister told the National Assembly on 17 July that more than 400 rural health facilities hold satellite kits that have never been switched on (National Assembly account).

Infrastructure

Phase 1 of the cross-border fibre corridor from Plumtree to Bulawayo was reported live and carrying traffic on 3 July 2026, at an initial equipped capacity of 800 Gbps on equipment rated above 10 Tbps, under a public-private partnership signed in June 2025; phase 2 to Livingstone is due in September 2026 and no investment amount was disclosed by either party (corridor account).

The listed infrastructure company reported for the quarter ended May 2026 that it deployed 90 new base stations, reinvested 17% of revenue into capital projects on an undisclosed revenue base, and commenced phase 1 of a 100 MW solar farm for its technology park; its fuel-management system was credited with reducing energy consumption with no percentage, litre, cost or uptime figure given (trading update).

The state fixed operator's chief executive told its annual general meeting on 26 June that government arrears had risen to close to US$42m, also given as ZiG1.2bn, from US$19.2m a year earlier (annual general meeting account). The state mobile operator terminated its chief executive's contract on 4 August following a board resolution (termination account).

Digital public infrastructure

The road authority took handover of an expanded toll plaza on 29 July with two of six lanes reserved for radio-frequency tag holders, a second plaza due the next day and a third under construction, against a stated programme covering all 29 tollgates (handover account). The announcement states nothing on retention, access or legal basis for the movement record the tags generate.

A Rwandan immigration delegation toured two border posts on 29 July under a 2024 bilateral memorandum, examining the online border management system Zimbabwe has been rolling out in phases since January 2025 (visit account). No port coverage, completion date or throughput figure is published.

A deputy registrar general told a meeting of African registrars general on 24 July that all ten provinces and 63 districts are computerised but only more than 20 of 209 sub-offices, with electronic passport enrolment centres in ten districts, eight provincial centres and one abroad, and seven-day issuance away from the capital (meeting account). The switch operator reported interoperable quick-response payments in rollout and participation in a pan-African settlement system, with no merchant, acceptance-point or transaction count (conference account).

Governance and regulation

The regulator ran a notice in the press on 26 July setting mandatory data-protection inspections from 1 September 2026, with a registration threshold of 50 data subjects, licence fees in four tiers, a US$30 application fee, and journalistic, historical and archival processing exempt from the Act but still required to register (notice account). Certification of every data protection officer is required, at US$1,250 for a Zimbabwean.

The justice minister told the Senate on 23 July that government has begun implementing the third constitutional amendment, transferring voter registration and management of the roll from the electoral commission to the registrar-general, and characterised the 2013 transfer to the commission as a response to legacy issues now considered resolved; no date, transition plan or safeguard was stated (Senate account).

The ICT ministry committed on 29 July to an artificial-intelligence ethics framework tabled by the end of the first quarter of 2027 and to mandated open interface standards for public-sector platforms, publishing neither a standard nor a compliance deadline (launch account).

Artificial intelligence and the technology sector

The whole-of-government resource planning system was launched on 29 July 2026, integrating finance, human resources, supply chain and asset management across the public sector and presented by the ministry as the clean data layer any government artificial intelligence would need; no ministry count, deployment schedule or cost was stated (launch account). The same address named a national high-performance computing centre, the state fixed operator and a private operator's data centre as the infrastructural spine, and committed to indigenous-language corpora work led by universities.

An implementing partner reported on 28 July that it had been building artificial-intelligence competency among research, committee and budget-office staff in Zimbabwe's Parliament, and that staff were using public tools on personal judgment, with limited monitoring of data flows into those platforms and no consistent approach to identifying fabricated output (partner account).

The listed infrastructure company described its remote monitoring system and digital twin as advancing rather than in production for the quarter ended May 2026 (trading update).

Inclusion, capacity and finance

The 2025 household survey, released on 20 July, reported household internet access at 75.5% against 50.1% in 2020, mobile handset ownership at 96.4%, and household computer ownership down to 11.2% from 14.2%, at 21.2% urban against 4.7% rural (survey account).

The health minister told the National Assembly on 17 July that more than 400 health facilities had received satellite kits with 300 more to be added, but that subscriptions had not been paid because the fees were too high; the ICT minister replied that the universal services fund currently covers subscriptions only for digital centres and schools, and only for one year (National Assembly account).

At a payments conference on 24 July a research firm reported that 95% of transport payments remain cash, that 56% of respondents found digital systems complicated with people with disabilities particularly affected (conference account). The ICT minister told a small-business meeting on 17 July that a digital skills programme targets 1.5 million people (meeting account).
