This report is an attempt to provide a comprehensive summary of the status of digital transformation in your selected country. It is grouped by chapter and topic.

The report only uses evidence from public sources catalogued in the Corpus repository. Corpus aims to discover and index all evidence that is available from public online sources. Pay-walled, password-protected and unpublished documents are not included. This means that while there should be no 'hallucinations' there may well be gaps.

The report is written by Claude Code and updated nightly whenever new relevant evidence arrives.

Two shorter country reports are also maintained. The Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months. The Maturity Assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators.

Governance

Strategies, plans and policies

Lesotho's communications minister signed three foundational digital policies in June 2026. They are a Broadband Infrastructure Sharing Policy, a Data Management Policy covering privacy, security and ethical use, and an AI Policy aimed at health, education and public administration. In August 2026 the ministry began drafting a National AI Strategy and a Data Centre Blueprint together, at a five-day workshop in Maseru run with the United Nations in Lesotho. There Deputy Prime Minister Justice Nthomeng Majara argued that capabilities should be assessed, and safeguards, infrastructure, policies and human capacity established, before the country scales AI investment.

The National Digital Policy 2024–2035 is the national frame for equitable access to digital services, citizen-centred digital government and digital skills. The National Digital Transformation Strategy 2024–2030 makes enterprise architecture, interoperability and data exchange a priority under its digital government pillar. The final text of the AI Policy was posted on 17 August 2026. The regulator's LCA Strategic Plan 2026–2029 makes universal and meaningful connectivity its top theme. The finance ministry's Budget Strategy Paper for 2027/28 to 2029/30 commits the government to fast-track the 2024–2030 strategy by deploying digital identity, data exchange and online payments. It directs medium-term budget support to five digital-skills and AI areas without attaching a sum to any of them.

Officials told a national planning meeting on 8 May 2026 that the country has no policy guiding spatial data. They proposed a National Spatial Development Infrastructure Policy to fill the gap. A two-day workshop opened on 26 August 2025 to develop a Digital Public Infrastructure Framework.

Lesotho scored 53.9 of 100 for overall governance in 2023, 21st of 54 African states. That is down 1.9 points and seven places since 2014, while the continental average rose. It scores below that average on 42 of the index's 96 indicators, and declined on 51 of them over 2014–2023. Public Administration is the third best-performing sub-category, at 62.4 of 100 and 13th.

Legislation and regulation

Lesotho's cyber legislation was split in two after stakeholder consultation: a Cybercrime Bill carrying offences and penalties, and a Cybersecurity Bill 2026 carrying institutions and the protection of critical digital infrastructure. The Cybercrime Bill was awaiting National Assembly approval in August 2026. The Cybersecurity Bill went to a second stakeholder consultation in September 2026, after its draft was circulated for review in August. The draft proposes a two-tier regulatory regime and the registration of cybersecurity professionals. The unenacted 2022 electronic transactions Bill was revised as the Electronic Transactions and Communications Bill, 2026 and taken to a stakeholder validation workshop from 28 September 2026. The ministry stated that Lesotho has no legal framework for electronic contracts, signatures or records. The Bill goes next to the Office of the Parliamentary Counsel. A Digital Government Act was still in drafting in 2025.

The Communications Act 2012 is in force. The National Identity Cards Act 2011 commenced on 1 July 2013. It establishes the National Identity Register and mandates the card for access to services. It was amended in 2022 to bring refugees into the register and open its data to the police, security services and Parliament. Also in force are the Data Protection Act 2012, the Payment Systems Act 2014, the Financial Consumer Protection Act 2022 and the Bureau of Statistics Act 2001.

Legal Notice 25 of 2026, gazetted on 27 March 2026, gave the Lekuka VAT e-invoicing framework its legal basis. The Payment System Bill, 2025 reached second reading on 5 May 2026. It would replace the 2014 Act and bring every payment-service provider, fintechs included, under one licensing law. A committee report on the electoral amendment bill tying voter registration and verification to the national identity card was adopted on 4 June 2026. A fourth Statistics Bill was in drafting as at December 2025.

The Lesotho Communications Authority issued the country's first satellite internet licence, a ten-year grant to Starlink on 14 April 2025. On 5 December 2025 it found Vodacom Lesotho in breach of consumer-protection rules for restricting Top-Up Flexi airtime without the required 21 days' notice.

Data protection

The Data Protection Commission provided for in the 2012 Act has never been appointed or made operational, leaving its complaint-handling, compliance-audit and enforcement provisions unimplemented. The Act commenced on 22 February 2012. Its section 23 requires notification of security compromises to that Commission and to affected data subjects as soon as reasonably possible. Delay is permitted only where the police or the Commission direct it for a criminal investigation. A Data Management Policy covering privacy, security and ethical use was among the three digital policies the communications minister signed in June 2026. In September 2026 the ICT ministry's Principal Secretary said the government had developed DPI Safeguards for Children with UNICEF Lesotho and was piloting them with the National University of Lesotho across five ministries.

Third parties reach the national identity register through memoranda of understanding and fees set at the Director's discretion under section 6 of the National Identity Cards Act 2011. Those agreements and their non-disclosure agreements are not published, and no purpose-limitation framework is publicly documented. The Act provides no identity-specific complaints, appeals or judicial-review mechanism, and no independent body oversees the register. Unresolved grievances fall back on the ordinary courts or the Ombudsman. Individuals have a statutory right to see and correct their record but must attend the registry in person. Nobody is told when their data is accessed or shared.

Section 43 of the Financial Consumer Protection Act 2022 makes financial institutions and payment service providers fully responsible for personal information. They must process it in line with the Data Protection Act, and stronger sector-specific safeguards prevail. The Central Bank of Lesotho may impose administrative penalties. SIM registration has been compulsory under regulations in operation since 24 June 2022. The regulator let the June 2023 deadline pass while it cleaned the data, and set 31 January 2024 as the last day before unregistered cards would be cut off.

Regional collaboration

Lesotho was one of eight Southern African states sending ICT ministry, regulator, AI-structure and data-protection officials to a data-governance workshop in Harare in August 2026. UNESCO ran it with Smart Africa and Zimbabwe's ICT ministry. Participants worked through UNESCO's Data Governance Toolkit and self-assessed towards a regional harmonisation roadmap, which has not been published.

Lesotho joined the 50-in-5 campaign in May 2024 as its fourteenth member. It undertook to implement safe, inclusive and interoperable digital public infrastructure within five years. It committed specifically to integrate civil registration and vital statistics with digital public infrastructure and upgrade the national identity card to a digital ID.

That five-year commitment runs to 2029.

Lesotho was welcomed into the Smart Africa Alliance at the eighth meeting of its council of ICT ministers in March 2026, one of six new members that took the alliance to 42.

SADC's digital agenda for its members rests on a draft Digital Transformation Strategy and Action Plan of 12 October 2022. South Africa took the SADC chairpersonship at the 46th Ordinary Summit in Durban on 17 August 2026, naming energy, water and digital security among its priorities. Three days earlier the incoming chair said Southern Africa is "exporting data and importing intelligence". He said regional industrialisation must extend to computing capacity, connectivity and skills. None of Lesotho's border posts is among the five one-stop border posts prioritised for the chairship. On 9 September 2026 the Lesotho–South Africa Bi-National Commission undertook to accelerate smart one-stop border posts at Maseru and Ficksburg.

Lesotho's country statement to the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance of 7 July 2026 set out the government's position. It named capacity building, AI-ready data, openness, interoperability and sector-led risk-based governance. The ministry's Principal Secretary Kanono Ramashamole said developing nations must be "co-creators, not only consumers" of AI. He named fragmented data and weak governance as the domestic constraints. On 3 October 2026 the communications authority and Eswatini's regulator signed a memorandum on cybersecurity cooperation. It provides for coordinated incident response and the exchange of information and resources.

Standards

The data-harmonisation exercise Lesotho joined in Harare in August 2026 works from four existing instruments. They are the African Union Data Policy Framework, the Smart Africa Data Governance Blueprint, the SADC Regional Data Strategy and UNESCO's Data Governance Toolkit. Across SADC there is still no harmonised eKYC standard and no shared consent-management framework. Localisation and legal-basis questions are unresolved before any exchange architecture is chosen. The regional telecommunications regulators' collaboration instrument, adopted in Malawi in April 2026, is voluntary and non-binding by its own terms.

The Central Bank publishes the electronic funds transfer clearing rules, the MIACH rules, the Lesotho Wire rule book and the 2017 electronic payment instrument regulations as free downloads. They need no registration. It oversees payment and settlement systems under a National Payment System Oversight Framework published in 2018. The government's enterprise architecture and e-government interoperability framework is being developed with an external consultant, which reported in March 2026 that it was also running GovStack training for institutional teams. No draft has been published. The Lesotho Standards Institution is an observing member of two ISO technical committees and three policy development committees (October 2026). Lesotho's 50-in-5 membership commits it to secure, inclusive and interoperable digital public infrastructure by 2028.

The Office of the Auditor-General's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, launched on 8 August 2026, names expanded IT-focused specialised audits and the digitising of its own business processes among its priorities. It carries no separately costed digital commitments or published milestone dates. No evaluation criteria have been published for the government's digital service-identification pilot, which began in April 2026.

Public debate and participation in policymaking

The public perception of freedom of speech score dropped 17.7 points over 2014–2023 to 61.5 of 100, 22nd of 54 African states. The expert-assessed freedom of expression and belief measure is one of the country's ten best scores, at 87.5 of 100 and 7th of 54, down 4.1 points. Media freedom fell 14.0 points to 48.6 of 100 and 35th place over the same period. Digital freedom, covering censorship, shutdowns and digital rights, fell 8.6 points to 77.1 of 100, and ranks 13th.

The three digital policies the minister signed in June 2026 were validated at a stakeholder exercise in 2025, and no adoption, publication or commencement date has been stated for any of them. On 9 August 2026 the ICT ministry and UNDP opened a Public Digital Services Experience Survey, asking citizens about access, usability, trust and skills gaps.

Starlink's launch in July 2025 drew objections that a wholly foreign entity without a physical address in the country could enable surveillance. The activist Kananelo Boloetse questioned the fairness of the regulatory treatment it received.

In 2023 Lesotho scored poorly on the disclosure and accessibility of public records. Accountability and transparency is its second worst-performing governance sub-category.

Finance

Financial sustainability

The 2025/26 budget carried LSL 62.2 million of African Development Fund loan money for e-government and LSL 39.1 million for tax modernisation, and the 2026/27 estimates print none for either. The government's own e-government line stays at LSL 10.0 million, against LSL 9.9 million in 2025/26.

The IFMIS gains a LSL 41.8 million capital project of the state's own in 2026/27, beside LSL 39.6 million for running it. The project pays for an upgrade from Epicor 10.2 to Epicor Kinetic that the finance ministry says will ensure continued vendor support and long-term sustainability. Software licences sit on the recurrent budget: LSL 5.75 million for crop services, LSL 4.6 million for immigration and LSL 1.1 million for the anti-corruption directorate (2026/27).

The digital lines of the 2026/27 budget come to LSL 941.2 million appropriated, against LSL 767.4 million in 2025/26, when LSL 101.2 million of it was African Development Fund loan money (2026/27).

The 2026/27 capital tables name each project's funder. The World Bank-financed Public Sector Foundations for Service Delivery project carries LSL 116.5 million of IDA credit beside LSL 15.3 million of the government's own (2026/27). That credit closes in March 2028. The 2026/27 recurrent tables print no financing source against their digital lines.

In 2024/25 the state's own tax-modernisation spending came to LSL 764,339 and its e-government spending to LSL 15.9 million. The 2024/25 draft budget had proposed LSL 8.0 million and LSL 17.0 million.

The Universal Service Fund, established under section 33 of the Communications Act 2012, finances telecommunications extension to unserved and underserved areas. It took over the UNDP-piloted Community Digital Inclusion Model in November 2025.

MoUs and other agreements

Lesotho signed a binding Memorandum of Agreement with the US firm Convalt Energy on 4 June 2026 for Project Kobong. It is a development framework that commits no money. Its progression turns on feasibility studies, permitting, financing, regulatory approvals and definitive project agreements still to be settled. The M98 billion attached to it is the government's figure for at least 1,200MW of pumped-storage hydropower and an integrated AI data centre in Mokhotlong District. It is a project value, not financing raised. Construction is targeted for the end of the decade, conditional on feasibility work not yet begun. The Lesotho project company is to sit inside a Convalt entity that has not been created. A wider partnership announced alongside the agreement extends to solar generation and battery storage outside the M98 billion, with no financing arrangement named.

Econet Telecom Lesotho and UNDP signed a memorandum of understanding in Maseru on 28 July 2026, renewing a partnership begun in 2020 and expanded in 2022. It covers digital public infrastructure and AI adoption, and an innovation and research pillar built around the University Innovation Pod and a proposed National AI Academy. It also covers digital entrepreneurship and AI-enabled business solutions for small firms. No commitment amount, term or value is stated for it. The proposed academy has no named timetable, governance structure, curriculum or host institution.

A memorandum of understanding with Vodacom Lesotho underpins the ministry's corporate work on digital inclusion (March 2026). A ministry-to-ministry memorandum with India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology covers digital public infrastructure (July 2025).

New investments

The African Development Bank's eGovernment Infrastructure Phase II project is the largest external digital commitment actively disbursing in Lesotho, and runs from 2019 to 2026. It is a loan of XDR 10,110,000, about USD 14 million on a 2026 conversion. It is the one entry on the record that reaches government digital services directly.

Two external digital commitments are captured for Lesotho, both active and both from multilateral lenders: an African Development Bank loan of about USD 14 million for eGovernment infrastructure from 2019, and a World Bank credit of about USD 12 million for public-sector foundations from 2024, in which digital government is one component. No bilateral donor, export credit agency or development finance institution is recorded. The Kobong agreement with Convalt Energy, put at about USD 6.2 billion in June 2026, is an investment envelope conditional on feasibility and financing, not committed money, and is not counted.

Vodacom Lesotho announced a phased network upgrade in March 2026, applying AI across network operations, customer service and human resources. The Kobong hydropower and AI data-centre scheme remains a proposed project value, with construction envisaged only from 2029.

ICT Infrastructure

Connectivity

Infrastructure was Lesotho's worst-performing of the sixteen governance sub-categories in 2023, at 31.1 of 100 and 39th of 54 African states on the Ibrahim Index. Its score moved by half a point over the decade. The public's own view of infrastructure fell 19.2 points over 2014–2023 to 9.7 of 100. Household internet and computer access and broadband affordability scored 22.3 of 100 and ranked 42nd of 54 in 2023. Mobile communications, at 67.6 and 28th, is the strongest of the country's infrastructure measures, though it slipped 1.6 points over the decade.

Mobile broadband penetration stood at 69.1% in 2023, and 83.1% of people aged 15 and over owned a mobile phone. Roughly half of individuals used the internet that year. 4G/LTE population coverage was 85.1% in 2024.

In March 2025 both mobile operators were part-locally held: Econet Telecom Lesotho 70/30 with the government, Vodacom Lesotho 80/20 with the Sekha-Metsi consortium. Econet ran a phased radio-access and transport upgrade across Maseru from May 2025. It grew its 5G footprint from 54 to 149 sites on the 700MHz band by June 2025, and launched a fixed-mobile convergence service that July. Vodacom Lesotho committed $40 million, about M700 million, to a phased network upgrade in March 2026.

Lesotho is landlocked, and in 2018 its international gateways ran through South Africa to the WACS, EASSy and SEACOM submarine cables. The internet exchange point, founded alongside the EASSy connection in 2010/11, was relaunched on 4 February 2026 with new switching and routing, hosting five internet service providers and two content delivery networks. Its public registry record declares 30G of capacity (May 2026). The regulator granted a first satellite internet licence in April 2025. Retail service arrived through an exclusive local reseller that June. Vodacom added a managed enterprise satellite service in August 2026, and launched it at an event in Maseru reported on 9 October 2026.

Data Storage

Lesotho had no operating AI data centre in June 2026. The green-powered facility that would be its first was then at announcement stage. The government and Convalt Energy signed a binding memorandum of agreement on 4 June 2026 for Project Kobong. Its progression depends on feasibility studies, permitting, financing, regulatory approvals and definitive project agreements still to come.

Vodacom Lesotho's service page names two data centres of its own, at Maseru West and Lekokoaneng, which host its business services (August 2026). Commercial hosting runs to small-scale facilities in Maseru (2026). The nearest hyperscale cloud region is in South Africa, where Google sited its first African region in 2022. A national government data centre is a commitment in the National Digital Transformation Strategy 2024–2030, not a facility in service.

The government had three data centres of its own by October 2021, when a contract was awarded to assess them for interoperability and bring the Mohale's Hoek facility into operation. An African Development Bank supervision mission found the Mohale's Hoek facility still unused in September 2024, and at risk of becoming obsolete. In September 2025 the finance ministry awarded a three-year LSL 35.6m contract to replace end-of-life government data-hosting equipment and build a business-continuity framework.

The internet exchange has hosted two content delivery networks since its February 2026 relaunch. The ICT ministry and the United Nations in Lesotho began drafting a National AI Strategy and a Data Centre Blueprint together at a five-day workshop in Maseru in August 2026.

Energy

Access to energy is one of Lesotho's ten most improved governance measures, up 14.6 points over 2014–2023 to 44.9 of 100. It still ranks 23rd of 54 African states. Electricity reached 57.3% of the population in 2023. The national figure hides a wide split: 80.6% of urban households against 37.7% of rural ones in 2021.

In 2024 Lesotho produced well under half the electricity it consumes, importing the rest chiefly from South Africa and Mozambique. What it does generate is almost entirely hydropower. The pumped-storage scheme proposed at Kobong, paired with an AI data centre, would add generation many times the installed base. It is a development framework, not a project under construction.

Firms reported 1.9 electrical outages a month in the World Bank's 2023 Enterprise Survey, fewer than every regional and income-group comparator. The power utility puts supply availability at more than 99% on transmission and around 95% on distribution (August 2026). Tariffs effective April 2025 set the standard domestic rate at M2.08 per kWh, with a lifeline rate of M0.93 for qualifying low-consumption households. Off-grid supply is extending under a dedicated mini-grid regulation and donor-backed portfolios.

In 2025 health workers using the eRegister named electricity outages and network connectivity as significant operational barriers. Vodacom Lesotho powers around a quarter of its base stations with solar (June 2026). SADC's target of 85% regional electricity access by 2030 is being approached from a regional rate of 60% in 2025.

Technical Capacity

The Ibrahim Index's Effective Administration score, which measures the professionalism and capability of Lesotho's civil service, fell 9.2 points over 2014–2023 to 45.4 of 100. It ranked 34th of 54 African states.

The national identity system is being migrated onto the open-source MOSIP platform under a digital ID work plan the ICT ministry signed with UNICEF in 2026. The same ministry is working with the National University of Lesotho and UNICEF to build X-Road as the government's secure inter-agency data exchange layer. Neither platform was operational in 2026. A high-performance computer donated by South Africa was launched at Mohale's Hoek in June 2022, with South Africa's Centre for High-Performance Computing setting it up and training Lesotho's system administrators. In October 2026 the ICT minister said the government is upgrading the high-performance computing infrastructure at the Mohale's Hoek data centre to support AI. No specification, cost or date was given.

The Auditor-General's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, launched on 8 August 2026 and costed at about M339 million, names digitising the office's own business processes among its priorities. It commits the office to expanded IT-focused specialised audits. The Land Administration Authority put its 2026–2029 strategic planning out to tender in August 2025. Its terms of reference name the adoption of new technology for land registration and cadastral services.

Cybersecurity

Lesotho launched an interim national Computer Security Incident Response Team in August 2026. It is interim because it operates ahead of the Cybersecurity Bill that would give it statutory powers. At the launch the government said it had appointed its first Chief Information Security Officer and was setting up a national Security Operations Centre. The Bill was still at stakeholder consultation in September 2026, its second round after a draft circulated in August, with the communications authority designated as the interim response team. The companion Cybercrime Bill was awaiting National Assembly approval as at August 2026.

The government warned the public in August 2025 about AI-generated deepfake videos impersonating King Letsie III and the Prime Minister, with no dedicated cyber law to enforce against them. The ICT ministry's principal secretary tied rising AI-enabled threats to the stalled bill in January 2026. No cross-government data-sharing legislation was in force in 2024.

In 2020 no comprehensive, independent or recurring security audit or penetration-testing regime covered the national identity system as a whole. Its interface access was controlled by IP whitelisting and passphrases. The country sits in the evolving tier of the ITU Global Cybersecurity Index for 2024.

In May 2026 a Mauritius-based firm and a local partner ran an executive workshop in Maseru on ransomware, cloud security, data sovereignty and immutable backup. International vendors supplied the content. SADC's Committee of Central Bank Governors approved frameworks on cybersecurity, climate-related financial risk and cross-border crisis management in July 2026.

DPI

Data Exchange

In 2024 Lesotho had no operational whole-of-government interoperability platform. The ICT ministry is working with the National University of Lesotho and UNICEF to stand up X-Road as the government's secure inter-agency exchange layer. The design work is done with the university's Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, not a commercial vendor. X-Road, the MOSIP identity platform and a decision-support system are treated as one stack (February 2026). On 20–23 October 2026 the ministry and UNICEF held an X-Road pilot planning workshop in Berea. It assessed ministries' readiness for the National Data Exchange and picked child-focused use cases. No go-live date has been published.

In December 2023 the national identity register offered verification to a limited number of public and private bodies, often through manual data sharing and with no interoperability framework in place. Users included banks, mobile network operators and insurers, and checks covered public servants, civil pensioners and social-programme beneficiaries. Company incorporation details lodged through the One-Stop Business Facilitation Centre are shared automatically with Revenue Services Lesotho (2025). In March 2025 it reported it had integrated with five institutions to verify registration data and automate PAYE compliance, with two more at an advanced stage.

In 2024 there was no automated link between the revenue authority and the National Identity and Civil Registry. The electoral commission and Home Affairs had not established one between the voters' roll and the identity register. The social registry's links to identity and birth registration were weak in 2021. The National Single Window, launched in August 2026 under a World Bank-financed project and run by the revenue authority, operates without its own law, whose drafting has only begun (September 2026).

A workshop opened in August 2025 to develop a Digital Public Infrastructure Framework. A UNICEF–government session on 1 April 2026 called for interoperability across the identity, civil registration, education and health databases. Across SADC, cross-border identity exchange runs on bilateral deals between financial service providers and registries. An assessment for the Committee of Central Bank Governors calls them unmanageable as their number grows (June 2026).

Digital Identity and CRVS

A government freeze on refugee identity cards and travel documents, imposed in July 2025 pending a corruption inquiry into two officials, entered its second year in August 2026. It applies to the whole refugee community, not only to the individuals charged. The 2011 Act limited the card to people aged 16 and over, and among non-citizens to holders of an indefinite residence permit. A 2022 amendment brought refugees into the register.

The register has run nationwide since July 2013 through 15 fixed enrolment centres and 10 mobile units. It takes ten fingerprints and a portrait from every applicant, on cards carrying a 2D barcode and no chip. Identity and civil registration are one department working one back-end database, with a single lifetime number from birth certificate to death certificate. In September 2025 the home affairs minister put card holding at around 90% of people aged 16 and over, and the birth registration rate at 61% in 2023 against 21% in 2019. Those left out are disproportionately in remote rural areas. The card is the sole legally recognised proof of identity under section 16 of the 2011 Act. It gates passports, driving licences, welfare, bank accounts and SIM registration. A first card is free, but renewals and replacements carry fees.

By September 2024 the department had issued no identity cards for over a year, and the government turned to a manufacturer dealing with it directly. On 30 December 2024 it ended the 2012 contract with the Israeli firm that had built and maintained the civil registration, identity, e-passport and border systems. The firm is contesting the termination with a US$2.24m claim (October 2025).

The ministry and UNICEF signed a work plan on 26 February 2026 to migrate onto the open-source MOSIP platform. Home Affairs held a five-day workshop from 29 June 2026 on a modernisation framework and systems architecture design. The registry has set a 2026–2030 roadmap for nationwide digital birth registration. In July 2026 Home Affairs and Police officials spent four days in Windhoek studying Namibia's integrated civil registration and identity management.

Digital Payments and Fintech

Vodacom Financial Services and STANLIB Lesotho launched Tsetela, a regulated investment product, through the M-Pesa wallet in Maseru on 22 July 2026. It takes individuals from M50 and group-savings customers from M300, with balances, daily interest and withdrawals run over USSD on *200#. It is set apart from the stokvel and Mokhatlo channels it is pitched against by regulation: a licensed manager, segregated funds, an annual audit and an independent trustee.

LeSwitch, the Central Bank's national payment switch, piloted instant payments in October 2022, launched in March 2024, added ATM transactions in October 2024 and point-of-sale in May 2025. On 11 May 2025 the central bank and four commercial banks moved local card payments onto it. A card from any participating bank now works on any participating terminal, and the transaction clears inside the country. Wholesale cross-border settlement runs through the SADC real-time gross settlement system. Low-value transfers with South Africa, Namibia and Eswatini stopped being treated as domestic South African payments on 30 September 2024. A Reserve Bank directive requires them to leave the SADC-RTGS for the TCIB retail scheme by 31 March 2027.

A government e-payment gateway was being developed under the African Development Bank's second e-government infrastructure project in December 2023. In September 2026 the gateway was still being developed, now by the Central Bank of Lesotho for integration with the national single window. The working business-to-government rail is the revenue authority's platform launched on 14 March 2023. It was extended by a payment gateway with Standard Lesotho Bank on 10 February 2026 covering that one bank.

In January 2025 social assistance was moving from cash delivered by cash-in-transit firms to mobile payments through selected network operators, with problems in the transition acknowledged. The Child Grants Programme had piloted M-Pesa and EcoCash payments, with evaluations putting cash delivery at 1.5 to 7 times the cost of mobile (2021). Two-thirds of adults held a mobile money account in 2021. The Payment Systems Act 2014 still governs, and its replacement is a bill before Parliament.

Registries

In 2023 Lesotho's social registry held data on more than 90% of the country's households. The National Information System for Social Assistance is a web-based registry and management information system built between 2014 and 2016 with UNICEF and EU support. Its links to the identity and birth registration databases were weak in 2021. A refresh of household data last collected in 2017 was scheduled for December 2025 to March 2026, with World Bank support in eight districts.

Digital birth registration launched under the Ntlafatsa Bana programme in April 2025 with EU and UNICEF funding. It runs on tablets carried by village health workers and civil registration officers, feeding the social assistance database, in selected community councils (2026). In the mountains registration is still predominantly paper, delivered by outreach and constrained by transport (January 2026). A civil registry paper put birth registration completeness at 66.1% in 2022, against 2.8% in 2008. It found the registration law makes no provision for digitised registration.

A single Land Administration Authority has held registration, cadastre, mapping and surveying since 2010. The 2016 census found 20% of households on registered leaseholds, 41% on unregistered certificates of allocation and 39% on neither. Leases, bonds and deeds are online through the Landfolio-based Land Administration Online System in business hours (2025).

There is no national address register under the 2011 regulations. In September 2026 the communications regulator studied Kenya's National Addressing System and its legal framework. In October 2026 a World Bank urban and land team told the ICT ministry it is interested in supporting a nationwide digital address system. No commitment or amount was stated.

In November 2025 a single window was deployed at the One-Stop Business Facilitation Centre. The Registrar of Companies runs an online registry with free public search, and incorporation data passes straight to the revenue authority. Beneficial owners above a 10% threshold must be filed with the Registrar within seven days under regulations of 2024. The electoral commission's Voter Registration Strategy was endorsed on 28 May 2026, and constituency-readiness assessments were under way in June 2026.

Sectoral management information systems

Lesotho's electronic patient register was piloted at four health centres and two hospitals in 2018, as the first stage of a planned rollout across 178 government facilities. It sends facility-level data automatically into the district health information system. It covers five programmes, among them tuberculosis, antenatal care and cervical cancer. Paper recording is to be phased out only when it is fully operational nationwide. In 2025 health workers reported generally positive views of the system, with network outages and power cuts the operational barrier. An AI chest X-ray tool, CAD4TB, is in use in tuberculosis screening programmes (2025). The HIV programme had 231 facilities reporting through the platform, the reporting rate falling from 97% in January to 95% in September 2025.

The Ministry of Education and Training began building a national education management information system on DHIS2 in September 2025, covering 4,819 institutions, with core ministry staff trained in March 2026. By mid-2026 the system reached ministry and district level only, and school-level capture remained on paper. Rural primary schools return Annual School Census forms that district offices verify and key in by hand over months.

Lesotho launched a police digital transformation policy and strategy, with an ICT initiative equipping stations for digital incident reporting, in April 2026. Stations across the country are to get desktop computers for case monitoring. Road accidents began moving off the manual LMPS 29 form with training of Thaba-Tseka police on the Road Accident Management System in February 2026. High Court rules gazetted in June 2024 make filing through an e-justice system mandatory for represented parties from a date still to be set. In July 2026 the Chief Justice said the judiciary is now introducing electronic filing with the ICT ministry.

Both fiscal administration indicators dropped by around 18 points over 2014–2023: tax and revenue mobilisation to 73.4 of 100, and budgetary and financial management to 44.4. Revenue Services Lesotho registers individuals and businesses under a single taxpayer number used across income tax, customs and excise, and VAT (2023). Invoice-level electronic reporting gained its legal basis in March 2026.

Other GovTech and e-Gov

Lesotho began a pilot of a citizen-facing digital service-identification system on 2 April 2026, with roughly 10,000 participants. No vendor, legal basis or evaluation criteria has been published (August 2026). In 2026 there was no centralised government portal offering public-facing identity authentication. Authentication happens only through agency-to-agency interfaces, with mobile and cross-service authentication targeted as the MOSIP rollout progresses.

The revenue authority's e-filing and e-payment platforms went live in 2023. Taxpayers can file and amend company, individual, trust, VAT and PAYE returns and see real-time balances on the e-filing portal (2025). Only Standard Lesotho Bank's Unayo posts payments immediately; payments from other banks need proof of payment uploaded. In 2024 no other government service was operationally integrated into a data exchange platform. A government-wide e-services platform covering Home Affairs, Public Service, Public Works and Transport and Finance cleared validation of its first phase in May 2026, with links to the ministries' back-end systems to follow in stages.

In May 2025 the Auditor-General gave an adverse opinion on the 2022/23 consolidated accounts, finding the IFMIS expenditure of eight ministries M140 million below the statements. In September 2026 a draft National Data Value Creation Blueprint was validated at a national workshop in Maseru with Smart Africa and GIZ. Lesotho is the first country in Africa to validate one. It has not been adopted, and its text has not been published. At the launch of the interim CSIRT in August 2026, officials said the government plans to move ICT services out of the civil service into a separate agency.

The Lehokela crime-reporting application, built with UNDP, the National University of Lesotho and Econet, was launched nationally in April 2026 after a multi-year pilot, with phased rollout to police stations from June 2026. A Digital Outreach Studio opened under Phase II of the African Development Bank-backed e-Government Infrastructure project (June 2026). That phase is designed to improve access to reliable digital services for rural and unserved citizens. The UN's 2024 E-Government Survey places Lesotho 145th of 193 countries.

Digitalisation

Digitalisation of sub-national government

In April 2026 the information minister told the UN Resident Coordinator that digital ambassadors are deployed across local councils to help communities with internet access and computer literacy. No council count has been published. In January 2018 the communications minister had said his ministry would connect Tenesolo Community Council's office to the internet, as part of a programme to connect community councils.

In April 2025 a construction permit system for the Maseru City Council was live, built by a Lesotho firm with a Kenyan partner. Architects apply online, upload documents, track review stages and pay, and the council issues permits digitally. No programme to digitalise the administration of the districts and community councils as a whole has been found.

Rural digital data capture

In May 2026 Lesotho's Department of Agricultural Research was validating agronomic content for an FAO-backed artificial-intelligence extension chatbot. The work sits under project TCP/LES/4002 of the Digital Villages Initiative, aimed at a pilot in Berea and Mafeteng. T-Connect handed over the country's first Starlink-served community hotspots at Matsieng, Thaba Bosiu and Mokhotlong in September 2025, through the principal chiefs. It extended the same priority connectivity to schools and clinics from Butha-Buthe in January 2026. A National Farmers Portal launched in August 2026, placing every farmer in a single digital registry.

In 2026 rural primary schools recorded enrolment, attendance and administrative data entirely on paper. District offices verify and key in the annual census forms by hand. The education information system on DHIS2 had reached only ministry and district level. Staff at facilities using the electronic patient register kept paper records alongside it (2024). Police stations are to be equipped with desktop computers for case monitoring (2026). In the mountain districts civil registration depends on transport to move applications between councils and the district office (January 2026).

In 2026 tablet-based birth registration operated in selected community councils through village health workers. The electronic patient register covers five programmes, among them tuberculosis, antenatal care and cervical cancer. The Lehokela community-policing platform went national in April 2026, with rollout to stations phased from June.

The rural economy scored 61.9 of 100 in 2023 and ranked 27th of 54 African states, up 3.0 points over 2014–2023. Rural economy support rose 23.7 points to 68.7, the third most improved of Lesotho's 96 indicators. Rural market access fell 11.7 points to 28.3 of 100 and 35th of 54.

Technology

AI

In 2026 no artificial intelligence or advanced analytics ran inside Lesotho's government data systems for data quality assurance, fraud detection or prediction. The decision support system meant to let ministries analyse data for planning and budgeting is a concept, with no timeline, funding or adoption. In October 2025 the revenue authority said it had built artificial intelligence into its customer service.

The responsible minister signed an AI policy in June 2026, with broadband infrastructure sharing and data management policies. The final text, posted on 17 August 2026, leaves AI risk regulation to existing sectoral regulators and carries an implementation plan to 2030. Drafting of a national AI strategy and a data centre blueprint began at a Maseru workshop with the United Nations in August 2026. In September 2026 the prime minister told the UN's Digital Cooperation Day that Lesotho is the first country to implement the UN AI Blueprint, and that Phase One of a National Compute Roadmap 2026–2029 has begun. The roadmap has not been published.

An AI chest X-ray reader is in use in tuberculosis screening (2025). The Department of Agricultural Research is validating agronomic content for an FAO-backed extension chatbot, ahead of a pilot in Berea and Mafeteng (May 2026). Econet applies AI in network operations with its 700MHz 5G expansion (June 2025). Vodacom does so in a phased network upgrade (March 2026). MICSTI, UNDP and the National University of Lesotho are compiling a Sesotho word bank so systems can be trained on the language (February 2026). Researchers have published SeSoDa, an open Sesotho-English dataset of 1,966 prompt-completion pairs (2025). They fine-tuned a small language model on it.

The foreign minister signed the agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization in Shanghai on 20 July 2026, making Lesotho one of 29 founding members. Econet and UNDP renewed a memorandum covering AI adoption and a proposed national AI academy (July 2026). In June 2026 the country had no operating AI data centre.

ICT Industry

In April 2025 Vodacom appealed the Starlink licence, asking that a local shareholding requirement be imposed on it. Both mobile operators carry local shareholdings: Econet Telecom Lesotho 70/30 with the government, Vodacom Lesotho 80/20 with the Sekha-Metsi consortium. The satellite operator is wholly foreign-owned.

Lesotho scored 41.7 of 100 on business and competition regulation in 2023, 43rd of 54 African states, after gaining 5.5 points across 2014–2023.

Econet ran a phased radio-access and transport network upgrade across Maseru under the banner "Re le Utloile" in May 2025. It launched Wi-Fi Plus, combining fixed wireless and mobile access on a single subscription, in July 2025. Its financial-services subsidiary migrated the EcoCash mobile-money platform onto a new system with stokvel and burial-society integration, payroll handling and stronger fraud detection (October 2024). Vodacom, the larger of the two, marked thirty years in the market in June 2026. In May 2026 a Mauritius-based firm and a local partner ran an executive workshop in Maseru on ransomware, cloud security, data sovereignty and immutable backup, with four foreign vendors.

Innovation ecosystem

Lesotho ranks 132nd of 139 economies in the 2025 Global Innovation Index. The state-backed Lesotho Entrepreneurship Hub and Seed Financing Facility, supported by a World Bank-funded project, targets 500 incubated enterprises by 2028 (2024).

A Digital Outreach Studio opened under the second phase of the African Development Bank-backed e-government infrastructure project in June 2026. A second digital studio broke ground at Makoanyane Square in Maseru on 11 June 2026, under the same project and the university innovation pod programme. It is not yet in operation.

In May 2026 Geddit, a digital-address platform assigning building-level codes and GPS pins, drew recognition at the University of Notre Dame's McCloskey New Venture Founders Summit. It operates commercially in Lesotho.

Capacity

Literacy

A national household survey by the Lesotho Communications Authority and the Bureau of Statistics, on ITU methodology, put 35.1% of individuals at basic or above-basic ICT skills in 2023. That splits into 19.4% at basic level and 15.7% above it (2023).

Human Resources in Education was Lesotho's fourth best-scoring Ibrahim Index indicator in 2023, at 95.1 of 100 and seventh of 54, after a rise of 17.1 points over 2014–2023. Equality in Education fell 14.2 points over 2014–2023 to 66.4 of 100, one of the country's steepest declines, though still tenth of 54. Education Completion rose 22.8 points to 60.9 of 100, 14th of 54. Education Enrolment fell 3.3 points to 37.3, 33rd of 54. The Education sub-category as a whole scores 59.3 of 100, 17th of 54, up 2.9 points over the decade.

In 2026 rural primary schools captured enrolment, attendance and administrative data on paper Annual School Census forms, sent to district offices for manual verification and entry. The cycle can run for months. T-Connect Impact, a Starlink connectivity programme for schools and clinics, launched at Soofia International School in Butha-Buthe in January 2026.

Training and skills

Lesotho's first digital-entrepreneurship and AI-tools training, an AI Skills Development Programme run with the US Embassy and its American Corner, launched in June 2026 under Prime Minister Matekane's Sebabatso initiative. The National Digital Policy 2024–2035 is the standing national instrument on digital skills training. In October 2026 the government said efforts were under way to establish the Smart Africa Digital Academy in the country. No host institution, budget or opening date was stated.

In August 2025 Vodacom Lesotho committed to free digital-skills training with Amazon Web Services. The National University of Lesotho's Faculty of Science and Technology offers BSc degrees in computer science, information systems and electronics, and BEng degrees in computer systems and networks. Econet Telecom Lesotho and UNDP renewed their partnership in Maseru on 28 July 2026. The renewal includes a pillar covering the University Innovation Pod and a proposed National AI Academy. No timetable, governance structure, curriculum or host institution has been named for that academy.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport began training police in Thaba-Tseka in February 2026 to record road accidents digitally. The system replaces the manual LMPS 29 form. Incubation capacity sits in the government-backed Lesotho Entrepreneurship Hub and Seed Financing Facility, the National University's Innovation Hub at Roma and Vodacom's Software Factory Hub in Maseru.

Research institutions

The SADC Executive Secretary told the Council of Ministers in Durban on 12 August 2026 that no member state meets either the regional 1%-of-GDP or the African Union's 1.5%-of-GDP target for research-and-development investment. Lesotho sits near the foot of the 2025 Global Innovation Index. A Research and Innovation Policy meant to turn research into commercial products was undergoing approval in October 2026.

The ICT ministry is designing the country's digital public infrastructure with the National University of Lesotho's Department of Mathematics and Computer Science (February 2026). It treats X-Road for data exchange, MOSIP for digital ID and a Decision Support System for planning and budgeting as one stack. The university also teaches BSc programmes in computer science, information systems and electronics and BEng programmes in computer systems and networks. MICSTI, UNDP and the university are compiling a Sesotho-language word bank to train locally relevant AI systems (February 2026).

A Botho University conference paper of December 2025 proposes a Lesotho-specific AI diagnostic model for tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and non-communicable disease. It is at the research and design stage.

Inclusion

Access to services

Enrolment in the digital service-identification pilot begun in April 2026 requires a valid national identity document and a registered SIM card before biometric registration. Anyone holding neither starts outside it, and no route in has been published. The national identity register's coverage is incomplete, and those missing from it are concentrated in remote rural areas. The identity card is the sole legally recognised proof of identity, required for welfare, financial-sector checks and SIM registration alike. Refugees have been unable to obtain identity or travel documents since a freeze imposed in July 2025.

The Persons with Disability Equity Act 2021 establishes a Persons with Disability Advisory Council. It gives persons with disabilities a right to receive public information in an accessible format. It obliges providers of public telephone services and hosts of public information broadcasts to accommodate them. A 4G-enabled digital library and Insight Centre, with computers, a Braille printer and eye-tracking equipment, opened at the State Library in Maseru in March 2026. The country's first Disability Accessibility Conference, convened in October 2025 with the Lesotho National Federation of Organisations of the Disabled, called for investment in accessible technologies.

Equal access to public services scored 55.9 of 100 in 2023, twelfth of 54 African states and 5.1 points up on 2014. The police's Lehokela reporting app launched nationally in April 2026. The Office of the Ombudsman went to market in October 2025 for an electronic complaints management system, to replace recording complaints by hand. The executive's National Contact Centre routes citizen complaints to each ministry's public relations officers, who are to report back within three days (2024). T-Connect Impact began Starlink connections for schools and clinics at Soofia International School in Butha-Buthe in January 2026. A Commonwealth Digital Roadshow in Maseru in April 2026 gave gender a dedicated Women in Digital Forum alongside its policy and youth tracks.

Digital divides

In 2023, 59.9% of men used the internet against 54.8% of women, a gap of 5.1 percentage points. Mobile phone ownership separated the sexes by 0.6 points. Most of the rural population was offline in 2025. Household electricity access is far lower in rural areas than in towns (2021). Household internet and computer access is one of the country's weakest measures against African peers (2023). Basic digital skills reach only a minority of individuals (2023).

In July 2026 the ICT ministry's Principal Secretary said Lesotho has full broadband coverage but only about half the population regularly uses it, and named affordable connectivity as the need. Econet's Hoa Khonahala campaign, running from October 2025, priced entry data at M10 a day, M25 a week and M165 for 4GB a month. T-Connect launched Starlink resale in June 2025 with session-based access at M10. In February 2024 the regulator set out what makes internet service affordable in Lesotho, naming the frameworks in place, what it expects of providers and what is unresolved.

The Community Digital Inclusion Model passed from a UNDP-run pilot to the Universal Service Fund in November 2025. Universal and meaningful connectivity is the regulator's leading strategic theme for 2026–2029, financed through that fund. The first Starlink community hotspots were handed over through the principal chiefs in September 2025.

Data

National statistics

An independent peer review by the African Union's statistics institute, on mission in August 2025, found Lesotho's National Statistical Council non-operational. The Bureau of Statistics Act 2001 remains the operative statistics law. A fourth Statistics Bill was in drafting when the review reported in December 2025. Three earlier attempts to replace the Act had not reached enactment.

On the World Bank's Statistical Performance Indicators for 2023, a population census, an agricultural census and a business establishment census all fell within the preceding ten years. Three or more household surveys and three or more agricultural surveys ran over the same period, against two health surveys and one labour survey. The weakest of the five pillars is data services, in the 20–49% band (2023). Data use scores 70–89%, and data products, sources and infrastructure 50–69% (2023). Lesotho's Capacity of the Statistical System score rose 5.0 points over 2014–2023 to 52.3 of 100, 24th of 54 African states.

The seventh population census, with a reference night of 11 April 2026, had finished enumeration by September, with mop-up operations still running in Maseru. In September 2026 a second phase of financial access points mapping was put out to tender. It is to update the 2018 baseline into a geo-referenced database of every access point across the ten districts.

In 2021 civil registration and the national identity system were run by one department on a shared back-end database. Birth and death registration modules are connected to the National Identity Register, though vital events were not yet digitised nationwide.

Open data

In 2024 natural resources data was the lowest-scoring category in Open Data Watch's inventory, at 2.5 out of ten. Energy and pollution scored 3, and agriculture and the built environment 4. No economic statistics category exceeds six: balance of payments and money and banking each score 6, prices 5.5, labour 4.5, trade and national accounts 4 and government finance 3.5. No social category scores above five. Population data scores 5, food security and gender 4.5, and poverty, education outcomes and reproductive health 4. Education and health facilities score 3.5, and crime 3.

Across the 23 categories assessed in that inventory, the median category score is four (2024).

In 2023 Disclosure of Public Records scored 35.2 of 100, and Accessibility of Public Records 28.4, 21st and 31st of 54 African states. Each rose around two points over 2014–2023. Accountability & Transparency is the second worst-performing governance sub-category, at 37.7 of 100, 28th of 54 and down 5.8 points over the decade.

In 2024 the Central Bank of Lesotho reported payment system performance annually through its statutory annual report and financial stability report, and kept quarterly monitoring internal. It puts the rules governing its payment systems online as free downloads, with no registration or request. Audited statements from licensed electronic payment issuers go to the regulator and are not published. The Bureau of Statistics posts its reports as free downloads, among them the 2024 labour force survey indicators, the 2023 national accounts and the consumer price index to March 2025 (2025).

Use of satellite data

In the 2025/26 season a satellite weather-index insurance pilot covered more than US$1.2 million of maize and beans in Mafeteng, Mohale's Hoek and Quthing. The agriculture ministry ran it with the World Food Programme. The African Risk Capacity supplied satellite-based monitoring and the calculation of payouts. The insurer LNIG Hollard plans to sell the cover to smallholders in all ten districts, with enrolment from 1 November 2026.

In June 2026 FEWS NET's food security outlook for Lesotho drew on satellite rainfall and other remote-sensing indicators to assess crop conditions and drought. A public land cover dashboard built from satellite imagery since 2017 serves the ReNOKA catchment programme at national, district and community council level (March 2026). It rests on an FAO land cover monitoring system that produced five standardised annual maps for 2017–2021 (2022).

In May 2023 the government launched a GIS platform in the prime minister's office with the World Food Programme, layering climate, soil, poverty and other data for planning. A consultancy's project page describes a nowcasting system for the weather service that integrates satellite data with observations and model output (2026). In September 2025 the meteorological observing network was described as fragmented across more than ninety stations, with modernisation help under way for multi-hazard early warning.

Officials told a national planning meeting in May 2026 that the country has no policy guiding spatial data. They proposed a National Spatial Development Infrastructure Policy to fill the gap.

Geopolitics

US / hyperscaler activities

Lesotho signed a binding memorandum of agreement with the US firm Convalt Energy on 4 June 2026. It sets out Project Kobong as a joint renewable energy and digital infrastructure development. Its progression depends on feasibility studies, permitting, financing, regulatory approvals and definitive project agreements still to be concluded.

Starlink received the country's first satellite internet licence in April 2025. The rights group Section 2 formally opposed the application because the operator is wholly foreign-owned (March 2025). Lesotho's precedent had required Basotho participation in telecommunications operators. The United States announced a 50% tariff on Lesotho's exports in the same month the licence was decided (April 2025). By the July 2025 launch the objections had moved from ownership to sovereignty. Critics said a company with no physical address in the country would be carrying Basotho traffic.

An AI Skills Development Programme run with the US Embassy and its American Corner launched in June 2026, under the Prime Minister's Sebabatso initiative. It is the country's first digital-entrepreneurship and AI-tools training of its kind.

China activities

Lesotho's Minister of Foreign Affairs signed the agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization in Shanghai on 20 July 2026. Lesotho is one of its 29 founding member countries. The foreign ministry signed, not the ICT ministry.

EU activities

The EU Delegation launched digital birth registration with the government and UNICEF in April 2025, under the Ntlafatsa Bana programme. The social registry was built between 2014 and 2016 with UNICEF and EU support. No dedicated EU digital-cooperation programme, financing instrument or bilateral digital agreement with Lesotho had been found as at September 2026.

Gulf/UAE activities

In June 2026 the ICT minister pitched Lesotho to Dubai Chambers as a data-centre site, citing its water resources for cooling. No Gulf investment, financing or cooperation agreement in Lesotho's digital sector had been found as at September 2026.

India activities

Lesotho signed a ministry-to-ministry memorandum of understanding on digital public infrastructure with India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology on 29 July 2025. Minister Nthati Moorosi signed during a Maseru visit by India's Minister of State for External Affairs, Shri Pabitra Margherita. No deliverable, workstream or funding under it has been found.

Digital sovereignty

Every ministry and council runs its finances on an Epicor-based IFMIS in place since 2009. The finance ministry is upgrading it from Epicor 10.2 to Epicor Kinetic to ensure continued vendor support (2026). Nothing found by September 2026 shows whether the state holds the IFMIS source code and operating skills. Nor does anything show whether it can exit its Epicor contract. The ICT ministry is designing X-Road and the open-source MOSIP identity platform with the National University of Lesotho, not a commercial vendor (February 2026).

The January 2025 draft of the Data Management Policy set out to regulate hosting services, prioritise cloud-based solutions for government systems and define rules for cross-border transfers that respect national data sovereignty. It was aligned with the AU Data Policy Framework at a validation workshop in October 2025. The minister signed it in June 2026, with no publication or commencement date stated (August 2026).

In May 2026 the communications minister credited a UN-India fund's upgrade of the state's high-performance computing with laying the foundation for Lesotho to host, process and govern its own digital public infrastructure. At the same meeting she pitched the country to hyperscalers as a data-centre destination. The Convalt Energy agreement's terms on data jurisdiction, portability and exit for the Kobong AI data centre had not been published by September 2026.
