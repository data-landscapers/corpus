Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national system was launched in December 2024, with 122 data managers trained across 51 health departments; clinics are not shown entering their own reports.

Governance, planning, finance. Decree 0292/PR/MS of 21 July 2024 charges the Ministry of Health's planning directorate-general with collecting, processing and disseminating health information nationwide. The national health development plan adopted on 23 February 2024 sets a DHIS2 platform integrating all health programmes for the end of December 2028, and costs its health information axis at 3,509,631,187 FCFA. DHIS2 was launched nationally on 6 December 2024 with financial and technical support from WHO Gabon and the Global Fund, as HISP Rwanda reported.

Tiers in use. WHO Gabon states that at its December 2024 launch DHIS2 integrated all functional public health facilities, with private facilities to follow in 2025.

Where data is digitised. WHO Gabon reports that those trained to use DHIS2 in 2024 were 122 data managers and 10 regional health directors, from all 51 health departments and 10 regions.

Last twelve months. WHO Gabon directed its 2025 support to data quality, reporting completeness and timeliness, training more than 76 professionals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The 2024-2028 plan provides for training data managers at the three levels of the health pyramid.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 Tracker for hypertension and diabetes follow-up. Single-function: the DHIS2 logistics indicators and dashboards, the SIMR weekly notification of deaths in hospitals, ODK Compagnon, ESPEN Collect, the SRMNIA scorecard and, planned for 2024 to 2028, the e-SIMR notification platform and computerised management tools for medicines and health products. Enabling: tele-echography under eGabon-SIS in four health departments of Ngounié, telemedicine in six selected medical centres and the 2024-2028 plan's tele-echography pilot.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and no source names the tiers that report. The published completeness figures disagree, and no dated event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A national patient record ran at 13 pilot sites in greater Libreville in February 2026, two of them health centres.

Governance, planning, finance. The state digital agency ANINF implements the health information system Gabon calls eGabon-SIS, with a project technical committee following it, as Gabonactu reported on 25 October 2025. Its supplier won a tender launched by the Ministry of Health, the contract handed over on 16 February 2024. The World Bank approved a US$56 million loan on 3 June 2016, US$40.08 million of it for the national health information system. The project was officially closed on 24 June 2026.

Tiers in use. On 11 February 2026 the system worked in 13 pilot sites across Grand Libreville, the Egypt-Gabon cooperation hospital among them, with the Libreville university hospital and the regional hospitals not yet covered, on Gabonactu's account.

Where data is digitised. On 23 October 2025 the chief doctor of the Lalala health centre in Libreville said a patient can now be found in different facilities under a unique identifier.

Last twelve months. On 11 February 2026 the health minister asked the World Bank, which agreed, to bring the system into the Libreville university hospital. At the project's closing on 24 June 2026 the authorities named maintenance, cybersecurity, training and extension to the whole territory as the next priorities.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Two health centres, Lalala and Glass, were confirmed using the system on 23 October 2025, among 13 pilot sites in Grand Libreville.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 Tracker for hypertension and diabetes follow-up. Single-function: the DHIS2 logistics indicators and dashboards, the SIMR weekly notification of deaths in hospitals, ODK Compagnon, ESPEN Collect, the SRMNIA scorecard and, planned for 2024 to 2028, the e-SIMR notification platform and computerised management tools for medicines and health products. Enabling: tele-echography under eGabon-SIS in four health departments of Ngounié, telemedicine in six selected medical centres and the 2024-2028 plan's tele-echography pilot.

Not held. No count of primary clinics using the system and no denominator is held, and use at a national or referral hospital is not established. Whether records are entered at the point of care is not stated, and no plan naming the system or domestic budget line is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

728 schools managed pupils' marks in a vendor's application in 2024; the share of primary schools is not known, as no total is given.

Governance, planning, finance. The learner identification is run by SOSUP, a state body, and announced by the ministry's Secretary-General as compulsory, on 4 November 2025. Xgest belongs to the start-up Groupe Tereza, with the State its client, as at 27 September 2024. On 24 September 2024 the ministry closed pupil enrolment on X-Gest after irregularities and ordered that no personal data pass through it.

Levels in use. The identification for 2025/2026 covers public and private pre-primary, primary and secondary schools, the Secretary-General stated on 4 November 2025. The World Bank's 2019 diagnostic put X-Gest in 92 secondary schools, expanding to the remaining secondary schools and to primary schools.

How the record is kept. Schools managed pupils' numbers and marks and published results in Xgest, on Gabonreview's account of 27 September 2024, and enrolments and re-enrolments for 2024-2025 were made on it. The 2025/2026 identification is made online once for the school year.

Last twelve months. On 4 November 2025 the ministry opened a second phase of school identification, announced the online learner identification for 2025/2026 and barred pupils of unidentified schools from it. Focus Groupe Media reported in March 2026 that SOSUP had been deploying eFormation, giving every establishment and every pupil a unique identifier.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Gabonreview reported on 27 September 2024 that Xgest is used by 728 schools, a count of schools of all levels together. The ministry posts the list of schools registered for the identification on SOSUP's site.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: KEWA, which publishes primary certificate results online, the ministry's examination management database and its school assets database. Learning: the distance education system. Enabling: the tablets and interactive screens delivered in 2025-2026, the tablets for seven primary schools of Ogooué-Ivindo, the 1,000 tablets and 148 connected schools of the national strategy and the connected multimedia rooms and digital classrooms.

Not held. The 728 schools come with no total and no level of schooling, so no share of primary schools is held. Whether the school or the family makes the yearly identification entry, how many schools are in the register, and any plan or budget for either system are not stated.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

No office registered births or deaths digitally in May 2026; a system is being prepared with funding to 2028; its first offices are not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Law 008/2022 of 15 June 2022 makes the Centre National de l'Etat Civil (CNEC) the public service that builds the digital, biometric and centralised civil register and directs the civil status centres. The World Bank's Digital Gabon Project keeps US$10.5 million for digitalising civil registration, in a project closing on 30 June 2028 (June 2026).

Events registered digitally. No digital registration of births or deaths is in service: the Gabon Digital general coordinator said in September 2026 that the digital platforms for managing civil registration come second, after the decisions on the rules for attributing identity.

How the record is made. Civil registration runs on manual procedures, which the coordinator said the reform should break with (September 2026). The World Bank described the civil registry as entirely paper-based in June 2021.

Last twelve months. The project's technical assistance was recruited in April 2026 and a mapping exercise to prioritise the departments whose offices will register digitally was under way at 31 May 2026. On 28 September 2026 an inter-ministerial workshop opened at Ntoum to define the conditions for putting the CNEC into operation.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The World Bank reports no administrative department with a civil registration office digitally registering vital acts at 31 May 2026, against a target of nine departments by June 2028.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the planned mapping of civil registry archives and feasibility study for digitising them. Certificate: Akiba, Poste S.A.'s application, announced on 9 October 2026. Outreach: the joint programme « Citoyenneté et Protection sociale » in Ngounié province. Identity: the planned Registre biométrique des personnes physiques and Système national d'identité digitale; the Numéro d'identification personnel; the Carte nationale d'identité électronique; and the biometric database IBOGA. Enabling: the state fibre network Réseau de l'Administration Gabonaise at Oyem; the universal service programme for mobile and internet coverage; and the planned kits, connectivity and building renovations for civil registration offices. Exchange: the planned interoperability of civil registration with the biometric population register, and the common biometric reference meant to connect the three identity projects.

Not held. No pilot office is named, and the count held is of departments, with no total, not of offices. No domestic budget line is stated, and the CNEC is not yet in operation.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A vendor presented a case-file system design in January 2026; whether any force uses it is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the digital driving licence, launched on 24 March 2026 with enrolment suspended in September 2026, the harmonised register of places of detention, at its validation workshop on 14 July 2026, the standardised custody register, a paper model presented in February 2025, and the digitalisation of road transport documents, noted as operational by the Council of Ministers on 29 January 2026. Identification: the national police's Fichier Automatisé de Recherche d'Identités et d'Antécédents Judiciaires, a project presented on 16 February 2026. Personnel: the Système Informatisé du Tableau d'Avancement, presented the same day. Public: the gendarmerie's Identity Secu mobile application, deployed at the PK9 brigade in Libreville on 8 May 2026. Enabling: the state network RAG, connecting the Oyem commissariat by April 2026, and the computer equipment given to the PK9 brigade.

Not held. The only records system held is a vendor's design presented at a Libreville seminar on 10 January 2026, with no adoption by the police, the gendarmerie or a ministry stated and no station using it. No dated statement says how stations record today, and no plan, finance or count of stations is held.
