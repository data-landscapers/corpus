Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One town council, Ezulwini, reported its own office internet line in the year to March 2024; how many councils are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Digital Eswatini Project is implemented by the Ministry of Information, Communications and Technology and financed by USD 65.0 million of World Bank loans and credit approved on 15 June 2026. Town councils meet their ICT costs from their own budgets, in Ezulwini's accounts for the year to March 2024 and Mbabane's for the year to March 2023.

What the office does over it. Ezulwini Town Council moved to Microsoft Office 365 for business email, installed virtual conferencing equipment and ran its telephone lines over the internet line in the year to March 2024. In Mbabane the council's IT Section has automated municipal activities, mail tracking among them (year to March 2023).

How the office is connected. Ezulwini Town Council carries its five office telephone lines over the office's internet line (year to March 2024). The Municipal Council of Mbabane pays for internet among its utility services (year to March 2023).

Last twelve months. The House of Assembly gave a second reading on 21 September 2026 to the three Bills enabling the project's loans. COMESA reports that from January to June 2026 the project was in preparation, with its infrastructure assessment begun.

Local governments connected. One urban local authority, Ezulwini Town Council, reports on its own office's internet line (year to March 2024), and the capital's council reports paying for internet at its offices (year to March 2023). Thirty-eight Tinkhundla centres are set to benefit from broadband, with rollout expected in 2027.

Noted, not assessed. Applications: the Electronic Government Procurement System, to be used by local governments (2024 budget speech); the National Postcode and Addressing Project, with Matsapha the first municipality to roll out (September 2026); the National Street Addressing and House Numbering Programme, piloted in Mbabane (July 2026); the National Addressing Standards, with Ezulwini a pilot municipality (July 2026); Ezulwini Town Council's Microsoft 365, Sage Evolution, PaySpace payroll, and GIS database with QField (year to March 2024); and Mbabane's automation of municipal activities (year to March 2023). Web: the website and social media of Ezulwini Town Council and of the Municipal Council of Mbabane. Public access: Piggs Peak Town Council's Wi-Fi hotspot in the central business district (2026); free public Wi-Fi zones in Mbabane, from April 2025; and the ICT ministry's free public Wi-Fi at 48 Tinkhundla centres (2024/25). City systems: Mbabane's Smart City, with a 5G network and 59 AI-enabled cameras (2025), and the 5G Government Network Smart City Project, funded by the Government of Taiwan and extended to Manzini and Matsapha by agreement in March 2026. Backbone: the national backbone of EPTC, the sole national backbone operator (2026); the RSTP National Data Centre at Matsapha (2026); SWAZI-NET and RSTP's hosting for ministries (2026); the nationwide fibre optic rollout (2025); optic fibre replacing copper cables (2024); the Government Wide Area Network, reached by health facilities (2023); the Backbone Network project's alternative route and the Government intranet (2023); a consultancy tender for data centre disaster recovery (October 2025); Critical Information Infrastructure Protection and Enhancement (2024/25); optic fibre in the Manzini/Mbabane corridor and on the electricity grid (2024/25); and the government core network and National Data Centre (2020). Capacity: the ICT ministry's Change Agent Training Workshop (June 2026); Ezulwini's cyber security training for all its ICT users (year to March 2024); and Mbabane's digital tools and GIS training for staff (2022/23). Central: Government in Your Hand and the Integrated Financial Management System, in a restricted pilot (2026); the Ministry of Finance's IBM mainframe systems and the Health Information Exchange (2026); government service payments at 35 post offices (2025); the ICT ministry's digital government services (2025); the Electronic Case Management System (2024); the Integrated Online E-Service Platform and the Deeds Registration and Information Management System (2024/25); the ESPPRA online tender portal (2025); the connection of remote veterinary sites to the Government network, and the livestock traceability system SLITS (2023); and the online study loan application system (2020).

Not held. No share or total of local governments with a connected office is published, and no Tinkhundla centre is reported connected. No national or shared system is named as worked in over the councils' connections.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One council, Manzini, billed ratepayers by account and took payment by phone in 2024; other local governments' records are not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The rates are billed and collected by the Municipal Council of Manzini, on its notice of April 2024.

What is recorded. For 2024/2025 the council issued rates bills to individual ratepayers by post, email and bulk SMS, and keeps their contact and ownership details; payments carry the plot or account number. In December 2020 it said rates had been billed in April 2020 and some property owners had not paid since.

Where the record is held. Ratepayers check their rates balance and pay on the USSD code *440#, as of April 2024.

Last twelve months. The latest dated event is of April 2024, when the council introduced the USSD code for checking balances and paying, alongside bulk SMS of bill amounts, for the 2024/2025 billing year.

Local governments recording. One council, the Municipal Council of Manzini, billed its ratepayers for 2024/2025, as it did in April 2020.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Where the record is held, who finances the billing, and whether any local government other than Manzini keeps such a record are not established; no total of local governments is given. Nothing is dated inside the last twelve months.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Hospitals and maternity units record births and deaths digitally (2022), and the national reporting system was introduced in 2024; clinic reporting is not yet shown.

Governance, planning, finance. WHO's country office places DHIS2 within the Ministry of Health's Strategic Health Information Department, run by its M&E unit, and reports that in 2024 it was hosted on UNICEF servers. MEASURE Evaluation's country profile of August 2019 records the Health Information System Strategic Plan 2010-2014 as not current.

Tiers in use. The Central Statistical Office's vital statistics report for 2022 shows an HMIS officer at the hospital or maternity recording birth and death outcomes in the Ministry of Health's inpatient system.

Where data is digitised. The same report shows the record made at the hospital or maternity itself, producing the ministry's hospital birth and death statistics, and HMIS supplied it with 28,541 health-institution births and 1,354 health-institution deaths for 2022.

Last twelve months. WHO's country office reports that DHIS2 was introduced nationally in 2024 as a single platform for health data that had sat in separate programme databases. The ministry's HMIS unit says that in the first quarter of 2026 reporting from CMIS, the national electronic medical record, to DHIS2 was strengthened with automated, FHIR-aligned data flows.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. MEASURE Evaluation's country profile of August 2019 records 95% of facilities as represented in HMIS information, and no electronic system for aggregating routine facility or community service data.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the eLMIS, the Integrated Disease Notification System, EIOS, DATIM and the integrated laboratory diagnostics system. Community: the Community-Based Health Information System (CBHIS), the Digital Community Health System and the CMIS community module. Enabling: the Eswatini Digital Project's broadband connectivity for health centres and MTN Eswatini's TruMD.

Not held. Nothing says which tiers send routine aggregate reports, where clinics' reports are keyed, or how many primary clinics enter their own. No plan or financing dated within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A shared electronic record runs in 73% of all health facilities (2026); it stays at stage 3 because no current plan or budget was found.

Governance, planning, finance. Data.FI described the Client Management Information System (CMIS) in April 2026 as an open-source, government-owned electronic medical record. The Ministry of Health's HMIS unit describes itself as custodian of the digital health systems, with its own in-country developers working on CMIS. Data.FI, a PEPFAR-funded USAID agreement, reported for the period ending March 2024 that it funds and carries out CMIS system development, security assessment and facility network support.

Tiers in use. Data.FI described CMIS in April 2026 as in use in the outpatient department of Piggs Peak Government Hospital and at local health clinics.

Where data is digitised. Data.FI said in April 2026 that clinicians open a client's full record on a unique ID number at their point of service, and that the record is available to clinicians at another clinic if the patient moves district.

Last twelve months. The HMIS unit reported in June 2026 that the integration of CMIS with eLMIS runs for ART medicines only and is ready for a facility-level pilot. In the first quarter of 2026 its developers completed training that produced automated, FHIR-aligned data flows from CMIS to DHIS2.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Data.FI said in April 2026 that CMIS is used in 73% of the country's health facilities, with 233 live sites. For the period ending March 2024 it reported CMIS in use at 75% (247 of 327) of healthcare facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the eLMIS, the Integrated Disease Notification System, EIOS, DATIM and the integrated laboratory diagnostics system. Community: the Community-Based Health Information System (CBHIS), the Digital Community Health System and the CMIS community module. Enabling: the Eswatini Digital Project's broadband connectivity for health centres and MTN Eswatini's TruMD.

Not held. No current plan naming CMIS and no domestic budget line for it is held. The 73% is a share of all health facilities with no denominator given, and no count of primary clinics alone is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A per-pupil record has covered all 976 primary and secondary schools since March 2024; how many primary schools enter attendance or results is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Education and Training designed and scaled the system, with help from UNICEF and civil society, and its departments presented their uses of it in February 2025. Its EMIS Unit runs regular webinars for users on data entry, validation and extraction, as of November 2025. The ministry is named as a funding partner beside UNICEF Eswatini, GPE KIX and UNESCO Eswatini, on a page of 2024.

Levels in use. The Budget Speech of February 2024 describes a web-based system designed to capture data on individual learners in Grade 0, primary and high schools by their Personal Identification Numbers. In October 2025 it covered primary and secondary schools.

How the record is kept. As of February 2025, information on each learner, such as demographics and performance, is captured with tools that collect it at the school level. The ministry holds learner-level data under unique personal identification numbers, including each learner's orphan status and bursary, as of November 2025.

Last twelve months. In November 2025 the team behind the software called the system fully operational.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The system, launched nationally in March 2024, covers all 976 primary and secondary schools, a figure for schools covered that a GPE KIX blog gave in October 2025. The app that takes attendance, which Eswatini calls SEMIS, was still a pilot with its roll-out to come in October 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Eswatini calls its school census an EMIS: the ministry's EMIS for aggregate data is national, and in it the head teacher enters enrolment, staffing and infrastructure; more than 900 school administrators and focal teachers were trained on its updated tools. The EMIS Unit runs the yearly Annual Education Census of all schools and the 16th Day Survey of enrolment and attendance. The census questionnaires were collected from schools on paper, and a system on MS Access was replaced from 2020. The Giga Schools Connectivity Initiative had 965 schools uploaded and 402 of them connected on 31 October 2025. A project financed by the Government of Taiwan gave 440 computers to 14 high schools and 11 primary schools in 2024/25, and the Digital School Initiative put 50 teachers through an online course.

Not held. No figure is held for primary schools entering attendance or results during the year, and how often a school adds to a learner's record is not stated. No current plan is held, and nothing dated in the past twelve months says whether the system is advancing or falling back.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths were entered in the national register at 14 service centres in 2022; the total number of offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Civil Registration Department of the Ministry of Home Affairs manages the system, and the register was developed in-house with the government's National Data Centre, which hosts it. A CRVS Strategic Plan 2022-26 was launched in 2022. The World Bank's appraisal of 8 June 2026 allots US$5.5 million to digital ID and civil registration.

Events registered digitally. Births and deaths are captured into the National Population Register on registration, with 47,524 birth and 5,968 death registrations in 2022.

How the record is made. The Central Statistical Office's report for 2022 says the registration officer fills the form by hand and a typist enters it into the register, which the service centres reach over a wide-area network, the registration being complete only once it is in the system.

Last twelve months. The World Bank appraised the Digital Eswatini Project on 8 June 2026, to upgrade the existing ID and civil registration systems. Its document says paper birth and death records are stored locally without centralised backups.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Central Statistical Office's 2022 report lists 14 civil registration service centres with births and deaths registered in the register that year. The Ministry of Home Affairs' report for 2024/25 gives 15 service centres and 5 hospital registration points. In answers collected from October 2022 to April 2023, Eswatini told an AfDB and COMESA assessment that entry of vital events is electronic in all its civil registration offices.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the conversion of manual birth, marriage and death certificates at headquarters and the service centres and a planned digitisation of paper records. Notification: paper notifications of birth and death given to families by health institutions, with no electronic movement to the registrar. Outreach: the Mopping Up campaign, which took registration into chiefdoms; the Nkwe Program of community outreach in 2024/25; mass registration of vital events in Lubombo and Shiselweni in 2022; trucks equipped for mobile registration; and planned mobile campaigns for hard-to-reach communities. Identity: national identity card enrolment at service centres from age 16; identity card production at ten service centres, resumed in February 2026; the Home Affairs system for identity card and passport applications, down in a network blackout on 16 September 2026; the ministry's record of Chief Codes; the population register of the Identification Order of 1998; the Government In Your Hand mobile app, launched 21 May 2025; and a planned national digital ID system. Exchange: the Personal Identity Number, created at birth registration, and the Ministry of Health's Client Management Information System, networked with the register; a link between the civil registration system and patient health records; planned modules connecting digital ID with the civil registry; and Mchumanisi, a proposed national data exchange platform.

Not held. No total of local offices is held for 2022, so the 14 centres are a count without a denominator, and no rural share or office figure from the last two years is held. The World Bank's June 2026 account of civil records on paper is not reconciled with the 2022 and 2023 accounts of electronic entry.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Police trainers were trained in 2025 to enter cases in a justice ministry system; whether any station enters cases is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Integrated Electronic Case Management System, which Eswatini calls IECMS, is the Ministry of Justice's programme, with a Technical Working Group supporting its training sessions and Synergy International Systems supporting implementation, Independent News reported in August 2025.

Records made digitally. Royal Eswatini Police Service officers were trained on entering new cases into the system and were walked through all the components of a docket at the ministry's training of trainers in August 2025.

How the record is made. At the Mbabane and Manzini One Stop Centres the police record by hand in occurrence books, and police, prosecution and social welfare keep separate paper registers, UNICEF's evaluators stated in 2025.

Last twelve months. The training of police trainers on the system was reported on 20 August 2025.

Stations doing the digital entry. At the Manzini One Stop Centre the police complete paper forms that go to headquarters every 2 to 3 months and are not kept on site, UNICEF's evaluators stated in 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the police's Digital Forensic Unit. Single-function: the national register for sexual offenders and the paper Police Service forms and RSP-88 form for cases of sexual and gender-based violence.

Not held. No station, region or start date is held for police entry of cases in the IECMS, and the paper recording is described at two One Stop Centres only, not at stations nationally. A plan, the finance and whether the police case file passes digitally to the court are not held.
