Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Eswatini has a Data Protection Act of 2022, applied through enforcement notices in 2024 and 2025; the number of decisions made is not known.

Authority. The Data Protection Act designates the Eswatini Communications Commission as the Eswatini Data Protection Authority. In June 2026 the Authority had powers to investigate breaches, resolve complaints and impose sanctions, and took complaints by website, email and phone.

Standing. The Data Protection Act, 2022 (No. 5 of 2022) was assented to and gazetted on 4 March 2022. In June 2026 the Commission's acting chief executive said the Act binds public and private bodies, ministries included, and requires a breach to be notified to the Authority within 72 hours. The deadline for organisations to comply was 31 March 2026.

Regulations. Notice No. 1/2024, issued in February 2024, made registration mandatory from 1 March 2024 for controllers and processors handling the data of more than ten people. General Notice No. 4/2024 directed every data controller to appoint a data protection officer by 30 May 2024.

Application. The Authority lists enforcement notices under section 41 of the Act issued to MTN Eswatini and Liberty Life Swaziland Limited in June 2024 and to the Royal Science Technology Park in March 2025. In June 2026 it opened a formal investigation into the Ministry of Home Affairs over unblurred national identity documents posted online.

Last twelve months. By February 2026 the Authority had launched its 2026 awareness campaign, "Take Control of Your Data". In September 2026 it was setting up information desks in major towns, after its own survey found 55.2% of the public finding the Authority difficult to access.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No count of decisions, sanctions or complaints settled is published for a stated year, and the newest enforcement notice on record is from March 2025. The outcome of the investigation opened in June 2026 is not reported.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law has been in force since March 2022; officials say arrests are possible, but no case under it is known.

Law in force. The Computer Crime and Cybercrime Act, 2022 (Act No. 6 of 2022) was published in the Government Gazette Extraordinary on 4 March 2022 and, by section 1(2), came into operation on the date of publication. On 1 October 2026 the Director of Public Prosecutions described the Act as the legal framework for offences committed through or against computer systems. The Act has therefore stood as Eswatini's cybercrime law since March 2022, and officials still cite it as the governing text in 2026.

Use of the law. On 27 August 2026 an officer of the communications regulator ESCCOM said that cyberbullying is a crime under the Computer Crime and Cybercrime Act of 2022 and that people can be arrested for it. The Director of Public Prosecutions said on 1 October 2026 that her office has trained prosecutors on the Act. These are official statements on how the law applies and on preparing prosecutors to use it.

Governance, planning, finance. The Integrated Electronic Case Management System, which Eswatini calls IECMS, is the Ministry of Justice's programme, with a Technical Working Group supporting its training sessions and Synergy International Systems supporting implementation, Independent News reported in August 2025.

Records made digitally. Royal Eswatini Police Service officers were trained on entering new cases into the system and were walked through all the components of a docket at the ministry's training of trainers in August 2025.

How the record is made. At the Mbabane and Manzini One Stop Centres the police record by hand in occurrence books, and police, prosecution and social welfare keep separate paper registers, UNICEF's evaluators stated in 2025.

Last twelve months. The training of police trainers on the system was reported on 20 August 2025.

Stations doing the digital entry. At the Manzini One Stop Centre the police complete paper forms that go to headquarters every 2 to 3 months and are not kept on site, UNICEF's evaluators stated in 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the police's Digital Forensic Unit. Single-function: the national register for sexual offenders and the paper Police Service forms and RSP-88 form for cases of sexual and gender-based violence.

Not held. No station, region or start date is held for police entry of cases in the IECMS, and the paper recording is described at two One Stop Centres only, not at stations nationally. A plan, the finance and whether the police case file passes digitally to the court are not held.
