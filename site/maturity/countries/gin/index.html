Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Guinea's 2023-2027 digital strategy is public and costed, with a lead ministry named, but whether any progress report exists is not known.

Governance and finance. The Stratégie nationale de digitalisation de l'État et de développement de l'économie numérique 2023-2027 names the Ministry of Posts, Telecommunications and Digital Economy as lead ministry, and its financing table puts the State at USD 209,711 thousand, 12 percent of USD 1,747,590 thousand, with development partners at 60 percent.

Standing. The strategy, titled Guinée Digitale 2027 in its own text, is the Transition Government's strategy in version 1.2 of 11 June 2024, validated in national workshops with UNDP and the African Development Bank.

Text. The full text is published as a PDF on the site of the state digitalisation agency, issued by the ministry.

Period. The strategy is titled 2023-2027 and sets 14 result indicators against 2022 references for a 2027 horizon. It follows the 2016-2020 ICT and digital economy strategy, which has ended.

Delivery plan. The roadmap sets 245 initiatives under 41 orientations and six axes, each with a responsible body, a cost in thousand US dollars and a duration.

Reporting. The lead ministry is responsible for drafting, coordinating, implementing and evaluating the strategy, with a national steering committee to be set up.

Last twelve months. The latest dated row is a round table on 4 and 5 December 2024, where the minister said the strategy had been in implementation since 2022.

Noted, not assessed. WARDIP (West Africa Regional Digital Integration Program); Simandou 2040; Plan to connect 2,200 public primary schools (GIGA); Vision Guinée 2040; Plan National de Développement Economique et Social (PNDES 2016-2020); Stratégie e-Administration de la Guinée (2015-2019); Stratégie digitale du Ministère de la Santé; Stratégie digitale du Ministère de l'Agriculture et de l'Elevage (2021-2024).

Not held. No implementation or results report is held, and no adopting act is named for the strategy. The period reads 2027 in the title and 2026 in one article.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Guinea has a 2016 cybersecurity and personal data protection law with no decision under it on record; its commencement date is not known.

Authority. The 2016 law defines an Autorité de Protection and names no constituted body or appointed members. On 20 June 2024 the CNT's commissions debated a bill to establish the Autorité de Protection des Données à caractère Personnel, with the designation of members and their mandates contested and plenary adoption pending.

Standing. Loi L/2016/037/AN on cybersecurity and personal data protection was adopted by the National Assembly and promulgated by President Alpha Condé in 2016, and covers personal data processing by individuals, companies and public institutions. Part Two of the text is a full personal data protection regime.

Regulations. Article 5 of the law sends processing declarations to the competent authority "designated by regulation".

Application. The law gives the Autorité de Protection the functions of receiving declarations, granting authorisations, approving transfers, investigating and issuing guidelines.

Last twelve months. On 19 November 2025 Minister Rose Pola Pricemou said Guinea is strengthening its regulatory framework with a new data-protection law.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. The date the 2016 law came into force, any regulation made under it, any decision applying it and the constitution of its authority. The stage and timetable of the new law announced in November 2025.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybersecurity law is in force; in 2025 the media regulator said it will be used against web media.

Law in force. The president of the Haute Autorité de la Communication said in July 2025 that the cybersecurity law, and not the press law, will be applied to web media that break the rules. The same statement, reported by Aminata on 26 July 2025, says the penalty is prison for any person. The law is referred to as the loi sur la cybersécurité, and the statement treats it as the one that governs web media conduct today.

Use of the law. The nearest fact held is the same statement of July 2025, in which the president of the Haute Autorité de la Communication said the law will be applied to web media that break the rules, with prison for any person. It is a statement of how the law is to be applied to online media, made by the media regulator's head.

Noted, not assessed. ANSSI Guinée, the Agence Nationale de la Sécurité des Systèmes d'Information, under the Ministère de la Communication, de l'Économie Numérique et de l'Innovation.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

No ruling or published figure from the authority is held.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Commune registry staff worked in a national civil-registration system in 2026, with rural offices on paper; how many communes are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Enabel, the Belgian development agency, issued the tender of 27 July 2022 for the internet connection of 10 pilot communes, the notice citing Belgium's bilateral portfolio of EUR 45 million for 2019 to 2023.

What the office does over it. The delegated civil-registry officer of Ratoma commune said, as reported on 2 September 2026, that his office goes on registering births in the digital civil-registration system, which runs from a data centre held elsewhere. Enabel's final evaluation, from fieldwork in April 2026, says civil-status officers and data-entry agents at civil registry centres work in the national civil register's central database, which it calls E-RENEC, with paper still in use in parallel. A policy brief of November 2025 says weak connectivity, high data costs and low digital literacy force rural municipal offices onto manual reporting and paper records.

How the office is connected. That tender was for a service contract for the internet connection of 10 communes, closing on 17 August 2022.

Last twelve months. The relocation of the system's data centre from the Maison centrale disrupted the service to commune offices, on top of a stockout of secure paper lasting about two weeks, as reported on 2 September 2026.

Local governments connected. The tender of 2022 names the 10 pilot communes: the 5 of Conakry, Kassa, Coyah, Kindia, Mamou and Tolo. The evaluation describes E-RENEC as the product of a pilot project and recommends a maturity diagnosis before any national extension.

Noted, not assessed. Civil registration: E-RENEC; the ten-commune pilot of a national digital civil registry; the digital civil status solutions the minister reports deployed in 28 communes in 2025; the Registre National de l'État Civil and the platform for digitising civil registration; the Registre National des Personnes Physiques; the communal civil-status register kept at the seats of urban and rural communes; the digital birth extract issued at commune centres; biometric birth certificates issued in Sanoyah commune; the digitisation centres for civil registry archives; and the civil registry management system the digital strategy plans to extend. Commune finance: TOMPRO and SYSCOHADA for communal accounting; the public finance management software for local governments' accountants; the integrated financial information system intended for all communes; and the computerisation of municipal financial statements. The procurement platform TELEMO. Equipment: computer kits for 21 local authorities and solar kits for 7 rural communes, with the commune accounting software they are bought to run, and IT equipment given to 15 municipalities under the Third Urban Development Project. Public access: 20 regional digital hubs under construction. Backbone: the national fibre backbone and the national Tier III data centre; the Conakry metropolitan fibre network; the state's integrated network, the Réseau Intégré de l'État; the state intranet in the digital agency's founding decree; and the data centre of the digital civil-registration system. Central: the one-stop shops for public services planned in the 13 communes of Conakry; the citizen service centres planned in the digital strategy; the administrative census programme PN-RAVEC; and the digital platform for managing NGOs.

Not held. Whether the internet connection tendered for the 10 pilot communes in 2022 was delivered, how commune offices are connected today, and how many communes work in the civil-registration system. Who owns and pays for the connection since 2022, and how many rural offices are without one.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All levels reported into the national system in early 2026, with clinics' paper reports entered at district offices.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene's Bureau de Stratégie et de Développement runs the national health information system (SNIS) and DHIS2 Guinée and publishes the quarterly SNIS bulletin, on its performance bulletin for the first quarter of 2026. The bulletin places the SNIS under strategic orientation 3 of the PNDS 2025-2034 and lists GFF/R4D as the partner supporting DHIS2 in that quarter.

Tiers in use. The ministry reports that in the first quarter of 2026 all regions and all levels of the health pyramid transmitted their data on time, with national completeness at 100% and promptness at 99%.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's PASA2 programme page, published in 2025, states that health posts, health centres and prefectural hospitals collect SNIS data on paper primary tools, which are keyed into DHIS2. A CSEA policy brief of November 2025 states that weak rural connectivity forces local health centres to rely on manual reporting and paper records.

Last twelve months. In the first quarter of 2026 the SNIS reached what the ministry calls a historic record of 100% completeness, WASH indicators were integrated into DHIS2 and prefectural DHIS2 dashboards were in development.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's statistical yearbook for 2021 says that the monthly reports of first-level health centres and improved health centres were either entered in DHIS2 or sent to the district team for entry.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SPAQ, REC maternité and DHIS2 Tracker. Single-function: DHIS2 Surveillance Guinée, the logistics system eSIGL and the epidemiological surveillance Tracker. Community: Kendeya. Enabling: the planned Réseau National Numérique de la Santé, the planned Réseau national de télémédecine, the telemedicine unit at Donka hospital and the telemedicine platform at Faranah regional hospital.

Not held. No share or count of health centres or health posts entering their own reports is held; the 100% completeness is data transmitted. No domestic budget line for the SNIS is named.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Three separate patient record systems ran in some hospitals and pilot facilities in 2026, with records entered during care; no totals are held.

Governance, planning, finance. Visa Medical, a digital medical record, is built and run by the private start-up Kouma Academy with the Ministry of Health's support, as reported in August 2026. The OpenClinic hospital information system was installed in five facilities with the support of the Belgian agency Enabel. The non-profit Clinic+O runs its record in collaboration with the Ministry of Health, and the Swiss Re Foundation funds the project from November 2025 to December 2026.

Tiers in use. By August 2026 Visa Medical was deployed in 19 public establishments, Dubréka hospital among them. A ministry mission of 17 to 22 August 2026 reviewed five digitised facilities in the Mamou and Dalaba districts running OpenClinic, the Dalaba prefectural hospital among them. The Swiss Re Foundation's page of March 2026 has the Clinic+O record in use in rural communities and at Community Health Centers.

Where data is digitised. At Dubréka hospital in August 2026 clinicians retrieved a patient's consultations and tests in real time from the Visa Medical record. In the five Mamou and Dalaba facilities data are entered directly into OpenClinic. Clinic+O's community health workers capture patient information on tablets in an offline-first electronic medical record.

Last twelve months. The Dalaba prefectural hospital deployed OpenClinic in January 2026. The Clinic+O project began in November 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Swiss Re Foundation stated in March 2026 that Guinea's rural Community Health Centers are often understaffed and rely on paper-based systems.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SPAQ, REC maternité and DHIS2 Tracker. Single-function: DHIS2 Surveillance Guinée, the logistics system eSIGL and the epidemiological surveillance Tracker. Community: Kendeya. Enabling: the planned Réseau National Numérique de la Santé, the planned Réseau national de télémédecine, the telemedicine unit at Donka hospital and the telemedicine platform at Faranah regional hospital.

Not held. No count of hospitals is held against which the 19 Visa Medical establishments and the five OpenClinic facilities can be set, and no count or district for the health centres using Clinic+O. Whether a Visa Medical record can be retrieved at another facility is not stated, and no plan naming any of the three systems is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In March 2026 a nationwide registration gave primary pupils a single school number, in secondary since 2022; how many schools took part is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Pre-University Education instituted the unique pupil number it calls the matricule unique, within a digitalisation run by its examinations directorate, which also coordinates training on the registration platform. A roadmap validated on 2 November 2021 provides for a single integrated system whose pupil module covers attendance, class assessments and examination results.

Levels in use. The matricule began in 2022 at collège and lycée and was extended to primary in a nationwide registration from 5 to 25 March 2026. In Labé prefecture a mission ordered on 30 March 2026 accompanied the registration of primary pupils, each photographed for the matricule.

How the record is kept. Each pupil is given one matricule in the registration, for which public and private school heads were mobilised in March 2026; transfers between schools are then detected automatically.

Last twelve months. The ministry announced the digital registration of primary pupils on 25 February 2026, with officers of the regional, prefectoral and communal education offices starting training. Registration was launched in Matoto on 5 March 2026 and ran school by school in Labé under the order of 30 March.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The March 2026 registration covered the whole national territory, public and private schools alike, by the account of Matoto's communal education director.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school data collection, which Guinea calls its Education Management Information System (EMIS), with the annual statistical collection (RSA) and the basic education data collection financed by the World Bank behind it; a planned mobile entry of yearly school-level information, cancelled in June 2024; the examinations management application and online registration of examination candidates; teacher personnel tools; paper school inspection tools and a planned pilot of unique codes for staff posts; teacher tablets for training; digital teaching and distance-training initiatives; and school connection initiatives.

Not held. No number or share of primary schools registered is given, and no source says who keys the record: those trained are officers of the education offices, not school staff. No plan dated within three years and no source of finance is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Commune offices made digital birth certificates in September 2026, when a paper shortage halted printing; how many offices do so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The 2023 civil status law makes the ministry in charge of civil status responsible for buying, installing and maintaining the equipment of civil status centres. In September 2026 an unnamed company in charge of the secure paper was reinstalling the data centre. An evaluation for Enabel found in April 2026 that national technicians had no access to the source code of the register system it names E-RENEC, and that the project closed in February 2026 with no maintenance or operating budget secured.

Events registered digitally. Ratoma commune's civil status officer said on 2 September 2026 that commune offices establish digital birth certificates on secure paper.

How the record is made. The officer said the system depends on a data centre, whose relocation disturbed it while registrations continued. In March 2026 Conakry's 13 communes received paper registers for the year's registrations, and in February 2026 registrars still used paper in parallel.

Last twelve months. By 2 September 2026 printing of digital birth certificates had been halted for about two weeks by a stockout of secure paper.

Local offices doing the digital entry. Ratoma's officer said the secure paper serves to establish digital birth certificates in all the communes of Guinea. Enabel, in a text published in August 2023, described a digital national civil registry piloted in ten communes.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the Registre national des personnes physiques and its identity verification platform, the numéro personnel d'identification, the interoperability platform of the project named Diama Djigui, the état civil biométrique enrolment centres, the biometric census of PN-RAVEC and its online verification platform, the biometric national identity card, the biometric passport, the SIM card holders' identity certification platform, the WURI foundational identification pilot and a planned national biometric reference file of persons. Archive: the digitisation and indexing of civil status archives, the digitisation of paper records in ten pilot municipalities, the migration of 445,000 acts into the central database and the archiving of old acts planned under RAVEC. Notification: a mobile application for birth notification from health centres, the declaration centres and declaration booklets of the 2023 law and a planned health-sector system for collecting births and deaths. Certificates: the extrait de naissance numérique, biometric birth certificates issued in Sanoyah and a national public services portal. Outreach: audiences foraines. Exchange: the link in law from the civil status register to the population register and identification number and a planned interface between the health and civil status information systems.

Not held. No count or share of offices is held, and no source states that the entry is made at the office or whether the digital birth certificate is the registration of the birth or a secure copy of it. No plan is stated.
