Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Niger's 2022 data protection law is in force, with compliance files and inspections in 2025; whether any decision was made under it is not known.

Authority. The law creates the Haute Autorité de Protection des Données à caractère Personnel (HAPDP), attached to the Presidency, with ten members named by decree. Its president said in July 2025 that he took office on 18 September 2024 and that the plenary deliberates on authorisations and sanctions. Africa Data Protection reported on 13 June 2026 that the government had abolished the HAPDP by decree and split its powers between the Ministry of Justice and the Ministry of the Interior.

Standing. Law no. 2022-59 of 16 December 2022 on personal data protection is the general law, held in the HAPDP's consolidated text of April 2026. It repealed laws 2017-28 and 2019-71.

Regulations. The law has been amended by Law 2023-31 of 4 July 2023 and by Ordinances 2024-16 of 26 April 2024 and 2024-29 of 24 June 2024.

Application. The HAPDP's president said in July 2025 that the authority had processed 139 compliance files since September 2024, covering declarations, video-surveillance declarations and processing and transfer authorisations, and had run four control missions on mobile operators in 2025.

Last twelve months. The abolition of the HAPDP and the transfer of its powers to two ministries was reported on 13 June 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No regulation made under the law is held, and no decision, sanction or count of complaints settled under it. The date the law came into force and the date of the decree abolishing the HAPDP are not held.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2019 cybercrime law is in force; in October 2025 it was used to charge six journalists.

Law in force. Niger has a cybercrime law dating from 2019. The military authorities amended it in 2024 to restore prison terms of up to five years and fines for disseminating data likely to disturb public order, and Human Rights Watch reports it was the law under which charges were brought in October 2025. The law is in force and the authorities apply it, including its public-order offence of disseminating data, with the charges dated to 30 October 2025.

Use of the law. The law was used against journalists. Per Human Rights Watch, police in Niamey arrested six journalists on 30 October 2025 and the authorities charged them with complicity in disseminating a document likely to disturb public order under the 2019 cybercrime law, for sharing an invitation to a state press conference. Three of the six were detained at Kollo prison, and the arrests took place in Niamey, the capital, on a single day.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No count of cases investigated, prosecuted or decided under the law, for a period ending in the last two years, is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Niger is under the regional central bank's rules, with one non-bank e-money issuer licensed in 2025; in 2026 all providers were ordered to connect.

Law or rules in force. Niger is bound by the rules of the BCEAO, the central bank of the eight-state West African Economic and Monetary Union, which licenses e-money issuers and supervises them with the union's banking commission. The commission's 2025 report counts one licensed e-money issuer in Niger at 31 December 2025, among 18 across the union.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. That issuer is not a bank. In April 2026 the BCEAO told every bank, e-money issuer and payment institution in the eight states, Niger included, to be connected to its instant payment platform, PI-SPI, by 30 June 2026. By a communiqué of 25 June it set 30 September 2026 as the new deadline for them, and 30 June 2027 for microfinance institutions.

Noted, not assessed. PI-SPI.

Not held. Nothing on the four questions.

Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A 2019 electronic transactions law lets administrations exchange data and sets a framework they must use; later use under it is not known.

Law or decree in force. Niger's Loi 2019-03 of 30 April 2019 on electronic transactions was promulgated and published in the Journal Officiel. Its Title VII on electronic administration lets administrations exchange information, documents and acts electronically (Art. 29). Art. 35 institutes a General Interoperability Framework for administrative information systems, to be set by decree.

Sharing required and the law applied. The same law exempts an applicant from supplying a supporting document when the administration can obtain it directly from the administration or services concerned (Art. 33), replacing it with a sworn declaration. The framework's data directories, norms and standards are those the administration must use (Art. 35). No regulation or order made under it is recorded, and no public body is named as exchanging data because it requires them to.

Noted, not assessed. Registre National de la Population (RNP).

Not held. No regulation, decree or order made under the law, and no public body named as exchanging data because it requires them to, dated in the last three years, is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2023 ministry guide has health centres enter their monthly reports into the national system; no figure shows how many do.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's monitoring guide of December 2023 makes its Direction des Statistiques responsible at central level for the monthly reports of the Système National d'Information Sanitaire (SNIS). The guide, written for the health development plan 2022-2026, names DHIS2 as the base for managing routine health data. The e-health strategy of 2019 planned USD 1.00 million of the SNIS project's USD 1.20 million from partners and nothing from the ministry.

Tiers in use. The guide has every health establishment key its monthly report into DHIS2, naming integrated health centres (CSI) and hospitals as data sources. Health posts send their data to their CSI by the 10th, where it is checked and keyed.

Where data is digitised. The circuit the guide sets in December 2023 is that each establishment fills a standard monthly activity report, validates it and keys it into DHIS2, the CSI by the 15th of the following month. DHIS2 has been online since September 2016.

Last twelve months. The guide of December 2023 sets yearly targets to 2026 for the timeliness and completeness of SNIS reports.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The guide gives, from the 2021 statistical yearbook, a CSI report completeness rate of 97.90% and a timeliness rate of 54.63% for 2021, as reports transmitted over reports expected. In September 2018 the ministry counted 1,026 integrated health centres and 2,443 health huts in the public network.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for the Men5CV post-campaign coverage survey. Single-function: Estelab, the weekly notifiable-disease reporting in DHIS2, the DHIS2 intra-campaign monitoring module and the ministry's plateforme e-surveillance. Community: mHealth and its mHealth Monitor dashboard in Gouré, the World Vision and University of Toronto mobile child-health follow-up of 2014 to 2016 and the Save the Children and USAID mobile follow-up of acute malnutrition of 2013 to 2016. Enabling: the Computerised Maintenance and Management System (GMAO), telemedicine at referral hospitals, the projet national de télémédecine and the Réseau Informatique National de l'Administration.

Not held. No count or share of integrated health centres keying their own reports into DHIS2 is held; the 2021 rates measure reports transmitted. No financing dated within the last three years is held, no dated event in the last twelve months, and no community system is shown exchanging data with the SNIS.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Computerised patient records were set up in health centres and district hospitals in three districts (November 2024); how many centres is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The mid-term evaluation for Enabel, of November 2024, states that the Enabel project PASS Sutura started the digitalisation with recruited consultancy firms, with the Ministry of Health's digital health directorate involved. PASS Sutura is a Belgian bilateral project with EUR 14.5 million for health over 2022-2026, and a Netherlands co-financing of EUR 3.2 million to 31 March 2025 puts particular emphasis on digitalisation. On 8 July 2026 the health minister opened the workshop validating the costed roadmap of the Schéma Directeur National de la e-Santé 2025-2029.

Tiers in use. By November 2024 the project had set up digitalisation, including computerised medical records and billing, in the integrated health centres (CSI) and district hospitals of Dioundiou, Gothèye and Gaya.

Where data is digitised. Enabel states that a hospital information system built around a computerised medical record was put in place at the district hospitals of Gaya and Gothèye after field missions in February and April 2024.

Last twelve months. At the workshop of 8 July 2026 the Directorate of e-Santé presented OpenClinic, its project for a hospital information system and computerised patient record, as already under way.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The evaluation of November 2024 places the digitalised health centres in the project's three target health districts only. In September 2018 the ministry's strategy reported electronic patient record activity in two health districts, Gaya and Gothèye, of 72.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for the Men5CV post-campaign coverage survey. Single-function: Estelab, the weekly notifiable-disease reporting in DHIS2, the DHIS2 intra-campaign monitoring module and the ministry's plateforme e-surveillance. Community: mHealth and its mHealth Monitor dashboard in Gouré, the World Vision and University of Toronto mobile child-health follow-up of 2014 to 2016 and the Save the Children and USAID mobile follow-up of acute malnutrition of 2013 to 2016. Enabling: the Computerised Maintenance and Management System (GMAO), telemedicine at referral hospitals, the projet national de télémédecine and the Réseau Informatique National de l'Administration.

Not held. No count of health centres using the computerised record is held, and no denominator. Nothing held says whether the record is made at the point of care or keyed afterwards, and no sites or counts are given for OpenClinic.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

School heads filled printed forms for the yearly school count in 2025; how schools keep pupils' records is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Ministry of National Education's recensement scolaire 2024-2025 ran on printed questionnaires that heads of pre-primary, primary and secondary establishments filled in April 2025; the regional directorates keyed them into the platform the country calls StatEduc2 from June 2025, and the census covered 17,904 of 18,282 primary establishments (97.9%). The higher-education ministry runs its own census of schools and universities once a year, partly on paper and partly in Excel, as it did for 2023-2024. The primary-education ministry's Annuaire statistique 2015 is an earlier edition of the school census yearbook. A World Bank project recorded on 14 July 2020 that the annual school census through new digital tools had not yet been conducted, with a target of 2026; the same project planned a national online education platform for teachers and digitised inspection and data collection reports for inspectorates in five regions. The iPayMoney platform handles online registration and fee payment at public universities, with more than 7,700 students enrolled online at the University of Maradi in 2023. The Projet Villages Intelligents, World Bank financed for 2020-2026, covers rural broadband, digital financial services and digital skills.

Not held. No dated statement says whether primary schools keep their pupils' records on paper, and nothing held describes a system with a record for each pupil.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The digital register ran in 101 of 266 communes in December 2024, a share of communes; digital death registration outside Niamey is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The civil-status system belongs to the Interior Ministry's Direction Générale de l'état civil, des Migrations et des Réfugiés, the ministry said on 11 August 2026. A civil registration strategic plan covers 2023 to 2027. The computerisation announced that day sits under the WURI-Niger project, which the World Bank finances.

Events registered digitally. The ministry's database for the five arrondissements of Niamey holds 72,459 births and 6,524 deaths registered in 2023, by the statistics office.

How the record is made. Events are declared at the centres and passed to the commune, where agents key the acts on machines connected to a server at the ministry's directorate, and each validated act is saved there, the statistics office wrote of 2023. Acts are printed on secured paper from the computerised system, by the government's communiqué of 22 April 2026.

Last twelve months. An ordinance of 22 April 2026 provides for the national civil-status register, and its decree followed on 18 May 2026. On 11 August 2026 the ministry announced the computerisation of all communes and declaration centres and a new platform as works to come.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The computerised system was in production in 101 of 266 communes and communal arrondissements in December 2024, by the statistics office, and the government counted 102 communes on 22 April 2026. Only towns in urban areas had electronic civil registration databases in 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the population register the ministry calls the Registre National de la Population, the Registre National des Nationalités, the biometric national identity card of the Alliance des États du Sahel, its biometric passport, the WURI-Niger project's foundational identification system and a pilot on the MOSIP platform. Outreach: the national awareness tour on the civil-status and identification reforms, mobile teams and mobile court hearings in the Diffa region, the campaign named Hakin Yara and the birth registration pilot in Filingué. Archive: the digitisation of old birth certificates, digitisation of acts in four pilot communes and the Norwegian Refugee Council's project to digitise birth certificates. Notification: declaration centres in health facilities and villages, on paper notification books. Certificate: electronic issue and electronic signature of acts, provided for in the ordinance of 22 April 2026. Enabling: a data centre and security operations centre planned for the identification agency and the Projet Villages Intelligents. Exchange: interoperability between the civil-status register and the population register, set out in the decree of 18 May 2026, and a planned interface between the health information system and civil status.

Not held. Digital registration of deaths is not established outside Niamey. Whether the declaration centres in villages and health facilities are computerised is not stated, and the gap between urban and rural offices has no figure.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2019 a team of eight in Niamey entered cases into the police database; whether any station outside the capital records digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The national police's Direction de la Police Technique et Scientifique in Niamey works the database, and its director is responsible for recording criminal acts in Niger, EUCAP Sahel Niger reported on 23 May 2019. The system, launched in Niger in 2012, is financed by the European Union under the Emergency Trust Fund for Africa and implemented by INTERPOL with EUCAP Sahel Niger.

Records made digitally. The database, which Niger calls the Système d'Information Policière en Afrique de l'Ouest (SIPAO), holds offences, criminals' identities and criminal proceedings, and the team recording criminal acts had entered more than 8,000 cases by May 2019.

How the record is made. Cases are entered by a team of 8 under the director in Niamey, while operators are being trained to record data directly from their own service, by the same report of May 2019.

Last twelve months. EUCAP Sahel Niger reported on 23 May 2019 that 700 new cases were recorded in the month.

Stations doing the digital entry. In May 2019 the entry was made in Niamey, by the team of 8.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the fichier d'identification et de traçage biométriques of the Direction de la surveillance du territoire, the Fichier automatisé des empreintes digitales and the fichier central de la Gendarmerie. Public: the portal service-public.ne.

Not held. Nothing since May 2019 says the system reached stations outside Niamey or still runs. No count of stations is held, and what the Niamey team enters from is not stated.
