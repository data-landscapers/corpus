Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Ministry's 2023 guide has health centres key monthly reports into DHIS2; no share or count of centres doing so is held.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's monitoring guide of December 2023 makes its Direction des Statistiques responsible at central level for the monthly reports of the Système National d'Information Sanitaire (SNIS). The guide, written for the health development plan 2022-2026, names DHIS2 as the base for managing routine health data. The e-health strategy of 2019 planned USD 1.00 million of the SNIS project's USD 1.20 million from partners and nothing from the ministry.

Tiers in use. The guide has every health establishment key its monthly report into DHIS2, naming integrated health centres (CSI) and hospitals as data sources. Health posts send their data to their CSI by the 10th, where it is checked and keyed.

Where data is digitised. The circuit the guide sets in December 2023 is that each establishment fills a standard monthly activity report, validates it and keys it into DHIS2, the CSI by the 15th of the following month. DHIS2 has been online since September 2016.

Last twelve months. The guide of December 2023 sets yearly targets to 2026 for the timeliness and completeness of SNIS reports.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The guide gives, from the 2021 statistical yearbook, a CSI report completeness rate of 97.90% and a timeliness rate of 54.63% for 2021, as reports transmitted over reports expected. In September 2018 the ministry counted 1,026 integrated health centres and 2,443 health huts in the public network.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for the Men5CV post-campaign coverage survey. Single-function: Estelab, the weekly notifiable-disease reporting in DHIS2, the DHIS2 intra-campaign monitoring module and the ministry's plateforme e-surveillance. Community: mHealth and its mHealth Monitor dashboard in Gouré, the World Vision and University of Toronto mobile child-health follow-up of 2014 to 2016 and the Save the Children and USAID mobile follow-up of acute malnutrition of 2013 to 2016. Enabling: the Computerised Maintenance and Management System (GMAO), telemedicine at referral hospitals, the projet national de télémédecine and the Réseau Informatique National de l'Administration.

Not held. No count or share of integrated health centres keying their own reports into DHIS2 is held; the 2021 rates measure reports transmitted. No financing dated within the last three years is held, no dated event in the last twelve months, and no community system is shown exchanging data with the SNIS.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Computerised records set up in health centres and district hospitals of three districts (November 2024); no count of centres or place of entry is held.

Governance, planning, finance. The mid-term evaluation for Enabel, of November 2024, states that the Enabel project PASS Sutura started the digitalisation with recruited consultancy firms, with the Ministry of Health's digital health directorate involved. PASS Sutura is a Belgian bilateral project with EUR 14.5 million for health over 2022-2026, and a Netherlands co-financing of EUR 3.2 million to 31 March 2025 puts particular emphasis on digitalisation. On 8 July 2026 the health minister opened the workshop validating the costed roadmap of the Schéma Directeur National de la e-Santé 2025-2029.

Tiers in use. By November 2024 the project had set up digitalisation, including computerised medical records and billing, in the integrated health centres (CSI) and district hospitals of Dioundiou, Gothèye and Gaya.

Where data is digitised. Enabel states that a hospital information system built around a computerised medical record was put in place at the district hospitals of Gaya and Gothèye after field missions in February and April 2024.

Last twelve months. At the workshop of 8 July 2026 the Directorate of e-Santé presented OpenClinic, its project for a hospital information system and computerised patient record, as already under way.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The evaluation of November 2024 places the digitalised health centres in the project's three target health districts only. In September 2018 the ministry's strategy reported electronic patient record activity in two health districts, Gaya and Gothèye, of 72.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for the Men5CV post-campaign coverage survey. Single-function: Estelab, the weekly notifiable-disease reporting in DHIS2, the DHIS2 intra-campaign monitoring module and the ministry's plateforme e-surveillance. Community: mHealth and its mHealth Monitor dashboard in Gouré, the World Vision and University of Toronto mobile child-health follow-up of 2014 to 2016 and the Save the Children and USAID mobile follow-up of acute malnutrition of 2013 to 2016. Enabling: the Computerised Maintenance and Management System (GMAO), telemedicine at referral hospitals, the projet national de télémédecine and the Réseau Informatique National de l'Administration.

Not held. No count of health centres using the computerised record is held, and no denominator. Nothing held says whether the record is made at the point of care or keyed afterwards, and no sites or counts are given for OpenClinic.
