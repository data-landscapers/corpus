Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

510 local government administration offices were counted on broadband in December 2025; how many offices there are, and what they do online, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The World Bank's BRIDGE project, approved on 6 October 2025, sets a target of 3,428 local government administration offices on broadband by September 2030. The Bank names the Federal Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy as implementing agency; a company in which private investors are to hold the majority, not yet incorporated on 4 August 2026, is to build the fibre. Of the Bank's US$500 million credit, nothing was disbursed at 24 July 2026.

What the office does over it. The Bank's results framework records the offices as connected to broadband internet at 17 December 2025. Local government area offices enter health facilities' monthly paper returns into the national DHIS2, on the Federal Ministry of Health's report for 2021.

How the office is connected. The Bank counts the local government administration offices themselves as facilities connected to broadband.

Last twelve months. The credit was signed on 30 December 2025 and the project declared effective on 27 April 2026. On 22 September 2026 the communications minister cut the delivery timeline from five years to three, with cable laying to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Local governments connected. The World Bank counts 510 local government administration offices connected to broadband at 17 December 2025, the same number as in July 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Backbone: the fibre backbone of Project BRIDGE, Bridge Open Access, the operators' national fibre network, MTN Nigeria's fibre network, the tower programme the government calls NUCAP, the 3,700 telecommunications towers, the National Anchor Institutions Connectivity Programme, Galaxy Backbone, MainOne, and the InfraCo Project, NICTIB and GOVNET of the 2019 digital economy strategy. State networks and platforms: the Lagos unified fibre network, the Lagos Identity Project, the Lagos PHC Digitization Project and the planned Smart Health Information Platform, the Lagos State Resident Identity Card, the Katsina State fibre network and Katsina's joint committee with MTN Nigeria and the regulator, the Ogun State fibre network, Smart Anambra, the Anambra State ICT Agency's AI system and its digital compliance assessment, Benue State's platforms, BENGIS among them, and its geographic information portal, start-up platform and civil servants' training, Ondo Pay, the Imo Digital Economy Agenda, the Enugu TradEX Programme, the NCC Digital Industrial Park and Learning Centre in Enugu, the Akwa Ibom Employment Portal, Primero in Oyo State, EdoCert, the state data-sharing platforms, the Nasarawa state data centre, the state digital land registries, the states' tax administration systems, the state e-procurement systems, the State EMIS and the State Addressing GIS Unit. Applications: SmartGov Suite in Anambra's 21 local governments, the Local Electronic Medical Records system of Ojodu Local Council Development Area, SORMAS, DHIS2, RI-SMS, the DHIS2 EMIS module, what the Federal Ministry of Education calls DNEMIS, NEMIS and the Local Government EMIS, the electronic health records of the health cooperation memorandum with the United States, the national digitised addressing system and the Local Addressing Database. Web: .gov.ng domains for Anambra's local governments and MyLagosApp. Public access: the Universal Service Provision Fund's projects, the community resource centres, the community-centred connectivity initiatives, the Siun Community Self-Built Connectivity Hub, the ARISE ICT hubs and Youth Centres of Akwa Ibom State, Anambra's free public Wi-Fi, the Digital Village initiative and the Zero-Rated Access Initiative. City systems: Cross River State's Smart Surveillance Infrastructure Project and the Lagos surveillance and traffic systems. Capacity: the proposed partnership of NITDA and Kwali Area Council and Benue's digital skills programme for state and local government staff. Central: VitalReg, the National Population Commission's civil registration platform, the National Identity Database, the National Social Register, the National Case Management Dashboard, the Nigeria Education Data Initiative, the UBEC digital quality assurance platform, EMID, the BPP Digital Submission Portal, the connected police stations initiative, WAPIS, the Emergency Communications Centres, the NCC Ease of Doing Business Portal, the alphanumeric postcode system, the postal service's Digital Addressing System and Address Verification System, the National Addressing Database, the Nigerian Postcode System database and Data.gov.ng.

Not held. How many local government administration offices there are, and so what share the 510 is; where the connected offices are, who provides or pays for their connections and what is done over them. No domestic budget line is stated, and no office is yet connected by the project.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

91.5% of all health facilities reported into the national system in June 2025, with local government offices entering the figures from paper.

Governance, planning, finance. The Federal Ministry of Health issues the monthly bulletin of the National Health Management Information System (NHMIS), and its annual report for 2021 places the single national DHIS2 instance in the ministry's Department of Health Planning, Research and Statistics. In the health memorandum signed with the United States on 19 December 2025, Nigeria commits to use DHIS2 as its national health data warehouse, and the United States plans to fund it over 2026-2030.

Tiers in use. The ministry's bulletin gives the national health facility reporting rate for the NHMIS monthly summary on DHIS-2 as 91.5% for June 2025, and charts the rate for primary, secondary and tertiary facilities, public and private.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's annual report for 2021 has facilities submit a paper monthly summary form to the LGA office by the 7th, where it is entered into DHIS2 by the 14th. Gavi's Zero-Dose Learning Hub assessment, published in November 2024, states that monthly reports are entered manually into DHIS2 at LGA offices. In June 2025 the ministry encouraged states to deploy mobile devices and computers for direct reporting to DHIS-2.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 19 December 2025 expects DHIS2 to collect and report all of its outcome and process metrics.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. A WHO-led study found that none of the 16 randomly selected public primary health care facilities it visited in Nigeria in 2016-2017 had electronic data collection.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: ViVA EMR, NigeriaMRS, LAMIS Plus, the National Data Repository, Electronic Management of Immunization Data (EMID) and the public health programme software for tuberculosis, HIV and others. Single-function: Last Mile Vaccine Stock Visibility (LMVSV), OpenLMIS, the Nigeria Health Logistics Management Information System (NHLMIS), M-Supply, the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS), SORMAS, the routine immunisation module of DHIS2, Routine Immunisation Short Message Service (RI-SMS), the Logistics Management Information System application and the programme monthly forms on the national DHIS2 instance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS), the Automated National Standing Orders app, CommCare and the Community Monthly Summary Form.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and the 2016-2017 sample does not carry the country. No dated event in the last twelve months is held, and Nigeria's own commitment to data systems is not broken down to DHIS2.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

100 of Lagos State's 326 primary clinics record care electronically as it happens (May 2025); there is no national figure.

Governance, planning, finance. The Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare's Nigeria Digital Health Architecture of December 2025 sets interoperable electronic health record systems across all levels of care as its first pillar. In the memorandum signed with the United States on 19 December 2025, Nigeria commits to roll digital medical records out in 20% of facilities by 2026 and 100% by 2030, and the United States plans approximately US$28 million for an enhanced electronic medical record system over 2026-2030.

Tiers in use. The Minister of State for Health told the National Council on Health on 20 November 2025 that over 200 hospitals are integrated with electronic medical records systems. In Lagos State an EMR was in use in primary health care centres in May 2025.

Where data is digitised. In May 2025 the Lagos State Primary Health Care Board described its EMR as working "at the point of care". The federal architecture of December 2025 states that health data systems remain largely siloed and paper based, especially in public facilities.

Last twelve months. On 16 September 2026 the minister of state for health said the electronic medical record systems deployed in health facilities are fragmented and that minimum standards would be set and enforced.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Lagos State Ministry of Health reported on 22 May 2025 the EMR platform deployed in 100 of the state's 326 primary health care facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: ViVA EMR, NigeriaMRS, LAMIS Plus, the National Data Repository, Electronic Management of Immunization Data (EMID) and the public health programme software for tuberculosis, HIV and others. Single-function: Last Mile Vaccine Stock Visibility (LMVSV), OpenLMIS, the Nigeria Health Logistics Management Information System (NHLMIS), M-Supply, the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS), SORMAS, the routine immunisation module of DHIS2, Routine Immunisation Short Message Service (RI-SMS), the Logistics Management Information System application and the programme monthly forms on the national DHIS2 instance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS), the Automated National Standing Orders app, CommCare and the Community Monthly Summary Form.

Not held. No national share or count of primary clinics using an EMR is held; the figure is Lagos State's alone, and the over 200 hospitals have no denominator or tier. No shared record on a unique identifier is evidenced, and Nigeria's own commitment to data systems is not broken down to the EHR.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A national learner number was launched in 2026 and issued first to examination candidates; whether any primary school enters learners' records is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Federal Ministry of Education launched the Learner Identification Number as its own system, on its statement of 9 April 2026. Biometric Update, on 5 August 2026, names the ministry as the body that, with NIMC, linked the learner numbers to the national identity number registry.

Levels in use. In the first phase the numbers went to candidates registered for the 2026 WAEC and NECO examinations.

How the record is kept. Admission into Edo State's public primary and junior secondary schools is entirely manual, on forms and photocopied certificates, state officials told the News Agency of Nigeria in a report of 31 July 2026. At Nassarawo Primary School in Adamawa State a teacher marks about 50 pupils' attendance each day by hand in a paper register, DPI Africa reported on 12 March 2026.

Last twelve months. The learner number began rolling out in April 2026, with 1.9 million issued. From 14 September 2026 every public and private school in Lagos State must take each learner's residents' registration number at admission and enter it in its records, towards a state database of learners.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Oyo State's system to give every child a learner number was still under development on 31 July 2026. Edo State plans a system tracking each student, and the one it runs takes school-level data, DHIS2 stated on 30 May 2025.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school count is the Annual School Census, whose 2025/26 round is to run entirely on what the Federal Ministry of Education calls its Digital National Education Management Information System, DNEMIS, with a Public DNEMIS Portal; the census system is also named as the Nigeria Education Management Information System, NEMIS, and a DHIS2 EMIS module is being rolled out in twelve states. Single-function: EdoEMIS, as Edo State names its school-level reporting system, the UBEC digital quality assurance platform, EdoCert, the Nigerian Education Repository Data Bank and the National Credential Verification Service. Learning: uLesson, Teesas, Tuteria, AltSchool Africa and the Nigeria Learning Passport. Enabling: GenU9ja and the Initiative for Zero-Rated Access to Educational Platforms and Content.

Not held. Which school levels use the learner number, who enters it, and whether any primary school fills the register. The paper statements cover one state's admissions and one school, and no plan or finance for the system is stated.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are entered digitally at ward and local government offices from July 2026; the share of local offices doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Population Commission runs the registration and its centres, and said in July 2026 that the platform is operated under a public-private partnership with a technical partner. The Registrar General's Department lists a Digital CRVS Roadmap for 2024 to 2026. A 2026 programme to speed up birth registration in Kano State is funded by the Government of the Netherlands.

Events registered digitally. Births and deaths have been registered digitally across the country since 1 July 2026, on the platform the Commission calls VitalReg.

How the record is made. The Registrar General's Department described in July 2026 an officer in ward and local government offices registering the birth on the platform, with real-time reporting of registration data. A registrar in Lagos said in August 2026 that his team takes details on a form when the network fails and calls parents back.

Last twelve months. The Commission says the platform became operational in all 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory on 1 July 2026, replacing paper registration.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Registrar General's Department reported 3,372 wards activated and 304 registration workstations deployed at 18 July 2026. The Commission said that month that it has 4,011 registration centres across the 774 local government areas.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the National Identification Number and the National Identity Database of the National Identity Management Commission, its National Identity Management System and Automated Biometric Identification System, and Smile ID, a private company's. Notification: a health-facility birth notification pipeline and community birth notification by health workers and traditional birth attendants. Outreach: registration of pupils in schools, mobile registration units and ad hoc registration exercises, the programme named Accelerating Birth Registration and Leave No Child Behind in Nigeria 2026 and the Renewed Hope Initiative birth registration project. Exchange: a NIN generated for the child at birth registration, integration of birth registration with primary healthcare and immunisation and the e-CRVS certificate required to change a date of birth on the NIN database.

Not held. The share of local offices entering on the platform: the wards activated have no total, and the count of registration centres does not say how many of them enter on it. No domestic budget line is held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Stations made statements, reports and the crime diary by hand in 2021 and 2022; whether that still holds is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Police Affairs' press unit said in June 2021 that divisional police stations lack basic ICT infrastructure.

Records made digitally. The ministry's press unit stated in June 2021 that statements and reports in police stations are made manually, and that most criminal records of offenders and property across the country are not made electronically. A paper by Jimoh, Wajiga and Garba, published in June 2022, found criminal records kept by hand in a crime diary or ledger at the stations its authors observed.

How the record is made. Sources writing to Canada's Immigration and Refugee Board in October 2020 described statements and police reports written on a standard paper form at the division, with some records kept electronically at state commands or police headquarters. In June 2021 the ministry said writing a statement, generating a report for citizens and entering biodata were done manually.

Last twelve months. The 2022 paper described crime reporting and detailing in stations as a manual method.

Stations doing the digital entry. A law-firm partner told the Board in October 2020 that use of computer networks and electronic databases was still very low among police formations.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the Nigeria Police Crime and Incident Database Centre and the National Criminal Data Fusion Centre, as the Ministry of Police Affairs named them in December 2024; the National Cybercrime Centre's forensic analysis; and a digital case management solution with a biometric database, being worked on in 2019. Public: the Vigilant App, launched on 1 September 2026; Hawk Eye (2017), Rescue Me (2021) and the National Cybercrime Centre's reporting portal; the 112 emergency number, approved and not yet rolled out in September 2026, with about 35 Emergency Communications Centres; and the Police Public Complaints Committee, revived in 2021. Surveillance: Lagos State's CCTV and integrated command and control centre; a CCTV project being reactivated in 2021; and the National Command and Control Centre, announced in December 2019. Enabling: the memorandum of 22 April 2026 to map and digitalise police stations, with its connected police stations initiative and national GIS database of stations; computers and solar power for police legal departments in 15 state commands, from UNDP; and Korean-funded training in cybercrime investigation, closed in June 2026.

Not held. Whether stations still record by hand after 2022 is not known. What the Smart Police Station launched in Gombe State in June 2026 records, who owns and funds any station record system, and how many stations enter records digitally are not held.
