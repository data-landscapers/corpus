The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Digital transformation strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria has a ten-year digital economy strategy unveiled in 2019, but no source found reports results against its targets and its funding.

Governance and finance. The Federal Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy was assigned its development and implementation, and the March 2020 broadband plan says a National Digital Economy Council was established to oversee it, but the text ties no budget line to it.

Standing. President Buhari unveiled the National Digital Economy Policy and Strategy on 28 November 2019, and a 2025 ITU repository lists it as the country's strategy.

Delivery plan. It sets eight pillars with listed government actions and dated targets, among them 70% broadband penetration in four years and 95% digital literacy in ten, with no cost stated.

Reporting. At the 20 December 2022 commissioning of the National Shared Services Centre, implementation was said to have produced "giant strides", with no figure against a target.

Nigeria's Data Protection Act of 2023 is in force and applied through fines and court orders; no source found counts decisions since mid-2024.

Authority. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission supervises the Act. National Commissioner Vincent Olatunji heads it, and it keeps a register of data processors of major importance.

Standing. President Tinubu signed the Nigeria Data Protection Act on 12 June 2023, and the High Court of the Federal Capital Territory applied it in a judgment of 29 July 2026. A Nigeria Data Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2026 was before the National Assembly in July 2026.

Regulations. The minister's office names the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 as the operating framework under the Act for all tiers of government. The Commission's Guidance Notice on Registration designates data controllers and processors of major importance.

Application. The Commission fined Multichoice Nigeria N766,242,500 in July 2025. On 29 July 2026 the court ordered Stanbic IBTC Bank to pay two former customers N15 million and erase their data. A Commission order fining Meta $32.8 million was set aside when the Federal High Court adopted a settlement, by consent judgment on 3 November 2025. By 11 June 2024 the Commission said sanctions on seven firms had brought in N400 million. By October 2026 41,000 organisations had registered, against 630 in the first year.

Last twelve months. The Federal High Court upheld the Commission's power to register controllers and processors of major importance, and on 28 July 2026 the Commission directed every unregistered one to register. It opened investigations into Temu in February 2026 and Remita and Sterling Bank on 1 April 2026.

Cybersecurity legislation

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law is in force and was used in 2026 against activist Omoyele Sowore, and in 2024 against journalists and critics.

Law in force. The Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act is an enacted law of 2015 and was amended in 2024. The amended Act contains interception provisions, and SERAP says the 2024 amendment retains ambiguous cyberstalking wording in section 24. Section 24 prescribes up to three years' imprisonment or a fine for sending a message known to be false. The Southern Africa Litigation Centre said on 15 October 2026 that section 24 is repeatedly used to arrest journalists for their reporting.

Use of the law. In December 2024 activist Olamide Thomas was arrested and charged for allegedly insulting the President's son on Facebook. The Department of State Services is prosecuting activist and presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore over his description of the President as a criminal, and its Director-General announced on 30 August 2026 that the prosecution would be suspended until after the 2027 election. SERAP says authorities continue to use section 24 to arrest and charge journalists, bloggers, activists and ordinary citizens. Reporters Without Borders reported in 2024 that at least eight journalists had been arrested, prosecuted or detained under the Act.

Also on file. Code of Practice for Interactive Computer Service Platforms, issued by NITDA.
