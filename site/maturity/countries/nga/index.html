Strategies, plans and policies

Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria has a ten-year digital economy strategy unveiled in 2019, but results against its targets and its funding are not known.

Governance and finance. The Federal Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy was assigned its development and implementation, and the March 2020 broadband plan says a National Digital Economy Council was established to oversee it, but the text ties no budget line to it.

Standing. President Buhari unveiled the National Digital Economy Policy and Strategy on 28 November 2019, and a 2025 ITU repository lists it as the country's strategy.

Text. The full text is held on the NITDA site.

Period. The strategy is titled and dated for 2020 to 2030.

Delivery plan. It sets eight pillars with listed government actions and dated targets, among them 70% broadband penetration in four years and 95% digital literacy in ten, with no cost stated.

Reporting. At the 20 December 2022 commissioning of the National Shared Services Centre, implementation was said to have produced "giant strides", with no figure against a target.

Last twelve months. NITDA's National Cloud Investment Strategy of August 2026 says the strategy continues to provide the overarching national policy framework.

Noted, not assessed. Project BRIDGE; Nigeria Universal Communication Access Project; Universal Service Provision Fund; e-Government Master Plan 2.0; Payments System Vision 2028; National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill; Project 774 Local Government Connectivity Initiative; e-Government Masterplan, phase 2 (2022-2026).

Not held. Results against the strategy's targets are not known: reporting is statements of progress only. The plan states no cost, and no funding source is known.

ICT strategy: sector policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria's telecommunications policy of 2000 is still in force and is being rewritten in 2026; its finance and reporting against its targets are not known.

Governance and finance. The National Telecommunications Policy 2000 assigns policy formulation to the Ministry of Communications and regulation to the Nigerian Communications Commission, and sets up a National Frequency Management Council in the Ministry.

Standing. The Minister of Communications' preface of May 2000 presents the policy as approved by the President, and the Commission's consultation paper of 9 February 2026 calls it the subsisting sector policy, in force for twenty-five years.

Text. The Ministry of Communications published the full text, in seventeen chapters, in May 2000.

Period. The consultation paper states no end date for the policy, whose Chapter 16 provides for review from time to time.

Delivery plan. The consultation paper proposes to add an implementation matrix with milestones, dependencies and responsible parties, which the 2000 text does not have.

Reporting. Chapter 16 of the policy names annual reports among its three implementation instruments, beside implementation machinery and a national forum.

Last twelve months. In February 2026 the Commission published fifteen proposals for rewriting the policy and invited comments by 20 March 2026, and in May 2026 it held a review workshop with regulators, operators and investors.

Noted, not assessed. Unified national policy and governance framework; Payments System Vision 2028 (PSV 2028); Project BRIDGE.

Not held. No report against the policy's own targets is held, and no source of finance is stated. The committees set up to review the policy belong to the rewrite, which is still being drafted and has not replaced the 2000 text.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria's Data Protection Act of 2023 is in force and applied through fines and court orders; the number of decisions since mid-2024 is not known.

Authority. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission supervises the Act, having replaced the Nigeria Data Protection Bureau in June 2023. National Commissioner Vincent Olatunji heads it, and it keeps a register of data processors of major importance.

Standing. President Tinubu signed the Nigeria Data Protection Act on 12 June 2023, and the High Court of the Federal Capital Territory applied it in a judgment of 29 July 2026. A Nigeria Data Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2026 was before the National Assembly in July 2026.

Regulations. The minister's office names the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 as the operating framework under the Act for all tiers of government. The Commission's Guidance Notice on Registration designates data controllers and processors of major importance.

Application. The Commission fined Multichoice Nigeria N766,242,500 in July 2025. On 29 July 2026 the court ordered Stanbic IBTC Bank to pay two former customers N15 million and erase their data. A Commission order fining Meta $32.8 million was set aside when the Federal High Court adopted a settlement. By 11 June 2024 the Commission said sanctions on seven firms had brought in N400 million.

Last twelve months. The Federal High Court upheld the Commission's power to register controllers and processors of major importance, and on 28 July 2026 the Commission directed every unregistered one to register. It opened investigations into Temu in February 2026 and into Remita and Sterling Bank on 1 April 2026, and moved into a new Abuja headquarters on 14 September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Section 37 of the 1999 Constitution, the right to privacy; the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation, issued by NITDA before the 2023 Act.

Not held. No count of decisions or sanctions for any period ending after 11 June 2024. The date on which the Meta order was set aside, and how far the Amendment Bill has gone in the National Assembly.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law is in force and was used in 2026 against activist Omoyele Sowore, and in 2024 against journalists and critics.

Law in force. The Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act is an enacted law of 2015 and was amended in 2024. The amended Act contains interception provisions, and SERAP says the 2024 amendment retains ambiguous cyberstalking wording in section 24. Section 24 prescribes up to three years' imprisonment or a fine for sending a message known to be false.

Use of the law. The Act has been used against critics, journalists and activists. In December 2024 activist Olamide Thomas was arrested and charged for allegedly insulting the President's son on Facebook. The Department of State Services is prosecuting activist and presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore over his description of the President as a criminal, and its Director-General announced on 30 August 2026 that the prosecution would be suspended until after the 2027 election. SERAP says authorities continue to use section 24 to arrest and charge journalists, bloggers, activists and ordinary citizens. Reporters Without Borders reported in 2024 that at least eight journalists had been arrested, prosecuted or detained under the Act.

Noted, not assessed. Code of Practice for Interactive Computer Service Platforms, issued by NITDA.

Not held. The number of cases investigated, prosecuted or decided under the law in a period ending in the last two years.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria's NIMC Act 2026, signed in June, makes the identity number foundational and provides for electronic verification. No rules made under it are known.

Law in force. Nigeria's National Identity Management Commission Act 2026 was signed into law on 26 June 2026, repealing the 2007 Act. The bill had passed second reading in the Senate in July 2024. A civil society speaker said on 29 July 2026 that the gazetted text was not yet available.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The Act reaffirms the national identification number as the foundational identity and makes the Commission responsible for verifying electronic identities. It makes the Commission the national repository for biometric data and, by one account, makes the number compulsory across fourteen sectors. The Commission described its work in September 2026 as in compliance with the Act. The Act was three months old at the end of September 2026, and no regulation made under it is on record.

Noted, not assessed. National Identity Number; National Public Key Infrastructure; Nigeria Digital Identification for Development Project; NINAuth; NIMC digital identity infrastructure.

AI legislation / regulations: AI law or binding rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2025 data protection directive sets duties for anyone deploying AI; several AI bills are before parliament and none has passed.

Binding rule on AI in force. Nigeria has a binding rule that names AI, inside its data protection rules. The data protection commission's General Application and Implementation Directive of 2025 requires an organisation deploying AI to document its parameters, assess unequal outcomes and test the system, under article 43, and to stop using AI systems that pose undue risks to human rights, under article 44.

General AI law and the law applied. No law on AI itself is in force. Four AI bills passed second reading in the House of Representatives on 3 December 2024, and a proposed Artificial Intelligence Bill with a licensing regime and an AI council was under debate in November 2025. In January 2026 the government was preparing to regulate AI through the National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill, with yearly impact assessments for high-risk systems.

Noted, not assessed. Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (NAIS); National AI strategy.

Not held. No source held says a law on AI itself, applying across sectors, is in force.

Law in force. Nigeria's law is the Freedom of Information Act, 2011, assented to on 28 May 2011, which gives any person a right to request information held by public institutions. The Federal High Court treats it as taking precedence over the Petroleum Industry Act where the two conflict.

Law put to work and applied. The courts enforce the Act. On 13 October 2025 the Federal High Court in Abuja held the national oil company a public institution under the Act and ordered it to supply information requested on a $3 billion loan. On 24 February 2026 the Federal High Court in Lagos ruled that the Nigeria Shippers Council had breached the Act over a journalist's request of 2020, and ordered disclosure or reasons. The Act has no information commission; rules made under it are not recorded.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nigeria's 2023 data protection law sets rules on sending personal data abroad; whether the authority has published lists, contract terms or guidance is not known.

Rule in force. The Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023, signed on 12 June 2023, has a Part VIII on cross-border transfers of personal data, in sections 41 to 43: the basis for transfer, the adequacy of protection, and other bases for transfer outside Nigeria. Vanguard reports the National Commissioner of the Nigeria Data Protection Commission saying that the Act, enacted in June 2023, mandates the Commission to decide the adequacy of other countries' data protection as a basis for cross-border transfer of personal data.

Means of complying published and rule applied. According to the National Commissioner, in Vanguard's report of 30 April 2025, the Commission approves binding corporate rules, codes and certification mechanisms as means of transferring personal data across borders under the Act. The Act's Part VIII on cross-border transfers is listed in the Official Gazette text as covering other bases for transfer outside Nigeria.

Noted, not assessed. None held. Nigeria's joining of a global cross-border privacy forum is reported by Vanguard in April 2025.

Not held. Whether the Commission has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad is not known, and nothing held shows how the rule has been applied.

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

510 local government administration offices were counted on broadband in December 2025; how many offices there are, and what they do online, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The World Bank's BRIDGE project, approved on 6 October 2025, sets a target of 3,428 local government administration offices on broadband by September 2030. The Bank names the Federal Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy as implementing agency; a company in which private investors are to hold the majority, not yet incorporated on 4 August 2026, is to build the fibre. Of the Bank's US$500 million credit, nothing was disbursed at 24 July 2026.

What the office does over it. The Bank's results framework records the offices as connected to broadband internet at 17 December 2025. Local government area offices enter health facilities' monthly paper returns into the national DHIS2, on the Federal Ministry of Health's report for 2021.

How the office is connected. The Bank counts the local government administration offices themselves as facilities connected to broadband.

Last twelve months. The credit was signed on 30 December 2025 and the project declared effective on 27 April 2026. On 22 September 2026 the communications minister cut the delivery timeline from five years to three, with cable laying to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

91.5% of all health facilities reported into the national system in June 2025, with local government offices entering the figures from paper.

Governance, planning, finance. The Federal Ministry of Health issues the monthly bulletin of the National Health Management Information System (NHMIS), and its annual report for 2021 places the single national DHIS2 instance in the ministry's Department of Health Planning, Research and Statistics. In the health memorandum signed with the United States on 19 December 2025, Nigeria commits to use DHIS2 as its national health data warehouse, and the United States plans to fund it over 2026-2030.

Tiers in use. The ministry's bulletin gives the national health facility reporting rate for the NHMIS monthly summary on DHIS-2 as 91.5% for June 2025, and charts the rate for primary, secondary and tertiary facilities, public and private.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's annual report for 2021 has facilities submit a paper monthly summary form to the LGA office by the 7th, where it is entered into DHIS2 by the 14th. In June 2025 the ministry encouraged states to deploy mobile devices and computers for direct reporting to DHIS-2.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 19 December 2025 expects DHIS2 to collect and report all of its outcome and process metrics.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. A WHO-led study found that none of the 16 randomly selected public primary health care facilities it visited in Nigeria in 2016-2017 had electronic data collection.
