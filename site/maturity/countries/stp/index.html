Cross-border data transfers

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2016 law limits sending personal data abroad to places with adequate protection, in force as of 2022; no source reports published ways to comply.

Rule in force. Law No. 03/2016, called Lei n.º 03/2016 in the country, was passed on 15 February 2016 and promulgated on 18 March 2016. It allows personal data to leave the national territory only where the receiving legal system ensures an adequate level of protection, with the National Data Protection Agency (ANPDP) deciding adequacy under Article 19. Article 20 sets exceptions: notification to the ANPDP for unequivocal consent or contractual necessity, and ANPDP authorisation backed by contractual clauses. Breaching Articles 19 and 20 carries fines of 45 to 90 million dobras. A factsheet dated 24 June 2022 lists the law as in place, with the authority operational.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest held fact is that the ANPDP decides adequacy and grants authorisation backed by contractual clauses, under Articles 19 and 20 of the law. The authority is listed as operational as of 24 June 2022.

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A 2024 law publishes interoperability standards for government systems and makes the mandatory ones compulsory; no system is known to have been checked.

Standards published. Lei 1/2024, passed by the National Assembly on 3 November 2023, promulgated on 30 November and gazetted on 29 January 2024, institutes the National Interoperability Framework (QNI), a minimum set of premises, policies and technical specifications governing how ICT is used to make e-government services interoperate. It applies to all public institutions and took effect 30 days after publication. Its annex sets the specifications out by segment, from interconnection to security, at version 1.2, naming the reference standard for each component, such as the open cloud computing interface (OCCI).

Standards required and checked. The law grades each specification as mandatory or recommended: a mandatory item has been adopted by the government as a norm after formal approval and must be adopted in every new IT product or project, while a recommended item is suggested but not compulsory. A transitional article keeps existing public-service systems valid until they are updated or replaced to conform to the framework. No source found names a system tested or certified against the specifications.
