Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Whether any district council office is connected is not known as at 2026, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Application: the state financial administration system the country calls SAFE; a public information management system with a territorial cadastre, a 2025 target of the Príncipe regional government's 2019 plan. Web: the yearly online publication of the Príncipe regional budget, an indicator of the same plan. Public access: the telecentres in the district capitals, set up under the G-net project. Backbone: the government network the country calls Rede do Governo, built with Brazilian cooperation from 2008; G-net, a data and voice network linking all ministries; the government fibre-optic backbone, serving 82 institutions by June 2020; the government's Centro de Dados; the Príncipe submarine cable, not in operation at 19 September 2025; Cabo Verde's national data centre in Praia, which lists São Tomé among its government customers.

Not held. Which institutions are on the government network, and whether any district council is. The digital governance strategy is held cut off before its network chapter.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national reporting system was introduced in all districts in 2024-2025; who uses it and where reports are entered is not yet known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health launched DHIS2 as the health information system on 18 December 2019. Its National Health Development Plan 2023-2032, dated September 2022, carries a costed health information pillar that names DHIS2 and states that the system has the support of WHO, Gavi, UNFPA and UNICEF.

Tiers in use. The plan of September 2022 states that the ministry approved DHIS2 for nationwide use and that, two years after implementation, its use covered only the vaccination, reproductive health, tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and malaria programmes.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's page on the 2019 launch describes DHIS2 as a web platform for digital forms and indicators, with data stored at national level.

Last twelve months. The WHO country office reports that during the 2024-2025 biennium DHIS2 was implemented in all districts and district health managers were trained on the platform, on data quality and on report generation.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The plan of September 2022 lists focal points in health units and equipment for health centres to run DHIS2 among its actions, and states that health professionals were not adhering to the system even where it was available.

Noted, not assessed. The ministry's DHIS2 Tracker, the HIV Patient Tracking, HIV testing (DIPS) and TB Patient Tracking modules, the Malaria Notification and Investigation, COVID-19 surveillance and Death Registry modules, integrated disease surveillance and response data, mSupply, the laboratory system the plan calls SIDLAB, the surveillance monitoring dashboard, Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS), Zzapp Malaria, telemedicine between health units, and the Piauí Saúde Digital teleconsultation platform.

Not held. Which tiers use DHIS2 routinely, where the data is keyed and how many primary clinics enter their own reports are not established. No statement of who finances the system is held from the last three years, and the all-district implementation of 2024-2025 carries no month.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A patient record was piloted at the central hospital and in one health area in 2022; whether it was extended is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Health Development Plan 2023-2032, dated September 2022, sets a strategic axis on expanding what it calls the single clinical process to the whole national health system. The Joint SDG Fund programme's final report of 26 July 2022 states that the Single Process module for patient files was developed inside DHIS2, the Ministry of Health's main information software, and that rolling it out beyond the pilot will be done with funding from other donors.

Tiers in use. In September 2022 the plan placed the record in a pilot phase at the Ayres de Menezes central hospital and the Água Grande Health Area.

Where data is digitised. The report of July 2022 states that patient data is entered from reception through the nurses and doctors, each patient's file reached by a bar code sticker and scanner.

Last twelve months. The plan of September 2022 states that the pilot is to be evaluated before expansion to the whole national health system.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. At 26 July 2022 the Single Process had not been rolled out to other districts and still needed consolidating in the first pilot district, Água Grande. The plan states that computerised clinical records are to be introduced gradually through a pilot model of health centres and the central hospital.

Noted, not assessed. The ministry's DHIS2 Tracker, the HIV Patient Tracking, HIV testing (DIPS) and TB Patient Tracking modules, the Malaria Notification and Investigation, COVID-19 surveillance and Death Registry modules, integrated disease surveillance and response data, mSupply, the laboratory system the plan calls SIDLAB, the surveillance monitoring dashboard, Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS), Zzapp Malaria, telemedicine between health units, and the Piauí Saúde Digital teleconsultation platform.

Not held. Nothing is dated after September 2022: whether the pilot was evaluated, consolidated or extended beyond Água Grande is not established. The health area's facilities are not named and no count of facilities using the record is given.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools sent data to the ministry's school-management system in 2023; whether primary schools keep a per-pupil record is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. São Tomé and Príncipe calls its school census an EMIS: the education ministry's Sistema Integrado de Gestão Escolar (SIGE), which the country also writes as EMIS/SIGE, is one that schools send data to, described in 2023 beside the ministry's Boletim Estatístico Anual. The ministry's planning directorate runs the Censo Escolar. The Sistema de Supervisão Pedagógica is an online platform on which 22 supervisors enter classroom observation forms for 111 pre-schools, and in May 2026 UNICEF was tendering for dashboards for national school supervision. The ministry ran the AALEB national learning assessment in grades 2, 4 and 6 in 2016 and 2019, and has a revamped inspection system. The country names a national digital learning platform, the Passaporte para a aprendizagem.

Not held. No dated statement says whether primary schools keep their pupils' records on paper, and nothing held describes a system with a record for each pupil that a school adds to during the year.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are entered directly in the national database at maternities, as of 2026; the share of local offices doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Direcção Geral dos Registos e do Notariado, an autonomous body within the justice ministry, is responsible for civil registration and developed its system with its own technicians, while the University of Aveiro in Portugal maintains and hosts it, the World Bank said in 2023. The same report found no strategic plan specifically for civil registration and identification.

Events registered digitally. The chief conservator of the civil registry said in March 2026 that births are registered by entering the data directly into the directorate's database. The World Bank said in 2023 that the system the directorate calls SIGA, implemented by 2018, registers births, deaths and marriages.

How the record is made. At the maternities the directorate's technicians and trained nurse-midwives make the registration directly in the system, on computers and printers there, the chief conservator said in March 2026. In the directorate's centres registration is done digitally, connected to servers hosted in Portugal, the World Bank said in 2023.

Last twelve months. In March 2026 the chief conservator said the directorate is eliminating paper by registering births directly from the maternities.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The chief conservator said in March 2026 that registration is done in all the country's maternities. The World Bank in 2023 counted 11 centres for all civil registration services and seven birth registration posts in maternities.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: birth records from 1920 to 2010 digitised into the central database, with births of 2010 to 2023 and other life events still to be digitised. Certificate: a document-issuing system at the embassy in Portugal, from April 2025, and QR code verification of certificates by authorised third parties. Outreach: awareness campaigns on birth registration for mothers who give birth at home. Enabling: equipment for the civil registry systems, handed over by the PReSE project in September 2025, and a satellite link and portable generator with the identity-card brigade. Identity: the identity card system and its central database, and the personal identification number issued at birth; a free identity card campaign, ended 15 March 2026; an identity card campaign for the diaspora in Portugal; a MOSIP-based digital ID pilot, begun in 2024; a new digital ID system in design at September 2025; the passport issuance system SEP 2.0; passport issuance without attendance for the diaspora; and the electronic passport, with a proposed digital identity and authentication platform. Exchange: Leginon, which gives other institutions controlled access to the register's data and links the civil registration and identification databases, and a proposed interoperability platform.

Not held. No share or count of the local offices entering records is published, and nothing says where deaths are entered. No finance fact from the last three years is held, and no current plan for civil registration.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A police crime-statistics system was being put in place in 2024; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Polícia Nacional's Centro de Comando e Controlo and its Sistema Zulu, which allows the compilation of crime statistics, expected to launch in August 2023 in the district of Água Grande and spoken of as being implemented in January 2024; at the Polícia Judiciária, XRY and Passware, in operational use as at June 2026, and the computer equipment handed over at its headquarters on 21 January 2026; the registo criminal of the Centro de Identificação Civil e Criminal, a central service in the city of São Tomé; DireitosAoPovo.st and its reporting modules VidaSemMedo and Denuncia, in development in June 2026; and the Sistema de Emissão de Documentos, which issues criminal record certificates and has run at the embassy in Portugal since April 2025.

Not held. No source held states whether stations record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
