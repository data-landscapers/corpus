Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Hospitals and health centres enter their own reports into the national system (2024); it is not known whether health posts do.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health holds full ownership of DHIS2's customisation and implementation, as assessed in June 2022, under a budgeted health information strategy and a 10-year strategic plan. The memorandum Ethiopia signed with the United States on 23 December 2025 names DHIS2 as the country's HMIS, and the United States plans US$18,850,149 of data-systems funding over 2026 to 2030, across all the systems it lists.

Tiers in use. In facility surveys of 2019 and 2020, 305 public hospitals and health centres reported a functional HMIS, and health posts have none of their own. The Ministry of Health counted more than 33,000 health facilities on DHIS2 in 2024.

Where data is digitised. Health information technicians key routine reports into DHIS2 at their own facilities, on interviews held from October 2023 to March 2024. Health posts record largely on paper and send monthly to their health centre, which compiles and submits, as surveyed in 2019 and 2020.

Last twelve months. The share of expected monthly facility reports received in DHIS2 rose from 90% in 2024 to 93% in 2025, on Countdown to 2030's figures.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. A study published in May 2023 states that more than 95% of all health facilities used DHIS-2, online or offline. A 2025 study puts over 4,000 primary health care institutions, 95% of those eligible, on DHIS2, counting district hospitals, health centres, health posts and district health offices.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the ART EMR, the PMTCT EMR and the HIV case-based surveillance system (CBSS). Single-function: the National Laboratory Information System, the laboratory database ETTORS, the logistics system Dagu, the pharmaceutical transaction service eAPTS, the DHIS2 routine surveillance system, the DHIS2 tracker for all-cause mortality surveillance, the Electronic Public Health Emergency Management system (ePHEM), Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS) and the Excel-based claim management for Community-Based Health Insurance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) and the paper Community Health Information System (CHIS). Enabling: HealthNet and telemedicine services.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held: both 95% figures count all or eligible health facilities, not primary clinics. Nothing dated after 2020 shows health posts entering their own data, and no exchange between eCHIS and DHIS2 is established.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

More than 70 facilities had electronic patient records by December 2024, 10 of them paperless; a national pilot is due in 2026.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health presents electronic medical records as a milestone of its own digital health work in December 2024. The memorandum signed with the United States on 23 December 2025 schedules a national multi-tenant EMR: piloting in 2026, 48 facilities by 2027 and 438 by 2030. The United States plans to support its customisation and roll-out, Ethiopia paying the licensing, cloud and hardware costs the United States does not.

Tiers in use. EMR systems are implemented in health centres and hospitals, as the Central Ethiopia Regional Health Bureau states. In June 2023 the Bahmni EMR was in use at one general hospital and three health centres in Addis Ababa.

Where data is digitised. Providers at those four facilities entered consultations, prescriptions and laboratory orders in the EMR during care in June 2023, and the record was used across the departments of the one facility. The Ministry of Health states that 10 of the facilities with an EMR operate entirely paperless in December 2024.

Last twelve months. On 23 December 2025 Ethiopia and the United States signed the memorandum, with activities from 1 April 2026 to 31 December 2030.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Ministry of Health states that electronic medical records were introduced in over 70 health facilities in the three years to December 2024. The memorandum of 23 December 2025 gives a baseline of 0% of hospitals and health centres implementing the full national EMR.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the ART EMR, the PMTCT EMR and the HIV case-based surveillance system (CBSS). Single-function: the National Laboratory Information System, the laboratory database ETTORS, the logistics system Dagu, the pharmaceutical transaction service eAPTS, the DHIS2 routine surveillance system, the DHIS2 tracker for all-cause mortality surveillance, the Electronic Public Health Emergency Management system (ePHEM), Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS) and the Excel-based claim management for Community-Based Health Insurance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) and the paper Community Health Information System (CHIS). Enabling: HealthNet and telemedicine services.

Not held. The tier of the 70-plus facilities and the total they are drawn from are not held, and the 0% counts the full national system only. Neither most referral and district hospitals nor primary clinics across any district are shown using an EMR; the named primary sites are three health centres in Addis Ababa.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry's web-based system, with a number for each pupil, was piloted by June 2025; whether any primary school uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Education developed and completed its web-based EMIS and finalised a directive for it, with World Bank support, by June 2025. It was built under a Global Partnership for Education grant through the World Bank. The ministry's sector plan for 2020/21 to 2024/25 planned a student database and a digital application for schools to report pupils' daily data.

Levels in use. At June 2025 the system covered federal, regional, woreda and school levels, and deployment to 4,500 primary schools in 156 weredas was planned for July 2025 to June 2026.

How the record is kept. The system gives unique identification numbers to students, teachers and schools, as reported at June 2025. A UNESCO-commissioned case study researched in late 2019 found that schools keep registers on paper and collect attendance without storing it centrally.

Last twelve months. In November 2025 UNICEF partnered with HISP Ethiopia and the HISP Centre to assist the ministry and regional education bureaus on DHIS2 for Education, working with schools in four regions, where tools to collect learning outcomes and student attendance were still to be developed.

Primary schools doing the digital input. UNICEF, the grant agent, reports that by June 2025 the digitised system had been finalised and piloted, with deployment to primary schools not begun; the tender for 4,500 primary-school computers was set for August 2025. The sector plan published in 2021 gave a baseline of none for a student database and zero institutions networked.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the annual school census, which Ethiopia calls EMIS, the Education Statistics Annual Abstract (ESAA) built on it, the refugee EMIS and the Education Cluster's mobile data collection pilot. Single-function: the Teacher Management Information System, the Geographic Information System, the regional bureaux' monthly school surveys and start-of-year enrolment returns, the planned digital school inspection system and digital licensing system for teachers and school leaders, the intended national online examination platform and the proposed national textbook tracking system. Learning: the National Academic Digital Library of Ethiopia, Digital content for Secondary Schools, the Ethiopia Learning Passport, the National Learning Management System, the School Net Cloud, Plasma TV broadcast lessons, the Ethiopian Education Cloud and the Abrebi app. Enabling: SchoolNet.

Not held. Nothing shows the web-based system in live use in a primary school: no source dated after June 2025 reports the planned deployment. The number of pilot schools, the levels piloted and what the piloted system records are not stated.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Digital registration of births and deaths is in pilot, in Addis Ababa and 1,300 of 19,202 kebeles in 2025; use beyond pilots is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Immigration and Citizenship Service is the registration organ, with structures from federal to the lowest administrative level, under Proclamation No. 1370/2025 of 18 March 2025. The national eCRVS, which Ethiopia calls the Civil & Family Registration System, is homegrown, driven by the Prime Minister's Office and carries a three-phase rollout plan (July 2026).

Events registered digitally. The national eCRVS lists birth and death registration and certificates among its services, alongside marriage, divorce and adoption (July 2026).

How the record is made. The eCRVS is a decentralised, multi-tenant system on a shared architecture in which city administrations and regions provide all services, with offline access, rural connectivity, power and tablets listed as gaps (July 2026). Civil registration is mainly paper-based and manual, three copies of the form passing from kebele to woreda to the Service (July 2025).

Last twelve months. On 5 December 2025 the mayor of Addis Ababa launched the Integrated Civil Registration and Residency Service Technology and announced full implementation in 50 woredas from 8 December.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The eCRVS rollout begins with a pilot in Addis Ababa, then Amharic-speaking regions, then the rest of the country (July 2026). A tablet system, ME-CRVS, has been piloted in 1,300 kebeles and health facilities, in a country the Service counts at 19,202 kebeles, 70% of which provide civil registration (July 2025). In Addis Ababa the city agency's digitisation was in a pilot phase in 21 of 116 woredas in January 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: Fayda, the national digital ID; the kebele ID; VeriFayda 2, EthSwitch's identity check for banks; UNHCR's refugee register PRIMES; and family registration as the 2025 proclamation provides it. Notification: birth and death notification by health facilities within the eCRVS, and health workers' birth notification. Certificate: Addis MESOB, a one-stop service centre in Addis Ababa. Outreach: a joint field mission on refugee birth registration in Afar; birth registration at sites for displaced people in Oromia; and a special civil registration drive for Fayda in Addis Ababa in October 2024. Exchange: the integration of civil registration with Fayda; the PRIMES Interoperability Gateway between the refugee and national ID systems; and the Central Statistical Agency's compilation of vital statistics from register copies.

Not held. Whether any digital system is past its pilot outside Addis Ababa, which rollout phase the eCRVS had reached by July 2026, and whether the entry is made at the office. No source of finance is stated for the eCRVS, and nothing establishes that registering a birth feeds the Fayda number.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A criminal records system covering crime report to sentence was still being built in January 2026; no station is known to use it.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Justice says CRIMS, as it names its Criminal Records Information Management System, is being developed by the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute for the ministry, the Federal Police Commission and the Addis Ababa Police Commission to work in jointly, steered by a coordinating committee, in an item published on 20 January 2026.

Records made digitally. The system is under development and not yet in use, and is to record digitally everything from the crime report through investigation, charge, trial and sentence, the ministry said in January 2026.

How the record is made. Its modules cover report intake, investigation, juvenile offenders, offence type, biometric data, and prosecutors' and courts' decisions, on the ministry's account of January 2026.

Last twelve months. In January 2026 the ministry reported a coordinating-committee forum, with the minister and senior leaders of the three bodies, at which the system was said to be in development.

Stations doing the digital entry. In August 2023 the ministry was training federal investigating police and prosecutors, about 100 in all, in order to launch the system widely.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the Smart Police Station, one unmanned pilot station in the Bole district of Addis Ababa launched on 9 February 2026, and the Federal Police's EFP App for reporting crimes. Surveillance: CCTV surveillance systems and communications monitoring infrastructure.

Not held. No station, place or way of making the record is given for the system, and no launch is recorded since the 2023 training. No plan or source of finance is stated.
