Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Hospitals and health centres key reports into DHIS2 (2024); 95% is of all health facilities (2023). Health posts entering their own data not established.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health holds full ownership of DHIS2's customisation and implementation, as assessed in June 2022, under a budgeted health information strategy and a 10-year strategic plan. The memorandum Ethiopia signed with the United States on 23 December 2025 names DHIS2 as the country's HMIS, and the United States plans US$18,850,149 of data-systems funding over 2026 to 2030, across all the systems it lists.

Tiers in use. In facility surveys of 2019 and 2020, 305 public hospitals and health centres reported a functional HMIS, and health posts have none of their own. The Ministry of Health counted more than 33,000 health facilities on DHIS2 in 2024.

Where data is digitised. Health information technicians key routine reports into DHIS2 at their own facilities, on interviews held from October 2023 to March 2024. Health posts record largely on paper and send monthly to their health centre, which compiles and submits, as surveyed in 2019 and 2020.

Last twelve months. The share of expected monthly facility reports received in DHIS2 rose from 90% in 2024 to 93% in 2025, on Countdown to 2030's figures.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. A study published in May 2023 states that more than 95% of all health facilities used DHIS-2, online or offline. A 2025 study puts over 4,000 primary health care institutions, 95% of those eligible, on DHIS2, counting district hospitals, health centres, health posts and district health offices.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the ART EMR, the PMTCT EMR and the HIV case-based surveillance system (CBSS). Single-function: the National Laboratory Information System, the laboratory database ETTORS, the logistics system Dagu, the pharmaceutical transaction service eAPTS, the DHIS2 routine surveillance system, the DHIS2 tracker for all-cause mortality surveillance, the Electronic Public Health Emergency Management system (ePHEM), Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS) and the Excel-based claim management for Community-Based Health Insurance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) and the paper Community Health Information System (CHIS). Enabling: HealthNet and telemedicine services.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held: both 95% figures count all or eligible health facilities, not primary clinics. Nothing dated after 2020 shows health posts entering their own data, and no exchange between eCHIS and DHIS2 is established.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Over 70 facilities had an EMR by December 2024, 10 paperless; national pilot due 2026. Tier, denominator and use across most hospitals not established.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health presents electronic medical records as a milestone of its own digital health work in December 2024. The memorandum signed with the United States on 23 December 2025 schedules a national multi-tenant EMR: piloting in 2026, 48 facilities by 2027 and 438 by 2030. The United States plans to support its customisation and roll-out, Ethiopia paying the licensing, cloud and hardware costs the United States does not.

Tiers in use. EMR systems are implemented in health centres and hospitals, as the Central Ethiopia Regional Health Bureau states. In June 2023 the Bahmni EMR was in use at one general hospital and three health centres in Addis Ababa.

Where data is digitised. Providers at those four facilities entered consultations, prescriptions and laboratory orders in the EMR during care in June 2023, and the record was used across the departments of the one facility. The Ministry of Health states that 10 of the facilities with an EMR operate entirely paperless in December 2024.

Last twelve months. On 23 December 2025 Ethiopia and the United States signed the memorandum, with activities from 1 April 2026 to 31 December 2030.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Ministry of Health states that electronic medical records were introduced in over 70 health facilities in the three years to December 2024. The memorandum of 23 December 2025 gives a baseline of 0% of hospitals and health centres implementing the full national EMR.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the ART EMR, the PMTCT EMR and the HIV case-based surveillance system (CBSS). Single-function: the National Laboratory Information System, the laboratory database ETTORS, the logistics system Dagu, the pharmaceutical transaction service eAPTS, the DHIS2 routine surveillance system, the DHIS2 tracker for all-cause mortality surveillance, the Electronic Public Health Emergency Management system (ePHEM), Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources (EIOS) and the Excel-based claim management for Community-Based Health Insurance. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) and the paper Community Health Information System (CHIS). Enabling: HealthNet and telemedicine services.

Not held. The tier of the 70-plus facilities and the total they are drawn from are not held, and the 0% counts the full national system only. Neither most referral and district hospitals nor primary clinics across any district are shown using an EMR; the named primary sites are three health centres in Addis Ababa.
