The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Digital transformation strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Ethiopia's Council of Ministers approved Digital Ethiopia 2030, running to 2030, in November 2025, but no source found says whether its text is public.

Governance and finance. The National Digital Transformation Council, chaired by Deputy Prime Minister Temesgen Tiruneh, met on 30 July 2026 and was said to provide strategic leadership for the Digital Ethiopia Strategy. Public ICT and digital allocations rose from about USD 250 million in 2020 to USD 500 million in 2024, which Paradigm Initiative attributes to the predecessor, Digital Ethiopia 2025.

Standing. The Council of Ministers approved Digital Ethiopia 2030 on 29 November 2025 and the Prime Minister launched it on 20 December 2025, as the successor to Digital Ethiopia 2025. On 9 September 2026 the Minister of Innovation and Technology reaffirmed the Ministry's work towards the objectives of the strategy.

Text. The Ethiopian News Agency describes Digital Ethiopia 2030 as a comprehensive national blueprint building on the Homegrown Economic Reform Agenda.

Period. Digital Ethiopia 2025 covered 2020 to 2025, and the Prime Minister said on 20 December 2025 that it had been completed. The Ministry of Innovation and Technology describes Digital Ethiopia 2030 as a five-year journey to 2030.

Delivery plan. CIO Africa, on 27 January 2026, lists the targets for 2030: 100 percent 5G coverage in 100 cities, 128 million mobile subscribers, a Fayda digital ID for every adult and digital payments up sevenfold.

Reporting. On 30 July 2026 the Council reviewed progress on the Digital Ethiopia Strategy, Fayda and the 5 Million Coders initiative.

Last twelve months. Digital Ethiopia 2030 was launched on 20 December 2025, succeeding Digital Ethiopia 2025.

Data localisation policies

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Ethiopia's communications authority has required telecom data to stay in the country since 2021; no order under the rule is known.

Rule in force. Ethiopia's rule covers personal data and banking data. Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024 entered into force on 24 July 2024. It requires data controllers and processors to store locally collected personal data on servers in Ethiopia. In October 2025 Birr Metrics reported that it lets data leave the country only under strict conditions. A law firm says National Bank of Ethiopia Directive No. SBB/94/2025 requires all banks to store and process customer and core banking data in Ethiopia. It covers foreign banks' subsidiaries and branches, and backups too.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The Ethiopian Communications Authority's consumer rights directive of August 2021 requires telecom providers to process consumers' personal data only in Ethiopia. The authority issued it for the operators it licenses. It also oversees compliance with the data protection proclamation. The National Bank issued its directive for the banks it licenses, and must approve in writing any transfer of core banking data abroad. No order, penalty or exemption under any of these rules has been found.

Law in force. Ethiopia's cybercrime law is Computer Crime Proclamation No. 958/2016, which the Federal High Court applied in a conviction in November 2024. A 2024 law journal article treats it as the current cybercrime law and describes a 2019 draft proclamation as a further reform. The Federal Negarit Gazette carries Critical Infrastructure Cyber Security Protection Proclamation No. 1426/2026, signed on 21 July 2026. Its article 28 brings it into force one year after publication, in July 2027. INSA's Director General said it sets 18 obligations for owners of 12 critical sectors.

Use of the law. In November 2024 the Federal High Court found Meskerem Abera guilty on two counts under Proclamation No. 958/2016. The case concerned two YouTube programmes of Ethio Neqat, which she founded, and her lawyer told BBC Amharic that sentencing was set for 11 November 2024. Birr Metrics reported in December 2025 that Yohanis Daniel faced an added charge under the computer-crime law. He had recorded and live-streamed an August 2024 flight incident on TikTok.

Law in force. Ethiopia's Digital Identification Proclamation, no. 1284/2023, was signed by the President on 18 April 2023 and came into force on its publication in the Federal Negarit Gazette. It is a proclamation of the federal parliament, written for the digital identity and for nothing else, and it is the law the identity system runs under.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The proclamation sets up a digital identification system that collects demographic and biometric data in a central database. The system it describes is digital from the start: a person is enrolled with biometric data and identified from the central database. The proclamation's second article sets out the terms the rest of the text relies on. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.
