The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Legislation covering digital id

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A 2020 ordinance, ratified by law, sets up a biometric population register and unique national number; no source found reports rules made since 2023.

Law in force. Chad's Ordinance 002/PR/2020 of 14 February 2020 on civil status, ratified by Law 006/PR/2020 of 29 May 2020, sets up the integrated population and secure titles system (SIGPTS), run by the national agency for secure titles, ANATS. The system issues a unique national identification number (NNI) through the national biometric population register.

Digital identity covered and rules made. Under article 8 of the ordinance the register holds the biographic and biometric data and the NNI of everyone on the territory, and article 2 obliges every resident or visitor to register. Article 10 makes the NNI unique, unintelligible and non-sequential. Because the register covers everyone on the territory, the number reaches citizens, foreign residents and visitors alike. These are the 2020 provisions as the UNHCR describes them in an April 2026 study of refugee inclusion in the system.

Rule in force. Chad has a rule in force on sending personal data out of the country. The law firm Cabinet Thomas Dingamgoto wrote on 6 July 2026 that articles 29 to 32 of Loi n°007/PR/2015 require a legal basis and adequate safeguards for sending personal data out of Chad. Its analysis adds that the sender must either notify or obtain prior authorisation from ANSICE, the national agency named in the law. The rule is therefore a condition on each transfer, set in the data protection law itself, and it applies to organisations sending data from Chad to a head office or other recipient abroad.
