Cross-border data transfers

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Since July 2020 the law bars sending personal data abroad without adequate protection or consent, but no approved country list or guidance is known.

Rule in force. South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) prohibits transfers of personal information outside the country unless the recipient is under adequate legal protection, the data subject consents, or a contract or the data subject's interest requires the transfer. The Act took effect in July 2020, with a grace period to 30 June 2021, so the rule has applied in full since mid-2021. It is a single statutory rule covering every responsible party that sends personal information out of South Africa, and the conditions for a lawful transfer are set out in the Act itself.

Means of complying published and rule applied. An article published on 17 January 2024 states that POPIA does not name the countries with adequate protection and creates no mechanism for the regulator to recognise such countries. On that account the party sending the data has to judge adequacy for itself, case by case, since the Act supplies no approved list to rely on.
