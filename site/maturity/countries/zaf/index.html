Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national system is used at facility, district, provincial and national level (July 2026); no figure shows how many primary clinics enter their own reports.

Governance, planning, finance. Under the District Health Management Information System Policy, the Director-General has overall ownership of the District Health Information System and the Department of Health owns all its data, in the National Department of Health's bid terms of 24 July 2026. The department invites the bids itself, under National Treasury prescripts and priced in rand, for three years of hosting, maintenance, support and enhancement.

Tiers in use. The department described the system in July 2026 as used at facility, district, province and national level, reporting by clinic, community health centre and hospital.

Where data is digitised. Nine provincial instances capture the routine data and the national instance is filled by synchronisation, on the department's account of July 2026. The same bid plans training for 920 data capturers at primary health care clinics and 400 at district hospitals over three years.

Last twelve months. On 24 July 2026 the department issued bid NDOH 09/2025-2026 for a provider to host, maintain, support and enhance the system for three years, covering nine provincial DHIS2 instances, the national data warehouse and training of about 6,000 officials.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Thabethe and Mathews state that all public health facilities in Eastern Cape province used DHIS2, with capture by staff in the facility, over April 2017 to March 2020.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: Tier.net and the Malaria Information System. Single-function: ART Quarterly Reporting, the National Institute for Communicable Diseases system, the Integrated School Health Programme instance, the Patient Experience of Care instance, the laboratory information system, the Stock Visibility System, the COVID-19 Tracing Database and the proposed Live Healthcare Console. Enabling: Turn.io.

Not held. No national share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the one figure covers a single province. No health information plan dated within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Hospital systems run in five provinces and 308 Western Cape primary facilities use electronic records (August 2025); there is no national count.

Governance, planning, finance. The Minister of Health told Parliament on 1 August 2025 that the National Department of Health is developing a single common Electronic Medical Record for primary health care facilities. The department's bid document of March 2026 says it developed the EMR itself and that its Digital Health Unit directs implementation.

Tiers in use. The same reply counts Clinicom in 53 Western Cape hospitals, the eHealth system in 42 KwaZulu-Natal public hospitals and HMS2 in 16 Free State hospitals. It states that Mpumalanga, North West, Limpopo and Northern Cape are not implementing such systems at hospitals.

Where data is digitised. The department describes its EMR, in March 2026, as capturing clinical information at the point of care, verified against the patient's identifier in the Health Patient Registration System.

Last twelve months. In March 2026 the department invited bids to scale up its EMR to all fixed primary health care facilities in eight provinces within 18 months. The Free State Department of Health won the Public Sector Digital Innovator Award for deploying its primary health care EMR, SAnews reported in May 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Minister's reply states that the National EMR beta ran in 36 primary health care facilities between 22 October 2024 and 13 February 2025 and that Western Cape's Primary Health Care Information System is implemented in 308. It counts 3,333 primary health care facilities reporting to the 2024/25 Ideal Clinic Assessment.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: Tier.net and the Malaria Information System. Single-function: ART Quarterly Reporting, the National Institute for Communicable Diseases system, the Integrated School Health Programme instance, the Patient Experience of Care instance, the laboratory information system, the Stock Visibility System, the COVID-19 Tracing Database and the proposed Live Healthcare Console. Enabling: Turn.io.

Not held. No count of primary facilities using the national EMR in 2026 is held, and no total of hospitals against which the provincial counts can be set. Nothing held shows a record retrieved at another facility, and no funding source is named.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools record attendance and marks digitally and a national register holds each pupil, in 2025; the share of primary schools doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Department of Basic Education's 2025/26 Annual Performance Plan says SA-SAMS, the school administration system, is developed and maintained by the Department. The plan commits the Department to modernise it into a web-enabled platform with the National Education Collaboration Trust and lists that modernisation among unfunded mandates.

Levels in use. The Minister of Basic Education's written reply says LURITS, the Department's learner register, holds data for each learner and tracked learners in Grades 1 to 11 across its 2020 to 2025 datasets. The plan's aim is that all public schools use SA-SAMS, with no level named.

How the record is kept. A study published in November 2024 found eight rural schools in iLembe district using SA-SAMS to manage attendance and track performance, with admin clerks entering the data at the school. The 2025/26 plan says schools are to upload General Education Certificate scores onto SA-SAMS, and term 4 schedules are extracted from it.

Last twelve months. The Minister's reply gives learner totals from LURITS for 2020 to 2025, the 2025 dataset preliminary.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The Department says about 99.9% of functional ordinary schools of all levels (24,850 schools in 2024) had their databases uploaded to LURITS and the provincial data warehouses as of July 2024, which is a share of schools whose database was uploaded. The plan measures the percentage of public schools reporting through SA-SAMS and says the Western Cape does not collect source data through it.

Noted, not assessed. South Africa calls its school census an EMIS: the Department's EMIS surveys are returned by each institution under its national EMIS number, by the 2011 coding standard. Single-function: Gauteng Province's Online Admission System, and the Education Facilities Management System, the Continuous Teacher Professional Development system and the payroll system PERSAL. Enabling: the GIGA Monitoring Dashboard App, which maps schools and their connectivity.

Not held. No share or count of primary schools entering attendance or results during the year is held; the one share held counts schools of all levels whose database was uploaded. Nothing dated in the last twelve months is held, and no budget line for the system.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered online at 242 equipped health facilities, as of March 2026; the share of local offices registering digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Department of Home Affairs itself rolls out the online birth registration system to health facilities, on its 2024/25 annual report. Its amended annual performance plan for 2025/26 targets nine health facilities and allocates R21 million to the rollout.

Events registered digitally. The department reports that its online birth registration system was available at 242 health facilities by the end of 2024/25. Its Annual Performance Plan 2026/27 schedules the digitalising of death registration for 2028/29.

How the record is made. The National Department of Health states for 2024/25 that births are registered and certificates issued at the health facility, by Home Affairs officials posted there, through an online system. Home Affairs stated in September 2025 that births are captured onto the National Population Register at local offices and health facilities from the paper Notice of Birth form.

Last twelve months. The department reported progress on online birth registration in the fourth quarter of 2025/26. The Portfolio Committee on Home Affairs reports that the 2025/26 target of automated birth registration at 47 health facilities was missed and the rollout deferred to 2026/27.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Annual Performance Plan 2026/27 counts 242 health facilities equipped with online birth registration systems, beside 325 front offices and 220 mobile offices. The National Department of Health counts 299 health facilities able to register births at the end of 2024/25, against 1,445 public health facilities with maternity wards.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation of civic records. Certificate: self-service kiosks and planned certificate applications at bank branches. Outreach: the mobile units' outreach programme and the planned 2028/29 campaign to document every undocumented citizen. Identity: the National Population Register, the planned Intelligent Population Register, the Smart ID card, the green ID book, live capture, e-HomeAffairs, the Automated Biometric Identification System, the Online Verification Service, the planned MyMzansi digital ID, smart ID services at bank branches, the My Home Affairs Online branch locator, the Branch Appointment Booking System, mobile offices, the draft real-time identity verification services, the proposed integrated national identification system, SASSA biometric enrolment, Secure Citizen and Smile ID. Exchange: the planned data exchange between government departments.

Not held. No share of local offices registering births digitally is held: the counts are of health facilities equipped, and how the front offices make the record is not described. No digital registration of deaths is held, and no figure for the last year's progress.
