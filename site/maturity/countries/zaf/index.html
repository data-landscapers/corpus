Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Africa has a final draft digital masterplan dated 2021, not public, with its 2020-2025 period ended; its adoption is not known.

Governance and finance. The final draft of the ICT and Digital Economy Masterplan for South Africa is dated 22 February 2021 and credits Genesis Analytics and Knowledge Executive as authors.

Standing. Economist Impact says South Africa has published numerous draft versions of the masterplan. The department's 2023/24 plan, approved 31 March 2023, lists a Digital Economy Framework and Strategy still to go to Cabinet.

Text. The Economist Impact profile says in a footnote that the plan is not publicly available. The text held is the final draft of February 2021.

Period. Economist Impact titles the masterplan 2020-2025.

Delivery plan. The final draft has a Priority Action Plan in section 6, with an appendix of actions, some with named executors and time limits of three or five years.

Reporting. The Portfolio Committee's report on the Department of Communications and Digital Technologies for 2024/25 states that implementation of the Digital Economy Masterplan was not achieved against the department's target for the year.

Last twelve months. The latest row held on the masterplan is the Portfolio Committee's report for 2024/25, dated 2025.

Noted, not assessed. FSCA digital transformation strategy; government's digital transformation roadmap; South Africa's Roadmap for the Digital Transformation of Government; Government-wide digital ID ecosystem; National e-Government Strategy and Roadmap; digital government strategy; National Development Plan 2030; CIPC Vision 2030 and Beyond (V30B) Strategy.

Not held. No row says the masterplan was approved or adopted, and Economist Impact says the final version is not public. Its 2020-2025 period has ended, and no governance facts are held.

Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Africa gazetted its National Policy on Data and Cloud in May 2024; no government cloud or list of providers is named.

Policy adopted. The Department of Communications and Digital Technologies published the National Policy on Data and Cloud as Notice 2533 of 2024 in Government Gazette 50741 on 31 May 2024. It covers national and provincial government and organs of state. It was published under section 3(1) of the Electronic Communications Act, 2005, and the department listed the drafting of a Data and Cloud Strategy among its work in October 2024.

Named hosting and moves. The policy says government data shall be stored in unified, cloud-enabled data centres in designated locations, names the State Information Technology Agency as the authority to source data infrastructure and cloud services for government, and makes cloud the first option for new ICT procurement. A law firm's reading of June 2024 is that it requires government data-centre infrastructure to be in the country and still needs implementing with the agency and departments.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 has been in force since 2021 and is applied; the planned amendment's text is not known.

Authority. The Information Regulator was established on 1 December 2016 to monitor and enforce the Act. Its annual report for the year to 31 March 2025 describes an independent body under section 39 of the Act.

Standing. The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 was signed into law in 2013 and has been in force since 1 July 2021, after a year's grace period, with fines of up to R10 million.

Regulations. Regulations on the processing of health information, made under section 112(2)(c) of the Act, were published in Government Gazette 54268 on 6 March 2026 and commenced on publication. An enforcement notice of 21 February 2024 cites earlier regulations under section 112(2), which prescribe the form for direct-marketing consent.

Application. The Regulator reports that in 2024/25 it received 1,355 complaints under the Act and resolved 745 of them. Its first fine, in July 2023, was R5 million on the Department of Justice and Constitutional Development, and it issued an enforcement notice to WhatsApp LLC on 10 September 2024.

Last twelve months. On 20 May 2026 the Regulator published an enforcement notice against Central Johannesburg TVET College, and in August 2026 it issued one to the South African Bureau of Standards over a 2024 ransomware attack. A commissioner said on 18 August 2026 that work is under way with the justice department to amend the Act to cover artificial intelligence and privacy by design.

Noted, not assessed. The Consumer Protection Act opt-out registry and the Consumer Protection Act pre-emptive block register.

Not held. No bill or draft of the amendment to the Act. The dates of the fines on the Electoral Commission and Lancet Laboratories are not given.

Law in force. South Africa's Identification Act, 1997 is in force: the Minister of Home Affairs made the fourteenth amendment of the regulations under it on 23 June 2025, in operation from 1 July 2025. Its replacement, the National Identification and Registration Bill, is due in Parliament only in 2027 or 2028, a September 2026 report says.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The 1997 Act is the law in force, and no source held says it provides for a digital identity. In May 2026 Home Affairs gazetted draft amended regulations under the Act for comment until 6 June 2026, which would regulate access to identity information. They were drafts at the end of September 2026. The Act of 1997 remains the law the population register and the identity document are kept under.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Africa's 1998 payment system act is in force and has no licence for non-banks; a bill to change that was promised in September 2026.

Law or rules in force. South Africa's National Payment System Act of 1998 regulates the payment system and gives the Reserve Bank responsibility for clearing and settlement; it is twenty-eight years old and still the law. The Reserve Bank governor said on 1 September 2026 that a National Payment System Bill, moving regulation from the kind of entity to the kind of activity, would soon go out for public comment.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. South Africa has no dedicated mobile money licence for companies that are not banks, an MTN executive said in September 2026, so fintech companies must work through a licensed bank to move money. The Reserve Bank is preparing an equivalent through an authorisation framework and the bill.

Noted, not assessed. PayInc.

Not held. No source held says the law or rules in force license payment providers or e-money issuers that are not banks, or names one licensed under them.

Governance, planning, finance. Mangaung Metropolitan Municipality told the National Council of Provinces in November 2025 that its network upgrade is its own project, run under a council-approved ICT governance framework, and that it has adopted a three-year ICT strategic plan naming broadband as an outcome. It said that it faces limited resources and an inadequate budget for ICT.

What the office does over it. The State Information Technology Agency said in July 2026 that municipalities across the Eastern Cape are operational on its Provincial Disaster Management Application System. The Road Traffic Infringement Agency names the integration of municipalities with the national traffic information system among the upgrades behind the AARTO rollout. Mangaung has moved its email to a cloud platform, migrating 900 user accounts beside 600 already there.

How the office is connected. By November 2025 Mangaung had appointed a service provider to upgrade its network infrastructure across its seven towns, with the first phase under way, and was still appointing an internet service provider for last-mile connectivity.

Last twelve months. The Eastern Cape disaster system went live on 21 November 2025, with training for provincial and municipal users. AARTO entered its second phase in July 2026, expanding to 62 municipalities.

Local governments connected. One metropolitan municipality, Mangaung, reports an upgrade of its own network. Interviews with nine managers of rural local municipalities in Limpopo, published in 2025, found that only communication had been digitally transformed, through social media and Microsoft Teams.

Governance, planning, finance. National Treasury's budget circular of 7 December 2023 has each municipality reconcile its valuation roll to its billing system and send the reconciliation to the Treasury every quarter, within 10 working days of the quarter's end.

What is recorded. Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Case dockets were captured digitally at 998 of 1,157 police stations in May 2021, 86.3% of all police stations; the rural share is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The South African Police Service runs the case docket system as its own. Its Strategic Plan 2025 to 2030, tabled on 4 April 2025, lists the enhancement of case docket management, with full functionality in 2027/28. The 2025/26 plan funds its information systems from Programme 1 of Vote 28.

Records made digitally. When a complaint is taken at a station and the elements of a crime exist, a case docket is opened and captured on what the police call the Crime Administration System and the Investigate Case Docket Management System, by the Acting Minister's reply to a question of 31 December 2025.

How the record is made. The docket is captured at the station's Community Service Centre, signed off on the system by the Shift Commander and verified on it at station, provincial and national level, with a physical docket kept beside it. In May 2021 the docket system depended on the station's network link.

Last twelve months. On 11 April 2025 the Police Minister announced a partnership with the private sector to digitise case dockets and upgrade five police stations.

Stations doing the digital entry. The police told Parliament's police committee on 4 May 2021 that the docket system was in 998 police stations with 159 still to be covered, 86.3% of all police stations. The 159 registered cases on the old system, which fed the same database. In 2023/24, 789 of 1,160 stations sent complainants text messages about a docket through the system.
