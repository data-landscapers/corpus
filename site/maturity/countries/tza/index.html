Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics report on paper, and council offices enter the figures into the national reporting system (2024).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Monitoring and Evaluation Unit coordinated the HMIS data drawn from DHIS2 for 2024, with the University of Dar es Salaam and HISP Tanzania giving technical support in maintaining the platform. The Health Sector Strategic Plan V, for July 2021 to June 2026, carried strategic directions for strengthening the HMIS. Informants interviewed in April and May 2022 said most money for the HMIS was pooled from development partners in the Basket Fund and managed by government.

Tiers in use. The ministry's guidelines of May 2019 describe MTUHA, its name for the HMIS, as collecting routine data from dispensaries, health centres and council hospitals through the councils, while national, zonal, specialised and private hospitals use different platforms.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's methodology for its 2024 statistics states that each facility aggregates its registers and tally sheets monthly and the figures are entered into DHIS2 at council level, for 11,805 public and private health facilities.

Last twelve months. The University of Dar es Salaam's DHIS2 Lab reported on 1 October 2025 that a workshop led by the Ministry of Health Zanzibar validated data flows from programme and point-of-service systems into ZHMIS, Zanzibar's HMIS, and moved the integration towards the live environment.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The guidelines of May 2019 state that aggregated data is entered into DHIS in some health facilities, and at council level where facilities use paper.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: biometric fingerprint identification at HIV care and treatment sites and the HIV and tuberculosis and leprosy programme systems. Single-function: eLMIS, eIDSR, HRHIS3, MPDSR, M-Mama, the electronic prescription system under development, the vaccine management digital systems and the Laboratory Management Information System. Community: Afya-Tek and the Unified Community System (UCS). Enabling: the telemedicine and diagnostics programme of Tanzania Data Lab with UNICEF and PATH and TeleHealth.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held. No financing for the HMIS dated within the last three years is held, and no plan period after June 2026.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A government system runs in 23.25% of all health facilities (2023 survey); records are not shown to be shared between facilities.

Governance, planning, finance. The mid-term review of the Health Sector Strategic Plan V, published in March 2025, states that the Ministry of Health developed and launched Afya eHMS for all regional hospitals and plans to phase out the vendor-dependent Medipro and E-Medical used in zonal and specialised hospitals. It records that the Digital Health Strategy 2019-2024 had run out and its 2025-2030 successor was still being developed.

Tiers in use. The Citizen reported on 1 September 2026 that electronic medical record coverage had reached 100 percent of national, specialised, zonal and regional referral hospitals, and that adoption had expanded across district hospitals, health centres and dispensaries. The mid-term review of March 2025 states that all 28 regional hospitals use Afya eHMS.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's guidelines of May 2019 state that most confidential health data is held in health facilities in paper-based registers.

Last twelve months. The Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Health said on 1 October 2025 that the country has an integrated electronic medical records system linking a patient's information from community level through health centres and district hospitals to national level.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The mid-term review, citing the SARA survey of 2023, gives 23.25% of all health facilities using GOT-HoMIS, the government's facility system, against 44.9% of health centres and 10.63% of dispensaries. In March 2025 it states that more than 3,000 primary health care facilities use it as their electronic medical record system.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: biometric fingerprint identification at HIV care and treatment sites and the HIV and tuberculosis and leprosy programme systems. Single-function: eLMIS, eIDSR, HRHIS3, MPDSR, M-Mama, the electronic prescription system under development, the vaccine management digital systems and the Laboratory Management Information System. Community: Afya-Tek and the Unified Community System (UCS). Enabling: the telemedicine and diagnostics programme of Tanzania Data Lab with UNICEF and PATH and TeleHealth.

Not held. Nothing held establishes where the patient record is digitised or whether it can be retrieved at another facility. No financing is stated, no dated event falls in the last twelve months, and no community system is shown exchanging with the EMR.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Heads and teachers in one council were trained on a school record system in January 2026; whether any primary school enters attendance is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Prime Minister's Office (TAMISEMI) directed that the School Information System come into official use when schools opened on 13 January 2026, Handeni Town Council's education officer says. The education ministry's Permanent Secretary announced a planned student-tracking system as the ministry's own in November 2025.

Levels in use. Handeni Town Council trained the heads of its primary and secondary schools and one teacher from each school on the system on 9 January 2026.

How the record is kept. A primary head teacher trained in Handeni says a pupil marked absent three times in a row triggers a message to the parent, and the head of Mshikamano Primary School says the school kept its data on paper before the system. An August 2025 investigation in Mbeya District Council describes digital systems designed to track attendance and examination results, which teachers are expected to use daily.

Last twelve months. The Handeni training of 9 January 2026 came ahead of the official start of use directed for 13 January. In November 2025 the government was preparing a system to follow each learner from early childhood to higher education.

Primary schools doing the digital input. In Handeni every head and one teacher per school was trained on 9 January 2026. In Mbeya District Council in August 2025, one school in every five surveyed had a single teacher with a basic understanding of the systems, none formally trained, the Rural Youth Collective reports.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the yearly school count, which Tanzania calls its EMIS, collected as the Annual School Census, held in BEMIS and coordinated through ESMIS. Single-function: the examinations council's planned electronic assessment for learning system, the Online Teacher Application System, the Schools Accreditation System and the School Quality Assurance System. Learning: the Tanzania Institute of Education's Open Public School platform and its teachers' professional development learning system.

Not held. Nothing shows a primary school entering attendance or results after the start directed for 13 January 2026, and no count of schools trained or using the system is held. No plan or source of finance is held.
