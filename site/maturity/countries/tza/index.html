Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tanzania's Digital Economy Strategic Framework 2024-2034 was launched in 2024 and is public; its cost and any report on its targets are not known.

Governance and finance. The government, through the Tanzania Business Council, created a Digital Transformation Committee of public and private members to drive the Digital Economy Strategic Framework 2024-2034.

Standing. The President, Dr Samia Suluhu Hassan, launched the framework on 29 July 2024, and the ministry's catalogue page gives the same date.

Text. The Ministry publishes the framework on ictc.go.tz, with a statement from the President.

Period. It runs for ten years, 2024 to 2034, and a ministerial statement of 13 May 2026 cites it as in force.

Delivery plan. The Citizen lists its dated targets: ICT at 3% of GDP, 80% broadband and internet penetration and 90% digital literacy, all by 2029.

Reporting. The Ministry presented 758 towers built by December 2025 as supporting the strategy.

Last twelve months. At the 9th Tanzania Annual ICT Conference on 19 January 2026, Minister Angellah Kairuki said delivery must meet the framework's targets on time.

Noted, not assessed. Digital4Tanzania (D4T) Twinning project / e-Governance Support Programme; Tanzania Development Vision 2050 (Dira 2050); National Five-Year Development Plan 2021/22-2025/26; Tanzania e-Government Strategy 2022; e-Government Strategy 2013; e-Government Authority Strategic Plan 2021/2022 - 2025/2026; e-Government Capability Maturity Framework; e-Government Technology Roadmap.

Not held. No finance line tied to the framework is held, and no published report against its targets. Its plan states dated targets without a cost.

Standing. The National ICT Policy 2016 was signed by the Minister for Works, Transport and Communication in May 2016 as the Government's revision of the 2003 policy. Reporting. The June 2023 successor text says the ministry will design a monitoring and evaluation system and evaluate after five years.

Last twelve months. In August 2026 The Citizen reported the ICT Commission's director general describing the 2016 policy as the one that followed the 2003 policy.

Noted, not assessed. Tanzania Digital Economy Strategic Framework 2024-2034; Mkakati wa taifa wa uchumi wa kidigitali wa miaka 10 (2024-2035); Digital Tanzania Project; National ICT Broadband Backbone (NICTBB).

Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tanzania approved hosting standards for public institutions in February 2026; the approved hosting environments are not named.

Policy adopted. The e-Government Authority's Data Center Standards and Guidelines for Public Institutions, version 2.0, was approved by its board chairperson on 18 February 2026. It is issued under section 49 of the e-Government Act No. 10 of 2019, which requires the authority to issue technical standards on storing and processing electronic data.

Named hosting and moves. The standard rests on section 25 of the Act, which requires a public institution to host its systems in a government-approved hosting environment. The authority says it wrote the standard to guide public institutions in deciding where to host government ICT applications, and to stop them setting up new data centres. It will also serve as the minimum requirements for judging whether an existing data centre can be an approved hosting environment.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The Bank of Tanzania's 2025 guidelines bar financial firms from hosting critical systems outside Tanzania; whether the rule has been applied is not known.

Rule in force. Tanzania's rule covers the financial sector. The Bank of Tanzania's Cloud Computing Guidelines for Financial Service Providers, dated August 2025, bar a licensed financial service provider from hosting a mission-critical system, or one whose data the Bank deems critical, in a primary data centre or cloud whose infrastructure is outside Tanzania. They are issued under the Banking and Financial Institutions Act 2006 and the National Payment Systems Act 2015.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The guidelines make the Bank of Tanzania the body that evaluates and approves providers' cloud arrangements and contracts, and say it may impose sanctions, including civil money penalties, for contravention. No approval, refusal or penalty under the guidelines is held, and no source counts the providers that have moved systems into the country.

Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act 2022, in force since 2023, was applied in a 2025 ruling; how many decisions have been made is not known.

Authority. The Act establishes the Personal Data Protection Commission as a body corporate, with a seven-member Board and a Director General. The Commission delivered a ruling on a complaint on 19 August 2025, and its Director General, Dr Emmanuel Mkilia, announced on-site inspections in Dodoma on 28 August 2026.

Standing. The Personal Data Protection Act, 2022 (Act No. 11 of 2022, Chapter 44) commenced on 1 May 2023 and applies to Mainland Tanzania and, for union matters, Zanzibar.

Regulations. The Personal Data Protection (Collection and Processing) Regulations, Government Notice No. 449C of 2023, apply alongside the Act. The Commission's ruling of 19 August 2025 applied regulation 17, on erasure, and regulation 18, on children's data in commercial advertisements.

Application. On 19 August 2025 the Commission ruled in Complaint No. PDPC/CMP/002/2025 against the operator of an Instagram business page, awarding the complainant TZS 20 million in damages, imposing a fine of TZS 5 million and ordering a newborn's photographs deleted within 14 days. Its preliminary assessments, reported on 28 August 2026, found inaccurate registration data and unlawful transfers of data abroad.

Last twelve months. On 8 January 2026 the Minister of Communication and Information Technology gave unregistered institutions a final three months to register and told the Commission to prepare nationwide compliance audits. On 11 February the Commission announced inspections from 9 April, on 12 June it gave institutions with incomplete registration seven days to finish, and on-site inspections began on 31 August.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No published count of the Commission's decisions for a stated year is held. The report of the August 2025 ruling does not name the respondent.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law has been in force in Tanzania since 2015; no case under it is known.

Law in force. The Cybercrimes Act (Cap. 443, Revised Edition 2023, originally Act No. 14 of 2015) states that it commenced on 1 September 2015 under GN No. 528 of 2015. It applies to Mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar, and it criminalises illegal access, interception, data interference, fraud and forgery. The consolidated text is the Revised Edition 2023 held by the Office of the Attorney General, and the Ministry of Information and Communication Technology cites the Act in 2026.

Use of the law. The ICT Minister said on 19 August 2026 that section 29 of the Cybercrimes Act provides a fine of not less than 100m/- or three times the damage, imprisonment of at least five years, or both, for damaging critical ICT infrastructure. The Ministry of Information and Communication Technology owns the Act in that report. The minister's statement concerns owners and operators of critical ICT infrastructure, for whom the Ministry has set up a registration system.

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The 2019 e-government act has a section on data sharing and exchange; whether it requires public institutions to share is not known.

Law or decree in force. Tanzania's e-Government Act, No. 10 of 2019, started on 19 September 2019 by Government Notice 964 of that year, and applies to all public institutions. Part VIII of the Act deals with the management of electronic government data, and its section 48 is headed Data sharing and exchange. The Act defines interoperability as systems exchanging data.

Sharing required and the law applied. The Act provides for data sharing and exchange between public institutions in its section 48. What that section obliges an institution to do is not recorded: nothing held says public institutions must share data, connect to a government exchange platform, or stop asking people for what another institution already holds. No regulation or order on data sharing made under the Act since 2023 is recorded, and no institution is named as exchanging data because the Act requires it.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

AI legislation / regulations: AI law or binding rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Regulations of January 2025 prohibit fabricated AI-generated content online; there is no law on AI itself.

Binding rule on AI in force. Tanzania has a binding rule on one use of AI. Government Notice 57 of 28 January 2025, made under the Electronic and Postal Communications Act, amends the Online Content Regulations of 2020 to add unethical, fabricated and AI-generated video, audio and still pictures to the content prohibited online. It obliges internet service providers and the owners of social media platforms to filter and remove such content.

General AI law and the law applied. The rule covers online content and not AI across sectors. A university centre wrote in September 2026 that Tanzania lacks a dedicated AI law and that the laws used for AI are mostly data protection laws. No bill or draft of a law on AI itself is named, and no order or penalty under the 2025 amendment is on record.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

By February 2023, 169 of 184 councils had started using a centrally hosted billing system; the share recording today is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The local government ministry, PMO-RALG, manages TAUSI, the revenue system the councils use, the Controller and Auditor General recorded for 2024/25, and the 2026/27 budget speech assigns its development to that ministry. In his report on 2023/24 the auditor recommended that the ministry secure funding for TAUSI's full rollout in all 184 local government authorities.

What is recorded. The auditor reviewed the bills issued through TAUSI to 30 June 2025 and found TZS 1.22 billion issued by seven authorities unpaid; the revenue manual he cites requires a bill to update the taxpayer's record on payment. A ministry official said on 10 September 2025 that some councils issue receipts that do not come from the system.

Where the record is held. TAUSI is a centrally hosted application (audit of 2024/25). Some councils and village governments still keep forest-product revenue in books and hard-copy documents, the forestry division said in August 2026.

Last twelve months. The budget speech of 11 June 2026 commits the ministry and the revenue authority to finish linking TAUSI to the national tax system, and to add modules for buildings and for registering traders.

Governance, planning, finance. On the mainland the Registration Insolvency and Trusteeship Agency (RITA), the statutory registrar of births and deaths, introduced the Civil Registration System in 2017 and runs it. Its Strategic Plan for 2021/22 to 2025/26 names that system for all its birth and death registration. For 2018/19 RITA reported a treasury allocation of USD 510,768 against a requirement of USD 27.5 million.

Events registered digitally. RITA's platform, which it calls e-RITA, registers births, deaths, marriages, divorces and adoptions, on its 2025 presentation. The system held 18,464,255 births as of September 2024 and 977,761 deaths registered since 2017.

How the record is made. At the under-five registration centres, which are ward offices and health facilities, officers record a birth by hand in a register and then enter the same details into the online system by phone, the national audit found in October 2024.

Last twelve months. Zanzibar's registration agency reported a 2025/2026 improvement of its system, with streamlined workflows and a unified mobile app. On the mainland RITA extended its system to all districts in October 2024.
