Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

84% of all health facilities entered their own data in June 2025; it stays at stage 3 because no current plan or budget was found.

Governance, planning, finance. UNFPA Djibouti states that the Ministry of Health ensures national governance of the integrated DHIS2 and GBVIMS-Health platform, which its Health Information Directorate administers, at a workshop dated 2 February 2025. The ministry began integrating gender-based violence data into what UNFPA calls the National Health Information System (DHIS2) with UNFPA's technical and financial support. The national health development plan for 2020 to 2024 committed to implementing DHIS2 across the whole national territory.

Tiers in use. WHO's country office, on 14 April 2025, listed the review and enhancement of DHIS2 as implemented at primary health care level as an action in progress.

Where data is digitised. The World Bank states that at 30 June 2025 some health facilities use DHIS-2 directly while others track data on paper and submit it immediately.

Last twelve months. The World Bank's results table shows the share of facilities with DHIS-2 data entry at the facility level rising from 69% at 31 December 2024 to 84% at 30 June 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The World Bank records 84% of all health facilities with DHIS-2 data entry at the facility level at 30 June 2025, against an end target of 65% for May 2027. Its baseline was 35% in May 2022.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: GBVIMS-Health and the dossier patient électronique de planification familiale, as the United Nations in Djibouti names it. Single-function: mSupply and the National Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS). Community: the planned système d'information à base communautaire of the 2020 to 2024 health plan. Enabling: the Djibouti Fondations Numériques fibre connection, the digital health equipment handed over by IGAD, the Medyc online platform and the planned Starlink teleconsultation pilot.

Not held. No current plan naming the system and no domestic budget line dated within the last three years is held. The 84% is of all health facilities, with no numerator or denominator, and no dated event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2023 school heads registered pupils and sent absences through a national per-pupil record; the share of primary schools doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The education ministry developed the pupil management system it calls GOSE and, as at 28 November 2023, planned its extension. The minister's circular of 1 September 2025 makes consolidating it a priority for the 2025-2026 school year. The World Bank's September 2019 appraisal costed the data systems work at US$2.55 million, US$0.8 million from an IDA grant and US$1.75 million from a Global Partnership for Education grant.

Levels in use. As at 28 November 2023 each pupil had a unique identifier in GOSE from pre-school to senior school, the World Bank reported.

How the record is kept. At that date the school principal registered and transferred pupils in the application, and pupil and teacher absences were transmitted through it to central management. Pupils' grades and history were additions the ministry planned.

Last twelve months. In an interview of 20 November 2025 the education minister said the ministry's platforms give it reliable data to detect pupils in difficulty before they drop out, and that more than 3,000 pupils had been supported through the system.

Primary schools doing the digital input. All public schools transmitted their data through GOSE as at 28 November 2023, the World Bank reported, with the rollout in phases and refugee and private schools to follow. The project's measure of primary schools transmitting on time, with a target of 70 per cent of public and 50 per cent of private schools, was still unmet at that date.

Noted, not assessed. OPAD and ED-ASSIST, the tools behind the yearly school statistics in 2019; the quality assurance system (SAQ), in more than 60 pilot schools; the national human resources platform; classroom observation tools used by inspectors and pedagogical advisors; school location mapping on Giga Maps; Moodle for distance learning and teacher training, and 8,600 tablets distributed to schools; and, in the 2019 appraisal, One Child, One Tablet, smart classrooms, ADSL and Wimax school connections, and planned portals for staff records and teacher training resources.

Not held. No share of primary schools entering absences, nor a count with its total, and no figure for rural schools. Nothing dated after 2023 says how many schools use the system, and the 2025-2026 priority rests on a circular, not a costed plan.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nine civil-status sites in Djibouti-Ville have entered births and deaths digitally since 2011, as stated in 2024; digital entry outside the capital is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ville de Djibouti states that it has held the civil-status service since the Ministry of the Interior transferred the competence in 2008, and that its communes have exercised it since 2011 through SIGEC, as the city names its computerised civil-status system. Terms of reference of February 2024 place a diagnostic of SIGEC and a budgeted action plan under PROGOUV, a EUR 8 million governance support project for the city.

Events registered digitally. The city stated on 28 April 2024 that the registration of births, marriages, divorces and deaths in Djibouti-Ville has rested since 2011 on software for entering, transmitting and using civil-status data. The February 2024 terms of reference state that maternity antennas enter birth declarations in the system and the central centre draws up death and marriage acts.

How the record is made. By the city's account of 2024, civil-status agents have fed its database continuously since 2011 as they make new entries. The terms of reference place the data on a central server in a room of the Hôtel de Ville.

Last twelve months. The city reported on 28 April 2024 a diagnostic of SIGEC by an international expert, commissioned under PROGOUV.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The February 2024 terms of reference list nine civil-status sites, all in Djibouti-Ville: the Mairie's central centre, three commune centres and five antennas in maternities.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the electronic archiving of civil-status acts at the Direction générale de la Population et de la Famille, the numérisation et indexation des registres existants of the Ville de Djibouti in 2011, and the saisie des antériorités des actes of decree 2020-150. Identity: the Registre National des Personnes Physiques, the Numéro National d'Identification, the carte nationale d'identité biométrique, the équipes mobiles équipées de kits biométriques, Mobile ID, the système d'interopérabilité for verifying an identity card, the base de données nationale de la direction générale de la population and the e-ID unique identifier system planned under a World Bank project. Enabling: Djibouti Fondations Numériques. Exchange: the plateforme d'interopérabilité for the national register, the X-ROAD interoperability platform, the planned interoperable digital platform of the Public Administration Modernization Project and the Système National d'Information Sanitaire.

Not held. Nothing dated 2025 or 2026 says any office outside Djibouti-Ville enters digitally, and no national count of offices, no plan and no event of the last twelve months is held. A 2025 ESCWA survey, held only in summary, says local offices hold no electronic records of births or deaths, which disagrees with the city's 2024 account.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Only body cameras (2026), a gendarmerie intranet and a fingerprint file are held; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. National police and road-safety officers wear body cameras (August 2026). The National Gendarmerie's services contribute to case registration in a gender-based violence system being integrated with the health ministry's information system (February 2026). The gendarmerie's intranet, which it calls IntraGend, was set up in October 2024 for messaging and file sharing between headquarters and its distant brigades. Its criminal investigations brigade holds an automated fingerprint file set up in 2004, whose replacement was due in 2022. The post office takes orders for the criminal record online (2025).

Not held. Nothing held states whether police stations or gendarmerie brigades record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
