Data localisation policies: localisation rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Since 2020 a decree requires the national register of persons' data to be stored in Djibouti; no body is known to enforce it.

Rule in force. Decree 2020-146/PR/MI, issued after the Council of Ministers of 7 July 2020 and published on 13 July 2020, states in Article 9 that the processed data of the National Register of Natural Persons (RNPP) are stored only on national territory. The decree governs access to, exploitation of and communication of the register, and Article 9 applies to that one register. The text gives its publication date and no separate date of entry into force.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the same decree, whose title sets the terms for access to, use of and communication of the register's data, with Article 9 as its storage provision. The register is a relational database run by the Direction Generale de la Population et de la Famille (DGPF), with ANSIE responsible for its development and security. Its users are public authorities, public and private bodies under Djiboutian law, and their subcontractors.

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Djibouti enacted a data protection law in June 2025; no decision under it is recorded, and its start date is not known.

Authority. The Digital Code's data-protection regime creates the National Commission for the Protection of Personal Data (CNDP), independent and with administrative and financial autonomy. Jones Day reported at the National Assembly's vote that the competent authorities were still to be operationalised.

Standing. The National Assembly passed Djibouti's first Digital Code on 30 June 2025, with a data-protection regime embedded in it, to be promulgated by the President and published in the Journal Officiel. TechHive Advisory states that the regime was enacted in June 2025.

Regulations. The data-protection regime runs to 156 articles and sits within the Digital Code.

Application. The Code's embedded data-protection regime establishes the CNDP, as reported at the Assembly's vote on 30 June 2025.

Last twelve months. TechHive Advisory, writing on 5 November 2025, described the regime as enacted in June 2025 and the commission as created by it.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A digital code with a cybercrime book passed parliament in 2025 but is only a bill so far; no use of it is known.

Law in force. Djibouti's National Assembly passed the Digital Code on 30 June 2025, covering cybersecurity and cybercrime and punishable online offences. The legislative section was still to be promulgated by the President and published in the Journal Officiel. TechAfrica News reported on 2 July 2025 that the Code has eight books and nearly 800 articles, and that Book VI deals with Cybersecurity and Cybercrime. It is Djibouti's first Digital Code and was passed in plenary.

Use of the law. The nearest fact is in the text itself: Jones Day reports, in a note of 1 July 2025, that the Digital Code makes online offences punishable. The Assembly adopted it on 30 June 2025, and the same report says its legislative section awaited promulgation by the President, then publication in the Journal Officiel. That adoption was reported within days: TechAfrica News carried it on 2 July 2025 under a headline saying Djibouti enacts the Code.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Djibouti's 2019 identification law sets up a unique number and biometric register; the last rules known under it date from 2020.

Law in force. Djibouti's law no. 39/AN/19 on the identification of natural persons was published on 21 January 2019. It creates the national identification number and the national register of natural persons, which the population directorate runs. A decree of 13 July 2020 was made under its article 9.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law creates a unique national identification number and a central register of biographic and biometric data, the single reference for the identity card and travel documents. Decree no. 2020-150 sets the number's composition and requires administrations, hospitals, schools, utilities and traders to carry it on their documents. A second 2020 decree governs access to the register. Those rules all date from before September 2023, and none made under the law since then is on record.

Noted, not assessed. Carte nationale d'identité biométrique.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Djibouti's digital code, published in September 2025, bars sending personal data abroad without equivalent protection; whether the authority has published means of complying is unknown.

Rule in force. Djibouti's Code Numérique, Loi n° 019/AN/23/9ème L, was published in the Journal Officiel on 18 September 2025. Under article 99, a controller or processor may not transfer personal data to a third country or an international organisation unless it ensures an equivalent and sufficient level of protection. Article 102 sets exceptions: the person's consent, a contract, public interest, legal claims or vital interests, or authorisation by the Commission nationale de protection des données.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the same law: the Code Numérique of 18 September 2025 sets the conditions in articles 99 to 103 and gives the Commission nationale de protection des données the power to authorise a transfer.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.
