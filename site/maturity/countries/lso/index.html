Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

231 facilities report HIV figures into the national system (2025); clinics entering their own data is shown only in four pilot districts, up to 2017.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Health Management Information System has run on DHIS2 since February 2015, and an officer of its HMIS Division presented how health data is collected from communities and facilities at a workshop reported on 23 September 2026. In 2025 Lesotho endorsed its National Digital Health Strategy 2025-2030, WHO's country office reports.

Tiers in use. A BMJ Open study covers monthly routine reporting by 60 health centres and 6 hospitals in the four pilot districts of Berea, Butha Buthe, Leribe and Mohale's Hoek over July 2013 to June 2017.

Where data is digitised. Under the primary health care reform pilot of July 2014 to June 2017, each health facility nurse in charge was given a computer and submitted the monthly report from the facility, while the registers stayed on paper. In November 2025 the ministry's HIV programme stated that facility reports are automated from its electronic medical record into DHIS2.

Last twelve months. WHO's country office reports that during 2025 Lesotho advanced interoperability across its HMIS, HRH, LMIS and surveillance platforms. The HIV programme states that 231 facilities report on HIV indicators and that reporting rates fell from 97% in January to 95% in September 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The four pilot districts where facilities submitted monthly reports electronically held 66 facilities, 60 health centres and 6 hospitals, and about 40% of the population, out of 10 districts, to June 2017.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Ministry of Health's electronic registers, the EpiC project's DHIS2 Tracker systems, the eRegister and the electronic medical record for HIV-positive patients. Single-function: the laboratory information system, the LMIS, the HRH platform, the IDSR surveillance platforms and the laboratory results systems. Community: the Bophelo ka Mosebeletsi App. Enabling: the T-Connect Impact Account.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and how facilities without the electronic medical record enter theirs is not stated. Nothing on tiers or clinics is dated after 2017, and no financing within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic register ran in 197 public facilities in November 2025, while rural health centres recorded general care on paper (2023).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's HIV programme stated in November 2025 that the plan is for the Government of Lesotho to completely own the EMR, using domestic resources. After the US stop-work orders of 2025 the government hired 10 IT and 10 HMIS personnel to support districts. The MCC compact of 12 May 2022 funds the expansion of the e-Register, which it says is limited to HIV and tuberculosis patients, into a national electronic medical record system covering all primary health care patients.

Tiers in use. A hospital-based EMR was introduced in 2013 across 16 public hospitals in the 10 districts. The ministry's assessment of 18 October 2017 found it discontinued in several facilities and in substandard use in others, and the HMIS Strategic Plan 2018-2022 records it at six hospitals, where clinicians and pharmacists used it inadequately.

Where data is digitised. MCC wrote on 11 December 2023 that at a rural health centre a pregnant woman who tests negative for HIV or TB has her care recorded on paper, in a health booklet, while an HIV-positive patient's care is recorded in an electronic medical record.

Last twelve months. The HIV programme states that the EMRs stayed in place through the 2025 US funding cuts, and that suboptimal use of the eRegister led to under-reporting of new ART initiations to September 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The HIV programme stated in November 2025 that what it calls the OpenMRS/Bahmni-based EMR/eRegister is in 197 public facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Ministry of Health's electronic registers, the EpiC project's DHIS2 Tracker systems, the eRegister and the electronic medical record for HIV-positive patients. Single-function: the laboratory information system, the LMIS, the HRH platform, the IDSR surveillance platforms and the laboratory results systems. Community: the Bophelo ka Mosebeletsi App. Enabling: the T-Connect Impact Account.

Not held. No denominator for the 197 facilities is held, nor a split of hospitals from clinics, and nothing shows the expansion to all primary care patients delivered. The hospital EMR's standing after 2018 is not established, and no plan naming the system is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2018 pilot tracked individual pupils in one hundred schools; their level, and whether it still runs, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. In 2018 the Ministry of Education and Training was itself implementing OpenEMIS as its new education information system. UNICEF supported the rollout, and the pilot drew on an existing World Bank project, the Lesotho Basic Education Improvement Project.

Levels in use. The 2018 pilot was set in one hundred low-performance schools, according to Development Gateway's diagnostic for UNICEF Lesotho.

How the record is kept. The pilot tracked individual learners in those schools, under OpenEMIS, the platform the Ministry was adopting that year.

Last twelve months. The latest dated event is from 2018, when, with UNICEF supporting it, the rollout was beginning with the pilot.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Development Gateway reported in 2018 that one hundred schools were in the pilot, a count of pilot schools and not a share of all primary schools.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school census, which the Ministry calls its EMIS: paper annual statistical forms keyed centrally, OpenEMIS used only to capture the Annual School Census, the Education Statistical Bulletin and its DHIS2 successor, LEMIS, into which district offices are to enter school data. Also a teacher management information system being introduced, the ChildSteps app and the Teaching Service's planned e-governance platforms, and the T-Connect Impact school connection.

Not held. The level of the pilot schools and who entered the records are not stated, and nothing dated since 2018 shows whether the pilot still runs or went further. No current plan or domestic budget for a per-learner system is held.
