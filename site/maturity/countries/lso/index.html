Digital transformation strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho's digital transformation strategy to 2030 was approved in February 2025 and is published, but its action plan and results are not known.

Governance and finance. The strategy's text makes the Chief Digital Office in the ICT ministry, under the Chief Digital Officer, accountable for developing the strategy and its implementation plans and for its monitoring and evaluation, with ministries reporting progress to it.

Standing. The finance minister told Parliament on 19 February 2025 that the Digital Transformation Strategy had been approved, without naming an approving act or body.

Period. The strategy is catalogued as Lesotho's national digital transformation strategy to 2030, self-dated 2024, and the 2027/28 Budget Strategy Paper of October 2026 commits government to fast-track the 2024-2030 strategy.

Delivery plan. The text sets initiatives by priority, the highest to start within about 12 months of operationalisation and medium ones within three to five years, and states no cost. The National Digital Policy lists development of a detailed action plan for the strategy as a task for 2024-2025.

Reporting. A government news item of 29 September 2026 says Lesotho is advancing the strategy, with priorities including connectivity, digital skills, innovation and technology for public services.

Legislation covering digital id

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho's 2011 identity cards law, in force since 2013, sets up a biometric register and unique number. No rules since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Lesotho's National Identity Cards Act, 2011 was published in the Government Gazette on 21 March 2011 and commenced on 1 July 2013. It repealed the National Identity Cards Act, 2004. It is an Act of Parliament and has been the law for the identity register and the card since its commencement.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The Act establishes a National Identity Register that records fingerprints and an image for everyone aged sixteen and over. It assigns each entry a unique national identity number and provides for an identity card with a chip that holds the fingerprints. The register, the number and the chip card are all set out in the Act itself. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.
