Strategies, plans and policies

Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho's digital transformation strategy to 2030 was approved in February 2025 and is published, but its action plan and results are not known.

Governance and finance. The National Digital Policy 2024-2035 says technical assistance from the International Telecommunication Union was used to draft the National Digital Transformation Strategy: Agenda 2030.

Standing. The finance minister told Parliament on 19 February 2025 that the Digital Transformation Strategy had been approved, without naming an approving act or body.

Text. The strategy's text is published on the government's website.

Period. The strategy is catalogued as Lesotho's national digital transformation strategy to 2030, self-dated 2024.

Delivery plan. The National Digital Policy lists development of a detailed action plan for the strategy as a task for 2024-2025.

Reporting. A government news item of 29 September 2026 says Lesotho is advancing the strategy, with priorities including connectivity, digital skills, innovation and technology for public services.

Last twelve months. On 29 July 2025 the minister said the memorandum of understanding with India was signed in the wake of the strategy's launch.

Noted, not assessed. Electronic Transactions and Communications Bill, 2026; National Strategic Development Plan II; Lesotho Communications Authority Strategic Plan 2026-2029; e-Government Infrastructure Phase II; Memorandum of understanding with India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology; Lesotho E-Government Infrastructure Project; Public Sector Foundations for Service Delivery Project; Digital Transformation Strategy of the Lesotho Mounted Police Service.

Not held. No adopting act or date is named for the strategy, and no governing body, finance or results report is held for it. Its action plan was still to be developed in 2024-2025, and no later one is held.

ICT strategy: sector policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho has a communications policy adopted in 2008 with no end year; its delivery plan and reporting are not known.

Governance and finance. The Lesotho Communications Policy 2008 makes the Lesotho Communications Authority the regulator for telecommunications, broadcasting and postal services, with the Minister setting basic policy. A 2025 tender notice says the ministry will review the policy with the authority.

Standing. The government adopted the policy in 2008. Before it came the Lesotho Telecommunications Policy 1999 and the ICT Policy for Lesotho 2005. The Lesotho ICT Policy 2021 was in draft in March 2022.

Text. The Ministry of Communications, Science and Technology published the full text, which the government's website hosts.

Period. The policy states no end year or review date.

Delivery plan. The policy states no budget line for its own implementation and provides for a Universal Service Fund drawing up to two percent of operators' net operating income.

Reporting. The National Digital Policy of December 2024 says the monitoring system planned under the ICT Policy for Lesotho 2005 was never established.

Last twelve months. The ministry sought consultants in mid-2025 to review the 2008 policy and draft a Lesotho Communications Policy (2025), with bids closing on 21 July 2025.

Noted, not assessed. National Digital Policy 2024-2035; National Digital Transformation Strategy: Agenda 2030; Communications Act 2012.

Not held. No delivery plan and no report against the 2008 policy is held, and its finance is known only from the 2008 text. The ICT Policy 2021 is held only as a draft and the 2025 review only as a tender, and nothing is dated in the last twelve months.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho has a Data Protection Act of 2011 with no commission appointed; whether it has ever been applied or has regulations is not known.

Authority. The Data Protection Act has a Data Protection Commission under it. Cenfri, in an article published on 23 March 2026, says the Commission has remained unappointed.

Standing. Lesotho's general law is the Data Protection Act, which Cenfri dates to 2011. Cenfri says, in the article it published on 23 March 2026, that the Act has been law for over a decade.

Regulations. The nearest dated position is Cenfri's, published on 23 March 2026: the Data Protection Act has been law for over a decade.

Application. The nearest dated position concerns the body under the Act: Cenfri says, in its article of 23 March 2026, that the Data Protection Commission has remained unappointed.

Last twelve months. The latest dated position is Cenfri's article of 23 March 2026, which says that the Act has been law for over a decade and that its Commission has remained unappointed.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No regulation made under the Act and no decision applying it is held, nor is any event in the twelve months to 30 September 2026. The source that says the Commission is unappointed gives no date for that position beyond its own publication on 23 March 2026.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cybercrime and cybersecurity bills were still awaiting Parliament in September 2026; no law in force is known, and no use of one.

Law in force. Lesotho's nearest held fact is a pair of bills. Cabinet split the 2024 bill into a Cybercrime Bill 2026 and a Cybersecurity Bill 2026, and the Department of Information reports a second stakeholder consultation on the Cybersecurity Bill 2026 after a draft circulated in August 2026, with the bill still to proceed through the next legislative stages. The Lesotho Times reports that a 2021 version was withdrawn from Parliament and the 2024 Computer Crime and Cyber Security Bill was referred to Parliament but not enacted. In August 2026 the Director General of ICT said both bills were ready and waiting for approval by the National Assembly.

Use of the law. The nearest held fact concerns the draft text. The Computer Crime and Cyber Security Bill, 2024 would commence on gazettal and be cited as the Act once enacted. In January 2026 the Principal Secretary at the communications ministry said it was working on the Cybersecurity Bill.

Noted, not assessed. The interim Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT), the National Cyber Security Incident Response Team and the National Cybersecurity Advisory Council.

Not held. No source held says a cybercrime or cybersecurity law is in force.

Data protection authority: supervisory authority

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Lesotho's 2012 law creates a commission, unappointed in 2025; no ruling against an organisation or published figures are known.

Named in law and appointed. The Data Protection Act (Act 5 of 2012) establishes a Data Protection Commission, but the law firm Z. Mayet and Associates states that the Commission and its Commissioner had not been appointed as at 31 October 2025, so the regulator did not exist in practice. Cenfri said the same of the Commission in an article published on 23 March 2026: the Data Protection Commission under the Act has remained unappointed, with no date given for that statement.

Rulings and published figures. No ruling against an organisation and no published figures are held. The nearest held fact is the position stated by Z. Mayet and Associates in October 2025, that neither the Commission nor its Commissioner had been appointed, so the regulator did not exist in practice.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Nothing held says the Commission or its Commissioner has since been appointed, or that any ruling or figures have been published.

Implementing and acting. The law firm Z. Mayet and Associates states that the Act establishes a Data Protection Commission, but that the Commission and its Commissioner have not been appointed, in a document published on 31 October 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Data Protection Commission.

Not held. No source dated in the last three years shows an appointed authority registering organisations, taking breach reports, issuing guidance or inspecting.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Two community councils had internet installed under a pilot by 2019; whether any connection has gone beyond the pilot is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The e-Government Project that built and connected two community council buildings is under the Ministry of Communications, Science and Technology, which handed them to the Ministry of Local Government and Chieftainship Affairs on 31 July 2019. The project is financially supported by the African Development Bank.

What the office does over it. The 31 July 2019 handover report states that the two buildings have the Internet installed. In January 2018 the secretary of Tenesolo Community Council said e-government would connect the office to internet access and to a data bank in Mohale's Hoek. In September 2025 the civil registration manager for Qacha's Nek said certificate services decentralised to councils fail without transport to carry applications to and from the district office.

How the office is connected. The Minister said on 31 July 2019 that the two buildings handed over under his ministry's project have been installed with the Internet. In January 2018 the head of the national web e-portal said council connections will use the Lesotho government data network.

Last twelve months. On 17 June 2024 the communications ministry's Principal Secretary said its plan is to roll out its 74 digital services by also connecting Community Councils.

Local governments connected. Two community councils, Dinizulu in Thaba-Tseka and Kubake in Maseru district, had the Internet installed by 31 July 2019 under the e-Government Pilot Project begun in 2014. In January 2018 the Minister said the plan is to assist in connecting all the councils.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: ten computers and a server for Tenesolo Community Council and the land registration capacity upgrade for eight local councils. Applications: civil registration services decentralised to councils, Maseru City Council's Construction Permit Automation System and the Decentralizing Access to Child Development Indicators project. Public access: the ministry's "digital ambassadors", the digital studio at Makoanyane Square and the UNIPOD studio at Lerotholi Polytechnic, and free Wi-Fi for communities around the two councils. Backbone: the Vodacom data centres at Lekokoaneng and Maseru West, the government's data hosting and business continuity solutions and the e-Government Infrastructure Phase I and Phase II projects, the Lesotho E-Government Infrastructure Project and the government network. Central: the CRVS and national ID system, the government e-payments gateway and the interoperability platform, the shared database of the Independent Electoral Commission and Home Affairs, IFMIS, CBMS, HRMIS and NID, and NISSA.

Not held. Whether any community council beyond the two pilot ones is connected, and how many councils there are in all. What system council staff work in over the connection, and any plan period or financing since 2019.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

231 facilities report HIV figures into the national system (2025); clinics entering their own data is shown only in four pilot districts, up to 2017.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Health Management Information System has run on DHIS2 since February 2015, and an officer of its HMIS Division presented how health data is collected from communities and facilities at a workshop reported on 23 September 2026. In 2025 Lesotho endorsed its National Digital Health Strategy 2025-2030, WHO's country office reports.

Tiers in use. A BMJ Open study covers monthly routine reporting by 60 health centres and 6 hospitals in the four pilot districts of Berea, Butha Buthe, Leribe and Mohale's Hoek over July 2013 to June 2017.

Where data is digitised. Under the primary health care reform pilot of July 2014 to June 2017, each health facility nurse in charge was given a computer and submitted the monthly report from the facility, while the registers stayed on paper. In November 2025 the ministry's HIV programme stated that facility reports are automated from its electronic medical record into DHIS2.

Last twelve months. WHO's country office reports that during 2025 Lesotho advanced interoperability across its HMIS, HRH, LMIS and surveillance platforms. The HIV programme states that 231 facilities report on HIV indicators and that reporting rates fell from 97% in January to 95% in September 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The four pilot districts where facilities submitted monthly reports electronically held 66 facilities, 60 health centres and 6 hospitals, and about 40% of the population, out of 10 districts, to June 2017.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Ministry of Health's electronic registers, the EpiC project's DHIS2 Tracker systems, the eRegister and the electronic medical record for HIV-positive patients. Single-function: the laboratory information system, the LMIS, the HRH platform, the IDSR surveillance platforms and the laboratory results systems. Community: the Bophelo ka Mosebeletsi App. Enabling: the T-Connect Impact Account.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and how facilities without the electronic medical record enter theirs is not stated. Nothing on tiers or clinics is dated after 2017, and no financing within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic register ran in 197 public facilities in November 2025, while rural health centres recorded general care on paper (2023).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's HIV programme stated in November 2025 that the plan is for the Government of Lesotho to completely own the EMR, using domestic resources. After the US stop-work orders of 2025 the government hired 10 IT and 10 HMIS personnel to support districts. The MCC compact of 12 May 2022 funds the expansion of the e-Register, which it says is limited to HIV and tuberculosis patients, into a national electronic medical record system covering all primary health care patients.

Tiers in use. A hospital-based EMR was introduced in 2013 across 16 public hospitals in the 10 districts. The ministry's assessment of 18 October 2017 found it discontinued in several facilities and in substandard use in others, and the HMIS Strategic Plan 2018-2022 records it at six hospitals, where clinicians and pharmacists used it inadequately.

Where data is digitised. MCC wrote on 11 December 2023 that at a rural health centre a pregnant woman who tests negative for HIV or TB has her care recorded on paper, in a health booklet, while an HIV-positive patient's care is recorded in an electronic medical record.

Last twelve months. The HIV programme states that the EMRs stayed in place through the 2025 US funding cuts, and that suboptimal use of the eRegister led to under-reporting of new ART initiations to September 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The HIV programme stated in November 2025 that what it calls the OpenMRS/Bahmni-based EMR/eRegister is in 197 public facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Ministry of Health's electronic registers, the EpiC project's DHIS2 Tracker systems, the eRegister and the electronic medical record for HIV-positive patients. Single-function: the laboratory information system, the LMIS, the HRH platform, the IDSR surveillance platforms and the laboratory results systems. Community: the Bophelo ka Mosebeletsi App. Enabling: the T-Connect Impact Account.

Not held. No denominator for the 197 facilities is held, nor a split of hospitals from clinics, and nothing shows the expansion to all primary care patients delivered. The hospital EMR's standing after 2018 is not established, and no plan naming the system is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2018 pilot tracked individual pupils in one hundred schools; their level, and whether it still runs, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. In 2018 the Ministry of Education and Training was itself implementing OpenEMIS as its new education information system. UNICEF supported the rollout, and the pilot drew on an existing World Bank project, the Lesotho Basic Education Improvement Project.

Levels in use. The 2018 pilot was set in one hundred low-performance schools, according to Development Gateway's diagnostic for UNICEF Lesotho.

How the record is kept. The pilot tracked individual learners in those schools, under OpenEMIS, the platform the Ministry was adopting that year.

Last twelve months. The latest dated event is from 2018, when, with UNICEF supporting it, the rollout was beginning with the pilot.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Development Gateway reported in 2018 that one hundred schools were in the pilot, a count of pilot schools and not a share of all primary schools.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school census, which the Ministry calls its EMIS: paper annual statistical forms keyed centrally, OpenEMIS used only to capture the Annual School Census, the Education Statistical Bulletin and its DHIS2 successor, LEMIS, into which district offices are to enter school data. Also a teacher management information system being introduced, the ChildSteps app and the Teaching Service's planned e-governance platforms, and the T-Connect Impact school connection.

Not held. The level of the pilot schools and who entered the records are not stated, and nothing dated since 2018 shows whether the pilot still runs or went further. No current plan or domestic budget for a per-learner system is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births were registered digitally at 30 health-facility stations in three districts in 2026; the total number of local offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Department of National Identity and Civil Registry, in the Ministry of Home Affairs, is the lead agency for civil registration, it said in July 2026. Its presentation lists a costed CRVS Strategic Plan 2026-2030 as still to be developed during 2026. The Government's 30 birth registration stations had support from the Government of Japan and UNICEF, UNICEF Lesotho said in August 2026.

Events registered digitally. Birth registration stations in health facilities are supported by an SMS-based platform for registering births, UNICEF Lesotho said in August 2026. The Ntlafatsa Bana project's digital birth registration system registers births on tablets, UNICEF reported in June 2025.

How the record is made. UNICEF reported in June 2025 that real-time tablet-based registration, operated by village health workers and civil registration officers, has replaced manual processes. A World Bank note of May 2022 says all 15 enrolment centres that allow for birth registration are linked to a central server.

Last twelve months. UNICEF Lesotho reported on 5 August 2026 that 30 birth registration stations had been established in health facilities in Maseru, Berea and Leribe. In July 2026 the Department listed nationwide scale-up of digital birth registration as an action for 2026-2027.

Local offices doing the digital entry. UNICEF Lesotho counts 30 birth registration stations in health facilities across three districts, in August 2026. The World Bank note of May 2022 counts 15 enrolment centres, across Maseru and each of the 10 districts.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the national identity management system of the Ministry of Home Affairs, a digital service identification system in pilot, an integrated national digital ID ecosystem on MOSIP and, for refugees, the Population Registration and Identity Management Eco System. Exchange: the Department's integrated system, which issues a unique personal identification number upon birth registration, the integration of birth registration with the social assistance database, an identity verification API, civil registry integrations with banks and mobile money providers, the transfer of civil registration records to the Bureau of Statistics, a national all-cause mortality surveillance and CRVS integration system in design and a planned whole-of-government data sharing platform. Outreach: about fifteen mobile registration units and the Department's outreach at Libobeng, Qacha's Nek.

Not held. No total of local registration offices is published, so neither count is a share, and the two counts are of different systems. Where the SMS platform's record is held, and whether deaths are entered at any local office, is not known.
