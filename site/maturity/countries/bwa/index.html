Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All public health facilities reported into the national system in 2016-18; they report on paper (2025), and it is unclear where the figures are entered.

Governance, planning, finance. WHO's country office report for 2024-2025 describes DHIS2 as the Government's selected "gold standard" platform for aggregate data. The Ministry of Health and Wellness's roadmap, dated 2019, set one integrated DHIS2 as the default reporting system for the sector, under the ministry's stewardship.

Tiers in use. MEASURE Evaluation's country profile of August 2019 records a national deployment of DHIS2 aggregating health data from all facilities in the country.

Where data is digitised. WHO's report for 2024-2025 lists facilities' heavy reliance on paper-based reporting as a challenge and recommends revising the DHIS2 dashboards and trackers "to enable onsite data capturing".

Last twelve months. WHO's report for 2024-2025 has DHIS2 used to monitor HIV, immunisation and reproductive, maternal and child health across districts.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. MEASURE Evaluation gives 100% of public health facilities as reporting through DHIS2, for 2016 to 2018, a share of all public health facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 trackers and the ministry's open-source cancer registry. Single-function: VigiFlow, the Electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), the electronic Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response System (eIDSR), Botswana Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), the disease surveillance system of the ministry's 2019 roadmap, the central medical stores warehouse management system and the Laboratory Information Management System (Senaite) of one private clinic. Community: PaperEMR. Enabling: the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS), the village connectivity programme, the Gabriel telemedicine platform, the telemedicine application Kgonafalo and the Government Data Network.

Not held. The level at which facilities' paper reports are keyed is not established, and no share or count of clinics entering their own reports is held; the 100% is of facilities reporting, and carries no numerator or denominator. No plan or financing dated within the last three years is held, and no dated event in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All public hospitals kept electronic patient records in 2019-20, and a central system reached 22 facilities in one district by March 2026.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's budget speech of 5 March 2026 states that it and the Ministry of Local Government and Traditional Affairs will roll out the completed EMR to the remaining 29 districts in 2026/27. The 2026/27 development budget request allocates P62,534,000 to the ministry's headquarters, naming the Digitalization of Health Services Project. A World Bank technical note published on 30 October 2025 costs the electronic medical record at US$1.531 million, from an IBRD loan.

Tiers in use. MEASURE Evaluation's profile of August 2019 states that all hospitals are on a centralised Integrated Patient Management System, the ministry's name for it. A 2019-20 survey names 13 public hospitals using it, from referral to primary hospitals.

Where data is digitised. The National eHealth Strategy adopted in March 2020 is quoted as saying the public hospitals' electronic medical record is real time. A survey of 2019-20 found most eRecord systems not interoperable within or between facilities, and no national unique patient identifier.

Last twelve months. On 19 November 2025 the Minister of Local Government told Parliament that the Electronic Medical Records system was being piloted in two districts. The March 2026 budget speech reports 21 more facilities in Okavango due by the end of that month.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Assistant Minister of Health told Parliament on 5 March 2026 that Phase 1 of the ministry's centralised Electronic Medical Record had been implemented in 22 health facilities in Ngami.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 trackers and the ministry's open-source cancer registry. Single-function: VigiFlow, the Electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), the electronic Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response System (eIDSR), Botswana Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), the disease surveillance system of the ministry's 2019 roadmap, the central medical stores warehouse management system and the Laboratory Information Management System (Senaite) of one private clinic. Community: PaperEMR. Enabling: the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS), the village connectivity programme, the Gabriel telemedicine platform, the telemedicine application Kgonafalo and the Government Data Network.

Not held. No unique patient identifier in use, and no retrieval of a record at another facility, is established. The types of the 22 facilities and a national denominator are not given, hospital coverage after 2020 is not held, and no plan dated within the last three years is held.
