Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2022 report says finance systems are implemented in all municipalities; how their offices are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National ICT Policy and Strategy 2019-2024 set the goal that all local authorities of the federal government and the member states have internet access by its third year, with costs left to each agency. It made the ICT Department of the Ministry of Posts, Telecommunications and Technology responsible for connectivity in government.

What the office does over it. The World Bank's Digital Economy Diagnostic of 2022 says the web-based financial management systems of the federal government, Puntland and Somaliland, which it names SFMIS, PLFMIS and SLFMIS, have each been implemented in all ministries, departments and agencies, state-owned enterprises and municipalities.

How the office is connected. The 2019 policy lists local authorities among the rural public services whose connectivity is to be built in a co-ordinated way.

Last twelve months. The policy that set the target for local authorities ran from 2019 to 2024.

Local governments connected. The 2022 diagnostic says the finance systems are implemented in "all" municipalities of the federal government, Puntland and Somaliland.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the ICT equipment planned for municipal civil registrars' departments. Application: the GIS property databases of Hargeisa, Boroma, Berbera, Garowe and Gardo, and municipal land registration in Garowe and Bossaso; the automation of revenue collections in districts; the Land Management System; the planned CRVS system; birth certificates issued at Mogadishu's district offices; the civil registers and identity cards of some of the 22 municipalities; and the Banadir ID card. Web: Baidoa Municipality's Fariin Maal citizen engagement platform. Backbone: GovNet; the government backbone network of 26 locations around Mogadishu; the government network upgrade, national backbone network and government data hosting of the 2023 World Bank grant, with its feasibility study for EARDIP; the Somalia National Data Center and the National Enterprise Service Bus; and the government intranet and national fibre backbone of the 2019 policy. Upper tier: the federal FMIS and its rollout to the Federal Member States; the Bisan FMIS, the Revenue Management System and the Biometric Attendance System of Jubaland, Hirshabelle, South West and Galmudug; South West State's State Land Registry; the communications rooms in ministries across the member states; and the Puntland ID and Somaliland's voter register. Central: the Somali National ID system; the biometric voter registration system and its registration equipment; SOMCAS; the cloud-based human resources and pension systems; the High School Certificate System; gov.so email; the pre-purchased international bandwidth for 25 ministries and the Central Bank; and the Burao district birth registration pilot.

Not held. No source says how any municipal office is connected, or that municipal staff work in the finance systems at their own offices, and no count or total of municipalities is given. Nothing on record is dated within the last three years.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter paper reports into the national system (2022), and 111 primary facilities report digitally (2025); their share of all clinics is unknown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's bulletin for July to September 2023 states that the ministry decided to use DHIS2 as the exclusive national HMIS platform, run by its HMIS Section, and that a five-year costed Health Information Strategy was prepared and finalised. Under a three-year agreement signed with UNICEF in August 2025, HISP maintains the DHIS2 platform and trains ministry staff.

Tiers in use. The ministry reports 579 public health facilities reporting to DHIS2 in July to September 2023, on data sets for health-centre outpatients and hospital outpatients and inpatients.

Where data is digitised. The WHO and ministry assessment of September 2022 found that most facilities record on paper registries and send monthly summary forms to the district HMIS officer, who enters them into DHIS2. The ministry's case study of 2023 to 2025 reports that in the 111 primary facilities running RAAD, its electronic health record, routine care documentation generates the DHIS2 aggregate reports automatically.

Last twelve months. ISDP ran a three-day HMIS training of trainers in Bosaso on 3 to 5 February 2026 for 16 health workers from the health centres and primary health units it supports.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's case study of 2023 to 2025 reports that 111 public primary healthcare facilities across six states produce their DHIS2 aggregate reports digitally from the RAAD record. A commentary of August 2026 by ministry HMIS staff says DHIS2-based reporting remains uneven across facilities and administrative levels.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Electronic Immunization Register, DHIS2 Tracker, the HIV Tracker, e-IDSR and OGOW. Single-function: the Pharmaceutical Information Management System (PIMS), the Electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), the Nutrition Module, the IDSR data set on DHIS2, the Early Warning, Alert and Response Network (EWARN), HeRAMS, the logistic management information system, the laboratory data set under DHIS2, the nutrition reporting system on ONA, the TB surveillance system, the malaria surveillance system, the HIV reporting system, AFP polio surveillance and measles surveillance. Community: Nyss.

Not held. No count of all primary clinics is held to set the 111 against, and no share of facilities entering their own reports is published. The period of the five-year Health Information Strategy is not given, and the only dated event in the last twelve months is one training.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Two electronic record systems run in over 40 facilities (April 2026) and 111 primary facilities (2025); their share of all clinics is unknown.

Governance, planning, finance. By its own account the Ministry of Health developed RAAD as a national electronic health record for primary healthcare, implemented between 2023 and 2025. Its bulletin for July to September 2023 says the pilot was supported by the Government of Sweden through SPIDER. The Caafimaad+ Digitalized Data System (C+ DDS) is run by the CaafimaadPlus consortium of ten NGOs, in partnership with the Federal Ministry of Health, and funded by EU Humanitarian Aid, as the consortium stated in April 2026.

Tiers in use. The ministry's case study places RAAD in public primary healthcare facilities only, between 2023 and 2025.

Where data is digitised. RAAD captures clinical data as routine care documentation, in place of an average of 15 paper forms, on the ministry's case study of 2023 to 2025. The consortium said in April 2026 that C+ DDS replaces manual reports with real-time entry and makes a family's record available at any facility on the system.

Last twelve months. The consortium reports growth from a five-facility pilot in mid-2024 to over 40 facilities by April 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. In April 2026 the consortium reported C+ DDS operational in over 40 facilities across 9 regions and more than 25 districts of South-Central Somalia. The ministry's case study reports RAAD deployed in 111 public primary healthcare facilities across six states between 2023 and 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Electronic Immunization Register, DHIS2 Tracker, the HIV Tracker, e-IDSR and OGOW. Single-function: the Pharmaceutical Information Management System (PIMS), the Electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), the Nutrition Module, the IDSR data set on DHIS2, the Early Warning, Alert and Response Network (EWARN), HeRAMS, the logistic management information system, the laboratory data set under DHIS2, the nutrition reporting system on ONA, the TB surveillance system, the malaria surveillance system, the HIV reporting system, AFP polio surveillance and measles surveillance. Community: Nyss.

Not held. No count of all primary clinics is held to set either figure against, and the two systems' facilities are not shown to be separate. No plan naming an electronic medical record, no unique patient identifier and no tier for the C+ DDS facilities is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A daily per-pupil attendance app was on trial in 42 schools in Puntland State in May 2022; what has happened since is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. In May 2022 the Puntland education ministry proposed to expand what it calls the Digital Attendance Application and took on the collection and analysis of its attendance data, with the roll-out placed under a US$3.35 million GPE programme running May 2022 to April 2023, UNICEF the grant agent.

Levels in use. The application's 2019 pilot in Puntland was in 3 primary schools.

How the record is kept. The application collects daily student and teacher attendance on mobile devices, offline where needed, and uploads it to the server of what Puntland calls its EMIS, as described in May 2022. In 2019 the pilot schools moved from a paper attendance tracker to the application, which records each student's attendance daily with reasons for absence.

Last twelve months. In May 2022 state-wide collection of attendance data was not in place in Puntland and the state's EMIS was under development.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The application was on trial in 42 schools in Puntland State in May 2022, after the 2019 pilot in 3 primary schools.

Noted, not assessed. Somalia calls its school census an EMIS: the federal education ministry's EMIS, started in 2016, collects data from schools once a year, as described in February 2024, and carries a national examination results function for grades 8 and 12. The 2023 joint education sector review lists an Annual School Census, and Puntland's ministry publishes the Puntland Education Statistics Yearbook. Single-function systems are the Teacher Management Information System, to be expanded under a project amended in October 2025; Somaliland's Education Registration and Certification System, listed as operational in 2022; and the National Digital Trust System, under development in April 2026 for verifying academic credentials. The ministry's distance learning platform is Fogaan-Dersi.

Not held. Nothing is dated after May 2022, so whether the planned roll-out to about sixty schools took place is not established, and the level of the 42 trial schools is not stated. Nothing held describes a per-pupil record outside Puntland.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

By January 2026 local administrations in 30 districts registered births digitally into one central database; the total of districts or offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Law No. 46, published on 5 April 2026, charges a Directorate under the Ministry of Interior, Federal Affairs and Reconciliation with creating and running a digital registration system with a central database. A government roadmap, reported on 11 August 2025, plans to scale the service to every district, subject to funding and infrastructure, and UNICEF, with the government and Germany, supported the foundational work.

Events registered digitally. The ministry said on 10 August 2025 that the platform enables local administrations to register births, deaths, marriages and divorces digitally. OpenCRVS's country page, published in January 2026, counts 28,391 completed birth registrations.

How the record is made. The ministry announced on 10 August 2025 that local administrations in Jubbaland, Southwest, Hirshabelle, Galmudug and Northeastern State register events on the platform and all records are stored in a centralised national database. OpenCRVS's page, published in January 2026, says the system captures births at the point of occurrence in the districts and linked hospitals.

Last twelve months. The platform, which the ministry calls the Unified Digital Civil Registration Service, was launched as a pilot in 19 districts on 10 August 2025.

Local offices doing the digital entry. OpenCRVS's country page, published in January 2026, states that the system operates in 30 districts and is linked to 160 hospitals.

Noted, not assessed. Notification: hospital birth notification, the notification of births and deaths by health institutions and community health workers planned under the 2021 policy and the UNICEF-supported birth registration pilot in Burao district. Outreach: the Baidoa national ID registration campaign for displaced persons. Identity: the national identification system of the National Identification and Registration Authority (NIRA), its National Identity Management System, the Digital National Identification System set out in Regulation 158, the eAqoonsi app, the Hubiye eKYC platform, the National ID Registration Initiative for Internally Displaced Persons, the NIRA service centre in Boondheere, the family register under Law No. 46 and the Mogadishu and Puntland ID cards and the passport system. Exchange: the interfaces from the civil registration platform to health, education, national ID and immigration services and the planned linkage of the civil registration system with the national ID system.

Not held. No total of districts or offices is held, so no share can be read, and only births are counted as registered. The roadmap carries no period, no domestic budget line is stated, and nothing dated falls inside the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Police used paper-based systems in April 2024, when a donor-funded case system was tendered for Mogadishu only; whether police now use one is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The International Development Law Organization (IDLO) procures the planned case management system with the Office of the Attorney General, which is to receive the source code on completion, and the vendor reports to IDLO (April 2024). The money is from the United States Department of State, under its two-year Somalia Justice Support Programme.

Records made digitally. IDLO stated on 19 April 2024 that the police use paper-based systems, and that the planned system would be the first electronic case management system for Somalia's federal justice institutions.

How the record is made. On the same date IDLO said the police and the Custodial Corps had never made a serious attempt to develop an electronic case management system.

Last twelve months. IDLO invited bids on 19 April 2024, due 3 June 2024, for a system to be developed over 15 months.

Stations doing the digital entry. The tender's criminal investigation module would let investigators create a complaint when it is received at a police station or call centre; its first phase covers only the Criminal Investigation Department in Mogadishu, and nothing was built in April 2024. In Puntland, a member state with its own police, computer stations for nine regional investigation offices, linked to a server in Garowe, were still to be installed in 2021, on equipment given by the European Union's mission.

Noted, not assessed. Statistics: a digital crime statistics system, created under UNODC's lead and populated with data reported by police across Somalia (2022). Identification: the Somali Police Force's Automated Biometric Identification System, housed at Aden Adde International Airport in Mogadishu (2025). Public: online filing of a complaint to a nearby police station, with payment of fees, a planned function of the tendered system in 2024. Enabling: nine police offices refurbished and given vehicles and IT equipment with UNODC's support (2022), and a stated strategy of connectivity for rural police stations in the national ICT policy of 2019.

Not held. Whether the police enter reports or cases in any system built since the 2024 tender is not known, and no station is named as doing so. A plan naming a station record system and anything dated in the last twelve months are not held.
