Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2022 meeting recommended fibre for localities that ask; whether any locality office is connected is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Upper tier: Khartoum State's government fibre and internet network and its standard systems and cloud data store; the state information centres and state informatics projects, coordinated by the National Information Centre; the states' network, which a meeting of 22 November 2022 recommended strengthening; the Isali electronic collection and payment system of Gadarif State and the Gadarif State information centre's network link between collection points, shown to a Kassala State delegation on 3 May 2026; Northern State's Isali electronic payment project, put at 95 per cent complete in June 2026; the websites of the 18 state governments, 89 per cent of them reachable from February to April 2024, and the Khartoum state government portal. Application: the Isali electronic collection and payment system at Abu Hujar locality, in its land offices and collection departments from 7 July 2026. Public access: the universal access centres funded by the Informatics Support Fund, as described in 2011. Backbone: the National Information Centre's National Information Network, its National Data Center, launched in 2010, and its National Internet Exchange Point; the Sudatel Data Centre Khartoum, operational since 2012; and Sudatel's East-Central and West-Central backbone routes, not operational for cross-border traffic in 2022. Central: the Sudan Police's public service complexes, its traffic licensing centres and national vehicle register and its traffic lights and surveillance cameras in Khartoum State; the Ministry of Interior's centralised electronic civil registry; and the national email, whose use the meeting of 22 November 2022 recommended making mandatory.

Not held. No source puts a locality office on a connection, names what an office does over one or counts the localities connected. The one statement held is a meeting's recommendation of 22 November 2022 that Sudatel connect by fibre the localities that ask, at their own cost, with no locality reported connected.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

State electronic collection reached a fourth state in April 2025; whether any locality records revenue digitally is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Bankak, a national banking app used for payments; the Khartoum State government portal, with downloadable forms for business licences and property tax and limited online payment of specific fees and fines; Isali, an electronic collection and payment system, launched in Gedaref on 26 April 2025 as the fourth state to apply it and awaiting launch in Khartoum State in April 2026, with localities named among the bodies to apply it.

Not held. Whether any locality records its own revenue digitally. No local government is named in what is held on the federal electronic collection system.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Local health offices entered facilities' reports into the system in 144 of 189 localities in 2020; nothing since April 2023 shows where data is entered.

Governance, planning, finance. The Federal Ministry of Health established an integrated health management information system in 2013 and deployed DHIS2 from 2016, WHO's 2020 assessment records. Its strategic plan for 2022-2024 names DHIS2 in three strategic interventions. Participants at the ministry's review meeting of February 2026 recommended continued technical and financial support from partners, the Global Fund and UNICEF among them.

Tiers in use. WHO's 2020 assessment states that DHIS2 had not been implemented in most hospitals. The ministry's plan gives 86.40 per cent of selected hospitals as using DHIS2 in 2021.

Where data is digitised. WHO's 2020 assessment states that facility data are entered into DHIS2 at the locality level, and that the platform covered 144 of 189 localities in 17 states, of which 122 reported in May 2020.

Last twelve months. The Federal Minister of Health told the 2025 annual health information performance review that data reporting coverage rose from 13 per cent at the onset of the crisis to more than 56 per cent by the end of 2025. At that meeting, in February 2026, its Director General of Health Information called for a full transition from paper-based to digital reporting through DHIS2.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Global Fund's Inspector General found in 2018 that DHIS2 was not used in health facilities, their results being consolidated on paper and keyed above them. The ministry's plan gives the DHIS2 reporting rate at primary health care level as 46.90 per cent in 2021.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for notifiable diseases and Tier.Net. Single-function: the Health Resources and Services Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS), the laboratory, pharmacy and disease surveillance components of the Hospital Management System, the national maternal death surveillance and response system, the gender-based violence information management system, the integrated disease surveillance and response system, the DHIS2 COVID-19 module, the National Medical Supplies Fund's ERP and electronic logistics management information systems, the TB reporting system and the separate MDR-TB data collection system and eVIN. Community: the community-based health information system. Enabling: IConnect, Care Connect and the ministry's tele-health services for maternal and child health providers.

Not held. Nothing dated after April 2023 says where data is keyed, which localities still enter it or whether any facility enters its own reports. The 56 per cent has no numerator or denominator, and whether hospitals key their own reports is not established.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A hospital system with electronic medical records was launched at two hospital sites on 11 May 2026; routine use is not yet shown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Digital Transformation and Communications launched the hospital management system (HMS) on 11 May 2026 as their own programme, on the Sudan News Agency's report. ICTworks' directory, as of July 2026, lists the Sudan eHealth Strategy of the WHO Global Observatory's 2014 cycle as the operative digital health instrument and states that no replacement has been promulgated.

Tiers in use. Phase one of the HMS started on 11 May 2026 at two hospital sites, Al-Nao hospital in Khartoum state and the diagnostic centre of Gedaref hospital, ahead of gradual expansion to health institutions in other states.

Where data is digitised. An MSc thesis at Sudan University of Science and Technology, of March 2021, states that hospitals in Sudan had no electronic medical records and kept patients' health records on paper.

Last twelve months. A directive published on 30 March 2026 has the Prime Minister ordering hospital systems digitised within two weeks through a first pilot phase at Soba teaching hospital and Gedaref hospital. The HMS launched on 11 May 2026 includes an electronic patient record and an electronic medical record.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The first phase directed in March 2026 covers two hospitals only, with wider expansion left to a later phased plan. The thesis of March 2021 states that government clinics had no electronic medical record and depended wholly on paper records.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module for notifiable diseases and Tier.Net. Single-function: the Health Resources and Services Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS), the laboratory, pharmacy and disease surveillance components of the Hospital Management System, the national maternal death surveillance and response system, the gender-based violence information management system, the integrated disease surveillance and response system, the DHIS2 COVID-19 module, the National Medical Supplies Fund's ERP and electronic logistics management information systems, the TB reporting system and the separate MDR-TB data collection system and eVIN. Community: the community-based health information system. Enabling: IConnect, Care Connect and the ministry's tele-health services for maternal and child health providers.

Not held. Whether the two sites are in routine use, the hospitals' level and where the record is entered are not established. No funding source is named for any system, and no sites or counts are held for SudaHealth, the Khartoum State platform.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry counts schools by survey (2026); how primary schools keep their pupils' records is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school count is taken by survey: a comprehensive educational statistical survey counting schools, students and teachers, begun in Khartoum, Al-Gezira and Sennar states and extended in phases to 7 May 2026, and a Rapid School Census Survey planned for all 18 states. The Ministry of Education's EMIS collects through parallel survey instruments, on a programme document of September 2025, and Sudan's statistics strategy of 2012 names EMIS as the vehicle for school censuses; an Education Statistic Yearbook is cited in a survey of 2007. Single-function: the ministry's ICT system for examinations, in the same 2007 survey. Enabling: Giga's Project Connect, a map of school connectivity, and computer labs in 4,363 of 11,752 public schools as counted in 2007.

Not held. Any dated statement of how primary schools keep their learners' records, on paper or otherwise, and any fact on a system holding a record per learner.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered in a national electronic registry, with one local office named in 2026; how many offices enter records, and where, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Landinfo's report of March 2023 places responsibility for civil registration with the General Directorate for Civil Registration under the Ministry of Interior.

Events registered digitally. The civil registry centre of South Gezira locality issued an electronic birth certificate at its Al-Hosh office on 17 May 2026. A child's birth is registered in the centralised electronic registry introduced in 2011, on Landinfo's report of March 2023.

How the record is made. Landinfo's report of March 2023 describes a centralised electronic registry in which registration is possible at any civil registry office in the country, a birth being registered there on application against a paper notice of birth.

Last twelve months. The Al-Hosh office resumed work in May 2026 after a stoppage caused by insecurity and displacement, and the governor of Gezira State inaugurated civil registry work in Umm al-Qura locality on 29 April 2026. A lawyer told the BBC in a report of August 2026 that the conflict in Darfur has shut down civil registry offices, and an article of June 2026 states that Khartoum's civil registry has ceased almost entirely.

Local offices doing the digital entry. One office is named as issuing an electronic birth certificate in 2026: the Al-Hosh office, which serves South Gezira locality and its surrounding rural villages.

Noted, not assessed. Notification: the paper notice of birth, and the health ministry's death notification form, birth notification form and notification by community leaders and teachers. Outreach: mobile registration units, the CRVS office's registration campaigns and birth registration within the Mother and Child Cash Transfer Plus programme. Identity: the digital identity the government calls SUDAPASS, the national identity number, the civil fingerprint and national number service, the national card, the personal identity card, the Foreign Number system and the Public Service Complexes. Exchange: links to the civil registry from SUDAPASS, the Bayan personnel platform, the customs system ASYCUDA, the Federal Ministry of Health, the Tax Chamber and 31 banks, and the Sudan Family Support Project's data-sharing platform.

Not held. Nothing since the war began says how or where the birth record is made, and no total of offices is held beside the one named. Digital registration of deaths is not evidenced, and no governance fact dates from the last three years.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Police stations took reports on paper in 2022, before the war; how they record now is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The police plan for 2024 to 2030, as reported in June 2026, lists digital systems development among its 39 projects in 21 programmes.

Records made digitally. A thesis abstract from Sudan University of Science and Technology, of 2022, proposes converting the reports taken in Sudanese police stations to an electronic system.

How the record is made. The same abstract describes the system for reports in police stations as the old paper one. Its subject is the report that opens the legal procedure for medical treatment after an incident, known as Form 8.

Last twelve months. Police databases were reported in June 2026 as restored after the war, and as having helped settle administrative and financial matters and the payment of entitlements.

Stations doing the digital entry. The 2022 thesis reports no electronic report system in use in police stations.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the police's electronic report platform, through which citizens had filed 80,902 reports from its creation in June 2023 to January 2025; the Salem application, renewing driving licences for citizens abroad; smart applications for passports, identity cards and driving licences, planned in September 2026; and cashless payment at the public service complexes. Single-function: the national vehicle database and the Ansab platform for transfers of vehicle ownership, the second under development in July 2026; the traffic system in which stolen vehicles were blocked from licensing and sale in 2023; and a unified national smart electronic traffic platform, not yet launched in June 2026. Surveillance: tracking of intercity buses and 20 cameras in Khartoum State.

Not held. How stations have recorded reports since 2022, and since the war began, is not known. Which records any station system would cover, who owns and funds it, and how many stations use one are not held.
