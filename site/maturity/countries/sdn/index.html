Rule in force. Sudan has no rule. Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law is in force and was used in 2026 to jail a pharmacist over anti-war messages and to arrest a journalist.

Law in force. Sudan has a Cybercrime Law, and courts apply it: a criminal court in El Obeid convicted a defendant under Articles 19 and 24 in August 2026. The Sudanese Journalists Syndicate cites the Cybercrime Act as the basis of a live arrest and calls for its repeal. The SudaPass terms cite the Cybercrime Law as the basis of criminal penalties of up to twelve years for electronic sabotage or forgery, and refer to a Cyber Security Act of 2025.

Use of the law. The law has been used against an anti-war critic and a journalist. In August 2026 a criminal court in El Obeid sentenced pharmacist Ahmed Suleiman Ahmed Al-Waqi to six years in total, two of them under Article 19 and one under Article 24 of the Cybercrime Law, after a phone search found anti-war messages, according to his lawyer. In August 2026 criminal investigation police arrested journalist Gurashi Awad in River Nile State on a complaint by a General Intelligence Service lieutenant colonel, the Sudanese Journalists Syndicate said.

Noted, not assessed. SudaPass, the Sudanese digital identity run by the National Electronic Authentication Authority.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Sudan's 2011 Civil Registry Act gives each citizen a unique national number in an electronic register. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Sudan's Civil Registry Act 2011 was signed by the President, repealed the 2001 Act and came into force on signature. It sets up the civil registry, run by the Ministry of Interior, which issues the national number and the identity card. It is an act of the National Assembly, and no later amendment is on record.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The Act defines the national number as a unique number given to each citizen on registration, never to be repeated. It provides for civil registry records to be stored electronically on computer and for a central database. The number and the electronic register are both in the Act's own text, which gives only the year 2011. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. SudaPass (Sudanese Digital Identity); Civil Registry Information System / National Number.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Sudan's 2020 central bank regulation licenses non-bank mobile payment companies; a 2026 circular asks them to connect to a switch not yet available.

Law or rules in force. Sudan's central bank regulates payments under its Regulation on Licensing and Organising Mobile Payment Financial Institutions 2020, approved on 28 December 2020 under the Banking Business Organisation Act, and a Payment Systems Regulation of 2013. Circular 7/2026 of 31 March 2026 cites both as its basis and sets binding minimum rules for financial services over USSD.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The 2020 regulation has the central bank license companies, not banks, to run mobile payment and open e-money wallet accounts; their names may not include the word bank. The 2026 circular lets those institutions and licensed electronic payment providers offer USSD financial services alongside banks. It asks providers to connect to a national switch once one is available, and sets no date.

Regional legal harmonisation: regional digital law taken into national law

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Sudan signed the African Union's cyber security and data protection convention in 2023 and has not ratified it.

African digital law signed and ratified. The African Union's register of its Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection, known as the Malabo Convention, dated 2 February 2026 records Sudan as having signed it on 15 March 2023, with no ratification or deposit. The register counts 21 signatures and 20 instruments deposited among the Union's 55 member states, and the convention has been in force since 8 June 2023. A July 2026 article describes Sudan as a signatory.

Taken into national law and applied across a border. No national law, decree or regulation passed to give effect to a shared African legal text is recorded for Sudan, and nor is a licence or signature recognised, a request between authorities answered or a ruling under one. The African Union's register lists the dates each state signed, ratified and deposited, and says nothing of national law. A July 2026 article says Sudan has no privacy law and has yet to carry the convention into national law.

Not held. No source held dates a ratification or accession.

Mobile affordability: the price of mobile data against income

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2026, 5 GB of mobile data for a month cost 5.97 per cent of average monthly income, on a stated price.

Cost of a month of mobile data against income, with its year. MTN Sudan's cheapest monthly plan of 5 GB or more is a 5 GB bundle at 12,500 Sudanese pounds including VAT, on its tariff page as at 29 August 2026. At that price 5 GB for a month comes to 5.97 per cent of average monthly income, on the World Bank's figure for income per person in 2025. It is the newest figure held, and sets the stage. The International Telecommunication Union's price figures put the cost at 9.46 per cent in 2022, 6.12 per cent in 2023, 6.66 per cent in 2024 and 6.62 per cent in 2025. The cost is within 10 per cent and 0.97 points above 5 per cent. Average monthly income is gross national income per person divided by 12, so the figure says nothing of what the poorest pay against what they earn. The stage rests on that one figure, the newest held for 2024 or later, whoever published it.

Noted, not assessed. Sudani daily, weekly and monthly internet bundles: price increase effective 6 September 2025, no resulting prices stated; MTN Sudan prepaid daily bundles (100MB to 3GB): SDG 1,500 to 3,900 a day; MTN Sudan prepaid weekly bundles (500MB to 3GB): SDG 5,000 to 8,500 a week; MTN Sudan daily bundles (100MB to 3GB): SDG 1,100 to 3,300 a day; MTN Sudan weekly bundles (500MB to 3GB): SDG 3,900 to 7,500 a week.
