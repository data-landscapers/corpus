Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A birth-records platform was at pilot in four communes in 2026 and town hall fibre had not begun; how town halls connect is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The digital ministry's resettlement plan of July 2026 names the ministry's project unit as promoter of the fibre extension that is to reach town halls, under the Projet d'Accélération Numérique du Togo, a 100 million dollar project with a target of 136 town hall buildings, implemented with the World Bank. KfW's records show a EUR 20 million grant committed in 2024 and a EUR 15 million grant committed in 2025 to connect town halls, hospitals and schools to the fibre network.

What the office does over it. Under a pilot in Vo prefecture, Togo First reported in May 2026, the mairie reaches in the platform Naissance Keva the newborn's data entered at the maternity, to draw up the birth record. In Lacs 1, UNDP reported in May 2021, the Kadodo application shares data in real time between health centres and the town hall's civil registry.

How the office is connected. The government's site said on 7 February 2026 that the town hall digitalisation project it calls PDiMa is to connect the 136 municipal buildings themselves.

Last twelve months. The Vo pilot has been deployed since February 2026, and the final resettlement plan for the Maritime region's fibre works was issued in July 2026, with the operator still to be recruited.

Local governments connected. The platform is a pilot in the four communes of Vo prefecture. The July 2026 plan states that the fibre works to the 136 town hall buildings had not begun.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the computers and printers handed to five civil-status pilot communes on 8 April 2026; the South Korean donation of computers and generators, part of it for six collectivités territoriales, received on 16 September 2026; the equipment of target town halls under KfW's e-Governance grants; the computer equipment of the central civil-status offices of the four Lacs communes; and UNDP's furniture and computer equipment for about sixty communes in 2020. Application: the building permit system SIGPC, in which mayors can consult their commune's files since 2022; the georeferenced census of households and economic activities of Golfe 2; the urban addressing databases of Greater Lomé; the plateforme numérique d'enregistrement des naissances, on which the civil-status centres of the 16 communes of the Savanes region were trained in 2024; Hospi-connect and Hospi-gestion, at pilot in Golfe 3 from 28 March 2024; the civil-status software tool of the Lacs communes; the digitalisation of municipal taxes and the geographic information system of Lacs 1; the revenue office's land title verification platform for mairies, developed in 2024; the planned digitalisation of civil registry services under KfW's project; the planned support for digitalised civil-status services and cadastral infrastructure in communes; and the registre d'état civil the communes keep under the law of 2009. Web: the online request for copies of civil-status records, at pilot in five communes since 19 December 2025, on the Services Publics national portal. Public access: the ministry's innovation pods, at pilot. Backbone: the réseau E-Gouv and the Togocom and Moov Africa fibre backbones, the first serving more than 600 public administration buildings in Lomé; the Lomé Data Centre; the national data centre project PNDN; the Yas Togo fixed and mobile network; the broadband connection of about 8,000 public institutions financed by the World Bank; and the Maroc Telecom West Africa submarine cable. Capacity: the cybersecurity agency's national awareness tour, at Kougnohou on 14 September 2026; and the Digital Academy planned under PDiMa. Central: the identification agency's e-ID Togo register and its Registre social des personnes et des ménages; the revenue office's digital cadastre database and its Gest-exo and SYDONIA systems; the port's GUFORD and the land agency's planned système national d'information foncière; the education ministry's SIGE, used at its regional and inspection levels; and the aid management platform, Goal Tracker and the National Assembly's collaborative platform.

Not held. Nothing says how any town hall is connected, and no figure is given for town halls connected by means other than the planned fibre. Outside the pilots, the one commune on record as working online is Lacs 1, in 2021, with no total.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One commune, Lacs 1, started a project to digitalise municipal tax registration in 2021; whether it went into use is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The municipal tax project in Lacs 1 is worked on with UNDP, which says it equipped about sixty communes with office furniture and IT equipment from 2020, as of May 2021.

What is recorded. After a visit on 27 May 2021, UNDP and the commune of Lacs 1, at Aného, were said to have initiated a project to digitalise the registration of municipal taxes, alongside one for civil-status acts.

Where the record is held. The project to digitalise the registration of municipal taxes belongs to the commune of Lacs 1 and UNDP, on UNDP's account of May 2021.

Last twelve months. The latest dated event is the visit of 27 May 2021, after which the tax project was initiated.

Local governments recording. One commune, Lacs 1, had the tax digitalisation project in May 2021, among the about sixty that UNDP says it equipped from 2020.

Noted, not assessed. SIG Recettes, used for taxpayer censuses in Bassar 1, with GIZ and KfW, and in Doufelgou 1 and Sotouboua 2, with GIZ.

Not held. What is recorded and where it is held are not established, and no start of use is stated for the Lacs 1 project. Nothing is dated after May 2021.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

96.5% of all health facilities entered complete reports into the national system in 2021, the ministry says; there is no separate figure for primary clinics.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's monitoring and evaluation plan for its national health development plan 2023-2027 of October 2023 names DHIS2 as the platform of what the ministry calls the SNIS, the national health information system, coordinated by its directorate DSNISI. The Global Fund's Office of the Inspector General reported in June 2025 that Global Fund investment in the health management information system and related systems is EUR 9.8 million in Grant Cycle 7, 2024 to 2026, and that Togo has no coordinated national digitalisation strategy for health.

Tiers in use. The plan names the peripheral care units (USP) and the CHP, CHR and CHU hospitals as the facilities producing and keying SNIS reports. The ministry's procedures manual of April 2022 states that DHIS2 was scaled up to all health facilities from 2018.

Where data is digitised. The plan requires each health facility to key its validated monthly or quarterly activity report into DHIS2 before the 5th of the following month.

Last twelve months. HISP's round-up for July 2026 reports a field mission to integrate the eRDQA data quality app into the national DHIS2 platform, tested at the regional hospitals of Sokodé and Dapaong and the health centres of Kadambara, Ataloté and Kpessidè.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's plan gives 96.5% for 2021 as the share of all health facilities due to report that keyed a complete SNIS report into DHIS2. WHO Togo's 2024 annual report gives a completeness rate of 89.2% for 2021.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: Digital Vaccine Passports through DHIS2, and the digital vaccination booklet and the mother and child health and vaccination follow-up planned in the Togo Digital 2025 strategy. Single-function: the national malaria repository and DHIS2 for infectious disease surveillance and non-communicable disease monitoring; the DHIS2 immunization package, which replaced DVD-MT; DHIS2 for dengue surveillance, ODK forms for surveillance of vaccine-preventable diseases and EIOS; DHIS2 in the 2023 mosquito net campaign, the paper-based LMIS, the planned eLMIS, EDT Web and the medical store's ERP; and the weekly epidemiological surveillance report. Community: SanteComTogo, the community health worker's monthly report keyed into DHIS2 and community DHIS2.

Not held. No share for primary clinics alone is held, nothing newer than 2021, and the 96.5% is printed without its counts. No domestic budget line is stated, and no count of community health workers keying their reports into DHIS2.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The World Bank reported in December 2025 that no electronic health record system was in place.

Governance, planning, finance. The World Bank's additional-financing paper of 24 December 2025 states that since October 2025 the health ministry has been responsible for the health and insurance systems layer, including the electronic health record (EHR). The financing pays EUR 866,112.50 on establishment of the system and EUR 173,223 for every 10 contracted facilities with an EHR.

Tiers in use. The paper records "No" at December 2025 for the establishment of an electronic health record system.

Where data is digitised. The Togo Digital 2025 strategy of April 2021 plans a digital and centralised medical record, as something to be developed.

Last twelve months. On 24 December 2025 the World Bank put forward additional financing, a EUR 60.6 million credit and US$11 million in grants, with new conditions for establishing an EHR in facilities contracted under the national health insurance scheme, starting with a pilot.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The paper's results framework gives a baseline of 0 facilities contracted with the national health insurance scheme holding an EHR at December 2025, out of about 335 contracted facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: Digital Vaccine Passports through DHIS2, and the digital vaccination booklet and the mother and child health and vaccination follow-up planned in the Togo Digital 2025 strategy. Single-function: the national malaria repository and DHIS2 for infectious disease surveillance and non-communicable disease monitoring; the DHIS2 immunization package, which replaced DVD-MT; DHIS2 for dengue surveillance, ODK forms for surveillance of vaccine-preventable diseases and EIOS; DHIS2 in the 2023 mosquito net campaign, the paper-based LMIS, the planned eLMIS, EDT Web and the medical store's ERP; and the weekly epidemiological surveillance report. Community: SanteComTogo, the community health worker's monthly report keyed into DHIS2 and community DHIS2.

Not held. No source states in terms that patient records are on paper at every tier, and the baseline does not cover facilities outside the insurance scheme. Where records are digitised is not held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry launched pupil numbering and designed its platform in 2023; whether any school yet uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The primary and secondary education ministry (MEPS) launched the registration of pupils with a number in 2023. A minister's service note of 2 August 2021 gave the building of a single platform for pupils' marks to the ministry's own centre, CNDP-TICE.

Levels in use. The marks platform ordered in August 2021 was for all public and private schools, consultable at every level of the system.

How the record is kept. The independent panel advising the Global Partnership for Education found on 8 April 2024 no clear evidence that Togo has student identification numbers.

Last twelve months. The joint sector review's aide-mémoire of June 2025 reports the 2023 launch of pupil numbering.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The 2023 work was the design of the enrolment platform that generates the pupils' numbers. The panel said in April 2024 that an automatic data collection system was still being developed.

Noted, not assessed. Togo calls its school census an EMIS: data from the 16,000 nursery, primary and secondary schools is collected on paper forms and entered into StatEduc at the education inspectorates, as described in 2022, and the SIGE on DHIS2, which the country also calls the EMIS, receives validated data weeks after collection, as reported in 2026. The annual statistical census and Statistical Yearbook are yearly, and a School report card is produced on DHIS2. The June 2025 aide-mémoire names the teachers' carte unique de compétences, a carte scolaire prospective numérique piloted in seven prefectures and a platform for teachers' continuing training. A World Bank report of January 2025 names a Human Resources Management Information System in test, a monitoring platform for school inspectorates, a distance learning platform prototype and the CNEA global dashboard for learning assessment. Also named are a school observation tool on Android devices, a WhatsApp bot for exam results and SYNECAT, a planned sample assessment of learning.

Not held. No school is named as using the pupil-numbering platform and no pilot is reported; the marks platform was ordered in 2021, not built. No level, plan, finance or event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A digital birth registration platform was piloted at one town hall in 2024; whether other offices register on it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. WHO's Togo office reports that a national diagnostic of the birth and death registration system in 2024 produced a national roadmap for the digitalisation of civil registration and vital statistics and a multisectoral task force. UNDP Togo states that in 2024 it supported the development, deployment and operation of the digital birth registration platform.

Events registered digitally. UNDP Togo's annual report for 2024 states that the platform, in its pilot phase at the Golfe 3 town hall, simplified the registration of births and of other civil status acts. On 27 May 2021 UNDP described an application the commune of Lacs 1 calls Kadodo as recording births, deaths and marriages.

How the record is made. WHO's assessment summary for Togo, on data from 2013 to 2018, records the exchange of civil registration data from local to regional and central offices as a paper-based system.

Last twelve months. The territorial administration ministry launched the re-reading of law 2009-010 on the organisation of civil status on 21 August 2026, with support from the OIF and UNECA, and reform proposals are expected before the end of 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. UNDP Togo states that in 2024 the platform ran in its pilot phase at one town hall, Golfe 3, and that the civil status centres of the 16 communes of the Savanes region were digitised and trained to use it.

Noted, not assessed. The e-Service du MAECIATE and the Guichet national des démarches administratives digitalisées, the Services Publics platform and the online request for copies of civil status acts in five pilot communes; e-ID Togo and the DGDN Database and National Identity Card; the national catch-up campaign for birth registration and the national campaign of supplementary judgments for children without a birth act; the Intégration CRVS-Education dashboard, the link between the identification system and civil registration and the communication of civil-status acts to the identity agency; the application « Naissance Kéva », the tele-declaration of births and deaths in Golfe 3, the systematic birth registration project in the Lacs prefecture and the registre d'attestation des naissances; and computer equipment for the five pilot communes and the central civil status offices of the Lacs communes.

Not held. How the record is made on the digital birth registration platform, and whether the 16 Savanes communes register on it. No total of offices is given beside them, and the roadmap's period is not stated.

Noted, not assessed. The Système d'Information Policière d'Afrique de l'Ouest (SIPAO/WAPIS), managed by the Centre d'Information Policière du Togo, a national centre in Lomé inaugurated on 29 April 2022; SAED, an automated fingerprint system installed in Togo between October and December 2024; the registre de garde à vue, a paper custody register handed to the investigation units of the police and the gendarmerie on 5 November 2019; the services the Togo Digital 2025 strategy names as Dépôt de plaintes and Demande d'extrait du casier judiciaire; and the new police commissariats at Mango and Cinkassé, in the Savanes region, with vehicles, furniture and computer hardware, inaugurated by December 2025.
