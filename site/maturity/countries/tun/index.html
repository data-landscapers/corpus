Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Electronic records are fully used in 50% of university hospitals and 32% of regional hospitals (2025), with pilot sites in one region's primary care.

Governance, planning, finance. WHO Tunisia stated in April 2024 that the Ministry of Health's Information Technology Centre develops and implements the hospital information management system, with a focus on electronic medical records. The draft National Health Policy of 2019 calls for the generalisation of a single, digitised medical record. The first-line record, which the programme calls the DMI, was developed under Essaha Aziza, a Ministry of Health and European Union programme.

Tiers in use. The World Bank's project document of 2 April 2025 gives the record as fully installed and used in 50 per cent of university hospitals and partially used in the other 50 per cent, and as fully operational in 32 per cent of regional hospitals, partially used in 52 per cent and absent from 16 per cent. In district hospitals it is neither installed nor used.

Where data is digitised. Of primary health care, the same document states that the health system remains largely paper-based and that the DMI pilot faces connectivity and functionality issues.

Last twelve months. The Ministry of Health confirmed on 21 July 2026 that the Sahetna.tn portal, through which citizens are to reach their medical records, had completed technical validation and a cybersecurity audit; it was not yet in service.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Essaha Aziza states that it monitored the DMI's deployment on pilot sites in Nabeul. DH Arab, on the ministry's account at 25 September 2026, has 99 healthcare facilities at various stages of implementing electronic medical records.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: a national management system for disease surveillance and response, planned, and the national medicines agency's project to digitise the path of medicines. Enabling: what the ministry calls the hôpital numérique, teleradiology, five regional telemedicine initiatives, the hôpital numérique intégré, telemedicine teams at Tozeur, Sfax and La Rabta, software for reading medical images remotely, in development with InstaDeep, and the Réseau National de Santé (RNS NG).

Not held. No count of hospitals or of primary clinics using the record is held; the 99 facilities carry no tier and the Nabeul pilot sites no number. The plan is an uncosted draft of 2019, and no domestic budget line for the record is named.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In December 2023, 92.7% of public primary schools were on the ministry's school system; how many enter attendance or results is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The education ministry's digital transformation strategy, presented by its national centre for technologies in education in December 2023, has the development of digital school services as one of its fields. The ministry runs the portal it calls Vie scolaire and publishes the enrolment figures from it.

Levels in use. The centre's December 2023 presentation names Madrassati for the primary stage and EduservPlus for preparatory and secondary.

How the record is kept. The services listed for schools in December 2023 include uploading pupils' absences, sanctions and marks, with the rate of uploading absences given as 2% for the primary stage and 71% for preparatory and secondary. Children entering the first year of basic education in 2025/26 were registered once, by their parents online or by the regional education delegations, and each given a unique identifier.

Last twelve months. Vie scolaire was launched at the end of July 2026, and ministry figures put 53 per cent of all pupils as enrolled on it for 2026/2027 at 20 August 2026. On 7 September 2026 the secondary teachers' federation called on school staff to boycott the ministry's new platform until it works, reporting failures in enrolment and pupil transfers.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The centre counted 4,322 of 4,662 public primary establishments (92.7%) and 52 of 721 private ones on Madrassati in December 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the drop-out prevention model the ministry calls M4D and remote registration for national examinations. Enabling: the school broadband programme Modern School on Every Hill.

Not held. No share or count of primary schools entering attendance or results is held, nor what the 2% rate for primary absences is a share of. The strategy's period, the source of finance and the school levels using the 2026 platform are not held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Registrars enter births and deaths into the national database after the paper register, all offices connected in 2020; how many register is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Births and deaths in the civil register come under the Ministry of Interior, on a 2025 country report. The agreement to rebuild the platform, signed on 19 March 2021, was financed by the African Development Bank and falls under the national plan Tunisie Digitale 2020.

Events registered digitally. The national civil status system, which Tunisia calls Madania, holds births and deaths as well as marriages and divorces in a centralised database, on a 2025 report. It held 18.53 million birth acts and 4.14 million death acts as of 2018.

How the record is made. The registrar writes each event by hand in the paper register and then records it directly in the central database, where it is available nationwide the next morning, on a 2020 account.

Last twelve months. On 20 November, 4 December and 11 December 2025 workshops trained civil status officers and heads of municipal districts from 72 municipalities on the system, organised by the Ministry of the Interior and the National Institute of Health.

Local offices doing the digital entry. All civil registration offices have interconnected computer systems and all municipalities have access to Madania, an IDRC chapter of 2020 states, counting 350 principal offices and 170 subsidiary offices, annexes and mobile units.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the back-entry of all civil registers, begun in 2000. Notification: the Ministry of Health's national birth declaration platform. Certificate: the Khadamet mobile application; the remote translation of the birth extract on e-bawaba.tn; the online request for translated civil status acts; the electronic exchange of civil status extracts between consular posts and municipalities; the electronic civil status extract and the online platform for baccalaureate pupils; the online request for birth certificate copies at etatcivil.gov.tn; the QR code on civil status certificates. Identity: the Unique Citizen Identifier and its register; the national identity card, its remote request and renewal and the biometric card and passport; Mobile ID, which Tunisia calls E-Houwiya; the Registre National Unifié de la Population; Smart Africa's SATA. Exchange: the national interoperability platform and its death notification service; Madania's electronic exchange with the social security funds and MADANIA 2; e-madhmoun, TunXRoad and Madania's data transfers to other bodies; vital registration data to the statistics institute, Amen Social's access to civil status documents and the identifier lookup platform.

Not held. No source gives the share or the number of offices that make the entry in Madania. Nothing on how the record is made or on offices is dated after 2020, and no plan or finance fact later than 2021 is held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Twenty-one model National Guard and National Security centres were under construction in 2025; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Ministère de l'Intérieur's 21 model centres under construction in September 2025, 10 of the National Guard and 11 of National Security, with digital fingerprint readers replacing ink, appointment booking by SMS, an online national identity card application for baccalaureate pupils and a digital residence certificate; the criminal record and its extract, bulletin no. 3, whose digitisation the ministry gave as its strategy in a written answer reported on 6 October 2026; the ministry's digital services platform for passports, as described in August 2026; the Khadamet national application, whose first release of 31 August 2026 shows a citizen their traffic offences; and CCTV surveillance and number-plate recognition for traffic enforcement in Tunis.

Not held. No source held states whether stations record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
