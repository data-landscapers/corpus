The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Cross-border data transfers

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tunisia's 2004 law bars sending personal data abroad without the authority's prior permission; no source says whether any transfer has been approved, refused or penalised.

Rule in force. Tunisia's Organic Law 2004-63 of 27 July 2004, in articles 50 to 52, bars sending personal data abroad where it would harm public security or Tunisia's vital interests. It allows a transfer only to a country with an adequate level of protection. In every case it requires the prior authorisation of the data protection authority, the INPDP, which must decide within one month. The source notes that no replacement law had been adopted when it was retrieved in 2026. A Tunisian lawyer wrote in October 2026 that the 2004 regime for transfers abroad is outdated, and that organic bill No. 095/2025, before the Assembly's Rights and Freedoms Committee, contains a revised framework for them.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The published means of complying is a list of countries judged adequate. A DIAL article of 15 June 2026 says Tunisia's 2018 adequacy list of countries for data transfers identified only five African countries. The 2004 law leaves the authorisation of each transfer to the INPDP, the authority it names as owner of the rule.
