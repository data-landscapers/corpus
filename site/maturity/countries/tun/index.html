E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tunisia has had an electronic exchanges and commerce law since 2000, reinforced in 2020, and no implementing rule is known.

Law in force. Law no. 2000-83 of 9 August 2000 on electronic exchanges and commerce was published in the Journal Officiel no. 64 of 11 August 2000 and gives electronic documents and signatures legal effect. Decree-law 2020-31 of 10 June 2020 (JORT no. 54) took effect on publication. It gives a reliable electronic signature the same evidentiary force as a handwritten one, and electronic documents the same force as paper.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. Law 2000-83 creates the national electronic certification agency (ANCE) and licenses certification providers. The same law sets consumer rules for online sales: disclosure duties, a 10-working-day withdrawal right and fines. Together with the decree-law, these are the rules held on how electronic documents and signatures are recognised and supervised.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tunisia's 2004 law bars sending personal data abroad without the authority's prior permission; whether any transfer has been approved, refused or penalised is not known.

Rule in force. Tunisia's Organic Law 2004-63 of 27 July 2004, in articles 50 to 52, bars sending personal data abroad where it would harm public security or Tunisia's vital interests. It allows a transfer only to a country with an adequate level of protection. In every case it requires the prior authorisation of the data protection authority, the INPDP, which must decide within one month. The source notes that no replacement law had been adopted when it was retrieved in 2026.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The published means of complying is a list of countries judged adequate. A DIAL article of 15 June 2026 says Tunisia's 2018 adequacy list of countries for data transfers identified only five African countries. The 2004 law leaves the authorisation of each transfer to the INPDP, the authority it names as owner of the rule.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source from the last three years names a country approved to receive data, an organisation given or refused permission, or an order or penalty for sending data abroad.

Consultation and what was published of it. The Instance Nationale des Télécommunications, the telecommunications regulator, ran a first public consultation from 26 September to 26 November 2025 on the conditions for sharing fibre-optic access infrastructure.
