Morocco's 2020 cybersecurity law requires sensitive data to be hosted only on national territory, with fines; the body that enforces it is not named.

Rule in force. Morocco's rule sits in its cybersecurity law. Article 11 of Law No. 05-20, published in the official bulletin of 6 August 2020, requires sensitive data to be hosted exclusively on national territory. Law in force. Morocco's law no. 72-18, on the targeting of beneficiaries of social support programmes, was promulgated on 8 August 2020 and published in the Bulletin officiel on 7 January 2021. Law in force. Morocco's Law 43-20 on trust services for electronic transactions sets the rules for trust services, cryptology means and services, and trust service providers and certificate holders. It also sets the powers of the national trust services authority. The law covers trust services, meaning signatures and certificates, rather than online selling. The DGSSI applies it, and its register of regulated services cites the law's Articles 8, 17, 46, 52 and 53 as the basis for what it regulates.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. Decree 2-22-687 was made to apply the law. Under Article 53 the DGSSI publishes a list of approved trust service providers, which as of September 2026 names three at the qualified level: EURAFRIC INFORMATION, Barid Al Maghrib and DamaneSign. It also lists the conformity certificates it has issued for qualified signature and seal creation devices, six of them current, the latest dated 18 March 2026.
