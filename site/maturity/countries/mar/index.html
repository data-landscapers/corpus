Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Standing. The ITU repository lists Digital Morocco 2030 among the strategies it says countries have adopted, and the Government Council heard a presentation of its general orientations on 25 January 2024. It follows Maroc Numeric 2013 and Maroc Digital 2020.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 cybersecurity law requires sensitive data to be hosted only on national territory, with fines; the body that enforces it is not named.

Rule in force. Morocco's rule sits in its cybersecurity law. Article 11 of Law No. 05-20, published in the official bulletin of 6 August 2020, requires sensitive data to be hosted exclusively on national territory. Article 49 sets fines of 200,000 to 400,000 dirhams for the heads of vital entities or infrastructures that host such data abroad, and article 53 makes the law operative once its implementing texts are published. A report of June 2025 says a 2021 law mandates that sensitive data be stored locally, which puts the requirement in effect.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the law's penalty article, which sets the fines and, in the text held, does not name the body that imposes them. No source held names a body that checks compliance with the rule or grants exemptions from it. No fine or exemption under article 11 is held.

Noted, not assessed. AWS Wavelength Zone in Orange data centres.

Not held. No source held names a body that enforces the rule: a regulator, agency or ministry unit that checks compliance or grants exemptions.

Morocco's 2009 data protection law is in force and its authority issues guidance; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. The Commission nationale de contrôle de la protection des données à caractère personnel (CNDP) was installed in 2010 and acts under Law 09-08. Its president, Omar Seghrouchni, said on 4 August 2026 that it is strengthening an alert system that can go as far as sanctions.

Standing. Law 09-08 on personal data is in force: the CNDP cited ten of its articles to political parties in August 2026, and its president spoke that month of a law more than fifteen years in force.

Regulations. The CNDP validated five deliberations on 28 November 2025 setting model declaration and authorisation forms for cookies, newsletters, patient follow-up, healthcare video surveillance and workplace access control.

Application. The CNDP published a national register of notified processing operations in January 2023. On 28 August 2026 it issued guidance to political parties and voters for the legislative elections of 23 September 2026 and opened an information line, 3020. It runs a programme with public and private controllers that it calls Data-Tika.

Last twelve months. A proposed recast of Law 09-08, prepared with the Justice Ministry, has been sent to the Secrétariat général du gouvernement, the CNDP president said on 4 August 2026. A draft framework law on AI, data governance and digital identity, called Digital X.0, was under review by the same secretariat in November 2025. The CNDP signed a cooperation memorandum with Portugal's data protection authority on 8 February 2026.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 law creates a population register against which identity is checked online. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Morocco's law no. 72-18, on the targeting of beneficiaries of social support programmes, was promulgated on 8 August 2020 and published in the Bulletin officiel on 7 January 2021. It creates the national population register and the national registers agency that runs it. It is a statute and no bill to replace it is on record.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law's second article defines authentication as checking declared data online and in real time against the national population register. An identity entered in the register is therefore one that can be checked electronically, which is what the law provides the register for. The register and the agency are the law's two creations. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. Registre national de la population.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Morocco's 2014 banking law licenses payment institutions that are not banks, and they were active in 2026; no rule requiring providers to connect is known.

Law or rules in force. Morocco's Law 103-12 on credit institutions, enacted on 24 December 2014, defines means of payment, electronic money among them, and payment services, and regulates payment institutions. Bank Al-Maghrib supervises them; in July 2026 it cut the cap on domestic card interchange fees from 0.65% to 0.50%, effective 1 October 2026.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The law defines payment institutions as entities offering payment services, separate from credit institutions, and requires them to obtain Bank Al-Maghrib's approval before operating. Bank Al-Maghrib and the Competition Council said in July 2026 that newly licensed payment institutions and older ones were taking up card acquiring. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other. Payment institutions also open accounts remotely alongside banks, under a 2020 ruling of the data protection commission.

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Law or decree in force. Morocco has a bill and no law in force. It was still being worked on before going to parliament, and the minister delegate for digital transition said it clarifies how data moves between government institutions.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 trust services law is in force, with a 2023 decree and three approved qualified providers listed in September 2026.

Law in force. Morocco's Law 43-20 on trust services for electronic transactions sets the rules for trust services, cryptology means and services, and trust service providers and certificate holders. It also sets the powers of the national trust services authority. The law covers trust services, meaning signatures and certificates, rather than online selling. The DGSSI applies it, and its register of regulated services cites the law's Articles 8, 17, 46, 52 and 53 as the basis for what it regulates.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. Decree 2-22-687 was made to apply the law. It sets transitional periods running from 13 July 2023, including six months for non-qualified trust service providers under its Article 33. Under Article 53 the DGSSI publishes a list of approved trust service providers, which as of September 2026 names three at the qualified level: EURAFRIC INFORMATION, Barid Al Maghrib and DamaneSign. It also lists the conformity certificates it has issued for qualified signature and seal creation devices, six of them current, the latest dated 18 March 2026.

Not held. Nothing on the four questions.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A revenue system was deployed in 969 of 1,590 local authorities of all tiers by 2020; how commune offices are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Interior Ministry's directorate for local governments is responsible for the programme under which GIR-CT, Rokhas, the civil registry system and Chikaya were rolled out to participating municipalities. That programme, the Municipal Performance Program, is financed by an IBRD loan with AFD co-financing and closes on 31 December 2026. The directorate drew up a digital transformation strategy for local authorities in 2021.

What the office does over it. More than 100 large municipalities manage the whole local taxation chain in the Integrated Revenue Management System for Local Authorities, GIR-CT, the World Bank reported in August 2025. Rokhas, the platform for issuing building permits and commercial licences, is operational in all participating municipalities, on the Interior Ministry's information of January 2026.

How the office is connected. The World Bank's identity diagnostic of 2016 reported no official statistics on computers at the roughly 2,200 commune civil registry offices, and said the municipal councils equip those offices from their own budgets.

Last twelve months. The World Bank's status report of 30 January 2026 counts 59 of 97 participating municipalities with the civil registry back-office system operational.

Local governments connected. GIR-CT was deployed in 969 of 1,590 local authorities of all tiers, 60.9 per cent, as of 19 May 2020, on the annex to the Interior Ministry's circular no. 32. Rokhas has been deployed in over 91 participating municipalities covering 16.9 million people, as of January 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Applications: Rokhas, GIR-CT, the civil registry back-office system and Chikaya, the GID accounting and budget system, Indimaj, the Bureau d'Ordre Digital, the Système de Gestion de l'État Civil and the Système d'Information Géographique, the base de données électronique de l'état civil and the nouveau système d'information of the civil registry, the registre national de l'état civil, the registre civil numérisé and the national central digital civil-status system of Law 36.21. City systems: the Rabat smart city platform, Rabat's CCTV surveillance and its smart toilets, and Casablanca's smart city. Upper tier: the Casablanca-Settat regional council's système d'information intégré and the monitoring platform for regional development plans. Central: the directorate's performance assessment platform and the RAMED database.

Communes issue local tax bills in a national system, in more than 100 large municipalities in August 2025; the share recording is not known.

The minister's circular of 19 May 2020 told authorising officers to equip their revenue offices and work with the treasury on extending it.

What is recorded. Communal tax officials issue the annual bills for local taxes in GIR-CT, and the treasury's accountants take the payments, as reported on 29 May 2025.

Where the record is held. The World Bank's status report on its mission of August 2025 has GIR-CT operating at national level, interfaced with local systems. In May 2025 the ministry told local authorities to upload their revenue orders in GIR-CT alone and to drop software they had bought or built. The treasury collects all municipal revenues except the professional tax, the World Bank said in November 2021.

Last twelve months. On 29 May 2025 taxpayers were being urged to declare and pay local taxes through the online platform set up with the treasury.

Local governments recording. The World Bank's status report of August 2025 puts GIR-CT in place in more than 100 large municipalities and gives no total of municipalities. The Interior Ministry's circular had it deployed in 969 of 1,590 local authorities on 19 May 2020.

Noted, not assessed. The tax directorate runs the Système intégré de taxation and manages the base of the communal services, housing and professional taxes for the municipalities, with the treasury collecting. The State has an online platform to fill and pay local taxes, which the treasury and the Interior Ministry call the plateforme électronique de déclaration et de paiement des taxes des collectivités territoriales. Spending and accounts run in GID and GID-CT, and staff records in Indimaj and @ujour-idm@j. Permits go through Rokhas and correspondence through the Bureau d'Ordre Digital.

Not held. How many local authorities record in GIR-CT, as a share: the newest count has no total and the only figure with one is a deployment figure of 2020. No plan or finance for the system, and nothing dated in the twelve months to September 2026.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A national reporting system was working in every public health centre by December 2019; no figure shows how many centres enter their own reports.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Health Compact of 6 December 2025 makes the Ministry of Health and Social Protection and the territorial health groups accountable for deploying an integrated health information system, which it calls the SIIS, in all primary health care establishments and all university hospitals. The World Bank ties US$37.5 million of its US$450 million loan to health data, with nothing disbursed against it at 20 June 2025.

Tiers in use. The World Bank's report of June 2025 gives as its baseline that an integrated and digitalised health information system was deployed at hospitals and that deployment had begun in primary health care centres. The World Bank reports an earlier system, which the ministry calls the SMIPF-SC, operational in public health centres in all regions by December 2019.

Where data is digitised. The SMIPF-SC gave service-utilisation data at facility level by December 2019, aggregated through dashboards at provincial, regional and central levels, the World Bank reports. Results data came from a paper-based system until the SMIPF-SC supplied them from 2018.

Last twelve months. The Compact gives the SIIS, at 6 December 2025, as being deployed in primary health care establishments and in progress in university hospitals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The World Bank's completion report gives the SMIPF-SC as operational in 100% of public health centres by December 2019. Its report of June 2025 has deployment of the integrated system begun in primary health care centres.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: NADIS, a record for HIV patients at the CHU Mohammed VI. Single-function: radiology information and picture archiving systems, laboratory information management systems, pharmacy information systems and the maternal and neonatal death surveillance system. Enabling: the AI-assisted ultrasound pilot, AI-assisted breast cancer screening, the national telemedicine pilot of October 2018 and peritoneal dialysis telemonitoring at the CHU Hassan II in Fez.

Not held. No share or count of health centres entering their own reports is held, and no source says who keys the data; the 100% is of centres where the system was operational, in 2019. No domestic budget line for the system is named.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A hospital record system is reported in all public hospitals (2024), and one province's primary clinics keep electronic patient files; sharing is not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. Framework law 06-22 of 9 December 2022 creates a shared medical record within the national health information system. Nichan reported on 23 July 2024 that the health ministry was preparing to buy a national system built on a shared patient file, at an estimated 189,999,000 dirham.

Tiers in use. A government document published in December 2024 states that the integrated hospital information system has been deployed in all public hospitals of the twelve regions. A purchase notice of 23 April 2024 places the HOSIX patient-file database in the primary health care establishments of Taounate province.

Where data is digitised. At the CHU Mohammed VI in Marrakech, doctors write observations and diagnoses in HOSIX and order laboratory tests there, with the paper record kept alongside, a thesis defended on 29 May 2024 reports. The Taounate notice buys 19,200 working hours of contracted staff to key patient files into HOSIX.

Last twelve months. Medias24 reported in July 2026 that the Government Council had adopted draft law 52.26, which creates a shared medical record for every patient. The health minister said on 1 June 2026 that the ministry is still working to put the unified medical record in place.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The same document has an information system for primary health care establishments in the course of deployment. The ministry said on 5 April 2026 that work continues with the generalisation of the electronic medical record.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: NADIS, a record for HIV patients at the CHU Mohammed VI. Single-function: radiology information and picture archiving systems, laboratory information management systems, pharmacy information systems and the maternal and neonatal death surveillance system. Enabling: the AI-assisted ultrasound pilot, AI-assisted breast cancer screening, the national telemedicine pilot of October 2018 and peritoneal dialysis telemonitoring at the CHU Hassan II in Fez.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics using a patient record is held, and no count of hospitals using the hospital system; primary-tier use is held for one province. No shared record is in service, and the financing of the planned national system is not stated.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Every school has registered its pupils in a national per-pupil record since 2014; how many primary schools enter attendance or marks is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. MASSAR was developed by the Ministry of National Education and Vocational Training and launched at the end of 2013, the World Bank's identity diagnostic states. On 7 May 2026 the ministry's secretary-general instructed the regional academies on who records and who validates absence in 'Massar+ Idara'.

Levels in use. In May 2014 MASSAR's register covered every child aged 6 to 18 enrolled in any national educational establishment, 6,512,192 pupils, by the same diagnostic. The national evaluation body's atlas of 2019 follows cohorts of primary pupils into lower secondary from MASSAR's individual records for 2014 to 2018.

How the record is kept. In June 2014 each establishment registered and vetted its own pupils into a central database, and each pupil kept one number for the whole of their schooling. By the 2019 atlas MASSAR has a pupil assessment component through which parents consult their children's marks online. In the pioneer middle schools, by the ministry's letter of 7 May 2026, teachers record absence at the start of each lesson and the school administration keys it into 'Massar+ Idara'.

Last twelve months. The letter of 7 May 2026 rolls out a new digital component for daily monitoring of pupil absence in the pioneer middle schools.

Primary schools doing the digital input. In June 2014 pupils were vetted at each of the country's 10,000 learning establishments, the World Bank's diagnostic states: a figure for schools of all levels registering pupils.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the recensement scolaire annuel 2025-2026. Single-function: the Tayssir identity register.

Not held. No share or count of primary schools entering attendance or results is held, and daily entry is shown for lower secondary schools only. Who keys primary pupils' marks, and the plan and financing, are not held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Offices enter births and deaths in a ministry system said in 2024 to reach every office; the share of offices registering digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of the Interior runs the civil-status management system, which it calls SGEC, under its civil-status modernisation programme, reported in October 2024. The World Bank's Municipal Performance Program, which counts a civil registry back-office system among its results, is financed by an IBRD loan with AFD co-financing and closes on 31 December 2026.

Events registered digitally. The ministry said in October 2024 that SGEC handles birth and death declarations digitally, and that over 1,300,000 acts had been declared or issued from it.

How the record is made. By the ministry's October 2024 account, an entry agent at the civil-status office enters acts in SGEC and the civil-status officer checks and validates them. In October 2025 Hespress reported communes short of the paper registers essential for registering births.

Last twelve months. The World Bank gives the civil registry back-office system as operational in 59 of 97 participating municipalities on 30 June 2024 and unchanged on 30 January 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The ministry said in October 2024 that SGEC was generalised to all the Kingdom's civil-status offices. In October 2025 its circular called for staff to be trained on the national digital platform, access to which was to be opened shortly.

Noted, not assessed. Back-capture: the reprise de l'historique de l'état civil, the digitisation of paper registers under Law 36-21 and the DGCT's tender for digitising the civil register. Notification: the preliminary declaration of births and deaths through the civil status portal. Certificates: watiqa.ma. Identity: the electronic national identity card and its online renewal, Mon e-ID, the national digital identity wallet, the national digital ID platform, the Registre national de la population, the identifiant digital civil et social and MOSIP. Exchange: the statistics office's access to the civil-status database.

Not held. No count of offices registering digitally against the country's total of offices is held. The modernisation programme's period and budget, and whether an identifier is assigned at birth in practice, are not held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

Police stations record complaints and cases in connected national systems, cases in 16 of 22 commands in 2025; the share of stations is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Direction Générale de la Sûreté Nationale (DGSN), the national police, built GESTARR with its own technical staff and holds its data on a central DGSN server. Its own teams built the case-management system added to GESTARR in 2024.

Records made digitally. Complaints lodged at a police arrondissement are managed in GESTARR (October 2019). In 2024 the DGSN added a system for crime and misdemeanour cases at the arrondissements, each case followed through investigation to submission to the judicial authorities. Its 2025 review says the system it calls Qadaya handles crime and misdemeanour cases in real time.

How the record is made. In October 2019 the arrondissements' information was stored on a central DGSN server and shared between arrondissements across the Kingdom, on a platform that links the arrondissements to each other and to the Sûreté nationale's central services.

Last twelve months. The DGSN's review of 2025 says Qadaya's rollout continued during the year and reached 16 of the 22 police commands, with national coverage in view.

Stations doing the digital entry. GESTARR was in almost all the Kingdom's arrondissements in October 2019, and its generalisation was completed in 2023, when the DGSN counted 454 police arrondissements and had the case system on trial in 350 police posts. The case system was in use in 12 commands in 2024, among them the regional sûretés of Ouarzazate, Errachidia, Taza and Al Hoceima, and Qadaya covered 16 of the 22 commands in 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the electronic extract of the criminal record, E-Blagh, for reporting offences committed on the internet, e-Police and CNIE.MA, online renewal of the national identity card on the police portal and the police recruitment portal. Surveillance: the Amane patrol vehicle and the Madar mobile platform, Rabat's camera network and command centres, the urban video-protection system, patrol geolocation and number-plate reading on urban cameras and the call-management application of emergency line 19. Identification: the identity card system's fingerprint identification, and the national database of suspects' composite portraits and the national forensic laboratory's evidence tracking. Single-function: the digitisation of traffic-accident reports, Tifli Moukhtafi, for missing children, and the road-control document applications and electronic payment of traffic fines. Personnel: E-formation. Enabling: the police's unified fibre-optic network and the citizen reception spaces' information system and the DGSN's electronic messaging system.

Not held. The share of police arrondissements entering their records: the DGSN counts commands, not stations. No plan or finance for the systems is stated, and nothing dated places the Royal Gendarmerie's brigades in or out of a record system.
