Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco has Maroc Digital 2030, listed as adopted in 2025, with MAD 11 billion for 2024 to 2026, but a public text is not known.

Governance and finance. The ITU repository names the Ministry of Digital Transition and Administration Reform as lead of Maroc Digital 2030, and Morocco World News says MAD 11 billion was allocated for 2024-2026.

Standing. The ITU repository lists Digital Morocco 2030 among the strategies it says countries have adopted, and the Government Council heard a presentation of its general orientations on 25 January 2024. It follows Maroc Numeric 2013 and Maroc Digital 2020.

Text. We Are Tech Africa says Maroc Digital 2030 was launched in September 2024.

Period. The ITU repository gives its time frame as 2024-2030.

Delivery plan. Morocco World News pairs the MAD 11 billion for 2024-2026 with targets of 3,000 start-ups, 100,000 people trained a year and 240,000 jobs by 2030.

Reporting. In its declaration to the African Telecommunications Union conference in Abuja, Morocco highlighted progress under Maroc Digital 2030, without figures.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 cybersecurity law requires sensitive data to be hosted only on national territory, with fines; the body that enforces it is not named.

Rule in force. Morocco's rule sits in its cybersecurity law. Article 11 of Law No. 05-20, published in the official bulletin of 6 August 2020, requires sensitive data to be hosted exclusively on national territory. Article 49 sets fines of 200,000 to 400,000 dirhams for the heads of vital entities or infrastructures that host such data abroad, and article 53 makes the law operative once its implementing texts are published. A report of June 2025 says a 2021 law mandates that sensitive data be stored locally, which puts the requirement in effect.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the law's penalty article, which sets the fines and, in the text held, does not name the body that imposes them. No source held names a body that checks compliance with the rule or grants exemptions from it. No fine or exemption under article 11 is held.

Noted, not assessed. AWS Wavelength Zone in Orange data centres.

Not held. No source held names a body that enforces the rule: a regulator, agency or ministry unit that checks compliance or grants exemptions.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2009 data protection law is in force and its authority issues guidance; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. The Commission nationale de contrôle de la protection des données à caractère personnel (CNDP) was installed in 2010 and acts under Law 09-08. Its president, Omar Seghrouchni, said on 4 August 2026 that it is strengthening an alert system that can go as far as sanctions.

Standing. Law 09-08 on personal data is in force: the CNDP cited ten of its articles to political parties in August 2026, and its president spoke that month of a law more than fifteen years in force.

Regulations. The CNDP validated five deliberations on 28 November 2025 setting model declaration and authorisation forms for cookies, newsletters, patient follow-up, healthcare video surveillance and workplace access control.

Application. The CNDP published a national register of notified processing operations in January 2023. On 28 August 2026 it issued guidance to political parties and voters for the legislative elections of 23 September 2026 and opened an information line, 3020. It runs a programme with public and private controllers that it calls Data-Tika.

Last twelve months. A proposed recast of Law 09-08, prepared with the Justice Ministry, has been sent to the Secrétariat général du gouvernement, the CNDP president said on 4 August 2026. A draft framework law on AI, data governance and digital identity, called Digital X.0, was under review by the same secretariat in November 2025. The CNDP signed a cooperation memorandum with Portugal's data protection authority on 8 February 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No decision on a named controller and no published count of decisions, sanctions or complaints is held. No regulation under Law 09-08 is held, and what a 2023 amendment to the law changed is not known.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 law creates a population register against which identity is checked online. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Morocco's law no. 72-18, on the targeting of beneficiaries of social support programmes, was promulgated on 8 August 2020 and published in the Bulletin officiel on 7 January 2021. It creates the national population register and the national registers agency that runs it. It is a statute and no bill to replace it is on record.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law's second article defines authentication as checking declared data online and in real time against the national population register. An identity entered in the register is therefore one that can be checked electronically, which is what the law provides the register for. The register and the agency are the law's two creations. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. Registre national de la population.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2014 banking law licenses payment institutions that are not banks, and they were active in 2026; no rule requiring providers to connect is known.

Law or rules in force. Morocco's Law 103-12 on credit institutions, enacted on 24 December 2014, defines means of payment, electronic money among them, and payment services, and regulates payment institutions. Bank Al-Maghrib supervises them; in July 2026 it cut the cap on domestic card interchange fees from 0.65% to 0.50%, effective 1 October 2026.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The law defines payment institutions as entities offering payment services, separate from credit institutions, and requires them to obtain Bank Al-Maghrib's approval before operating. Bank Al-Maghrib and the Competition Council said in July 2026 that newly licensed payment institutions and older ones were taking up card acquiring. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other. Payment institutions also open accounts remotely alongside banks, under a 2020 ruling of the data protection commission.

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Law or decree in force. Morocco has a bill and no law in force. A framework bill known as Digital X.0, covering artificial intelligence, data governance, digital identity and interoperability, was under review by the General Secretariat of the Government in November 2025. It was still being worked on before going to parliament, and the minister delegate for digital transition said it clarifies how data moves between government institutions.

Sharing required and the law applied. Binding rule on AI in force. Morocco has a binding rule on one use of AI. E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's 2020 trust services law is in force, with a 2023 decree and three approved qualified providers listed in September 2026.

Law in force. Morocco's Law 43-20 on trust services for electronic transactions sets the rules for trust services, cryptology means and services, and trust service providers and certificate holders. It also sets the powers of the national trust services authority. The law covers trust services, meaning signatures and certificates, rather than online selling. The DGSSI applies it, and its register of regulated services cites the law's Articles 8, 17, 46, 52 and 53 as the basis for what it regulates.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. Decree 2-22-687 was made to apply the law. It sets transitional periods running from 13 July 2023, including six months for non-qualified trust service providers under its Article 33. Under Article 53 the DGSSI publishes a list of approved trust service providers, which as of September 2026 names three at the qualified level: EURAFRIC INFORMATION, Barid Al Maghrib and DamaneSign. It also lists the conformity certificates it has issued for qualified signature and seal creation devices, six of them current, the latest dated 18 March 2026.

Noted, not assessed. DamaneSign; AfricTRUST CertiSign+ / CertiSeal+; Barid eSign; Chaabi eSign; BADR; PortNet.

Not held. Nothing on the four questions.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Morocco's law sets a condition on sending personal data abroad, as stated in 2012, with a 2015 country list; recent application is not known.

Rule in force. Morocco's Act 09-08, named in Morocco as Loi n° 09-08 and overseen by the supervisory authority CNPD, lays down protection of transfers of personal data to foreign countries, according to the Council of Europe Consultative Committee in a document of 18 October 2012. Its articles 43 and 44 apply that protection in particular where the receiving country does not provide an adequate level of protection, the same document states.

Means of complying published and rule applied. A DIAL expert comment states that Morocco's 2015 adequacy list, a list of countries personal data may be sent to, did not recognise a single African country. The year is the only date the comment gives for the list, so a published list of receiving countries is the means of complying that the evidence dates.
