Digital transformation strategy

Governance and finance. The Plan stratégique Cameroun numérique 2020 gives delivery to the postal and telecommunications ministry. It works with the other ministries, ANTIC, ART and CAMTEL. Last twelve months. In October 2026 the minister said a draft national strategy for the digital economy was being finalised. It builds on the national AI consultations. Cameroon's 2024 data protection law came into force in June 2026, with its authority not installed; no source found reports a decision under it.

Authority. The law defines the Autorité as an independent public body in charge of data protection and gives it supervision, notification and enforcement functions. The OBS-CC observatory reported on 12 September 2026 that the authority had not yet been installed.

Standing. Cameroon's general law is Loi n° 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on the protection of personal data. The Minister of Posts and Telecommunications told the National Assembly in April 2025 that it had been adopted. A July 2025 review describes an 18-month grace period from enactment. Regulations. The implementing decree called for by article 53 had not been taken by 12 September 2026, the OBS-CC observatory reported. The law itself sets principles, controller obligations and data subject rights.

Application. The law has been in force since 23 June 2026. Enforcement under it is a function of the Autorité.

Last twelve months. The law entered into force on 23 June 2026, which the budget directorate reported on 6 July 2026.

Cameroon's 2024 civil registration law provides a unique personal identification number for every citizen. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Cameroon's Law 2024/016, the new civil registration law, is the law the national civil status office is implementing, as reported in October 2025. It is an ordinary law of 2024 and replaces the earlier civil registration regime. The report describes the office as working under it, with the registers being moved onto digital systems.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law authorises digital birth, marriage and death certificates and a unique personal identification number for every citizen. The number makes the law an identity law as well as a civil registration law. Each citizen is to hold one number, issued from the civil register. The certificates drawn from that register may be digital. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

E-commerce legislation

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2010 e-commerce law is in force with a 2011 application decree, but no use of it is dated in the last three years.

Law in force. Law No. 2010/021 of 21 December 2010, adopted by the National Assembly and promulgated by the President, governs electronic commerce in Cameroon. It defines electronic signatures, certificates and certification authorities. The Prime Minister's decree of 15 June 2011 describes it as a made law and fixes its application rules. At a CENADI workshop on 14 August 2024, Professor Jean Louis Fendji listed the e-commerce law among the local texts that frame aspects of data protection.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. Decree no. 2011/1521/PM of 15 June 2011 fixes the modalities of application of law no. 2010/021 on electronic commerce. It defines the scope of e-commerce activity under the law. The decree was issued by the Prime Minister and is published on the Ministry of Commerce website, where the law is also published. It follows the law of 21 December 2010 that it implements, by about six months, and is the rule-making instrument for e-commerce in Cameroon.
