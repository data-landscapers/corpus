Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cameroon has a published digital plan adopted in 2016 whose period has ended, and an update in preparation, so progress is not known.

Governance and finance. The Plan stratégique Cameroun numérique 2020 assigns delivery to the postal and telecommunications ministry with the other ministries, ANTIC, ART and CAMTEL. Its financing column draws on the public investment budget, the telecommunications special fund and the State budget, alongside external financing.

Standing. The plan, dated May 2016, calls itself the adopted strategic plan and names no adopting act. A workshop on 3 July 2025 discussed the diagnostic for updating it, with the next step to follow government approval.

Text. The plan's full text, 72 pages, is published on the ministry's site.

Period. The plan began in 2016 and, on both its own text and the ITU repository, ended by 2025.

Delivery plan. The priority action plan lists projects per axis with indicators, 2020 targets and an amount in FCFA each, such as 125,000,000,000 for a broadband backbone extension.

Reporting. The plan assigns monitoring and evaluation to the PPBS committees of each administration and their monitoring-evaluation cells.

Last twelve months. The ITU repository lists Digital Cameroon 2020 as the country's strategy in its 2025 edition.

Noted, not assessed. Projet d'Accélération de la Transformation Numérique au Cameroun, loi e-Gov, Law on Personal Data Protection 2024, Digital Master Plan for decentralized entities, Stratégie Nationale de Développement 2020-2030, Plan Stratégique National de Santé Numérique 2026-2030, CAB-CORRIDOR.

Not held. The update of the plan has no text or period yet. No report against the plan is held.

Data interoperability framework / roadmap: government interoperability framework

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cameroon's draft e-government law, validated by officials in November 2025, would set interoperability rules for public bodies; it is not adopted.

Framework adopted. Cameroon's rule is in a draft law. A workshop at Ebolowa from 3 to 14 November 2025 gave technical validation to the draft e-government bill on access to public information and the digital transformation of the administration, after which the text was ready for ministers to finalise. The experts' recommendations covered interoperability.

Required and followed. The project that supports the reform says the bill provides that administrations must make their information systems compatible, with GovTech Cameroun setting the security and interoperability norms. That is what the law would require once passed. The bill has not been adopted, and no body is yet bound by it. The same account, published on 20 November 2025, gives no date for its passage through parliament.

Noted, not assessed. MINDDEVEL decentralisation digitalisation programme (384 councils); Guichet unique maritime; MINFI budget and customs information systems; UMAC/BEAC interoperable payment QR code; Plateforme nationale unifiée (point d'accès unique aux services publics numériques); Unified Social Registry (RESUC) MIS; Common ID System; Civil Registration System; Disability Registry.

Not held. No source held says a government interoperability framework is adopted and published.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cameroon's 2024 data protection law came into force in June 2026; no decision under it is recorded, and its authority's appointment is not known.

Authority. The law defines the Autorité as an independent public body in charge of data protection and gives it supervision, notification and enforcement functions.

Standing. Cameroon's general law is Loi n° 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on the protection of personal data. The Minister of Posts and Telecommunications told the National Assembly in April 2025 that the country had adopted it. The Ministry of Finance's budget directorate states that the deadline for its entry into force was reached on 23 June 2026 and that the law is now in force.

Regulations. The law itself sets the principles, the obligations of controllers and the rights of data subjects, as the minister described it to the Assembly.

Application. The law has been in force since 23 June 2026, and enforcement under it is a function of the Autorité.

Last twelve months. The law entered into force on 23 June 2026, which the budget directorate reported on 6 July 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No regulation, decree or order made under Loi n° 2024/017 is held, and no decision, sanction or judgment applying it. No source states that the head or members of the Autorité have been appointed.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law from December 2010 is in force and was reviewed at a June 2026 workshop; no use against anyone is known.

Law in force. A Nkafu brief published on 4 September 2026 says Cameroon's legal framework is anchored in a 2010 cybersecurity law and 2012 decrees, and it calls for the 2010 law to be revised. The law is law no. 2010/012 of 21 December 2010 on cybercrime, which Le Jour treats as the existing law in Cameroon. The Nkafu brief of September 2026 and the Le Jour report of June 2026 therefore point to the same 2010 text as the law the country works under, a law dating from December 2010 that the brief wants revised, sixteen years before either piece appeared.

Use of the law. The nearest held fact is a workshop reported by Le Jour on 16 June 2026. Held on 8 and 9 June 2026 in Yaounde, the workshop reviewed articles of law no. 2010/012 of 21 December 2010 on cybercrime.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

Data protection

Data protection authority: supervisory authority

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cameroon's 2024 law creates a data protection authority, but no appointment, ruling against an organisation or published figures are known.

Named in law and appointed. Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on the protection of personal data defines the Autorité as an independent public body in charge of data protection. The same law is published by the Presidency of the Republic in French, and in English as Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 relating to personal data protection in Cameroon, so the definition is the one the Presidency itself puts before readers.

Rulings and published figures. The nearest held fact is what the same law of 23 December 2024 gives the Autorité to do: supervision, notification and enforcement functions. These are powers written into the text of the law, which carries the date 23 December 2024, and they describe what the body may do under that law, in 2024 terms, as stated by the Presidency.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the authority's head or members have been appointed, and no ruling or published figures are held.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Two commune offices in the East Region had an internet connection in August 2026; how many of the 360 communes are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. On 2 June 2026 the government presented DIGI-CoR, a CFA 92 billion programme over three years to improve connectivity and equip decentralised local authorities, and was still seeking its financing from partners. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications is contracting authority for PATNUC, whose connectivity for local authorities is paid from a World Bank loan of US$100 million. In August 2026 the commune of Bertoua 1er had a budget line of its own for maintenance and the internet connection.

What the office does over it. APC's policy brief of 2023 says the pilot networks at Mayo-Oulo and Bibemi give the connected sites, the two town halls among them, internet access and offline access to the GOON platform of local content.

How the office is connected. UNDP Cameroon reported in August 2026 that Bertoua 1er budgets for the internet connection of its digitised civil status service. The town halls of Mayo-Oulo and Bibemi were each fitted with a Wi-Fi access point on a long-distance Wi-Fi link, by APC's brief of 2023.

Last twelve months. On 3 October 2025 the posts ministry sought a consultant for a 120-day needs assessment under component 2 of PATNUC. DIGI-CoR went to funders in Yaoundé on 2 June 2026.

Local governments connected. UNDP Cameroon's monitoring of six East Region sites in August 2026 states an internet connection at two commune offices: Bétaré-Oya, where it is available but unstable, and Bertoua 1er. Cameroon has 360 communes and 14 urban communities.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the structured computer networks of local authorities and the FEICOM-financed equipment. Applications: BUNEC's national civil status database, its fichier national de l'état civil, SIGEC and SANEC, its numérisation de l'état civil and the Système d'enregistrement des faits d'état civil; the digitalised civil-registration system of ten municipalities; the programme de digitalisation des communes et des régions; the digital solutions deployed in councils with ANTIC's help and the National Civil Status Registry platforms; the systèmes d'information des collectivités territoriales décentralisées; Douala's cadastre numérique and certificat de possession numérique; the Guichet Unique of the Communautés Urbaines; the digitalised administrative and financial management system of the CTD and the computerised principal civil status centres that the 2026 finance law measures; and Bibey commune's planned computerisation of financial management and payroll. Web: the council websites, the municipal portals, the Programme de digitalisation des communes et villes camerounaises, Taxi-Yaounde.com, the plateforme GOON and the site électronique de la Collectivité Territoriale the law of 24 December 2019 requires. Public access: the Télécentres Communautaires Polyvalents, CYBERVILLAGE TIC AFRICA and the Centres multimédias communaux, and the télécentre communautaire of Bibey. City systems: the Plan d'action national pour le développement des villes intelligentes and the Intelligent Urban Video Surveillance System. Backbone: the national fibre-optic backbone, its phase 4, the metropolitan loops of Yaoundé and Douala, the CAMTEL fibre and 4G/LTE networks, PATNUC's mobile broadband for 362 rural localities, the government intranet and email domain and the Data Center de Zamengoe. Capacity: the decentralisation ministry's Digital Master Plan, the digital curricula of the National School of Local Administration and the ANTIC ICT capacity-building seminar for municipal councils. Upper tier: the online complaint form of the Public Independent Conciliator of the South-West Region. Central: the CAMTEL network serving the Ministry of Secondary Education, the inter-administrative platform linking civil registration and national ID, and AIGLES, the dematerialised procedures of MINDDEVEL, the cadastre polyvalent pilot of Soa, the Cadastre National numérique, Harmony, the postal contact points connected to the internet, COLEPS, Cameroun.data.gouv.cm, the Registre social unifié du Cameroun and the management information system of the Social Safety Nets Project.

Not held. No share or total of communes connected is published; the two offices are those one region's monitoring names. Who pays for the connection at Bétaré-Oya, what staff do over the connection, and whether any commune office works in a national system held elsewhere are not stated.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national system is in use countrywide with entry at the facility (2022 ministry plan), but no figure shows how many primary clinics do so.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Public Health's digital health plan for 2026 to 2030 calls DHIS2 the ministry's national health information system, adopted in 2017, and costs work on its management plan and interoperability framework. The ministry states that under the 2020-2024 plan DHIS2 was financed through partner conventions, with no dedicated national digital health budget.

Tiers in use. The national statistics institute states that DHIS2 carries aggregate data from the monthly activity reports of all health facilities across the national territory, and used those reports for 2018 to 2022. The ministry's DHIS2 plan of April 2022 counts 197 health districts and 6,200 facilities.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's DHIS2 plan of April 2022 states that a nationwide HMIS is in place for aggregate data, with data entry at the facility level. Facilities still fill paper registers and monthly reporting booklets, which the plan budgets to reprint for 6,200 facilities.

Last twelve months. The ministry published its digital health plan for 2026 to 2030 in March 2026, with 2026 budget lines for DHIS2 deployment, maintenance, servers and interoperability.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's family health directorate gives completeness of monthly activity reports in DHIS2 as 96% nationally for October to December 2025, and timeliness as 89%, read from DHIS2 on 23 February 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2 Tracker, the mHealth software of MK WBC, the DHIS2 forms for declaring births, deaths and causes of death, the TB tracker, the HIV tracker, the malaria case management tracker, the RMNCH tracker, the immunization tracker, the disease surveillance tracker, the NTD tracker, DAMA and N-SAMBA. Single-function: the digitalised community surveillance of the Pandemic Fund project, eLMIS, DHIS 2-NACC, BLIS, VINDATA, OSP-SIDA, SIGLe and the public health events notification system. Community: the DHIS2-based CHIS and NYSS. Enabling: the national telemedicine platform.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the 96% measures reports received in DHIS2. Use is not broken down by facility level, and nothing dated since April 2022 states where data is entered.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry's patient record was installed in about fifty pilot facilities between 2020 and 2024; routine use in hospitals is not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Public Health's digital health plan for 2026 to 2030 budgets for 2026 the selection of a partner and a public-private partnership contract to deploy an electronic medical record, and names the record among projects that drew on partner financing under the 2020-2024 plan. SmartMobileClinic, launched in 2022, is run by the German association Mapubi e.V. with the Health and Research Organisation Cameroon.

Tiers in use. At Yaoundé Central Hospital, a national referral hospital, a clinical information system built on MediBoard was deployed in the gynaecology-obstetrics department and counted 113 users and 1,278 patient records after six months, on its authors' account published in October 2023. Staff stopped entering data when the per-entry payment ended.

Where data is digitised. In that department data were entered on computers in consultation and treatment rooms, with paper service registers kept in parallel. EcoMatin reported in September 2023 that in almost all public hospitals patient files are kept on paper.

Last twelve months. From September 2025 SmartMobileClinic was extended to further rural communities, with 12 rural campaigns between November 2025 and January 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry states that its electronic medical record, which it calls the DME and runs on Bahmni, was deployed in about fifty pilot health facilities during the 2020-2024 plan. The SmartMobileClinic project runs campaigns with local health facilities in 12 rural communities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2 Tracker, the mHealth software of MK WBC, the DHIS2 forms for declaring births, deaths and causes of death, the TB tracker, the HIV tracker, the malaria case management tracker, the RMNCH tracker, the immunization tracker, the disease surveillance tracker, the NTD tracker, DAMA and N-SAMBA. Single-function: the digitalised community surveillance of the Pandemic Fund project, eLMIS, DHIS 2-NACC, BLIS, VINDATA, OSP-SIDA, SIGLe and the public health events notification system. Community: the DHIS2-based CHIS and NYSS. Enabling: the national telemedicine platform.

Not held. The level of the fifty or so pilot facilities is not stated, and no count of facilities using SmartMobileClinic is given. Routine use at any hospital is not established, and no national record has been procured.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In September 2026, 60 of 432 principal civil status centres were digitised, 13.9 per cent; the share of all local offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. BUNEC, the national civil status office, works under the supervision of the decentralisation ministry. Its National Strategic Plan for the Modernisation of the Civil Status System 2025-2029 was launched on 11 September 2025. The equipment in the communes comes through UNDP projects, Legal Identity II in the East Region and a Denmark-funded one in ten municipalities.

Events registered digitally. UNDP reported in September 2025 that Maroua 3 council issues birth, marriage and death certificates, producing birth certificates on the system while the parent waits. In the East Region birth acts were being produced in the system UNDP names SIGEC by August 2026.

How the record is made. Each East Region commune receives its own server, workstations and network with the software installed, UNDP reported in August 2026. In an interview published on 8 October 2026 BUNEC's Director General describes paper as the dominant process, with digital registration in selected councils only.

Last twelve months. On 7 January 2026 Limbe I Council issued the first birth certificate from a digitalised system in the North-West and South-West regions. By August 2026 two East Region centres, Bertoua 1er and the Bertoua City Council, were in production.

Local offices doing the digital entry. On the figures BUNEC's Director General gave on 14 September 2026, 60 of the country's 432 principal civil status centres were digitised, 13.9 per cent, against 42 in April 2022. The country also has 2,439 secondary centres.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: BUNEC's retro-digitalisation of legacy paper archives, planned for all centres, the Sous-système d'archivage numérique de l'état civil (SANEC) in the East Region communes and the European Union's PROCIVIS scanning and indexing of paper registers. Notification: the health ministry's birth and death declaration forms entered in DHIS2. Outreach: the PAREC school birth certificate drive and its special mobile court hearings, BUNEC's catch-up registration exercise for Baka people at Djoum, the Civil Registration project of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie in the Far North and the mobile registration units and birth registration campaigns in remote areas. Identity: the national identity card system of the General Delegation for National Security, in its new generation from February 2025, its idcam.cm pre-enrolment platform and its digital archive of birth certificate copies. Enabling: the programme de digitalisation des communes et des régions in preparation, the Digital Master Plan for decentralised entities and the PATNUC broadband connectivity of public entities. Exchange: BUNEC's inter-administrative platform linking civil registration and the national identity card, being deployed, the interoperability framework for birth declaration between DHIS2 and BUNEC's system, the unique personal identification number planned at birth and the assessment of the Civil Registration System for integration with the Unified Social Registry.

Not held. The share of all local offices is not published: the 60 are counted against principal centres only, and the secondary centres are outside the figure. The evidence does not establish that a record made in a commune is held in the national register, and it states no national budget line.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A digital system for investigation and case information existed in 2020 and was barely used; which stations use it, if any, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. GIZ's factsheet on its support to the Cameroonian police, status June 2020, presents the digitised investigations and proceedings system as the Délégation générale à la sûreté nationale's own, and says the DGSN must also overcome unreliable internet connection, electricity and computer equipment.

Records made digitally. In June 2020 the police had a digitised system for processing and transmitting information on investigations, criminal proceedings, arrest warrants, fines and current sanctions.

How the record is made. The system processes and transmits information on investigations and criminal proceedings, on the June 2020 factsheet's account.

Last twelve months. At June 2020, the date of GIZ's factsheet on its programme with the Cameroonian police, the digitised system for investigations and criminal proceedings was barely used.

Stations doing the digital entry. GIZ put the system's slight use down to weak institutional and management capacity at the Délégation générale à la sûreté nationale in its factsheet of June 2020.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the criminal records kept on paper index cards at the courts of first instance and the Ministry of Justice, and the police judiciaire's digitised fingerprint database. Single-function: the DGSN's planned digital platform for users' complaints and its internal mechanism for complaints against police officers. Public: the 1500, as the police name their telephone number for complaints, and the mobile numbers for following a national identity card.

Not held. Which units use the system, whether any is outside the capital, where and how entries are made and how many stations enter are not held. Nothing is held since June 2020.
