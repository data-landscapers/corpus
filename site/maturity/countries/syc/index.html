Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' Data Protection Act 2023 is in force and its commission handled 14 cases in 2025; whether any ended in a decision is not known.

Authority. The Act names the Information Commission, established under the Access to Information Act, as the competent authority, with powers to investigate, audit, issue enforcement notices and impose administrative fines of up to SCR200,000. The Commission took on the mandate at the end of December 2023 and in 2025 had a Chief Information Commissioner, two Information Commissioners and a chief executive in post.

Standing. The Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 24 of 2023) was assented to on 19 December 2023 and bears the operative date 22 December 2023; it repeals the former Data Protection Act. The Commission administered it during 2025.

Regulations. The Act itself gave controllers and processors already processing data 18 months from commencement, to 22 June 2025, to conform.

Application. The Commission received four data protection cases in 2024, mostly from the non-banking financial sector, and 14 in 2025: six on data transfers, five breaches, two refused deletion requests and one consultation. It handled the 2025 cases by guidance, assessment and corrective advice.

Last twelve months. In January 2026 the Commission advertised a Senior Data Protection Analyst post. The National Assembly lists the Commission's annual report for 2025 as published on 13 April 2026. In that report the Chief Commissioner states that the Act remains at risk of being largely unenforced because the one budgeted technical post is unfilled.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No decision or sanction on a named controller and no count of cases settled is held, only cases received and handled. Whether regulations have been made under the Act is not held.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law has been in force since 2022 and was widened in December 2025; no case under it is known.

Law in force. The Cybercrimes and Other Related Crimes Act 2021 (Act 59 of 2021) was assented on 15 December 2021 and repeals the Computer Misuse Act (Cap 254). Act 23 of 2025 amends it. The National Assembly passed that amendment on 15 December 2025 and the President assented to it on 22 December 2025. It widens the offences and amends an Act already in operation, so Seychelles has a working cybercrime statute that was extended at the end of 2025.

Use of the law. The 2025 amendment, assented on 22 December 2025, adds search and interception powers to the Act alongside the wider offences. These are powers for investigating offences under it. The amendment states no commencement date of its own. Both texts are published in the official gazette. They sit on top of the 2021 Act, which replaced the earlier Computer Misuse Act, Cap 254.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' identity cards law, amended in 2023, is in force. Whether it provides for a digital identity is not known.

Law in force. Seychelles' National Identity Cards Act, Cap. 294, is a statute in force. The Miscellaneous Amendments Act 2023, assented to on 8 November 2023, amended it. The amending Act is Act 22 of 2023 and changes several laws at once, the identity cards law among them.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The 2023 amendment adds a card for non-resident citizens and new rules on disclosing card information. The first is a new section 4A of the identity cards Act. Those are the changes to the law on record, and neither is described as setting up a biometric register or an identity that is checked electronically. The Act is the country's identity law and has no replacement bill on record. The 2023 Act is the newest amendment to it.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' 2014 payment system act and 2022 e-money regulations license issuers that are not banks; no rule requiring providers to connect is known.

Law or rules in force. Seychelles' National Payment System Act, 2014 commenced on 18 August 2014. Under it the Central Bank made the Electronic Money Regulations, 2022, which commenced on 12 December 2022, and on 15 December 2025 amended its licensing and authorisation regulations.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The 2022 regulations set capital and liquidity rules for electronic money issuers other than banks, with initial capital of SCR 1,000,000, and treat issuers that are banks or credit unions as separate cases. The 2025 amendment sets the conditions on which the Central Bank may license any company incorporated under the Companies Act as a payment service provider. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other. The amended regulations exempt banks and credit unions from annual fees only.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No rule held requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch.

Law in force. The Electronic Transactions Act, 2001 (Act 8 of 2001) commenced on 20 December 2001 and gives legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures. The consolidation published by SeyLII shows the Act as amended and current to 29 March 2018. The Act is the law that governs electronic dealings in Seychelles, and it has been in force since 2001.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The consolidated text lists one further amendment as outstanding, Statutory Instrument 12 of 2024, which is not yet worked into it. It names no regulation on electronic signatures, on those who certify them or on those who sell online, and it records no accreditation, licence, registration or penalty under the Act.

Share of people who own a mobile phone, with its year. In 2024, 95 per cent of people aged 10 and over in Seychelles owned a mobile phone, an estimate by the International Telecommunication Union. In 2025, 5 GB of mobile data for a month cost 1.43 per cent of average monthly income, on international price figures.

Cost of a month of mobile data against income, with its year. In 2025, the cheapest 30-day plan of at least 5 GB from the largest operator in Seychelles cost 1.43 per cent of average monthly income, on the International Telecommunication Union's price figures. The same figures put the cost at 5.37 per cent in 2022, 3.18 per cent in 2023 and 1.35 per cent in 2024. Since 2022 the cost fell by 3.94 points.
