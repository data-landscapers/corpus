Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' Data Protection Act 2023 is in force and its commission handled 14 cases in 2025; whether any ended in a decision is not known.

Authority. The Act names the Information Commission, established under the Access to Information Act, as the competent authority, with powers to investigate, audit, issue enforcement notices and impose administrative fines of up to SCR200,000. The Commission took on the mandate at the end of December 2023 and in 2025 had a Chief Information Commissioner, two Information Commissioners and a chief executive in post.

Standing. The Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 24 of 2023) was assented to on 19 December 2023 and bears the operative date 22 December 2023; it repeals the former Data Protection Act. The Commission administered it during 2025.

Regulations. The Act itself gave controllers and processors already processing data 18 months from commencement, to 22 June 2025, to conform.

Application. The Commission received four data protection cases in 2024, mostly from the non-banking financial sector, and 14 in 2025: six on data transfers, five breaches, two refused deletion requests and one consultation. It handled the 2025 cases by guidance, assessment and corrective advice.

Last twelve months. In January 2026 the Commission advertised a Senior Data Protection Analyst post. The National Assembly lists the Commission's annual report for 2025 as published on 13 April 2026. In that report the Chief Commissioner states that the Act remains at risk of being largely unenforced because the one budgeted technical post is unfilled.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No decision or sanction on a named controller and no count of cases settled is held, only cases received and handled. Whether regulations have been made under the Act is not held.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A cybercrime law has been in force since 2022 and was widened in December 2025; no case under it is known.

Law in force. The Cybercrimes and Other Related Crimes Act 2021 (Act 59 of 2021) was assented on 15 December 2021 and repeals the Computer Misuse Act (Cap 254). Act 23 of 2025 amends it. The National Assembly passed that amendment on 15 December 2025 and the President assented to it on 22 December 2025. It widens the offences and amends an Act already in operation, so Seychelles has a working cybercrime statute that was extended at the end of 2025.

Use of the law. The 2025 amendment, assented on 22 December 2025, adds search and interception powers to the Act alongside the wider offences. These are powers for investigating offences under it. The amendment states no commencement date of its own. Both texts are published in the official gazette. They sit on top of the 2021 Act, which replaced the earlier Computer Misuse Act, Cap 254.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' identity cards law, amended in 2023, is in force. Whether it provides for a digital identity is not known.

Law in force. Seychelles' National Identity Cards Act, Cap. 294, is a statute in force. The Miscellaneous Amendments Act 2023, assented to on 8 November 2023, amended it. The amending Act is Act 22 of 2023 and changes several laws at once, the identity cards law among them.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The 2023 amendment adds a card for non-resident citizens and new rules on disclosing card information. The first is a new section 4A of the identity cards Act. Those are the changes to the law on record, and neither is described as setting up a biometric register or an identity that is checked electronically. The Act is the country's identity law and has no replacement bill on record. The 2023 Act is the newest amendment to it.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the law in force provides for a biometric register, an identity number unique to each person, or an identity that can be checked electronically.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles' 2014 payment system act and 2022 e-money regulations license issuers that are not banks; no rule requiring providers to connect is known.

Law or rules in force. Seychelles' National Payment System Act, 2014 commenced on 18 August 2014. Under it the Central Bank made the Electronic Money Regulations, 2022, which commenced on 12 December 2022, and on 15 December 2025 amended its licensing and authorisation regulations.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The 2022 regulations set capital and liquidity rules for electronic money issuers other than banks, with initial capital of SCR 1,000,000, and treat issuers that are banks or credit unions as separate cases. The 2025 amendment sets the conditions on which the Central Bank may license any company incorporated under the Companies Act as a payment service provider. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other. The amended regulations exempt banks and credit unions from annual fees only.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No rule held requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch.

Data protection authority: supervisory authority

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seychelles has an authority, active since 2023; no ruling against an organisation and no figures for its rulings are known.

Named in law and appointed. Section 5 of the Data Protection Act, 2023 names the Information Commission as the authority, with powers to investigate, audit, issue enforcement notices and impose fines up to SCR200,000. The Commission says it took on the mandate at the end of December 2023. Its 2025 annual report shows a Chief Information Commissioner, two Information Commissioners and a chief executive in post.

Rulings and published figures. The Commission reports each year on the Act: its 2024 annual report notes recruitment and staffing challenges for the new Act, and the National Assembly lists the 2025 report as published on 13 April 2026. It is advertising a Senior Data Protection Analyst post. No ruling against a named organisation is held.

Noted, not assessed. Data Protection Act, 2023.

Not held. No ruling of the authority against a named organisation in the last three years is held. What the annual reports record about enforcement is not known.

National data protection readiness: data protection system

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A data protection law is in force and the authority is working, handling 14 cases in 2025; no ruling against an organisation is known.

Law in force. The Data Protection Act, 2023 was assented to on 19 December 2023 and bears the operative date 22 December 2023. The Commission's page states the Act came into effect, and Appleby reports an 18-month transition for controllers and processors from that date.

Malabo Convention. The African Union's status list of 2 February 2026 records no signature and no ratification of the Malabo Convention by Seychelles.

Implementing and acting. The Commission handled 14 cases under the Act in 2025: 6 on data transfers, 5 breaches, 2 refused deletion requests and 1 consultation. In 2024 it received four data protection cases, mostly from the non-banking financial sector. In 2026 it marked Data Protection Day with guidance to organisations and the public.

Central: the Agency for Social Protection's Management Information System, pending at May 2025, and its Electronic Social Protection System, in development in December 2024; and the Department of Information and Communications Technology, under the Office of the Vice-President (2025).

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A ministry system that includes electronic medical records has run since the end of 2021 (reported January 2024); who uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Seychelles Nation reports the health minister saying on 17 October 2023 that the Ministry of Health is investing in an Electronic Health Information System encompassing Electronic Medical Records, the ministry's name for it. The National Health Strategic Plan 2022-2026 sets specific objective 6.8, "Implement eHIS", and names the Health Care Agency as lead entity, with the Ministry of Health and the Public Health Authority contributing.

Tiers in use. The minister said in October 2023 that the system is to be accessible to all health professionals throughout the health system.

Where data is digitised. At its digital health visioning meeting of 22 January 2024 the ministry said it was trying to link the system with laboratories and X-ray so that doctors get results on their computers.

Last twelve months. A ministry press release carried by Seychelles Nation in January 2024 states that the system has been operational since the end of 2021.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The 2022-2026 plan sets three go-live phases, the first being client registration and appointments across all relevant units.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the Ministry of Health's disease surveillance system, its laboratory digital platforms, its imaging department digital platforms and its stock management digital platform.

Not held. The tiers using the system, where the record is entered and the primary clinics using it are not established. Finance is not stated, and nothing dated inside the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A computerised record of vital events was kept beside the manual register in 2018; where it is entered is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Civil Status Act as in force on 1 November 2022 makes the Chief Officer of Civil Status, appointed by the President and answering to the Minister responsible for civil status, keeper of the registers of births and deaths.

Events registered digitally. A contributed article in Seychelles Nation of 10 August 2018 states that the Civil Status records were computerised in 1989, and that a computerised record of vital events is kept beside the manual register and updated daily from the occurrences the Civil Status registers.

How the record is made. The Act as in force on 1 November 2022 prescribes the registers as numbered pages kept in duplicate, and its section 107A lets the Chief Officer accept statements, forms and signatures through an electronic medium.

Last twelve months. The Civil Status (Amendment) Act 2021 was assented to on 4 November 2021, to commence on a date the Minister appoints by Gazette notice.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Act as in force on 1 November 2022 provides a Central Civil Status Office in Victoria and offices at Praslin, La Digue and Anse Royale.

Noted, not assessed. Electronic storage registers under section 18A; notification of birth and of death by medical practitioners, nurses and midwives; CertExpress; SeyID, with its ID-Wallet, Minor ID and eKYC; the National Identity Card; the National Population Database and the National Identity Number; the digital ID for permit holders; the monthly list of deceased sent to the Electoral Commission; the communication of civil status information to the Chief Registration Officer; the verification of eKYC against the Civil Status System; and the submission of vital records to the National Bureau of Statistics.

Not held. Where the computerised record is entered, at the offices at Praslin, La Digue and Anse Royale or centrally, and how many local offices enter it. Nothing dated after 1 November 2022 on governance or on change in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Police stations are all connected to a national case system, by a 2025 vendor account; how many enter their own records is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The vendor Winsoft, in an account published on 6 January 2025, says the Seychelles Police Department wanted the case app and brought in Transmedia, a British Claris partner, to build it, and that the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime funded and supported the project.

Records made digitally. The app manages cases from start to finish and is the repository of every case recorded, by Winsoft's account of January 2025. Development began with the intake form for incident reports, the record of a single crime; digitising the Occurrence Book itself, the daily record of incidents, was listed as an enhancement to come.

How the record is made. The app runs on desktop computers or iPads as a central resource holding every case, and officers search crimes from other districts (January 2025).

Last twelve months. Winsoft's account of 6 January 2025 says the department has continued to evolve its use of the app since deployment.

Stations doing the digital entry. The detective commander of Mont Fleuri station is quoted by Winsoft, in January 2025, saying all police stations are connected. The same account says the database is bedding in at headquarters in Victoria.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: an eFines system for road traffic offences, in development and an eight-week pilot of video enforcement of traffic offences in Roche Caiman. Surveillance and enabling: digital dispatch consoles and radio communication equipment handed to the police on 8 May 2026. Enabling: renovation of the Central Police Station and new stations on Praslin and Ile Perseverance, in the 2023 budget.

Not held. The number of police stations and the share of them entering their own records: the one figure is a station commander's statement that all are connected, with no year. No plan is stated, and nothing is dated in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.
