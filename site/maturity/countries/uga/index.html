Strategies, plans and policies

Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Uganda published its Vision for a Digital Uganda 2040 in 2022 with a costed plan; any report on progress is not known.

Governance and finance. The Vision for a Digital Uganda 2040 names a Digital Transformation Program Working Group of heads of ministries, departments and agencies to coordinate delivery, with the Ministry of ICT and National Guidance leading, and lists budget appropriations, concessional loans and grants as financing measures.

Standing. The Ministry published the Vision in September 2022 with a foreword by President Museveni; a newer Digital Transformation Roadmap was launched by the Government on 17 August 2023.

Text. The Vision is published on the Ministry's site.

Period. The Vision sets a 2040 horizon with targets for 2025, 2030 and 2040, and the Roadmap covers 2023/24 to 2027/28.

Delivery plan. The Vision carries a cost implementation matrix putting first-phase financing to 2030 at UGX 6.08 trillion, with a results framework of 2030 targets.

Reporting. The Vision has the working group prepare quarterly, semi-annual and annual implementation reports and run annual performance reviews.

Last twelve months. The Vision sets a mid-term evaluation in FY2028/29 and a final evaluation in FY2039/40.

Noted, not assessed. Uganda Vision 2040; Fourth National Development Plan (NDP IV); Government Systems Prototype Showcase; Digital Government Strategy; E-Government Masterplan; National eGovernment Policy Framework 2011; e-Government Strategy; National Development Plan III (NDPIII).

Not held. No report against the Vision is held, and its governance rows date from September 2022. The Digital Transformation Roadmap has no adopting act or public text on record.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act is in force and was applied in decisions of 2025 and 2026; yearly decision counts are not known.

Authority. The Personal Data Protection Office is established under the Data Protection and Privacy Act and headed by a director, Baker Birikujja. It keeps a register of data collectors, controllers and processors.

Standing. The Data Protection and Privacy Act, Cap 97, is in force. The office's director said in August 2026 that it requires data collectors and processors to register and makes failure to register a criminal offence. The office has held that the Act applies to foreign entities serving persons in Uganda.

Regulations. The office's decision of 20 February 2026 was made under the Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021. Its decisions apply Regulation 15 on registration, Regulation 30 on cross-border transfers, Regulation 46 on appeal and Regulation 48 on offences.

Application. On 18 July 2025 the office found Google LLC in breach of the Act's registration and cross-border transfer duties and ordered it to register within 30 days. On 20 February 2026 it partly upheld a complaint and issued five corrective orders to WhatsApp LLC, with no fine.

Last twelve months. On 3 December 2025 a magistrate fined the finance manager of Vuka Africa Ltd sh100,000 for failing to register with the office. By letter of 17 July 2026 the office confirmed that WhatsApp LLC had implemented the corrective measures. In August 2026 its director said 14,697 of an estimated 50,000 entities required to register had done so.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No count of decisions, sanctions or complaints settled for a stated period. The year the Act was passed and the date it commenced are not given.

Use of the law. In September 2026 Kampala Metropolitan Police arrested and held three men over alleged fake news and unlawful obtaining or disclosure of personal data on social media, in an investigation the report places under the Computer Misuse Act. The incidents date to July 2026, and charges were not disclosed.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2022, 60.6 per cent of districts, municipalities and town councils answering a survey had office internet; the share of all is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. NITA-U, the state's IT authority, was the internet provider of 70.1 per cent of connected local governments in its 2022 survey. The Uganda Communications Commission reports that the fourth National Development Plan, for 2025/26 to 2029/30, sets a target of extending high-speed internet to local government offices. On 6 December 2023 Parliament authorised borrowing of up to RMB 1,050 million from the Export-Import Bank of China for Phase V of the national backbone.

What the office does over it. A gender ministry study states that as of July 2022 all local governments had been migrated to the Integrated Financial Management System. NITA-U's 2022 survey found that local governments used the internet mostly for email, 90.9 per cent, and for services to the public, 74 per cent.

How the office is connected. In NITA-U's 2022 survey local governments gave staff internet over institution wireless networks and institution-owned USB modems.

Last twelve months. On 22 September 2026 the Ministry of Local Government launched its web-based accounting tool, which it calls SCART, in Hoima city's two divisions and supplied a laptop and a router for it. On 1 December 2025 the President launched the backbone's extension in Kanungu District, to connect the district headquarters and sub-counties.

Local governments connected. NITA-U's National IT Survey, of January to April 2022, found that 60.6 per cent of the local government administrations that answered it had institutional internet access; 95 of 128 sampled districts gave full data, with 17 municipalities and 10 town councils.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Every level reported into the national system in 2023/24, but in 2026 a mid-level health centre carried its report to the district office.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Division of Health Information manages DHIS2, which the ministry rolled out as the national reporting system to 112 districts in 2011, according to a study published in October 2025. The National Health Compact 2025-2030, adopted in December 2025, commits to scale DHIS2 up to all facilities, with 100% of hospitals and 80% of Health Centre IVs and IIIs reporting in real time by 2028.

Tiers in use. The ministry coded 6,546 health facilities in DHIS2 in FY 2023/24 and publishes DHIS2 figures by level, from national referral hospitals to Health Centre IIs and clinics. A census of all 265 government Health Centre IVs and hospitals, published in October 2025, found DHIS2 in use in 98.8% of them.

Where data is digitised. At a launch on 11 August 2026 the Uganda Muslim Medical Bureau said many Health Centre IIIs lack computers and connectivity to submit reports, and Ziiru Health Centre III carries its report to Buvuma district headquarters. In Health Centre IVs and hospitals, paper registers capture patient data, which is later aggregated and entered into DHIS2, on the October 2025 study's account.

Last twelve months. Makerere University's METS programme reported in April 2026 that reporting through DHIS2 rose from 58 percent in 2020 to 98 percent in 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry states that decentralising data entry to facilities continued in FY 2023/24.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: HIV case-based surveillance, TB eCBSS, DREAMS Tracker, PREV Tracker, the EID and viral load dashboards, Uganda EMR, the DHIS2 case-based analytics for TB, immunization and COVID-19 vaccination certification and the TB and leprosy case-based surveillance system. Single-function: eIDSR, the SMS-based COVID-19 death reporting platform, 6767, the Weekly Stock Status System, PIRS, ALIS, LabExpert, the Integrated Outbreak Management System, NMS+, its client self-service portal, JMS ERP, mTrac, Rx Solution, the Viral Load Information System and eLMIS. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS), the HMIS 097B report of Village Health Teams and the VHT/ICCM quarterly report. Enabling: the E-Ward Round Platform, the Faith Medical Bureau-UCC Digital Skilling Project, the METS ICT infrastructure and the planned national telehealth and telementoring hub.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and facility entry is established only for Health Centre IVs and hospitals. How DHIS2 is financed is not stated, and the 58 to 98 percent reporting rate has no stated measure or denominator.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry's electronic record ran in referral and general hospitals and seven health centres in 2025; records are not shown to be shared.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's guidelines of 18 September 2024 name the ministry as owner of the Electronic Medical Records System. The US-Uganda memorandum signed on 10 December 2025 names eAfya and ClinicMaster as the Government of Uganda's electronic medical records and sets a functional EMR in all facilities down to Health Centre III by 2028. Under it the United States plans US$113,102,289 for data systems over 2026-2030.

Tiers in use. The ministry says that as of 2025 the EMR is in referral and general hospitals and in some health centres. In Kiryandongo district it was in use in September 2026 at the general hospital, Nyakadoti Health Centre III and Mpumwe and Apodorwa Health Centre IIs.

Where data is digitised. The in-charge of Nyakadoti Health Centre III said on 19 September 2026 that patient data must be captured in the EMR at every stage of the visit. A census of all 265 government Health Centre IVs and hospitals, published in October 2025, found 94.3% keeping both paper and digital patient records.

Last twelve months. On 31 March 2026 Makerere University's METS programme handed the UgandaEMR repositories to the Ministry of Health. Health workers told a baraza on 19 September 2026 that the EMR was slowing care in Kiryandongo facilities because of poor connectivity, unstable power and too few computers.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry counts 5 Health Centre IVs and 2 Health Centre IIIs using the EMR as of 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: HIV case-based surveillance, TB eCBSS, DREAMS Tracker, PREV Tracker, the EID and viral load dashboards, Uganda EMR, the DHIS2 case-based analytics for TB, immunization and COVID-19 vaccination certification and the TB and leprosy case-based surveillance system. Single-function: eIDSR, the SMS-based COVID-19 death reporting platform, 6767, the Weekly Stock Status System, PIRS, ALIS, LabExpert, the Integrated Outbreak Management System, NMS+, its client self-service portal, JMS ERP, mTrac, Rx Solution, the Viral Load Information System and eLMIS. Community: the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS), the HMIS 097B report of Village Health Teams and the VHT/ICCM quarterly report. Enabling: the E-Ward Round Platform, the Faith Medical Bureau-UCC Digital Skilling Project, the METS ICT infrastructure and the planned national telehealth and telementoring hub.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2025, 58.9% of all primary schools uploaded pupils' enrolment details to the national register; whether any enter attendance or results is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Guidelines of 8 June 2026 make the Ministry of Education and Sports primary custodian of all information entered into the system. An Auditor General's report, reported on 9 June 2026, finds that its administration and configuration remain largely with the vendor, SMSOne, under the ICT ministry's supervision.

Levels in use. For the 2025 academic year 7,408 pre-primary, 26,597 primary and 4,571 secondary schools responded, the ministry reports.

How the record is kept. The system collects data on each learner, issues a Learner Identification Number and tracks transfers, with 11,787,396 primary learners registered at December 2025. School heads register the learners.

Last twelve months. In January 2026 the ministry published its first annual statistical abstract since 2017, compiled from the system for the 2025 academic year. It launched the new guidelines on 8 June 2026, with sanctions for institutions that fail their data obligations, and the contractor was reported on 9 June 2026 to be upgrading the platform to a second version.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The ministry reports that 26,597 of the country's 45,164 primary schools (58.9%) responded to the data upload for 2025: 12,511 of 12,544 government schools and 14,086 of 32,620 private schools.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: TELA, the examinations board's results portal and the module for registering and licensing schools.

Not held. No source describes schools entering attendance or results: what is held is the registration of learners, transfers and a yearly upload. No plan, source of finance or share for rural schools is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter births and deaths into the central register, in most districts in 2020; how many offices do so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Identification and Registration Authority (NIRA), under the Minister of Internal Affairs, holds all of Uganda's civil registration systems, it said on 18 July 2026. Its Strategic Plan III for 2025/26 to 2029/30 was finalised in 2025/26, and its 2026/27 budget estimate of UGX 155.948 billion is all Government of Uganda money.

Events registered digitally. NIRA's system, which it calls the Mobile Vital Records System, registered 498,439 live births and 22,182 deaths in 2024, on the statistics bureau's figures. On 18 July 2026 NIRA said it had built a births and deaths module on its identity system that issues an identity number once a birth is notified.

How the record is made. A paper notification form is entered into the web-based system at district offices and health facilities, and each entry is recorded directly in the central register, on a 2020 brief.

Last twelve months. In 2025/26 to 31 December 2025 NIRA registered 123,526 births and 9,935 deaths.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The system was used in at least 112 of 135 districts and in 222 of the 5,230 health facilities that handle births, an IDRC brief said in 2020.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the National Marriage Registration System's digitising of marriage records; the collection and digitisation of legacy civil registration records. Notification: the birth and death notifications of hospitals and DHIS2; the community death notification workflow of eCHIS, the SMS platform 6767 and the paper HMIS death forms; the HMIS e-register, the eAFYA medical records system and the digital mortuary registers; the electronic Medical Certificate of Cause of Death and the digital registers for birth and death notification; the rapid mortality surveillance system; the Mobile Vital Record System of 2013; the Ministry of Health registry on causes of death and births. Certificate: the Civil Registration Information Management System. Outreach: the birth registration campaign and community registration exercises; the instant birth registrations at referral hospitals; the on-site birth registration teams at health facilities; the mobile civil registration vehicles; the mobile registration units; the registration drives in refugee-hosting communities. Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Digital case files were piloted in ten Kampala divisions in 2017, with upcountry stations outside in 2021; use since is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Uganda Police Force's ICT Directorate developed the Crime Records Management System itself, and the force resolved in October 2022 to embark on the Electronic Policing Information System, ePIS. A UNODC consultancy notice of 2023 gives the ePIS programme four interventions: business process reform, automation of case management, enabling infrastructure and digital skilling.

Records made digitally. The case file. The force said in December 2017 that its crime records system processes and tracks the progress of case files. A journal article of June 2024 says ePIS is to manage all cases registered and investigated by the police from inception to conclusion.

How the record is made. The UNODC notice of 2023 calls the force's collection, processing and sharing of data largely manual. The Independent reported in 2021 that stations had one records officer for case files and detectives kept the files of their own cases.

Last twelve months. In June 2024 ePIS was in its design phase.

Stations doing the digital entry. In December 2017 the crime records system was being piloted in ten divisions of Kampala Metropolitan. The Independent reported in 2021 that it had been upgraded and tested in Kampala, with tests in upcountry stations to follow. The justice sector's bulletin of May 2024 lists lack of internet connectivity at upcountry police stations among the obstacles.

Noted, not assessed. Surveillance: the Kampala Safe City CCTV network and the National CCTV project. Enabling: computers for all 186 district police stations, radio communication in 150 of the 186 police districts and divisions, and solar power at 20 facilities, at half year 2025/26, and the National Backbone Infrastructure's last-mile connection of police posts in Kanungu. Single-function: the Certificate of Good Conduct system and the Road Crash Database System. Personnel: the Human Resource Management System. Public: the UPF chatbot, announced in September 2026, the 999 and 112 emergency and toll-free lines, the UPF Mobi app and call response centres at 20 sub-county police stations. Identification: the Integrated Ballistic Identification System, the Criminal Automated Biometric Identification System and the Electronic Criminal Records Office. Statistics: the Crime Data Analysis Centre.
