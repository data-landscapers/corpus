Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The government network connected all 274 planned sites of metropolitan, municipal and district assemblies in 2024; how many of the 261 assemblies is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Communications and Digitalisation tendered the government network it calls eGovNet in September 2021, awarded it to AirtelTigo and Ascend Digital Solutions in September 2022 and signed a 10-year contract in December 2022. On 3 September 2026 the Head of the Local Government Service presented a programme his office calls LITISIP to give the 261 metropolitan, municipal and district assemblies a secure digital backbone.

What the office does over it. The minister said in February 2024 that the network gives the connected agencies, the assemblies among them, faster communication and access to government shared services. The Ministry of Finance's budget guidelines of 29 August 2025 carry instructions to the assemblies with a part on GIFMIS, the national financial management system.

How the office is connected. The minister said in February 2024 that the network connects government agencies, the assemblies included, by fibre backbone, last-mile fibre and microwave links with satellite backup, on a 10-year indefeasible right of use. At a signing reported on 3 September 2026 the Head of the Local Government Service described the state the Service is moving from as "disconnected offices".

Last twelve months. The Local Government Service signed a memorandum of understanding with Celltel Networks, reported on 3 September 2026, with improved connectivity of local government offices among its expected results.

Local governments connected. The minister's table of February 2024 gives the assemblies 274 of 274 planned sites connected, within 950 of 951 government sites.

Noted, not assessed. Revenue systems: dLRev, the Local Government Service's revenue software, in 35 municipal assemblies, with training on it for about 150 officers in July 2026; Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly's Digital Revenue Mobilisation Strategy; Ga West Municipal Assembly's digital revenue mobilisation system; Ekumfi District Assembly's database of businesses; a centrally monitored revenue system for the assemblies pledged in August 2026, and the street naming and property addressing system; and a GIS-based revenue management system supported by German cooperation. Other applications: GIFMIS; the procurement system GHANEPS; the District Development Data Platform; the planning system LUPMIS and the Ghana National Spatial Planning and Land Use Database; the Births and Deaths Registry's IT system; the payments platform Ghana.gov; and the Flood Intelligence Agent in development at BigData Ghana. Web: the Tech for Inclusive Governance platform at South Tongu District Assembly; CitizenApp, the Accra Metropolitan Assembly's shortcode payment system and an open data portal in Accra; the discontinued e-Services platform; and the Ghana Open Data Initiative portal. Public access: a technology hub for local government services planned by the Local Government Service and Community ICT Centres. City systems: CCTV surveillance, traffic monitoring and a smart waste collection pilot in Accra and the Accra Metropolitan Area IT-enhanced traffic management project. Backbone: GovNet and the Eastern Corridor Fibre Optic Backbone; the e-Government network managed by NITA and SmartInfraco; the National Data Centre and private government cloud; and the Ghana Government Enterprise Architecture and eGovernment Interoperability Framework. Capacity: the ICT Enabled Distance Learning Project for the Institute of Local Government Studies. Central: the local government ministry's management information systems and databank; the Air Quality Data Hub; the Ghana Card Number at Birth System and the Births and Deaths Registry's digital registration platform, its SRBD platform and its electronic database of registered deaths; the health systems DHIMS 2 and the RMNCAH platform; the Ghana Case Tracking System; the West African Police Information System; and the complaint and redress system of the Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice, the Citizens' Complaints Centre and the beneficial ownership register.

Not held. How many of the 261 assemblies the 274 connected sites cover, and how many assembly offices are without a connection in 2026. What LITISIP costs, who pays for it and when it is due, and which systems assembly staff work in over the network.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Some assemblies record payers and bills in a shared national system, nine of 260 using it in 2019; how many do now is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Office of the Head of the Local Government Service, a state body, runs the training on the revenue software it calls dLRev, under a programme the World Bank supports. GIZ funded the application in 2019. Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly's own system is a company's software as a service, as audited at 31 December 2024.

What is recorded. dLRev holds a register of businesses and properties with their owners, and manages the billing and collection of property rates and business operating permits, as described in 2019. Korle Klottey's system held a property database, payer accounts and bills at 31 December 2024.

Where the record is held. Since 2017 dLRev has been hosted on the national platform of the National IT Agency, with no standalone operation in a district.

Last twelve months. A training of 350 staff of 35 Municipal Assemblies on the software began in February 2026, and about 150 officers in six regions were trained in July 2026. Ga West Municipal Assembly introduced a digital revenue system in one zonal council, with data capture set for April 2026.

Local governments recording. Nine of Ghana's 260 assemblies, 3.5 per cent, used dLRev throughout 2019, by a paper GIZ commissioned. In February 2020 47 of GIZ's 90 partner districts had properties registered in it. The Ministry of Local Government reports that in 2021 it rolled the software out in 114 of the 260.

Noted, not assessed. Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly's Digital Revenue Mobilisation Strategy, under which properties and businesses were mapped; the Ministry of Local Government's Permit Processing System.

Not held. No figure for assemblies recording in dLRev is dated after February 2020: the 2021 figure is a roll-out and the 2026 figures are staff trained. No current plan for the software is held, and nothing on its hosting is dated after 2017.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter clinics' paper reports into the national system, and some community health posts enter their own (2024); how many is not published.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ghana Health Service's Policy, Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Division owns DHIMS2, the service's District Health Information Management System, which runs on a national scale, on the mapping GIZ published with the service in December 2023. The Ministry of Health's Health Information System Strategic Plan 2022-2025, of June 2022, budgets GHS 31,354,750, of which GHS 28,219,275 is from development partners. The service's Policy and Strategy on Digital Health 2023-2027 lists DHIMS II first among its digital health solutions.

Tiers in use. Community health officers in 86 CHPS zones across six Northern Region districts reported monthly into DHIMS 2 at CHPS level, in a PLOS One survey of March 2024.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's plan of June 2022 states that most facilities validate and enter their data into DHIMS2 themselves, while those without computers or internet submit manually to the sub-district for entry. CHPS staff in Karaga, Mion and Sagnarigu said in March 2024 that their compounds lacked login details and computers, and that paper reports went to the sub-district or the district office for upload.

Last twelve months. The donor-funded projects that paid for the servers hosting DHIMS2 had ended by December 2023, leaving the annual server costs unfunded.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. A WHO-led study found no electronic data collection in any of the 16 public primary facilities it visited in four districts of Ghana in 2016-2017.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: E-Tracker and the rsLog application. Single-function: the Ghana Integrated Logistics Management Information System (GhiLMIS), the Surveillance Outbreak Response Management and Analysis System (SORMAS) and the KoboCollect interface for nutrition data.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the one figure on clinics is from 16 facilities in 2016-2017. No plan dated within the last three years is held, and no dated event on DHIMS2 in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national hospital record system's contract lapsed in December 2024 and a replacement was ordered in October 2025; its reach is not yet known.

Governance, planning, finance. By a directive of 28 October 2025 the Ministry of Health named its Chief Director to lead the national rollout of the Ghana Health Information Management System (GHIMS), with Axon Information Systems implementing it under government oversight. The ministry's Health Sector Medium-Term Development Plan 2026-2029, of October 2025, carries a national indicator for the percentage of health facilities with functional EMR systems in use.

Tiers in use. The Vice President said on 31 October 2024 that the Lightwave system LHIMS was deployed in all six teaching hospitals, 10 regional hospitals and 243 district hospitals. Its vendor's contract expired on 31 December 2024.

Where data is digitised. OneHealthEHR Inc. said in September 2026 that on its platform, in use in Liberia and Ghana, clinicians record patient information during care and synchronise it when a connection returns.

Last twelve months. The directive of 28 October 2025 ordered GHIMS rolled out nationwide in November 2025 as the only authorised platform for patient registration and clinical documentation, and cited nationwide disruptions to the existing clinical information and billing systems. OneHealthEHR says its platform went into live clinical use in Liberia and Ghana on 1 September 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's plan of October 2025 gives 15% as the first value against its indicator for the percentage of all health facilities with functional EMR systems in use. OneHealthEHR reported seven active clinics and health centres across Liberia and Ghana together by late September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: E-Tracker and the rsLog application. Single-function: the Ghana Integrated Logistics Management Information System (GhiLMIS), the Surveillance Outbreak Response Management and Analysis System (SORMAS) and the KoboCollect interface for nutrition data.

Not held. Which tiers and how many facilities use GHIMS is not established, nor whether LHIMS was shut down outright or disrupted in some hospitals. The 15% has no numerator, denominator or baseline year, GHIMS's financing is not stated, and the OneHealthEHR figures give no count for Ghana.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In November 2025, 733 primary schools reported each pupil's literacy and numeracy results on a digital platform; their share of primary schools is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Schools reporting on what the World Bank calls the DL+ Platform is a condition carrying US$25 million under a project financing that is grant-financed by GPE, SCALE funders and the Early Learning Partnership and had disbursed nothing by November 2025.

Levels in use. The programme whose results schools report on the platform is for lower and upper primary, the World Bank said in November 2025. Senior high schools upload student records to the examinations council's Student Transcript Portal, live since 2024.

How the record is kept. Primary schools assess and report literacy and numeracy outcomes for individual learners on the platform. Secondary schools have uploaded over 60 million academic records since 2024, at a 97.2% national average completion rate, with completion reviewed monthly by regional and national committees.

Last twelve months. The count of schools reporting on the platform stood at 733 in August 2024, in June 2025 and in November 2025. The World Bank says the training of teachers to start reporting was delayed by the finance ministry's disbursement cap, in force from January 2025.

Primary schools doing the digital input. By the World Bank's count, 733 schools were reporting on the platform at 27 November 2025, against a target of 16,000 by December 2028.

Noted, not assessed. The ministry's yearly school census, which Ghana calls its Education Management Information System (EMIS); the CloudSys senior high school placement system and the one before it; the National Standardized Tests; weekly teacher attendance data; the Mobile School Report Card and the paper School Report Card; the Edmodo learning management system; and One-Teacher-One-Laptop.

Not held. No total of primary schools is given beside the 733, so their share of all primary schools is not known. Nor is it stated how often a school reports, who owns the platform or whether a current plan names it.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births were registered digitally at two Accra registry centres in 2024; how many offices do so, and how the record is made, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Births and Deaths Registry, under the Ministry of Local Government, develops and administers the births and deaths registration system under Act 1027 of 2020. A Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Strategic Plan for 2025 to 2030 was validated at a workshop in July 2025. The 2025 budget gives births and deaths registration GHS 7,409,170, of which GHS 6,909,170 is internally generated funds and nothing is from donors.

Events registered digitally. Births in Ablekuma West, a municipal district of Greater Accra, are registered on the Registry's digital registration platform, 1,722 of them from March to October 2024. The Registry's electronic database is the source of its count of 50,992 deaths registered in 2022.

How the record is made. In 2022 a district registration officer entered a death in the register and completed a paper form, which was scanned into an electronic database at the national office.

Last twelve months. In a 2026 report the Eastern Regional Registrar said officers are expected to complete birth registrations digitally in communities and that poor internet connectivity is a major obstacle.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The two registry centres of Ablekuma West, Dansoman and Gbegbeyise, registered 1,045 and 677 births on the digital platform from March to October 2024. In April 2025 the Acting Registrar said the Registry was taking steps to digitise all offices.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the Ghana Card, the National Identity Register, the Identity Verification System, the Ghana Card PIN as taxpayer identifier, the registration system for children aged 0 to 5, the Foreigner Identification Management System and Smile ID. Notification: the DHIS e-tracker and Lightwave, and health facilities' sharing of birth data and medical certificates of cause of death with the Registry. Certificates: a digital system issuing certified copies at regional offices, the e-Services platform and a one-stop portal for e-services. Outreach: mobile registration exercises under the EARLY action, automated systems used by field officers and district registrars' community visits. Enabling: the Ghana Rural Telephony and Digital Inclusion Project. Exchange: the Ghana Card Number at Birth System and the integration of data from LHIMS, eTracker, the Registry and the National Identification Authority.

Not held. No national count or share of local offices entering births digitally is held, and no source states how a birth record is made or whether it is entered at the office. No dated event falls in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Case dockets were held digitally in 55 police stations across seven regions in 2022; the total of stations, and anything since, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Government of Ghana launched what it calls the Case Tracking System in 2018, with the support of USAID, as a common platform for the Police Service, the Attorney General's office, the Prisons Service, the Legal Aid Commission, the Economic and Organised Crime Office and the Judicial Service.

Records made digitally. The Asokwa Municipal Police Commander said in May 2022 that the system had eliminated the use of paper as case dockets in the prosecution of criminal cases. The Legal Resources Centre reported in 2022 that 2,475 cases had been registered on it from December 2021.

How the record is made. The commander said he could monitor cases in all stations within his jurisdiction from his office through the system, with unstable internet and electricity hindering it. In the Volta Region, 104 laptops, 95 tablets and 93 connectivity units were given out to run it, by the centre's 2022 report.

Last twelve months. On 23 May 2022 the project was being supported by USAID, whose Justice Sector Support Activity also paid for an awareness campaign.

Stations doing the digital entry. The Legal Resources Centre reported in 2022 that 55 police stations and 3 prisons services were using the system, in seven regions and over 40 districts: Greater Accra, Ashanti, Northern, Volta, Bono, Western and Eastern. In July 2019 the West African Police Information System had more than 35 units in the capital, shared among police, immigration and prisons, and none in the regions.

Noted, not assessed. The police's suicide mortality data, compiled into district and national crime statistics; the automated fingerprint identification system, the Criminal Records Database System and the Police Gazette of the Criminal Data Service Bureau; the National Crime Automated Biometric Identification System; the Cybercrime Unit's digital forensic hardware and software, and the Cyber Security Authority's 24-hour reporting line; forensic laboratories, a Real-Time Crime Centre and a crime-reporting mobile app; the traffic enforcement system the police call Traffitech-GH; CCTV surveillance in Accra; CCTV in crime-prone areas and online surveillance with the regulator and internet providers; and the eGovNet network, the online fingerprint check and Ghana.gov.

Not held. No total of stations to set beside the 55, and nothing on the system since 2022. Whether the first report of an occurrence is recorded digitally, and whether rural stations use the system.
