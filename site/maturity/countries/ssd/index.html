Data protection legislation: general law

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Sudan has no data protection law; a 2025 bill was being reviewed in May 2026, and whether it has reached parliament is not known.

Authority. The draft bill, when passed, will establish a Data Protection Commission to safeguard the personal data of online users, Radio Tamazuj reported on 20 November 2025.

Standing. South Sudan has no data protection law: the World Bank's identity diagnostic, published on 1 May 2026, states that the country has no national data protection or privacy law. A bill is being drafted. Digital security experts finalised what Radio Tamazuj calls the first Data Protection Policy Bill in October 2025, with the National Communication Authority and civil society, and a post dated 5 May 2026 reports a two-day workshop of over 30 stakeholders to review and validate the Data Protection Bill, 2025 (Version 1.0).

Regulations. Koneta's Executive Director said in a release dated 17 October 2025 that existing ICT regulations are limited.

Application. The World Bank diagnostic states that South Sudan has no national data protection and privacy regime.

Last twelve months. Paradigm Initiative and Koneta Hub convened lawmakers, civil society organisations and officials in Juba to review the draft bill and seek a parliamentary commitment to table it, in a release dated 17 October 2025. The ICT minister, Ateny Wek Ateny, said in December 2025 that the government would introduce the bill in 2026, drafted jointly by the ministries of information and justice. The validation workshop was reported on 5 May 2026, with members of parliament, civil society, the UNMISS legal unit and the National Communication Authority taking part.

Noted, not assessed. Article 22 of the Transitional Constitution of 2011; the National Civil Registry and Identity Regulations of 2024.

Not held. Any bill tabled in parliament or any law enacted. No regulation and no decision on a controller is held, and what has happened to the bill since May 2026 is not held.

Under Presidential Directive No. 03 of 2026 the National Cyber-Security Task Force held its first operational meeting on 16 July 2026. In September 2026 more than 20 civil society, security and media participants were trained in Yei River County on the Act, which the report treats as current law.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

South Sudan's 2018 Civil Registry Act and 2024 regulations provide for a personal identification number; the World Bank calls them under-implemented.

Law in force. South Sudan's Civil Registry Act of 2018, with the Nationality Act of 2011, is the legal foundation of its identification system, a World Bank diagnostic of May 2026 says. The Bank says the Act and regulations made under it in 2024 have established the legal foundation for the country's identity system, and calls the framework under-implemented.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The Civil Registration and National Identity Regulations, 2024, were made under sections 33 and 39 of the Act and regulate the issue of legal documents. The Bank says the Act and the regulations lay the foundation for an interoperable digital identity system based on a personal identification number. Both are in force on paper, by the Bank's account.

Noted, not assessed. Nationality Act 2011; National ID & Passport System; DCRNPI Civil Registration System (planned); DHIS2 CRVS Module; National Identity Card (NIC) and Personal Identification Number (PIN).

Not held. Nothing on the four questions.

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Governance, planning, finance. Maban County has two satellite terminals, one for the Office of the Commissioner and one for the Health Department, on an assessment of eleven local governments that the International Organization for Migration (IOM) carried out from September to December 2021.

Last twelve months. IOM's assessment of the eleven locations dates from September to December 2021.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: county governments' computers and printers, which five of the eleven locations assessed in 2021 did not have. Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry says 30% of all health facilities entered their own reports into the national system in 2025, without giving the numbers behind it.

Governance, planning, finance. In the ministry's report for 2024-25 the Minister of Health calls DHIS2 the ministry's official national health information platform. The Health Sector Strategic Plan 2023-2027 sets targets of 55% of health facilities reporting directly through DHIS2 by 2025 and 90% by 2027. The World Bank's mission of November 2025 records HMIS strengthening as financed by IDA grants and a multi-donor trust fund and implemented by WHO.

Tiers in use. WHO states that monthly reporting vouchers for hospitals, primary health care centres and primary health care units were configured in DHIS2 in the third quarter of 2025. The ministry finds for 2024-25 that many hospitals submit DHIS2 reports regularly.

Where data is digitised. The ministry states for 2024-25 that hospitals and primary health care centres often lack the computers, stable internet and trained staff to enter data directly into DHIS2, so entry is frequently centralised or delayed. Its assessment with WHO of February 2025 finds DHIS2 implemented at national and sub-national or district levels.

Last twelve months. The World Bank's mission of November 2025 records that facilities in DHIS2 were verified with State Ministries of Health and closed or non-existent ones removed. A pilot of the FLLOW-M malaria and climate dashboard inside the national DHIS2 instance was launched in July 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry reports that 30% of all health facilities, those equipped with tablets and computers, reported directly through DHIS2 in 2025, against the plan's target of 55%.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the ART E-register and the COVID-19 DHIS2 Tracker. Single-function: DHIS2 disease surveillance, DHIS2 for ITN campaign planning and management, the FLLOW-M dashboard, the mSupply eLMIS, EWARS, PLMIS, the HERMES platform, eSurv and the Companion App, Open Data Kit real-time supportive supervision, Epidemic Intelligence from Open Sources, the national electronic Laboratory Information Management System, the Viral Load Management System, NIS for nutrition, the IDSR reporting system, the ODK mobile data collection tool, the AFP and Environmental Surveillance dashboard and a pharmaceutical system. Community: the Boma Health Initiative Digitalisation Project's DHIS2 Tracker program and the Boma Health Initiative reporting form in DHIS2.

Not held. The 30% carries no numerator or denominator, and the tier of the facilities entering their own reports is not stated. The level at which centralised entry is done is not named.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Teachers reported each absent pupil by text message in 2024; the share of primary schools doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The schools' attendance system, which the country calls SSSAMS, was developed under and is supported by the Girls' Education South Sudan programme of the Ministry of General Education and Instruction, funded by UK aid, as its site stated in December 2024. A separate system for refugee learners is managed by UNHCR, on a working paper of September 2025.

Levels in use. The attendance system's tables for academic year 2024 count primary, secondary and other schools in every state. The refugee system reports on early childhood, primary and secondary education, in refugee-hosting schools and camps only.

How the record is kept. The attendance system takes enrolment from the class registers every year; head teachers add and remove pupils by SMS, and class teachers report each absent pupil by SMS to the free number 6464, as of December 2024. An African Union release of March 2026 states that school-level collection relies heavily on handwritten classroom registers and school record books.

Last twelve months. In March 2026 the African Union reported visits to public schools in Juba, where data moved from paper registers to state-level aggregation and national reporting.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The attendance system's panel for academic year 2024 showed 6,650 schools of all levels as having reported attendance at least once, beside 5,915 open schools holding enrolment for the year and 1,321 messages from 3 phones in the last month. An app holding individual pupil records was being piloted in South Sudan in September 2024.

Noted, not assessed. South Sudan calls its school census an EMIS: the ministry's Education Management Information System is described through the yearly census in the sector plan for 2023 to 2027, and is fed once a year by a paper questionnaire or by enumerators with tablets. The Annual Education Census captured 7,737 schools in 2023. The ministry's school performance monitoring tool and Child-friendly School Index are forms collected by Payam education supervisors. The ministry received 84 computers, 33 motorbikes and one vehicle for monitoring and supervision on 5 November 2025.

Not held. No share of primary schools reporting attendance is held, nor a count of them given with its total. No current plan for the attendance system is held, and nothing dated in the past twelve months says whether it is advancing or falling back.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A digital civil registration system was announced in July 2026 and no birth is registered digitally; its design and start date are not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The World Bank's diagnostic of May 2026 gives the Directorate of Civil Registry, Nationality, Passports and Immigration as operator and owner of the planned civil registration system. Its director-general said on 28 July 2026 that implementation would start with support from partners, and named none.

Events registered digitally. The World Bank's May 2026 diagnostic found that birth registration is yet to be implemented and birth certificates do not exist, with about 90 per cent of births in no official register.

How the record is made. Births may be declared at civil registration or identification offices within 45 days, which the World Bank said in May 2026 is uncommon in practice.

Last twelve months. On 24 July 2026 the government announced plans for a digital system recording births, deaths, marriages and divorces. On 31 July the Directorate was reported to be planning to link civil registration with the national ID system, with births declared from health facilities. At the handover of 10 August 2026 the outgoing director-general cited a memorandum of understanding, signed in Nairobi, for an integrated civil registration system.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The World Bank's May 2026 diagnostic lists the Directorate's civil registration system as planned only and describes no office registering digitally.

Noted, not assessed. Notification: a Notification of Birth letter issued in seven of the country's 73 hospitals, the Ministry of Health's DHIS2 database for birth notifications and a DHIS2 CRVS module; birth notification at 2 per cent of live births in 2024-25, and death notification in a pilot phase; a birth notification system in several health centres, non-operational since 2018; an electronic birth notification register piloted in seven health facilities with UNICEF; and planned notification of births from health facilities to the Directorate. Certificate: a bank-linked digital payment system for passports and national ID in Juba, from 19 January 2026. Exchange: a planned link between civil registration and the national ID system.

Not held. No design, procurement, pilot office or start date for a digital system is described, and whether the birth-registration pilot in Juba is digital is not stated. A case system shared by courts, justice ministry and police ran in Juba only in 2021; how many stations use it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. UNDP supported the setup in Juba of a unified case management system for the courts, the justice ministry and the South Sudan National Police Service, its rule of law report on 2021 states.

Records made digitally. UNDP reports that 10,836 cases, 2,508 of them brought by female claimants, were recorded in 2021 through the system, which it names the Case Monitoring Information Management System, set up for the judiciary, the Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Affairs and the police service.

How the record is made. The police service uses a paper-based system for handling crime reporting, with crime files misplaced or lost from police shelves, the abstract of a thesis listed on 7 June 2026 states.

Last twelve months. The thesis listed on 7 June 2026 describes a web application for crime reports that its researcher built and tested for Jubek State; the application is the researcher's own.

Stations doing the digital entry. UNDP's report on 2021 places the case management system in Juba only.

Noted, not assessed. Statistics: the crime data analysis centre at Buluk police division in Juba. Identification: the forensic laboratory equipment for digital forensic investigations and the scheduled electronic fingerprint technology. Public: the Emergency Call Centres in Juba and Wau. Surveillance: the Smart City project and the security cameras on Juba's major streets. Personnel: the headcount and biometric registration of the organised forces.

Not held. No count of police stations using the Juba system is held, nor how many of its cases the police entered or whether the file passes digitally to the court. Nothing on the system is dated after 2021, and the thesis gives no year of its own.
