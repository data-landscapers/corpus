The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Digital payments legislation

5 Leading Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

South Sudan's amended e-money rules took effect in 2026, licensing non-bank issuers and requiring every payment platform to connect through a licensed switch.

Law or rules in force. South Sudan's Electronic Money Regulation 2017 Amendment 2025 took effect as reported on 1 September 2026, issued under the Bank of South Sudan Act 2011 Amendment 2023 and binding licensed financial institutions and non-bank e-money issuers. A broader National Payment System Bill, 2025 is still a draft: the Bank of South Sudan opened a five-day workshop on 10 August 2026 to validate it, and at its close the governor called for faster progress.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. Under the amended regulation non-bank e-money issuers are licensed: standalone payment providers must set up a separate company, seek the central bank's approval and pay a filing fee of 65 million South Sudanese pounds. The same rules require every digital payment platform to connect to the National Payment System through a licensed switch, so that customers can send money between providers.
