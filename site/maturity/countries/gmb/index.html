Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics reported on paper, and regional health offices entered the figures into the national reporting system, in 2024.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's National Health Sector Policy 2021-2030, published in February 2024, calls DHIS2 the central reporting system for all national health information and puts the HMIS programme under the ministry's Directorate of Planning and Information. The policy sets the strategy of migrating fully from paper recording to a computerised system at all levels and lists a costed strategic plan for 2021-2025 as its implementing document.

Tiers in use. The policy places the electronic HMIS at the seven regional health directorates and the ministry. The ministry's service statistics report for 2021, drawn from DHIS2, carries data from referral hospitals, community clinics and the village health service.

Where data is digitised. The policy states that the HMIS is paper at the facility, in registers and monthly return forms, and electronic at the regional health directorates and the centre. From 1 October 2023 one secondary-level hospital, Bundung Maternal and Child Health Hospital, had its monthly indicators integrated into DHIS2 automatically from scanned forms, in a pilot.

Last twelve months. Between June and November 2024 the 11 sites of a tuberculosis pilot kept generating their monthly reports for the DHIS2 aggregate system from paper registers.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The policy gives electronic entry only at the regional health directorates and centrally. A World Bank assessment of July 2023 states that many lower-level facilities continue to collect HMIS data on paper.

Noted, not assessed. Smart Paper Technology, the TB e-Tracker, the immunisation programme's MyChild Solution and Vaccine Visibility System, the pharmaceutical directorate's Logistics Management Information System, the electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response, and community health workers' service delivery reports, integrated into the HMIS.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is published, and no dated event falls in the twelve months to 30 September 2026. No statement of who finances the HMIS is held from the last three years.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One referral hospital piloted an electronic medical record in 2023; it is not known whether that pilot, or a contracted four-facility one, still runs.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Health Sector Policy 2021-2030, published in February 2024, states the need to establish an Electronic Medical Record System across all levels and for the Ministry of Health to integrate existing databases into one system. In June 2023 the World Bank stated that the Bluesquare contract for the electronic National Health Insurance System, under the Bank's Essential Health Services Strengthening Project, was amended to include an electronic health records system using OpenClinic.

Tiers in use. The Point reported in August 2023 the pilot launch of an Electronic Medical Records system at Edward Francis Small Teaching Hospital, the country's main referral hospital, developed by the Medical Research Council Unit The Gambia. The policy of February 2024 states that the health sector does not have a robust electronic medical record system in place.

Where data is digitised. The policy sets the strategy of migrating fully from paper-based recording to a computerised system at all levels of care.

Last twelve months. The World Bank's identity diagnostic of 2025 describes what it calls the Health Management Information System as managing patient records and issuing health cards, with the national identification number of the civil registration system identifying each patient.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. In June 2023 the World Bank described the OpenClinic system as still to be piloted, in four health facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Smart Paper Technology, the TB e-Tracker, the immunisation programme's MyChild Solution and Vaccine Visibility System, the pharmaceutical directorate's Logistics Management Information System, the electronic Logistics Management Information System (eLMIS), Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response, and community health workers' service delivery reports, integrated into the HMIS.

Not held. Whether the teaching hospital's pilot of 2023 or the contracted pilot in four facilities was running at 30 September 2026 is not established, and no primary clinic figure is held. No facility is named for the patient record system described in 2025.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools record each pupil in a national register, scaled up from 200 pilot schools in 2021; how many primary schools enter data is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The system is the Ministry of Basic and Secondary Education's, run by its own unit, as GPE KIX described it in October 2025. An October 2022 account says it was aligned to the Education Policy 2016-2030 and built with financial support from GPE-KIX and Norad.

Levels in use. A learner census run with the Gambia Bureau of Statistics collected individual-level data on children attending primary schools, DHIS2 reported in October 2022, with work continuing to extend the system to all basic and secondary schools.

How the record is kept. Schools record each learner's socio-economic background, attendance and progression in the ministry's digital student registry under a unique ID, DHIS2 reported in August 2025, with modules for daily attendance and exam results.

Last twelve months. GPE KIX in October 2025 described attendance tracking and digital school report cards in use at school level.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The registry began as a pilot in 200 schools in 2020 and the ministry scaled it to national level in 2021. By August 2025 it included nearly 80% of Gambian learners, a share of learners, by DHIS2's account.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school census, which The Gambia calls its Education Management Information System (EMIS): the Annual School Census, its aggregate forms and the statistical yearbook and its summary report; the Teacher Attendance System; and separate databases for human resources, examinations and infrastructure.

Not held. No count or share of primary schools entering data is published, and the sources disagree on whether the school application is still a pilot. Finance within the past three years and any dated event in the last twelve months are not held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered on a central digital system, with 79 local offices holding digital registers in 2025; the total number of offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health holds the mandate for birth registration, under the Registrar General of Births and Deaths. The system was deployed under a World Bank lending operation that ended in August 2025. A National Digital ID and Digital Transformation Strategy for 2023 to 2028 went to a validation workshop in May 2024.

Events registered digitally. The Registrar General said that by June 2026 over 1.4 million people were registered in the system he calls the eCRVS and issued a birth certificate. A 2025 World Bank diagnostic has its birth module in operation and a marriage module developed.

How the record is made. The diagnostic describes a centralised register, with registration carried out locally by Ministry of Health staff in each district and civil registration offices holding digitalised registers. Before 2022 the processes were manual and paper-based.

Last twelve months. A Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Bill had its first reading in the National Assembly on 5 March 2026. Registrations on the system rose from 1.17 million in February 2023 to over 1.4 million in June 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The diagnostic counts 79 civil registration offices with digitalised registers issuing birth certificates in 2025, beside 258 health facility outlets where Ministry of Health staff carry out registration.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the Biometric National Identity Card, the National Identity Management System, named GAM ID, the Semlex biometric identification system, the Gambia Biometric Identification System of 2009, the eID system, the proposed National Digital ID System and the Biometric Passport. Archive: the digitisation of paper birth and death registers in six of the seven health regions. Outreach: the mass registration campaign of August 2022 to February 2023. Enabling: satellite internet in health facilities and user devices for registration centres. Exchange: the integration of the eCRVS with the electronic ID system, and its interoperability with the health human resource and health insurance systems, the interoperability of the social registry, education systems, CRVS and health insurance system sought under a World Bank project, the planned integration of the social registry with the civil registry, the proposed data exchange from the civil registration database to the social registry, the planned integration of the National Identity Management System with civil registration, the proposed registration at birth under the digital ID programme and the linkage of the education and health information systems.

Not held. The total number of civil registration offices is not held, so the 79 have no share, and no source states that the record is keyed at the registration point when the birth is registered. No death module is in operation and no domestic budget line is stated.
