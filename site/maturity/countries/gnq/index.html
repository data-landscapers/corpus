Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Equatorial Guinea has had a personal data protection law since 2016; whether it has been applied, or its supervisory body created, is not known.

Authority. Articles 15 and 29 of the law name the Órgano Rector de Protección de Datos Personales as the supervisory body, with a Secretaría Técnica and a Registro General under it. The law states that the body is to be created by a decree that will fix its structure.

Standing. Ley Núm. 1/2016, de Protección de Datos Personales, was sanctioned and promulgated by the President on 22 July 2016. It sets rules for the processing of personal data in the public and private sectors, and enters into force twenty days after its publication in the Boletín Oficial del Estado.

Regulations. The law leaves the structure of the Órgano Rector to a decree.

Application. The law places the power to sanction with the Minister of Telecommunications and New Technologies.

Last twelve months. The law in force is the one dated 22 July 2016.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Whether the decree creating the Órgano Rector was issued, whether any regulation was made under the law, and whether any decision or sanction has been taken under it. Nothing dated after 22 July 2016 is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Equatorial Guinea is under the 2018 regional payment services regulation, which licenses non-banks; none was licensed there by 2023.

Law or rules in force. Equatorial Guinea is bound by CEMAC regulation n°04/18 of 21 December 2018 on payment services, which the regional central bank's financial inclusion strategy, published in February 2025, describes as governing payment establishments and mobile money across the six member states of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The 2018 regulation created payment establishments, licensed to provide payment services without being tied to a bank; subsidiaries of mobile operators and fintech companies can be licensed where they meet its requirements. The strategy lists six licensed across the region by July 2023: two in Cameroon, one in Chad, two in Congo and one in Gabon. None of the six was in Equatorial Guinea.

Noted, not assessed. GIMACPAY; Muni Dinero.

Not held. No rule held requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2024 the government's network did not reach local government offices, which worked largely on manual systems; whether any has another connection is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The World Bank's digital economy diagnostic of 2024 describes a central network of the government, partly hosted by CNIAPGE. It puts manual working at the local level down to a want of funds and adequate infrastructure.

What the office does over it. The diagnostic states that many ministries at the centre operate on manual systems, and that this is more pronounced at the local level. Data pass between ministries by hand, USB drive or e-mail.

How the office is connected. In 2024 the World Bank stated that the government's central network does not extend to local government branches.

Last twelve months. On 18 December 2025 Gabon's Council of Ministers approved an agreement to interconnect the fibre-optic networks of Gabon and Equatorial Guinea, which are the two countries' national networks.

Local governments connected. The World Bank's statement of 2024 is about local government branches as a whole, which the central network does not reach.

Noted, not assessed. Backbone: the central network of the government, the government servers and the government mobile service the country calls GOTA; CNIAPGE's private government network and its TIER III data centre; the Sipopo Datacenter in Malabo, and the NOSi National Data Centre in Cabo Verde, which lists Equatorial Guinea among its customers; the interconnection of the fibre-optic networks of Gabon and Equatorial Guinea. Application: the civil registration of births, marriages and deaths, kept on paper at registrars', mayors' and local authority offices. Central: CNIAPGE's digital platform for administrative files; the business single window the government calls Ventanilla Unica Empresarial; the digitised map layers the statistics institute calls Base Cartográfica.

Not held. Whether a local government office holds any connection other than the government's central network, and how many local governments there are to connect. No owner, plan or finance for connecting them, and no event in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national reporting system was piloted in two districts, Baney and Añisok, in 2025; a planned national expansion in 2026 is not confirmed.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry announced the conclusion of the DHIS2 pilot on 25 November 2025 and presents the platform as its tool for the national health system. It states that the pilot ran under a primary-care agreement financed by the Spanish cooperation agency AECID. UNDP, on 1 March 2025, quotes the health minister placing the digitalisation within the national health development plan PNDS 2021-2025.

Tiers in use. EHAS states that the first pilot, in 2025, let health facilities in two districts, insular Baney and continental Añisok, enter real data.

Where data is digitised. EHAS, in 2025, states that health facilities filled paper register sheets, which ministry staff periodically collected and transferred to spreadsheets. In the Baney and Añisok pilot data is entered from the health facility with the DHIS2 Android or web app where digital means are available, and at the District Office otherwise.

Last twelve months. On 25 November 2025 the ministry declared the pilot in Baney and Añisok successfully completed and said it would be the reference for a national expansion of DHIS2 foreseen for 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry states that the DHIS2 pilot ran in 2025 in two health districts, Baney and Añisok, with 104 health professionals trained.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 individual register and follow-up of routine vaccination and the DHIS2 follow-up of HIV patients. Single-function: the DHIS2 data on health workforce availability and qualification, the DHIS2 inventory control in medicine dispensaries, the DHIS2 malaria eLMIS of the Bioko Island Malaria Elimination Project and the Sistema Nacional de Vigilancia Epidemiológica, as the ministry names it. Enabling: the Data Center at the ministry's Malabo headquarters and the proposed GALE digital health pilot of eClinics telemedicine stations.

Not held. No source names the tiers or counts the facilities entering data in the Baney and Añisok pilot, and nothing dated after November 2025 shows the national expansion foreseen for 2026 under way. No plan naming DHIS2 and no domestic budget line dated within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A digital pilot registers births in Malabo only, since 2018, and the seven regional offices use paper in 2024; its present state is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Justice put the system it calls SIREC into service in December 2018, with the support of UNICEF and the European Union. The statistics strategy for 2022 to 2026 sets a civil registry system established as an expected result. The World Bank said in 2024 that funding to extend the digital registry to the mainland is lacking.

Events registered digitally. The pilot in Malabo allows the digital registration of new births, on a platform developed to collect births and deaths, the World Bank said in 2024.

How the record is made. The seven regional offices collect and store data by manual, paper-based processes, and local judges registering at commune level share manually collected data with them, on the same account of 2024.

Last twelve months. The World Bank's diagnostic, published in 2024, reports UNICEF as saying the pilot is operational and functions well.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The pilot was launched in late 2018 exclusively in the capital, Malabo, by the Ministry of Justice with UNICEF.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation of historic civil registry records in Malabo and the conversion of 192,000 birth records of 1980 to 2019 to digital form. Notification: Pre-registro de Nacimientos, being implemented in Bata in March 2025. Certificate: APP RC, for electronic certificates with QR codes. Outreach: a free civil registration campaign for children aged 0 to 5, 10 to 14 August 2026. Identity: CNEDOGE's issuance of the identity document it calls the DIP and of passports, its provincial offices in Mongomo and Ciudad de la Paz and online appointment system, its documentation mission to Annobón, its national DIP tour, its internal database of document issuance, the CEMAC biometric passport, and what the World Bank's ID4D dataset lists as the National Identity Document. Enabling: the e-Gob development platform.

Not held. Nothing on the pilot is dated after 2024. How the pilot makes the record, and how many offices in Malabo use it, are not held.
