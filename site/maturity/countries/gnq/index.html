Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Equatorial Guinea has had a personal data protection law since 2016; whether it has been applied, or its supervisory body created, is not known.

Authority. Articles 15 and 29 of the law name the Órgano Rector de Protección de Datos Personales as the supervisory body, with a Secretaría Técnica and a Registro General under it. The law states that the body is to be created by a decree that will fix its structure.

Standing. Ley Núm. 1/2016, de Protección de Datos Personales, was sanctioned and promulgated by the President on 22 July 2016. It sets rules for the processing of personal data in the public and private sectors, and enters into force twenty days after its publication in the Boletín Oficial del Estado.

Regulations. The law leaves the structure of the Órgano Rector to a decree.

Application. The law places the power to sanction with the Minister of Telecommunications and New Technologies.

Last twelve months. The law in force is the one dated 22 July 2016.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Whether the decree creating the Órgano Rector was issued, whether any regulation was made under the law, and whether any decision or sanction has been taken under it. Nothing dated after 22 July 2016 is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Equatorial Guinea is under the 2018 regional payment services regulation, which licenses non-banks; none was licensed there by 2023.

Law or rules in force. Equatorial Guinea is bound by CEMAC regulation n°04/18 of 21 December 2018 on payment services, which the regional central bank's financial inclusion strategy, published in February 2025, describes as governing payment establishments and mobile money across the six member states of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The strategy lists six licensed across the region by July 2023: two in Cameroon, one in Chad, two in Congo and one in Gabon. None of the six was in Equatorial Guinea.
