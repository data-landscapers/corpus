Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

Zambia adopted a public national digital transformation strategy in 2023, running to 2027, with no cost stated; progress against its targets is not known.

Governance and finance. The National Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2027 makes the technology and science ministry the coordinating agency, responsible for quarterly monitoring and reporting. It is to be financed by the Government with cooperating partners, with no amount given.

Standing. A 2023 journal study says the strategy was adopted in 2023. The Ministry of Technology and Science published it on 12 October 2023.

Text. The strategy's full text is hosted on the Ministry of Technology and Science's site, from October 2023.

Period. The strategy states its period as 2023-2027, a five-year implementation framework.

Delivery plan. It sets out interventions with numeric targets, indicators and lead institutions, for example 998 communication towers and 200 district digital transformation centres.

Reporting. The strategy's results framework carries targets for 2026.

Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A 2023 regulation requires public bodies to store personal data in Zambia; which hosting they must use is not known.

Policy adopted. Statutory Instrument No. 43 of 2023, the Electronic Government (General) Regulations, gazetted on 6 October 2023 under the Electronic Government Act 2021, requires every public body to store the personal data it processes on infrastructure domiciled in Zambia, under regulation 8 of the regulations, which cite the Act as their authority. Storage abroad needs authorisation from the Electronic Government Division and the Data Protection Commissioner.

Named hosting and moves. The regulations require all public bodies to integrate their services with the Government Service Bus, run by the Electronic Government Division, and refer to the Government Wide Area Network for e-government services where applicable. They set the rule for where personal data is stored, not which hosting to use. Authorisation for storage abroad rests with two bodies, the Electronic Government Division and the Data Protection Commissioner.

Noted, not assessed. National AI data centre; Paratus; INFRATEL; Zambia National Data Centre (ZNDC); Government Wide Area Network; Government Service Bus.

Not held. No source held says the government has named where public bodies must host, whether a government cloud or data centre the policy tells them to use, or a list of approved providers.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Zambia's 2021 Data Protection Act requires sensitive personal data to stay in the country, enforced by a commissioner since 2023; no recent case is known.

Rule in force. A D4D Hub research paper states that Zambia's Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021 requires sensitive personal data (health records, biometrics, religious beliefs and political opinions) to be stored within the country. It quotes Section 70: sensitive personal data can only be stored in Zambia, and other personal data goes on servers in the Republic unless a transfer is approved. The Act is dated 2021.

The paper quotes Section 70 as allowing personal data to leave Zambia only with the approval of both the data subject and the Data Protection Commissioner. The Commission is the body that holds that approval power.

Law in force. Zambia's National Registration Act is in force. The National Registration Regulations, 2019 were made under its section 14; the Act provides for the national registration card, with a Chief Registrar and registrars. The regulations are Statutory Instrument No. 34 of 2019.

Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The 2021 electronic government act lets public bodies share data and does not require it; an amendment bill was presented in January 2026.

Law or decree in force. Zambia's Electronic Government Act, No. 41 of 2021, was assented to on 19 May 2021. It provides for public bodies sharing data: one public body may retrieve a document from the body that issued it, with the consent of the person concerned, and public bodies may run joint automated procedures. In January 2026 an amendment bill was presented to stakeholders.

Sharing required and the law applied. The Act allows sharing between public bodies and does not require it: each of the two provisions says a public body may act, and neither obliges it to exchange data or to connect to a platform. The amendment bill is described as giving legal standing to electronic submissions and digital authentication. No regulation made under the Act since 2023 is recorded, and no public body is named as exchanging data because the Act requires it.

Noted, not assessed. Electronic Government Act No. 41 of 2021; National Spatial Data Infrastructure (NSDI) Policy and NSDI Portal.

Not held. No source held says the law in force requires public bodies to share data, to connect to the government's exchange platform, or not to ask a person for what another public body already holds.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Zambia's Data Protection Act, passed in 2021, sets conditions for sending personal data abroad; whether it has come into effect is not known.

Rule in force. Zambia's Data Protection Act No. 3 of 2021, enacted on 24 March 2021, sets the conditions for sending personal data outside the Republic in Part X, sections 70 and 71. The Act says it commences on a date the Minister appoints by statutory instrument, and the published text gives no date.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The Act sets out three routes. The first is the data subject's consent together with standard contracts or intragroup schemes approved by the Commissioner. The second is a transfer to a country the Minister has prescribed, on a test of an adequate level of protection. The third is a transfer the Commissioner approves where it is necessary, case by case.

Records made digitally. In 2024 the occurrence books, crime register books and docket movement books were kept on paper at headquarters and across the service.
