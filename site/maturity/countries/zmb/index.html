Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Government counted 93 of 116 councils on its network in 2025 and all 116 in March 2026; the rural share is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Electronic Government Plan 2023-2026 commits to extending the Government Wide Area Network to the 116 district administrative centres and local authorities. It names the Electronic Government Division as the network's deployer and says implementation will depend largely on domestic financing through annual budget allocations.

What the office does over it. Smart Zambia's national coordinator said on 24 March 2026 that data entry into the Constituency Development Fund system was under way across councils. A survey of 2023 found every local authority it covered undertaking e-procurement on the national e-GP platform, and 70% reporting constraints including unstable internet.

How the office is connected. The budget address of 26 September 2025 says Government connected local authorities to the Government Wide Area Network. SMART Zambia said on 7 July 2026 that all local authorities in North-Western Province were on it over fibre.

Last twelve months. Smart Zambia called the connection of all 116 councils complete on 24 March 2026, and announced North-Western Province's local authorities connected on 7 July 2026.

Local governments connected. Each government count puts at least 93 of the 116 councils on the network, the finance minister's figure of 26 September 2025. Smart Zambia's chief technical officer said on 24 March 2026 that all 116 were connected, as did its national coordinator in a briefing published the next day. An article of 26 August 2026 reports one council, Sinazongwe, given high-speed internet under an initiative that seeks to connect all 116.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: telepresence systems, desktops, laptops and solar power at Sinazongwe Town Council; 2,000 laptops and desktops for the 116 local authorities, handed to the local government ministry in September 2025; and solar energy installed at all 116 councils. Applications: the Constituency Development Fund management information system and the e-Council system for revenue management and financial reporting; the Government Services Bus, on which eight local authorities took electronic payments from June 2026, with the Government Payment Gateway and ZamPay; the e-GP procurement platform; the E-Payslip Self-Service Portal; and Lusaka City Council's GIS unit, its manual property rates collection and a digital base map of Lusaka. Web: ZamPortal, carrying 178 services of eight local authorities on the finance minister's account of 30 September 2026; the platform the local government ministry calls e-Council, live in eight local authorities by 2 July 2026, among them Lusaka and Solwezi; local authority websites, which 5 of those surveyed had in 2023, and their Facebook pages; and Lusaka City Council's challenge brief for an AI Citizen Service Assistant. Public access: Digital Transformation Centres, two pilots in Mongu and Kitwe by December 2022, and LoRaWAN in Smart Villages. City systems: Lusaka's CCTV surveillance with a command and control centre and Chinese surveillance systems in urban areas. Backbone: the Government Wide Area Network at six border posts; GovLink, a private 4G network launched on 15 July 2026; the One Government Network's bulk international capacity; the Digital Zambia Acceleration Project's backbone extension; fibre points of presence planned in all 116 districts and the government data centres; the INFRATEL data centres; and the Lusaka Internet Exchange Point. Capacity: training for ICT staff at Sinazongwe Town Council and a planned local authorities digitalisation plan. Central: the Lupososhi District Administration's connection, being configured for the Government Wide Area Network; 525 Starlink kits for government departments, constituency offices, post offices and youth centres; the ZNSDI GeoPortal; the infrastructure ministry's Property Management Information System; the national registration department's INRIS, in eleven districts; the Zambia Police Service's network project for 40 stations; IFMIS; the SMART Office Management System, with Luapula Province its pilot; the labour ministry's ZELMIS; and UNHCR's ProGres refugee database.

Not held. The rural share of the councils connected, and whether the 116 stated in March 2026 included Sinazongwe and the North-Western Province councils reported connected later that year. Whether council staff enter Constituency Development Fund data at their own offices, and a budget line for council connections.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter paper reports into the national system each month, with a few facilities entering directly (April 2024).

Governance, planning, finance. Gavi's programme audit of April 2024 calls DHIS2 the Ministry of Health's own system and "the approved country data management system". The National Health Strategic Plan 2022-2026, revised in February 2023 and costed at ZMW 158.3 billion, names the HMIS as a basis for monitoring the plan and sets out to integrate health information systems at different levels. For its 2023-2025 grant cycle the Global Fund finances the upgrade and maintenance of the DHIS2-based health management information system, as its case study of April 2025 states.

Tiers in use. Interviews of May and June 2019 found the HMIS collecting monthly reports from rural health posts, rural and urban health centres, and district and provincial hospitals.

Where data is digitised. Gavi's audit of April 2024 states that data entry into DHIS2 is done monthly at the district level, with a few health facilities reporting directly. The 2019 interviews describe facilities aggregating patient data onto paper reports each month and district information officers keying them into DHIS2.

Last twelve months. The Countdown to 2030 country team, from the University of Zambia and the Ministry of Health, states that 99% of expected monthly facility reports were in DHIS2 in 2024, against 93% in 2019.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Gavi's audit of April 2024 says that a few health facilities report directly into DHIS2, and cites 3,320 health facilities countrywide from a 2022 cold chain inventory.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Zambia Immunisation Electronic Register (ZIER) and the 'Smart Card' project for HIV continuity of care. Single-function: the electronic integrated disease surveillance and response system (eIDSR), the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response system (IDSR), the electronic logistics management information system (eLMIS), the Logistimo eLMIS for vaccine stores, the laboratory information system, the laboratory system DISA, the Laboratory Information Management System and national malaria data reporting by mobile phone. Community: the electronic community health information system and the digitized community health package. Enabling: the GovLink private 4G network, the Government Wide Area Network (GWAN) and health facility solar power and internet connectivity.

Not held. No count or share of facilities entering their own reports is held; the 99% is a completeness rate and does not say where the data are keyed. The account of the tiers dates from 2019, and no dated event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic record system was in use at 877 of 3,540 health facilities of all kinds (April 2025); the share of primary clinics is unknown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Global Fund's case study of April 2025 states that the Ministry of Health developed SmartCare and describes it as co-financed by the Global Fund. SMART Zambia's national coordinator said on 7 July 2026 that government aims at full national coverage of SmartCare Pro.

Tiers in use. SmartCare 2.0 Hospital Edition went live as a pilot at Ndola Teaching Hospital on 27 April 2026. Interviews of May and June 2019 found some larger health centres and hospitals collecting data in the electronic health record, in select districts.

Where data is digitised. The programme managers' presentation of March 2025 describes SmartCare Pro as capturing the patient encounter at facility level, on a central server that identifies each client uniquely and tracks patients between facilities. The implementers described in January 2026 a nurse at a rural clinic in Kafulafuta identifying a patient by fingerprint to reach his record.

Last twelve months. ITWeb Africa reported on 16 July 2026 that SmartCare Pro and SmartCare 2.0 will be migrated to the government-owned GovLink network, with three Copperbelt hospitals connected first.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Global Fund states that SmartCare Pro is in use at 877 health facilities, against 3,540 health facilities in Zambia, at April 2025. The March 2025 presentation puts SmartCare Legacy and SmartCare Plus together in approximately 1,800 healthcare facilities. SMART Zambia's national coordinator said on 7 July 2026 that SmartCare Pro has been deployed in approximately 80% of health facilities nationwide.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Zambia Immunisation Electronic Register (ZIER) and the 'Smart Card' project for HIV continuity of care. Single-function: the electronic integrated disease surveillance and response system (eIDSR), the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response system (IDSR), the electronic logistics management information system (eLMIS), the Logistimo eLMIS for vaccine stores, the laboratory information system, the laboratory system DISA, the Laboratory Information Management System and national malaria data reporting by mobile phone. Community: the electronic community health information system and the digitized community health package. Enabling: the GovLink private 4G network, the Government Wide Area Network (GWAN) and health facility solar power and internet connectivity.

Not held. No count or share of primary clinics using SmartCare is held: every figure counts health facilities of all kinds, and the 80% is of deployment, with no numerator or denominator. Use at the primary tier rests on 2019 interviews and one rural clinic, and nothing held shows a record retrieved at a second facility in practice.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools report enrolment yearly on paper, by a 2025 account; how they keep pupil records is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the annual reports from schools, which schools send yearly on paper and the Ministry of General Education enters into its database, enrolment only. Single-function: what its user story calls an EMIS in Namwala District, run by Akros with the ministry from 2016, takes one monthly and one termly report entered on tablets; the KGS digital checklist verification, a tablet application for school guidance teachers in 25 of 28 schools in Lavushimanda District, reported in September 2026; the Ministry of Education's textbook management system, in place in June 2026; its GIS school mapping, on which provincial and district offices have been oriented; the Teacher Development Data Platform, not yet developed in June 2026; and the Teaching Council of Zambia's teacher database, due to be enhanced.

Not held. No dated statement of how primary schools keep their learners' records is held. No system holding a record for each learner that a primary school adds to during the year is described.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered in a national system deployed in eleven districts in 2026; how many local offices enter records themselves is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Minister of Home Affairs and Internal Security describes the Integrated National Registration Information System, INRIS, as the ministry's national and civil registration management system, run through the Department of National Registration, Passport and Citizenship, in a 2026 statement to the National Assembly. The Eighth National Development Plan 2022-2026 commits the Government to operationalise it. The Minister puts the cost of the exercise at K1,069,366,200.

Events registered digitally. The Minister states that enrolment has commenced in the INRIS districts, including registration of births and issuance of birth certificates using the system. The ministry said in December 2025 that about 1.3 million citizens have been enrolled on INRIS and over 500,000 births and deaths registered.

How the record is made. A preprint of September 2025 states that the department runs a paper-based system for birth and death registration, and that completed death notification forms are carried weekly from local offices to provincial or national headquarters for entry into INRIS. The Lavushimanda district officer said in September 2026 that the district office is implementing digital civil registration through INRIS.

Last twelve months. The Lavushimanda district office registered over 3,000 children under five in the four months to September 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Minister's 2026 statement has INRIS deployed in eleven districts. A CDC Foundation case study of 2025 says the expansion of INRIS has brought access to civil registration services to 81 of 116 districts.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation of legacy civil registration records. Notification: the electronic Form 13 death notification pilot, the e-death notification system, the draft e-MCCD system within SmartCare, the birth and death notification desks in health facilities and the INRIS API Manager. Certificate: the government e-services portal, ZamPortal. Outreach: the 2025 registration campaign for birth certificates and National Registration Cards and the refugee enrolment campaigns with mobile registration camps. Identity: the National Registration Card, the biometric National Registration Cards, the electronic National Registration Cards, the planned MOSIP-based digital ID system, the computerised issuance systems for national registration cards, passports and citizenship documentation and UNHCR ProGres. Enabling: the stationary biometric kits and the planned IT upgrading at ministry offices in three refugee settlements. Exchange: ZamConnect, the INRIS-SmartCare integration and the link from birth registration records to the national registration card number.

Not held. No count of local offices that enter events in INRIS themselves is held, and the accounts differ on whether entry is made at the office or at provincial or national headquarters. No source of funds is stated for the current exercise, and nothing dated in the last twelve months shows a national advance or retreat.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Stations recorded on paper in 2024; network equipment for 40 arrived by February 2026, and whether any yet records digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Zambia Police Strategic Plan 2023-2026 gives guidelines for an e-record management system for the service's electronic records, by a University of Zambia study. The service's Technical Services Directorate, the Ministry of Home Affairs and Internal Security and the SMART Zambia Institute build and run the police's electronic systems.

Records made digitally. In 2024 the occurrence books, crime register books and docket movement books were kept on paper at headquarters and across the service. Headquarters had a Police Information Management System, a database of police records including crime reports and case files, officials told the study between November 2024 and January 2025. UNDP said in November 2021 that an Electronic Occurrence Book had been rolled out to the Victim Support Unit.

How the record is made. Statements are recorded and dockets opened by hand, so complainants and witnesses attend a station in person, informants told the study between November 2024 and January 2025.

Last twelve months. The SMART Zambia Institute received routers, switches, access points and firewalls for 40 police stations, to connect them to a centralised digital system, as reported on 3 February 2026.

Stations doing the digital entry. In 2024 the occurrence book, crime register and docket were digitised nowhere in the service, and rollout from headquarters to divisions and stations was planned.

Noted, not assessed. All at headquarters unless stated, by the University of Zambia study: for the public, police e-services on ZamPortal and online payment of fines; identification, Interpol motor vehicle clearance, police clearance by fingerprint and a Biometric Identification System; single-function, firearm certificates and cross-border permits; surveillance, CCTV cameras and the Safe City cameras and National Command Centre in Lusaka; personnel, the police cooperative's e-thrift management system, introduced in 2023; statistics, a Crime Intelligence Analysis Centre, planned; enabling, computers and Digital Communication Systems. Also identification: cyber-investigation and forensics tools for the Forensics and Cybercrime Department, the headquarters forensic department and a DNA laboratory. Also enabling: the Zambia Police Service LAN and Last Mile Connectivity Project, for 40 stations, and the Government Wide Area Network, which had connected 27 of a target of 442 police stations and posts in 2022.

Not held. No fact shows a station entering a report or a case digitally, and who enters the records held in the headquarters database is not stated. The centralised system the 40 stations are to join is not yet in place, with no pilot, station count or source of finance given for it.
