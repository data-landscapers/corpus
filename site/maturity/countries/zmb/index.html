Data storage / cloud strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A 2023 regulation requires public bodies to store personal data in Zambia; no source found says which hosting they must use.

Policy adopted. Statutory Instrument No. 43 of 2023, the Electronic Government (General) Regulations, gazetted on 6 October 2023 under the Electronic Government Act 2021, requires every public body to store the personal data it processes on infrastructure domiciled in Zambia, under regulation 8 of the regulations, which cite the Act as their authority. Storage abroad needs authorisation from the Electronic Government Division and the Data Protection Commissioner.

Named hosting and moves. The regulations require all public bodies to integrate their services with the Government Service Bus, run by the Electronic Government Division, and refer to the Government Wide Area Network for e-government services where applicable. They set the rule for where personal data is stored, not which hosting to use. Authorisation for storage abroad rests with two bodies, the Electronic Government Division and the Data Protection Commissioner.

Law or decree in force. Zambia's Electronic Government Act, No. 41 of 2021, was assented to on 19 May 2021. It provides for public bodies sharing data: one public body may retrieve a document from the body that issued it, with the consent of the person concerned, and public bodies may run joint automated procedures. Sharing required and the law applied. The Act allows sharing between public bodies and does not require it: each of the two provisions says a public body may act, and neither obliges it to exchange data or to connect to a platform. The amendment bill is described as giving legal standing to electronic submissions and digital authentication. No regulation made under the Act since 2023 is recorded, and no public body is named as exchanging data because the Act requires it.

Zambian law requires sign language or subtitles in television newscasts, and ZICTA gave screen-reader computers to a special school in 2024; no audit is known.

Accessibility rule. A 2025 legal analysis by CIPESA covers the Persons with Disabilities Act 2012. It says section 49 requires television stations to provide a sign language inset or subtitles. This applies to all newscasts and educational programmes. In May 2025 ZAPD said draft national accessibility standards had been developed. They cover built and digital environments.

Delivered. In 2024 ZICTA gave Chinsali Special School 15 desktop computers. Five carry JAWS screen-reading software. A braille printer and internet connectivity came with them. The school has 768 learners, 213 of them with disabilities.
