Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

The ministry began training staff on the national reporting system in December 2023, starting with a team of 20; nothing yet shows it in use.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health, with WHO support, began training health practitioners and IT staff to use the DHIS2 platform, adapted to the national context, the WHO Algeria office reports. The national digital health strategy under which the training began was still a draft in November 2023, when a pre-validation workshop of sixty participants worked on it ahead of its submission to the national authorities.

Tiers in use. The training is meant, the WHO office states, to make the national health information system yield information for decision-makers at every level.

Where data is digitised. The first session, five days in December 2023 led by an international expert, taught its participants to use the platform.

Last twelve months. The WHO office reports that further training sessions were to be programmed in the 2024-2025 work plan.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The first session trained a first team of 20 health practitioners and IT staff.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the weekly notification forms for priority diseases and events and Go.Data in the Sahrawi refugee camps at Tindouf, the GIS for surveillance of immunisation and polio indicators, and the laboratory system IKOLAB and radiology system RISPACKS at the Tizi-Ouzou university hospital. Enabling: the fibre-optic connection of health structures in the wilaya of Annaba.

Not held. Nothing held says which tiers report through the platform, where data is keyed, how many primary clinics enter their own reports or how it is financed. No use of the platform after the first training is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic medical record is being introduced in hospitals (September 2026); how many is not known, and nothing covers primary clinics.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health updated the patient's electronic record itself, drawing on nearly 700 proposals from the sector, and its minister presented the new version on 26 February 2024. In Annaba the ministry's services coordinate preparation of what Seybouse Times calls the Dossier médical électronique with the wilaya health directorate and Algérie Télécom, as of 16 August 2026.

Tiers in use. An El Moudjahid editorial of 8 September 2026 places the electronic medical record system in the country's hospital establishments. From 2018 the ministry put an earlier record, DEMDZ, into certain pilot hospitals, the Tizi-Ouzou university hospital among them. At that hospital, Salmi, Bouchelaghem and Mohellebi wrote in 2022, the use of DEMDZ failed and only the laboratory and radiology parts of a computerised patient record were in use.

Where data is digitised. The version presented to hospital directors on 26 February 2024 carries a national identification number for each patient and holds all the patient's personal and medical data. The minister said any hospital establishment in the country can access it.

Last twelve months. On 16 August 2026 the Annaba health directorate, the Ministry of Health and Algérie Télécom met to speed up the fibre-optic connection of health structures, in preparation for the record's implementation in the wilaya, Seybouse Times reported.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Seybouse Times describes the record's progressive roll-out in the health structures of the wilaya of Annaba as still forthcoming in mid-August 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the weekly notification forms for priority diseases and events and Go.Data in the Sahrawi refugee camps at Tindouf, the GIS for surveillance of immunisation and polio indicators, and the laboratory system IKOLAB and radiology system RISPACKS at the Tizi-Ouzou university hospital. Enabling: the fibre-optic connection of health structures in the wilaya of Annaba.

Not held. No count or share of hospitals using the record is held, and nothing on primary clinics. Where the record is keyed, a plan naming it and its financing are not stated.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

It is not known whether primary schools keep pupil records on paper or in a system, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Electronic tablets for primary schools, and the distance education platforms of the national distance education office, presented by the education minister in February 2026; and the teacher recruitment competition run digitally, reported in May 2026.

Not held. Whether primary schools keep their pupils' records on paper or in a system, at which levels, and in how many schools. Nothing held says who runs or pays for such a system.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered in a national digital register across 1,541 communes, a 2019 count; the share of local offices entering digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Law 14-08 of 9 August 2014 creates the national automated civil-status register under the Ministry of the Interior and Local Authorities. A university article published on 10 October 2025 places the register within the e-municipality programme of the national e-Algeria strategy, which it dates 2008 to 2028. The ministry's presentation of July 2026 lists the availability of financing among the levers of the digitalisation.

Events registered digitally. The same presentation states that since 2018 each newborn has a birth act and a national identification number at the end of its registration, the number generated automatically from the register. The ministry's deck of 19 June 2019 shows the register holding three databases, of births, deaths and marriages.

How the record is made. The 2019 deck places entry, control and update at commune level, with 48 wilaya databases for error correction and a national database at the ministry. Under the 2014 law each commune still inscribes acts on three paper registers kept in duplicate and transmits a digitised copy of every act to the national register.

Last twelve months. In July 2026 the ministry presented the register as its own, created in 2014, with unified national governance of civil-status data.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The 2019 deck shows 1,541 commune databases where entry, control and update are done. The 2025 article states that paper documentation persists alongside the digital services.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the scanning and keying of 102 million civil-status acts into the national database. Certificate: electronic civil-status documents requested online, the online request for the 12S birth certificate, the issue of acts at any commune or administrative annex, the online counter at prestations.interieur.gov.dz and the foreign ministry's appointment-booking platform for its civil-status service. Identity: the Numéro d'Identification National, the Registre National de la Population, the Carte Nationale d'Identité Biométrique Electronique, the passeport biométrique électronique and its remote renewal platform for the diaspora, the Biometric Driving License, the communes' electronic counter for the biometric service and the national digital identity system of the High Commission for Digitalisation. Enabling: the national sovereign cloud and the fibre-optic network and national data centres. Exchange: the connection of administrations and services to the register, the link between the national vehicle registration file and the register and the dispositif d'interopérabilité of decree 23-316.

Not held. The 1,541 is a 2019 count of commune databases with no total of local offices, communes and their annexes together, and nothing held says the entry is made at the commune office when the event is registered. No event of the last twelve months and no rural figure is held.
