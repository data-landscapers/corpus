Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Algeria adopted its national digital transformation strategy for 2025 to 2030 in May 2025, but costs and results against its targets are not known.

Governance and finance. The High Commission for Digitalisation, created by presidential decree 23-314 of 6 September 2023, designs the strategy and follows its implementation, and on 4 May 2026 it signed a project with UNDP, supported by the Government of Japan to support its implementation.

Standing. El Chaab says the National Digital Transformation Strategy 2025-2030 was adopted, approved and published in May 2025, and on 12 May 2025 the High Commissioner called it the approved strategy.

Text. The text is published on the High Commission's site, in an August 2024 version with twenty-five strategic objectives.

Period. The ITU repository gives its time frame as 2025 to 2030.

Delivery plan. The minister said on 4 May 2026 that the implementation plan for 2025-2026 is under way, with an executive plan for 2027-2030 in preparation.

Reporting. The High Commission published a review of April 2024 to September 2026 listing its projects, and said on 2 September 2026 that the second national data centre at Blida implements its first axis.

Last twelve months. The National Digital Services Centre was inaugurated on 5 July 2026, and the Council of Ministers of 12 July 2026 gave administrations one month to connect to the National Data Center.

Noted, not assessed. e-Algeria; Presidential Decree 25-320 on data governance; Law No. 25-11 amending the 2018 data protection law; Digital Law; draft law on digital identity and trust services; digital ID and trust services framework; data exchange platform among ministries; National Data Center interconnection.

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Algeria's 2018 data protection law has been in force since 2023 and its authority issues rules; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. The Autorité nationale de protection des données à caractère personnel (ANPDP) adopted a deliberation by a majority of its members on 15 July 2026, signed by its president. Samir Bourehil was its president in July 2026. It issues authorisations and receives declarations of processing.

Standing. Law n° 18-07 of 10 June 2018 came into force on 10 August 2023, the US International Trade Administration states. Law 25-11, promulgated in July 2025, amended it, adding duties to appoint a data protection delegate, keep a processing register and log automated operations. The law was in force in July 2026.

Regulations. Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026, in eleven articles, sets conditions for processing and publishing images and recordings of natural persons. In the months before it the ANPDP published rules on workplace video surveillance and on appointing data protection delegates. A compliance package of guidelines and four templates was released in 2025.

Application. The authority decided in January or February 2026 that a positive or negative test result alone is standard health data, so employers need no prior authorisation. It runs an online complaint service for data subjects, and by December 2025 it had held its first study day and a series of training meetings with national bodies.

Last twelve months. Délibération n° 04 treats publication on foreign-run platforms as a transfer abroad. A study day at the national police college in July 2026 announced a unified guide for administrative, security and judicial procedures.

Noted, not assessed. The National Data Governance Framework, which Algeria calls the dispositif national de gouvernance des données.

Not held. No decision or sanction on a named controller is held. No count of decisions, sanctions or complaints settled is published for a stated period.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Algeria's 2025 amendment requires an adequacy assessment before data go abroad for criminal-justice purposes; whether any list, contract terms or procedure exists is not known.

Rule in force. Law 25-11 of 24 July 2025, published in the Journal Officiel that day, amends Law 18-07 with a new article 45 bis 13. Before data are transferred to a foreign State or international organisation, the competent authority assesses whether it gives an adequate level of protection. This applies to transfers for criminal-justice purposes. Transfer on insufficient safeguards is allowed only in listed cases: vital interests, a threat to public security, and individual criminal cases. The Authority's Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026 extends the law's rules to images published on social platforms and treats publication on foreign-run platforms as a transfer abroad.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest fact held is the Authority's Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026, which applies the transfer rule to images published on foreign-run platforms. It is a ruling on one kind of transfer, and the law's article 45 bis 13 is the assessment it rests on.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.
