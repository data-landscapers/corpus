Data protection legislation

Algeria's 2018 data protection law has been in force since 2023 and its authority issues rules; no source found reports a sanction on a controller.

Authority. The Autorité nationale de protection des données à caractère personnel (ANPDP) adopted a deliberation by a majority of its members on 15 July 2026, signed by its president. Samir Bourehil was its president in July 2026. It issues authorisations and receives declarations of processing.

Standing. Law n° 18-07 of 10 June 2018 came into force on 10 August 2023, the US International Trade Administration states. Law 25-11, promulgated in July 2025, amended it, adding duties to appoint a data protection delegate, keep a processing register and log automated operations. The law was in force in July 2026.

Regulations. Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026, in eleven articles, sets conditions for processing and publishing images and recordings of natural persons. In the months before it the ANPDP published rules on workplace video surveillance and on appointing data protection delegates. A compliance package of guidelines and four templates was released in 2025.

Application. The authority decided in January or February 2026 that a positive or negative test result alone is standard health data, so employers need no prior authorisation. It runs an online complaint service for data subjects, and by December 2025 it had held its first study day and a series of training meetings with national bodies.

Last twelve months. Délibération n° 04 treats publication on foreign-run platforms as a transfer abroad. A study day at the national police college in July 2026 announced a unified guide for administrative, security and judicial procedures.

Also on file. The National Data Governance Framework, which Algeria calls the dispositif national de gouvernance des données.

Cross-border data transfers

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Algeria's 2025 amendment requires an adequacy assessment before data go abroad for criminal-justice purposes; no source says whether any list, contract terms or procedure exists.

Rule in force. Law 25-11 of 24 July 2025, published in the Journal Officiel that day, amends Law 18-07 with a new article 45 bis 13. Before data are transferred to a foreign State or international organisation, the competent authority assesses whether it gives an adequate level of protection. This applies to transfers for criminal-justice purposes. Transfer on insufficient safeguards is allowed only in listed cases: vital interests, a threat to public security, and individual criminal cases. The Authority's Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026 extends the law's rules to images published on social platforms and treats publication on foreign-run platforms as a transfer abroad.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest fact held is the Authority's Délibération n° 04 of 15 July 2026, which applies the transfer rule to images published on foreign-run platforms. It is a ruling on one kind of transfer, and the law's article 45 bis 13 is the assessment it rests on. Digital Policy Alert said in April 2025 that the ANPDP had not issued the list of adequate countries or the declarations for transfer authorisation.

Also on file. None found.

Not found. No source found says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.

Agricultural use of satellite data

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A farming service using satellite data was in use in 2025; regular published outputs are not on record.

Satellite service. The CGIAR tool series says the WaPOR-based Water Use Tool is used by the WaPOR Digital Unit at the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, hosted by its Department of Statistics and Strategies, and uses 20 m WaPOR satellite data to compute dekadal irrigation water use and map irrigated and rainfed parcels, with a dashboard for the Mitidja West scheme. The Algerian Space Agency (ASAL) says it works with the Ministry of Agriculture on pilot satellite mapping of cereals (Sidi Bel Abbes, Sentinel-2 NDVI time series) and of potatoes (Mostaganem), and plans to customise CropWatch for these two pilot zones (2023, before the three-year window). The agriculture ministry's secretary general said in February 2025 that Algerian Space Agency imagery let the country control the locust invasion, with 23,000 hectares treated preventively. The space agency's first national conference on geospatial agriculture, in November 2023, heard of Alsat-1B soil studies and CropWatch crop monitoring in Algeria.
