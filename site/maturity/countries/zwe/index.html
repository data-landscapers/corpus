Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All levels of the health system report on paper, and district offices enter the figures into the national system (2023).

Governance, planning, finance. The U.S. President's Malaria Initiative (PMI), in a profile published in 2023, describes DHIS2 as the Ministry of Health and Child Care's system, to which donors and partners have very limited access. The ministry's National Health Strategy 2021-2025 names DHIS2 among its routine aggregate systems for monitoring and evaluation. The Global Fund said in August 2025 that its financing supports data analysis and capacity building at sub-national levels of the national health management information system.

Tiers in use. PMI's 2023 profile states that the HMIS collects routine aggregate information from four levels: over 1,700 primary health centres, clinics and rural hospitals, about 140 district-level hospitals, eight provincial hospitals and six quaternary hospitals.

Where data is digitised. The same profile states that monthly collection is on paper at village, health facility and hospital levels, and that the district health information officer enters the paper records into the Zimbabwe DHIS2. A 2025 abstract on the DHIS2 community site states that facilities running Impilo, the ministry's patient record system, send data to DHIS2 by real-time, mobile-backup and manual-backup pipelines.

Last twelve months. The Countdown to 2030 synthesis report, written with ministry staff, states that the national reporting rate of health facility data in DHIS2 was above 92% in 2024.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. PMI's routine table gives 1,776 health facilities reporting into the HMIS/DHIS2 in 2022, with 96% of expected monthly reports received.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Zimbabwe National AIDS Council's DHIS2 Tracker for HIV prevention and the malaria programme's DHIS2 Tracker Capture case-based surveillance system. Single-function: the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS), the electronic logistics management information system (eLMIS), the Zimbabwe Assisted Pull System (ZAPS) ordering system, the Rapid Disease Notification System and Weekly Disease Surveillance Database, the eSample transport system, the National Pharmaceutical Company's warehouse management system and PMI's Malaria Data Integration and Visualization for Eradication (MDIVE). Enabling: the Starlink kits at rural health facilities, the Starlink Pilot Program in Matabeleland South, Solar for Health and the medical school's telemedicine application.

Not held. No count or share of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the 96% counts reports received. No strategy current at 30 September 2026 is held, and no dated event in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic record system ran in 1,254 health facilities of all types in August 2025, out of 1,900 planned; shared records are not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Global Fund said in August 2025 that the Government of Zimbabwe has been developing a national EMR system since February 2015, culminating in Impilo, for use in public health facilities. The ministry's National Health Strategy 2021-2025 commits to supporting the deployment of what it calls the electronic health record (Impilo) system. The Global Fund has put over US$43.1 million into digital health in Zimbabwe since 2014, US$9.85 million of it in the 2024-2026 grant cycle.

Tiers in use. The Permanent Secretary for Health and Child Care told The Sunday Mail on 17 November 2024 that the facilities with Impilo are spread across provinces and districts and include clinics, hospitals and other health centres.

Where data is digitised. A 2025 abstract on the DHIS2 community site states that Impilo captures real-time clinical and administrative data for services including HIV testing, antiretroviral therapy and maternal and child health, stored in centralised repositories and a national data warehouse.

Last twelve months. The Herald on 23 September 2026 described Impilo as the Ministry of Health's own electronic health records platform.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Global Fund reports 1,254 health facilities implementing Impilo as of August 2025, with deployment planned for all 1,900 health facilities. The Permanent Secretary put deployment at 1,178 health facilities on 17 November 2024. The Matabeleland South provincial health team reported 110 of the province's 153 health facilities (71.9%) with Impilo fully functional in May 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Zimbabwe National AIDS Council's DHIS2 Tracker for HIV prevention and the malaria programme's DHIS2 Tracker Capture case-based surveillance system. Single-function: the Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS), the electronic logistics management information system (eLMIS), the Zimbabwe Assisted Pull System (ZAPS) ordering system, the Rapid Disease Notification System and Weekly Disease Surveillance Database, the eSample transport system, the National Pharmaceutical Company's warehouse management system and PMI's Malaria Data Integration and Visualization for Eradication (MDIVE). Enabling: the Starlink kits at rural health facilities, the Starlink Pilot Program in Matabeleland South, Solar for Health and the medical school's telemedicine application.

Not held. Nothing held says an Impilo record can be retrieved at another facility. The counts are of facilities of every type where Impilo is deployed, not of primary clinics entering records, and no strategy current at 30 September 2026 is held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry said in 2026 its system tracks each pupil's absence and grades; how many primary schools enter them is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education presents the system it calls EMIS, and the report drawn from it, as its own, with its spokesperson speaking for it in June 2026.

Levels in use. The ministry's EMIS report counts 39,736 learners who dropped out in 2025, 13,032 at primary and 26,704 at secondary level, and the ministry describes its Early Warning System without limiting it to either level.

How the record is kept. The ministry's spokesperson said in June 2026 that the Early Warning System, now integrated into EMIS, flags individual learners by tracking persistent absenteeism and falling grades and produces a real-time list of learners absent for extended periods.

Last twelve months. The spokesperson said in June 2026 that the Early Warning System is being rolled out with re-entry and community tracing, and that the government will keep strengthening it.

Primary schools doing the digital input. School-based response teams act on the system's flags, and district and school authorities use its list of absent learners, the ministry said in June 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Zimbabwe calls its yearly school census an EMIS: the ministry's Annual School Census on form ED 46 was a 20-page questionnaire filled in by hand at about 8,953 schools, with a 95% response rate for 2019, and climate and disaster-risk questions were added in 2025. Single-function: the ministry's Information Management System for school registration, not yet built in April 2026. Standalone: CAMFED's cloud-based database, with a record for each student it supports. Enabling: the Universal Service Fund's connectivity subscriptions for schools, covered for one year; Giga, with 6,111 schools targeted for connectivity in 2022; and the pilot to solarise 150 schools, 36 of them done by 2022.

Not held. Who enters the attendance and grades, and where, is not held, nor any count or share of schools in the Early Warning System. No plan or source of finance for it is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are entered digitally at computerised offices; at least 93 of 282 offices were computerised in 2026, and how many register digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Registrar General's department presents the computerisation of its provinces, districts and sub-offices as its own decentralisation programme, in July 2026. UNICEF says Zimbabwe developed a strategic action plan for civil registration and vital statistics for 2023-2027. The Appropriation (2026) Act appropriated ZiG 1,470,460,000 to the Civil Registration programme, and the ten sub-offices announced in August 2026 are funded by China and delivered by UNICEF.

Events registered digitally. UNICEF Zimbabwe stated in July 2025 that at the computerised sub-office at Mpilo Hospital mothers register newborns and obtain a birth certificate before discharge.

How the record is made. UNICEF Zimbabwe stated in July 2025 that the computerised registry offices and sub-offices allow real-time data entry at the office. UNICEF's chief of child protection said on 7 August 2026 that some decentralised sub-offices are not computerised.

Last twelve months. In October 2025 the Minister of Home Affairs and Cultural Heritage announced plans to computerise sub-offices across the country. On 7 August 2026 UNICEF and the government announced a China-funded project to computerise, digitalise and solar-power 10 sub-offices in eight districts.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Deputy Registrar General said on 22 July 2026 that all 10 provinces and 63 districts have been computerised and more than 20 of 209 sub-offices. UNICEF Zimbabwe named, in July 2025, digital registration as operational at Mpilo Hospital, Kariangwe in Binga, Bubi, Manama Mission Hospital, Mahusekwa, Murambinda and Epworth.

Noted, not assessed. Notification: the BD1 forms issued by traditional leaders and the birth and death notifications from health facilities. Certificate: the planned online application for birth certificates and passports. Outreach: the national mobile registration exercise of 2022, the mobile registration exercises in Binga district, the targeted mobile registration of the 2026 programme and its outreach services for legal identity documents. Identity: the Zimbabwe Population Registry System, the National Population Register, the Registrar-General's national population database, the e-passport system and the planned national digital ID. Enabling: the National Data Centre and the Mutare Provincial Registry Complex. Exchange: the CRVS-Health interoperability platform, the Civil Registry integration with mobile money providers, the integration of the Tax and Revenue Management System with the Civil Registry, the Civil Registry integration with the High Performance Computing centre and the proposed social registry integration with civil registry systems.

Not held. No count of offices registering births digitally is held: the Registrar General's count is of offices computerised, and what a computerised office does is not stated. Where the digital record is held is not known, and no digital entry of deaths is held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic docket system was announced in December 2025 as coming soon; whether any station yet records digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Home Affairs minister said on 17 January 2024 that computerising police operations, under what he called the ZRP Integrated Information System, is part of the ministry's ICT strategy approved by Cabinet in 2022, and that the police had finished a needs assessment and were looking for a partner. In December 2021 the Information minister said government would bear all costs of digitising rural stations.

Records made digitally. The officer commanding Harare Province said, in remarks reported on 9 December 2025, that the police would soon move to an electronic system for processing all dockets.

How the record is made. The force's Horizon 2025 strategic plan, as reported on 16 December 2021, decries the manual management of information, which it says makes information hard to retrieve and leads to its loss and destruction.

Last twelve months. In December 2025 the Zimbabwe Republic Police presented the planned electronic docket system as a measure against bribery in the handling of case files.

Stations doing the digital entry. The Information minister said after Cabinet on 14 December 2021 that a pilot study for digitising rural police stations had been completed, with gadgets and connectivity still to be provided.

Noted, not assessed. An Electronic Traffic Management System; the ZRP Smart Traffic Management System; the Smart Transport and Traffic Management System planned in 2021; point-of-sale machines planned in October 2025 for all police roadblocks, with Starlink connectivity; drones acquired to monitor traffic and public spaces; a smart traffic management system launched in Harare under the Safe City Project; the Presidential Internet Scheme, which by 2025 had connected over 100 government institutions, police stations among them.

Not held. Which stations, if any, entered reports or dockets in a system by 30 September 2026, and which record the planned system will hold. How the system is to be paid for has not been stated since 2021.
