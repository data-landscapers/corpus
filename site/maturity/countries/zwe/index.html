Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Zimbabwe has the Cyber and Data Protection Act of 2021, applied through licensing and notices; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. Section 5 of the Act designates the Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (POTRAZ) as the Data Protection Authority. Business Times reported in May 2026 that it has a Director for Data Protection and powers to audit, investigate, warn, direct and initiate prosecutions.

Standing. The Cyber and Data Protection Act, No. 5 of 2021, was gazetted on 11 March 2022. A June 2025 overview describes it as the main data protection law, applying to all public and private organisations, and in May 2026 it provided criminal sanctions for accountable officers, a fine or up to seven years in prison.

Regulations. The Minister of ICT made licensing regulations, Statutory Instrument 155 of 2024, under section 32 of the Act in September 2024. They require organisations processing the personal data of 50 or more people to hold a data controller licence, at US$50 to US$2,500 a year, with a first deadline of 12 March 2025.

Application. POTRAZ handed the first licences to 570 companies in June 2025, and the ICT minister said on 15 October 2025 that 721 controllers were licensed and 830 data protection officers trained. Its Director for Data Protection said in May 2026 that administrative sanctions were not yet in place.

Last twelve months. Regulatory Notice 2 of 2026, published in July 2026, announced mandatory compliance inspections of data controllers from 1 September 2026. In September 2026 POTRAZ consulted on guidelines under the Act and on amendments to the regulations covering administrative fines.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No named and dated sanction, order or decision on a controller, and no count of decisions for a stated period.

Law in force. On 24 January 2026 the Director General of POTRAZ stated that Zimbabwe has no standalone cyber crimes act. They cover hacking, data interference, cyber-bullying, child abuse material and others, in sections 163 to 168.

The POTRAZ Director General set them out on 24 January 2026 while stating that the country has no separate cyber crimes act. They include hacking, data interference, cyber-bullying and child abuse material. The Act, as amended in 2021, is the law in force on these offences, according to the Director General, who gave the date of 24 January 2026 for his statement.

Rule in force. On 13 November 2024 POTRAZ, the designated data protection authority, issued guideline CDPG 5 of 2024 under sections 6(1)(a) and 28(3) of the Cyber and Data Protection Act. In a report published on 26 December 2025, POTRAZ said 24 cross-border transfers of personal data have been authorised.
