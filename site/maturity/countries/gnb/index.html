Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health centres' paper reports were entered into the national system at 11 health directorates (June 2023); how many clinics enter their own is not published.

Governance, planning, finance. The DHIS2 project's country profile, updated July 2026, describes Guinea-Bissau's DHIS2 systems as locally owned, with the Ministry of Health leading the work on them with Saudigitus (HISP Mozambique). The Ministry of Public Health's PNDS III, of June 2023, places the national health information system, the SNIS, under INASA and names a strategic plan for it, PENSIS 2022-2026. Support agreed in October 2026 is coordinated by UNDP and paid for by the Global Fund.

Tiers in use. INASA's health statistics yearbook for 2022 tabulates the monthly reports of health centres entered in DHIS2: 1,747 of 2,073 expected reports were entered, a completeness of 82.2 per cent.

Where data is digitised. PNDS III, of June 2023, states that all the SNIS paper forms and reports are digitised in the 11 health directorates, on the DHIS2 platform.

Last twelve months. On 22 December 2025 the DHIS2 project announced that the HISP network, selected by the Global Fund, will give technical assistance to seven African countries including Guinea-Bissau to integrate climate data into their national HMIS. On 6 October 2026 it announced an agreement to support INASA on the national system's data quality, with new tools to track patients with HIV or tuberculosis.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. INASA reported in January 2019 that tablet entry of data directly from the health areas entered a pilot phase in 2018. The 2022 yearbook gives the public network as 111 type C, 19 type B and 5 type A health centres.

Noted, not assessed. On DHIS2, disease surveillance, bed-net campaign planning and management, Tracker registries for maternal and child health and immunisation, the COVID-19 surveillance packages and individual tuberculosis data. Beside them, an eLMIS, an Excel and Access laboratory data tool and an epidemiological surveillance data flow. For community health workers, UNICEF's mWater, the concept named Rota Segura and what INASA calls GBSIS Comunitário. Also the National Telemedicine Center.

Not held. No number or share of health centres keying their own reports is published, and no later fact follows the 2018 tablet pilot. No coverage fact is dated after June 2023, and no domestic budget line for the system is stated.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One Bissau office registers births and deaths digitally in 2026, and health posts in three regions register births; the share of offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The system the country calls SIREC was implemented at the Ministry of Justice's Direção-Geral de Identificação Civil, Registo e Notariado, launched in January 2025. The digital transformation strategy approved in March 2025 names the civil registry among the services to consolidate and expand. UNDP reports for 2025 that the Swiss Confederation, the European Union and Portuguese Cooperation financed it.

Events registered digitally. Officials at the Santa Luzia Civil Registry Office in Bissau record births, marriages and deaths directly on SIREC, UNDP said in April 2026. Health workers at registration posts in hospitals and health centres register births on a tablet, UNICEF reported in September 2025.

How the record is made. Santa Luzia has replaced paper register books with workstations connected to the national platform, with records entered directly and held centrally, on UNDP's account of April 2026.

Last twelve months. In the first quarter of 2026 Santa Luzia became the first civil registry office in the country to operate entirely digitally. In June 2026 a mobile caravan was to take birth registration on SIREC to 16 communities in Cacheu, Oio, Bafatá and Gabu.

Local offices doing the digital entry. UNDP names one office, Santa Luzia, as entirely digital in April 2026. UNICEF said in September 2025 that digital registration is available in hospitals and health centres of Bissau, Tombali and Quinara. The statistics office counts 13 conservatórias and 80 registry posts in its yearbook for 2018 to 2022.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation of civil registry books, over 2.4 million records in 11,745 registers by April 2026, and GESTDOC. Certificate: requests for birth, marriage and death entries from any location, announced in June 2025. Outreach: the mobile operations the country calls Candonga di Justiça, the Registo Móvel vehicle, birth registration in vaccination campaigns and birth registration at school enrolment. Identity: the biometric national identity card production centres, the ECOWAS biometric identity card, the planned legal framework for digital identity and authentication platform, and the biometric registration kits and biometric passport system. Enabling: national connectivity, with no national backbone in August 2025. Exchange: the planned interoperability of SIREC with the identity card, voter register, social protection and judiciary, the unique citizen identification number SIREC is to manage, the planned digital platform supporting voter registration, health workers registering on the registry's behalf, the national statistical report on civil registration, and RapidPro and INNOSTATS for vital statistics.

Not held. The number of offices and posts entering records digitally, against the country's total, is not held. Nor is the platform used at the health-service posts, or whether it is connected to the national register.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2024 student project states that police stations have no system for recording occurrences; how stations record is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A steering committee of several entities oversees the centre for collecting and processing criminal information that Guinea-Bissau calls SIPAO, and INTERPOL accompanies it and the European Union finances it, the Minister of Justice and Human Rights said on 17 April 2023.

Records made digitally. Guinea-Bissau does not have a system for recording criminal occurrences in police stations, the author of an undergraduate final project at IFBA in Brazil stated in August 2024.

How the record is made. The author gave that absence as the first reason for building a prototype, a web application tested in 2024 by ten users of Guinean nationality.

Last twelve months. INTERPOL described the West African Police Information System on 12 September 2025 as strengthening information exchange and coordination among the region's law enforcement agencies.

Stations doing the digital entry. A new SIPAO service beside the Guarda Nacional command in Bissau was described in August 2024 as a centre where all police forces work and share information. The centre's director named extension to all law-enforcement institutions as the main challenge still ahead in 2024.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the criminal register at Buba, digitised in a test, and the fingerprint identification system for which Guinea-Bissau was to be prepared, as at 2022. Public: the Polícia Judiciária's pj.gw online services portal and the justice portal for online criminal record applications, planned in 2023. Enabling: the Model Police Station at Sintchan Tombom, Gabú, opened in 2024.

Not held. How stations record is not held, and the statement that no station system exists is one author's, with no source cited. No station is named as entering its cases at the criminal-information centre in Bissau, and nothing is held on a plan, on the last twelve months or on stations.
