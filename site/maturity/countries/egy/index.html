Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Egypt adopted a Cloud First Policy in 2024, in force since August 2024; where public bodies must host under it is not known.

Policy adopted. The Supreme Council of Digital Society adopted the Cloud First Policy on 13 March 2024; it requires data classified top secret or secret to be hosted only within Egypt. The policy, with that requirement, entered into force on 22 August 2024. The Cabinet's spokesperson said the council, at its first meeting after being reconstituted under the Prime Minister, approved and launched the cloud computing policy in line with the Digital Egypt strategy.

Named hosting and moves. The ICT ministry is preparing a national data-centre strategy with other ministries and bodies, to expand data centres and their capacity inside Egypt, the minister told a cabinet meeting on 20 September 2026. He said on 8 September that it covers storage and compute needs, investment, regulation, standards, skills and digital sovereignty, and in July called it a core component of digital sovereignty.

Noted, not assessed. National data centre strategy.

Not held. No source held says the government has named where public bodies must host: a government cloud or data centre the policy tells them to use, or a list of approved providers.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Egypt's personal data protection law dates from 2020; its authority has issued guidance, but whether any decision has been made under it is not known.

Authority. The supervisory authority is the Personal Data Protection Center, which the executive regulations confirm as responsible for implementation, monitoring and enforcement. In November 2025 it was run by an acting chief executive, Soussan El-Okbawy, who chaired a session at Cairo ICT on 16 November.

Standing. Egypt's general law is the Personal Data Protection Law, Law No. 151 of 2020, which the Cairo Review treated in June 2025 as the country's operating data protection law. Access Partnership said in February 2026 that enforcement is expected from 1 November 2026, the end of a one-year grace period.

Regulations. The executive regulations were still pending in February 2025. They were issued by ministerial decree on 1 November 2025 as Executive Regulations No. 816/2025.

Application. In January and February 2026 the Personal Data Protection Center published guidelines on consent, lawful bases, direct marketing, data protection officers, records of processing, licences and permits and privacy notices, with templates for notifying a breach.

Last twelve months. The executive regulations were issued on 1 November 2025, and the Center's guidelines followed in January and February 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No sanction, order or decision on a named controller under the law, and no published count of cases settled. The date the law came into force is not held either.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2018 law on information technology crimes is in force; two bills in April 2026 would amend it. No use of it is known.

Law in force. Egypt's Law 175 of 2018 on combating information technology crimes is treated as the current law in a bill an MP put forward on 22 April 2026, which would tighten its provisions. A separate bill from an MP, referred to committees by the House speaker, also amends Law 175, which its sponsor calls the current law, with penalties too weak.

Use of the law. The nearest fact is amendment, not enforcement. Both bills take Law 175 of 2018 as the baseline to be changed, one to tighten its provisions and one, according to its sponsor, because the penalties are too weak. Both were reported on the same day, 22 April 2026, by Egyptian outlets El Watan News and El Balad. The House speaker referred the second bill to committees in April 2026, and the law remains the current text while it is amended.

Noted, not assessed. Egypt National Cybersecurity Strategy 2023-2027.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Egypt's Law 15/2004 on e-signatures is in force and has executive regulations, per 2025 legal guides; no dated licence or penalty is known.

Law in force. Egypt's Law No. 15/2004 regulating e-signatures establishes ITIDA, and its confidentiality duties bind licensed certification authorities, as an ICLG chapter written by a law firm lists it in July 2025. A Chambers guide of March 2025 also cites the law as part of Egypt's framework and states what it requires of applicants for e-signature services. Biometric Update states that digitally signed contracts are legally binding in Egypt.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The law has executive regulations. The ICLG chapter states that they require applicants for e-signature services to implement systems securing the confidentiality of information, and the Chambers guide states that applicants must keep a secure system preserving the confidentiality of customers' data. Both statements date from 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Digified eKYC and e-contracts platform; منظومة الهوية الرقمية / التحقق البيومتري e-KYC; منظومة التحقق البيومتري الإلكتروني (الهوية الرقمية); electronic biometric verification system; Digital Trust Services (e-signature and e-seal); Digital Egypt platform.

Law in force. Egypt's statistics law is Law No. 35 of 1960 on statistics and censuses, issued at the Presidency on 9 February 1960 and amended by Law No. 28 of 1982. The statistics agency publishes it as the law that regulates statistical work.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The nearest held fact is the agency's own publication of the law. The agency presents Law 35 of 1960, as amended in 1982, as the law regulating statistical work. The text is the one source held for Egypt. It gives the law's date of issue and its one amendment, twenty-two years later, and it shows the agency treating a law of 1960 as its governing text. The law is the oldest statistics law in force among the countries assessed. No decree, regulation or order made under it is named in what is held, and no bill to replace it.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2024 one governorate was the pilot for linking districts and local units; whether any local unit office is connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Egypt's project to automate local administration and governorate headquarters, as its minister of communications named it on 15 January 2024, is carried out by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology with the Ministry of Local Development and the governors. It sits within the state's institutional digital transformation project.

What the office does over it. The project is to start at governorate headquarters, move to governorate capitals and districts, and end with local units.

How the office is connected. The minister expected the Minya governorate headquarters to be linked electronically to the governorate's districts and local units during 2024.

Last twelve months. The minister described the project and named its pilot on 15 January 2024.

Local governments connected. Minya was chosen as the pilot for the first digital governorate headquarters, with the rest of local administration to follow it.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: technology devices for the government service complexes of Decent Life villages. Web: the Digital Egypt platform; the public shops licensing service; the national regularisation system; the National Open Data Portal; Cairo e-government services. City systems: the Cairo CCTV surveillance network, the integrated command and control centre, intelligent transport systems and the New Administrative Capital smart city. Backbone: the closed government network; the government digital switch; the automation of the technological infrastructure of the administrative body; the fibre connection of government buildings; the national information infrastructure project; fibre to the home in Decent Life villages. Capacity: the training of public employees in ministries, governorates and affiliated directorates; the Digital Decent Life training of governorate staff. Upper tier: the governorates' information systems and digital transformation units, and the project to standardise digital applications and systems across governorates. Central: the electronic birth and death registration system; the Decent Life service complexes and the Government Services Card; the postal offices developed into integrated service centres; the Egyptian Tax Authority electronic system; the National Property Identification System.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are entered at district level into the national health information system (2025); routine clinic reporting is not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. Egypt's answers to the WHO EMRO civil registration questionnaire, published in 2025, describe the Ministry of Health's national health information system, into which birth and death notifications are processed.

Tiers in use. On those answers, notifications of births and deaths made at health offices go as copies to the health department at district level, and are then reviewed at directorate and central level.

Where data is digitised. The district health department processes the notifications electronically into the national system, on the answers published in 2025; the health offices below it send copies. From the directorates to the central level the work is electronic.

Last twelve months. The answers published in 2025 state that automation of the health offices is under implementation.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The same answers report a pilot of electronic registration at health-office level in some governorates, the level at which births and deaths are first notified.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2, as part of the national health information system. Enabling: the Egypt Healthcare Authority's telemedicine and remote diagnosis and the telemedicine and telehealth solutions planned in the National Health Strategy 2024-2030.

Not held. Nothing held shows clinics' routine reports of patients treated entering the system: the one source describes birth and death notifications only, and the latest year it cites is 2011. No tier, clinic share, financing or event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Electronic health records are at an early pilot stage (government compact, December 2025); no count of hospitals or clinics is given.

Governance, planning, finance. The Egypt Healthcare Authority runs a unified electronic medical record, as it said on 19 August 2024. The National Health Strategy 2024-2030 names a unified electronic medical record among its key actions, with hospitals of all providers and all primary care units to be automated to support it. The health ministry disclosed neither the cost nor the financing of the platform it proposed in September 2026.

Tiers in use. The Authority said on 19 August 2024 that, in the governorates where Universal Health Insurance applies, its hospitals work on a fully computerised system, its primary healthcare centres and units are fully digitised, and referral from them to higher levels is electronic. CIPIT's report of June 2025 states that Egypt has deployed electronic health record systems in public hospitals.

Where data is digitised. A Cairo University study of Omrania family health centre in Giza, observed from September 2016 to December 2019, found patient records kept as paper family files, fetched by a clerk and handed to the physician.

Last twelve months. The health minister discussed a proposed "Egypt Smart Healthcare Platform" with MTS and Inspur, to hold unified patient records shared between facilities, and a pilot for its first phase, as reported on 14 September 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The government's National Health Compact of 6 December 2025 describes electronic health records in Egypt as at the stage of early pilots.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2, as part of the national health information system. Enabling: the Egypt Healthcare Authority's telemedicine and remote diagnosis and the telemedicine and telehealth solutions planned in the National Health Strategy 2024-2030.

Not held. No count of hospitals or clinics with a denominator is held, nor the number of governorates the Authority's record covers, and its 2024 statement is not reconciled with the compact's early pilots of 2025. No dated fact after 2019 shows where the record is entered, and the September 2026 proposal carries no timetable.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

Governance, planning, finance. Law 143 of 1994 gives the Civil Status Organization of the Ministry of Interior the registration of births and deaths.

Events registered digitally. Egypt's presentation to the 2026 APAI-CRVS registrars-general meeting states that a central database for births and deaths runs on a secure platform and directly updates the statistics office's population clock.

How the record is made. A conference paper on the system implemented in 2017 states that data entry is done at the health office, which prints the birth or death certificate once entry is complete, with the offices linked online to a central database. The 2026 presentation describes automated health offices feeding the central database.

Last twelve months. An article of 20 January 2026 reports that the health ministry instructed all health affairs directorates to unify and improve the quality of data entered on the automated births and deaths registration system at health offices.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The 2026 presentation gives full nationwide automation of 4,571 health offices across the 27 governorates. The planning ministry's report of 2 August 2025 says 4,666 health offices are electronically linked.

Identity: the National ID System, the electronic biometric verification system, the unified digital identity project, the digital identity application, MOIEG-PASS, the central bank's Haweya, the biometric eKYC system for SIM registration, the Arqamy service of the My NTRA application, the Secured and Smart Documents Complex, the registration and ID card for foreign residents and the Financial Regulatory Authority's Digital Identification Process. Exchange: the interoperability of the births and deaths database, the health ministry's electronic notification to the civil registration authority, the integration platform with the Civil Status Organisation, the government digital switch, the integration of judgment data with the civil status system and the national ID database's links to external parties.
