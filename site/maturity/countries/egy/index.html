Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2024 one governorate was the pilot for linking districts and local units; whether any local unit office is connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Egypt's project to automate local administration and governorate headquarters, as its minister of communications named it on 15 January 2024, is carried out by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology with the Ministry of Local Development and the governors. It sits within the state's institutional digital transformation project.

What the office does over it. The project is to start at governorate headquarters, move to governorate capitals and districts, and end with local units.

How the office is connected. The minister expected the Minya governorate headquarters to be linked electronically to the governorate's districts and local units during 2024.

Last twelve months. The minister described the project and named its pilot on 15 January 2024.

Local governments connected. Minya was chosen as the pilot for the first digital governorate headquarters, with the rest of local administration to follow it.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: technology devices for the government service complexes of Decent Life villages. Applications: the state private land database a 2026 decree requires of each administrative body; local units' information and asset management systems for public land. Web: the Digital Egypt platform; the public shops licensing service; the national regularisation system; the National Open Data Portal; Cairo e-government services. City systems: the Cairo CCTV surveillance network, the integrated command and control centre, intelligent transport systems and the New Administrative Capital smart city. Backbone: the closed government network; the government digital switch; the automation of the technological infrastructure of the administrative body; the fibre connection of government buildings; the national information infrastructure project; fibre to the home in Decent Life villages. Capacity: the training of public employees in ministries, governorates and affiliated directorates; the Digital Decent Life training of governorate staff. Upper tier: the governorates' information systems and digital transformation units, and the project to standardise digital applications and systems across governorates. Central: the electronic birth and death registration system; the Decent Life service complexes and the Government Services Card; the postal offices developed into integrated service centres; the Egyptian Tax Authority electronic system; the National Property Identification System.

Not held. Whether any governorate headquarters, district or local unit office is connected or works over a connection, and whether the Minya link expected for 2024 was made. What the offices would do over it, the kind of connection, a plan, a funder and any event in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are entered at district level into the national health information system (2025); routine clinic reporting is not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. Egypt's answers to the WHO EMRO civil registration questionnaire, published in 2025, describe the Ministry of Health's national health information system, into which birth and death notifications are processed.

Tiers in use. On those answers, notifications of births and deaths made at health offices go as copies to the health department at district level, and are then reviewed at directorate and central level.

Where data is digitised. The district health department processes the notifications electronically into the national system, on the answers published in 2025; the health offices below it send copies. From the directorates to the central level the work is electronic.

Last twelve months. The answers published in 2025 state that automation of the health offices is under implementation.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The same answers report a pilot of electronic registration at health-office level in some governorates, the level at which births and deaths are first notified.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2, as part of the national health information system. Enabling: the Egypt Healthcare Authority's telemedicine and remote diagnosis and the telemedicine and telehealth solutions planned in the National Health Strategy 2024-2030.

Not held. Nothing held shows clinics' routine reports of patients treated entering the system: the one source describes birth and death notifications only, and the latest year it cites is 2011. No tier, clinic share, financing or event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Electronic health records are at an early pilot stage (government compact, December 2025); no count of hospitals or clinics is given.

Governance, planning, finance. The Egypt Healthcare Authority runs a unified electronic medical record, as it said on 19 August 2024. The National Health Strategy 2024-2030 names a unified electronic medical record among its key actions, with hospitals of all providers and all primary care units to be automated to support it. The health ministry disclosed neither the cost nor the financing of the platform it proposed in September 2026.

Tiers in use. The Authority said on 19 August 2024 that, in the governorates where Universal Health Insurance applies, its hospitals work on a fully computerised system, its primary healthcare centres and units are fully digitised, and referral from them to higher levels is electronic. CIPIT's report of June 2025 states that Egypt has deployed electronic health record systems in public hospitals.

Where data is digitised. A Cairo University study of Omrania family health centre in Giza, observed from September 2016 to December 2019, found patient records kept as paper family files, fetched by a clerk and handed to the physician.

Last twelve months. The health minister discussed a proposed "Egypt Smart Healthcare Platform" with MTS and Inspur, to hold unified patient records shared between facilities, and a pilot for its first phase, as reported on 14 September 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The government's National Health Compact of 6 December 2025 describes electronic health records in Egypt as at the stage of early pilots.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2, as part of the national health information system. Enabling: the Egypt Healthcare Authority's telemedicine and remote diagnosis and the telemedicine and telehealth solutions planned in the National Health Strategy 2024-2030.

Not held. No count of hospitals or clinics with a denominator is held, nor the number of governorates the Authority's record covers, and its 2024 statement is not reconciled with the compact's early pilots of 2025. No dated fact after 2019 shows where the record is entered, and the September 2026 proposal carries no timetable.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry's 2023-2027 plan is to build a per-pupil record; whether primary schools use one is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The ministry calls its yearly count of students, teachers, classrooms and schools its EMIS, the source of the statistical yearbooks; the same 2023-2027 plan names planned online enrolment application platforms, the Egyptian Knowledge Bank and learning management system, and school internet and computers. Also noted are the yearly verified national education dataset and the smart school criteria; the Kirio platform; the Takaful and Karama cash transfer programme's management information system, which holds school attendance monitoring information on beneficiary children; and the Digital Content Dashboard and the tablet-based assessment platform for grades 10 to 12.

Not held. At which levels a system of pupil records is in use, how the record is kept and how many primary schools enter data. How the planned system is financed is not stated, and nothing dated in the past twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are entered digitally at 4,571 health offices, as stated in 2026; the share of all offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The development plan for fiscal year 2026/27, as Youm7 reported it on 2 August 2026, credits the Ministry of Health and Population and the bodies concerned with developing the electronic birth and death registration system and names it within the digital-transformation axis of the national family development project. Law 143 of 1994 gives the Civil Status Organization of the Ministry of Interior the registration of births and deaths.

Events registered digitally. Egypt's presentation to the 2026 APAI-CRVS registrars-general meeting states that a central database for births and deaths runs on a secure platform and directly updates the statistics office's population clock.

How the record is made. A conference paper on the system implemented in 2017 states that data entry is done at the health office, which prints the birth or death certificate once entry is complete, with the offices linked online to a central database. The 2026 presentation describes automated health offices feeding the central database.

Last twelve months. An article of 20 January 2026 reports that the health ministry instructed all health affairs directorates to unify and improve the quality of data entered on the automated births and deaths registration system at health offices.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The 2026 presentation gives full nationwide automation of 4,571 health offices across the 27 governorates. The planning ministry's report of 2 August 2025 says 4,666 health offices are electronically linked.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the electronic backups of birth and death records at the Ministry of Health and the Civil Status Authority. Notification: the unified death notification form and the health ministry's national health information system. Certificate: the interior ministry's smart self-service machines for civil status documents, its equipped vehicles serving as mobile civil status offices and model civil status centres, the automated issuing stations of Law 143 of 1994 and the Digital Egypt platform. Identity: the National ID System, the electronic biometric verification system, the unified digital identity project, the digital identity application, MOIEG-PASS, the central bank's Haweya, the biometric eKYC system for SIM registration, the Arqamy service of the My NTRA application, the Secured and Smart Documents Complex, the registration and ID card for foreign residents and the Financial Regulatory Authority's Digital Identification Process. Enabling: the technology devices for Decent Life government service complexes, the Government Service Centers and the fibre optic network to Decent Life villages. Exchange: the interoperability of the births and deaths database, the health ministry's electronic notification to the civil registration authority, the integration platform with the Civil Status Organisation, the government digital switch, the integration of judgment data with the civil status system and the national ID database's links to external parties.

Not held. No total of health offices is held against the 4,571 or the 4,666, and no rural share is published. Egypt's undated answers to a WHO questionnaire published in 2025 describe paper at registration points and electronic entry at the governorate centre, which disagrees with the 2026 account.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A rights group said in 2021 that stations often lack the means to type reports; digital recording is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the interior ministry's biometric verification and authentication platform, in trial operation on 14 February 2026, the mobile technology centres issuing the criminal status certificate, ten of them handed over by 18 January 2026, the cabinet's unified government complaints system and the Public Prosecution e-portal. Surveillance: the Cairo CCTV network and the city's integrated command and control centre, with the emergency number 112. Enabling: the communications ministry's digital training for the law enforcement system, which had reached 13,493 people by the end of 2023. Identification: the Ministry of Interior's criminal records system. Statistics: the Public Prosecution's digital reporting and statistics system, named in December 2025.

Not held. The one statement on stations, from January 2021, says they often lack the technology to write reports on a computer; it names no system, no place and no count of stations, and does not say that stations record on paper only. Who would own a station system, and anything from the last twelve months, is not held.
