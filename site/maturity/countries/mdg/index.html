Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Madagascar has the Plan Stratégique Numérique 2023-2028, presented in 2024 with a USD 800 million estimate, but its adoption and public text are not known.

Governance and finance. UNDP says the Plan Stratégique Numérique 2023-2028 is coordinated by the digital ministry, supported by the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale attached to the Presidency, and the ministry estimates its budget at USD 800.169 million, of which USD 152.334 million is available.

Standing. Newsmada reports the digital minister presented the plan on 19 January 2024, and UNDP calls it formalised and in implementation in December 2025. A national digital roadmap is in preparation after the Digital Readiness Assessment report was handed over on 17 December 2025.

Text. 2424.mg reports the ministry delivered a national Plan stratégique du numérique 2023-2028 on 26 October 2023.

Period. UNDP names the plan as the national digital strategy for 2023-2028.

Delivery plan. The ministry costs implementation at USD 800.169 million, with USD 647.835 million still to find, across four axes: legal framework, telecommunications, human capital, and digital and financial inclusion.

Reporting. UNDP says the plan is in implementation, in a consolidation phase.

Last twelve months. Tia Tanindrazana wrote on 8 September 2026 that the plan aims to raise the digital sector to 6% of GDP by 2028.

Noted, not assessed. Inclusive Digitalisation for East and Southern Africa (IDEA) Programme, PRODIGY, DECIM, Programme national de numérisation, national digitalization strategy of the technical and vocational education sector, programme to train 6,000 people in emerging digital professions, the government's first digital transformation roadmap.

Standing. A JICA study of March 2022 describes the 2004 policy as the Malagasy government's main ICT policy.

Text. The JICA study reports the policy as setting seven strategic objectives.

Last twelve months. The latest account of the policy is JICA's of March 2022.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Madagascar has a data protection law of 2015 and an authority seated in 2025; whether the law has been applied is not known.

Authority. Article 28 of the law institutes the Commission Malagasy de l'Informatique et des Libertés (CMIL) as an independent authority of nine members named by decree in the Council of Ministers. Seven members were sworn in at the Cour suprême on 4 August 2025, and the commission elected a president and two vice-presidents on 14 August 2025.

Standing. Loi n° 2014-038 on the protection of personal data was adopted by the Assemblée nationale on 16 December 2014 and promulgated on 9 January 2015. The commission's procedures manual, approved in October 2025, takes the law as its legal basis.

Regulations. Décret n°2023-1541 of 6 December 2023 sets the commission's attributions, organisation and operation and governs the processing of personal data; Décret n°2025-696 of 18 June 2025 amended it. The commission's report of January 2026 cites a significant delay in adopting the law's application decrees.

Application. The commission reported in January 2026 that no processing declaration had been registered, that citizens' complaints had gone untreated for ten years and that it had neither premises nor staff. Its manual sets procedures for declarations, authorisations, audits, complaints and administrative sanctions, with a decision on a complaint due within 30 working days.

Last twelve months. The commission approved its first procedures manual on 17 October 2025, published on 3 December 2025. It joined the AFAPDP on 24 November 2025 and obtained a budget line of Ariary 2,411,579,000 in the 2026 Finance Law.

Noted, not assessed. The African Union's Malabo Convention, ratified by Madagascar in 2024; Loi n°2024-004 of 21 June 2024, authorising that ratification.

Not held. No decision, sanction or count of cases under the law. The date the law came into force is not stated.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2014 cybercrime law is in force; a replacement bill was postponed in September 2026. No use of the law is known.

Law in force. Madagascar's cybercrime framework still rests in part on loi n°2014-006, adopted in June 2014, which defined offences such as fraudulent access to information systems and data breaches. The government tabled bill n°042/2026 on combating cybercrime at the National Assembly on 28 August 2026, with penalties of two to ten years and fines of 100,000 to 100 million ariary. The Council of Ministers had adopted the draft bill recasting the law on 7 July 2026. On 4 September deputies voted to postpone adoption of the 96-article text, and it was not among the 17 bills on the agenda of the extraordinary session opening 17 September 2026.

Use of the law. The 2026 bill would create the Upin digital police. A digital-law specialist interviewed by Midi Madagasikara says it would require suspicious-transaction reports on Mobile Money transfers of 10 million ariary or more and tighten SIM identification.

Noted, not assessed. Unité de protection et d'investigation numérique (Upin), CIRT Madagascar, UN Convention against Cybercrime.

Not held. No arrest, charge, prosecution, conviction or penalty under the law in the last three years is held.

Law or rules in force. Madagascar's Loi n°2016-056 on electronic money and electronic money institutions was adopted by both chambers in December 2016, cleared by the High Constitutional Court on 27 January 2017 and promulgated on 2 February 2017. The banking supervision commission, the CSBF, applies it: it issues instructions and audits the institutions.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Madagascar has had electronic transactions and signature laws since 2014, and a 2025 draft decree on electronic signature is not known to be adopted.

Law in force. Madagascar has a law on electronic transactions and a law on electronic signature, both dated 10 December 2014 and cited by a presidential decree as the legal basis it is made under. The transactions law, Loi no. 2014-024, has 35 articles in four parts, defines electronic signature and regulates e-commerce. Newsmada describes the signature law as the legal equivalent of the handwritten signature. Decree 2024-1911, published on 13 November 2024, gives documents signed through a certified electronic signature device the same recognition as paper.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The 2024 decree is the nearest rule held that is made under the laws, and its Article 10 on certified electronic signatures is the part that applies them. In September 2025 Newsmada reported that a draft decree on electronic signature and interoperability was in preparation, and that the ministry held a validation workshop on the regulatory framework on 13 September 2025. The evidence does not say the draft decree has since been adopted.

Noted, not assessed. X-Road; Orinasa.

Not held. No source held names a decree, regulation or order made under the law, other than the 2024 decree, and none says the draft decree has been adopted.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

About 10 per cent of communes had a secure connection to a State finance system, on a 2024 plan; the figure's year is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Plan National de Décentralisation Emergente 2023-2030, validated by decree on 2 August 2023, makes local governments' secure connection to the State's strategic information systems a product, with the ministries of decentralisation, digital development and finance responsible. It names no source of funds for the product's 23,092,000 thousand ariary. The April 2025 handover was made through PRODUIR, which the World Bank finances.

What the office does over it. The plan's connection indicator is communes with direct access to the Système d'Information Financière Communal (SIFC), a State information system. In December 2025, 336 communes were using the digital civil registration system SIECM. Communes of Greater Antananarivo use the ministry's online building-permit tool.

How the office is connected. The plan measures communes having a secure connection to the SIFC, and costs annual connection fees for 1,000 communes over three years. On 30 April 2025 the decentralisation ministry handed internet boxes and a subscription to all 38 communes of the Antananarivo agglomeration.

Last twelve months. The number of communes using SIECM rose from 75 to 336 in the six months to December 2025.

Local governments connected. The plan, published in June 2024, gives a baseline of 10 per cent of the country's 1,695 communes with direct access to the SIFC over a secure connection, sourced to the finance ministry, with a target of 50 per cent for 2030. The World Bank puts the 336 communes of December 2025 at 40 per cent of the project's target.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the computers, printers and scanners handed to the 38 communes of the Antananarivo agglomeration on 30 April 2025, the Lalankely III handover of computers to 28 peripheral communes of Greater Antananarivo on 3 July 2026, the physical servers handed to the territorial authorities of Anosy, Androy and Atsimo Andrefana in June 2026, the Smart Village computer equipment for 11 local entities in Androy and Anosy, the PRODIGY equipment kit planned in June 2020 for the civil registration offices of all 1,695 communes, the package of digital services and equipment for selected communes under the PRODIGY credit of 8 October 2020 and the decentralisation plan's computer, printer, inverter and solar kit for each local government. Applications: SIECM and the digitisation and indexing of civil registers, 2.9 million records digitised by December 2025; the Plateforme d'Enregistrement Systématique des Naissances, operational in several communes in March 2026; the PRODIGY birth module named by the Cour des comptes; the EC-MADA digitisation of nearly 10 million civil registration records; the register of the Centre National d'Etat Civil et de l'Identité, fed by the communes' civil-status services under the decree of 2 December 2020; the Système Informatisé de l'Etat Civil Malagasy of the 2018 civil status law; biometric registration at commune civil registries, a priority stated on 15 September 2026; e-Fokontany, the household booklets and database kept below the commune; MATAC/Hanorina; the e-Zaha platform, with data collected at commune level; the Guichets Fonciers, commune land offices in 524 communes surveyed from February to May 2026; the Plan Local d'Occupation Foncière, used for land certification in 49 communes; the decentralisation plan's SIFC, SALOHY revenue software, digital civil status centre and web platform of available resources; and the Antananarivo urban commune's digitalisation of its markets with merchant cards. Public access: the Smart Village community digital centres, six sites operational in Androy and Anosy in February 2026. Backbone: the temporary data centre at Iavoloha, in service by December 2025, the Data Center Secondaire at Iavoloha and the Data Center Principal at Tsimbazaza, both with fit-out works planned in August 2025, Telma's fibre-optic backbone of more than 10,000 km as at 2019 and its metropolitan fibre network in Antananarivo as at January 2009. Capacity: the digital literacy training of 940 communal officials in the three southern regions and the MATAC 2 training of communes. Central: the Izaho Tokana mass biometric enrolment, run by mobile teams since April 2026, and its identity management system, IDMS; the Orinasa business-creation platform; Mahatoky, a tool for post office staff; the Registre Social Unique; the finance ministry's SIGRNF for non-tax revenue, launched on 9 May 2025; the SIIGFP public finance system and the GasyNet customs network as at January 2009; and the decentralisation plan's SIGOB monitoring system and the State's AUGURE human resources application.

Not held. The 10 per cent baseline carries no year and is marked to be verified in the plan, and where those communes are is not stated. How the 336 communes using SIECM are connected is not stated, their count is given against a project target and not the national total, and the 38 communes handed a subscription are all in the capital's agglomeration.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The 2023 plan states that an electronic monthly report keyed directly at health facilities has been piloted since 2021 and is available in 10 pilot districts, with scale-up being planned.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Communes register births digitally, 336 of them in December 2025; the share of all communes this represents is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A decree of 2 December 2020 set up the Centre National d'État Civil et de l'Identité under the technical supervision of the Interior ministry to keep the national digital civil status and identity registers. The digital registration system was rolled out under the PRODIGY project, financed by an IDA credit of US$133 million and a GFF grant of US$3 million and closing on 30 June 2026. The national decentralisation plan to 2030, published in June 2024, carries the share of communes with a functional digital civil-status centre as an indicator.

Events registered digitally. By December 2025 61,892 birth certificates carrying a unique identity number had been issued through the digital civil registration system, which Madagascar calls SIECM.

How the record is made. Communes themselves use SIECM to register civil-status events and issue birth certificates with a national unique number, the World Bank reported in December 2025; the system's offline module had been in demonstration since November 2025.

Last twelve months. Communes using SIECM rose from 75 to 336 in the six months to December 2025. In March 2026 the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale described the birth registration platform as being deployed progressively at national scale.

Local offices doing the digital entry. In December 2025 336 communes were using SIECM, which the World Bank gives as 40% of the project's target; a further 112 communes had been trained and were not yet operational.

Noted, not assessed. Back-capture: the EC-MADA digitisation of civil registration records and PRODIGY's digitisation and indexing of civil status acts. Notification: the digitalisation of notifications from health centres and health workers and the declaration of births and deaths by basic health centres and matrones. Outreach: Fitsarana mitety vohitra and the Ezaka Kopia ho an'ny Ankizy programme. Identity: the Izaho Tokana biometric enrolment, the identity management system IDMS and the biometric national identity card, e-Fokontany and the registre national numérique de la population. Enabling: Smart Village, DECIM and the IT kits planned for commune civil status offices. Exchange: the government interoperability platform and the interoperability between the Justice Department, the Civil Registry and other public services.
