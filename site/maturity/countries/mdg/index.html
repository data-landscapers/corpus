Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

About 10 per cent of communes had a secure connection to a State finance system, on a 2024 plan; the figure's year is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Plan National de Décentralisation Emergente 2023-2030, validated by decree on 2 August 2023, makes local governments' secure connection to the State's strategic information systems a product, with the ministries of decentralisation, digital development and finance responsible. It names no source of funds for the product's 23,092,000 thousand ariary. The April 2025 handover was made through PRODUIR, which the World Bank finances.

What the office does over it. The plan's connection indicator is communes with direct access to the Système d'Information Financière Communal (SIFC), a State information system. In December 2025, 336 communes were using the digital civil registration system SIECM. Communes of Greater Antananarivo use the ministry's online building-permit tool.

How the office is connected. The plan measures communes having a secure connection to the SIFC, and costs annual connection fees for 1,000 communes over three years. On 30 April 2025 the decentralisation ministry handed internet boxes and a subscription to all 38 communes of the Antananarivo agglomeration.

Last twelve months. The number of communes using SIECM rose from 75 to 336 in the six months to December 2025.

Local governments connected. The plan, published in June 2024, gives a baseline of 10 per cent of the country's 1,695 communes with direct access to the SIFC over a secure connection, sourced to the finance ministry, with a target of 50 per cent for 2030. The World Bank puts the 336 communes of December 2025 at 40 per cent of the project's target.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the computers, printers and scanners handed to the 38 communes of the Antananarivo agglomeration on 30 April 2025, the Lalankely III handover of computers to 28 peripheral communes of Greater Antananarivo on 3 July 2026, the physical servers handed to the territorial authorities of Anosy, Androy and Atsimo Andrefana in June 2026, the Smart Village computer equipment for 11 local entities in Androy and Anosy, the PRODIGY equipment kit planned in June 2020 for the civil registration offices of all 1,695 communes, the package of digital services and equipment for selected communes under the PRODIGY credit of 8 October 2020 and the decentralisation plan's computer, printer, inverter and solar kit for each local government. Applications: SIECM and the digitisation and indexing of civil registers, 2.9 million records digitised by December 2025; the Plateforme d'Enregistrement Systématique des Naissances, operational in several communes in March 2026; the PRODIGY birth module named by the Cour des comptes; the EC-MADA digitisation of nearly 10 million civil registration records; the register of the Centre National d'Etat Civil et de l'Identité, fed by the communes' civil-status services under the decree of 2 December 2020; the Système Informatisé de l'Etat Civil Malagasy of the 2018 civil status law; biometric registration at commune civil registries, a priority stated on 15 September 2026; e-Fokontany, the household booklets and database kept below the commune; MATAC/Hanorina; the e-Zaha platform, with data collected at commune level; the Guichets Fonciers, commune land offices in 524 communes surveyed from February to May 2026; the Plan Local d'Occupation Foncière, used for land certification in 49 communes; the decentralisation plan's SIFC, SALOHY revenue software, digital civil status centre and web platform of available resources; and the Antananarivo urban commune's digitalisation of its markets with merchant cards. Public access: the Smart Village community digital centres, six sites operational in Androy and Anosy in February 2026. Backbone: the temporary data centre at Iavoloha, in service by December 2025, the Data Center Secondaire at Iavoloha and the Data Center Principal at Tsimbazaza, both with fit-out works planned in August 2025, Telma's fibre-optic backbone of more than 10,000 km as at 2019 and its metropolitan fibre network in Antananarivo as at January 2009. Capacity: the digital literacy training of 940 communal officials in the three southern regions and the MATAC 2 training of communes. Central: the Izaho Tokana mass biometric enrolment, run by mobile teams since April 2026, and its identity management system, IDMS; the Orinasa business-creation platform; Mahatoky, a tool for post office staff; the Registre Social Unique; the finance ministry's SIGRNF for non-tax revenue, launched on 9 May 2025; the SIIGFP public finance system and the GasyNet customs network as at January 2009; and the decentralisation plan's SIGOB monitoring system and the State's AUGURE human resources application.

Not held. The 10 per cent baseline carries no year and is marked to be verified in the plan, and where those communes are is not stated. How the 336 communes using SIECM are connected is not stated, their count is given against a project target and not the national total, and the 38 communes handed a subscription are all in the capital's agglomeration.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Nothing dated by 30 September 2026 shows a commune keeping its revenue record digitally, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Ministry of Economy and Finance runs SIGRNF, its information system for the State's non-tax revenues. The tax directorate's SAFI, the Système d'Administration Fiscale Intégré, manages State taxes and local taxes. The national decentralisation plan provides for connecting communes to SIFC, the State's Système d'Information Financière des Communes.

Not held. Nothing dated on or before 30 September 2026 shows a commune recording its payers or their payments in a system.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health centres in all 114 districts report into the national system, with district offices entering their paper forms (2023).

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's plan for its health information system, the PSRSIS, assigns routine data to the ministry's DEPSI directorate and records that the ministry adopted DHIS2 as its data warehouse under the 2018-2022 plan. The plan runs from 2023 to 2027, carries a costed budget by strategy and names DHIS2 throughout.

Tiers in use. The plan states that DHIS2 was scaled to all 22 regions and 114 districts in January 2019 for the basic health centres' monthly activity report, and that the district hospitals' and community reports were integrated from July 2020. What the plan calls the CHU/CHRR/ES report was last updated in 2011, and DHIS2 is only to be brought forward for it.

Where data is digitised. As at the 2023 plan, the basic health centres' data go up to the districts on the integrated monthly report in paper form, which is keyed into the DHIS2 Routine platform.

Last twelve months. WHO Madagascar lists among the key actions of 2024-2025 an audit of the DHIS2 platform and secured cloud hosting, the deployment of electronic registers including the monthly activity report, and the distribution of tablets with DHIS2 training.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The 2023 plan states that an electronic monthly report keyed directly at health facilities has been piloted since 2021 and is available in 10 pilot districts, with scale-up being planned.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2 Tracker for vaccination. Single-function: the DHIS2 indicators for antimicrobial resistance surveillance, Vigimobile, the DHIS2-MTN module, the Geographic Tracking System, e-Surv, the Surveillance Épidémiologique Intégrée et Électronique, the Système d'Information de Gestion Logistique, the AMR Data Center, EIOS, the Système Intégré d'Information des Dotations CSB, the Malaria Routine Data Quality Assessment tool, DHIS2 Programme, DHIS2 Ressources, SYGMA, VIDA, HNQIS, ODK, AUGURE and CHANNEL. Community: CommCare. Enabling: the Smart Village programme, DECIM connectivity, Workflow for DHIS2 user accounts and telemedicine initiatives.

Not held. No count or share of basic health centres keying their own monthly report is held, for the pilot districts or for the electronic registers deployed in 2024-2025. No financing statement dated within the last three years is held, and no event dated within the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Some private establishments and some health centres kept electronic records in 2024; no numbers are held, and no hospital use is shown.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's PSRSIS 2023-2027 lists as an activity the development of a patient management and electronic reporting system for all care structures. The computerised patient record software used in community medical centres is Datasanté's, under a partnership with the community doctors' association AMC-MAD renewed by a convention signed on 6 November 2024, which provides for Datasanté's continued logistical and financial support.

Tiers in use. At a round table on 5 November 2024 attended by the health ministry, the electronic personal medical record was described as functional in some private establishments and some basic health centres.

Where data is digitised. A community doctor at Matielona, equipped by Datasanté in November 2019, states that she keeps no paper register and that everything is in each patient's computerised record, entered on a tablet. A 2021 assessment of 30 health centres in two regions names paper registers as their primary documents for recording patient information.

Last twelve months. In January 2025, 2424.mg reported that two pilot sites had been chosen to start the hospital digitalisation project, the CHU Ravoahangy Andrianavalona and the CHUSSPA at Analakely, a project initiated by the digital development ministry with the Ministry of Public Health.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry's digital health strategy of January 2023 states that management of clinical data and of the hospital information system remains at an experimental and local stage, and that patient-management applications are used in private practices and private health centres.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: DHIS2 Tracker for vaccination. Single-function: the DHIS2 indicators for antimicrobial resistance surveillance, Vigimobile, the DHIS2-MTN module, the Geographic Tracking System, e-Surv, the Surveillance Épidémiologique Intégrée et Électronique, the Système d'Information de Gestion Logistique, the AMR Data Center, EIOS, the Système Intégré d'Information des Dotations CSB, the Malaria Routine Data Quality Assessment tool, DHIS2 Programme, DHIS2 Ressources, SYGMA, VIDA, HNQIS, ODK, AUGURE and CHANNEL. Community: CommCare. Enabling: the Smart Village programme, DECIM connectivity, Workflow for DHIS2 user accounts and telemedicine initiatives.

Not held. No count, share or district is held for the facilities keeping an electronic record, and no hospital is shown using one. Nothing held is dated within the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A pilot gave 180,000 pupils in one district a permanent number in 2026; whether any primary school beyond the pilot has enrolled is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The digital enrolment of learners is carried out by the Ministry of National Education and the Unité de gouvernance digitale, within the PRODIGY project, by the report of its launch on 11 May 2026. The World Bank's mission of December 2025 counted its field activities among the items to draw down the remaining funds of the Bank-financed PRODIGY project before 30 June 2026.

Levels in use. The Numéro Unique d'Identification Scolaire, as the ministry names it, gives each learner a permanent identifier from pre-school to lycée, in public and private establishments, the ministry said in May 2026.

How the record is kept. Learners are enrolled digitally, once, to receive the identifier, in an operation of May 2026.

Last twelve months. In December 2025 the enrolment's field activities were still to be carried out, the World Bank's mission recorded. On 11 May 2026 the scale-up was launched at a public lower-secondary school in Ambatolampy district, with a ministry target of 4.2 million learners from 19,303 establishments in 48 school districts by 12 June 2026.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Between March and April 2026 the pilot in the Atsimondrano school district enrolled 180,000 learners from 792 public and private establishments of all levels, by the report of May 2026: a figure for schools covered by the enrolment.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the annual school census and statistical yearbooks, which the education ministry calls its EMIS and its SIGE, the FPE collection application, the in-house Excel collection tool of the planning directorate and the planned StatEduc for the Fiches d'enquête rapides. Single-function: the online consultation of examination results, the HRMIS enrolment of state employees and geolocation of schools, the teacher attendance tracking system and the TMIS. Learning: the pilot of WhatsApp and Messenger bots for French lessons. Enabling: Smart Village, DECIM connectivity and devices and school devices and connectivity.

Not held. No total of establishments in the pilot district and no separate count of primary schools is held, and for the scale-up only the target is held, not a result. Who keys the record and any plan are not held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Communes register births digitally, 336 of them in December 2025; the share of all communes this represents is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A decree of 2 December 2020 set up the Centre National d'État Civil et de l'Identité under the technical supervision of the Interior ministry to keep the national digital civil status and identity registers. The digital registration system was rolled out under the PRODIGY project, financed by an IDA credit of US$133 million and a GFF grant of US$3 million and closing on 30 June 2026.

Events registered digitally. By December 2025 61,892 birth certificates carrying a unique identity number had been issued through the digital civil registration system, which Madagascar calls SIECM.

How the record is made. Communes themselves use SIECM to register civil-status events and issue birth certificates with a national unique number, the World Bank reported in December 2025; the system's offline module had been in demonstration since November 2025.

Last twelve months. Communes using SIECM rose from 75 to 336 in the six months to December 2025. In March 2026 the Unité de Gouvernance Digitale described the birth registration platform as being deployed progressively at national scale.

Local offices doing the digital entry. In December 2025 336 communes were using SIECM, which the World Bank gives as 40% of the project's target; a further 112 communes had been trained and were not yet operational.

Noted, not assessed. Back-capture: the EC-MADA digitisation of civil registration records and PRODIGY's digitisation and indexing of civil status acts. Notification: the digitalisation of notifications from health centres and health workers and the declaration of births and deaths by basic health centres and matrones. Outreach: Fitsarana mitety vohitra and the Ezaka Kopia ho an'ny Ankizy programme. Identity: the Izaho Tokana biometric enrolment, the identity management system IDMS and the biometric national identity card, e-Fokontany and the registre national numérique de la population. Enabling: Smart Village, DECIM and the IT kits planned for commune civil status offices. Exchange: the government interoperability platform and the interoperability between the Justice Department, the Civil Registry and other public services.

Not held. The share of all communes using the system is not held, nor where the record is kept. No digital death registration is reported, and nothing held says what has funded the system since the project's financing closed on 30 June 2026.

The police opened an online platform for reporting violence and disappearances in 2026; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Police nationale's online platform for reporting violence and disappearances, vbg.pn.gov.mg, a website and Android application reported on 30 July 2026; the Gendarmerie nationale's Fichier central, set up at its Direction de la Police Judiciaire, with a digital version in development with UNDP support on 16 July 2026; the software for tracking judicial files in the technical and scientific police, for which a UNDP notice of June 2025 sought a developer; a digital criminal records platform in development, announced on 2 June 2026; the registre de garde à vue, a paper custody register compulsory in every service where custody is held; and the Smart Village computer equipment for 11 local entities in Androy and Anosy, gendarmerie services among them, with six sites operational on 3 February 2026.

Not held. No source held states whether stations record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
