Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health zone offices entered clinics' paper reports into the national system in 2022; how many clinics enter their own is not published.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's 2022 statistical yearbook says the ministry adopted DHIS2 from 2014 as its platform for managing health data, under what it calls the Système National d'Information et de Gestion Sanitaires (SNIGS). The ministry's programme budget for 2024-2026 puts its Direction des Systèmes d'Information in charge of the information systems action and funds that action from domestic resources alone, at 590,406,313 FCFA in 2024. The same document sets targets of 80 per cent in 2024 and 85 per cent in 2025 and 2026 for health facilities whose statistics are integrated in SNIGS.

Tiers in use. In 2019 SNIGS collected a monthly return from health centres and another from hospitals, on standard paper forms, and reported indicators for the national teaching hospital.

Where data is digitised. In 2022 a facility sent its paper report to the zone sanitaire before the 5th of each month and the zone keyed it into DHIS2 before the 20th, in every zone sanitaire. Certain equipped facilities and community relays keyed their own monthly forms directly into the data warehouse.

Last twelve months. The programme budget of December 2023 allocates the information systems action the same 590,406,313 FCFA in 2026 as in 2024 and 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Abt Global, in November 2023, names one private facility, the Minonkpo Clinic in Cotonou, as recording its data directly into DHIS2 since 2020, against 752 private health facilities in the country.

Noted, not assessed. The health ministry's Sous-système de surveillance épidémiologique et de détection des épidémies and its Sous-système d'information à assise communautaire, and the plateforme Alafia.com, used by community health workers.

Not held. No number or share of primary clinics entering their own reports is published, and no tier below the health centre is named. No dated event falls in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Primary schools entered enrolments and class lists themselves in 2026; how many do, and whether any enter attendance or results, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Minister of Nursery and Primary Education, in a letter of 16 February 2026, calls EDUCMASTER a management tool for the sub-sector and orders its use by his departmental directors, district heads and school heads. The government's progress report of December 2020 presents the system, which it calls the SIGE, as a reform of its own action programme.

Levels in use. The December 2020 report says use of the SIGE, which it also calls Educmaster, is compulsory in all public and private pre-primary, primary, secondary and technical schools. La Nation reported a public primary school head in Cotonou entering her pupils on 18 December 2024.

How the record is kept. The minister's letter of February 2026 says schools themselves enter enrolments and update class lists. In secondary schools, the ministry's information systems director said in December 2024, collèges and lycées enter enrolments, transfers and absences and load each pupil's end-of-semester averages.

Last twelve months. On 16 February 2026 the minister ordered every school to complete its enrolments for all nursery and primary classes by 23 February, with heads of schools that did not to be barred from examination work in June 2026. In July 2026 the Conseil National de l'Éducation recommended following up that heads of secondary establishments record girls' enrolment exhaustively.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The letter of February 2026 says several schools enter only their final-year examination candidates and leave the other nursery and primary classes not updated.

Noted, not assessed. The primary ministry's emp.educmaster.bj, where primary schools and parents looked up candidates' primary-certificate marks in August 2026, and the government's examination results portal, eresultats.bj, in service in December 2020.

Not held. No number or share of primary schools entering data is published, and nothing shows primary schools entering attendance or results during the year. How the system is paid for is not stated, and no current plan naming it is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births are registered digitally at maternity desks in ten pilot health facilities since August 2025; how other local offices make the record is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. ANIP, the national identification agency, runs the one-stop birth registration desk with its own teams and the Ministry of Health's health workers. Benin's playbook of May 2026 states that ANIP is attached to the Presidency and is in charge of the population register and the national civil-status file. ANIP began the reform with the support of the WURI project and UNICEF.

Events registered digitally. From 20 August 2025 the declaration and registration of a birth are done on site in about ten public health facilities. In six months 17,071 children were declared there and 13,069 registered, ANIP reported on 24 February 2026.

How the record is made. Desk agents at Natitingou and Djougou register the child in the system at the maternity, working on tablets and centralised databases, La Nation reported on 16 July 2026. Paper declaration forms remained in use during the pilot.

Last twelve months. The pilot ended in February 2026 and a consolidation phase is running in the same ten facilities, with generalisation three to four months behind the initial calendar, ANIP's director general said on 16 July 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The desk's pilot was launched on 20 August 2025 at CHU-MEL Cotonou and runs in ten public health facilities. On 24 February 2026 the desk was running in 10 health facilities, with gradual national scale-up among the prospects.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the dématérialisation duplicative des registres-papiers, as the 2018 decree names the scanning of paper registers, the fichier central de l'état civil built from it, Dematec, the reconstitution of old birth acts and Family Search International's genealogical data and archive documents. Notification: the formulaire de déclaration de naissance filled in at the health service. Certificate: the ANIP BJ application, the portal eservices.anip.bj, the *151# USSD code, secured birth act delivery and the Fon-language voice AI model. Outreach: the free birth-act campaign for under-14s of 16 to 30 September 2026, mobile counters in communes and the enregistrement à titre dérogatoire à l'état civil, as the 2018 decree names it. Identity: the Registre National des Personnes Physiques, RAVIP, the fID certificate, which ANIP calls the C'est moi card, the Certificat d'Identification Personnelle, the Carte Nationale d'Identité Biométrique and its online renewal, the registres communaux des personnes physiques, the registre national des documents d'identification, the carte nationale d'identité électronique, the eID database of 2019, ANIP's authentication and eKYC services, the Mobile ID Bénin app, ePass, the planned citizen mobile wallet, tablets in embassies, the pensioners' proof-of-life application and the Je Suis Citoyen Béninois card drive. Enabling: the 2,050 mobile registration terminals received in October 2024, Natitingou's equipping of its arrondissements and training of registrars and motorbikes for identification agents. Exchange: the interoperability of the population register and the national civil-status file, the Numéro Personnel d'Identification, the link between the population register, civil registry and electoral database, the assignment of unique identifiers to newborn birth registrations, the links from services to the fID system, the système de communication électronique des données de l'état civil the 2018 decree provides for and the interconnection of state databases promised on 21 March 2026.

Not held. How ANIP's communal units and the civil-status centres make the record is not described, so deaths and offices beyond the ten pilot facilities are not established. No plan with a period is on record.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Only fingerprint, criminal-file and public online services are held, to 2026; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The Police républicaine put an automated fingerprint and palm-print file into service on 30 September 2025 at its headquarters, and the judicial police's forensic service reopened the same day, renovated and re-equipped. The data-protection authority gave a favourable opinion on 19 August 2016 to a computerised national criminal file kept by the Centre de Documentation de la Sécurité Publique; its report for 2015-2020 also lists the centre's 2017 authorisation to process identification data on persons prosecuted, arrested or wanted, and video surveillance declared by police headquarters in 2019 and by the centre in 2020. From 1 October 2026 loss certificates are issued only through the police's online platform, and stations no longer issue them. The Centre national d'investigations numériques takes complaints on digital offences by email, as reported in October 2026. Citizens request the criminal-record extract on the national public services portal (2025). A March 2026 programme describes the application it calls Justice BJ, for following court cases, as being generalised, and the justice sector's plan for 2026-2033 provides for a single portal for lodging a complaint online. SECUROUTE, as Benin names it, centralises road enforcement data (January 2026). The state's digital agency gave 300 police officers its tools for signing and encrypting exchanges, 150 of them arrondissement commissioners, in training from 26 June to 8 July 2026.

Not held. Nothing held states whether stations record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
