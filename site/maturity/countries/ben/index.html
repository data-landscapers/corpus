Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Benin's data protection law is in its 2018 digital code and was applied in 2025; how many regulations are in force is not known.

Authority. The Autorité de Protection des Données Personnelles (APDP) is constituted: seventeen of its officers were sworn in before the Court of First Instance of Cotonou on 14 September 2026. It succeeds the CNIL, whose members were installed in October 2010.

Standing. The data protection rules are Book V of the Code du numérique, Law no. 2017-20, promulgated on 20 April 2018. It replaced Law no. 2009-09 of 22 May 2009 and was amended by Law no. 2020-35 of 6 January 2021. In September 2026 La Nation treated the Code as the framework governing personal data processing.

Regulations. Decree 2022-107 of 16 February 2022, taken under the Code, authorises the interior ministry's national video-protection data processing. Decree 2020-187 of 11 March 2020, also taken under it, authorises the national police to collect traveller identity and fingerprint data at borders.

Application. For 2025, La Nation's account of the APDP's annual report gives 907 files examined, 793 validated, 64 inspection missions and 20 complaints treated. The authority counted 347 decisions from 2016 to 15 December 2020, among them a 2020 injunction to a controller to guarantee a complainant's right of deletion.

Last twelve months. The swearing-in of seventeen APDP officers on 14 September 2026 was done under Article 468(2) of the Code. The APDP's president spoke at a regulators' forum in Johannesburg on 1 to 3 September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. How many regulations are in force under the Code, and the date the Code commenced, are not stated. The 2025 figures are held through a newspaper's account, not the APDP's report itself, and no ratification of the Malabo Convention is stated.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The 2018 Code du numérique is in force; in 2025 about 1,500 online-fraud suspects were arrested.

Law in force. Loi n° 2017-20 of 20 April 2018, the Code du numérique, was adopted by the National Assembly and promulgated by the President. Its Livre VI defines illegal access to and penetration of an information system. The code carries the date 20 April 2018, and its Livre VI treats unauthorised entry into computer systems as an offence, in two forms: illegal access and penetration.

Use of the law. Benin's Centre national d'investigations numériques reported over 6,500 digital-offence complaints in 2025, against about 4,000 the year before, and about 1,500 arrests of online-fraud suspects. During INTERPOL's Operation Sentinel, from 27 October to 27 November 2025, Benin took down 43 malicious domains and shut 4,318 extortion and scam accounts, leading to 106 arrests. The complaints figure covers the whole of 2025, and the arrests figure covers suspects in online fraud.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Benin's 2017 identification law, amended in 2025, sets up a biometric register and a personal number. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Benin's law no. 2017-08 of 19 June 2017 on the identification of natural persons is the law behind the national register and the personal identification number. In June 2025 the National Assembly adopted law no. 2025-12 amending its article 6, extending who the law covers, and the 2017 law stands as amended.

Digital identity covered and rules made. Article 6 as amended provides for a national biometric population register and a personal identification number for each person entered in it. The 2025 amendment adds special numbers for foreigners and for naturalised persons of African descent, so the register and the number now reach beyond citizens. The number is the one each person keeps for life in the register. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. Registre national biométrique de la population (RNPP); Certificat du numéro personnel d'identification (Cnpi/fID, carte C'est moi); Guichet unique d'enregistrement des naissances; Numero Personnel d'Identification (NPI); Carte Nationale d'Identite Biometrique (CNIB).

Benin is under the regional central bank's 2024 payment rules, which license non-banks; in 2026 it ordered all providers to connect.

Law or rules in force. Benin is bound by the rules of the BCEAO, the central bank of the eight-state West African Economic and Monetary Union. Instruction n°001-01-2024 on payment services, applied since January 2024, obliges every company offering payment services in the union to hold a licence or stop, and distinguishes payment institutions from banks, microfinance institutions and e-money institutions.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. In April 2026 the BCEAO told every bank, e-money institution and payment institution to connect to its instant payment platform, PI-SPI, by 30 June, and on 25 June moved the deadline to 30 September 2026, with microfinance institutions given until 30 June 2027.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Benin's 2018 digital code is in force and sets duties for trust service providers; no decree or rules made under it are known.

Law in force. Benin's Loi n° 2017-20, the Code du numérique, was promulgated by the President on 20 April 2018 after adoption by the National Assembly and review by the Constitutional Court. The code is Benin's main digital law and includes a title on the obligations of trust service providers. The text is published by the Secrétariat général du gouvernement.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The nearest held fact is that title itself. Articles 305 and 306 of the Code du numérique set out the freedom of establishment of trust service providers and their duty of declaration, which is the part of the code that bears on electronic signatures and certification. A trust service provider may therefore establish itself in Benin freely, and must declare itself once established.
