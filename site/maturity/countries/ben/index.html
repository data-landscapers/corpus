Data protection legislation

Benin's data protection law is in its 2018 digital code and was applied in 2025; how many regulations are in force is not known.

Authority. The Autorité de Protection des Données Personnelles (APDP) is constituted: seventeen of its officers were sworn in before the Court of First Instance of Cotonou on 14 September 2026. It succeeds the CNIL, whose members were installed in October 2010.

Standing. The data protection rules are Book V of the Code du numérique, Law no. 2017-20, promulgated on 20 April 2018. It replaced Law no. 2009-09 of 22 May 2009 and was amended by Law no. 2020-35 of 6 January 2021. In September 2026 La Nation treated the Code as the framework governing personal data processing.

Regulations. Decree 2022-107 of 16 February 2022, taken under the Code, authorises the interior ministry's national video-protection data processing. Decree 2020-187 of 11 March 2020, also taken under it, authorises the national police to collect traveller identity and fingerprint data at borders.

Application. For 2025, La Nation's account of the APDP's annual report gives 907 files examined, 793 validated, 64 inspection missions and 20 complaints treated. The authority counted 347 decisions from 2016 to 15 December 2020, among them a 2020 injunction to a controller to guarantee a complainant's right of deletion.

Last twelve months. The swearing-in of seventeen APDP officers on 14 September 2026 was done under Article 468(2) of the Code. The APDP's president spoke at a regulators' forum in Johannesburg on 1 to 3 September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.
