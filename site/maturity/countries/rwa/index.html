Standing. The Government of Rwanda issued the Smart Rwanda Master Plan in October 2015, which INCLUDE in September 2022 says was adopted in 2015, and the October 2023 sector review lists Smart Rwanda Master Plan II among policies under development.

Broadband strategy: national plan

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Rwanda updated its National Broadband Policy and Strategy in 2022; whether money is committed to it or results are published is not known.

Plan adopted. The government updated its National Broadband Policy and Strategy in 2022, Telecom Review Africa reported in October 2025. The update ended the commitment to a single wholesale network. It introduced technology neutrality, so that mobile operators can reuse spectrum bands for several technologies at once, 4G and 5G among them.

Money and results. To support the change, more spectrum was allocated in the 700 MHz, 900 MHz, 1800 MHz and 2100 MHz bands, opening the way for more than one operator to build advanced networks. The same report estimates 4G coverage reaching 100% by the end of 2025, with 3G coverage at 99.71%. It sets neither figure against a target of the policy, and it names no money committed to it.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Rwanda has required critical government systems to be hosted in the National Data Center since 2012; which bodies have moved recently is not known.

Policy adopted. Rwanda's rule is Ministerial Instructions N° 001/2012, issued by the Minister in the Office of the President in Charge of ICT and in force from their signature on 12 March 2012. They include a rule on hosting critical government data and systems, and state no end date and no replacement.

Named hosting and moves. Article 17 of the instructions requires all government IT systems and applications that process, store or provide critical government data to be hosted in the National Data Center, website, email and shared applications included. Article 18 requires institutions that keep data centres of their own to arrange disaster recovery from the National Data Center. The instructions name the centre as the one place for critical systems, and both articles have stood since 2012.

Noted, not assessed. Data Sharing Platform; Shared Government Data Hub; AWS Outposts; Shared Government Data Hub and Data Interoperability Platform; National Data Center.

Not held. No source from the last three years naming or counting public bodies that have moved onto the named hosting is held.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A market brief of July 2025 says there is no blanket localisation requirement in Rwanda, but some data must be stored locally depending on the circumstances, under the 2021 Law on Data Privacy and Protection. Enforcer named and rule applied. No certificate granted or refused is held, and no source names an organisation ordered to bring data into Rwanda or penalised.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Rwanda's 2021 Data Protection Act is in force and the authority registers and trains controllers; whether any sanction or decision has been made is unknown.

Authority. The National Cyber Security Authority acts as the supervisory authority for personal data protection and privacy under the Data Protection Act.

Standing. Rwanda's Data Protection Act was introduced on 15 October 2021. It is in force: its two-year grace period ended on 15 October 2023, the date from which controllers and processors are held to it.

Regulations. Under the Act the authority issues registration certificates to data controllers and processors.

Application. By January 2026 the authority had registered data controllers and processors, trained over 100 Data Protection Officers in the public sector and run sector workshops for pharmacists and local government entities, all over the previous four years.

Last twelve months. For Data Privacy Week, 26 to 30 January 2026, the authority adopted the theme "Compliance is Beyond Registration". It moved from the awareness phase of 2022 to 2024 to capacity building, and now expects controllers to prove that they comply.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source says whether regulations under the Act have been issued or are pending. No dated sanction or decision under the Act is on record, and no count of decisions is published.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Rwanda's single digital identity law, reported in 2026, ends old identity cards by June 2027. No rules made under it are known.

Law in force. Rwanda has a law governing registration into its single digital identity system. In March 2026 The New Times reported that the law sets a June 2027 deadline after which existing national identity cards are no longer valid, and that under it citizens cannot hold two government-issued identities. The law's number and date are not given.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The system the law governs is built on a unique single digital identification number, a derived token and a card with a QR code, with authentication that relies on biometric data. Enrolment under it takes fingerprints, a facial image and an iris scan, carried out by the national identification agency. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. e-Indangamuntu (digital national ID); Single Digital ID (SDID).

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Rwanda's central bank directive of January 2026 requires banks and e-money issuers to route all transfers through one national platform from July 2026.

Law or rules in force. The National Bank of Rwanda issued Directive No. 45/2026 on 9 January 2026. It required domestic retail payments between banks and electronic money issuers to move to the eKash platform, the national instant payment rail. RSwitch gave the account of the directive that ITWeb Africa reported.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. From 14 July 2026 every transfer between bank accounts and electronic money issuers, account to wallet, wallet to account, account to account and wallet to wallet, is routed through eKash. The Rwanda Bankers' Association states that the directive took effect on that date, so that banks and e-money issuers all run on the single rail. The directive binds the e-money issuers with the banks; the law under which those issuers are licensed is not named.

Not held. Nothing on the four questions.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices worked in the national civil registration system in 2024 and 2025; how many of the districts are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Governance and Decentralisation Sector Strategic Plan 2024-2029, dated 14 November 2024, costs an intervention giving local government entities ICT equipment and internet connectivity, with the Ministry of Local Government among those responsible. The World Bank-funded Digital Acceleration Project, running 2022 to 2026, plans a government network linking institutions at central, district, sector and cell levels, lawmakers were told on 7 July 2025.

What the office does over it. The civil registration system the National Institute of Statistics calls NCI-CRVS was fully operational at district, sector and cell offices for 2025, as it was in 2024. Engineers at City and District One Stop Centers process building permits in Kubaka, launched in April 2025, and sector offices make all their financial transactions in the national financial management system, on the sector plan of November 2024.

How the office is connected. Ministerial instructions of 12 March 2012 prescribe state-procured bandwidth delivered to central and local government institutions over the national fibre backbone, each institution paying the access fee. Cells, the sub-offices below the sector, have no internet connectivity, on the Ministry of Local Government's assessment published in September 2025.

Last twelve months. Births and deaths in communities are registered at the cell, where before they were registered at the sector, the Ministry of Local Government stated in June 2026.

Local governments connected. Connectivity remains a problem especially at sector and cell level in peri-urban and rural areas, a study published in September 2022 found.

Noted, not assessed. Applications: the NCI-CRVS with its Electronic Document Management System, ODK central system for verbal autopsy and civil records digitalisation, the National Population Registry, IremboGov, the social registry the Ministry of Local Government calls Imibereho, the digital beneficiary management systems for social transfers and VUP Digital Payment, IFMIS, the building permit system Kubaka, the Land Administration Information System, the land management information system, the digital land registry, e-taxation and digital revenue collection at district level, the property valuation model CAMA, the Imihigo Barometer, the planned Local Government M&E information system and the monitoring, evaluation and information system for the lower administrative levels. Web: Irembo, the Mbaza Digital Citizen Engagement Portal, e-Citizen Complaints, the planned centralized complaints management system, government websites under .gov.rw, the Ubutaka App and the Land Query Notification System. Equipment: Biometric Data Collection Kits at sector offices. Public access: the Digital Ambassadors Programme and planned public Wi-Fi in key commercial areas. City systems, in Kigali: the CCTV surveillance network and command centre, the LoRaWAN network, smart waste bins and Tap&Go; and the Public CCTV Project. Backbone: the One Government Network, the Modernizing Government Network project, the Ubuntu Network, the National Backbone Optic Fiber, the e-Government wide area network, the nationwide fibre transmission infrastructure and the National Data Center. Capacity: the Local Government Capacity Building Strategy. Central: the Single Digital ID system, the Rwanda Revenue Authority's CAMA System for Property Tax Administration and its collection of local taxes and fees, E-Ubuzima, the Rwanda Community Abroad Management Information System and the School data management system.

Not held. The share or number of district offices connected, and who provides or pays for a district office's connection. Whether the government network planned to link the districts has been built, and any event at a district office in the last twelve months.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Every health facility enters its own reports (audit to 2023); it stays at stage 3 because no current plan or budget was found.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health upgraded the HMIS, established in 1998, to web-based DHIS2 from 2012. The National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda describes the system in 2025 as operating countrywide under the ministry's management.

Tiers in use. The Global Fund's Inspector General states, for the period to December 2023, that DHIS2 has been rolled out at all health facilities, a network of referral, provincial and district hospitals, 505 health centres and 1,250 health posts. The ministry's booklet for fiscal year 2023/24 reports HMIS counts by facility level for referral, provincial and district hospitals, medicalized health centres and medical clinics.

Where data is digitised. Data is reported into DHIS2 at the health facilities, after monthly validation meetings led by the heads of facilities, on the Inspector General's account of the period to December 2023. In 2017 heads of service filled paper forms from registers and the facility's own data manager uploaded the data into DHIS2 by the 5th of the month.

Last twelve months. The National Institute of Statistics describes the system as it stood in 2025 as still capturing aggregated births and deaths to check the civil registration system.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Inspector General states that DHIS2 is rolled out at all health facilities, in the audit covering July 2021 to December 2023. Authors at the University of Rwanda and the Rwanda Biomedical Center held HMIS immunisation reports for October to December 2022 for all 520 primary health facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the e-Tracker electronic immunization registry, DHIS2 HIV case-based surveillance and the 2005 electronic record for tuberculosis and HIV care. Single-function: the pharmacy management system, the national laboratory information management systems, the national health commodity inventory management system, DHIS2 for the Marburg outbreak response, eLMIS and the laboratory data systems for HIV viral load. Community: the community health workers' standardized forms and mobile tools. Enabling: the planned virtual hospital.

Not held. No plan or financing for the HMIS dated within the last three years is held, and no dated event in the last twelve months. No measured share of clinics entering their own reports is held, and whether the health posts key theirs is not established.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All 42 district hospitals used electronic patient records in 2023, and the national system reached most public facilities by 2026; shared records are not shown.

Governance, planning, finance. Rwanda Biomedical Centre slides of April 2026 present eBuzima as the national system for all public health facilities, served from the Ministry of Health's own domain. In a memorandum signed on 5 December 2025 and running from April 2026 to December 2030, the government commits to expand national EMR systems across all health facilities. The United States plans to allocate US$6,806,292 over 2026 and 2027 to the e-Buzima rollout, and Rwanda commits to pay the ongoing licensing, cloud and hardware costs the United States does not.

Tiers in use. A survey of April and May 2023 found all 42 district hospitals using an electronic medical record system, 35 of them OpenMRS. King Faisal Hospital Rwanda, a referral hospital in Kigali, runs an Integrated Health Management Information System that gives each patient a single record, the Tony Blair Institute reported in July 2026.

Where data is digitised. The April 2026 slides describe eBuzima as centralised, web-based and synchronising records across public facilities.

Last twelve months. By 8 April 2026 eBuzima had been rolled out to the majority of public health facilities, with its HIV key-population and PrEP modules running in one Kigali pilot site.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Tom Frieden wrote in a piece published in September 2026 that eBuzima is in more than two thirds of health centres. The 2023 survey found 71 of 215 randomly sampled public health centres (33.0%) using an electronic medical record system.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the e-Tracker electronic immunization registry, DHIS2 HIV case-based surveillance and the 2005 electronic record for tuberculosis and HIV care. Single-function: the pharmacy management system, the national laboratory information management systems, the national health commodity inventory management system, DHIS2 for the Marburg outbreak response, eLMIS and the laboratory data systems for HIV viral load. Community: the community health workers' standardized forms and mobile tools. Enabling: the planned virtual hospital.

Not held. No unique patient identifier and no record retrieved at another facility is established. No count of facilities using eBuzima is held, nor who enters the record at the health centres or where it is keyed.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Primary schools keep a profile per pupil with exam results, and a daily attendance app is live (2026); the share of schools is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. HISP Rwanda, on a page of 7 May 2026, names the Ministry of Education as owner of the KARAME attendance app. The Education Sector Strategic Plan 2024-2029 lists improving digital attendance tracking and an early warning response system among its interventions.

Levels in use. UNICEF Rwanda, on 12 June 2024, describes the School Data Management System, SDMS, in use at Nyarubuye Primary School in Rwamagana District, where the head teacher enters the data.

How the record is kept. SDMS holds a profile for each student and captures national examination results, a World Bank study presented in February 2024 found. In KARAME, teachers and headteachers record each student's daily attendance at the school, and the data syncs to the national education server, on HISP Rwanda's account of May 2026.

Last twelve months. The ministry rolled out a new digital attendance tracking system, The New Times reported on 23 December 2025. HISP Rwanda reported on 11 February 2026 a mission to strengthen the absence tracking system and move its technical support to the local team.

Primary schools doing the digital input. KARAME is live and supports the monitoring of 177,000 reintegrated students, as HISP Rwanda stated in May 2026. The same body names two schools using the absence tracking system, and the World Bank study describes SDMS as in use by schools and teachers.

Noted, not assessed. The yearly school count is the school census behind the Education Statistical Yearbook, whose 2023/24 edition draws on SDMS; the ministry's Partnership Compact of March 2023 calls the system producing its annual statistical reports the Education Management Information System, EMIS. Single-function: the Teacher Management Information System, the ICT in Education Management Information System, the Teaching and Learning Materials Management Information System and the Quality Assurance Management Information System. Standalone: the Berulo Foundation attendance monitoring system and Brain Ink's grading and assessment tool. Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are entered digitally at all categories of registration office in 2025, the statistics office says; the rural share is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Identification Agency, under the Ministry of ICT, supervises and coordinates the civil registration system nationally, on the statistics institute's account for 2025. The statistics strategy for 2024/25 to 2028/29 names strengthening the system a top priority. The finance law of 30 June 2026 appropriates RWF 116,184,997 to civil registration for 2026/27, domestically financed.

Events registered digitally. The system the country calls NCI-CRVS registered 356,838 births and 39,355 deaths that occurred in 2025, among the nine events registered through it.

How the record is made. The statistics institute states that in 2025 the system captures events in real time at the site where official registration takes place, as a single point of entry. The Agency states that all civil registers have been available only in electronic format since 17 February 2023.

Last twelve months. Birth registration completeness rose from 90.3% in 2024 to 92.9% in 2025, and death registration completeness from 46.1% to 50.5%. The share of deaths registered within the legal 30 days fell from 96.3% to 89.9% over the same year.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The statistics institute states that in 2025 the system is fully operational at all five categories of registration office: health facilities, cells, sectors, districts and embassies. The Agency counted 3,220 civil registration service points in its procedures of December 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitalisation of civil records and an earlier digitisation of birth records. Notification: a phone-based community death notification system and the E-death notification system. Certificate: IremboGov and the Electronic Document Management System. Outreach: a week-long civil registration campaign in Nyamagabe District and the CRVS week of 2017. Identity: the digital identity card the Agency calls e-Indangamuntu, the National Single Digital Identity System, the National Population Registry, the national identity card and national identification number, an online identity verification portal and eRainbow. Exchange: the link from NCI-CRVS to the National Population Register, the statistics institute's CRVS web-based application, the Rwanda Health Management Information System, the Verbal Autopsy system, the link to the Electronic Immunization Registry, the Imibereho Dynamic Social Registry and the Health Intelligence Center.

Not held. The statement that the system runs at every category of registration office comes with no count of the offices using it, and no figure gives the rural share.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

The investigation bureau processes criminal case files in the justice ministry's electronic system, by its 2024 history; whether stations enter them is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Justice manages the Integrated Electronic Case Management System, the Rwanda Investigation Bureau's history of its first years, published in October 2024, states.

Records made digitally. The bureau has incorporated the system to process criminal investigation files, by the same history, which the bureau wrote of its own work and which covers the years 2017 to 2023.

How the record is made. The handling of cases is digitalised from investigation to final judgment through the system, the bureau's history states.

Last twelve months. The bureau's history, published in October 2024, covers 2017 to 2023 and gives the system as the place its investigation files are processed.

Stations doing the digital entry. The history gives the entry of criminal investigation files as the bureau's own, in the system the justice ministry manages.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: speed enforcement cameras and automated violation detection, automated driving tests and the ISANGE system for GBV cases. Not held. Whether the bureau's stations and posts enter the case file themselves, where and how the entry is made, and how many stations do it are not established. Nothing is dated inside the last twelve months.
