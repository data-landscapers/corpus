Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health centres enter their own reports into the national reporting system (2023), but no figure shows how many of them do.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's Direction du Système National d'Information Sanitaire manages the health information system, and its IT cell administers DHIS2, as the ministry's statistical yearbook of July 2024 states. In a memorandum signed with the United States on 6 February 2026 the government commits to use DHIS2 as its national health data warehouse, and the United States plans $188,526 for it in 2026.

Tiers in use. The ministry collected its 2023 data through the national DHIS2 platform from health centres, district hospitals and national hospitals, with report forms set for the community, the health centre and the hospital.

Where data is digitised. Facilities record by hand in paper registers and the entry into DHIS2 is made at the facility, by the deputy head of a health centre and by a hospital's information officer, the World Bank reported in March 2023. Community health workers' monthly reports go to the health centre for entry, by the method note of the ministry's 2023 yearbook.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 6 February 2026 runs to 31 December 2030 and costs United States support for DHIS2 at about $609,872 over the term, for linking it with SIDAinfo and other systems and for bringing in supply chain and laboratory data.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Reports in DHIS2 were more than 95% complete for health centres at 30 June 2022, in the World Bank's assessment of March 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SIDA Info. Single-function: eLMIS Medexis, DataToCare, the entrepôt national de données paludisme, Health Pulse, Open RBF, GMAO, Labware, SIARP, ODK, SMIR, DHIS2 Tracker, Ibipimo, eLMIS, Sage, e-Surv Compagnon and GLASS. Community: Kira Mama, the gestion des intrants de l'ICCM, Connecting with Sarah and M-Health.

Not held. No share or count of health centres keying their own reports, with a denominator, is published, and nothing on the point is dated inside the last three years. The one community application described as interfaced with DHIS2 was still in test in 2023.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

41 of 71 hospitals kept electronic patient records and six health centres were piloting them in 2023; records are not shown to be shared.

Governance, planning, finance. OpenClinic sits under Enabel and PROGISSA, the health informatics programme the ministry created in 2020, the World Bank reported in March 2023. In a memorandum signed on 6 February 2026 the government commits to use SIDAinfo as its electronic medical records system, and the United States intends to fund it with $565,579 in 2026.

Tiers in use. Forty-one of 71 public and faith-based hospitals are computerised, patient health records included, and 39 of them run OpenClinic, the World Bank reported in March 2023, citing PROGISSA. They are not interconnected and each has its own patient identifier.

Where data is digitised. In the computerised hospitals OpenClinic GA runs on a local server in each hospital, with data passing between clinical services, laboratory, imaging and pharmacy in real time without further manual entry, the ministry's plan drawn up in 2019 states.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 6 February 2026 commits Burundi to roll SIDAinfo out in 460 facilities by 2026 and 572 by 2028.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Six health centres are computerised under a pilot financed by Enabel, the World Bank reported in March 2023, with 29 more on Enabel's financing and four on the government's planned during that year. The ministry's 2019 plan puts first-level facilities at more than 1,000.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SIDA Info. Single-function: eLMIS Medexis, DataToCare, the entrepôt national de données paludisme, Health Pulse, Open RBF, GMAO, Labware, SIARP, ODK, SMIR, DHIS2 Tracker, Ibipimo, eLMIS, Sage, e-Surv Compagnon and GLASS. Community: Kira Mama, the gestion des intrants de l'ICCM, Connecting with Sarah and M-Health.

Not held. No count of health centres on a patient record is dated since March 2023, and the 460 facilities of the 2026 memorandum are a commitment with no tier given. The 41 hospitals are not broken down by tier, and whether SIDAinfo, recorded in 2023 as a record of people living with HIV, serves general care is not established.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2023 the yearly school count was collected on paper; whether primary schools keep per-pupil records is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Burundi calls its yearly school census an EMIS, in French the Système d'information et de gestion de l'éducation (SIGE): in 2023 the census was collected on paper at the school and entered electronically at central level, and it is published as the Annuaire statistique de l'éducation, whose 2024-2025 edition is dated December 2025. Two systems do one job each: the education ministry's human resources module for the SIGE, being built in August 2024, and a high frequency survey in which 40 municipal inspectors captured monthly data on tablets from a sample of 200 schools in 2020/21 and 2021/22. On equipment and connections: computers for 39 écoles centrales, put to tender by UNICEF in August 2026; the PAFEN-ONATEL rural network coverage contract of April 2026, for 92 collines; the PAFEN-Lumitel high-speed internet project of March 2026, which is to connect nearly 300 schools; and the rural 4G extension of PAFEN and the Fonds de Service Universel, to 178 collines.

Not held. No dated statement says whether primary schools keep their pupils' records on paper, and nothing held describes a system with a record for each pupil that a school adds to during the year.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Digital civil registration runs in about twelve pilot communes in 2026, most acts still on paper; how the pilot record is made is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The interior ministry presents the civil registration reform and its pilot digitalisation as the government's, with the World Bank, UNICEF, UNECA and UNDP as supporting partners, in its presentation of 18 July 2026. Its director general for territorial administration put the platform's cost at about BIF 15 billion on 6 May 2026.

Events registered digitally. The project launched at Rumonge on 19 July 2024 is for the digital registration of births and deaths at the commune's civil status service, at its seat and in its zones.

How the record is made. The ministry's director general told the African registrars general on 18 July 2026 that the majority of civil-status acts are still recorded in physical registers and that Burundi has no single national database of them. The ministry's director general said on 6 May 2026 that the legal framework still rests on manual registration procedures.

Last twelve months. On 6 May 2026 the ministry presented a digital platform for civil registration and biometric identity card production, its infrastructure nearly complete and awaiting a decree. On 11 August 2026 it stated that the government continues to scale up the platform.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The ministry's presentation of 18 July 2026 records a pilot digitalisation project in 12 communes. On 10 August 2026 thirteen communes had a biometric registration system, with civil registration digitalised in pilot communes.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the statistics institute's Saisie numérique des faits d'état civil 2025. Notification: the planned notification through chefs de collines of events at home. Outreach: late registrations and registration in the Congolese refugee camps. Identity: the national digital identity system to be procured in 2026/27, the carte nationale d'identité biométrique, the Fichier National de l'Identification and Carte Nationale d'Identité, the national ID smartcard pilot launched in 2013, and UNHCR's proGres and BIMS. Exchange: the interoperability between civil registration and the health services and the interoperability module in the platform for the statistics institute and health facilities.

Not held. No source says the system is past its pilot, how the digital record is made in the pilot communes or where it is held. No plan with a current period and no domestic budget line for the platform is on record.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Only traffic, statistics and staff systems are held, the latest from 2026; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. The interior ministry was developing a data platform in March 2026 to collect figures from the communes up to central level, public security among its domains, with a pilot planned for May 2026. Its report for the third quarter of 2025/26 names software for producing driving licences. A central platform for the National Police's road-safety data was being set up in December 2025, its pilot not yet launched. The ministry's revised procurement plan for 2024/25 carries BIF 791,990,000 for biometric career-management software under police equipment. The telecoms regulator's 2017 annual report lists emergency short numbers assigned to police services. The 2023-2033 digitalisation master plan provides for an e-Justice system through which litigants file complaints and follow a case.

Not held. Nothing held states whether stations record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
