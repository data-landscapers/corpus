Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Burundi's 2018 payment system law is in force, and a 2024 regulation licenses non-bank payment institutions; no rule to connect providers is known.

Law or rules in force. Burundi's Loi n°1/07 of 11 May 2018 on the national payment system is in force, and the central bank's oversight framework, published in November 2025, cites it as the current law, whose article 9 lets the bank issue regulations. Under it the bank made Règlement n°002/2024, revising a 2017 regulation on payment services and payment institutions: no one may provide payment services without the central bank's licence.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The central bank licenses payment institutions under the 2024 regulation, and its framework lists them apart from commercial banks and microfinance institutions as operators of e-money platforms. Every licensed payment service provider is to be inspected on site at least every two years. The regulation's date within 2024 is not given, and no rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other.

Law in force. Burundi's statistics law dates from 25 September 2007 and was amended in 2021. The held text is Loi n°1/08 of 20 May 2021, which modifies the 2007 law on the organisation of the national statistical system. It takes effect on the day of its promulgation. The law is the one text held on the subject for Burundi, and it carries the amendment and not the 2007 law it changes.

The law of 20 May 2021 changes the 2007 statute on how the national statistical system is organised, and names the national committee on statistical information and its technical committee as the system's bodies. It was made by parliament and is a change to the law, so it is counted as the law in force.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health centres enter their own reports into the national reporting system (2023), but no figure shows how many of them do.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's Direction du Système National d'Information Sanitaire manages the health information system, and its IT cell administers DHIS2, as the ministry's statistical yearbook of July 2024 states. In a memorandum signed with the United States on 6 February 2026 the government commits to use DHIS2 as its national health data warehouse, and the United States plans $188,526 for it in 2026.

Tiers in use. The ministry collected its 2023 data through the national DHIS2 platform from health centres, district hospitals and national hospitals, with report forms set for the community, the health centre and the hospital.

Where data is digitised. Facilities record by hand in paper registers and the entry into DHIS2 is made at the facility, by the deputy head of a health centre and by a hospital's information officer, the World Bank reported in March 2023. Community health workers' monthly reports go to the health centre for entry, by the method note of the ministry's 2023 yearbook.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 6 February 2026 runs to 31 December 2030 and costs United States support for DHIS2 at about $609,872 over the term, for linking it with SIDAinfo and other systems and for bringing in supply chain and laboratory data.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Reports in DHIS2 were more than 95% complete for health centres at 30 June 2022, in the World Bank's assessment of March 2023.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

41 of 71 hospitals kept electronic patient records and six health centres were piloting them in 2023; records are not shown to be shared.

Governance, planning, finance. OpenClinic sits under Enabel and PROGISSA, the health informatics programme the ministry created in 2020, the World Bank reported in March 2023. In a memorandum signed on 6 February 2026 the government commits to use SIDAinfo as its electronic medical records system, and the United States intends to fund it with $565,579 in 2026.

Tiers in use. Forty-one of 71 public and faith-based hospitals are computerised, patient health records included, and 39 of them run OpenClinic, the World Bank reported in March 2023, citing PROGISSA. They are not interconnected and each has its own patient identifier.

Where data is digitised. In the computerised hospitals OpenClinic GA runs on a local server in each hospital, with data passing between clinical services, laboratory, imaging and pharmacy in real time without further manual entry, the ministry's plan drawn up in 2019 states.

Last twelve months. The memorandum of 6 February 2026 commits Burundi to roll SIDAinfo out in 460 facilities by 2026 and 572 by 2028.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Six health centres are computerised under a pilot financed by Enabel, the World Bank reported in March 2023, with 29 more on Enabel's financing and four on the government's planned during that year. The ministry's 2019 plan puts first-level facilities at more than 1,000.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SIDA Info. Single-function: eLMIS Medexis, DataToCare, the entrepôt national de données paludisme, Health Pulse, Open RBF, GMAO, Labware, SIARP, ODK, SMIR, DHIS2 Tracker, Ibipimo, eLMIS, Sage, e-Surv Compagnon and GLASS. Community: Kira Mama, the gestion des intrants de l'ICCM, Connecting with Sarah and M-Health.

The 41 hospitals are not broken down by tier, and whether SIDAinfo, recorded in 2023 as a record of people living with HIV, serves general care is not established.
