Data protection legislation

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cabo Verde's 2001 data protection law, last amended in 2021, is in force and cases reach its authority; whether any was decided is not known.

Authority. The Comissão Nacional de Protecção de Dados (CNPD) began work in 2015–2016 and completed a decade in 2025, under its president, Faustino Varela. The National Assembly's own budget gave it CVE 36,313,115 for 2025, with no revenue of its own.

Standing. Law 133/V/2001 is the general data protection regime, applying within the country and to the processing of data of people located in it. The CNPD's president dates the base law to 2001 and says the commission has worked from it since 2015–2016.

Regulations. The law was amended in 2013 and again in 2021: the first amendment led to the CNPD being set up, and the second added the obligation to appoint a data protection officer.

Application. The CNPD's president said in February 2025 that cases involving artificial intelligence and big data have reached the commission, among them two of images manipulated with generative AI and a first image-manipulation case in 2021. Cases that may be criminal are sent to the Ministério Público.

Last twelve months. In March 2025 the CNPD held an international conference on personal data protection for its tenth anniversary, opened by the Prime Minister.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Cross-border data transfers

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cape Verde law since 2013 lets personal data go abroad only to countries judged adequate or on listed grounds; its application is not known.

Rule in force. Cape Verde's Data Protection Law (Law 133/V/2001, amended by Law 41/VIII/2013 and Law 121/IX/2021) permits international transfer of personal data, as published in January 2025, only to a country with an adequate level of protection, or otherwise with the data subject's consent or in listed situations such as contract necessity or vital interests. Law 41/VIII/2013, published on 17 September 2013, made article 19 allow transfer abroad only to a country assuring an adequate level of protection, with the national data protection commission (CNPD) deciding which foreign states do.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The same 2013 amendment lets the CNPD permit a transfer to any other country on consent, contract necessity, public interest, vital interests or a public register. Article 20 also lets the CNPD authorise a transfer where the controller gives sufficient guarantees, such as adequate contractual clauses.

Noted, not assessed. A cooperation memorandum between the Cape Verde and Macau data protection authorities, published on 2 June 2026, covering training, investigations and studies; and a 2025 interview with the CNPD president that mentions Convention 108 and accession to the Malabo Convention.

Not held. No source held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.
