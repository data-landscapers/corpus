Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mali has a 2013 data protection law, applied by its authority with fines and complaints handled in 2025; the law's commencement date is not known.

Authority. Article 20 of the law institutes the Autorité de Protection des Données à caractère Personnel (APDP) as an independent administrative authority of 15 members. It sat in ordinary session on 3 August 2026 with all commissioners present and runs regional offices in Kayes, Sikasso, Ségou and Mopti.

Standing. Law no. 2013-015 on the protection of personal data was adopted by the National Assembly on 9 May 2013 and promulgated on 21 May 2013. On 3 August 2026 the APDP's president cited the law, as amended, as the basis for the authority's sanctions.

Regulations. Decree no. 2024-0300/PT-RM of 14 May 2024, made under the law, sets the fees payable to the APDP for declarations, authorisations and certification, and exempts non-profit public services.

Application. For 2025 the APDP's annual report, as summarised by Sahel Tribune, counts fines totalling 22 million FCFA and 51 complaints received, of which 28 were handled and four referred to the cybercrime prosecutor. The fines include 5 million FCFA on a Bamako clinic for obstructing a check of its video surveillance and 5 million FCFA on Société Aminata Konaté SARL for refusing a verification mission.

Last twelve months. The APDP opened an extraordinary session on 6 October 2025 to review about 2,000 declaration files and opened a regional office at Ségou on 4 December 2025. Its session of 3 August 2026 had over 1,000 files to examine.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. The date the law came into force is not held: its text states none. The APDP's 2025 annual report is held only through a press summary, and no figure is held for sanctions issued in 2026.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mali's 2006 law gives each person a unique identification number; the last rule known under it is a decree of November 2022.

Law in force. Mali's law no. 06-040 of 11 August 2006 institutes the national identification number and a national identification register. It was adopted by the National Assembly on 13 July 2006 and promulgated by the President. A decree of 3 November 2022 instituting the biometric national identity card cites it as its basis.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law gives each person a unique number held in a national register, to be shown on the identity card, the passport and civil status records. A decree of 18 October 2006 fixes the fourteen-digit number and obliges public services to use it. The 2022 decree requires the number for any biometric identity card application. Those rules all date from before September 2023, and none made under the law since then is on record.

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mali's 2016 law on electronic transactions regulates e-commerce and is cited by the state in 2025, but rules made under it are not known.

Law in force. Mali's Law no. 2016-012 of 6 May 2016 on electronic transactions, exchanges and services regulates electronic commerce. It gives legal effect to electronic writings, electronic signatures, electronic certificates and electronic archiving, and it was published in the Journal officiel of 27 May 2016. A second text, consumer protection law no. 2015-036 of 16 July 2015, sets rules for distance sales including by electronic means and bans email advertising without consent. It covers online selling only in part, and it states that an electronic contract is validly concluded under the law on electronic exchange of legal data.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The nearest held fact on application is a statement from the state. In November 2025 the Director General of Taxes cited the law on electronic transactions as part of a solid legal framework for digitalising public payments. The evidence names no decree, regulation or order made under the 2016 law, and no case of its provisions being applied to a particular seller or platform.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mali's 2013 data protection law sets the rule on sending data abroad; whether the authority has published lists, model terms or guidance is not known.

Rule in force. Law no. 2013-015 of 21 May 2013 on the protection of personal data was adopted by the National Assembly on 9 May 2013 and promulgated. Article 11 lets a data controller transfer personal data to a foreign state when the data protection authority finds the receiving state's protection sufficient. The law as held gives no repeal, so it is the rule on sending data abroad that the 2013 law sets today.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest held fact is in the same law. Article 11 also covers a transfer made under contract clauses or internal rules, and for that case the authority's decision is what allows the transfer. The law names these two routes, a finding of sufficient protection and a decision on clauses or rules, and places both with the data protection authority.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.
