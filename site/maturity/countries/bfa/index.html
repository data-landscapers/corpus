Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Town halls are on the state's network and 78 civil status centres were networked in 2025; the share of the 351 communes is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The government network, RESINA, is built and run by ANPTIC, a state establishment under the ministry of the digital economy, and the State financed its reinforcement and extension with FCFA 346.2 million in 2020. The national civil status strategy for 2023-2027, steered by the territorial administration ministry, counts the networking of principal civil status centres among its 2025 results.

What the office does over it. ANPTIC says the connection gives interconnected structures, local governments' buildings among them, access to the government intranet's computing resources, to email and to the web (January 2024).

How the office is connected. ANPTIC lists the interconnection of local governments' buildings to RESINA, with internet access at no monthly fee over WiMAX or LTE, microwave or fibre (January 2024). In 2020 its regional antennas gave the Dédougou town hall a local network and RESINA access and connected sites of the Ouagadougou town hall by fibre.

Last twelve months. The strategy's results for 2025, validated on 25 June 2026, report 78 principal civil status centres networked and the declaration software deployed in 30 principal and 140 secondary centres; the ministry names insufficient financing and too few qualified staff as brakes.

Local governments connected. The territorial administration ministry gives 78 principal civil status centres networked in 2025. ANPTIC's report for 2020 names five town halls on RESINA or being put on it: Dédougou, Fada N'Gourma, Tenkodogo, Manga and Ouagadougou.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: equipment given to Koloko's civil status agents (2026) and equipment for municipalities in the pilot areas of an education data project, with its data collection tools (2025). Applications: VENEEM, the civil status declaration software, in 30 principal and 140 secondary centres at the end of 2025; the scanning of Padéma's civil status registers (2026); civil status registers digitised in 30 communes (2025); the national digital civil status register instituted by the 2025 family code bill; a birth registration platform and archiving solution for Kaya, Tenkodogo and Koupéla (2025-2028); an integrated civil status management system (2024); Ouagadougou's street addressing and address database (2025); and the document management system SIGED at the Ouagadougou town hall (2020). Public access: Espaces publics numériques planned for rural communes (2024). City systems: SMART Burkina, video surveillance in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso (2024). Backbone: the national interministerial network, with 3,140 public buildings connected, and two modular data centres (2026); a new network for the public administration, with bids under evaluation at 30 June 2026; the operations centre of the national backbone, under construction in August 2026; the government cloud, G-Cloud (2026); the national fibre backbone of 2,001 km and the PACTDIGITAL project (2024); and five satellite stations (2024). Capacity: the Banque d'outils offered to local governments (2026) and Ouagadougou's information systems master plan (2020). Upper tier: the Portails des Régions for the 17 regions, launched on 16 June 2026, and RESINA at the regional councils of Dédougou, Fada N'Gourma, Tenkodogo and Manga (2020). Central: the Maisons du citoyen, which Burkina Faso calls Zama Tchè, 80 launched by October 2026; FASO ARZEKA, the state's payments platform (2025); the Registre social unique (2025); the tax administration's e-cadastre (2026) and its land information system, SYC@D and SINTAX (2025); the health data warehouse Endos-BF (2024); the e-Burkina platforms and e-services (2024); the administration's business software, email and IP telephony (2024); and RESINA at governorates, high commissions and prefectures (2020).

Not held. No share of the 351 communes' town halls connected is published: the 78 centres networked in 2025 are a count with no total. Nothing held says commune staff work in a national system over the connection, or how the connection has been financed since 2020.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics and hospitals reported into the national system in 2024, entered at district or facility; the share of clinics entering their own is not published.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Direction générale des études et des statistiques sectorielles coordinates the national health information system, under its procedures manual of July 2023. Enabel reported in July 2026 that a strategic plan for the system for 2026-2030 is being implemented. The ministry's digital health strategy of January 2025 is costed at 42.6 billion FCFA, against 2.8 billion FCFA of available funds.

Tiers in use. In 2024 Endos-BF, the ministry's name for its health data warehouse, took the monthly activity reports of public and private health facilities and of the units of the regional and university hospitals. The Countdown to 2030 country team states that 83% of expected monthly facility reports were received nationally through DHIS2 in 2024.

Where data is digitised. In 2024 health facilities sent their monthly reports to the district, where data managers keyed them into Endos-BF. The ministry's strategy of January 2025 lists the scaling-up of decentralised entry of routine data in health facilities among the health system's innovations.

Last twelve months. Enabel reports that district management teams and head nurses of health posts were trained in data analysis and use, including quality control of DHIS2 data, at Dédougou and Tenkodogo in July 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. In 2024, in the health districts implementing decentralised entry, the heads of health facilities entered their own monthly activity reports. In 2021 entry by the primary health centres themselves was operational in the Boucle du Mouhoun, one of 13 regions.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: IeDA, REC-Maternité, IeDA-maternity, REC-PCIME, the diabetes and orthopaedics patient records of one university hospital and three electronic consultation registers. Single-function: NetSIGL 2.0, ReDoP-BF, MS Surveillance and the weekly surveillance telegram, the TLOH. Community: eSanteCom, the community health workers' digital tools, the community-based information sub-system, the community health workers' reports in EnDoS and the mHealth platform of the 2021 roadmap. Enabling: the Ministry of Health's mini-datacenter.

Not held. No number or share of primary clinics, or of districts, keying their own monthly reports is held. Who finances the system is not stated, and no community system is shown exchanging data with Endos-BF.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Electronic patient records ran at two pilot hospitals in 2025 and one university hospital in 2023; primary clinics are not shown using them.

Governance, planning, finance. NetAfrique reported on 18 July 2025 that the Ministry of Health, through its Direction des systèmes d'information, trains the administrators of OpenClinic, a tool chosen at a workshop with the directors general of all university and regional hospitals. A paper on the 2024-2025 pilot describes OpenClinic GA as a nationally governed open-source hospital information system, customised under a national reference framework. ICTworks lists the Plan stratégique de la santé numérique 2025-2029, signed in January 2025, as the operative strategy.

Tiers in use. OpenClinic, a hospital management system with clinical and nursing records, is deployed at pilot sites, the CHUR of Ouahigouya and the CHR of Manga, NetAfrique reported on 18 July 2025. At Manga the 2024-2025 pilot ended in full digitisation of admissions and use of the electronic patient record across multiple departments. A study with fieldwork from September 2022 to April 2023 found one patient-record package fully deployed in a public university hospital.

Where data is digitised. A study with fieldwork from September 2022 to April 2023 found that the predominant data collection instruments in health facilities were physical consultation registers.

Last twelve months. NetAfrique reported on 18 July 2025 the ministry's second training session for OpenClinic administrators, in the Nazinon region.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. By April 2023 the public health system lacked computerised patient records, those in existence being electronic registers or isolated specialty records in a hospital, a study of 35 digital health interventions found.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: IeDA, REC-Maternité, IeDA-maternity, REC-PCIME, the diabetes and orthopaedics patient records of one university hospital and three electronic consultation registers. Single-function: NetSIGL 2.0, ReDoP-BF, MS Surveillance and the weekly surveillance telegram, the TLOH. Community: eSanteCom, the community health workers' digital tools, the community-based information sub-system, the community health workers' reports in EnDoS and the mHealth platform of the 2021 roadmap. Enabling: the Ministry of Health's mini-datacenter.

Not held. The position at primary clinics is not established, nor whether the pilot hospitals record at the point of care or key afterwards. Nothing held is dated within the last twelve months, and who finances the systems is not held.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A national pupil register was in pilot in 2024 and enrolment had not begun by June 2025; the number of schools is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The education ministry's statistics directorate issued the pilot-phase report on FIUE-BF, its name for a national file of pupils with unique identification, in February 2024. On 25 June 2025 the ministers of digital transition and basic education reviewed the national pupil file their technical teams are building, with technical and financial support from the WURI project. The World Bank's appraisal of 14 April 2025 finances a student management platform from IDA and the Global Partnership for Education.

Levels in use. At 25 June 2025 the national platform was meant to follow pupils from primary school to university, and enrolment had not begun.

How the record is kept. LuxDev states that in 2023 the establishments in the FIUE-BF pilot handled enrolment, the publication of school results and school identity cards online. The World Bank's appraisal of 14 April 2025 states that key education data are stored mainly as hard copies.

Last twelve months. LuxDev's project page shows the programme that supported tracking each pupil by a unique identifier as closed, having run from 4 May 2018 to 31 December 2025.

Primary schools doing the digital input. In February 2024 FIUE-BF was at its pilot phase, with generalisation still ahead. At 14 April 2025 the student management platform, with a smartphone application for schools, was still to be designed, and at 25 June 2025 progressive enrolment of pupils was due to start at the next school year.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the primary school statistical survey, keyed in STATEDUC 2, the Annuaire statistique primaire 2023-2024 and the Annuaire statistique 2019-2020 du MENAPLN. Single-function: the planned shared platform for examination and learner-assessment data. Learning: the planned digital platforms for in-service teacher training. Enabling: the Digital Acceleration Project.

Not held. The number of pilot establishments, and whether they are primary or secondary, is not held, and no source reports the pupil file past its pilot. No current plan naming the system is held, nor whether results are entered during the school year.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Birth-entry software was deployed in 170 civil registration centres in 2025; the total number of centres, and how many register in it, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Direction générale de la modernisation de l'état civil trains commune agents on the platform it calls VENEEM. The Stratégie nationale de l'état civil 2023-2027 was adopted by joint order on 25 January 2024. In 2024, 2.52% of its financing came from the state budget, 33.46% from local authorities and 64.01% from partners.

Events registered digitally. The country's delegate told an Abidjan symposium in December 2024 that births are declared directly from health centres through a national application. Agents of the commune of Pouni were trained from 31 August to 2 September 2026 to enter births, marriages and deaths.

How the record is made. The Pouni training covered entering events in real time by smartphone, at central offices and secondary centres alike. A November 2024 account says the platform captures events where they occur, on computers and smartphones. The Code des personnes et de la famille, in its 2025 text, institutes a national digital civil-status register.

Last twelve months. Agents of the commune of Koloko were trained on the software and given equipment, as reported on 22 June 2026. On 25 June 2026 the director general for the modernisation of civil status said deployment in communes was continuing.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The ministry reported the software deployed in 30 main and 140 secondary centres in 2025, and in 20 main and 96 secondary centres in 2024.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation and securing of civil-status acts; fully digitised registers in 30 communes; the register missions of the Direction générale de la modernisation de l'état civil at Padéma; the digitisation of birth registers at Safané; the Système d'Archivage électronique; the digitisation of existing acts; AMADE's digitisation and archiving of birth certificates; the digitisation of civil registers in Nobéré. Notification: the auxiliaires de l'état civil; the national application for the systematic declaration of civil-status events. Certificate: dematerialised civil-status procedures; Service Public BF; the plateforme de demandes d'actes d'état civil en ligne; the e-service. Outreach: birth certificates for people without one; the issue of civil-status acts to internally displaced people; UNHCR's delivery of birth certificates to displaced people; AMADE's identification and regularisation of children without birth certificates; the unités mobiles d'enregistrement; the opérations spéciales de transcription. Identity: the Carte Nationale d'Identité Burkinabè; the identité digitale; WURI; the identifiant unique électronique; the registre national de l'identifiant unique de la personne physique; the Autorité nationale de coordination de l'identification; UNHCR's registration of displaced people for identity cards; the Identifiant Unique du Citoyen à l'Etat Civil and the regional SIGIEC; the identity database of the Office National d'Identification. Enabling: Zama Tchè, the Maisons du citoyen; the networking of main civil-status centres; the public administration's modular datacentres; RESINA. Exchange: the interoperability protocol between the statistics institute and the civil-status directorate; interoperability between civil registry services and health facilities; the unique identifier.

Not held. The total number of centres, how many of the 170 register in the software and whether the 2025 count includes 2024's. Where the record is held, and whether deaths are entered beyond one commune's training.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A computerised occurrence book for police and gendarmerie stations was over 80% built in December 2023; whether any station uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The computerised main courante platform, as the security ministry names its occurrence-book system, belongs to the Ministère de l'Administration Territoriale, de la Décentralisation et de la Sécurité, whose director of information systems reported on it to a delegation of the digital transition ministry in late December 2023.

Records made digitally. In late December 2023 the platform was not yet operational: the digital transition ministry put it at more than 80 per cent built.

How the record is made. The platform is to replace the physical registers in which main courantes are kept at police commissariats and gendarmerie brigades, and in late December 2023 those registers were still the record.

Last twelve months. The ministry's director of information systems said in late December 2023 that the platform would be operational by mid-January 2024, once amendments were taken in.

Stations doing the digital entry. In late December 2023 no commissariat or gendarmerie brigade was using the computerised main courante.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the online criminal-record request, online cybercrime complaints and reporting, Justice Pénale en ligne, as the justice ministry names its platform for filing and following a complaint, the ministry's e-Permis de Communiquer for visits to detainees and the cybercrime brigade's reporting platform Alerte BCLCC. Single-function: video fining of traffic offences in Ouagadougou, in service since 1 August 2026. Enabling: the office equipment of the Galgouli border police post.

Not held. Nothing dated on or before 30 September 2026 says the platform went live in any commissariat or brigade. No plan, source of finance, count of stations or event in the last twelve months is held.
