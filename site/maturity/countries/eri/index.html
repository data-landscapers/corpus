1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Eritrea has no data protection law as of 2026 and no supervisory authority; whether a bill is being drafted is not known.

Authority. No supervisory authority has been established in Eritrea, as of 1 August 2026. The Horn of Africa Initiative's update of June 2025 recorded the country as without regulatory oversight for data protection, while Kenya, Somalia and Ethiopia had supervisory authorities.

Application. No enforcement decisions are published and none could be found, as of 1 August 2026.

Last twelve months. The position was unchanged at 1 August 2026: no law, no regulator and no enforcement.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No bill or draft of a data protection law is on record, and whether one is in preparation is not held. Nothing is held on regulations.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Reports are entered at zone and sub-zone offices (2024); no primary clinic is shown entering its own.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health implemented DHIS2 as its health information system, installed on the ministry's own local area networks, with HISP Tanzania supporting customisation, upgrades and training, as a DHIS2 Community post of March 2024 described it. The ministry's sector plan, HSSDP III, covers 2022 to 2026 and treats the HMIS and DHIS2 in its Data and Information section. It places what it calls the National Health Information System under the Minister's Office, with HMIS, ICT and System Development units.

Tiers in use. HSSDP III, dated October 2021, states that DHIS2 had been rolled out to about 40 per cent of facilities at secondary level and above.

Where data is digitised. WHO Eritrea states that by 2019 DHIS 2 was installed and rolled out in all zobas, the regional level, in its offline version. In 2022 it reported a functional DHIS-2 at national level, at sub-national level and in 44% of the districts. A DHIS2 Community post of March 2024 states that data enters the national DHIS2 from zones and sub-zones over the ministry's local networks, running offline.

Last twelve months. WHO Eritrea reports that in 2022 the DHIS 2 server was upgraded to the latest version, with new data analysis and data quality applications.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. HSSDP III states that, as of October 2021, data is collected from all health facilities through paper and electronic channels.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Ministry of Health's DHIS2 tracker program. Single-function: Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), and two systems the joint external evaluation of August 2025 recommended, a USSD-based national electronic reporting system and a mobile-based data system for essential health service use during emergencies.

Not held. Nothing held shows a primary clinic entering its own reports, and no share or count of clinics is held. How the system is financed is not stated, and no event dated within the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths were handwritten at local offices and entered in regional electronic registers one level up in 2017; current practice is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Eritrea has developed a five-year Strategic Civil Registration and Vital Statistics Plan for 2024-2028, recorded by the African Committee of Experts on the Rights and Welfare of the Child in observations adopted in April 2026.

Events registered digitally. Births and deaths are both registered in the sub-zoba's digital database after entry in the kebabi's handwritten register, according to a Dutch foreign ministry report of February 2017 cited by the Danish National ID Centre, which adds that death registration has no fixed or automatic routine.

How the record is made. A birth is first entered in the handwritten family register at the local kebabi office, which issues a transfer letter that the parents take to the sub-zoba office, where the data is entered into the database and the hard copies are archived (2017). A death goes the same way, by a "death letter" from the kebabi to the sub-zoba.

Last twelve months. The death certificate issued by the local municipality is computerised and carries a registration number, the US State Department said in 2025.

Local offices doing the digital entry. Each of the six zobas keeps an electronic population register, first introduced in zoba Maekel in 2003 and updated by the 54 sub-zoba administrations, while each of about 2,000 kebabi administrations keeps a handwritten family register, the Danish National ID Centre states, citing the European Asylum Support Office in May 2015.

Noted, not assessed. The civil registry office of the municipality of Asmara, which issues birth, marriage, divorce and death certificates; the reference number on residence cards, which Eritrea calls the ASC number; the digital population register kept in each zoba; the national identity card of the Department of Immigration and Nationality; and the Washington embassy's Consular Affairs Processing System for identity card applications from abroad.

Not held. How a birth is entered today, how many offices do the digital entry, who owns and pays for the registers, and whether the 2024-2028 plan names a digital system. The record-making facts date from 2015 and 2017, and nothing is held for the last twelve months.
