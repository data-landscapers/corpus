Maturity Assessment

Useful? Wrong? Tell us↓ PDF

The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Law in force. Eritrea has no separate cybercrime act. The Council of Europe cybercrime office's legal profile, in its version of 5 May 2020, maps the Penal Code to the Budapest Convention's offence articles as the domestic equivalent. Use of the law. The nearest held fact is the scope of the provisions themselves. The 2020 legal profile places unauthorized computer use and interference with data and systems under Article 374 of the Penal Code, and violation of privacy under Article 298, as the offences that stand in for a cybercrime act.

Standards body and its standards. Eritrea's standards body is the Eritrean Standards Institution, established by Proclamation No. 75 of 1995 as an autonomous body with legal personality. The proclamation makes it the sole national organ for standardisation, quality assurance and metrology, and gives it the power to prepare compulsory Eritrean Standards. The international standards organisation's member record gives 446 national standards published, and calls the institution the sole standardisation body in the country. The proclamation names no standard on information technology or data.

Certification. The 1995 proclamation covers quality assurance as well as standardisation and measurement. The record held names no scheme for certifying an organisation against a standard on information technology or data, and no organisation the institution has certified against any standard.
