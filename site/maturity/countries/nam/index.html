Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2007 one local authority of two surveyed gave some staff e-mail; how many are connected is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Application: the Digital Job Card tool, co-developed with the town councils of Rehoboth, Helao Nafidi, Opuwo and Rundu. Web: the CityMender Namibia reporting app, a private company's. Public access: public Wi-Fi hotspots at Radio Access Network sites and Rural Information Communication Technology Centres, the ministry's planned Community Access Points, the multi-purpose community centres planned in 2005 for each of the 13 regions, and digital kiosks proposed to the Windhoek municipality by an activist. City systems: the Innovate4Water smart-water pilot at Ongwediva, a N$245 million smart-city grant from China, and the smart city legal framework, beginning with the City of Windhoek, that Cabinet directed in September 2025. Backbone: the City of Windhoek's fibre-optic network, the 18,719.5 km national fibre-optic network and the Universal Service Fund's Radio Access Network sites, the public service's GRNNet and the GRN Intranet/Internet Gateway, the planned national datacentre, Paratus's Armada datacentre and the Virtual Space Data Centre, the Telecom Namibia Windhoek Datacenter and Paratus's Data Center at Brakwater. Capacity: the City of Windhoek's People-Centred Smart City Strategy 2026-2036. Upper tier: the regional councils' network link to their ministry and the Decentralisation Extension Programme, begun in 2006. Central: the Roads Authority's Project Control System, the satellite ground-data receiving station handed over in February 2026, and the regional offices of the Ministry of Information and Communication Technology.

Not held. The only evidence of a connection is a 2007 survey in which one unnamed local authority of two installs e-mail for some of its staff. Whether that authority is outside the capital, how many local authorities are connected, and anything since 2007 on governance or on the last twelve months are not held.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The capital city billed water accounts to named customers in its own system in 2024; whether any other local authority records revenue is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The City of Windhoek's billing system is its own, with the vendor BCX supplying the financial management software, The Namibian reported for February 2024.

What is recorded. The City of Windhoek bills water accounts for named customers in the system it calls Solar, with payments and arrears recorded against each account. A city memo of February 2024 found accounts imported from the financial system with wrong customer information, and a payment history missing from one account.

Where the record is held. In February 2024 the billing system ran on the city's own servers.

Last twelve months. On 2 July 2024 the City of Windhoek confirmed irregularities in its water billing system, with forensic investigators appointed and nine accounts found with wrong figures. The system keeps its logs for 30 days.

Local governments recording. The Namibian's report of July 2024 covers the City of Windhoek's billing system, the capital's, and names no other local authority.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Any local authority outside Windhoek, a total of those recording, and any of the city's accounts other than water. Nothing on who plans or pays for the systems, and no event in the last twelve months.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics report on paper, and district officers enter their monthly forms into the national reporting system (2026 study).

Governance, planning, finance. Managers interviewed for a study published in March 2026 said that the Ministry of Health and Social Services manages DHIS2 centrally, on a server at headquarters where national staff alone change its design, add indicators and run backups. They said that external donors pay for customising DHIS2, fund the training and buy the computers. Namibia launched a National Digital Health Policy for 2026 to 2036, a ten-year framework to strengthen health information systems, in February 2026 on WHO's account.

Tiers in use. The same managers said that DHIS2 is used at district, regional and national levels.

Where data is digitised. District HIS officers key the monthly paper summary forms sent in by health facilities into the DHIS2 aggregate module at the district office, on the managers' account of March 2026.

Last twelve months. The Ministry of Health and Social Services launched the National Digital Health Policy (2026-2036) in February 2026, which WHO says is to address fragmentation across health information systems.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The health minister told the National Assembly on 6 May 2025 that health data collection had expanded to over 90 clinics beyond district and hospital levels. The managers said in the study of March 2026 that primary health facilities still report on paper tools.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module, the Electronic Patient Management Information System (EPMIS), the Electronic Tuberculosis Register (ETR), PTracker, the Electronic Patient Monitoring System (EPMS) and the Electronic Dispensing Tool (EDT). Single-function: the Pharmacy Management Information System (PMIS), Meditech, SysPro and the COVID-19 electronic data system and dashboards. Community: the ministry's Digital Community System (DCS).

Not held. Which tiers use DHIS2 is not established, and no share of primary clinics entering their own reports is published: the count of over 90 clinics comes with no total. Nothing in the last twelve months changes where reports are entered.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic system is fully in place at only two hospitals; most hospitals and clinics keep paper records (2025 study).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health and Social Services launched its Integrated Health Care Information Management System (IHCIMS) in Windhoek in 2011, and its own IT department supports the ministry's information systems. Managers interviewed for a study published in March 2026 said the ministry has developed an e-Health Strategy, cited as the National eHealth Strategy 2021-2025, to guide a digital platform for capturing patient data. The minister's budget speech of 6 May 2025 asked N$62.5 million for health data and policy support, with expanding the e-Health network among its priorities, and Namibia launched a National Digital Health Policy for 2026 to 2036 in February 2026.

Tiers in use. IHCIMS, launched for every facility, was fully deployed only at Windhoek Central Hospital and Oshakati State Hospital, on the study of September 2025.

Where data is digitised. Practitioners reach a patient's record only through the paper health passport and manual records, and there is no unique patient identifier, the same study states. The minister said in March 2026 that the system still faces paper-based records.

Last twelve months. The managers said in the study of March 2026 that the platform was starting with a billing module on pilot at Katutura and Windhoek Central hospitals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The health minister said on 6 May 2025 that five health centres and 18 clinics had the e-Health system's network in operation. The study of September 2025 states that the ministry still relies on paper records in most hospitals and clinics.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module, the Electronic Patient Management Information System (EPMIS), the Electronic Tuberculosis Register (ETR), PTracker, the Electronic Patient Monitoring System (EPMS) and the Electronic Dispensing Tool (EDT). Single-function: the Pharmacy Management Information System (PMIS), Meditech, SysPro and the COVID-19 electronic data system and dashboards. Community: the ministry's Digital Community System (DCS).

Not held. No count or share of primary clinics keeping an electronic record is held: the five health centres and 18 clinics of May 2025 are sites with the network in place. No budget line names the record system itself.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2018, 807 of 1,848 schools were connected to the national school system and 103 piloted daily attendance entry; anything since is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. School-Link, an EDUPAC product, came to the Ministry of Education, Arts and Culture in 2013, was approved as the national system for all schools and is offered to them free, with a helpdesk in the ministry's ICT division. The 2017-18 attendance pilot cost NAD 291,000, met jointly by ADEA and the ministry.

Levels in use. Primary and secondary. The 103 schools that piloted attendance capture in School-Link from May 2018 include primary, combined and secondary schools.

How the record is kept. The pilot schools captured each day's teacher and learner attendance, with the reason for absence, and updated School-Link weekly through the trimester ending August 2018, recording 71,387 cases of learner absence. School-Link carries a learner unit record module, which the country calls LURITS. Outside the pilot, the ministry's November 2018 report with ADEA has paper registers marked daily by teachers.

Last twelve months. The attendance pilot ran from May to the end of July 2018.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The ministry's report with ADEA, published in November 2018, says 807 public schools out of 1,848 schools are connected to School-Link, at all levels together. Daily attendance was entered by the 103 pilot schools.

Noted, not assessed. Namibia calls its school census an EMIS: the ministry's Education Management Information System division has the education regions collect data twice a year from all state and private schools and publishes the Fifteenth School Day Report and the Annual Education Census, with 2,036 schools counted in January 2024. School-Write, a commercial school administration program on annual subscription, was in a considerable number of schools in 2018, and most private schools and a few urban public schools used an electronic biometric clocking system. National examinations are managed in OpenEMIS Exams, which gained verification, case management and online examination features in 2025. A September 2026 report names EduTV for school students, NOLeaMS for technical education and school connectivity and hotspots under the Universal Service and Access Fund and the operators.

Not held. Nothing held is dated after 2018, so the system's present ownership, plan, budget and direction are not established. The 807 schools are connected, not shown entering data, and no figure separates primary schools.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Local offices enter births and deaths in the national register, as reported in 2024; the share of offices doing so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety and Security registers births and deaths on the National Population Registration System, the statistics agency said in April 2024. The system was developed in the country with its own resources, by a UNICEF and UN case study of September 2023. Cabinet approved a civil registration plan for 2015/16 to 2020/21 in 2015.

Events registered digitally. The system's death module was deployed in 2012 and its birth module in 2013. The statistics agency counts 85,853 births and 24,388 deaths registered and captured on it in 2021.

How the record is made. The local civil registration office captures the application on the system and issues the certificate, and the record is available in real time across the 14 regions, the agency said in April 2024. Of births registered in 2021, 68.9 per cent were captured within 14 days, and 89.6 per cent of deaths.

Last twelve months. In December 2025 the ministry said it had deployed its staff to 24 state hospitals to issue birth certificates right after delivery.

Local offices doing the digital entry. Some registration offices in remote areas lack computers or internet connectivity, which delays capture, the statistics agency said in April 2024. Birth and death registration took place at local registration centres and 24 health facilities and hospitals, by the case study of September 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: historical birth records scanned into a store the ministry calls M-files, a project capturing old death records, the ministry's scanning unit and the Automation Project begun in 2010. Notification: the e-birth and e-death notification systems and a mobile identity prototype for submitting a birth from a phone. Certificate: a portal for verifying birth, marriage and death certificates and verification systems provided for in the 2024 Bill. Outreach: mobile registration campaigns, identity registration at Khomas schools, integrated outreach registration for San communities, the mass registration campaign of 2024 and planned mobile verification units. Identity: the electronic identity card, which the ministry calls the e-ID, the national identity card and its fingerprint system, remote biometric enrolment on hand-held devices, the African Guest Identity Card, the passport system and the card system IMAGO with its track-and-trace barcodes. Enabling: the National Public Key Infrastructure, regional and sub-regional civic affairs offices built or renovated and the government wide area network. Exchange: the data-exchange platform NamX, the National Data Exchange Service Bus, the register's integration with social protection, identity verification for social grants, a planned link from the grants system for reporting deaths, the transfer of registration data to the statistics agency and the transfer of death information to the Electoral Commission.

Not held. No count or share of local offices registering digitally is published, and the gap at remote offices has no figure. No civil registration plan later than 2020/21 is named.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2022 study of one region found stations recording by hand; the rest of the country is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Enabling: the police force's digital mobile radios, acquired in 2024/25 to replace the analogue system. Surveillance: the planned Smart City Project of cameras in Windhoek and Swakopmund, as at May 2025, and the CCTV coverage deployed to some facilities by April 2018. Identification: Forensic Science Services, which processed 2,898 cases in 2024/25, the Namibian Police Criminal Record Bureau and N-ABIS, the force's biometric identification system, as the ministry named it in April 2018.

Not held. The one statement that stations record by hand is a 2022 study of traffic-offence records in the Omusati region, on eight officers interviewed; nothing newer and nothing national is held. No source says what the force's e-policing system does or which stations use it, and no governance fact, station count or event of the last twelve months is held.
