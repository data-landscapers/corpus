Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics report on paper, and district officers enter their monthly forms into the national reporting system (2026 study).

Governance, planning, finance. Managers interviewed for a study published in March 2026 said that the Ministry of Health and Social Services manages DHIS2 centrally, on a server at headquarters where national staff alone change its design, add indicators and run backups. They said that external donors pay for customising DHIS2, fund the training and buy the computers. Namibia launched a National Digital Health Policy for 2026 to 2036, a ten-year framework to strengthen health information systems, in February 2026 on WHO's account.

Tiers in use. The same managers said that DHIS2 is used at district, regional and national levels.

Where data is digitised. District HIS officers key the monthly paper summary forms sent in by health facilities into the DHIS2 aggregate module at the district office, on the managers' account of March 2026.

Last twelve months. The Ministry of Health and Social Services launched the National Digital Health Policy (2026-2036) in February 2026, which WHO says is to address fragmentation across health information systems.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The health minister told the National Assembly on 6 May 2025 that health data collection had expanded to over 90 clinics beyond district and hospital levels. The managers said in the study of March 2026 that primary health facilities still report on paper tools.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module, the Electronic Patient Management Information System (EPMIS), the Electronic Tuberculosis Register (ETR), PTracker, the Electronic Patient Monitoring System (EPMS) and the Electronic Dispensing Tool (EDT). Single-function: the Pharmacy Management Information System (PMIS), Meditech, SysPro and the COVID-19 electronic data system and dashboards. Community: the ministry's Digital Community System (DCS).

Not held. Which tiers use DHIS2 is not established, and no share of primary clinics entering their own reports is published: the count of over 90 clinics comes with no total. Nothing in the last twelve months changes where reports are entered.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic system is fully in place at only two hospitals; most hospitals and clinics keep paper records (2025 study).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health and Social Services launched its Integrated Health Care Information Management System (IHCIMS) in Windhoek in 2011, and its own IT department supports the ministry's information systems. Managers interviewed for a study published in March 2026 said the ministry has developed an e-Health Strategy, cited as the National eHealth Strategy 2021-2025, to guide a digital platform for capturing patient data. The minister's budget speech of 6 May 2025 asked N$62.5 million for health data and policy support, with expanding the e-Health network among its priorities, and Namibia launched a National Digital Health Policy for 2026 to 2036 in February 2026.

Tiers in use. IHCIMS, launched for every facility, was fully deployed only at Windhoek Central Hospital and Oshakati State Hospital, on the study of September 2025.

Where data is digitised. Practitioners reach a patient's record only through the paper health passport and manual records, and there is no unique patient identifier, the same study states. The minister said in March 2026 that the system still faces paper-based records.

Last twelve months. The managers said in the study of March 2026 that the platform was starting with a billing module on pilot at Katutura and Windhoek Central hospitals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The health minister said on 6 May 2025 that five health centres and 18 clinics had the e-Health system's network in operation. The study of September 2025 states that the ministry still relies on paper records in most hospitals and clinics.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 tracker module, the Electronic Patient Management Information System (EPMIS), the Electronic Tuberculosis Register (ETR), PTracker, the Electronic Patient Monitoring System (EPMS) and the Electronic Dispensing Tool (EDT). Single-function: the Pharmacy Management Information System (PMIS), Meditech, SysPro and the COVID-19 electronic data system and dashboards. Community: the ministry's Digital Community System (DCS).

Not held. No count or share of primary clinics keeping an electronic record is held: the five health centres and 18 clinics of May 2025 are sites with the network in place. No budget line names the record system itself.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2018, 807 of 1,848 schools were connected to the national school system and 103 piloted daily attendance entry; anything since is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. School-Link, an EDUPAC product, came to the Ministry of Education, Arts and Culture in 2013, was approved as the national system for all schools and is offered to them free, with a helpdesk in the ministry's ICT division. The 2017-18 attendance pilot cost NAD 291,000, met jointly by ADEA and the ministry.

Levels in use. Primary and secondary. The 103 schools that piloted attendance capture in School-Link from May 2018 include primary, combined and secondary schools.

How the record is kept. The pilot schools captured each day's teacher and learner attendance, with the reason for absence, and updated School-Link weekly through the trimester ending August 2018, recording 71,387 cases of learner absence. School-Link carries a learner unit record module, which the country calls LURITS. Outside the pilot, the ministry's November 2018 report with ADEA has paper registers marked daily by teachers.

Last twelve months. The attendance pilot ran from May to the end of July 2018.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The ministry's report with ADEA, published in November 2018, says 807 public schools out of 1,848 schools are connected to School-Link, at all levels together. Daily attendance was entered by the 103 pilot schools.

Noted, not assessed. Namibia calls its school census an EMIS: the ministry's Education Management Information System division has the education regions collect data twice a year from all state and private schools and publishes the Fifteenth School Day Report and the Annual Education Census, with 2,036 schools counted in January 2024. School-Write, a commercial school administration program on annual subscription, was in a considerable number of schools in 2018, and most private schools and a few urban public schools used an electronic biometric clocking system. National examinations are managed in OpenEMIS Exams, which gained verification, case management and online examination features in 2025. A September 2026 report names EduTV for school students, NOLeaMS for technical education and school connectivity and hotspots under the Universal Service and Access Fund and the operators.

Not held. Nothing held is dated after 2018, so the system's present ownership, plan, budget and direction are not established. The 807 schools are connected, not shown entering data, and no figure separates primary schools.
