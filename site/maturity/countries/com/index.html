Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national reporting system reached all 91 public health facilities (WHO, 2025), but it is not known where their reports are entered.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's digital health strategy for 2022 to 2026, dated July 2021, puts the deployment of DHIS2 under its Direction de l'Information et des Statistiques Sanitaires. It schedules the extension of the national health information system with DHIS2 for January 2022 to December 2025 at US$0.60 million, all of it set against technical and financial partners. The World Bank reported in November 2024 that DHIS2 was deployed under an IDA-financed project implemented by the ministry.

Tiers in use. WHO Comoros reports that DHIS2 was extended to all 91 public-level health structures in 2025, which it calls a national extension.

Where data is digitised. The ministry's strategy of July 2021 describes the national health information system, then on GESIS, as semi-electronic and computerised at the level of the regions, with data passing from districts to regions by hand or by e-mail.

Last twelve months. During 2025, with WHO support, validation rules were put into DHIS2, a data quality self-assessment was run and a manual of standard data-management procedures was written.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. DHIS2 states that almost 99% of all public health facilities used DHIS2 for health service reporting in 2024. The World Bank counted 69 public health facilities reporting data in DHIS2 at 31 August 2024, against a target of 80 by December 2025. WHO Comoros gives the 91 structures reached in 2025 as 100% of all public-level structures.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the registre électronique de vaccination (REV). Single-function: DHIS2 disease surveillance, the DHIS2 Climate & Health instance for malaria early warning, the National Malaria Control Programme's weekly malaria data collection, the eLMIS tools DHIS2 Real-Time Stock Management and Open mSupply, the vaccination campaign platform WHO Comoros calls ODK/PADACOR, Surveillance Intégrée de la Maladie et la Riposte (SIMR) data entry in DHIS2 and KoboCollect, EIOS, the système d'information des ressources humaines en santé, GLASS, the ODK geolocation of suspected vaccine-preventable disease cases and adverse events, Kobo Collect in the 2024 cholera vaccination campaign, the cholera digital case register and dashboard and the planned Système National Automatisé de Collecte et de Saisie des naissances et des décès. Community: DHIS2 community health. Enabling: the planned Réseau National Numérique de la Santé (RNNS) and the planned Réseau national de télémédecine.

Not held. Nothing held says where DHIS2 reports are keyed or what share of primary clinics enter their own; the 99%, the 69 and the 100% count all public facilities. No tier is named, and no event dated within the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The Ministry of Health stated in July 2021 that the country has no electronic patient records; nothing since confirms or changes this.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's digital health strategy for 2022 to 2026, dated July 2021, lists the electronic patient record, which it calls the Dossier Electronique du Patient, among the components absent from the country. It plans a modular application for reception, consultations, tests, hospitalisation and billing, in more than 40 structures by the end of 2024.

Tiers in use. In July 2021 the ministry stated that the electronic patient record does not exist in the country.

Where data is digitised. The same strategy lists telemedicine, m-health applications and online training as absent alongside the patient record.

Last twelve months. The World Bank recorded a hospital management information system as not functional at 31 August 2024, against a target of functional by December 2025.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. In July 2021 only CHN El Maarouf, CHRI Hombo and the medical imaging centre had internet, and district health centres were rarely connected.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the registre électronique de vaccination (REV). Single-function: DHIS2 disease surveillance, the DHIS2 Climate & Health instance for malaria early warning, the National Malaria Control Programme's weekly malaria data collection, the eLMIS tools DHIS2 Real-Time Stock Management and Open mSupply, the vaccination campaign platform WHO Comoros calls ODK/PADACOR, Surveillance Intégrée de la Maladie et la Riposte (SIMR) data entry in DHIS2 and KoboCollect, EIOS, the système d'information des ressources humaines en santé, GLASS, the ODK geolocation of suspected vaccine-preventable disease cases and adverse events, Kobo Collect in the 2024 cholera vaccination campaign, the cholera digital case register and dashboard and the planned Système National Automatisé de Collecte et de Saisie des naissances et des décès. Community: DHIS2 community health. Enabling: the planned Réseau National Numérique de la Santé (RNNS) and the planned Réseau national de télémédecine.

Not held. Nothing dated after July 2021 confirms or replaces the ministry's statement, which does not say in words that records are on paper. The hospital management information system is not said to hold the clinical record.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools have entered marks since June 2025, and 45% of public primary schools were integrated by February 2026; the share entering marks is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of National Education supervises e-Msomo, validates its data and authorises the printing of report cards, its deputy secretary general said on 25 November 2025. The digital agency ANADEN places the platform within the Comores Numérique 2028 strategy. In June 2025 it was reported as funded by UNICEF.

Levels in use. Primary and secondary. At 2 February 2026 the ministry counted all public secondary schools and 45% of public primary schools as integrated into e-Msomo.

How the record is kept. School heads enrol pupils and generate report cards, and teachers log in to enter marks that cannot be changed afterwards; each pupil gets a school identification number (November 2025). In February 2026 schools' administrative teams were entering pupil lists, teacher lists and marks statements.

Last twelve months. By 25 November 2025 the ministry gave registration as 100% in Ngazidja and Mwali and 65% in Ndzuwani. On 2 February 2026 it gave late schools one more week and announced that the platform's single report card will be the only document accepted for university enrolment.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The ministry's secretary general put the rate of integration into e-Msomo at 45% of public primary schools on 2 February 2026. It is a rate of schools integrated.

Noted, not assessed. e-Shiyo, an online library of approved textbooks for pre-primary and primary pupils.

Not held. The number of schools behind the 45%, the share of primary schools entering marks, and any figure for private primary schools. Whether a national plan names e-Msomo, and any domestic finance for it.
