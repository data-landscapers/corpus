Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Comoros enacted a personal data protection law in 2021; no decision under it is on record, and whether it is in force is not known.

Authority. The law institutes a supervisory body, which its Article 19 names the Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés, with nine members. As of mid-2024 the authority had not been formally constituted or made operational.

Standing. The Assembly of the Union adopted Loi n°14-029/AU on the protection of personal data, in 68 articles, in plenary session on 26 June 2014. The Union of Comoros enacted its Protection of Personal Data Law on 29 June 2021, as a comprehensive framework.

Regulations. Under Article 24 of the law, the nine members of the commission are named by decree.

Application. The authority that the law gives the task of supervision was not operational as of mid-2024.

Last twelve months. The latest dated position is that of mid-2024, when the authority had not been formally constituted.

AI legislation / regulations: AI law or binding rule

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A media code in force since February 2026 requires AI-generated content to be labelled; there is no law on AI itself.

Binding rule on AI in force. The Code of Information and Communication, adopted by Law 25-021/AU of 26 December 2025 and promulgated by decree on 2 February 2026, requires any content generated or heavily modified by AI to be labelled as such, under its article 231. The media regulator, the Conseil national de la presse et de l'audiovisuel, can impose penalties of up to 1,000,000 Comorian francs.

General AI law and the law applied. The rule in force covers one use, AI-generated content in the media, and sits in a code that governs the press and broadcasting. The Comoros has no law on AI itself applying across sectors, and no bill or draft of one appears in any source dated to September 2026. No penalty imposed under article 231 is on record either.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The 2021 data protection law governs sending personal data abroad on a risk-based model, and it is not known whether the rule has been applied.

Rule in force. The Protection of Personal Data Law, enacted on 29 June 2021, governs international transfers of personal data, according to TechHive Advisory's review of the Comoros data protection law, published on 23 July 2025. The review describes a risk-based model for sending data abroad. Under it, a transfer can rest on an adequacy finding about the receiving country, on appropriate safeguards, or on one of the derogations the law provides.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest fact held is the model itself: the 2021 law sets three routes for an international transfer, which are adequacy of the receiving country, appropriate safeguards and derogations. The 2025 review sets out these three routes as the law's way of governing transfers, and it dates the law to 29 June 2021, the day of its enactment. That date is the latest one held for the rule on sending data abroad.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Nothing held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad, or has applied the rule to a transfer.

Mobile penetration: people who own a mobile phone

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2024, 63.1 per cent of people aged 10 and over owned a mobile phone, by an international estimate.

Share of people who own a mobile phone, with its year. In 2024, 63.1 per cent of people aged 10 and over in Comoros owned a mobile phone, an estimate by the International Telecommunication Union. That leaves 36.9 per cent without a phone of their own. The same series put the share at 60.6 per cent in 2021, 62.9 per cent in 2022 and 63 per cent in 2023. Since 2021 the share rose by 2.5 points. Every figure since 2021 is an estimate, not a survey result. The share is past half and 1.9 points short of 65 per cent, which is two in three. The figure counts a person once, whatever the number of SIM cards they hold, and counts a phone as owned where the person has it for their own use.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No figure of 65 per cent or more is held. No national survey or census figure for 2024 or later is held.
