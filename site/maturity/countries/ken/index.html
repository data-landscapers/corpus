Governance and finance. The plan names the ICT Authority as the main coordinating and implementing agency, under an inter-ministerial committee and an oversight committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary (2022).

Data storage / cloud strategy: hosting policy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Kenya's Cloud Policy took effect in May 2025 and gives State bodies 24 months to migrate; the list of approved providers is not yet published.

Policy adopted. The Kenya Cloud Policy, 2025 was signed on 7 April 2025 and took effect on 2 May 2025 on publication in the Kenya Gazette. It requires government data classified as sensitive, top secret, secret or restricted to be stored within Kenya. Implementation guidelines published by the Ministry of ICT and the Digital Economy bar public entities from hosting systems and data in their own server rooms without the authorisation of a Cloud Adoption Committee, and give all State organs 24 months to migrate systems that are not exempted.

Named hosting and moves. As reported on 6 August 2026, applications for accreditation as a government cloud service provider are open, to a Cloud Adoption Committee that is yet to be established. One provider is to be chosen from the accredited list to host all State data, and the list of eligible providers is yet to be published.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Kenya's 2019 Data Protection Act is in force and its commissioner has fined businesses; the number of decisions in any one year is not known.

Authority. The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner was established under the Data Protection Act, 2019, and its first commissioner has been in office since 2020. In March 2025 it had offices in Nairobi, Kisumu, Mombasa, Eldoret, Machakos, Nyeri and Nakuru.

Standing. The Data Protection Act, 2019 is in force. The ICT Cabinet Secretary told the Senate in May 2026 that the Act is being enforced, and the office has issued fines under it since 2022.

Regulations. The Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021 are in force under the Act. They set 24-month registration certificates and make processing on an expired certificate an offence.

Application. As at September 2024 the office had fined at least nine businesses a total of KES 26,275,000. Its bulletin states that by March 2025 it had issued 215 determinations and imposed penalties on 10 entities.

Last twelve months. In August 2026 the office published a draft guidance note on transfers of personal data to offshore AI providers. On 28 August 2026 it gave holders of expired registration certificates until 11 September 2026 to renew or face enforcement.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Law in force. The Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act stands. On 3 September 2026 Kenya launched an official guide to investigating and prosecuting 76 listed cyber offences under it, presented as the common operational framework under the Act. The Nation reports that it has been amended, widening investigators' and courts' powers. Two false-publication sections were struck down by the Court of Appeal, and the Act otherwise stands.

Use of the law. The nearest held fact is the machinery built on the Act: Kenya's cybersecurity framework rests on the National Computer and Cybercrimes Coordination Committee under the Act, to which the new National Cybersecurity Agency (Legal Notice 89 of 2026) adds capacity. The Act was also put in place to address risks on digital platforms. Both items date from September 2026, the same month as the prosecution guide, and the Committee and the Agency sit side by side in that framework.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Kenya's digital identity system rests on a 2018 executive order and 2020 regulations, added to a 1947 act; it has no law of its own.

Law in force. Kenya has no stand-alone digital identity law. Its digital identity system, the National Integrated Identity Management System, began under executive order no. 1 of 2018, in which the president directed a central master population database to be built. The government then wrote the system into the 1947 Registration of Persons Act by an omnibus amendment, and no Act of its own was passed for it. The High Court ruled in January 2020 that an appropriate and comprehensive regulatory framework should be enacted before the system was rolled out.

Digital identity covered and rules made. With no identity law of its own in force, none provides for a digital identity. The system is anchored on the Huduma Namba regulations of 2020, and civil society organisations objected that regulations cannot create a system of that weight. The inserted section 9A has the system keep a national population register and assign each person a unique number. Kenya therefore answers the first question only.

Noted, not assessed. Electronic birth notification (e-notification) system; Unique Personal Identifier (UPI); Digital Maisha Card; Maisha Namba; Huduma Namba; National Integrated Identity Management System (NIIMS).

Not held. No source held says a national identity law is in force.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Thirty-three of 47 counties had automated revenue collection by 2020, issuing electronic receipts; whether they hold a register of payers is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. On 7 February 2019 the President directed a task force to deliver one revenue management system for all 47 counties, and none had been delivered by September 2020. A 2018 policy brief says most counties that had automated did not own their system. Standards for county revenue automation, gazetted on 27 July 2026, say counties are to own their systems and revenue data. An October 2026 commentary puts about 70 per cent of counties on contracts that pay a vendor up to 15 per cent of collections, on the writer's own figures.

What is recorded. A 2020 paper describes automation as the move from paper receipts to an electronic system, with point-of-sale machines. In 2018, 97 per cent of the 34 counties with a system used only its collection module.

Where the record is held. A 2020 survey of 24 counties found that most could not host their revenue data, which the vendor hosted. In 2018, 14 of the 34 counties used one vendor's cloud service.

Last twelve months. The Vision 2030 Delivery Secretariat's review of all 47 counties, dated 23 February 2026, names five with well-established systems and cashless collection: Murang'a, Nairobi City, Machakos, Laikipia and Nyeri.

Local governments recording. Thirty-three of the 47 counties, 70 per cent, had automated their revenues, some of them in part, by a paper in the European Scientific Journal of September 2020. In February 2026 six of the 47 relied heavily on manual and cash-based systems, by the Secretariat's review.

Noted, not assessed. The national government's Human Resource Information System, to which counties are moving their payrolls; the National Treasury's IFMIS; Kiambu County's CountyPro, its accounting and reporting system.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Facilities across the country enter their reports online into the national system (2024), but no figure shows how many primary clinics do so themselves.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health uses the Kenya Health Information System (KHIS) to collect health data from facilities nationwide, as IDinsight reported in October 2024. The ministry's strategic plan for July 2023 to June 2027 takes most of its indicator baselines and targets from KHIS. USAID's HealthIT grant committed US$22,528,318 over 2016 to 2021 to DHIS2 implementation and use.

Tiers in use. KHIS Aggregate collects data from health facilities nationwide and is designed for every level of the health system, from the national down to the community, as IDinsight described it in October 2024.

Where data is digitised. Facilities record services in paper daily registers, summarise them at the end of the week or month, and enter and submit the summaries online in KHIS, on IDinsight's account of October 2024.

Last twelve months. Legal Notice 77 of 2025, in force from 11 April 2025, made the Digital Health Agency responsible for maintaining the Health Management Information Services Platform for reporting.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Ministry of Health's mid-term review reports that 94% of all health facilities submitted reports within KHIS in 2019/20, up from 89% in 2017/18.
