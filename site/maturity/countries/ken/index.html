Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Kenya's digital identity system rests on a 2018 executive order and 2020 regulations, added to a 1947 act; it has no law of its own.

Law in force. Kenya has no stand-alone digital identity law. Its digital identity system, the National Integrated Identity Management System, began under executive order no. 1 of 2018, in which the president directed a central master population database to be built. The government then wrote the system into the 1947 Registration of Persons Act by an omnibus amendment, and no Act of its own was passed for it. The High Court ruled in January 2020 that an appropriate and comprehensive regulatory framework should be enacted before the system was rolled out.

Digital identity covered and rules made. With no identity law of its own in force, none provides for a digital identity. The system is anchored on the Huduma Namba regulations of 2020, and civil society organisations objected that regulations cannot create a system of that weight. The inserted section 9A has the system keep a national population register and assign each person a unique number. Kenya therefore answers the first question only.

Not held. No source held says a national identity law is in force.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

4 Operating Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Kenya gave electronic signatures legal effect and made certification rules by 2020; nothing is known of licences or penalties in the last three years.

Law in force. The Business Laws (Amendment) Act, 2020 came into force on 18 March 2020 and allows advanced electronic signatures in place of wet-ink signatures in nearly all legal documents, amending among others the Law of Contract Act. Under the Kenya Communications Act 1998 as amended in 2009, an advanced electronic signature has the same legal effect as a handwritten one.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The Kenya Communications (Electronic Transactions) Regulations, 2009 set licensing, operating and liability rules for certification service providers. Valid advanced electronic signatures are issued by providers licensed under the Kenya Information and Communications (Electronic Certification and Domain Name Administration) Regulations 2020. On 1 September 2020 the Communications Authority required all foreign electronic certification providers to apply for recognition within 30 days. As at October 2020 only two certification service providers were licensed, and neither had yet started issuing advanced electronic signatures.
