Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All 47 counties were on the government network by 2017 and work in national systems; how many county offices are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy presents the County Connectivity Project as its own, with two further phases started in 2022. The ICT Authority's plan for 2024 to 2027 states that county governments have not prioritised ICT investment and rely on national government initiatives for their connectivity.

What the office does over it. The Controller of Budget's review of the year to 30 June 2026, as the Daily Nation reports it, has county governments processing payroll in the national Human Resource Information System, which Kenya calls HRIS-Ke, with at least Sh8.3 billion in salaries still paid by manual payroll outside it. The National Treasury's draft payment policy of August 2026 says the Central Bank rolled out internet banking for county governments.

How the office is connected. The ministry states that the counties were connected by secure connections into the national private governmental voice and data network, which it calls NOFBI, the last of them by 2017.

Last twelve months. On 25 August 2026 the National Treasury's Cabinet Secretary announced that all 47 county governments must finish integrating the national electronic procurement platform with its financial management system by 30 September 2026. In the year to June 2026 counties cited connectivity challenges among their reasons for manual payroll.

Local governments connected. The ministry states that Phase I, in 2012 and 2013, connected 28 counties and Nairobi, and Phase 2, completed in 2017, the remaining 18 of the 47 it names.

Noted, not assessed. Application: the payroll system HRIS-Ke; the Central Bank of Kenya's internet banking for government; the Treasury's financial management system, IFMIS; the electronic government procurement system, e-GP; the Integrated Personnel Payroll Database; county revenue collection systems and internal office automation, scored in all 47 counties; the County Revenue Management Systems for which standards were gazetted on 27 July 2026; Nairobi's county revenue collection; digital identification promised for Nairobi's county enforcement officers; Nakuru County's case-management platform for gender-based violence and persons with disabilities; the county digital land records programme of FAO and the Council of Governors; the farm marketplace Mkulima Bora, piloted in five counties; the county health data banks the Digital Health Act requires; the Social Protection Registry; the National Addressing System, in development; and the Kenya Integrated Agricultural Management Information System, proposed in a draft Bill. Web: eCitizen, with services of at least 35 county governments; the proposed Kenya Diaspora Investment Platform; Nairobi Pay; county front-end service platforms, scored in all 47 counties; the Nairobi City County website. Public access: the national digital hubs and public Wi-Fi; ward ICT hubs, 285 of 1,450 completed; the JiConnect public Wi-Fi hotspots; nine public Wi-Fi hotspots in Kirinyaga County; 300 public Wi-Fi hotspots under a World Bank project; 37 partner digital hubs in Garissa and Turkana counties. City systems: the Nairobi Safe City surveillance network, intelligent transport system and smart water meters; Nairobi's traffic management agreement with IBM, and Konza Technopolis; urban surveillance systems supplied by Chinese firms; the National Police Service's facial recognition on live CCTV feeds in Nairobi and Mombasa. Backbone: the national fibre backbone, NOFBI; the One Government Network; the Digital Superhighway; the national fibre optic connectivity network; the national backbone and cross-border links put to tender on 15 September 2026; the World Bank-financed middle-mile fibre and last-mile framework; the last-mile county connectivity network's maintenance budget; a proposed harmonised framework for fibre deployment and wayleave charges; the Konza National Data Centre and the ICT Authority's two government data centres. Central: Huduma centres; the land-services platform ArdhiSasa; the Development Partners Management Information System; the health information system the Digital Health Act establishes; the Civil Registration and Vital Statistics System.

Not held. The share of county government offices connected: the ministry's 47 of 47 counts counties, and nothing since 2017 gives a figure. Where the staff working in the national systems sit, how those offices are connected, and any plan, finance or connection event dated in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Thirty-three of 47 counties had automated revenue collection by 2020, issuing electronic receipts; whether they hold a register of payers is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. On 7 February 2019 the President directed a task force to deliver one revenue management system for all 47 counties, and none had been delivered by September 2020. A 2018 policy brief says most counties that had automated did not own their system. Standards for county revenue automation, gazetted on 27 July 2026, say counties are to own their systems and revenue data. An October 2026 commentary puts about 70 per cent of counties on contracts that pay a vendor up to 15 per cent of collections, on the writer's own figures.

What is recorded. A 2020 paper describes automation as the move from paper receipts to an electronic system, with point-of-sale machines. In 2018, 97 per cent of the 34 counties with a system used only its collection module.

Where the record is held. A 2020 survey of 24 counties found that most could not host their revenue data, which the vendor hosted. In 2018, 14 of the 34 counties used one vendor's cloud service.

Last twelve months. The Vision 2030 Delivery Secretariat's review of all 47 counties, dated 23 February 2026, names five with well-established systems and cashless collection: Murang'a, Nairobi City, Machakos, Laikipia and Nyeri.

Local governments recording. Thirty-three of the 47 counties, 70 per cent, had automated their revenues, some of them in part, by a paper in the European Scientific Journal of September 2020. In February 2026 six of the 47 relied heavily on manual and cash-based systems, by the Secretariat's review.

Noted, not assessed. The national government's Human Resource Information System, to which counties are moving their payrolls; the National Treasury's IFMIS; Kiambu County's CountyPro, its accounting and reporting system.

Not held. No source says counties hold a register of payers, and no count of counties recording is dated after 2020. No source says any county has complied with the July 2026 standards.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Facilities across the country enter their reports online into the national system (2024), but no figure shows how many primary clinics do so themselves.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health uses the Kenya Health Information System (KHIS) to collect health data from facilities nationwide, as IDinsight reported in October 2024. The ministry's strategic plan for July 2023 to June 2027 takes most of its indicator baselines and targets from KHIS. USAID's HealthIT grant committed US$22,528,318 over 2016 to 2021 to DHIS2 implementation and use.

Tiers in use. KHIS Aggregate collects data from health facilities nationwide and is designed for every level of the health system, from the national down to the community, as IDinsight described it in October 2024.

Where data is digitised. Facilities record services in paper daily registers, summarise them at the end of the week or month, and enter and submit the summaries online in KHIS, on IDinsight's account of October 2024.

Last twelve months. Legal Notice 77 of 2025, in force from 11 April 2025, made the Digital Health Agency responsible for maintaining the Health Management Information Services Platform for reporting.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Ministry of Health's mid-term review reports that 94% of all health facilities submitted reports within KHIS in 2019/20, up from 89% in 2017/18.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: KenyaEMR, KHIS Tracker, TIBU, KeHMIS, the National Data Warehouse, Ushauri, Text for Adherence and SPICE. Single-function: the National Public Health Intelligence Information System, the All Disease Outbreak Module, the ports of entry surveillance module, the outbreak response data system, the National Logistics Management Information System, the KEMSA iLMIS, the Laboratory Information Management System, the blood system DAMUKe, the National Logistics Management Information Services Platform, the logistics management information system for county orders, KHIS Event Capture, the event-based surveillance system, the Pharmacy and Poisons Board's reporting system, the Track-and-Trace system for pharmaceuticals, Mobile Laboratory and the MYDAWA pharmacovigilance system. Community: the electronic community health information system (eCHIS), TIBU lite, the reach52 platform and the community health information system of USAID's 2016 grant. Enabling: Maisha Namba, Biometric Health Identification, the national and county data banks and national health cloud, the National Health Data Centre and the National Optic Fibre Backbone Infrastructure links.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the 94% is a reporting rate. No financing for KHIS dated within the last three years is held, and no dated event in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Public dispensaries, health centres and sub-county hospitals were reported moved onto the government's digital health system in September 2026; shared records are not confirmed.

Governance, planning, finance. The US-Kenya Cooperation Framework signed on 4 December 2025 describes TaifaCare HMIS, a hospital management system with clinical modules, as the Government of Kenya's flagship system for health facilities, deployed and maintained by the government. It sets deployment at 2,000 public and faith-based facilities by 2026, 4,000 by 2027 and 8,000 by 2028. The United States intends to provide US$175 million for data systems, and Kenya intends to pay the ongoing licensing, cloud and hardware costs the United States does not.

Tiers in use. By 30 September 2026 all public Level 2 to 4 facilities were reported migrated from the Social Health Authority's provider portal to HMIS, the government's name for the facility system.

Where data is digitised. At the private network Penda Health, clinical officers recorded consultations directly in a cloud-based EMR at the point of care between April and July 2025. Regulations in force from 11 April 2025 require certified systems to query and update a shared health record within twenty-four hours of each encounter.

Last twelve months. The government set 30 September 2026 for facilities to move to HMIS, then extended the deadline to 30 October. By 6 October 38 of 518 faith-based facilities had moved, beside 6,427 public ones.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Framework states that TaifaCare HMIS was deployed in 1,500 public health facilities by 4 December 2025. Penda Health ran the same EMR in its 16 primary care clinics in Nairobi and Kiambu counties in 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: KenyaEMR, KHIS Tracker, TIBU, KeHMIS, the National Data Warehouse, Ushauri, Text for Adherence and SPICE. Single-function: the National Public Health Intelligence Information System, the All Disease Outbreak Module, the ports of entry surveillance module, the outbreak response data system, the National Logistics Management Information System, the KEMSA iLMIS, the Laboratory Information Management System, the blood system DAMUKe, the National Logistics Management Information Services Platform, the logistics management information system for county orders, KHIS Event Capture, the event-based surveillance system, the Pharmacy and Poisons Board's reporting system, the Track-and-Trace system for pharmaceuticals, Mobile Laboratory and the MYDAWA pharmacovigilance system. Community: the electronic community health information system (eCHIS), TIBU lite, the reach52 platform and the community health information system of USAID's 2016 grant. Enabling: Maisha Namba, Biometric Health Identification, the national and county data banks and national health cloud, the National Health Data Centre and the National Optic Fibre Backbone Infrastructure links.

Not held. Nothing held shows a patient's record retrieved at another facility in practice, or what share of primary clinics have clinicians entering patient records. Use across the national referral hospitals is not established, and no budget line for the system is named.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Primary and secondary schools register each pupil on the national system (2026); how many primary schools do so, and any attendance entry, is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Education's National Education Sector Strategic Plan 2023-2027 names the Kenya Education Management Information System (KEMIS), as the ministry calls it, and carries costed yearly lines for developing it. Techweez reported in September 2026 that KEMIS is funded through the World Bank-backed Kenya Primary Education Equity in Learning Programme.

Levels in use. NEMIS, the system KEMIS replaces, covered all basic education institutions, primary and secondary, as Development Gateway and IREX reported in May 2024. KEMIS is built to hold data from early childhood education through to university, on Techweez's account of September 2026.

How the record is kept. A ministry circular published on 17 August 2026 tells schools to enter and correct each learner's record in KEMIS themselves: examination or assessment number, names, demographic details and parent or guardian identification. Schools entered pathway, subject and senior-school choices against the records of nearly 1.2 million Grade 9 learners in September 2026.

Last twelve months. KEMIS became the required platform for the 2026 Grade 10 selection, and its Learner Validation Engine was operational by August 2026. In September 2026 schools reported failed logins, slow responses and submissions that did not save. In late August 2026 the Principal Secretary told the Public Accounts Committee that the latest capitation payment used manually verified data because KEMIS was not yet complete.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The World Bank reported in March 2024 that about 60 per cent of primary school learners were registered in NEMIS.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the capitation verification exercise of September 2025, the education data validation exercise of 2025 and the Education Statistical Booklets. Single-function: the Teacher Management Information System, the Financial Management Information System, the Teachers Service Commission's TPAD system and the online item portal for competency-based assessment. Standalone: Nyansapo AI. Learning: the Digital Literacy Programme.

Not held. No source describes schools entering attendance or results for each learner during the year, and no rural figure is held. No share or count of primary schools entering data is held for either system: two figures for 2021, 65 and 90 per cent, disagree and count schools that had submitted data.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

More than 270,000 births were registered digitally from hospitals in two months of 2026; how many offices or hospitals use the system is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Civil Registration Services sits in the Ministry of Interior and National Administration's State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services, under an executive order of January 2023. ESTDEV, the implementing partner, said in September 2026 that it funded the development of the digital birth registration service, with project activities funded by Germany and the European Union. A costed plan for 2018 to 2022 listed the upgrade and rollout of the registration system among its high priorities.

Events registered digitally. More than 270,000 births were registered in two months through the new digital birth registration solution, ESTDEV said in September 2026. The principal secretary said the same system may in future be used for deaths.

How the record is made. Parents confirm the birth and enter the data while still in the hospital, and it goes directly to the population registry, with the certificate issued the same day or within days, on ESTDEV's account of September 2026.

Last twelve months. The solution's first two months were the first time Kenyan parents could register a birth online, ESTDEV said in September 2026. The Star reported in March 2026 that the Civil Registration Service led the validation of the Births and Deaths Registration Bill 2025, which gives a legal framework for digital registration.

Local offices doing the digital entry. Civil Registration Services reported for 2023 that it had offices in 153 of 380 sub-counties, where registration was manual, on paper registers.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitisation of more than 42 million birth registration records in fiscal year 2025/2026 and the earlier digitisation of historical births and deaths records. Notification: electronic birth notification from hospitals to the civil registration office, which the government calls e-notification, the planned e-notification for deaths, and two ended pilots, MoVE-IT and a smartphone birth registration form. Certificate: birth certificate application, download and printing on eCitizen, Huduma Centres, marriage certificates on e-Citizen and certificates printed by sub-county registrars. Outreach: the Rapid Results Initiative in Bungoma, an integrated service outreach in Kwale, Civil Registration and Digital Identity sensitisation and a nationwide mobile registration campaign. Identity: the Unique Personal Identifier, which the country calls Maisha Namba, the Maisha Integrated Population Register, Live Capture Units, the online ID application verification service, the digital Maisha Card, the National Registration Bureau database, the revised national identification card registration system, the Maisha Digital ID pilot, the National Integrated Identity Management System, the Automated Fingerprint Identification System, Biometric Health Identification, the refugee database, the refugee ID system, the register of persons and Smile ID. Enabling: portable registration kits for civil registration, requested in October 2025. Exchange: integration of the civil registration system with hospital management systems, in progress, integration of civil registration with the national ID, the planned exchange between the social protection registry and Civil Registration Services, the link from the learner identifier in KEMIS to civil registration records, learner registration in NEMIS by birth certificate number, the planned automatic national ID registration at 18 from civil registration data, the planned integration of the anti-corruption commission's systems with the population register, the Maisha Integrated Database and the MS Excel template for monthly statistics.

Not held. The number of hospitals or offices using the system is not held. Nor is whether the entry is made at a registration office, or a plan for the present period.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Seventy-six stations around Nairobi had a digital occurrence book in 2023; in 2026 one was still being procured, and its use now is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Police Service presented the programme that carries digital Occurrence Books to Parliament on 13 May 2026 as its own investment, and the Interior Cabinet Secretary said on 1 April 2026 that the government was itself procuring the digital occurrence book. On 26 March 2025 the service asked development partners for Sh20 billion for digitisation, the digital Occurrence Book included.

Records made digitally. The occurrence book. The service's annual report for 2022 says its Digital Occurrence Book was launched at Kasarani Sub-County, Nairobi, on 21 November 2019, and on 30 June 2023 the Interior Cabinet Secretary said complaints, statements and details of criminal activity made at stations are recorded in it.

How the record is made. On 30 June 2023 the Deputy Inspector General of Administration Police said reports entered at the station were open to monitoring by ward, county and regional commanders and the Inspector General.

Last twelve months. On 13 May 2026 the Inspector General told a parliamentary committee that digital Occurrence Books would come within three to four months. On 28 August 2026 the Interior Cabinet Secretary said they would accompany police communication centres set up first in Nairobi, Mombasa, Nyeri, Nakuru, Eldoret and Kisumu.

Stations doing the digital entry. The Star reports that 76 police stations in and around Nairobi had their Occurrence Books digitised by 30 June 2023. On 13 May 2026 the Inspector General told MPs the service did not yet have digital Occurrence Books.

Noted, not assessed. Surveillance: the IC3 command centre at police headquarters and its number-plate recognition cameras; the Integrated Public Safety Communication and Surveillance System and the CCTV Installation Project; the Nairobi Safe City system; a CCTV facial recognition network proposed for six cities; the planned IC4 system and body-worn cameras; police communication centres and CCTV cameras in about 1,200 police stations. Single-function: instant fines for traffic offences and the Electronic Crash System, e-Crash. Personnel: the Utumishi Project's HR information system, duty roster and patrol system, the automation of police administration, the National Police Reservists' data and an AI-powered deployment system. Statistics: the Utumishi Project's report generator for crime trends. Identification: the DCI National Forensic Laboratory. Public: police services on eCitizen, and the police complaints system and police clearance on Gava Mkononi. Enabling: network connectivity and ICT equipment sought from development partners in March 2025.

Not held. What became of the Nairobi system after June 2023, and whether any station outside the capital area records digitally. No domestic budget line and no dated plan naming the system.
