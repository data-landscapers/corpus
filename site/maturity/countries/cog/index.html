Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In February 2026 a World Bank-funded project was asked to extend internet access to town halls; how many communes are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Internet access for town halls falls under the Projet d'accélération de la transformation numérique (Patn), which the World Bank finances for US$100 million and whose 2026 work plan and budget was adopted at XAF 21.872 billion on 26 February 2026, according to ACI. The Bank's appraisal of May 2022 expects shared connectivity to be supplied to decentralised local authorities.

What the office does over it. The access is meant to let public administrations interconnect with the state's digital platforms.

How the office is connected. The project is to provide internet access within the public administrations themselves, town halls included.

Last twelve months. At its fifth session on 26 February 2026 the Patn steering committee recommended continuing and intensifying the extension of internet access and approved reallocating funds towards the connectivity component.

Local governments connected. Town halls are named with ministries and hospitals among the administrations to be given access. The May 2022 appraisal planned fibre for 22 ministries, departments and agencies in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire, national and local government service providers alike.

Noted, not assessed. Application: the Plateforme de dématérialisation des recettes des collectivités locales (PDRCL), the Système intégré des faits d'état-civil (Sifec), the FOUTA electronic payment platform for public revenue, the platform for evaluating the transparency code, the système de gestion de l'état civil of the state reform plan and the Système de localisation mixte for national addressing. Backbone: the Projet de Couverture Nationale (PCN), the Central African Backbone, the government network of the ministry of posts, telecommunications and digital economy, the Intranet gouvernemental, the ARPCE data centres in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire and the Congo National Data Center, the national data centre of the digital roadmap, and the Data Center National and interoperability layer of the national digital architecture. Public access: the Patn's centres multimédias. Equipment: computer equipment supplied by UNHCR to civil-status services, the town hall of Mvoumvou among them. Web: the online presence of municipalities. Central: the Registre social unique, SIGFIP and e-Tax, the NIU project, the national file and network of the direction de l'identification civile, the Portail e-Citizen and the national digital identity, the one-stop shop of the agence congolaise pour la création des entreprises and the chaîne pénale and chaîne civile software.

Not held. No source says any town hall is connected: no count, share or place is given, and no use over a connection is named. Who owns the connection is not held within the last three years.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A state platform for local governments' revenue was validated in February 2026; whether any local government yet records on it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Plateforme de dématérialisation des recettes des collectivités locales (PDRCL) was given to the state's Agence congolaise des systèmes d'information (ACSI) by the 2025 finance law. ACSI's procurement notice of 4 September 2025 lists equipment for it from the agency's 2025 investment budget, with the Ministry of the Interior as client.

What is recorded. The finance ministry said in February 2026 that the platform has a back-office module for local government agents and a market-collector module, Zando collecte, that enrols taxpayers, records revenue by payment mode and tracks each taxpayer's operations.

Where the record is held. The ministry said on 14 February 2026 that the platform brings the revenues of local governments into one information system.

Last twelve months. The platform passed final validation at Kintélé on 14 February 2026 and ACSI presented it to the Mayor of Brazzaville on 30 April 2026.

Local governments recording. The platform was validated after workshops with the communes of Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire. On 24 February 2026 ACSI was preparing its deployment, and in 2026 a pilot was to follow at the Ndjindji central market in Pointe-Noire once municipal staff had been trained.

Noted, not assessed. Central: the Caisse unique des recettes de service (CURS), for state service revenue, and FOUTA, the Treasury's payment platform for all public revenue, that of local governments included.

Not held. No local government is stated as recording on the platform, in Brazzaville, Pointe-Noire or elsewhere. No plan naming the platform is held.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All 52 health districts enter clinics' paper reports into the national system (2024); no figure shows how many clinics enter their own.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's national health development plan for 2023 to 2026, published in September 2024, names DHIS2 as the principal platform for collecting and processing health information, within what the ministry calls the Système national d'information sanitaire (SNIS). The plan lists a health information directorate in the ministry and a database manager in every health district, and says the platform was built with Global Fund and Gavi support.

Tiers in use. The plan of September 2024 states that standard collection tools exist in health centres, district referral hospitals and private facilities, and that general hospitals' forms are configured in DHIS2 with their monthly reporting still to begin.

Where data is digitised. The same plan states that nearly all public, private and faith-based facilities compile their reports on paper and send them to the health districts for entry, all 12 departments and 52 districts having been trained on DHIS2. WHO reports data completeness of 87% and timeliness of 47.9% in 2024.

Last twelve months. WHO's annual report for 2025 says that during the year, with its support, 401 data collectors were equipped for electronic transmission of data and the national list of health indicators was drawn up.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The plan of September 2024 states that facilities produce their reports on paper and the health districts key them into DHIS2, and that paper collection tools remain much used while the computer tools provided are not often used.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the logistics system e-LMIS, the epidemic prevention and health information component of the Projet d'accélération de la transformation numérique, routine immunisation data in DHIS2, the weekly surveillance system EWARS, the Covid-19 response system SIRCOV, the stock system SAGE at the medicines store CAMEPS, epidemiological surveillance and sentinel influenza surveillance.

Not held. No count or share of primary clinics entering their own reports is held, and nothing on health posts. The tier, total number and software of the 401 data collectors equipped in 2025 are not stated, and no domestic budget line for the system is named.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A November 2023 decree creates a per-pupil record that schools are to fill; whether any school uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Decree 2023-1751 of 2 November 2023 creates an education management information system, which Congo calls the SIGE, inside the ministry of preschool, primary and secondary education and literacy. The ministry's information systems directorate administers it and the state agency ACSI maintains it. The decree finances it in public schools from the State budget, gifts and partners, and names no amount.

Levels in use. No level uses a school-level system. In May 2023 a digital workspace for primary and secondary schools, the ENT, existed only as a specification written for the ministry by a consultant of the AUF's APPRENDRE programme.

How the record is kept. The decree assigns data entry to the schools or their inspectorates: each learner registered under a unique number, with grades, report cards and school record books digitised.

Last twelve months. The decree creating the system is dated 2 November 2023.

Primary schools doing the digital input. None. The May 2023 specification proposed a trial from September 2023 in a few schools to be chosen by the ministry, for example two primary schools and one secondary.

Noted, not assessed. Congo calls its yearly school count the SIGE: paper questionnaires collected from schools once a year for the annuaire statistique, with a répertoire des établissements scolaires planned. The same count appears as the annuaire scolaire, as the EMIS at the ministry, as the Annuaire Statistique 2017-2018 and as the awaited Statistical Yearbook 2025-2026. Planned: an integrated EMIS for all education ministries and a comprehensive teacher database. Single-function: Exatrust, for checking state examination results, the tablet survey locating examination centres and the biometric registration of teachers. Learning: Ecole à domicile and RESSEN.ORG.

Not held. Whether staff were trained, equipment delivered or any school took part in a trial. How schools keep their learners' records, and anything dated after the decree of 2 November 2023.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A civil registration system was built and validated by September 2026 but no office uses it; whether older urban systems still run is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The state agency ACSI designed and built the civil registration system Congo calls SIFEC for the Interior ministry's territorial administration directorate. On 20 February 2026 the validation workshop's main recommendation was that the human, material and financial means to deploy it be provided. The World Bank's Congo Digital Acceleration Project counts an operational civil registration database among its results.

Events registered digitally. None. SIFEC's Births, Marriages and Deaths modules were under validation in February 2026, and the World Bank scored a national digital database of civil registration records not operational at 31 May 2025.

How the record is made. On paper: in February 2026 the Interior ministry's security adviser called the system long manual and fragmented. In 2021 the World Bank found electronic records stored locally in civil registration centres of Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire, and rural areas largely paper-based.

Last twelve months. On 20 February 2026 a workshop at Kintélé validated 17 recommendations on SIFEC. On 7 August 2026 the Interior minister told the Senate its rollout was imminent. On 22 September 2026 ACSI handed the system's operating documents to the territorial administration directorate.

Local offices doing the digital entry. None. After the handover of 22 September 2026 seven steps remained, user training and deployment among them. In 2020 the World Bank counted 2 of Brazzaville's 10 civil status offices as digitised.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: a project with FamilySearch to digitise civil status registers and national archives, under a protocol being formalised in September 2024. Outreach: a pilot at Talangaï town hall issuing birth certificates, identity cards and NIU numbers to vulnerable households from 15 January 2026, the Ministry of Justice's audiences foraines in Likouala and Sangha, which issued 558 birth certificates in 2022, and birth registers for 10,000 certificates handed over that year. Enabling: computer equipment supplied by UNHCR to civil status services in Pointe-Noire. Certificate, all planned: the Portail e-Citizen, the Services numériques de l'État and an eGov portal with none of its 10 targeted services online at 31 May 2025. Identity: the carte nationale d'identité informatisée et sécurisée, the fichier national de l'identification civile, a second card and passport production centre at Pointe-Noire, the NIU biometric card, whose production was relaunched from September 2025, a card distribution campaign in Pointe-Noire from 9 September 2025, and a planned digital identity on MOSIP with a Digital Wallet. Exchange, planned: an exchange layer on X-Road, an interoperability framework and exchange platform financed by the EU and the EIB, and interoperability of the social register with the civil registration databases.

Not held. Whether the electronic records kept in some Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire centres in 2020 and 2021 are still kept. Who pays for SIFEC's deployment, and the period of the civil status digitisation roadmap.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2014 survey of two towns found police stations working by hand; the rest of the country is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Public: the criminal-record extract in the first wave of state services to go online, planned in July 2026, the eGov portal to be set up under European Investment Bank financing, the planned Portail e-Citizen and the numéro vert d'alerte for violence against children, which the UN country team reported in December 2025 as not working. Single-function: eTraffrika, traffic-offence software in a pilot in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire in September 2025, and the special register a commissariat keeps of lost, stolen or destroyed identity cards under the decree of 21 November 2024. Surveillance: two mobile radars in the same pilot. Identification: the central criminal records service, for which a network with the judicial police was planned in 2014, and the national civil identification file, alphabetical and fingerprint, organised by orders of June 2022.

Not held. The one statement that stations work by hand is a survey of commissariats in Pointe-Noire and Nkayi from January 2014; nothing is held on Brazzaville, the rest of the country or the gendarmerie beyond one brigade, and nothing dated since. Who would own a station system, and anything from the last twelve months, is not held.
