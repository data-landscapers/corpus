The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Digital transformation strategy

Libya's Prime Minister approved a government digital transformation strategy in April 2025, to 2030, but no source found reports its approved text.

Governance and finance. Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dabaiba of the Government of National Unity approved the National Strategy for Government Digital Transformation, prepared by the Supreme Committee for Digital Transformation.

Standing. The Prime Minister approved the strategy on 21 April 2025, following the General Information Authority's December 2022 proposal.

Legislation covering digital id

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Libya's 2014 national number law gives each citizen a unique number in a biometric database. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Libya's Law No. 8 of 2014 on the National Number was issued by the General National Congress on 24 March 2014 and took effect on issue. It sets up the national number and a national database, run with the Civil Registry Authority. It is an ordinary law and no amendment to it is on record.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law provides for a twelve-digit national number unique to each citizen and a national database completed with ten fingerprints, an iris print, a photograph and a signature. The number and the biometric database are both in the law's own articles, the second and the third, and the database is the reference the number is checked against. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Libya's 2022 electronic transactions law is in force and covers online sellers; its executive regulation was unpublished in 2023, and no later rules are known.

Law in force. Libya's Law No. 6 of 2022 on Electronic Transactions was issued by the House of Representatives on 4 October 2022. It gives electronic signatures, records and contracts legal effect. The repository that holds it lists it as in force, and its Article 87 makes it effective from the date of issue. The law is not limited to signatures: it also sets pre-contract disclosure and return rules for online sellers, so it reaches the sale of goods and services over the internet as well as the validity of electronic documents. Eltumi Partners also states that Libya passed the law in October 2022.

The same text sets the return rules that online sellers must follow. In December 2023 Eltumi Partners stated that the government had not yet published the executive regulation to the law. No regulation, order or decision made under the law is named, and none is described as applying its rules to a particular seller or platform.
