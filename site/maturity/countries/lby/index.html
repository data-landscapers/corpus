Libya

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

DHIS2 rolled out to 465 primary care facilities and 51 hospitals in 2023; no share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Health Information Centre gives technical support to the DHIS2 platform, as WHO described it in 2024. WHO states that all health information system activities in 2024 followed the 2024-2025 HIS operational plan. The 2023 rollout was enabled by WHO with UNICEF, IOM, GIZ, the International Rescue Committee, the International Medical Corps and UNFPA.

Tiers in use. WHO states that in 2023 it and the ministry rolled out DHIS2 in 40 municipalities, covering 465 primary health care facilities and 51 hospitals.

Where data is digitised. WHO states that 655 health staff from public primary health care facilities, hospitals and municipal health administration were trained on DHIS2 in 2023 and given laptops, desktops and tablets to enable data entry. In December 2024 WHO and the ministry visited five health facilities in Suq Aljuma municipality, Tripoli, to assess their DHIS2 data entry compliance and the timeliness and completeness of their reporting.

Last twelve months. Libya Review reported on 7 November 2025 a meeting of the ministry and WHO that explored the feasibility of extending DHIS2 to pharmacy management, supply forecasting and service monitoring in public facilities.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. WHO counts 465 primary health care facilities among the 516 facilities where DHIS2 was rolled out in 2023. WHO also states that DHIS2 reached 100% national geographic coverage by March 2023 and that consistent reporting from health facilities remains a challenge.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its unified electronic system, DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture and the national cancer registry. Single-function: the ministry's electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines, the National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting, HeRAMS, the integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools, event-based surveillance, EMFLU 2.0, the LIVE Oxygen Platform, EWARN, RASED and the EPI data management systems.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held: the 465 are facilities where DHIS2 was rolled out, with no denominator. The sources name hospitals and primary health care facilities, not their levels, and nothing held shows the operational plan running beyond 2025 or financing dated after 2023.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One teaching hospital partly applying a system, reported in 2025, and a two-clinic trial in 2022; nothing shows the trial continuing.

Governance, planning, finance. Libya Herald described the Unified Electronic Health File System in May 2022 as a Ministry of Health project under implementation for more than five years. The National Centre for Health System Reform, under the ministry's supervision, launched the National Comprehensive Health Care Programme and the Health Number System in Tripoli in January 2024, as the ministry's strategic option for implementing the electronic health file. WHO states that in 2024 the ministry and WHO developed a digital transformation roadmap for 2025-2030 that promotes electronic health records and hospital management systems on open-source platforms.

Tiers in use. A Sabratha University field study published in December 2025 says that Sabratha Teaching Hospital has moved to apply an electronic medical records system and that difficulties prevent its full use.

Where data is digitised. At the May 2022 trial of the Unified Electronic Health File System, its technical committee showed information being entered directly from patients attending the clinic. The Sabratha study's author states that most public hospitals in Libya use traditional paper records.

Last twelve months. The Sabratha study, published on 24 December 2025, states that the move towards electronic medical records is slow or stalled in many government hospitals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Libya Herald reports that the trial operation of the system was inaugurated in May 2022, with the first phase covering two sites, the Ghout al-Shaal clinic in Tripoli and the al-Kish clinic in Benghazi.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its unified electronic system, DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture and the national cancer registry. Single-function: the ministry's electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines, the National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting, HeRAMS, the integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools, event-based surveillance, EMFLU 2.0, the LIVE Oxygen Platform, EWARN, RASED and the EPI data management systems.

Not held. Nothing held shows the 2022 two-clinic trial continuing, or the electronic health file launched in January 2024 in use anywhere. No finance source is stated, and no dated event in the last twelve months is held.
