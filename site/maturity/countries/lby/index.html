Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Three municipalities' offices had unreliable internet access in a 2021 study; how many of Libya's municipalities are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Local Government's digital transformation project director said on 2 November 2023 that official e-mail accounts would be created for the heads of administrative units in the municipalities, inside the ministry's electronic correspondence system, to replace paper correspondence. The system is the ministry's own, run under its digital transformation project.

What the office does over it. In the three local authorities studied by Wynn, Bakeer and Forti, published in 2021, the information systems are isolated in-house or third-party systems on departmental networks, and data is exchanged on paper. Sabratha's finance department uses online services occasionally, to display and update information on the authority's website.

How the office is connected. The study finds internet access at the authorities' offices that is slow, often disconnected or not always available. Sabratha's finance department has fifteen networked computers and an internet connection that is often disconnected, and in Rujban the internet is normally unavailable in community services and unreliable in human resources.

Last twelve months. On 2 November 2023 the e-mail accounts for municipal units were announced and not yet in use.

Local governments connected. The 2021 study covers three local authorities, Misurata, Sabratha and Rujban, by questionnaire and interviews with eighteen officials, and concludes for the three that access to the internet was not always available.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: local area networks inside single departments in Sabratha and Rujban, with no network across an authority. Applications: an electronic archiving programme in the human resources department of Abu Salim municipality, from 11 September 2026; an integrated database of state property for Jufra municipality, in preparation on 26 June 2026; and, in the 2021 study, Sabratha's Purchasing System of 2010, its Warehouse Management System of 2008 and its accounts management and payroll system, and Rujban's in-house systems for civilian details and for personnel and payroll. Web: BaladiyaBOT, a digital assistant whose development Tripoli Centre municipality announced on 19 April 2026, funded by the European Union and Germany; and the three authorities' web portals, one-way and carrying basic information. For the public: coding academies set up in four municipalities under Expertise France's Raqam-e project. Backbone: Hatif Libya's fibre-optic backbone and access networks; the fibre networks overseen by LPTIC, a state monopoly; the Medusa submarine cable, inaugurated at Misrata on 10 May 2025; the National Data Transmission Network defined in the draft law of 2022 on the national information system; and the General Information Authority's national data exchange network, linking national bodies' systems to its database. Central: the civil registry and national number system, run centrally by the Civil Status Authority, whose 257 offices had matched electronic data to paper records for 97% of Libyan households by 3 September 2024; the Ministry of Local Government's database of municipal council mayors, members and deputies, in preparation on 27 March 2026; LyGIF, an interoperability framework proposed in December 2025; DHIS2, rolled out in 40 municipalities in 2023, with HeRAMS at hospitals and the EWARN and RASED disease surveillance systems; electronic disease surveillance reporting, piloted in 10 municipalities in 2024; the Health Number System, launched in Tripoli in January 2024; electronic voter identity verification at election centres in four municipalities in August 2024; and the Social Solidarity Fund's beneficiary databases.

Not held. No total or share of Libya's municipalities with a connected office is held, nor who provides or pays for the offices' internet. The period and the finance of the ministry's strategy are not stated, the newest fact on the offices' connection is of 2021, and nothing dated in the twelve months to 30 September 2026 is held.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The national system was rolled out to 465 primary care facilities and 51 hospitals in 2023; how many clinics enter their own reports is unknown.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's Health Information Centre gives technical support to the DHIS2 platform, as WHO described it in 2024. WHO states that all health information system activities in 2024 followed the 2024-2025 HIS operational plan. The 2023 rollout was enabled by WHO with UNICEF, IOM, GIZ, the International Rescue Committee, the International Medical Corps and UNFPA.

Tiers in use. WHO states that in 2023 it and the ministry rolled out DHIS2 in 40 municipalities, covering 465 primary health care facilities and 51 hospitals.

Where data is digitised. WHO states that 655 health staff from public primary health care facilities, hospitals and municipal health administration were trained on DHIS2 in 2023 and given laptops, desktops and tablets to enable data entry. In December 2024 WHO and the ministry visited five health facilities in Suq Aljuma municipality, Tripoli, to assess their DHIS2 data entry compliance and the timeliness and completeness of their reporting.

Last twelve months. Libya Review reported on 7 November 2025 a meeting of the ministry and WHO that explored the feasibility of extending DHIS2 to pharmacy management, supply forecasting and service monitoring in public facilities.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. WHO counts 465 primary health care facilities among the 516 facilities where DHIS2 was rolled out in 2023. WHO also states that DHIS2 reached 100% national geographic coverage by March 2023 and that consistent reporting from health facilities remains a challenge.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its unified electronic system, DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture and the national cancer registry. Single-function: the ministry's electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines, the National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting, HeRAMS, the integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools, event-based surveillance, EMFLU 2.0, the LIVE Oxygen Platform, EWARN, RASED and the EPI data management systems.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held: the 465 are facilities where DHIS2 was rolled out, with no denominator. The sources name hospitals and primary health care facilities, not their levels, and nothing held shows the operational plan running beyond 2025 or financing dated after 2023.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One teaching hospital was partly using a system in 2025, and two clinics ran a trial in 2022 that is not shown continuing.

Governance, planning, finance. Libya Herald described the Unified Electronic Health File System in May 2022 as a Ministry of Health project under implementation for more than five years. The National Centre for Health System Reform, under the ministry's supervision, launched the National Comprehensive Health Care Programme and the Health Number System in Tripoli in January 2024, as the ministry's strategic option for implementing the electronic health file. WHO states that in 2024 the ministry and WHO developed a digital transformation roadmap for 2025-2030 that promotes electronic health records and hospital management systems on open-source platforms.

Tiers in use. A Sabratha University field study published in December 2025 says that Sabratha Teaching Hospital has moved to apply an electronic medical records system and that difficulties prevent its full use.

Where data is digitised. At the May 2022 trial of the Unified Electronic Health File System, its technical committee showed information being entered directly from patients attending the clinic. The Sabratha study's author states that most public hospitals in Libya use traditional paper records.

Last twelve months. The Sabratha study, published on 24 December 2025, states that the move towards electronic medical records is slow or stalled in many government hospitals.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Libya Herald reports that the trial operation of the system was inaugurated in May 2022, with the first phase covering two sites, the Ghout al-Shaal clinic in Tripoli and the al-Kish clinic in Benghazi.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: what the National Diabetes Authority calls its unified electronic system, DHIS2 Tracker Data Capture and the national cancer registry. Single-function: the ministry's electronic system for tracking the movement of medicines, the National Centre for Disease Control's epidemiological reporting, HeRAMS, the integrated disease surveillance electronic reporting tools, event-based surveillance, EMFLU 2.0, the LIVE Oxygen Platform, EWARN, RASED and the EPI data management systems.

Not held. Nothing held shows the 2022 two-clinic trial continuing, or the electronic health file launched in January 2024 in use anywhere. No finance source is stated, and no dated event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All civil registration offices were automated and linked to one database, the health ministry said in 2022; whether events are entered there is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Civil Registration Authority, of the Ministry of Interior, manages the collection of births and deaths data and issues the legal documents, by the health ministry's vital statistics report published in 2022. In January 2025 the Ministry of Interior said the Authority was continuing the Intilaqa project, under the supervision of the Attorney General's office, matching the electronic data in the civil status system against its paper originals.

Events registered digitally. The Authority's electronic system recorded 231,670 births and 31,051 deaths in 2020, says the Ministry of Health's Health Information Centre, which generated its report for that year from it.

How the record is made. The Health Information Centre's report, published in 2022, states that all of the Authority's offices are fully automated and electronically linked to its centre, working through a single automated system on the same database.

Last twelve months. On 1 March 2026 the Attorney General's office reported that he had discussed with the head of the Authority the latest completed phase of the project to create the electronic civil registry database.

Local offices doing the digital entry. All of the Authority's offices are automated and linked to the single database, the Health Information Centre said in 2022; its report counts 12 branches, 133 issuing offices and at least 380 service offices.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the Intilaqa project, matching and scanning the paper registers. Identity: the National Number project, the General Information Authority's national digital identity system, which it calls LY-DNID, its Digital Identity Management System for Citizens and its national database, the electronic identity card and electronic passport issuing system, the passport issuing system and a biometric data system the Civil Status Department intended in 2019. Exchange: the national ID provided at birth since 2013, the General Information Authority's data exchange, which it calls LY-NDEX, and its electronic linkage service to the national database, the supply of births and deaths data to the health ministry's information centre, the data flow to the Bureau of Statistics and Census, manual data sharing between the Civil Registry and social assistance agencies and the health ministry's DHIS2. Enabling: a national data transmission network in a draft law of 2022.

Not held. Whether a birth or death is entered at the office when it is registered is not stated, and the office figure is undated and gives no rural share. No plan or finance for the system is held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Tripoli's police stations got a digital system for investigation reports in December 2025; use outside the capital is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Tripoli Security Directorate launched its digital transformation system itself at its headquarters on 31 December 2025, with the Attorney General, the security director and a representative of the acting Minister of Interior present, the Libyan News Agency reports.

Records made digitally. Among the components inaugurated that day is a system for the evidence-gathering reports of police stations and traffic sections. On 3 May 2025 Tripoli's security director told the heads of investigation of the police stations and traffic departments, whose officers receive reports and complaints and open the case minutes, to attend to the running of "the digital system".

How the record is made. The instruction of May 2025 names the digital system as a primary source for the database and for documenting legal rights.

Last twelve months. The directorate inaugurated its digital transformation system, including the police-station investigation-reports system, on 31 December 2025.

Stations doing the digital entry. The agency's report of the launch names the system as one for police stations and traffic sections of the Tripoli Security Directorate.

Noted, not assessed. Statistics: the Tripoli directorate's criminal statistics system. Identification: the Public Prosecution's criminal record cards issued electronically and the interior ministry's criminal status certificate issuing system. Single-function: the directorate's motor vehicle registration system. Public: the ministry's online criminal status request form. Surveillance: the directorate's AI-assisted traffic and security monitoring system.

Not held. No station outside the Tripoli Security Directorate is shown using a records system, and no count of stations, plan or source of finance is given. How the record is made is not stated.
