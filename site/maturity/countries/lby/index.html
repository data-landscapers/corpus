Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Libya's Prime Minister approved a government digital transformation strategy in April 2025, to 2030, but its approved text is not known.

Governance and finance. Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dabaiba of the Government of National Unity approved the National Strategy for Government Digital Transformation, prepared by the Supreme Committee for Digital Transformation.

Standing. The Prime Minister approved the strategy on 21 April 2025, following the General Information Authority's December 2022 proposal.

Text. The General Information Authority publishes the December 2022 proposal for the strategy, marked as a proposal on its cover.

Period. The vision is that by the end of 2030 everyone in Libya will enjoy world-class government services, with 2030 targets set against 2022 baselines.

Delivery plan. The proposal sets 2030 targets against 2022 baselines, such as a rise in Libya's e-government development index rank from 162 to 100, and lists eleven action areas with initiatives. It says a national plan of projects and the budgets are still to be set.

Reporting. At the April 2025 launch the General Information Authority chairman said some of the strategy's plans had already been implemented in a number of ministries and bodies.

Last twelve months. On 19 April 2026 the General Information Authority described Level 3 biometric digital identity trials as part of implementing the National Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030, the name it also uses for the strategy.

Noted, not assessed. Civil Service Ministry digital transformation plan; Civil Service Ministry strategic plan for digital transformation; National Strategy for Primary Health Care 2023-2028; Digital Identity Management System for Citizens; Tax Authority's digital transformation plan; National Digital Identity System (LY-DNID); National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2025-2029; eLibya.

Not held. The text of the strategy as approved in April 2025 is not held, only the December 2022 proposal. No budget, mandated body or results report is held for it.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Libya's central bank circular of 2025 bars banks and payment firms from storing personal or financial data abroad from July 2026, with the bank enforcing.

Rule in force. Libya's rule covers the financial sector. A write-up of the Central Bank of Libya's Circular No. 18, which it dates 1 June 2025, says the circular's data protection regulation prohibits storing personal, financial, credit or sensitive data outside Libya. It binds banks, branches of foreign banks, fintechs, payment providers, exchange companies and leasing companies, from 1 July 2026.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The central bank, which issued the regulation, supervises the institutions it covers and sets a fine of 100,000 dinars for each violation. It may revoke a licence for repeated gross violations, and each institution must appoint a Libyan data officer as its liaison with the bank. No fine, revocation or exemption under the regulation is held.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Libya's 2014 national number law gives each citizen a unique number in a biometric database. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Libya's Law No. 8 of 2014 on the National Number was issued by the General National Congress on 24 March 2014 and took effect on issue. It sets up the national number and a national database, run with the Civil Registry Authority. It is an ordinary law and no amendment to it is on record.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law provides for a twelve-digit national number unique to each citizen and a national database completed with ten fingerprints, an iris print, a photograph and a signature. The number and the biometric database are both in the law's own articles, the second and the third, and the database is the reference the number is checked against. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. National Number / national database.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Libya's 2022 electronic transactions law is in force and covers online sellers, but no rules made under it are known.

Law in force. Libya's Law No. 6 of 2022 on Electronic Transactions was issued by the House of Representatives on 4 October 2022. It gives electronic signatures, records and contracts legal effect. The repository that holds it lists it as in force, and its Article 87 makes it effective from the date of issue. The law is not limited to signatures: it also sets pre-contract disclosure and return rules for online sellers, so it reaches the sale of goods and services over the internet as well as the validity of electronic documents.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The nearest held fact is Article 87, which makes the law effective from issue, so it applied from the date of issue. The same text sets the return rules that online sellers must follow. No regulation, order or decision made under the law is named in the evidence, and none is described as applying its rules to a particular seller or platform.

Noted, not assessed. PTS unified digital system.

Law in force. Libya's statistics law is Law No. 16 of 1963 on statistics and censuses, issued by King Idris I on 19 September 1963. The Law Society's database of legislation lists it as in force.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The nearest held fact is that listing. The database carries the text of the 1963 law and marks it as in force. It is the one source held for Libya on the subject. The law predates every later constitution and government of the country and is sixty-three years old, the second oldest statistics law in force among the countries assessed. The law therefore stands as the database gives it. Its title covers both the regular statistics of the state and the taking of censuses.

Share of people who own a mobile phone, with its year. In 2024, 87.3 per cent of people aged 10 and over in Libya owned a mobile phone, an estimate by the International Telecommunication Union. That leaves 12.7 per cent without a phone of their own. The same series put the share at 88.5 per cent in 2021, 86.5 per cent in 2022 and 87.4 per cent in 2023. The figure counts a person once, whatever the number of SIM cards they hold, and counts a phone as owned where the person has it for their own use.

Mobile affordability: the price of mobile data against income

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2026, 5 GB of mobile data for a month cost 0.68 per cent of average monthly income, on a stated price.

Cost of a month of mobile data against income, with its year. Almadar Aljadid's cheapest monthly plan of 5 GB or more is 6 GB, valid 30 days, for 20 Libyan dinars, on its tariff page as at 3 September 2026. At that price 5 GB for a month comes to 0.68 per cent of average monthly income, on the World Bank's figure for income per person in 2025. It is the newest figure held, and sets the stage.
