Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter clinics' paper reports into the national reporting system (2020 procedure, 2023 assessment).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's National Digital Health Roadmap 2024-2026 lists DHIS2 as the health management information system, deployed by the Ministry in all districts. The landscape assessment by GIZ and the Ministry, dated January 2023, states that its 2022 statutory budget made no provision for digital health.

Tiers in use. The January 2023 assessment states that all 34 secondary and all 10 tertiary facilities it surveyed had M&E Officers whose role was predominantly data capture and upload on DHIS2.

Where data is digitised. Under the Ministry's HMIS procedures manual of April 2020, peripheral health units compile monthly reports from paper registers and send hard copies to the District Health Management Team, where a data entry officer keys them into DHIS2, and a hospital's M&E officer enters the hospital's data. The January 2023 assessment found M&E Officers at district level capturing and uploading data on DHIS2.

Last twelve months. Statistics Sierra Leone, the Ministry of Health and APHRC give DHIS2 completeness of expected monthly facility reports as 98% nationally in 2024, with all districts at 90% or more.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The April 2020 manual has no peripheral health unit entering its own data, and notes a plan for 2020 to move electronic entry to the community health centres. The January 2023 assessment states that DHIS2 was deployed in 96% of the 246 health facilities it surveyed across the 16 districts, 202 of them primary.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: PReSTrack, SPICE, eCBDS and the HIV tracker. Single-function: eIDSR, the National Disease Surveillance System, the Integrated National Digital Mortality Surveillance, Registration and Certification System, the Last-Mile Logistics Management Information System, mSupply, the electronic Stock Management Tool, the Laboratory Information Management System and Partners In Health's Laboratory Information System. Community: CHIS and the Community Health Workers mobile application. Enabling: Starlink internet, solar home systems for health facilities, telehealth platforms and the Mobile Device Management system.

Not held. No share or count of primary clinics entering their own reports is held; the 96% counts deployment in the facilities surveyed. Nothing dated since April 2020 says whether entry moved to the community health centres, and no financing since 2022 or dated event in the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Electronic records run at one government hospital and one clinic (2026); a national system is not known to have been bought.

Governance, planning, finance. OpenMRS's case study of April 2026 describes the PIH EMR as Partners In Health's own OpenMRS distribution. The National Health Compact of 6 December 2025 commits the Government to roll out Electronic Medical Records to all medical facilities by 2030 and names a Ministry of Health directorate as lead. The Ministry's request for expressions of interest of 18 December 2025 states that a donor grant finances the initial phase.

Tiers in use. The case study states that Koidu Government Hospital, a referral centre, uses the PIH EMR and that the EMR has run at Wellbody Clinic since 2015. The Ministry reported on 11 October 2023 that it and Smart Systems Sierra Leone Ltd had launched an EMR pilot at Connaught Hospital in Freetown.

Where data is digitised. The case study states that the EMR at the Maternal Center of Excellence in Koidu is entered at the point of care, with retrospective entry also possible.

Last twelve months. The Maternal Center of Excellence opened on 14 February 2026 with the EMR covering all its inpatient wards. On 18 December 2025 the Ministry's project unit asked firms to express interest in deploying an OpenMRS-based EMR across 300 health facilities.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The case study of April 2026 names one clinic using the PIH EMR, Wellbody Clinic. The Compact of 6 December 2025 gives a baseline of 5 health facilities implementing the system, in a network it puts at about 1,463 public facilities.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: PReSTrack, SPICE, eCBDS and the HIV tracker. Single-function: eIDSR, the National Disease Surveillance System, the Integrated National Digital Mortality Surveillance, Registration and Certification System, the Last-Mile Logistics Management Information System, mSupply, the electronic Stock Management Tool, the Laboratory Information Management System and Partners In Health's Laboratory Information System. Community: CHIS and the Community Health Workers mobile application. Enabling: Starlink internet, solar home systems for health facilities, telehealth platforms and the Mobile Device Management system.

Not held. What share of primary clinics keep patient records electronically is not held, nor the present state of the pilots at other primary facilities. The Compact's baseline of 5 facilities is undated, and the Ministry's national EMR is not shown procured.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2022-23 a rollout gave 300 primary schools tablets to enter each pupil's daily attendance; how many do so is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. What the country calls Wi De Ya is led by the Teaching Service Commission with the Ministry of Basic and Senior Secondary Education and was built by CGA Technologies, as stated in 2024, with funding from the Multi Donor Trust Fund of the World Bank, GPE, the EU, Irish Aid and FCDO. The ministry's Unique Learner ID initiative, reported in April 2026, is funded by GPE and sits within a 2024-2027 programme aligned with the Education Sector Plan 2022-2026.

Levels in use. Wi De Ya was rolled out to primary schools in academic year 2022-23 and was not in use in early childhood centres or secondary schools.

How the record is kept. School leaders enter each learner's profile and daily attendance on the school's tablet, submitting attendance at the end of the school day, and the data appears on dashboards for the commission and the ministry, as reported in 2023.

Last twelve months. The ministry has launched an initiative to build a learner-centred data system, reported in April 2026, whose Unique Learner ID system is to be piloted in four districts, in at least 20 schools in each.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The rollout phase of 2022-23 covered 300 selected primary schools, each given a tablet and training; reporting rates for learner attendance varied by chiefdom and were lower in some rural ones. Rollouts to 1,500 and then 4,000 schools were planned.

Noted, not assessed. Sierra Leone calls its school census an EMIS: the ministry's EMIS lists the annual school list and the census questionnaires and reports as its data. The Annual School Census covered 12,466 schools in 2022, with enumerators keying a paper questionnaire into tablets on a visit, and the Education Data Hub publishes the 2019 census beside the West African Examinations Council's national examination results for 2016-2019. Single-function systems are the Teaching Service Commission's Teacher Management Information System; the Situation Room, which tracks indicators for a sample of schools in every district; the foundational literacy and numeracy assessments, school quality assurance data and examination results the national data hub is to connect; the SQAO AI Support Tool, tested by five quality assurance officers and reported in March 2026, with the SQA Form officers use on school visits and an SQAO management system not yet in place; the School Catchment Area Plan and Policy App, rolled out in June 2024; an online platform for school subsidies and approvals, planned at November 2024; and the Accountant General's teacher payroll. Learning systems named in the digital learning strategy for 2025-2030 are the Learning Passport, the One Billion tablets initiative and Teacher.AI. GIGA, UNICEF's school connectivity mapping with the ministry, found 205 of 11,200 schools with internet.

Not held. How many of the 300 schools enter data, and the total of primary schools to set them against, are not given. Nothing describes Wi De Ya in use after 2023, or says whether the rollouts to 1,500 and 4,000 schools took place.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2023 births were entered digitally at the registration authority's hospital centres and sent to a central registry; the number of offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Civil Registration Authority (NCRA), the state agency formed under the 2016 Act, presented the integrated eCRVS and ID management system as its own in May 2024. Its Director General said in August 2023 that the system was developed with European Union support through UNOPS.

Events registered digitally. NCRA told the ID4Africa meeting of 22 May 2025 that over 80% of its integrated civil registration and identity services are digitised, birth and death registration among the services listed. UNICEF's representative said in August 2023 that its support covered the piloting and scale-up of a mobile application for the notification and registration of births.

How the record is made. UNICEF reported in March 2023 that at NCRA's registration centres in hospitals the child's details are entered with the application when the mother registers the birth, then transferred through the central server, which generates the identification number, to the Central Registry System.

Last twelve months. NCRA's presentation of 22 May 2025 says the 2016 Act mandates the Authority to deliver the integrated civil registration and identity services.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Calabash reported in September 2024 that an NCRA project had distributed 48 laptops and internet modems to 48 selected health facilities across the 16 districts. UNICEF reported in March 2023 more than 200 babies registered a month with the application at Ola During Hospital in Freetown.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: NCRA's digitisation of historic paper records. Notification: the planned Integrated National Digital Mortality Surveillance, Registration and Certification System. Certificate: NCRA's securitised certificates of births, deaths, marriages, divorce and adoption. Outreach: NCRA's mobile registration teams. Identity: the National Identification Number and National ID card, the National Biometric Identity Management System, the MOSIP pilot and migration, the identity verification platform, the Centralized ID Verification System, the Securitised and Multipurpose eID Card, the Non-National ID card, the ECOWAS national identity card, the National Inmates Identity Management System, the NCRA civil registration database from which voters are extracted and the mass civil registration of 2017. Enabling: NCRA's Tier III data centre, its ICT infrastructure at headquarters and district registration offices and the European Commission's IT and Physical Infrastructure Support to Civil Registration. Exchange: the integration of civil registration with identity management, the identification number generated at birth registration, the services linked to the NIN, the voter register extracted from the civil register, the Data Exchange Layer agreed by NCRA and the communication ministry, the Government Service Bus prototype and the interoperability between NCRA and the health ministry.

Not held. No count or share of offices entering events digitally is held, and death registration with the application is not described. No plan covering the present, no domestic budget line and nothing dated inside the last twelve months is held.
