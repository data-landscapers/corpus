The maturity assessment dashboard compares the progress of all 54 countries across 115 indicators. The indicators are grouped into 10 chapters and 35 topics. The criteria for classifying each indicator is derived from a norm or target stated in one of the African Union's foundational instruments.

Corpus provides country analysis in three separate reports. In addition to this report the Status Report provides a more detailed summary of all aspects of digital transformation, and the Progress Report tracks developments that have taken place over the past 12 months.

These assessments are updated overnight whenever new relevant evidence arrives. And once a month each indicator is rebuilt entirely.

Digital payments legislation

5 Leading Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

Sierra Leone's 2022 payment systems act licenses non-bank providers, and since April 2025 banks and mobile money operators must route payments through the national switch.

Law or rules in force. Sierra Leone's National Payment Systems Act, 2022 was assented to on 2 June 2022, commenced on 23 June 2022 and repealed the Payment Systems Act 2009. The Bank of Sierra Leone's Guidelines for E-money have been completed and gazetted, with guidelines on remittance business and directives for the national switch.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. Section 6 of the Act bars anyone from providing payment services or operating a system without a Bank of Sierra Leone licence, and sets conditions for e-money issuers; section 8 exempts only banks and financial institutions from needing a new licence. The licence is therefore one for companies that are not banks, and the Act gives mobile money operators a seat on its payments council. Directives issued in December 2024 require all commercial banks and mobile money operators to route instant, card and point-of-sale payments through the national switch from 1 April 2025.
