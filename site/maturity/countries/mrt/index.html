Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritania approved a costed national digital agenda for 2022 to 2025 that has now ended, and results against its targets are not known.

Governance and finance. The agenda states that the State has set up a Haut Conseil du Numérique to follow its execution. The digital ministry says the World Bank-supported WARDIP is to attract private investment and co-financing for the agenda's activities.

Standing. The government drew up and approved the Agenda national de transformation numérique 2022-2025 in 2022. On 4 December 2025 the digital ministry announced the start of preparation of an Agenda National de la Transformation Numérique 2030.

Text. The ministry hosts the agenda's text on its official site, dated June 2022.

Period. The agenda is a four-year strategy for 2022-2025 with a calendar of projects. Agence Ecofin refers to it in the past tense on 30 June 2026.

Delivery plan. The agenda is broken into 25 initiatives and 77 projects for a global investment of 400 million dollars, with cost, funding mode and owner given for each project.

Reporting. At a workshop on 4 December 2025 evaluating the agenda, the ministry's secretary general said it brought considerable advances in digital infrastructure, electronic platforms and access to public services.

Last twelve months. On 4 December 2025 the digital ministry opened preparation of the 2030 agenda, with the World Bank taking part by video link.

Noted, not assessed. Loi n°2024-002 relative aux startups technologiques et innovantes; WARDIP; WARCIP; Arabic-titled digital transformation acceleration project; APD strategic plan 2023-2026; Stratégie de Croissance Accélérée et de Prospérité Partagée (SCAPP) 2016-2030; national whole-government digitalization strategy planned under WARDIP subcomponent 4.3; online service portal project.

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritania's 2017 data protection law is applied by its authority through opinions and rules; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. The Autorité de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (APD) was created by article 64 of the law as an independent body attached to the Prime Minister, and decree 2022-13 of 18 February 2022 governs its composition and operation. It met on 30 June 2026 under its president, with its members and the Government Commissioner present.

Standing. Law 2017-020 on the protection of personal data was promulgated on 22 July 2017. The APD's 2023-2026 strategic plan places itself under the law's entry into force, and its deliberation 002/2026 of 24 March 2026 applies articles 7, 11, 27, 37 and 40.

Regulations. Deliberation 004/2025 of 14 July 2025 requires the APD's prior authorisation for transfers to countries not on its list and lists 105 countries as adequate. Deliberation 005/2025 of 30 July 2025 requires it for public and private controllers using cloud services abroad.

Application. On 24 March 2026 the APD gave a favourable opinion, conditional on its remarks, on the joint decision authorising the system Maaden Mauritanie calls Maadeni. On 19 November 2025 it examined complaints 004-2025, 006-2025 and 007-2025, one of them with a bank's reply.

Last twelve months. On 19 November 2025 the APD approved a control reference framework and empowered its sworn agents to take part in inspection missions. On 29 April 2026 it approved amendments to deliberations 004/2025 and 005/2025 and discussed complaints 008-2025 and 001-2026. Its 30 June 2026 session had authorisation requests and ministries' compliance answers on its agenda.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source gives a sanction, order or decision on a named controller, or a published count of decisions for a stated year, and the outcomes of the APD's sessions are mostly not stated. The law's commencement date, whether Mauritania has ratified the Malabo Convention and whether the law is under revision are not held.

Digital payments legislation: payments law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritania's 2021 electronic payments law and 2022 instruction license payment institutions that are not banks; no rule requiring providers to connect is known.

Law or rules in force. Mauritania's Loi n°2021-014 of 5 July 2021 on electronic payment services and means regulates payment service providers and e-money issuers, and repealed a 2006 ordinance. Under it the central bank issued Instruction no. 03/GR/2022 of 31 March 2022 regulating payment institutions. The law has 113 articles and was published in Journal Officiel n°1490 of 30 July 2021.

Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. The law bars anyone except banks from carrying on the business of a payment institution, or issuing and distributing e-money, without the central bank's prior licence. The instruction defines payment institutions as legal entities licensed by the central bank other than credit institutions, with minimum capital and a guarantee deposit of 20 million ouguiya each. No rule is named that requires providers to connect to each other.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No rule held requires mobile money providers and banks to connect to each other or to a national or regional switch.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritania has barred sending personal data abroad without adequate protection since 2017 and published a country list in 2025; no case applied is known.

Rule in force. Articles 20 to 25 of Law 2017-020, of 22 July 2017, bar a controller from sending personal data to a third country unless it ensures a sufficient level of protection, with authorisation by the data protection authority (APD) on guarantees such as contract clauses. Deliberation 004/2025, adopted by the APD on 14 July 2025, sets the conditions, rules and procedures for sending personal data abroad. Deliberation 005/2025, adopted on 30 July 2025, requires controllers using cloud services abroad to obtain prior APD authorisation.

Means of complying published and rule applied. Deliberation 005/2025 refers to a list of countries, published in deliberation 004/2025, that determines which host countries offer adequate protection. Where the host country is not on that list, a separate foreign-transfer authorisation request is required, in the text amended and published on 30 April 2026. The APD's monthly meeting of 29 April 2026 listed amending both deliberations on its agenda.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source from the last three years names a country approved to receive data, or an organisation given or refused permission, ordered to comply or penalised for sending data abroad.

Standards body and its standards. Mauritania's standards body is the Office National de Normalisation et de Métrologie, which Decree 2023-113 of 7 September 2023 says was instituted by a decree of 9 May 2023. Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A police information system launched in June 2025 runs in Nouakchott's commissariats, 34 centres linked; use by stations outside the capital is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The central office for collecting police information, in the judicial police directorate of the General Directorate of National Security, supervises the police information system, which the directorate calls WAPIS and which its Director General launched on 19 June 2025.

Records made digitally. The system is meant to digitise the work of police commissariats and to document the reports and complaints made at commissariats and their offices, a report of the launch states.

How the record is made. Central commissioners and heads of judicial police sections in the commissariats register in a database held by the central office, to which police centres are being linked, by the same report of 19 June 2025.

Last twelve months. On 19 June 2025 the Director General put the system to work for central directors, the regional directors of the Nouakchott wilayas, central commissioners and heads of judicial police sections in the commissariats.

Stations doing the digital entry. At the launch the director of the central office gave the centres linked as 27 of the National Police and 7 of the National Gendarmerie, and the report puts the rollout across Nouakchott's commissariats at 90 per cent complete.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the technical solutions for identifying wanted persons, the red list system for forged number plates, the DNA comparison and matching system, the digitised casier judiciaire, the planned laboratoire central d'investigation numérique, the AFIS and the registre central du casier judiciaire, whose draft decree was approved on 27 August 2026. Single-function: the traffic security and traffic-offence system, the motorcycle, owner and rider registration system, the police minor-offences system, the loss-certificate system, the firearms licence management system and the management system of the central office against drug trafficking. Public: the interior ministry's plateformes numériques for security services, the National Police digital platform, the proposed online request for a criminal record extract, the proposed guichet de suivi des plaintes, des procès-verbaux et des affaires auprès des tribunaux and the portail Khidmaty. Surveillance: the centre de commandement et de contrôle de la circulation routière, the urban surveillance system, the AI vehicle-overload detection system, the AI seat-belt detection system, the automatic red-light offence recognition system, the automatic wrong-way driving recognition system, the phone-use-while-driving recognition system and the Urban Security and Monitoring System in Nouakchott.
