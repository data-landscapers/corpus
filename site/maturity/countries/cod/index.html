Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Health zone offices enter facilities' paper reports into the national system (2021), which covers 75.3% of all health facilities (2022).

Governance, planning, finance. DHIS2.org in December 2025 calls the national DHIS2 locally owned and managed infrastructure of the Ministry of Health, in use at national scale since 2013, and names Gavi, the Global Fund and US CDC among its funders. The ministry's health informatics plan for 2020 to 2024 states that DHIS2 has been adopted as the software of its Système National d'Information Sanitaire (SNIS).

Tiers in use. The Sud-Kivu provincial health division's report of December 2021 lists paper reporting forms for health centres, health posts and general referral hospitals, and DHIS2 data sets for basic, secondary and hospital services in all 34 health zones. Nationally, the SNIS division counted 52,625 basic-services reports in DHIS2 of 53,736 expected in the second quarter of 2020.

Where data is digitised. A HISP evaluation that visited five health zones in Kinshasa and Haut-Katanga in late 2021 found that facilities send their reports on paper to the health zone office, where they are entered into DHIS2; some large facilities enter their own.

Last twelve months. In February 2026 Lualaba became the first province to make the HIV Insight Dashboard, an analysis application built inside the national DHIS2, its primary tool for provincial meetings and data validation.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Ministry of Public Health's Countdown to 2030 report, signed on 14 August 2022, states that 12,050 of the 16,010 health facilities listed in DHIS2, 75.3% of all health facilities, report in it.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 Tracker configuration for the Ebola response, RTI International's Ebola outbreak surveillance and response platform, the DHIS2 Ebola tracking tool used in Bulape and Mweka, DHIS2 Tracker in several provinces and the Salvation Army's patient-record digitisation project on Visa Médical. Single-function: the HIV Insight Dashboard, DHIS2 for disease surveillance, DHIS2 mpox surveillance and vaccination, the DHIS2 animal health surveillance system, the mpox surveillance application integrated with DHIS2 piloted in three Sud-Kivu health zones, SORMAS, EWARS, eIDSR on DHIS2 in Haut Katanga, the weekly surveillance data set Relevé Epidémiologique Hebdomadaire, the facility stock data set SIGL FOSA, the HIV, tuberculosis, malaria sentinel-site and blood bank reporting modules, the SURVEPI data set, the malaria programme's analysis database and dashboard and computerised medication management in seven Kananga hospitals. Community: the SSC App, whose monthly variables are exported to the SNIS communautaire, the ministry's community instance of DHIS2, community-level capture synchronised with DHIS2 in the Sud-Kivu mpox pilot and community health workers' paper reporting. Enabling: the computers, tablets and routers ANICNS supplies as a kit digital and VSAT and 3G modem links for Sud-Kivu's health zones.

Not held. No share or count of facilities entering their own reports is held, and no coverage figure dated after 2022; the 75.3% counts facilities whose reports are in DHIS2. No plan dated after 2024 is held, and the tiers are named for Sud-Kivu alone.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Oct 2026 Stage last moved Reassessed Oct 2026 Methodology · On the map

A government agency has delivered computers and hospital systems to named hospitals since 2024, but none is yet said to use electronic patient records.

Governance, planning, finance. The national agency ANICNS leads the health-sector digital transformation project and approves the hospital information system to be put into service, Actualite.cd reported on 23 November 2024, and the project is financed entirely by the Congolese government. Separately, the private firm Kouma Academy has begun deploying its Visa Medical patient record in the country, in partnership with Sanofi, Guinéenews reported on 13 August 2026.

Tiers in use. On 12 July 2024 ANICNS handed digital kits and three hospital information systems to the Lufungula general hospital, the Ngaba mother-and-child centre and the Lufungula health centre in Kinshasa, to allow the first modules to be implemented.

Where data is digitised. A University of Kananga survey of seven hospitals in that city, covering 2023, found 4.7% of 86 staff managing patient information electronically.

Last twelve months. On 16 June 2026 ANICNS opened a call for the now-mandatory approval of hospital information systems, each to include a complete computerised patient record.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. ACP reported on 13 February 2025 that ANICNS handed the Kasaï-Oriental provincial health division 516 computers, 206 tablets and 206 routers to equip 206 care establishments.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 Tracker configuration for the Ebola response, RTI International's Ebola outbreak surveillance and response platform, the DHIS2 Ebola tracking tool used in Bulape and Mweka, DHIS2 Tracker in several provinces and the Salvation Army's patient-record digitisation project on Visa Médical. Single-function: the HIV Insight Dashboard, DHIS2 for disease surveillance, DHIS2 mpox surveillance and vaccination, the DHIS2 animal health surveillance system, the mpox surveillance application integrated with DHIS2 piloted in three Sud-Kivu health zones, SORMAS, EWARS, eIDSR on DHIS2 in Haut Katanga, the weekly surveillance data set Relevé Epidémiologique Hebdomadaire, the facility stock data set SIGL FOSA, the HIV, tuberculosis, malaria sentinel-site and blood bank reporting modules, the SURVEPI data set, the malaria programme's analysis database and dashboard and computerised medication management in seven Kananga hospitals. Community: the SSC App, whose monthly variables are exported to the SNIS communautaire, the ministry's community instance of DHIS2, community-level capture synchronised with DHIS2 in the Sud-Kivu mpox pilot and community health workers' paper reporting. Enabling: the computers, tablets and routers ANICNS supplies as a kit digital and VSAT and 3G modem links for Sud-Kivu's health zones.

Not held. No source held states that any facility uses an electronic patient record: the hospital information systems ANICNS approves are described as delivered or to come, and Visa Medical's deployment names no site. The one survey held, of seven hospitals in Kananga in 2023, does not carry the country.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Ministry staff, not schools, registered pupils by app in a 20-primary-school pilot in April 2026; whether it will go national is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A Corus International article of 4 August 2026 by the director of SERNIE, the education ministry's service for individual learner data, states that ownership and use of the system it calls EDUSER rest with the ministry, operated by SERNIE with technical support from CGA Technologies. It says the pilot was funded and its future is uncertain without continued investment.

Levels in use. The April 2026 pilot of EDUSER was run in 20 primary schools of Kasai province.

How the record is kept. SERNIE's director writes that since 1991 its staff have recorded learners in schools on paper forms carried to Kinshasa for central data entry. Under EDUSER, SERNIE staff on their usual school visits register each learner by app, replacing the paper forms.

Last twelve months. In April 2026 SERNIE completed the first test of EDUSER in schools, a three-week pilot, after training its staff at national, provincial and local levels. The director says it laid the basis for a national rollout, which is not funded.

Primary schools doing the digital input. SERNIE teams registered 8,634 learners in 20 primary schools of Kasai province in the three weeks, which the director calls only a pilot phase.

Noted, not assessed. Census: the application the country calls e-SIGE-DRC and its EMIS, used for the 2022/23 school-year data collection, and the yearly statistical yearbook data collection. Single-function: the teacher payroll roster of DINACOE, formerly SECOPE, the SECOPE teacher payroll and teacher databases and the grievance mechanism Allô Ecole. Learning: SchoolAp, Klasroom Eteyelo, VODAEDUC, Maths-Whizz and EducTV with the PEQPESU YouTube channel and podcast. Enabling: the PEQPESU distribution of desktops and laptops to secondary schools, the universal service fund's pilot telecommunications sites and community digital learning centre at Nkamba and offline solar-powered tablets.

Not held. No primary school is shown entering its own learners' records, and no national count or share of schools is held. No plan naming EDUSER is held, nor who funded the pilot.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In August 2025 one commune of the capital was piloting digital civil registration; whether any birth or death has been registered digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Interior and Security is responsible for civil registration through its Civil Registration Division, as of March 2019. The one pilot is executed by four United Nations entities and financed by the UN's Joint SDG Fund, UNFPA reported on 18 August 2025. A national civil-registration strategy and a master plan for digitising the centres were still to be written on 1 October 2026.

Events registered digitally. UNFPA stated on 18 August 2025 that the Masina pilot aims to automate the collection and management of data on births, marriages and deaths.

How the record is made. Government terms of reference posted on 3 April 2026 state that the civil registers are mostly manual and are not integrated into any central file. In March 2019 the Civil Registration Division reported that each act is written on paper with four carbon copies.

Last twelve months. A UNDP tender closing on 25 April 2026 sought a consultant to assess the digitalisation of civil registration and support the design of digital registration tools. On 23 July 2026 a sector office in Walikale, North Kivu, had been out of birth registers for several months.

Local offices doing the digital entry. One commune, Masina in Kinshasa, has a pilot at its municipal civil-status office, UNFPA reported on 18 August 2025. In March 2019 the Civil Registration Division counted 979 local offices, 102 urban and 877 rural, none using a computer or mobile device.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitising of historical records listed as Italian and World Bank support in March 2019. Notification: health facilities notifying births and deaths. Outreach: catch-up registration campaigns through schools and the leniency measure for late birth registration of refugee children. Identity: RDC-Pass, launched on 13 June 2026, ONIP's fichier général de la population and carte d'identité nationale, the planned Système National d'Identification de la Population, the diaspora digital identification programme and the Carte Okapi project, the biometric passport enrolment system, the refugee identity cards and UNHCR's refugee database, the communal and territorial population registers and the Registre National de la Population. Enabling: a data centre for citizen data. Exchange: the automatic update of the population file through civil status provided for in the decree of 2 March 2022, the interoperability of the population file with sector registers planned under the identification master plan and the linking of sector databases proposed in a bill of 12 June 2026.

Not held. No fact says an event has been registered digitally: the Masina pilot's births and deaths are stated as its aim, and its own dates and results are not given. No count of offices is held newer than the 979 of March 2019.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Police documents were typed or handwritten on paper in 2023; whether any station has had a digital system since is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A police statement must by law be written out and signed by the officer who drafts it, on pain of nullity, the programme officer of the Ligue des droits de la personne dans la région des Grands Lacs told Canada's Immigration and Refugee Board on 15 March 2023.

Records made digitally. A case is referred to the Congolese National Police by a written and signed complaint, the Executive Secretary of Héritiers de la justice told the board on 9 March 2023.

How the record is made. Police documents in criminal proceedings are typed, and the record of the parties' hearings is handwritten in most cases, he said in March 2023 after consulting lawyers at the South Kivu Court of Appeal.

Last twelve months. The samples supplied to the board in March 2023 were blank paper forms of findings, arrest and witness testimony reports.

Stations doing the digital entry. The typed and handwritten police documents were described in March 2023 for the country as a whole.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: SIGCJ, the criminal records system, and the crime-analysis software of the Police judiciaire's bureau d'analyse criminelle. Single-function: the mobile devices for checking biometric chip driving licences and the police's digital revenue service system. Public: KIN ALERTE. Surveillance: the road surveillance system for number plates. Personnel: the biometric identification of police personnel and the police's Human Resources Information System. Enabling: the interior ministry's telecommunication and transmission equipment.

Not held. Nothing dated after March 2023 is held on how stations record, and neither account of that month says in terms that no digital system exists. Nothing is held on a system for stations, its owner, plan or finance, or on the last twelve months.
