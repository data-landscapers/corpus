Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

DR Congo validated a national digital plan in 2019, now past its period, and a 2026 successor whose adoption is not known.

Governance and finance. Paradigm Initiative lists the Presidency, the posts and telecommunications ministry and the regulator ARPTC as lead institutions of the Plan National du Numérique Horizon 2025, and reports public digital budget allocations of USD 103.7 million in 2025. The plan leaves its own budget to the Government and the ADN.

Standing. The national validation workshop of 3-5 September 2019 validated the plan, and no cabinet act is named. Its successor, the Politique sectorielle de l'économie numérique 2026-2030, was validated on 4 August 2026 and is due before the Council of Ministers.

Text. The full text, dated September 2019, is on the Presidency's site.

Period. The plan carries the strategy to the horizon 2025, with target years from 2020 to 2025.

Delivery plan. Its timetable gives 68 priority projects with indicators, baselines and targets dated 2020 to 2025.

Reporting. Agence Ecofin reported on 11 October 2025 that the ministry estimates the plan is about 60% complete.

Last twelve months. The Government's action programme for 2024-2028 sets putting the plan's remaining projects into action, and the successor was technically validated on 21-22 July 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Programme d'Actions du Gouvernement 2024-2028, Digital Code, RDC-PASS, National e-Government Strategy 2025-2030, Plan Directeur des systèmes d'information 2019-2025, Monacosat satellite project.

Not held. No adopting act is on record for the plan or for its successor. The successor has no action plan, steering body or monitoring yet, and the plan's progress is a ministry estimate.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The 2023 Digital Code, in force, covers personal data; a data protection officers' network launched in 2026; any decision under it is not known.

Authority. Article 262 of the Digital Code creates an independent Autorité de protection des données with legal personality and its own budget. A ministerial order of 17 August 2024 provisionally transferred its missions to the telecommunications regulator, ARPTIC. In March 2026 the Minister of Digital Economy described the authority's creation as forthcoming.

Standing. Ordonnance-loi n° 23/010 of 13 March 2023, the Digital Code, sets the rules on the protection of personal data in its Book III. It entered into force in 2023.

Regulations. The Code provides that a Prime Minister's decree will fix the authority's organisation and functioning. On 6 August 2024 the think tank Law & Technologies called on the government to draft and sign it.

Application. On 12 March 2026 the Ministry of Digital Economy launched a national network of data protection officers in Kinshasa, to standardise personal data practice and support compliance in the public and private sectors, backed by RUDI International and Droit-Numérique.cd.

Last twelve months. The network launched in March 2026 was reported as part of plans to establish a dedicated Data Protection Authority. On 20 April 2026 the co-author of a book on personal data protection said the Code's Book III replaces the embryonic provisions of the 2020 telecommunications law.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No decision, sanction or count of decisions under the Digital Code is held, and no regulation made under it. No source shows the authority constituted, or ARPTIC acting in its place.

This is an instruction to the officers who bring cases, issued in the Code's fourth year, and it covers the offences the Code creates. ACP also reports that the offences the Code du numérique names include cyberharassment and privacy breaches.

Binding rule on AI in force. A law-faculty dean writing on droitnumerique.cd published an article on 23 August 2024 about AI policy and automated decision-making in the DR Congo. The dean says the DRC has not yet developed its hard law on AI and that soft law still largely prevails. The article names no draft law. General AI law and the law applied. No general AI law is described in the same 23 August 2024 article on droitnumerique.cd. The article is the dean's account of how the DR Congo governs AI and automated decision-making, and it describes soft law as the instrument in use.
