Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District offices enter paper reports from 1,266 facilities into the national system (2023); how many health centres enter their own is not published.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health has collected aggregated facility data since 2002 through a unified HMIS under its Central Monitoring and Evaluation Division, which runs on DHIS2, on a study published in December 2024. The Global Fund's Inspector General reported in late 2023 that the Fund had invested US$21 million in the system since Grant Cycle 4.

Tiers in use. As of January 2023, 1,266 health facilities, government-owned and private, were reporting in the HMIS on DHIS2. Countdown to 2030, with the ministry, puts completeness of monthly facility reporting in DHIS2 at 97% of expected reports nationally in 2025, up from 83% in 2020.

Where data is digitised. An August 2023 survey of six facilities and three district health offices found that facilities compile monthly reports on paper and deliver them to the district health office, where the data are entered on computer. A study published in December 2024 states that paper forms are compiled at facilities and entered into DHIS2 at the district HMIS office.

Last twelve months. A project that began in December 2025 and runs to March 2027 will deploy DHIS2 climate and health tools on the national HMIS, first in four districts.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The ministry states that expanding DHIS2 Mobile to the 24 remaining districts in 2023 allows direct data entry into DHIS2 by all health centres. Neno district began moving DHIS2 data entry to its health centres in November 2022.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the TB Electronic Medical Record system and the HIV programme's Open EMR. Single-function: the national disease surveillance and reporting systems, the One Health Surveillance Platform (OHSP), the Early Warning, Alert and Response (EWARS) system, the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) weekly report, OpenLMIS, iHRIS, the Electronic Health Information Network (eHIN) and the warehouse management system at the Central Medical Stores Trust. Community: the integrated Community Health Information System (iCHIS). Enabling: the Government Wide Area Network (GWAN).

Not held. No count or share of health centres entering their own reports is published, and whether health centres key their reports in practice is not established. No current plan naming the system is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A national record system is being piloted at one central hospital and selected health centres (September 2026); how many centres is not stated.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health and Sanitation has rolled out the Malawi Health Care Information System, which it calls Mahis, as reported on 3 September 2026. The Digital Health Governance Framework 2025-2030, signed in June 2025, sets the system's nationwide scale-up across public and private facilities and states that digital health in Malawi continues to rely heavily on donor funding.

Tiers in use. On 3 September 2026 Mahis was in pilot at Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital in Blantyre and at selected health centres nationwide.

Where data is digitised. A newspaper's check of the hospital's outpatient department on 3 September 2026 found that registered patients no longer produced health passport books, and first-time patients had their details captured at the registry and were issued a barcode. The ministry says each patient gets a unique identifier held on a server that facilities reach through an interoperability layer. In Zomba District in September 2024, most facilities, particularly rural government clinics, kept no out-patient files, so the paper Health Passport was the only medical record for most people.

Last twelve months. In April 2026 the link between the iCHIS community module for non-communicable diseases and MaHIS at health facilities was being finalised.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Mahis was under pilot at selected health centres nationwide on 3 September 2026. In 2025 the ministry piloted linking the National ID to electronic medical record systems in five health facilities in Karonga District.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the TB Electronic Medical Record system and the HIV programme's Open EMR. Single-function: the national disease surveillance and reporting systems, the One Health Surveillance Platform (OHSP), the Early Warning, Alert and Response (EWARS) system, the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) weekly report, OpenLMIS, iHRIS, the Electronic Health Information Network (eHIN) and the warehouse management system at the Central Medical Stores Trust. Community: the integrated Community Health Information System (iCHIS). Enabling: the Government Wide Area Network (GWAN).

Not held. The number of health centres in the pilot is not given, and no hospital beyond Queen Elizabeth Central is named as using the system. Nothing held shows a record retrieved at a second facility, or the community system's link in use.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools kept each pupil's record in paper registers made by hand in 2026; whether any digital record is being prepared is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. Cohort tracking, which gives each pupil a Learner Identification Number on entry to school, is the Ministry of Education's, and sits inside what the country calls its Education Management Information System.

Levels in use. Cohort tracking passes from schools to zonal and district officers.

How the record is kept. On paper. A May 2026 policy brief from the Unlocking Data Initiative says cohort tracking rests on paper records and several manual registers: class attendance and performance, master enrolment, and admission.

Last twelve months. Nothing moved. The same brief says the cohort tracking data has rarely been analysed since 2012 and is difficult to use for planning, and in May 2026 digitising it was a recommendation.

Primary schools doing the digital input. Schools make the registers themselves, by hand, as of May 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Malawi calls its school census an EMIS: the Ministry of Education's Education Management Information System is a web-based system developed with UNICEF support, and the only collection described for it in 2024 is the Annual School Census, taken in all schools once a year and published as the Malawi Education Statistics Report; a 2023 assessment says the EMIS covers primary education only. A teacher deployment monitoring and reporting system was planned for public primary schools in all 34 education districts by October 2025. In Blantyre district, Teaching at the Right Level collected learner performance data on tablets in 48 public primary schools in 2025. The BEFIT learning tablets reached 500 public primary schools in 2023-2024, and Press Trust planned off-grid electric power for at least 200 hard-to-reach primary schools.

Not held. Nothing held names a school level, a count of schools, or an owner, plan or budget for a digital record of each pupil. No preparation or pilot of one for primary schools is on record.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths were entered digitally at 87 local service points in five districts in 2025; the share of local offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Registration Bureau, under the Ministry of Homeland Security, is the body responsible for civil registration, by its plan of September 2025. That plan, for 2025 to 2030, costs civil registration at MWK 66.06 billion and names limited domestic budget allocations as a threat to the service. UNDP's basket fund spent USD 2,107,228 on digital identity and civil registration in 2025.

Events registered digitally. In 2025 registration officers at Traditional Authority offices began registering community births and deaths on a mobile application the Bureau calls Nzika. Kits at district registration offices register births and deaths and print certificates, by the plan of September 2025.

How the record is made. The district kits synchronise with the central system over the government network or mobile links, by the same plan. Nzika runs on mobile kits with biometric authentication, works offline and is integrated with the national civil registration system, UNDP reported for 2025. In some remote areas the digital birth system had not fully replaced paper by 2024, and forms were lost or not entered centrally.

Last twelve months. Nzika registration moved from pilot to 87 service points during 2025, and national rollout is planned for 2026.

Local offices doing the digital entry. UNDP counts 87 service points in five districts in 2025, against 277 Traditional Authorities and 138 Sub-Traditional Authorities in 28 districts listed for expansion by 2030. Certificate printers were in 58 health facilities in 2025.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the National Registration and Identification System and its online pre-registration, a digital identity wallet the Bureau calls Nzika, portable biometric registration kits, e-KYC verification services, a digitally signed QR code and identity verification kit and biometric SIM registration against the national identity system. Outreach: the National Birth Registration Campaign and the Mass Child Registration campaign of 2022. Notification: birth notification in health facilities. Certificate: the Bureau's redesigned website, with pre-registration and application tracking. Exchange: the national identity link to the Malawi Social Registry, its pilot link to electronic medical records in Karonga, the Bureau's database integration with the Malawi Revenue Authority and the Immigration Department, government systems connected to the identification system and a planned integrated system joining identification and civil registration.

Not held. No share of local offices registering digitally is published, and the health facilities registering births carry no total. The gap in remote areas has no figure.
