Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Côte d'Ivoire has a published digital strategy for 2021-2025, now past its period, and a 2025 successor whose adoption is not known.

Governance and finance. The published text has the Commission Nationale de Développement de la Société de l'Information steer the Stratégie nationale de développement du numérique 2021-2025, with a monitoring committee led by the digital ministry. Paradigm Initiative ties national allocations of about USD 91 million for 2024 and USD 101 million for 2025 to its cycle.

Standing. Fraternité Matin reports it adopted in January 2022. Its successor, the Stratégie nationale de transformation numérique 2025-2035, was validated at a workshop on 19-22 August 2025.

Text. The ministry published the strategy's own text, version 1.4 of 26 January 2022, on its site.

Period. The strategy covers 2021-2025, with 2025 targets for its 36 strategic indicators. The successor is named for 2025-2035.

Delivery plan. The action plan for seven pillars lists projects with start date, priority and budget in million FCFA, such as 95,000 million FCFA to connect public administrations to the national broadband network.

Reporting. We Are Tech Africa credited the strategy on 15 July 2025 with over 5,207 km of fibre, several online service portals and an operational National Cybersecurity Agency.

Last twelve months. A workshop on 3 September 2026 opened work toward 700 digital public services and a paper-free state by 2030.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Côte d'Ivoire's data governance strategy says critical data must be stored in the country, but no rule enacting that is held.

Rule in force. Côte d'Ivoire has a rule in preparation and none in force. The Ministry of Digital Transition's national data governance strategy to 2030 sets as a principle that all data classified as critical information infrastructure must be processed and stored inside Côte d'Ivoire. It adds that the staff handling it should be at least 20 per cent Ivorian, and that a copy of data must be kept in the country for law enforcement. The strategy carries no date of its own and was published in 2024. No law, decree or regulation enacting the principle is held.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the strategy itself, which states the storage principle and names no body to enforce it. No source held names a regulator or agency given that job, and none reports the principle applied to any organisation.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Côte d'Ivoire has a 2013 personal data protection law, with compliance checks made in 2022; whether any controller has been sanctioned is not known.

Authority. Decree no. 2015-79 names ARTCI as the body that receives declarations, rules on authorisation requests within one month and imposes withdrawals and fines under the law. ARTCI was handling compliance and authorisation requests in July 2026.

Standing. Law no. 2013-450 of 19 June 2013 on personal data protection is the general law, and the 2015 decree made under it sets filing fees, prior authorisations and fines of up to FCFA 500 million on repeat breach. On 18 May 2026 the digital minister cited the 2013 law as the country's existing framework.

Regulations. Decree no. 2015-79 of 4 February 2015, signed by the President, sets the filing, authorisation, withdrawal and fine-recovery procedures under the law.

Application. The minister listed compliance control operations carried out in 2022 and national awareness campaigns launched from 2015. In July 2026 ARTCI was handling about 100 paper compliance and authorisation files each month.

Last twelve months. On 2 July 2026 ARTCI launched Certinum, an online platform for compliance and processing-authorisation requests, with the Treasury and ONECI the first two bodies to use it. The minister said a national register of data protection correspondents was created in 2026.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No named decision or sanction on a controller, and no published count of decisions, sanctions or complaints settled for a stated year. The 2022 control operations carry no count and no sanction.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2024 cybersecurity ordinance is in force; the cybercrime platform handled 12,100 files in 2024, with the law used not named.

Law in force. Ordinance No. 2024-950, a cybersecurity text, is in force, and on 16 June 2025 the Senate unanimously adopted the bill ratifying it. The same bill updates the 2017 ordinance on electronic exchanges. The source describes the measure as a cybersecurity bill and reports no dissent in the vote.

Use of the law. The national information-security agency's report for 2024, presented on 22 May 2025, says its cybercrime platform received and handled 12,100 files in 2024, up from 8,132 the year before. The largest groups were 2,822 files on attacks on human dignity and 2,326 on online fraud, and the files also covered fraudulent access to information systems. The agency put the financial loss at about 7 billion CFA francs, down from more than 9 billion a year earlier. The report as relayed does not name the law the files were handled under.

Law or rules in force. Côte d'Ivoire is bound by the rules of the BCEAO, the central bank of the eight-state West African Economic and Monetary Union. Non-banks licensed and providers required to connect. In April 2026 the BCEAO told every bank, e-money institution and payment institution to connect to its instant payment platform, PI-SPI, by 30 June, and on 25 June moved the deadline to 30 September 2026.

Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2017 ordinance binds every administrative authority to connect to the state's exchange platform; whether it has been applied since 2023 is not known.

Law or decree in force. Cote d'Ivoire's Ordinance 2017-500 of 2 August 2017, made by the President in the Council of Ministers, governs electronic exchanges between users and administrative authorities and between the administrative authorities themselves. It has a chapter on the interoperability of electronic services, and no repeal is recorded.

Sharing required and the law applied. Chapter V of the ordinance says the state sets up an interoperability platform for electronic services to which every administrative authority is bound to connect, and that the authorities must follow the general interoperability framework. The ordinance also bars an authority from asking a user again for information the administration already holds. No decree or order applying the ordinance since 2023 is recorded, and no authority is named as connected to the platform because the ordinance requires it.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Decree 2015-79 of 2015 requires the telecoms regulator's prior authorisation to send personal data abroad; whether any transfer has been authorised is not known.

Rule in force. Côte d'Ivoire has a rule in force on sending personal data abroad. Decree 2015-79 of 4 February 2015, issued under law 2013-450, requires prior authorisation from the telecoms regulator ARTCI before personal data is transferred to a third country. The receiving country must give protection equal to or higher than that of Côte d'Ivoire, and the requirement is set out in article 6 of the decree. The regulator publishes the decree on its own website, and the decree is the only instrument held on this point.

Means of complying published and rule applied. The nearest held fact is the same decree, which sets sanctions for non-compliance with the transfer rule in article 8. Its title states that it fixes how declarations are filed, how requests are presented, and how authorisations are granted and withdrawn for the processing of personal data, so the decree itself is the one published procedure held. Nothing held shows the rule being applied to a particular transfer.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held says the authority has published a list of countries, model contract terms, a permission procedure or guidance on sending data abroad.

Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A loan-financed programme to connect the communes' town halls was ratified in February 2026; how many are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Digital Transition and Digitalization implements phase 1 of PADCI, with the Republic as borrower. The project runs from 2025 to 2030 and carries US$45 million for broadband to targeted public institutions, town halls among them, in Abidjan, Yamoussoukro and twelve regions. In September 2025 a separate project for the connectivity of town halls was funded by the European Union and implemented by CIVIPOL.

What the office does over it. ONECI's civil-status project lead said on 11 August 2026 that birth data entered by health staff on new digital terminals is transmitted in real time to the civil-status office, notably the mairies or sous-préfectures.

How the office is connected. The digital ministry's plan for PADCI, dated May 2025, targets public facilities, mairies among them, that have electricity but no internet, and gives the government's ambition as broadband for all mairies.

Last twelve months. Côte d'Ivoire and IDA concluded the loan agreement for PADCI phase 1 on 17 October 2025. On 4 February 2026 the Council of Ministers ratified the loan of 84,356,070,200 FCFA by decree.

Local governments connected. The World Bank's appraisal document of 18 September 2025 says the project aims to connect more than 1,300 schools, hospitals, courts and municipalities to high-speed internet, phase 1 covering selected regions and phase 2 the country.
