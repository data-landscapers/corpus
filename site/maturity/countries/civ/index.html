Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A loan-financed programme to connect the communes' town halls was ratified in February 2026; how many are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Digital Transition and Digitalization implements phase 1 of PADCI, with the Republic as borrower. The project runs from 2025 to 2030 and carries US$45 million for broadband to targeted public institutions, town halls among them, in Abidjan, Yamoussoukro and twelve regions. In September 2025 a separate project for the connectivity of town halls was funded by the European Union and implemented by CIVIPOL.

What the office does over it. ONECI's civil-status project lead said on 11 August 2026 that birth data entered by health staff on new digital terminals is transmitted in real time to the civil-status office, notably the mairies or sous-préfectures.

How the office is connected. The digital ministry's plan for PADCI, dated May 2025, targets public facilities, mairies among them, that have electricity but no internet, and gives the government's ambition as broadband for all mairies.

Last twelve months. Côte d'Ivoire and IDA concluded the loan agreement for PADCI phase 1 on 17 October 2025. On 4 February 2026 the Council of Ministers ratified the loan of 84,356,070,200 FCFA by decree.

Local governments connected. The World Bank's appraisal document of 18 September 2025 says the project aims to connect more than 1,300 schools, hospitals, courts and municipalities to high-speed internet, phase 1 covering selected regions and phase 2 the country. The ministry's plan sets the connection of mairies for 2025 to 2030.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: the IT equipment of the civil registration centres in town halls and sub-prefectures and the computer equipment of Minignan town hall. Applications: ONECI's Citiweb, its national civil registration software and the birth-registration devices it calls terminaux numériques; the Système de Gestion Electronique de l'Etat Civil; the digitised civil status registers of Grand-Bassam; the automated processing of civil-status data that the decree of 2 October 2019 provides for; Yopougon's interactive digital kiosks for civil registration extracts; the electronic signature; the Treasury's TresorPay and Net-Collect Services; SYGIDAN-CTI; and the digital applications for communication between authorities planned for greater Abidjan. Web: the UVICOCI official website and UVICOCI's planned Plateforme interactive de Gouvernement Ouvert Local. Public access: the planned Wi-Fi public of 10,000 hotspots. City systems: the Géoportail d'urbanisme du Grand Abidjan, Plateau's shared underground network for telecommunications operators, the Abidjan CCTV surveillance network and the Abidjan intelligent transport and smart parking systems. Backbone: the Integrated Government Network, the administration's broadband networks and backbone, the modernised fibre optic network and the state data centre the digital transition minister described on 4 September 2026, the 5,000km optical fibre transmission network, the SNDI Government Data Centre and the National Data Centre at Grand Bassam, the Cloud National and the Réseau national de Vsat and the private Raxio data centre at Grand-Bassam. Capacity: the SNDI ICT training programme for civil servants. Central: e-Justice, SIGFU, the Public Service Houses, MYONECI+ and the Registre National des Personnes Physiques, the Plateforme nationale intégrée d'interopérabilité, Milie and the Open Data platform, TrésorMoney, the foundational identification system under WURI and the justice ministry's base de données centrale de contrôle. Upper tier: none held.

Not held. No source states a town hall connected and in use, and none gives a count or share of town halls connected or to be connected. The civil-status data sent to the mairies is not described as arriving over a connection of the mairie's own.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A ministry-built register system was in a 22-commune pilot of 201 in December 2025; whether any commune records in it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Directorate General of Decentralisation and Local Development (DGDDL) developed the SYGIDAN Recettes software in its own services and directs the PAMREC project. In April 2024 the project was financed by the African Development Bank and the Ivorian State, together 3,003,901,293 FCFA.

What is recorded. In April 2024 the DGDDL's director described SYGIDAN Recettes, then at pre-validation, as digitalising all of a commune's revenues, with traceability each time a tax is collected. The project's design of July 2021 registers taxpayers and records payments, balances, arrears and penalties.

Where the record is held. The tool is to centralise the data from all the actors collecting local revenue. The 2021 design was to connect it to the systems of the tax directorate, the Treasury, the DGDDL or the Court of Auditors.

Last twelve months. On 9 December 2025 the DGDDL handed the commune of Assinie-Mafia its taxpayer database, within the PAMREC pilot.

Local governments recording. The African Development Bank's appraisal of July 2021 places the pilot in 22 of 201 municipalities. In April 2024 the tool was to be deployed to the 22 communes and then distributed to all from January 2025.

Noted, not assessed. The Treasury's Net-Collect Services platform for the electronic collection of the resources of communes, regions and districts, and its TrésorMoney payment service; TresorPay; the Abidjan District addressing project, PADA; the Géoportail du Grand Abidjan; the E-Commune geospatial database; SIGE, the education ministry's management system.

Not held. No fact says the system is in use in any commune, and no count of communes recording is given. Whether the distribution to all communes announced for January 2025 took place.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2020 departmental health offices entered clinics' paper reports into the national system; nothing more recent shows where reports are entered.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's annual report for 2020 calls DHIS2 the national database of routine data and places its checking with the ministry's own information directorate. The ministry's costed health plan for 2021 to 2025, dated October 2021, carries 30 million FCFA a year to make DHIS 2 interoperable with four other systems. USAID's President's Malaria Initiative financed support to routine reporting and the use of DHIS2 data in 20 health districts in 2022.

Tiers in use. The October 2021 plan states that DHIS 2 is deployed in all regional and departmental health directorates, all regional hospital centres and general hospitals, and in a few first-contact health establishments. In 2020 the general hospitals and regional hospital centres had their data keyed into DHIS2 by their own data managers.

Where data is digitised. In 2020 each first-contact establishment drew up a monthly report from its registers, and the district's epidemiological surveillance officer keyed those reports into DHIS2 at the departmental health directorate. PMI Measure Malaria reported for 2022 that health centres reporting on paper had no access to the information in DHIS2.

Last twelve months. On 11 September 2025 the ministry's Health Information Directorate organised a DHIS2 technical working group meeting on managing the national DHIS2 database, which it calls SIGSANTE.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. By the ministry's account for 2020, first-contact establishments did not enter their own reports. District reports reached 100% completeness and 98.23% timeliness in 2020.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SIGDEP, SIGDEP 2, the Carnet de vaccination numérique and the eSkinHealth app. Single-function: OpenELIS, DHIS2 as used for infectious disease surveillance, MAGPI, SORMAS, DVD-MT, E-SIGL and the mobile dashboard and performance-score applications for malaria. Community: the mHealth platform, also written mHealth.ci, which its publisher TICANALYSE says transmits community-level data automatically to the national DHIS2, and the pilot digital tools for collecting community data.

Not held. Nothing held after 2022 says where clinics' reports are keyed, and nothing says the few first-contact establishments with DHIS 2 enter their own reports. No plan or financing dated within the last three years is held, and no dated event in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The ministry's patient record ran throughout one general hospital from August 2024; 268 establishments were connected by March 2025, of unstated type.

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's digital health directorate presents the Dossier Patient Informatisé (DPI) as a platform of the ministry, deployed in August 2024 by a team the directorate led. On 25 March 2025 the project's steering committee set itself to define a detailed plan for digitalising the health system. The World Bank's appraisal of 18 September 2025 states that Phase 1 of RAPID-CI, an IDA credit of EUR 128.6 million, will support the rollout of an electronic patient records system across several levels of care.

Tiers in use. The DPI has been operational at the Hôpital Général de Ferkessédougou since 16 August 2024, used by all the hospital's services, the directorate reports.

Where data is digitised. At Ferkessédougou the platform is installed on the hospital's local server and every service registers and follows patients on it, from 16 August 2024.

Last twelve months. On 22 April 2026 the health ministry noted the deployment of the DPI as an advance under way and prioritised 150 first-contact establishments for connectivity in 2026. A national workshop closing on 4 September 2026 retained the computerised patient record among ten quick wins to be put into service before the end of 2026.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The project unit told the steering committee on 25 March 2025 that the first phase had connected 268 health establishments to the hospital information system and the DPI. The health minister counted both installed in 193 health establishments in 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: SIGDEP, SIGDEP 2, the Carnet de vaccination numérique and the eSkinHealth app. Single-function: OpenELIS, DHIS2 as used for infectious disease surveillance, MAGPI, SORMAS, DVD-MT, E-SIGL and the mobile dashboard and performance-score applications for malaria. Community: the mHealth platform, also written mHealth.ci, which its publisher TICANALYSE says transmits community-level data automatically to the national DHIS2, and the pilot digital tools for collecting community data.

Not held. The tiers of the 268 establishments and the total they are drawn from are not stated, and use of the record is shown at one general hospital only. Whether a patient's record can be retrieved at another facility is not established, and no primary clinic is shown using it.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A per-pupil performance record started in five Abidjan pilot schools, level not named, in January 2024; whether it went further is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The education ministry's national strategy for digitalising education, covering 2024 to 2028, names the project it calls DOBCONNECT among the achievements it builds on and carries a budgeted action matrix. The vendor's 2019 page for the platform named SMART ECOLE says it was designed for the ministry by its information systems directorate and is run by its schools directorate.

Levels in use. SMART ECOLE's page invites pupils of nursery schools, primary schools, lycées and collèges to identify themselves online for school year 2019-2020.

How the record is kept. The strategy says DOBCONNECT collects information on pupils' school performance so that each can be followed individually, from January 2024 in its pilot establishments. SMART ECOLE, by its 2019 page, manages schools from online enrolment to the proclamation of school results.

Last twelve months. The strategy gave DOBCONNECT's general rollout as due in May 2024.

Primary schools doing the digital input. DOBCONNECT was started in January 2024 in 5 pilot establishments of the Abidjan regional directorates, the strategy says.

Noted, not assessed. Single-function: the digital national school map the ministry is preparing, bursary payment by mobile money and the ministry's mail platform MENA-GEC. Learning: the girls' application NZASSA GIRL and the UVCI digital spaces and Orange Fab training modules.

Not held. Nothing shows DOBCONNECT beyond its five pilot establishments, their level, or who enters its data, and no count of schools using SMART ECOLE is held. No source of finance is held, and nothing dated in the last twelve months.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In March 2026 the registrar said 98 per cent of civil status centres were computerised and connected; whether any registers births digitally is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. ONECI, the national civil status and identification office, is the executing arm of the national civil status policy, the Minister of the Interior and Security said on 16 June 2025. A convention of 2 March 2026 implements the Stratégie nationale de l'état civil et de l'identification. The 2026 finance law appropriates FCFA 500 million from domestic revenue for modernising civil status.

Events registered digitally. The software ONECI supplied to the centres, which AIP names Citiweb, is intended for the digital registration of births, marriages, deaths and other civil status acts.

How the record is made. The World Bank said on 18 September 2025 that the registry is still on paper. The decree of 2 October 2019 keeps the paper register as the legal record and permits acts to be established by computer beside it.

Last twelve months. At the end of 2025 ONECI supplied all town hall and sub-prefecture centres with computers carrying the software, and began training agents. From 27 to 29 July 2026 it trained 44 agents of the Gôh region.

Local offices doing the digital entry. ONECI's director general said on 2 March 2026 that 98 per cent of civil status centres were computerised and connected to the software, and on 11 August 2026 that its Système national de gestion de l'état civil was deployed in 699 centres.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: ONECI's digitisation of more than four million civil status acts, as of 11 August 2026, the digitisation of the Grand-Bassam registers in 2024, the numérisation des registres provided for with the Sant'Egidio community, and the 2019 decree's base de données centrale de contrôle and electronic relevé des tables annuelles. Notification: the terminaux numériques handed to health facilities of the Tonkpi region on 11 August 2026, the solutions mobiles d'état civil planned for health centres, the points de collecte communautaires planned for villages, the birth declaration by SMS or app proposed in the e-services review of 18 November 2025 and the decree's paper registres de collecte kept by health and community agents. Certificate: MaPoste, e-justice.ci, the interactive kiosk at Yopougon town hall and the electronic kiosks planned in 50 municipalities and civil registry centres. Identity: the Registre national des personnes physiques, the national authentication platform and digital identity wallet under feasibility study from 5 October 2026, the SSO national proposed on 4 September 2026, the biometric national identity card, the MYONECI+ application and the mobile enrolment campaigns, ONECI's online purchase of enrolment stamps, its online authentication platform, its e-services and e-ID and its VERIF application, the WURI unique identifier, the national refugee database and the biometric temporary resident card. Enabling: broadband connectivity for 835 civil registry offices, targeted for July 2029. Exchange: the plateforme nationale d'interopérabilité UXP.

Not held. The evidence does not say that any centre registers a birth in the software: every figure is of centres equipped, computerised or with the system deployed, and neither the 98 per cent nor the 699 comes with a total of centres. How the record is made at an equipped centre is not established.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

No evidence Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Only cameras, fingerprint files and online court documents are held, to 2026; how stations record reports is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. A decree adopted on 28 April 2021 created the police information system the government names SIP CI, to be shared by all law-enforcement services. The steering committee of the regional fingerprint project recommended on 21 July 2022 that the country's existing fingerprint system be restored or upgraded as a priority, and the national police ran biometric checks against files of suspected terrorists during the football tournament of January and February 2024. The government's record for 2011-2025 lists urban video protection in the 12 police prefectures, a platform against cybercrime, a project to buy counter-terrorism software and computer equipment at the Didiévi commissariat, which opened on 25 April 2025. Abidjan has a city-wide camera network with a command centre (2026). The justice ministry's e-Justice platform had all 39 first-instance tribunals connected by 29 June 2026 and issues criminal-record extracts online. The post office's application, launched on 16 January 2026, takes requests for criminal records. A November 2025 review of digital services lists a portal for vehicle registration, licences and fines, and recommends an electronic criminal record.

Not held. Nothing held states whether police stations or gendarmerie brigades record reports or cases on paper or digitally, in what system, or in how many stations.
