Governance and finance. The telecommunications ministry, MINTTICS, put the proposed Livro Branco das TIC 2023-2027 to public consultation in 2024, and its minister writes that the policy aligns the sector with the National Development Plan 2023-2027.

The decree text holds no numeric coverage, speed or price target for the 2023-2027 period, and no delivery figures are given there.

Data localisation policies: localisation rule

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2020 decree keeps Angola's credit bureaux' data centres in the country and names an enforcer; whether it has been applied is not known.

Rule in force. Angola's rule covers credit information bureaux. Presidential Decree 275/20 of 21 October 2020 requires private credit bureaux to keep their primary data processing centres in national territory. Their replica facilities for data recovery must also be inside the country, at least 30 km from the primary centre. The decree gives no later date for compliance and nothing held says it has been repealed.

Enforcer named and rule applied. The decree makes the Agência de Protecção de Dados responsible for authorising, supervising, inspecting and fining private credit bureaux. The agency must also approve any use of cloud storage for their data. Breaching the regulation, including adopting cloud storage without that approval, is a contravention that carries a fine. No order, fine or exemption under the rule is held.

Data protection legislation: general law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Angola's 2011 data protection law is in force and its agency fined named companies in 2025 and 2026; a count of decisions is not known.

Authority. Lei n.º 22/11 creates the Agência de Protecção de Dados (APD) as a public-law body of seven members. Its organic statute was approved on 10 October 2016, and three board members were appointed on 6 September 2019. Its board took a sanction decision on 10 August 2026.

Standing. Lei n.º 22/11, the Lei da Protecção de Dados Pessoais, was published on 17 June 2011 and entered into force that day. On 25 June 2026 the Assembleia Nacional rejected a UNITA bill to amend it.

Regulations. Decreto Presidencial n.º 60/21 of 10 March 2021 sets the fees the APD charges for registering files and authorising processing. Decreto Presidencial n.º 214/16 approves the APD's organic statute.

Application. The APD fined Lizíria, Lda., owner of Hotel Diamante, the kwanza equivalent of USD 115,000 on 10 August 2026 for breaching articles 30 and 31 of the law. Valor Económico reported on 26 June 2026 fines of about USD 505,000 on Crescer Tech and USD 112,000 on Fast Digital Center, and fines of USD 100,000 on TAAG and USD 75,000 on Banco de Desenvolvimento de Angola, disclosed in September 2025.

Last twelve months. The law is under revision: an APD administrator said on 17 June 2026 that the public consultation was complete and the bill would go to the Assembleia Nacional in the next legislative year. On 21 April 2026 terms of reference were issued for drafting a new organic law for the APD.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No count of the APD's decisions for a stated period ending by 30 September 2026. The companies it sanctioned in June and July 2024 are not named.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Angola's identity law, amended in 2017 and 2021, gives each citizen a unique number on a chip card. No rules since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Angola's identity card rests on Lei n.º 4/09, the law on civil identification and the issue of the citizen's identity card. The National Assembly amended it in August 2017 and again in January 2021, each amendment in force on publication. On 29 April 2026 the Council of Ministers reviewed a civil and criminal identification bill that would gather the rules on the identity card and criminal records into one law.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law as amended gives each identity card a number that acts in law as the unique citizen number, and the card carries an encrypted QR code. Since 2017 the card has also carried a microprocessor holding the holder's tax, social security, birth-record and voter numbers, so that it can be read by public and private institutions.

Noted, not assessed. Bilhete de Identidade de Cidadão Nacional; Plataforma de Gestão Integrada da Identificação Civil e Criminal.

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Cross-border data transfers: rule on sending data abroad

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Angola's 2011 law requires notice or the regulator's authorisation to send data abroad; a firm was fined in 2026; guidance is not known.

Rule in force. Angola's Law 22/11 on personal data protection came into force on publication, 17 June 2011. Articles 33 and 34 make sending personal data abroad to a country with adequate protection subject to notification to the data protection agency (APD), and to a country without it subject to APD authorisation on stated grounds such as the data subject's written consent. Hunton reported in 2011 that the APD must approve transfers to countries without adequate protection.
