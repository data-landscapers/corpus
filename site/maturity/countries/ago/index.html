Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2026 counters in municipal administrations work in a national database, in the 11 municipalities of one province; the national count is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. A presidential order reported on 8 June 2026 created an interministerial commission, coordinated by the Minister of State and head of the Casa Civil, to reinforce the network of Balcões Únicos de Atendimento ao Público (BUAP), as the Government names its public service counters, and charged it with the network's power, computing equipment, communication systems and connectivity. The Ministry of Territorial Administration runs a national programme of V/SAT satellite antennas for commune seats.

What the office does over it. Since 15 June 2026 adult citizens confirm their electoral data at counters installed in municipal and commune administrations, feeding the national database of adult citizens. In Zaire province more than 24,000 adults updated their data at the counters between 15 July and 14 August 2026.

How the office is connected. The ministry's antennas are to guarantee communications and internet at commune seats so that the counters there can work, and ten Zaire commune seats with no UNITEL network were to get them in the days after 14 August 2026.

Last twelve months. The data update began on 15 June 2026 with 254 counters in a first phase and 634 intended. The Cubango provincial government approved on 18 December 2025 a project that provides for internet access in all its municipal administrations, to start in January 2026.

Local governments connected. Twelve counters were working in the 11 municipalities of Zaire province on 14 August 2026, on the provincial director's word.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: Projecto Njila's purchase of computer equipment, among projects executed in Huambo and Bié by December 2025, and the planned hardware and software for 58 target municipalities. Applications: the Janela Única de Concessão de Direitos Fundiários, instituted by Presidential Decree 84/25, SAGIT and the municipal cadastres, the Registo Eleitoral Oficioso, the Base de Dados dos Cidadãos Maiores, the tourism platform SIGTUR, the municipal register of local accommodation, the Sistema Integrado de Gestão do Programa de Investimento Público, the Sistema Integrado de Gestão do Património do Estado, the Cubango provincial government's planned SIGDA document system, the planned sistema de gestão integrado das autarquias locais, the health sector's planned FPOM municipal planning tool, the database of the municipal administrations under the place-names regulation, SIGAS, and SIGFE and SNCPE, planned for municipal budgets and procurement. Web: Cubango's planned citizen app, the planned online service for issuing and paying building licences, the Portal do Munícipe, the planned websites of the 58 selected municipalities and the Portal do Cidadão. Public access: Angola Online, with about 141 free access points on 20 January 2025, Angola Digital, N'gola Digital school computer rooms, Conecta Angola, the ANGOSAT-2 connectivity point at Quirimbo, reconnected on 28 August 2026, and the ANGOSAT-2 VSAT at the University of Namibe. City systems: Benguela's prepaid and smart water meters, the Centro Integrado de Segurança Pública, whose Cabinda centre was inaugurated on 9 December 2025, and the National Police's Centros de Comando e Controlo in five Luanda municipalities, begun in February 2026. Backbone: Angola Telecom's national backbone, the Rede Nacional de Fibra Óptica, the Rede Nacional de Banda Larga, Angola Telecom's fibre broadband network, whose first link was inaugurated at Lobito on 16 September 2026, the broadband infrastructure in 13 provinces that Huawei described in August 2026, the Projecto Nacional de Banda Larga, the planned domestic submarine cable, the announced Luanda metropolitan fibre network, Ilumina Angola, ANGOSAT-2, UNITEL's national fibre network and its branch of the 2Africa cable, the Rede Privativa do Estado, the rede básica of the 2011 law, the Data Center e Cloud do Governo, inaugurated at Camama on 28 April 2026, INFOSI's Backup Data Center, the Centro Nacional de Dados and the .AO domain and GOV.AO subdomain. Capacity: Projecto Njila's 18 training actions in 2025 for municipal administration staff in Huambo and Bié. Upper tier: the Benguela provincial water utility's customer database. Central: the Serviço Integrado de Atendimento ao Cidadão, with 16 subunits in 12 provinces in September 2026, the Cadastro Social Único, launched on 20 July 2026, the identity card printing posts announced on 1 July 2026 for all 21 provincial capitals, the civil and criminal identification and civil registration posts, the identification platform PGIBI, the civil registration systems RCI, SIRC and PROMOREN, the civil registry database of Lei 6/15, the Bilhete de Identidade Integrado, the Centro Nacional de Impressão do Bilhete de Identidade, the Base de Dados de Identificação Civil and the Ficheiro Informático de Cidadãos Maiores, the birth registration posts rehabilitated in Bié in 2025, Nascer com Registo, the Guichê Único da Empresa and GUE Online, the tax administration's SIGT, Plataforma ECM, Portal do Contribuinte and AGT Mobile and RUPE payment reference, the electronic processing system of the INSS, the Ombudsman's virtual hearing rooms and complaints-management system, the Kwenda management information system and its grievance redress mechanism, the education ministry's SIGE, the national cadastre system and property registration system, the Registo Informatizado do Cadastro Predial, the health ministry's eSurv Companion, and the planned health-sector internet network, VID-R and DHIS2 of the 2023-2027 national plan.

Digitalisation of local government records: revenue records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A finance ministry system recording municipal payers and fees was put in all 164 municipalities by 2021; how many record in it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Finance launched and runs the Portal do Munícipe under Presidential Decree 47/18. In November 2021 its Secretary of State presented it as a ministry tool, run with the ministry's SETIC-FP IT service, that the municipalities use.

What is recorded. In November 2021 the Portal held the records of residents and their households and tracked the whole cycle of fees, licences and fines billed by local administrations, with payment by the state's single payment reference.

Where the record is held. The ministry describes the Portal as a centralised system reached over the web, with revenue deposited in a Treasury sub-account. In August 2019 it let central bodies see the amounts local administrations collect.

Last twelve months. The ministry officially launched the Portal on 10 November 2021 across the 164 municipalities.

Local governments recording. The Secretary of State for Finance said on 10 November 2021 that the Portal was implemented in all 164 municipalities, with telecommunications problems in some. In August 2019 the ministry said it had created the technical conditions for it in 152 of 164.

Noted, not assessed. SIGT, the tax authority's integrated tax management system; SIGFE, to be adopted for municipal budget preparation and execution, and SNCPE, to digitalise municipal procurement; RUPE, the national payment reference; the tax authority's Front Office for internet payments.

Not held. No share or count of the municipalities whose staff record in the Portal. Nothing dated after November 2021, so the system's current plan, finance and progress are not shown.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Clinics report on paper, and municipal or provincial offices enter the figures into the national reporting system (2025).

Governance, planning, finance. The health ministry's Office of Studies, Planning and Statistics, GEPE, oversees the health information system and has been moving it onto DHIS2 since 2015, as an article of June 2025 states. WHO supported the drafting in 2024 of a strategic plan for the system running to 2030, and the European Union finances that plan's preparation. The World Bank's appraisal of 4 November 2025 proposes US$5 million to extend DHIS2's functions and geographic coverage.

Tiers in use. A technical mission in March 2026 found the DHIS2-based national system had more than 4,700 registered users.

Where data is digitised. Health facilities collect and report their data on paper, and the figures are aggregated and entered into DHIS2 at municipal or provincial level, as the June 2025 article states.

Last twelve months. In March 2026 a joint WHO Angola and HISP UiO mission with Saudigitus assessed the platform, and the ministry and its partners agreed to upgrade it, harmonise data across information flows, build dashboards for decision-makers and train staff at all levels.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. Health facilities report in paper format, and entry into DHIS2 is made at municipal or provincial level, on the June 2025 article's account.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: ReDIV, as the ministry names its vaccination register, the TB EMR system piloted in Luanda and the immunization registry system. Single-function: the ministry's stock system IOTA, eLMIS, the health logistics and supply chain management platform, Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response, the neglected tropical diseases programme's DHIS2-based reporting, the drug dependence data held on DHIS2 and the National TB Program's digital database. Enabling: telemedicine.

Not held. No source names the tiers using the system, and no count or share of primary clinics entering their own reports is published. The strategic plan is not reported as adopted, and no domestic budget line is tied to the system by name.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

One hospital was testing a prototype electronic record for maternity care in 2019, entered after the event; nothing is known since.

Governance, planning, finance. The electronic obstetric record prototype at the Irene Neto Maternity Hospital in Lubango was part of a doctoral research project at the University of Beira Interior, Portugal, adapting ObsCare, an application owned by Virtual Care, as its authors stated in November 2019.

Tiers in use. The prototype ran at one 180-bed maternity hospital in November 2019.

Where data is digitised. At that hospital the clinical processes of deliveries from the fourth quarter of 2016 were being entered retrospectively in the admission, archive and medical statistics section in November 2019. Luanda's two reference tuberculosis facilities kept clinical and surveillance data entirely on paper records and registers between March and September 2019.

Last twelve months. The prototype was in its test phase in November 2019.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Anti-Tuberculosis Dispensary of Luanda recorded on paper medical records and registers in 2019.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: ReDIV, as the ministry names its vaccination register, the TB EMR system piloted in Luanda and the immunization registry system. Single-function: the ministry's stock system IOTA, eLMIS, the health logistics and supply chain management platform, Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response, the neglected tropical diseases programme's DHIS2-based reporting, the drug dependence data held on DHIS2 and the National TB Program's digital database. Enabling: telemedicine.

Not held. Nothing is dated after 2019, so whether the prototype still runs is not established. No source names the tiers holding an EMR, any primary clinic using one, or who governs one.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Schools answered a twice-yearly school count questionnaire in 2021/22; whether any primary school keeps a per-pupil record is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Angola calls its school census an EMIS: the education ministry's Sistema de Informação de Gestão Escolar (SIGE), which the country also calls the EMIS and the School Management System, is a questionnaire that schools answer, described in 2022. The ministry's Censo Escolar Anual covered every school in operation in all 18 provinces in 2021/2022, in two collections a year, and is published as the Anuário Estatístico da Educação. The yearly national return is also called the Cadastro. In a pilot in Huíla province reported in 2017, 28 technicians visited 1,843 primary and secondary schools and keyed the principal's answers on tablets.

Not held. No dated statement says whether primary schools keep their pupils' records on paper, and nothing held describes a system with a record for each pupil that a school adds to during the year.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Since 2023 births are entered digitally at offices in Luanda and three other cities; most offices use paper, and the digital count is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Justice and Human Rights runs civil registration through its directorate DNIRN and introduced RCI, as it names its civil registration system, in 2023. Presidential Decree 267/24 of 28 November 2024 approved a plan to December 2026 for a single database fed daily by civil registration. Nascer com Registo, the government's maternity registration programme, is financed by the European Union, ANGOP reported on 4 March 2026.

Events registered digitally. Births have been registered in RCI since 2023 at civil registration posts. Angola told the registrars general on 18 July 2026 that biographic and biometric data are collected when the birth entry is issued.

How the record is made. In RCI the birth is entered at the post, in a system with a centralised database. The older SIRC has no central database and the majority of civil registration units still rely on paper records, as of 2024.

Last twelve months. Novo Jornal reported on 5 May 2026 that identification and civil registration posts in almost the whole country had been without their system for four months. A civil registration post opened on 3 March 2026 in the provincial hospital's maternity at Mbanza Kongo.

Local offices doing the digital entry. RCI is in Luanda's registries and in one post each in Huambo, Benguela and Lubango, as of 2024. The World Bank's diagnostic counts 39 civil registration units in Luanda province and at least 255 in the country in 2023.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the outsourced digitisation of civil registration books in eight Luanda services and the planned digitisation of the national civil registry archive. Notification: the birth and death declaration books of health units and the health-unit transmission of birth data to the registry. Outreach: the Campanha de Universalização do Registo Civil e do Bilhete de Identidade, as the justice ministry names it, its mobile brigades, Cabinda's Universalização do Registo de Nascimento e Emissão de BI programme, the registration of children in schools, Projecto Njila's mass registration campaigns, the Mass Registration Program for Civil Registration and for Obtaining an Identity Card, the planned 600 fixed and mobile data-capture kits, the Registo de Nascimento nas Escolas campaign of 2018 and the 2019 school and parish registration campaign in Cacuaco. Identity: the Bilhete de Identidade, its platform PGIBI, the Sistema de Gestão do Bilhete de Identidade and the multifunctional digital identity database the justice ministry lists, the Base de Dados de Identificação Civil, the Centro de Produção e Controlo do Bilhete de Identidade, the Plano de Universalização do Bilhete de Identidade, the Plano de Municipalização do Bilhete de Identidade, decentralised printing of the card, the identification posts, data capture for the card at the Balcões Únicos de Atendimento ao Público, BI ao Domicílio, as the ministry names its home service, the Digital Identification Program, the Plataforma Nacional de Identidade Digital e Assinaturas Electrónicas put to tender in August 2026, the national identity database, the biometric border control platform, the biometric passport issuance system and UNHCR's proGres. Enabling: Projecto Njila's rehabilitation and equipping of birth registration posts. Exchange: the Número Único do Cidadão, the link between RCI and PGIBI, the interoperability between the birth registration and identity card systems the minister described on 1 July 2026, the planned integration of the identity card, civil registry and adult-citizen databases, the transmission of identity-card files to the civil registry and, under Lei 6/15, the communication of birth data to social, health and tax services and the interconnection of the civil registry and civil identification databases.

Not held. No source counts the offices registering in the central system, and deaths in a digital system are not described. No source says the outage reported on 5 May 2026 has ended.

Identification: the justice ministry's Sistema de Gestão do Registo Criminal, the system of the civil and criminal identification posts, down at posts for about four months to May 2026, the Plataforma de Gestão Integrada da Identificação Civil e Criminal contracted in January 2017, the BI Integrado system for identity cards and criminal record certificates, listed as implemented by February 2018, the criminal record certificate service of the identification offices, as at May 2019, the forensic laboratory and school planned for 2023-2027 and the database of identification and criminal-interest data required by the law of 23 April 2020.
