Rule in force. Angola's rule covers credit information bureaux. Authority. Lei n.º 22/11 creates the Agência de Protecção de Dados (APD) as a public-law body of seven members. Its organic statute was approved on 10 October 2016, and three board members were appointed on 6 September 2019. Its board took a sanction decision on 10 August 2026.

Standing. Lei n.º 22/11, the Lei da Protecção de Dados Pessoais, was published on 17 June 2011 and entered into force that day. On 25 June 2026 the Assembleia Nacional rejected a UNITA bill to amend it.

Regulations. Decreto Presidencial n.º 60/21 of 10 March 2021 sets the fees the APD charges for registering files and authorising processing. Decreto Presidencial n.º 214/16 approves the APD's organic statute.

Legislation covering digital id

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Angola's identity law, amended in 2017 and 2021, gives each citizen a unique number on a chip card. No rules since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Angola's identity card rests on Lei n.º 4/09, the law on civil identification and the issue of the citizen's identity card. The National Assembly amended it in August 2017 and again in January 2021, each amendment in force on publication. On 29 April 2026 the Council of Ministers reviewed a civil and criminal identification bill that would gather the rules on the identity card and criminal records into one law.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The law as amended gives each identity card a number that acts in law as the unique citizen number, and the card carries an encrypted QR code. Since 2017 the card has also carried a microprocessor holding the holder's tax, social security, birth-record and voter numbers, so that it can be read by public and private institutions.
