Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

Unplaced Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A 2019 policy targeted internet access at every county Superintendent office; whether any is connected is not known, so no stage is set.

Noted, not assessed. Equipment: desktop computers, laptops, printers and UPS units handed to the County Development Planning Units of five counties in March 2026, making nine of 15 equipped, under a UNDP programme financed by Sweden; and the county administrative buildings' equipment, about 99% of which, computers and printers included, the Ministry of Internal Affairs says has outlived its usefulness. Applications: IFMIS, the government's financial management system, in 86 ministries, agencies and commissions at 30 September 2025, with four County Treasury Offices connected against a target of 15 by December 2030; and e-County, a county-level system the national digital strategy for 2025-2029 says Liberia lacks. For the public: community Wi-Fi in public areas of Gbarnga, Ganta, Kakata and Sanniquellie, and a 20-seat digital learning laboratory at J.W. Pearson Public School in Ganta, both from CSquared's programme completed in September 2026; the Digital Village Program, whose first village was launched in Lofa County in May 2026; and public Wi-Fi and access points at schools, hospitals and County Service Centers, and Community Digital Access Centers, both proposed by the infrastructure conference of January 2026. Backbone: GovNet, the government network, with 52 ministries, agencies and commissions connected directly to fibre since its 2019 rollout and a conference recommendation of January 2026 to extend it to the counties; the dark fibre connecting ministries, agencies and commissions; Libtelco's secure government network, on fibre in Monrovia in 2019; CSquared's fibre network, over 250km in Monrovia and Montserrado and over 350km to the Côte d'Ivoire and Guinea borders; the national fibre backbone, about 1,000 km short, mainly in the southeast, with nationwide fibre envisaged by 2029; the National Data Center, hosted at LTC-Mobile and being upgraded under the World Bank's GREAT project in March 2026; the LTC Mobile National Data Centre in Monrovia, a carrier-neutral colocation facility; the Amilcar Cabral submarine cable under WARDIP-SOP2, financed by the World Bank with US$50 million and landing in the Buchanan area; and the CLSG network at the Buchanan substation, where linking the cable to it was assessed. Central: the County Service Centers, one in each of the 15 counties, where the Ministry of Internal Affairs' 2024 assessment found 35 of the 135 computers supplied in 2015 working, no printers and no internet connectivity; the GREAT project's upgrade of their IT infrastructure, financed by the World Bank with US$5 million, for which a consultant assessed all 15 in 2024; One-Stop-Shop Service Centers planned in Zwedru, Grand Bassa and Lofa in August 2026; county treasuries, which UNDP's programme aims to set up in all 15 counties, and a Vehicle and Driver's License Hub in Ganta City; the Ministry of Internal Affairs' own ICT network, with internet restored to three headquarters buildings in 2024, and its LERN conflict early-warning platform, shut down for lack of funding; the Liberia Revenue Authority's ASYCUDA, in 11 of 17 Customs Business Offices, and LITAS, in 5 of 18 Tax Business Offices, in August 2026, and its real property register, piloted in Margibi County from 15 December 2021 and extended to Buchanan in 2022, with GIS and valuation systems in the two counties; the national biometric identification system, with enrolment offices being prepared in counties and districts in August 2026; the Birth Registration Information Management System, in all 15 counties and 58 centres in December 2024; the Ministry of Education's county data hubs for the Annual School Census, at County Education Offices in all 15 counties and its digitized Monitoring and Supervision System, piloted in five counties in March 2026; the eLiberia Portal, developed and with no services online in May 2024; the Case Management Information System of the courts, county attorneys and police; the police's Crime Statistics Information Management System; and the West African Police Information System, whose first equipment was handed over on 5 July 2019.

Not held. Whether any county Superintendent office is connected, how, what it does over the connection and how many are. The one target on record, internet access at every Superintendent office, belongs to the national ICT policy for 2019-2024, and no dated statement that the offices lack a connection is held.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All facilities report monthly into the national system (2024), with district and county clerks entering the figures from paper forms (2022).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's report for 2024 states that its HMIS runs on DHIS2 and is managed within its Department of Policy, Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation. The ministry's Data Quality Improvement Plan of June 2022 runs 2022 to 2026 and is costed at US$7,312,887.50. The memorandum signed with the United States on 9 December 2025 plans US$1,338,372 of US funding for HMIS/DHIS2 over 2026 to 2030.

Tiers in use. Every health facility, public and private, is required to report service data monthly, and counties submit to the central ministry by the 17th of the following month, on the ministry's account for 2024.

Where data is digitised. Evidence Action's survey found that all 48 facilities surveyed in Montserrado, Margibi and Grand Bassa filled paper HMIS forms monthly, which district or county data clerks entered into DHIS2, in records for May to July 2022. The Liberian team's Countdown to 2030 synthesis of June 2022 says facilities' monthly reports are normally collected by the County Health Team for entry into DHIS2.

Last twelve months. WHO reports that in 2025, with Gavi support, it delivered 31 laptops with statistical software for data analysis in the health management information and surveillance systems.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Countdown to 2030 synthesis counts 856 functional health facilities submitting reports through DHIS2, of 964 on the Master Facility List, for data of January 2017 to December 2021.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 e-tracker, the DHIS2 Tracker for HIV client-level data and the DHIS2 Tracker of the Key Population program. Single-function: eIDSR, the Lab Information System, mSupply and eLMIS and the pharmacy system sLMIS. Community: the Community Based Information System (CBIS) and its electronic form, eCBIS.

Not held. No national figure for clinics entering their own reports is held. Nothing on where data is keyed is dated after July 2022, and no event dated inside the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A vendor's system runs at a handful of pilot clinics and health centres (September 2026); the exact number is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. OneHealthEHR Inc. deploys and runs its platform with a consortium of European firms and research bodies, with the National Public Health Institute of Liberia as surveillance partner, as reported in September 2026. In the memorandum signed with the United States on 9 December 2025, Liberia commits to an open-source EMR in clinics, health centers and hospitals, with targets of 20% of all public health facilities in 2026 and 100% in 2030, and the United States plans US$2,676,744 for EMR systems over 2026 to 2030.

Tiers in use. The OneHealthEHR platform is in use at clinics and health centers, as reported in September 2026.

Where data is digitised. The company said in September 2026 that clinicians, nurses and registration staff record patient information in the platform during routine care, offline, with synchronisation when a connection returns.

Last twelve months. The company says its platform went into live clinical use on 1 September 2026 at four facilities across Liberia and Ghana. The Ministry of Health, through its National Health Intelligence Center, launched enumerator training on 8 May 2026 for a nationwide EMR readiness assessment. UNFPA issued a request for quotation dated 27 October 2025 for an EMR at Careysburg and Duport Road Health Centers.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. By the company's account, the deployment grew from four to seven active clinics and health centers, counted across Liberia and Ghana together, within about three weeks of 1 September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 e-tracker, the DHIS2 Tracker for HIV client-level data and the DHIS2 Tracker of the Key Population program. Single-function: eIDSR, the Lab Information System, mSupply and eLMIS and the pharmacy system sLMIS. Community: the Community Based Information System (CBIS) and its electronic form, eCBIS.

Not held. The number of Liberian facilities live is not held: the count of seven covers Liberia and Ghana together. Whether the OpenMRS system launched at J.J. Dossen Hospital in 2020 still runs is not on record.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

1 Absent Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

The Ministry said in 2026 that its school data system is paper-based and a per-pupil record planned; whether any pilot has begun is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Education is drafting the EMIS Policy itself. Its policy dialogue of 1 to 5 September 2025 validated a costed three-year action plan whose priorities include a national unique learner and teacher ID. The plan is for USD 4.5 million, 60 per cent from the government and 40 per cent from development partners.

Levels in use. The African Union's peer review of 1 and 2 September 2025 found unique learner and teacher IDs absent, and describes a system that reaches about 1.5 million learners in 6,500 schools through a school census.

How the record is kept. At a consultation on 28 and 29 September 2026 the Ministry's planning director described the existing system as paper-based and called for a digitised one. In September 2026 the Ministry opened a five-day session to develop and validate a record-keeping booklet for school records on students, teachers and attendance.

Last twelve months. The Ministry held the two-day consultation of September 2026 to draft, review and validate an EMIS Policy for a real-time school data system, and said a study tour for its technicians was being arranged.

Primary schools doing the digital input. The Deputy Minister for Administration said at the close of the consultation that systems at schools feeding student and teacher attendance to a central dashboard are a goal not yet reached, and that the Ministry still relies largely on periodic data collection.

Noted, not assessed. The Annual School Census, which the Ministry calls its EMIS, with the Ministry's integrated data dashboard, planned school report cards and School Quality Assessment tool; the Ministry's Digitized Monitoring and Supervision System; WAEC exam data; Orange's Digital Schools and the Wologizi Computer Lab; education technology in public and community senior secondary schools; and Bridge Liberia's digital teacher guides and the Ministry's Teach by Radio.

Not held. Nothing shows staff trained, equipment delivered, an agreement signed or pilot schools chosen for a school-level system or the learner ID. The Ministry's paper statement names no level and no school count, and no budget line for the plan is shown.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

County teams registered births online in 2024, with deaths added to the platform; how many of the 58 centres enter them is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health registers and certifies births and deaths through its Bureau of Vital Statistics, by its report for 2024, and that year developed a National Birth and Death Registration Policy. UNICEF, the World Bank and other partners helped it decentralise birth registration, and the Ministry says budget constraint limits its ability to sustain the birth registration centres.

Events registered digitally. The Ministry's report for 2024 says birth and death registration have been integrated into the DHIS2 platform. As of 2023, 93,031 birth registrations had been recorded in DHIS2, which the Ministry gives as 67 per cent of all birth registrations and certifications. UNICEF Liberia calls the system the Birth Registration Information Management System.

How the record is made. County birth registration teams register and certify births online on a web-based platform, the Ministry reports for 2024, collecting application forms from health facilities and returning certificates to them. In May 2024 the World Bank found most services at the 15 County Service Centers, birth registration for ages 0 to 12 among them, remained paper-based.

Last twelve months. In 2024 the Ministry trained over 45 birth registration staff on the platform and procured 35 antivirus licences for county birth registration computers.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Ministry says birth registration centres have been established in all 15 counties since 2010. UNICEF Liberia in December 2024 counted birth registration services in all 15 counties and 58 centres.

Noted, not assessed. Identity: the National Biometric Identification System of the National Identification Registry, its Identity Query Portal and e-Verification platform and a UNICEF pilot enrolling children. Exchange: a proposed NID-CRVS system, a UNICEF-supported pilot integrating birth registration with the national ID system, a planned government service bus and an interface for data sharing between the identification system and the health ministry's birth and death records. Enabling: the County Service Centers. Certificate: the eLiberia Portal. Outreach: the Ministry's birth registration campaigns, UNICEF-supported mobile registration and certification campaigns and a planned national birth registration census for children under five. Notification: the Community Health Assistant program for the registration of community events and health facilities' Birth Notification.

Not held. How many of the 58 centres enter births on the platform is not known, and no source gives a total of local offices. Nothing is dated inside the last twelve months, and whether the 2024 policy names the platform is not stated.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Six officers in the capital's county began entering crime reports on tablets in a 2021 pilot; whether any station outside it does is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Liberia National Police was the first to roll out the case management system under the Ministry of Justice, in 2021. The UN Joint Rule of Law Programme supported its design, development and roll-out, and the crime statistics system was developed with UNDP support.

Records made digitally. UNDP reported in March 2023 an officer entering crime reports into a tablet as they come in, with suspects' biodata, photos and GPS locations. The case management system records the parties, the offences, the proceedings and the outcome of a case, and was up and running at the police in June 2022.

How the record is made. The Crime Statistics Information System, as UNDP names it, is online and in two parts: a smartphone or tablet on which the officer enters the report, and a portal that aggregates the reports for police decision-makers. Case data is captured on a web and mobile application that works online and offline, by UNDP's account of April 2022.

Last twelve months. FrontPageAfrica reported in 2026 that Nyehn Town Police Station, the only police facility serving Todee District, has no computers.

Stations doing the digital entry. The crime statistics system went live at police headquarters on 25 January 2021, with training for 15 personnel. Six officers were chosen to pioneer it in Montserrado County, the seat of the capital, that month.

Noted, not assessed. Crime statistics: 43 reporting officers had a refresher on the Crime Statistics Information System in Monrovia in August 2025, and UNDP states that a Crime Statistics Information Management System was rolled out nationwide in 2024. For the public: an online police clearance platform, launched on 1 May 2026; the police call centre and the 911 number, to be refurbished and reactivated under an agreement with Orange Liberia of 10 March 2026; and a cybercrime reporting channel, planned. Single-function: a National Cybercrime Management System, planned in the same strategy. Identification: a Digital Forensic Lab at the police training academy, not yet operating in March 2026. Enabling: a closed user group plan on the Orange network for calls and SMS among officers; internet service to police facilities from the Liberia Telecommunications Corporation, as the police stated on 21 August 2026; and VHF and UHF radio frequencies assigned in 2010.

Not held. No fact shows either system in use at a station outside Montserrado County, and no count or share of stations entering records is held; the newest facts on use are of 2021 to 2023. Nothing is held on who owns, plans or pays for the systems since 2022, or on any change in the last twelve months.
