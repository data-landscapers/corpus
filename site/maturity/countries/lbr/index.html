Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Liberia has a National Digital Strategy 2025-2029, adopted per a March 2026 policy, with a costed plan, but results against its targets are not known.

Governance and finance. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications developed the strategy and is responsible for implementing it, according to the National Data Governance Policy of March 2026, and the strategy's costs are to be met from government, multilateral donor and other sources.

Standing. The National Data Governance Policy of March 2026 lists the National Digital Strategy (2025-2029) among the instruments Liberia has adopted, after a validation workshop on 11 December 2024.

Text. The strategy's full text is published on the ministry's website.

Period. The strategy covers 2025 to 2029.

Delivery plan. In the text published on 3 February 2026, Chapter 6 estimates US$151.813 million over 2025-2029 from government, multilateral donor and other sources, and a separate US$776.250 million for about 1,500 cell towers through public-private partnership. Chapter 7 sets an implementation roadmap with timelines.

Reporting. The Digital Transformation Working Group, activated in October 2025, is to coordinate Liberia's digital activities.

Last twelve months. The strategy was officially launched at the National Coordination Session of Digital Liberia Week, held during 28-30 October 2025.

Noted, not assessed. West African Regional Digital Integration Project; National Financial Inclusion and Education Strategy; ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development; National Switch; Executive Order 163; Health Information System & ICT Strategic Plan; School Connect Program; e-Government Strategy (2014-2018).

Not held. No progress report against the strategy's targets is held. No adopting act or date is named for the strategy, and no budget line for it is named.

Legislation and regulation

Data protection legislation: general law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Liberia approved a data protection act in March 2026; it has no recorded decision, and whether the Commissioner has begun acting is not known.

Authority. The Act confers data protection functions on the Independent Information Commissioner, the oversight body created by the Freedom of Information Act of 2010.

Standing. The Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act of 2024 was approved on 9 March 2026 and printed on 23 March 2026. It takes effect on publication into handbills, and existing businesses have one year from the effective date to comply. President Boakai's promulgation of the law was announced on 19 August 2026.

Regulations. The Act requires implementing rules within 24 months of its taking effect.

Application. The Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection said on 13 September 2026 that it is aware of its responsibilities under the Act, and that it took part in validating the legal framework.

Last twelve months. The House of Representatives had referred the President's bill to committee on 10 June 2025. The Act was approved on 9 March 2026, and its signing was reported on 20 August 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Article 16 of the Constitution of Liberia; a policy to implement the ECOWAS Data Protection Act, being drafted in February 2025.

Not held. Any regulation issued under the Act, and any decision, sanction or count of decisions under it. Whether the Independent Information Commissioner has begun acting under the Act is not held.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cybercrime law in force since 2026; used in September 2026 to arrest a commentator over images of the Vice President.

Law in force. Liberia's Cybercrime Act of 2025 was signed into law by President Boakai in 2026, and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications has published the approved Act. One report says the Act requires regulations within six to twelve months, and another says the country has entered the enforcement phase. The Act is being operationalized by the Liberia Telecommunications Authority and the Ministry of Justice, and ten civil society organisations plan to engage lawmakers on amendments.

Use of the law. In September 2026 police arrested the commentator Jerry Nyantee Wrato over AI-generated images of the Vice President, then charging him and forwarding him to court. The Inspector General's letter of 21 September resolved to prosecute him under sections 10.1, 15.1, 18.1 and 19.1 of the Act, covering misuse of devices, cyberstalking, incitement and harmful data messages. A court ordered him released on habeas corpus on 22 September. The arrest was presented as the Act's first major public test.

Noted, not assessed. The Liberia National Cybersecurity Center (LNCC) and the Liberia Cybersecurity Emergency Response Team (LCERT).

Not held. No count of cases investigated, prosecuted or decided under the law, for a period ending in the last two years, is held.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

4 Operating Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Liberia's 2011 National Identification Registry Act sets up a biometric identification system. No rules made under it since 2023 are known.

Law in force. Liberia's National Identification Registry Act was approved by the President on 1 August 2011 and published on 8 August 2011. It repeals a 1983 decree that had established the earlier identification system and sets up the National Identification Registry as the body in charge. The Act took effect on publication.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The Act sets up a National Biometric Identification System and a biometric card for each person, enrolled with a fingerprint and a photograph. The Registry the Act creates is the body that runs that system. The Act is therefore a law for a biometric identity from its first day, and no later amendment to it is on record. No regulation or decree made under the law since September 2023 is on record.

Noted, not assessed. National Biometric Identification System (NBIS).

Not held. No regulation or decree applying the law, and no rule made under it that others must check identity against the system, dated in the last three years, is held.

Legislation enabling data interoperability: data sharing law

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An executive order of April 2026 mandates interoperable government systems, with legislation due within a year; no law is in force.

Law or decree in force. Liberia has an executive order and no law. Executive Order No. 163, signed by President Boakai on 22 April 2026, creates a national digitalisation and modernisation initiative and an office of technology, digitalisation and innovation. Draft legislation to make the arrangement permanent is due within twelve months of the order.

Sharing required and the law applied. The order mandates interoperable digital systems across ministries, agencies and commissions and introduces a government interoperability framework. With no law in force, nothing has been made under one and no ministry is named as exchanging data because a law requires it.

E-commerce legislation: e-commerce law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Liberia's 2002 Electronic Transactions Law gives electronic contracts and signatures legal effect, but no regulation under it is known.

Law in force. Liberia's Electronic Transactions Law, Chapter 13 of Title 14 of the General Business Law, was approved on 8 January 2002 and published on 19 June 2002. It took effect on publication in handbills. The law gives electronic records, contracts and signatures legal effect, so a contract concluded electronically is recognised in Liberian law. It is a chapter of the General Business Law rather than a stand-alone act, and it dates from 2002.

Rules made under the law and the law applied. The law applies at once to matters under Titles 5 and 21 of the General Business Law. Anywhere else it applies only as regulations of the Minister of Foreign Affairs bring it into effect. That makes the Minister's regulations the route by which the law reaches the rest of the commercial code, and the nearest held fact on rules made under it is this delegation, not a regulation itself.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No source held names a decree, regulation or order made under the law.

They show the Act cited to respondents twenty years after it was approved. A survey carried out is not counted as the law applied, and the entries name no regulation.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All facilities report monthly into the national system (2024), with district and county clerks entering the figures from paper forms (2022).

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's report for 2024 states that its HMIS runs on DHIS2 and is managed within its Department of Policy, Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation. The ministry's Data Quality Improvement Plan of June 2022 runs 2022 to 2026 and is costed at US$7,312,887.50. The memorandum signed with the United States on 9 December 2025 plans US$1,338,372 of US funding for HMIS/DHIS2 over 2026 to 2030.

Tiers in use. Every health facility, public and private, is required to report service data monthly, and counties submit to the central ministry by the 17th of the following month, on the ministry's account for 2024.

Where data is digitised. Evidence Action's survey found that all 48 facilities surveyed in Montserrado, Margibi and Grand Bassa filled paper HMIS forms monthly, which district or county data clerks entered into DHIS2, in records for May to July 2022. The Liberian team's Countdown to 2030 synthesis of June 2022 says facilities' monthly reports are normally collected by the County Health Team for entry into DHIS2.

Last twelve months. WHO reports that in 2025, with Gavi support, it delivered 31 laptops with statistical software for data analysis in the health management information and surveillance systems.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The Countdown to 2030 synthesis counts 856 functional health facilities submitting reports through DHIS2, of 964 on the Master Facility List, for data of January 2017 to December 2021.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 e-tracker, the DHIS2 Tracker for HIV client-level data and the DHIS2 Tracker of the Key Population program. Single-function: eIDSR, the Lab Information System, mSupply and eLMIS and the pharmacy system sLMIS. Community: the Community Based Information System (CBIS) and its electronic form, eCBIS.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A vendor's system runs at a handful of pilot clinics and health centres (September 2026); the exact number is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. OneHealthEHR Inc. deploys and runs its platform with a consortium of European firms and research bodies, with the National Public Health Institute of Liberia as surveillance partner, as reported in September 2026. In the memorandum signed with the United States on 9 December 2025, Liberia commits to an open-source EMR in clinics, health centers and hospitals, with targets of 20% of all public health facilities in 2026 and 100% in 2030, and the United States plans US$2,676,744 for EMR systems over 2026 to 2030.

Tiers in use. The OneHealthEHR platform is in use at clinics and health centers, as reported in September 2026.

Where data is digitised. The company said in September 2026 that clinicians, nurses and registration staff record patient information in the platform during routine care, offline, with synchronisation when a connection returns.

Last twelve months. The company says its platform went into live clinical use on 1 September 2026 at four facilities across Liberia and Ghana. The Ministry of Health, through its National Health Intelligence Center, launched enumerator training on 8 May 2026 for a nationwide EMR readiness assessment. UNFPA issued a request for quotation dated 27 October 2025 for an EMR at Careysburg and Duport Road Health Centers.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. By the company's account, the deployment grew from four to seven active clinics and health centers, counted across Liberia and Ghana together, within about three weeks of 1 September 2026.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the DHIS2 e-tracker, the DHIS2 Tracker for HIV client-level data and the DHIS2 Tracker of the Key Population program. Single-function: eIDSR, the Lab Information System, mSupply and eLMIS and the pharmacy system sLMIS. Community: the Community Based Information System (CBIS) and its electronic form, eCBIS.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health registers and certifies births and deaths through its Bureau of Vital Statistics, by its report for 2024, and that year developed a National Birth and Death Registration Policy. UNICEF, the World Bank and other partners helped it decentralise birth registration, and the Ministry says budget constraint limits its ability to sustain the birth registration centres.

Events registered digitally. The Ministry's report for 2024 says birth and death registration have been integrated into the DHIS2 platform. As of 2023, 93,031 birth registrations had been recorded in DHIS2, which the Ministry gives as 67 per cent of all birth registrations and certifications. UNICEF Liberia calls the system the Birth Registration Information Management System.

How the record is made. County birth registration teams register and certify births online on a web-based platform, the Ministry reports for 2024, collecting application forms from health facilities and returning certificates to them. In May 2024 the World Bank found most services at the 15 County Service Centers, birth registration for ages 0 to 12 among them, remained paper-based.

Last twelve months. In 2024 the Ministry trained over 45 birth registration staff on the platform and procured 35 antivirus licences for county birth registration computers.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Six officers in the capital's county began entering crime reports on tablets in a 2021 pilot; whether any station outside it does is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Liberia National Police was the first to roll out the case management system under the Ministry of Justice, in 2021. The UN Joint Rule of Law Programme supported its design, development and roll-out, and the crime statistics system was developed with UNDP support.

Records made digitally. UNDP reported in March 2023 an officer entering crime reports into a tablet as they come in, with suspects' biodata, photos and GPS locations. The case management system records the parties, the offences, the proceedings and the outcome of a case, and was up and running at the police in June 2022.

How the record is made. The Crime Statistics Information System, as UNDP names it, is online and in two parts: a smartphone or tablet on which the officer enters the report, and a portal that aggregates the reports for police decision-makers. Case data is captured on a web and mobile application that works online and offline, by UNDP's account of April 2022.

Last twelve months. FrontPageAfrica reported in 2026 that Nyehn Town Police Station, the only police facility serving Todee District, has no computers.

Stations doing the digital entry. The crime statistics system went live at police headquarters on 25 January 2021, with training for 15 personnel. Six officers were chosen to pioneer it in Montserrado County, the seat of the capital, that month.

Noted, not assessed.
