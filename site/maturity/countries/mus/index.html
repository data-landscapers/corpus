Digitalisation of sub-national government

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All 12 municipal and district councils worked online in a shared ministry system in 2024; how their offices are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Local Government's report for financial year 2023-2024 says the system its councils work in was built in house by a team of the District Council of Moka, extended to the other local authorities by Government decision of 16 March 2020, and is run with a Digital Office at the ministry.

What the office does over it. The same report says all employees of the twelve local authority digital offices do their daily tasks in real time in what the ministry calls the Council Project Management system, and that the local authorities processed 24,393 Building and Land Use Permit applications in the online National Electronic Licensing System in the year to 30 June 2024.

How the office is connected. The 2023-2024 report says the ministry's Digital Office works in real time with the twelve digital offices, whose employees can also work remotely.

Last twelve months. The Prime Minister told the National Assembly on 29 July 2025 that the back end of the Citizen Support Portal connects 583 ministries, departments, local authorities, parastatal bodies and some state-owned companies.

Local governments connected. The ministry's 2023-2024 report says its I-Council project, piloted at Moka from 27 August 2018, took three years to implement in all local authorities, and that 12 of the 12 it counts, five municipal councils and seven district councils, work in the system. Its permit table for the year to 30 June 2025 lists all 12.

Noted, not assessed. Application: the Council Project Management system, the National Electronic Licensing System and the Citizen Support Portal. Web: the planned Mobile-ID. Public access: the 99 Digital Services Centres in post offices. City systems: the police's Safe City system. Backbone: the Government Online Centre and the Government Intranet Network Systems, the GOC Data Centre, the Government Wide Area Network, InfoHighway, the Government Data Centre to be established and Mauritius Telecom's data centre at Rose-Belle. Central: the Finance Ministry's Electronic Management System, the one-stop public service portal and e-GP, the Digital cadastral database and LAVIMS and the Central Business Registration Database and CBRIS.

Not held. How the council offices are connected, who provides and pays for the connection, and where the shared system is hosted. No plan, finance or rural share is stated, and nothing is dated in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In January 2022 the national system was used only at regional and national level, with entry by clinics still planned; nothing more recent is held.

Governance, planning, finance. DHIS2's own country story of January 2022 states that the government, through the Ministry of Health and Wellness, has begun implementing DHIS2 as the national health information system, the work done by the health ministry and the Ministry of Information Technology, Communication and Innovation with support from WHO, Gavi, the Global Fund, HISP UiO and HISP Uganda. The implementation is carried out under the Health Sector Strategic Plan 2020-2024.

Tiers in use. In January 2022 DHIS2 was in use at regional and national level, on the same account.

Where data is digitised. WHO's SCORE assessment, on data from 2013 to 2018, rates a standardised system of electronic data entry for aggregate reporting at district or comparable level as 'not there', and completeness of reporting above 75% for public primary care facilities, public hospitals and private facilities. The ministry's strategic plan of 2020 states that the health information system in Rodrigues is largely manual.

Last twelve months. Decentralising DHIS2 further will need more training, staff and IT infrastructure, the story of January 2022 states.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. In January 2022 the health authorities planned to decentralise DHIS2 to district and primary health care level, and to Rodrigues and Agalega.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Electronic Immunization Registry and AEFI Tracker programmes on DHIS2. Single-function: Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response on DHIS2, the laboratory system OpenELIS, the laboratory information management system of e-Health phase 1, VigiFlow, the national human resources health information system (HRHIS), DHIS2 for case detection and surveillance of notifiable diseases, the Laboratory Management and Information System (LMIS) and the paper-based inventory management system for medicines in Rodrigues.

Not held. Nothing on the HMIS dated after January 2022 is held, so whether the planned decentralisation to district and primary level has happened is not established. Nor is which tiers use DHIS2 routinely, or whether it carries routine reporting of patients treated.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: EMR

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

An electronic health system runs at one regional hospital and several primary care sites in its region (May 2026); how many sites is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. UNDP's case study of February 2025 states that UNDP Mauritius ran the procurement that selected TrioTree Technologies and its consortium for the project it names One Patient, One Record, and that the project has been allocated MUR 600 million, divided into phases. allAfrica reported on 8 January 2026 that Digital Health Company Ltd is a public company under the Ministry of Health and Wellness.

Tiers in use. L'Express reported in May 2026 the health minister saying that the system, which he calls e-Health, is operational at New Flacq Hospital, where testing began on 23 August 2025, and in several primary health care structures of the same region.

Where data is digitised. The system covers the full outpatient cycle from registration to follow-up, with nursing, doctor and pharmacy modules added to phase 1, the minister said. By 30 April 2026 it had recorded more than 126,000 patient registrations, 225,000 visits and 221,000 medical consultations.

Last twelve months. L'Express reported in May 2026 the minister telling Parliament that deployment is scheduled at Jawaharlal Nehru Hospital by September 2026, Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam National Hospital in March 2027 and Dr A.G. Jeetoo and Victoria hospitals no earlier than December 2027.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. The primary structures using the system in May 2026 are in the New Flacq region. The ministry's Health Sector Strategic Plan 2020-2024 gave the share of all health facilities using EMR as 0 in 2019, against a target of 100% by 2024.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Electronic Immunization Registry and AEFI Tracker programmes on DHIS2. Single-function: Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response on DHIS2, the laboratory system OpenELIS, the laboratory information management system of e-Health phase 1, VigiFlow, the national human resources health information system (HRHIS), DHIS2 for case detection and surveillance of notifiable diseases, the Laboratory Management and Information System (LMIS) and the paper-based inventory management system for medicines in Rodrigues.

Not held. The primary structures using the system are neither named nor counted, so no share or count of primary clinics is established. Who pays the MUR 600 million, a current plan naming the system, and whether a record can be retrieved at another facility are not stated.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools: EMIS

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

A per-pupil school record system was being designed in 2019; whether it was built or any school uses it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. UNESCO IITE reported after a mission of 11-15 February 2019 that the system is the Ministry of Education's to establish, coordinated by its Director of E-Education with UNESCO technical support, and that the needs assessment and design reports were to support including its resource needs in the 2019/2020 budget.

Levels in use. In February 2019 the system was planned for national, zone and school levels, from pre-primary to tertiary.

How the record is kept. The 2019 mission set out to design a school integrated management information system that is to track each student's progress, including classroom assessment.

Last twelve months. The education ministry's indicative procurement plan for financial year 2026-2027, dated 17 August 2026, names an EMIS.

Primary schools doing the digital input. In February 2019 the system was still to be developed, over twelve months following a three-month technical document.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. Nothing dated since February 2019 says whether the system was built, piloted or used in any school, at which level, or how pupils' records are kept. No ownership, plan or financing dated within the last three years is held.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices: civil registration

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Births and deaths are registered online at computerised civil status offices, 35 listed in 2019; the share of all offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The CSD System is the Civil Status Division's own, under the Prime Minister's Office and headed by the Registrar of Civil Status. The Division spent Rs 387,863,137 against government budget estimates of Rs 445,600,000 in 2023/2024, with no donor named. Its Strategic Directions 2023/2024 name the revamping of the CSD System.

Events registered digitally. The Division's report for 2023/2024 states that the CSD System, operational since November 2001, provides for the registration of births, deaths and marriages. Offices opened on 6 and 7 September 2025 for the registration of births and deaths during the system's revamp.

How the record is made. The Registrar stated in June 2019 that civil events have been registered online since November 2001 at computerised civil status offices, a national identity number being generated on each birth registered. The record is stored in the Division's single Civil Status Database, the Division stated in 2024.

Last twelve months. The Civil Status Division announced on 2 September 2025 that its CSD System was being revamped, with a transition period in which delays might be met.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The Registrar listed 35 Civil Status Offices by district, two of them in Rodrigues, in June 2019, and described them as computerised.

Noted, not assessed. Archive: the digitalisation of the civil status records. Notification: the Notification of Birth and the link from the health ministry and private clinics to the Civil Status Division. Certificate: MoKloud, the online application for civil status extracts, the SuperApp digital wallet and Smart Credentials. Identity: MauPass, the Mauritius National Identity Card system, MNIC 3.0, the MNIC Central Database, Mobile ID, the identity register, the MNIS Certification Authority System and the MauSign Certification Authority System. Enabling: none held. Exchange: the Central Population Database, InfoHighway, the Registrar's electronic system for sharing information, the Mauritius Revenue Authority's electronic access to the Civil Status Database, the Central KYC system and the sharing of line lists of births registered with the immunisation programme.

Not held. No share of offices making the digital entry is held: the 35 computerised offices of 2019 are a count with no total, and the communique of September 2025 gives no count. No current plan naming the system and nothing dated inside the last twelve months is held.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All police stations had computers linked to the headquarters complaint and case system in 2025; how many stations enter records in it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Crime Occurrence Tracking System is the police's own, answered for by the Commissioner of Police through the Prime Minister as Minister of Home Affairs, with the government's Central Informatics Bureau working with the police on its replacement, as the National Assembly heard on 17 June 2025.

Records made digitally. The Prime Minister told the Assembly on 17 June 2025 that the system manages and tracks complaints and declarations from initial reporting to final case disposal, and has been operational since 2011. Défi Media reported in March 2023 that statements made at police stations, once handwritten in Occurrence Books, have been entered in it since 2015.

How the record is made. The system is a digital platform connecting all police stations to the main server at Police Headquarters, used on personal computers in the stations (June 2025). The entries are made directly on computers linked to a server (March 2023).

Last twelve months. On 17 June 2025 the Prime Minister said the system had been enhanced but never upgraded since 2011, and that the police had begun to replace it with a new case management system, with a special consultant to be retained.

Stations doing the digital entry. The Prime Minister said on 17 June 2025 that all police stations are equipped with personal computers for the system, which Défi Media's account of his reply also carries.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the Criminal Attribute Database, the Automatic Fingerprint Identification System under procurement, an Automated Biometric Identification System and the Forensic Science Laboratory building, a Cyber Forensic laboratory to be set up, and the National Flagging Database and Digital Forensic Laboratory proposed in the National Crime Agency Bill. Public: the police hotlines 999 and 112, Crime Stoppers 148 and the complaint line 800 2345, the L'Espwar hotline, the Sekirite app, and the online application for a Certificate of Character and the Panic Button. Single-function: the digitalisation of the Fixed Penalty Notice and the Integrated Driving License Management System, a planned database on violence against women, children and elderly persons, and the Photographic Enforcement Device and Point of Sales machines in stations. Statistics: the crime map of station areas and the Crime Records Office's offence statistics. Surveillance: the Safe City system and the coast guard's CSRS/AIS stations, the leased CCTV system, the Coastal Surveillance Radar and the PertSol interception system. Enabling: Smart Radio and the public asset upgrade programme, which covers police stations.

Not held. The number of police stations and the share of them entering records in the system: the one figure is of stations equipped. No plan or finance for the system is stated, and nothing is dated in the twelve months to 30 September 2026.
