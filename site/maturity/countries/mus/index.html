Data protection legislation: general law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritius has the Data Protection Act 2017, and its office reported complaints closed and transfers decided in 2024; figures for 2025 are not known.

Authority. The Data Protection Office has been operational since 16 February 2009 and enforces the Data Protection Act 2017. Drudeisha Madhub is the Data Protection Commissioner.

Standing. Mauritius adopted its current data protection regime in 2017, after a first law in 2004 that was partly implemented in 2009. The Office reports on its enforcement of the Data Protection Act 2017 during 2024.

Regulations. Controllers and processors register under the Data Protection (Fees) Regulations 2020. Registrations since 1 August 2020 reached 21,219 controllers and 1,157 processors by 31 December 2024.

Application. For January to December 2024 the Office reports 179 new complaints, 39 complaints closed, 105 personal data breaches notified, 112 cross-border transfers authorised and 12 rejected, and four compliance inspections. The Commissioner said in May 2026 that her office issues 3,000 enforcement actions a year, 99 per cent of them resolved without prosecution.

Last twelve months. The Minister made regulations on 17 June 2026 requiring every controller to designate a data protection officer, to come into operation on 1 January 2027. The Commissioner said in May 2026 that Mauritius is going for the EU adequacy assessment in 2026, and amendments to bring in administrative fines are being introduced. In July 2026 the ICT Minister spoke of forthcoming amendments to the Act.

Noted, not assessed. None held.

Not held. No figures for 2025 are held. The draft bill to amend the Act for EU adequacy carries no date beyond 2024, and the 2026 regulations are not yet in operation.

Legislation covering digital id: national identity law

5 Leading Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Mauritius's 1985 identity card law is in force; 2024 regulations under it set a new card with a digital seal and fingerprints.

Law in force. Mauritius's identity card rests on the National Identity Card Act, No. 60 of 1985, which the identity card unit lists as the law behind the card system. The Act is an Act of Parliament, and regulations made under its section 10 came into operation on 19 February 2024, when the new card began to be issued.

Digital identity covered and rules made. The National Identity Card Regulations 2024 were made by the minister on 16 February 2024. They set the new identity card, its digital seal and an application made with fingerprints, and they revoke the regulations of 2013. Through them the 1985 Act now provides for a card that is biometric and can be checked electronically. The new card has been issued since 19 February 2024.

Noted, not assessed. Mauritius National Identity Card (MNIC 3.0) / Mauritius National Identity Scheme; MauPass; MauSign; Mobile ID; MNIC 3.0 new identity card and MNIC System.

Births and deaths are registered online at computerised civil status offices, 35 listed in 2019; the share of all offices is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The CSD System is the Civil Status Division's own, under the Prime Minister's Office and headed by the Registrar of Civil Status. The Division spent Rs 387,863,137 against government budget estimates of Rs 445,600,000 in 2023/2024, with no donor named. Its Strategic Directions 2023/2024 name the revamping of the CSD System.

Events registered digitally. The Division's report for 2023/2024 states that the CSD System, operational since November 2001, provides for the registration of births, deaths and marriages. Offices opened on 6 and 7 September 2025 for the registration of births and deaths during the system's revamp.

How the record is made. The Registrar stated in June 2019 that civil events have been registered online since November 2001 at computerised civil status offices, a national identity number being generated on each birth registered. The record is stored in the Division's single Civil Status Database, the Division stated in 2024.

Digitalisation of rural police stations: station records

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

All police stations had computers linked to the headquarters complaint and case system in 2025; how many stations enter records in it is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The Crime Occurrence Tracking System is the police's own, answered for by the Commissioner of Police through the Prime Minister as Minister of Home Affairs, with the government's Central Informatics Bureau working with the police on its replacement, as the National Assembly heard on 17 June 2025.

Records made digitally. The Prime Minister told the Assembly on 17 June 2025 that the system manages and tracks complaints and declarations from initial reporting to final case disposal, and has been operational since 2011. Défi Media reported in March 2023 that statements made at police stations, once handwritten in Occurrence Books, have been entered in it since 2015.

How the record is made. The system is a digital platform connecting all police stations to the main server at Police Headquarters, used on personal computers in the stations (June 2025). The entries are made directly on computers linked to a server (March 2023).

Last twelve months. On 17 June 2025 the Prime Minister said the system had been enhanced but never upgraded since 2011, and that the police had begun to replace it with a new case management system, with a special consultant to be retained.

Stations doing the digital entry. The Prime Minister said on 17 June 2025 that all police stations are equipped with personal computers for the system, which Défi Media's account of his reply also carries.

Noted, not assessed. Identification: the Criminal Attribute Database, the Automatic Fingerprint Identification System under procurement, an Automated Biometric Identification System and the Forensic Science Laboratory building, a Cyber Forensic laboratory to be set up, and the National Flagging Database and Digital Forensic Laboratory proposed in the National Crime Agency Bill. Public: the police hotlines 999 and 112, Crime Stoppers 148 and the complaint line 800 2345, the L'Espwar hotline, the Sekirite app, and the online application for a Certificate of Character and the Panic Button. Single-function: the digitalisation of the Fixed Penalty Notice and the Integrated Driving License Management System, a planned database on violence against women, children and elderly persons, and the Photographic Enforcement Device and Point of Sales machines in stations. Statistics: the crime map of station areas and the Crime Records Office's offence statistics. Surveillance: the Safe City system and the coast guard's CSRS/AIS stations, the leased CCTV system, the Coastal Surveillance Radar and the PertSol interception system.
