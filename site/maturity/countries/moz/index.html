Digital transformation strategy: national strategy

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2019 Mozambique approved a national information society plan running to 2028, but its delivery plan and published results are not known.

Governance and finance. Decreto n.º 4/2026, approved on 10 March 2026, creates the Agência de Transformação Digital e Inovação, whose Technical Council is to ensure execution and permanent monitoring of the plan's projects. Paradigm Initiative says execution depends heavily on donor cycles, with World Bank projects financing broadband and governance work.

Standing. The Council of Ministers approved the Plano Estratégico para a Sociedade de Informação 2019-2028 and its Plano Operacional by Resolução n.º 52/2019, on 26 February 2019. A National Digital Transformation Strategy entered drafting when the process was launched on 12 February 2026.

Text. The text is printed in the Boletim da República I Série n.º 199 of 16 October 2019, hosted on the INTIC site.

Period. The plan sets its priorities for the horizon 2019-2028.

Delivery plan. The resolution approved the plan together with its Plano Operacional.

Reporting. INTIC's 2022 activity report has a section on evaluating the degree of implementation of the plan.

Last twelve months. Decreto n.º 4/2026 of 10 March 2026 tasks the new agency with mobilising resources for the Information Society Policy and its Strategic and Operational Plans.

Noted, not assessed. Government's 2025-2029 Five-Year Program; Plano Operacional de Transformação Digital; National roadmap to integrate public digital systems; National Development Strategy 2025-2044; Public sector digital transformation strategy; Plano Quinquenal do Governo 2020-2024; Estratégia de Governo Digital; Data Center Regulation (Decree 71/2025).

Not held. No delivery plan is stated for this plan in the evidence, and no published report against its targets is held. The National Digital Transformation Strategy is still in drafting and would replace it.

Mozambique has no data protection law; a bill was still being prepared in August 2026, and when it goes to parliament is not known.

Authority. No data protection authority exists. The Data Protection Office was not yet established in October 2025, and a June 2025 report found no data regulatory authority. The draft law reviewed in September 2025 gives the role to the existing Regulatory Authority for ICT.

Standing. Mozambique has a draft law and no law. The minister for communications and digital transformation said on 22 July 2026 that a personal data protection law was still in preparation, and the president of INTIC said on 25 August 2026 that the authorities were preparing to submit the draft to parliament.

Regulations. A June 2025 inception report says Mozambique has drafted a Data Protection Bill and has not enacted a comprehensive data protection law.

Application. A World Bank and African Development Bank diagnostic, with evidence to spring 2025, recommends that the government adopt a data protection law.

Cybersecurity legislation: cybercrime and cybersecurity law

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

Cybersecurity and cybercrime laws were signed in 2026. No use of the law is known.

Law in force. President Chapo promulgated the Cybersecurity Law in June 2026, protecting networks, information systems and critical infrastructure, with a regulatory body and penalties up to 160 minimum wages. Africa AI News states that Mozambique already has a Cyber Security Act and a Cybercrimes Act as part of its legal foundation. The Penal Code (Law 24/2019, amended by Law 17/2020) also provides cybercrimes such as database intrusion, punishable by up to two years, and electronic fraud, punishable by one to five years.

Use of the law. On 28 July 2026 the communications minister said the government has approved the Cybersecurity Law and the Cybercrime Law and that implementation is mandatory for companies operating critical infrastructure. In April 2026 Parliament unanimously approved the Cyber Security Law, making INCM technical coordinator for cyberspace security and creating a Cyber Security Fund, a proposal the government had submitted the previous Thursday.

ICT infrastructure for local government: office connection

2 Preparing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

In 2026 the government network reached public institutions in 141 districts, with expansion planned; how many local government offices are connected is not known.

Governance, planning, finance. The European Union's action document of June 2026 describes GovNet as the government's own network, with INAGE, the national e-government institute, managing e-government platforms and data centres. Its 2026 action plans to expand GovNet in selected districts, in support of the national Internet for All 2030 strategy, at a total estimated cost of EUR 7.1 million, of which EUR 5 million is the EU budget contribution.

What the office does over it. Local administrations, with schools, health and justice facilities, are still hardly providing digital public services, so citizens rely on in-person procedures, the document of June 2026 states.

How the office is connected. On the same document's account of June 2026, GovNet has connected about 1,000 public institutions and organs at central level and across 141 districts, and underinvestment in middle- and last-mile infrastructure leaves many local administrations insufficiently connected.

Last twelve months. The EU's annual action plan for Mozambique for 2026, dated 17 June 2026, carries a EUR 7.1 million Team Europe action to expand GovNet in selected districts.

Local governments connected. As at June 2026 the EU document gives GovNet's reach as about 1,000 public institutions across the central level and 141 districts, and says many local administrations remain insufficiently connected. In February 2020 the municipal component of the state finance system was being piloted in Maputo, Matola and Boane.

Rural digital data capture

Digitalisation of rural health clinics: HMIS

3 Establishing Last assessed Sep 2026 Stage last moved First assessment Methodology · On the map

District health offices entered facilities' monthly paper forms into the national system in 2019; nothing more recent is held.

Governance, planning, finance. The Ministry of Health's own page states that its Departamento de Informação para Saúde manages SIS-MA, the ministry's aggregate health information system. The ministry's Plano de Melhoria de Qualidade de Dados 2025-2029, dated January 2025, is a national plan for the quality of data in SIS-MA.

Tiers in use. A 2019 assessment by the University of Oslo, Eduardo Mondlane University and the ministry found that SISMA's users, services and health facilities were registered and configured centrally at the ministry, with support passing from district and provincial statisticians to its technical teams.

Where data is digitised. The same assessment, covering 14 districts, found that facilities' monthly data reached the district health office on paper forms and were entered into SISMA there. A study led with the ministry found in 2016-17 that public primary care facilities submitted monthly paper forms to the district management office, where the data were entered.

Last twelve months. The data quality plan of January 2025 followed the ministry's 2022-23 assessment of 175 facilities in all 11 provinces, which found that only 9% of records matched exactly between facility registers and SIS-MA.

Primary clinics doing the digital input. None of the 16 randomly selected public primary care facilities visited in four districts had electronic data collection in place in 2016-17, the study led with the ministry reports.

Noted, not assessed. Trackers: the Electronic Patient Tracking System (SESP), MGDH-Birth and MozART. Single-function: Ferramenta Central, the SIS-RME pharmacy module, iDMED, the Laboratory Information System (SIS-LAB), DISA, the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response Platform (eVIDR), e-IDSR and BES and nSIMAM. Community: upSCALE.

Governance, planning, finance. The health memorandum signed with the United States on 15 December 2025 states that the Integrated Electronic Medical Record System (SIS-RME), as it names it, is owned and operated by the Government of Mozambique, and names it the core product of the national digital health platform. Tiers in use. Jembi Health Systems states that in December 2024 Mavalane General Hospital in Maputo became Mozambique's first digital hospital, its Hospital Health Information System (SIS-H) digitising admissions and inpatient care and replacing paper records.

Governance, planning, finance. The National Directorate of Registries and Notaries holds the main database of the system it calls e-RCEV. A ten-year plan, SIRCEV 2019-2029, targets 80 per cent birth registration of children under five, the National Director said in 2025. Cooperation partners support 100 new posts in maternities, mainly with computer equipment, reported in September 2026.

Events registered digitally. The registrar's portal, read in August 2026, gives the registration of births and deaths as the system's initial focus. UNICEF's evaluators state that births and deaths were registered electronically as of October 2018. The National Director listed registering deaths in the community as a current challenge in 2025.

How the record is made. District registration offices connect to the central platform and enter registrations directly into the central database, UNICEF's evaluators stated in 2021. At 534 community registration points births and deaths are taken on forms that the district office then processes.

Last twelve months. Jornal Notícias reported on 24 September 2026 that the justice sector is installing 100 civil registration posts in health units with maternity wards, beside about 250 birth registration posts already working, about 25 per cent of some 1,000 health units.

Local offices doing the digital entry. The National Director of Registries and Notaries said in 2025 that all 68 civil registration conservatories are digitised. By 2018 the system was functional in 75 districts and 183 administrative posts, UNICEF's evaluators state.
