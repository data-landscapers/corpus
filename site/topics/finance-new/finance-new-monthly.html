Angola

Tertiary Education, Science and Technology Project (TEST)

Sete instituições do ensino superior recebem mais 20 milhões de dólares — ANGOP — Agência Angola Press, 6 September 2026

Angola's higher education ministry signed results-based agreements on 4 September 2026 with seven universities and institutes, sharing US$20,470,284 in the second funding round of the Tertiary Education, Science and Technology Project (TEST). Recipients include the Agostinho Neto, Katyavala Bwila and Mandume ya Ndemufayo universities. TEST is financed by a US$150m World Bank loan and a US$50m Global Partnership for Education grant.

Angola Tertiary Education, Science, and Technology Project (TEST) — Project Appraisal Document — World Bank (mirrored by Angola's public consultation portal), 15 November 2023

The World Bank's appraisal document for Angola's Tertiary Education, Science and Technology Project describes a US$150m IBRD loan and a US$50m Global Partnership for Education grant, approved in December 2023 as the first phase of a US$550m programme. The project became effective in December 2024 and runs to 2028.

38,7 milhões USD para financiar cursos alinhados à economia — Expansão, 1 October 2026

Angola's higher education ministry has awarded USD 38.7 million under results-based agreements to 13 public universities and institutes to expand science, technology and engineering courses, under a project co-financed by the World Bank and GPE. Only USD 710,000 had been paid out when this was reported on 1 October 2026.

US EXIM loan to Africell for network technology

EXIM Bank dos EUA anuncia empréstimo de 99,6 milhões de dólares à Africell para investimento em tecnologia em Angola — Novo Jornal, 11 September 2026

The Export-Import Bank of the United States announced a US$99.6m loan to the mobile operator Africell for network technology in Angola. Its statement says the loan will pay for American and European equipment and help Angola diversify its technology sources and keep national control of its infrastructure. No approval date or terms are given.

Digital acceleration project

IDEA Half-Year Implementation Progress Report: January-June 2026 and Outlook for July-December 2026 — COMESA Secretariat (IDEA project), 1 October 2026 DRCMalawiZambiaEswatiniEast AfricaSouthern Africa

COMESA's half-year progress report on the World Bank-financed Inclusive Digitalisation in Eastern and Southern Africa programme, a US$2.48 billion envelope, covers January to June 2026 in Angola, the DRC, Malawi, Zambia and Eswatini. The COMESA secretariat had spent 9% of its US$3.71 million 2026 budget by June, and moved its AI and regional digital inclusion consultancies from procurement to implementation.

Cameroon

EU financing for Digital Business Boost for Africa and Digital Acceleration in Cameroon

Cameroon, EU Shape New SME Agenda Around Finance and Digitalization — Business in Cameroon, 11 September 2026 Central Africa

Cameroon's SME minister, Achille Bassilekin III, and the new EU ambassador agreed in Yaoundé on 11 September 2026 to focus cooperation on finance, digitalisation and value chains for small businesses. Financing agreements signed on 16 June 2026 total about CFAF37.16bn, including CFAF9.83bn each for Digital Business Boost for Africa and Digital Acceleration in Cameroon. The EU says MyBusiness.cm has helped formalise more than 25,000 businesses.

Cape Verde

Digital Cabo Verde Project

UGPE — Digital Cabo Verde project mission review: 80%/40% disbursement, school connectivity priority — UGPE (Unidade de Gestão de Projetos Especiais), Cabo Verde, 24 September 2026

A World Bank mission reviewed the Digital Cabo Verde project between 22 and 26 September, reporting about 80% disbursement of the original US$20 million financing (2020-2025) and about 40% of the US$20 million additional financing (2025-2028). School connectivity was named the priority for the additional financing, with stronger coordination across ministries and a clearer role for the DGTED.

Central African Republic

Central Africa digital integration programme component

Centrafrique : connectivité, services publics, données – les chantiers du CARDIP de la Banque mondiale — Agence Ecofin, 15 September 2026

The country's component of the Central Africa digital integration programme is to launch in April 2027, after a week of work in Bangui by a financier mission covering connectivity, public services, data and cybersecurity.

Chad

Projet d'appui a la transformation numerique

Le Tchad présente à la Banque mondiale ses exigences pour réussir la transformation numérique — Journal du Tchad, 16 September 2026

Chad's telecoms minister Haliki Choua Mahamat told the World Bank's country representative that its digital transformation support project has implementation shortcomings, and asked that the 500 sites it is to build be placed in areas with no network. He said six provinces lack fibre and a further 8,000km is needed, and asked for AI laboratories at the national ICT school.

Congo

National data centre financing

Congo/Infrastructures : le data center opérationnel d'ici fin 2026 — Agence Congolaise d'Information, 24 September 2026

The Republic of Congo's vice-prime minister, Jean-Jacques Bouya, said on 22 September 2026 that the African Development Bank-funded national data centre at Bacongo, Brazzaville, will be operational by the end of 2026, with works at 82%. He inspected it with digital minister Frédéric Nzé alongside the 12 MW Kintélé solar farm meant to back its power supply.

Cote d'Ivoire

Lender support for public-service digitalisation

Connectivité des infrastructures publiques : la Côte d'Ivoire se lance dans la modernisation de son administration — Afrik Soir, 4 September 2026

The World Bank is backing Côte d'Ivoire's digitisation of public services with USD 150 million, its representative said at a national prioritisation workshop in Abidjan that opened on 3 September 2026. The minister has set a target of 700 digitised services, and the state IT company SNDI counts 315 already online. No approval or signature date for the financing is given.

United States export-credit guarantee for the national data centre

EXIM Board Meeting Minutes, 21 August 2025 — Côte d'Ivoire National Data Center Guarantee — Export-Import Bank of the United States (EXIM), 21 August 2025

The US Export-Import Bank's board approved a guarantee of about US$66.1m to Citibank on 21 August 2025 to finance Côte d'Ivoire's national data centre, with the finance ministry as borrower and Washington-based Cybastion as exporter. EXIM describes it as the first data centre it has backed in sub-Saharan Africa.

E-government strengthening support project (PARAE)

Côte d'Ivoire : Un nouveau datacenter à Yamoussoukro pour centraliser les données de l'administration publique — Sika Finance, 22 September 2026

A delegation led by the African Development Bank visited the Yamoussoukro site of Côte d'Ivoire's planned Tier 3 administration data centre in September 2026. The centre sits within the PARAE e-government project, budgeted at FCFA 60bn and scheduled to run from January 2025 to 2028.

Côte d'Ivoire-AIP/ Administration électronique: le comité de pilotage du PARAE préconise le renforcement de la coordination — Agence Ivoirienne de Presse (AIP), 22 September 2026

The steering committee of Côte d'Ivoire's PARAE e-government project met on 18 September 2026 and called for closer coordination to speed up works on the Yamoussoukro administration data centre, which are running late. It also approved the project's revised 2026 work plan and procurement plan.

Djibouti

Private investment pipeline identified by diagnostic

Djibouti Country Private Sector Diagnostic — International Finance Corporation, 13 September 2026

The International Finance Corporation published a Country Private Sector Diagnostic for Djibouti in September 2026, prepared with the World Bank Group. It names off-grid solar energy, data centres and tourism as the three subsectors where private investment could grow, and identifies energy reform as a priority. It also notes state-owned enterprises' control of key sectors including telecommunications.

World Bank Group Report Identifies Significant Private Investment Potential for Djibouti — World Bank Group, 14 September 2026

The World Bank Group's private sector diagnostic for Djibouti, released on 13 September 2026, identifies reforms that could unlock at least $600 million of private investment and about 12,000 jobs over five years in off-grid solar, data centres and tourism. Data centres could draw $160 million to $240 million; existing capacity, served by eight submarine cables, is nearly full.

DR Congo

Vodacom Congo network investment programme

RDC : Vodacom veut porter à 670 millions USD ses investissements réseau pour accélérer la transformation numérique — Zoom Eco, 9 October 2026

Vodacom Congo said at a Kinshasa forum on 8 October 2026 that it plans a long-term US$670 million network investment programme, against an average of US$140 million a year. It reported 26.4 million subscribers, 4G coverage of 46.1 per cent and 10 million M-Pesa customers. Prime Minister Judith Suminwa said state and citizens' data must be hosted in the country.

Egypt

Funding round for a checkout-identification payment company

Égypte : Zeal lève 10 millions de dollars pour dynamiser le commerce physique — wearetech.africa, 10 September 2026

An Egyptian technology company has raised US$10 million for payment tools that add a layer to shop card terminals, identifying customers at the checkout so merchants can build loyalty. The money is to take the product into the Middle East, Europe and Africa; no investor is named.

Eswatini

Digital Eswatini Project

EPTC seeks E455m loan for digital transformation — Times of Eswatini, 10 September 2026

Eswatini's posts and telecommunications corporation is seeking a US$26 million World Bank loan to finance the Digital Eswatini Project, under a bill tabled in Parliament. The bill would authorise the finance minister to issue a government guarantee of up to that amount for the loan.

E455m EPTC loan puts taxpayers on the line — Independent News Eswatini, 21 September 2026

Eswatini's House of Assembly took the second reading of three bills on 21 September 2026 financing the US$65 million World Bank Digital Eswatini Project. MPs focused on the US$26 million (about E455 million) loan to the state telecoms company EPTC, which the government would guarantee. The finance minister said that loan pays for EPTC's financial stabilisation and a change in its pension scheme, repayable from 2034 to 2051; the other two facilities fund broadband to Tinkhundla centres, schools and clinics, online government services, digital ID, payments and skills training. The bills now go to committee.

MPs BACK E1.14BN DIGITAL LOANS - Eswatini Positive News — Eswatini Positive News, 21 September 2026

Eswatini MPs spoke in support of the three World Bank facilities, together worth US$65 million (about E1.14 billion), that fund the Digital Eswatini Project, at the bills' second reading. The debate is summarised under the Eswatini Independent's report.

IDEA Half-Year Implementation Progress Report: January-June 2026 and Outlook for July-December 2026 — COMESA Secretariat (IDEA project), 1 October 2026 AngolaDRCMalawiZambiaEast AfricaSouthern Africa

COMESA's half-year progress report on the World Bank-financed Inclusive Digitalisation in Eastern and Southern Africa programme, a US$2.48 billion envelope, covers January to June 2026 in Angola, the DRC, Malawi, Zambia and Eswatini. The COMESA secretariat had spent 9% of its US$3.71 million 2026 budget by June, and moved its AI and regional digital inclusion consultancies from procurement to implementation.

Who was held accountable for EPTC pension crisis?- Sen. Tony — The Independent News Eswatini, 6 October 2026

Eswatini senators asked on 5 October whether anyone had been held to account for the pension crisis at the state posts and telecommunications corporation, after Parliament approved the government guarantee for its US$26 million World Bank loan under the Digital Eswatini Project. The ICT minister said E26 million of the loan goes to the post office pension fund, affecting about 500 employees, and that the bank will manage and audit the loan.

Ghana

UN capital development fund partnership on small-business digital finance

UNCDF, FIDO begin phase two partnership to expand digital finance for MSMEs — Ghana News Agency, 3 September 2026

The UN Capital Development Fund began a second phase of work with Ghana's FIDO Microcredits on 3 September, providing a US$200,000 grant expected to catalyse US$1 million in loans and savings for small businesses led by women and young people. A US$30,000 first phase engaged 470 businesses in three regions. The project is expected to end in July.

Seevcash remittance platform funding

Ghana's Seevcash Raises $333,000, Launches Visa Card — We are Tech (wearetech.africa), 7 September 2026

A Ghanaian remittance startup moving money from the United States to Ghana raised US$333,000 from a blockchain foundation's community fund, to launch an international payment card usable online and in stores. The commitment date is not stated in the account.

Kenya

Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project

Kenya Accelerates Digital Economy Investments Under KDEAP — Tukio, 14 September 2026

Kenya's ICT principal secretary, John Tanui, and a World Bank delegation reviewed progress on the Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project, covering fibre, public Wi-Fi, digital government services and skills. Its Digital Smartboards Programme, run by the ICT Authority with the education ministry, has reached 10,384 public junior schools in all 47 counties and trained 62,364 teachers.

Kenya, World Bank Take Stock of Digital Economy Project KDEAP's Gains and Challenges — Ecofin Agency, 15 September 2026

Kenya and the World Bank reviewed the $390 million Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project on 14 September 2026; more than 62,000 teachers have been trained and over 5,000 smart boards distributed. A 2025 World Bank aide-memoire found only eight of 25 results indicators on target and $10.6 million disbursed, 3.1% of the appraised amount. The project closes on 31 October 2028.

National Infrastructure Fund

MPs find gaps in the rules for the KES 340 billion fund built from the Safaricom sale — Tech-ish, 14 September 2026

Kenya's parliamentary finance committee, chaired by Kuria Kimani, recommended a near-total rewrite of the investment policy for the National Infrastructure Fund, reported on 14 September 2026. It found the policy silent on project risk, selection, borrowing and liquidity limits and exposure caps. The fund holds KES 340bn, mostly from the sale of the state's Safaricom stake, and ICT is one of its five sectors.

Sh320bn raised for National Infrastructure Fund, Ruto says — The Star, 15 September 2026

The president said about Sh320bn has been raised for the National Infrastructure Fund, which is now established in law with its top management appointed and its investment policy framework complete. The money is to finance roads, energy and water.

Vodacom's Safaricom deal struck down by Kenyan high court — TechCentral, 15 September 2026 South Africa

Kenya's High Court on 15 September 2026 declared the government's sale of a 15 per cent Safaricom stake to Vodacom's Vodafone Kenya unconstitutional and ordered the shares returned to the state. The three-judge bench found public participation inadequate and the buyer's identity concealed. The June sale raised KSh204.3 billion; Vodacom said it will appeal and seek a stay.

DigiKen loan portfolio guarantee with a commercial bank

Co-op Bank, UNCDF launch financing facility to boost Kenya's digital — Bizna Kenya, 24 September 2026

Co-operative Bank of Kenya and UNCDF launched a US$900,000 loan portfolio guarantee under the DigiKen programme, backing up to US$1.8 million of lending to fintechs, digital credit providers, e-commerce and digitally enabled small firms, and innovation hubs. UNCDF covers 50% of outstanding principal, rising to 70% for borrowers in the Arid and Semi-Arid Lands.

Malawi

Digital Acceleration Project implementation review

World Bank begins review of Malawi’s $150 million Digital Acceleration Project — techreviewafrica.com, 1 September 2026

The World Bank opened a five-day implementation support mission on Malawi's US$150 million Digital Acceleration Project, to review progress and set priorities for the next phase. No disbursement or results figure accompanies it.

Mauritania

WARDIP digital transformation programme

World Bank — WARDIP SOP1 additional financing, Mauritania, 2026 — Future Afrique (AMI communique), 16 September 2026

The World Bank's International Development Association signed a EUR 25.8 million loan with Mauritania on 16 September 2026 as additional financing for the first phase of the West Africa Regional Digital Integration Programme (WARDIP). The money is for broadband infrastructure, online services and electronic payments.

Conseil des Ministres : le Président de la République enjoint d’accélérer les composantes du programme du gouvernement — Future Afrique, 14 October 2026

Mauritania's Council of Ministers on 14 October 2026 adopted bills to ratify World Bank IDA financing agreements, including a EUR 25.8m additional loan for the WARDIP regional digital programme covering broadband, e-services and e-payments. Another agreement, a EUR 19.7m credit and SDR 5.6m grant signed on 21 August, supports the social registry and the Takaful safety-net programme.

Morocco

Insurance technology and inclusion programme

InsurTech in Morocco: AfDB backs a programme to modernise insurance — Canal212, 27 September 2026

Morocco's insurance supervisor (ACAPS) launched an InsurTech programme with the African Development Bank, a US$680,000 technical assistance package of which the bank gives US$510,000 as a grant and ACAPS US$170,000. Running to early 2027, it begins with a tendered study of regulatory barriers to digital insurance.

Digital Transformation Acceleration Program

Numérique et climat : le Maroc consolide deux chantiers de long terme avec 567,3 M€ — La Vérité, 4 October 2026

Morocco's Bulletin officiel of 1 October 2026 published decrees approving two World Bank loans: €218.2 million, signed on 25 June, for the Digital Transformation Acceleration Program, and €349.1 million, signed on 30 June, for climate and risk finance. The digital programme plans a single public services portal and a digital wallet linked to the electronic ID card, with results expected by 2031.

Mozambique

Donor ICT equipment for disaster risk management

KOICA - INGD Mozambique, ICT equipment for disaster risk management, 2026 — Programa das Nacoes Unidas para o Desenvolvimento, 9 September 2026

The UN Development Programme, with funding from the Korea International Cooperation Agency, handed over ICT equipment worth more than US$300,000 to Mozambique's National Institute for Disaster Risk Management and Reduction in Maputo on 9 September 2026. The equipment is meant to improve information management, communication and coordination in disaster risk management at national, provincial and district level.

Namibia

Universal Service Fund annual accounts

Namibia — Universal Service Fund Integrated Annual Report 2025 — Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia (CRAN), for the Universal Service Fund, 1 September 2026

The Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia published the Universal Service Fund's 2025 integrated annual report, which carries the Fund's first financial statements, covering the 18 months to 31 December 2025. It also reports the Fund's Phase 1 (2024/25) and Phase 2 (2025/26) mobile radio access network deployments.

Universal service fund phase 2 award to the mobile operator

Namibia accelerates network modernization to meet growing connectivity needs - Namibia Daily News — Namibia Daily News (Xinhua), 28 September 2026

Namibia's Mobile Telecommunications Company said on 28 September 2026 it has extended 5G to Grootfontein, Mariental, Okahandja, Otjiwarongo and Tsumeb and is building 19 new sites in nine regions. It was awarded N$32.3 million from phase 2 of the regulator CRAN's Universal Service Fund for 10 towers in underserved areas; ICT minister Emma Theofelus put 4G coverage at about 89%.

Nigeria

World Bank ID4D financing

Nigeria Digital Identification for Development Project (P167183) — Implementation Status & Results Report, August 2026 — World Bank, 11 August 2026

The World Bank's August 2026 status report on Nigeria's digital ID project counts 117.5m people with a National Identification Number at 30 June 2026, against a target of 180m by December. Monthly enrolment would have to rise from under 2m to about 7.4m to reach it, and the NIMS 2.0 integrator contract was due to be signed by end-July.

Rwanda

IDA additional financing for a digital-adoption project

Cabinet Meeting: Rwanda appoints new officials at Rwanda FDA, Justice Ministry and other institutions - AE News — AE News, 19 September 2026

Rwanda's Cabinet, chaired by President Paul Kagame on 18 September 2026, approved a bill to ratify an International Development Association loan of additional financing for a project to accelerate digital adoption, signed in Kigali on 11 July 2026. It also approved the Rwanda Development Cooperation Policy 2026–2050. No loan amount is stated.

Rwanda Secures $19 Million to Extend Government Digitalization Drive — wearetech.africa, 9 October 2026

Rwanda's parliament ratified EUR 17 million of World Bank financing for digital identity, public data management and cybersecurity, restoring part of US$20 million cut from the project in 2024. Delays to the government data hub have pushed the project's deadline to June 2028.

Senegal

Cybastion US$300m investment announcement

Transformation numérique : Cybastion annonce un investissement de 300 millions de dollars au Sénégal — Le Soleil, 14 September 2026

US company Cybastion announced a US$300 million investment in Senegal's digital infrastructure, data security and sovereignty, in Washington. No date, instrument, projects or timetable are given.

Senegal Secures $300m Cybastion Commitment for Digitalisation Projects — Tech Africa News, 16 September 2026

US cybersecurity company Cybastion committed to mobilise about US$300 million for digital projects in Senegal, presented to President Bassirou Diomaye Faye in Washington on 14 September 2026. The money is named for cybersecurity, data centres, technology transfer and capacity building; no instrument or timetable was given.

Seychelles

AfDB Economic Resilience and Green Recovery Support Programme, phase III (ERGRSP-III)

African Development Bank Group — Seychelles, Economic Resilience and Green Recovery Support Programme phase III, 2026 — African Development Bank Group, 22 September 2026

The African Development Bank's board approved a US$34 million loan to Seychelles on 22 September 2026 for the third and final phase of its Economic Resilience and Green Recovery Support Programme. Reforms include electronic invoicing for tax compliance, a national e-procurement system and a one-stop investment portal; the bank's financing across the three phases totals US$84 million.

Seychelles: African Development Bank approves $34 million loan to strengthen public services, private sector growth and climate resilience — African Development Bank Group, 23 September 2026

The African Development Bank approved a US$34 million loan to Seychelles on 22 September 2026 for the third and final phase of its Economic Resilience and Green Recovery Support Programme. The phase supports electronic invoicing for tax, a national e-procurement system and a one-stop investment portal, and brings the bank's financing across the three phases to US$84 million.

World Bank Transformative and Resilient Inclusive Digital Economy project (P519389)

Initial Project Information Document (PID) - Seychelles Transformative and Resilient Inclusive Digital Economy - P519389 — World Bank, 7 October 2026

The World Bank is preparing a Seychelles project to widen internet access, increase use of digital services and build digital skills. Its project document notes that 87% of people used the internet in 2023 but broadband is costly, slow and unreliable, the regulator's enforcement capacity is limited, and digital government is fragmented despite the SeyID digital identity.

South Africa

Global business services centre investment (Cape Town)

SA's global business services presents opportunities for economic growth — SAnews.gov.za, 1 September 2026

Atain, a company selling AI-driven customer experience and business process services, opened a R70 million global business services centre in Cape Town that will take its South African workforce from about 1,500 to 2,100. South Africa's deputy trade minister John Steenhuisen spoke at the opening. No date for the investment decision is given.

Compliance platform investment (Instarc)

Instarc Secures EUR1.25 Million to Scale Cloud-Native Compliance Platform in South Africa — Tech Africa News, 2 September 2026

Instarc, a Tallinn-based regulatory-technology firm, raised a €1.25 million strategic investment from HFO Investments to commercialise its compliance platform for South African accountable institutions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act. The platform covers client onboarding, identity and ownership verification, configurable workflows, document management, retrievable audit records and APIs into existing systems, and the company says it is built to adapt to other regulatory regimes. The date the investment was agreed is not stated in the reporting held.

Recapitalisation of the Vivica fibre and telecoms group

SA investment firm ploughs R2.1bn into digital economy — ITWeb South Africa, 14 September 2026

South African investment firm DNI announced more than R2.1 billion across Frogfoot, Vox and Hypa, Mission Mobile and KnowRoaming. Its consortium holds 34.8% of Frogfoot and Vox at an aggregate R14.4 billion valuation, with Frogfoot's capital aimed at 360,000 lower-income homes over 12 months.

Proparco invests US$20 million in Frogfoot, Vox and Hypa to expand affordable fibre connectivity in South Africa — Proparco, 5 October 2026

Proparco, the French development finance institution, said on 5 October 2026 that it is investing US$20 million in South African fibre and connectivity companies Frogfoot, Vox and Hypa, in a consortium led by Metier with BII, BIO, DEG, Ke Nako Capital, Nedbank and Standard Bank. The funds are for extending fibre into lower-income and township areas. No signing date is given.

United States development finance equity in a Johannesburg-headquartered operator

US govt agency pouring billions into African digital infrastructure firm with HQ in Joburg — EWN (Eyewitness News), 17 September 2026

The United States development finance corporation approved an equity investment in a Johannesburg-headquartered operator of undersea cables, fibre networks and data centres in more than 30 African countries, naming two investment partners. Its board published no amount; a news organisation puts the investment at around R2.5 billion, up to US$155 million, as a ceiling rather than a committed sum.

Tanzania

Digital Tanzania Project

Tanzania Yapiga Hatua Kubwa Katika Ujenzi wa Miundombinu ya Digital — Jamhuri Media, 14 September 2026

Tanzania's Digital Tanzania Project, funded since 2021 by a US$150 million World Bank loan, is due to close on 31 October 2026, an ICT director in the President's Office said on 14 September 2026. Officials said it has built 758 new towers, upgraded 304 to 3G and 4G, added 60 Gbps of government internet capacity, and opened 32 one-stop service centres in post office buildings.

World Bank hints at DTP Phase II — Daily News (Tanzania), 15 September 2026

With the US$150m Digital Tanzania Project due to complete on 31 October, its financier signalled support for a second phase in which artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies would take a central place. Nothing about a second phase is committed or costed.

KCB acquisition of a 22.23% stake in Pesapal

Tanzania regulator reveals KCB's 22.23% stake in payments firm Pesapal — TechCabal, 22 September 2026 Kenya

Tanzania's Fair Competition Commission put KCB Group's stake in Kenyan payments firm Pesapal at 22.23%, the first public figure for a deal KCB announced in 2025 without terms. The stake gives KCB indirect control of Pesapal Tanzania and is under the commission's review; its value is undisclosed.

Digital infrastructure investment envelope to 2031

Infrastructure numérique : la Tanzanie prévoit 2,6 milliards $ d’investissements d’ici 2031 — Agence Ecofin, 30 September 2026

Tanzania presented a digital-infrastructure investment envelope of TZS 7,000 billion (about US$2.6bn) to 2031 at an EU-Tanzania business forum in Helsinki, funds still to be raised. The portfolio includes fibre-to-the-home, the Kilimanjaro One submarine cable and a technology park; the national backbone stood at 13,820 of a planned 16,280 km.

Togo

Increased Digital Connectivity and Electricity Access project (IDEA)

Togo Launches Tender for 5,200 Smart Meter Devices for State Power Utility — Togo First, 21 September 2026

Togo's energy ministry launched an international tender for 5,200 smart-metering devices for the state power utility CEET, reported on 21 September 2026: 1,200 modems for medium-voltage meters and 4,000 data concentrators for low-voltage meters. It is financed under the World Bank's US$200m IDA-backed IDEA project, and bids are due on 28 October 2026. CEET's losses are put at nearly 16%.

Zambia

Digital Zambia Acceleration Project

IDEA Half-Year Implementation Progress Report: January-June 2026 and Outlook for July-December 2026 — COMESA Secretariat (IDEA project), 1 October 2026 AngolaDRCMalawiEswatiniEast AfricaSouthern Africa
