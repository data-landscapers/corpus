Other GovTech and e-Gov: progress report, September 2025 – September 2026

Edition of 2026-09-25-2  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
54 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.

Progress values. Movement — some form of progress, however minor, has been recorded. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the repository holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the repository holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Movement, regulations still pending.

Country Developments Progress
Algeria The national portal carried 150 services by 9 September 2026 and one million registered users by 22 September, both on the high commission's account, while the interior minister named ministry projects as stalled.
Full recordThe portal was announced as a single window in October 2025 alongside an older e-government portal whose relationship to it is not stated, and had 21 services live at July 2026 with a free helpline from 29 July. By 9 September it carried 150 services on the high commission's account, after water-sector additions and five tax certificates.
Against that, 2026-08-10 - the interior minister named all-digital vehicle registration and civil-status services among ministry projects that have stalled and are to be accelerated; the press describes citizens still attending in person for procedures presented as online, and on 26 August he convened wilaya directors to evaluate the vehicle-registration system.
Outside the portal: a police lost-document platform launched in November 2025, a driving-licence and fines convention, the public works ministry's online filing of qualification and design-office files from 1 September 2026, and the fuel distributor's e-Mahata platform, made the sole channel for ordering tyres.
2026-09-14 to 17 — seven youth-sector services and a sports job service were added, with no new total.
2026-09-22 - the high commission reported one million registered users and five million visitors, its own count; no transaction figure is published.

Angola No inventory of online services exists — the transition agenda counts projects, not services — while an at-home identity-card service and a public-finance programme launched.
Full recordWhat is missing first: no service catalogue is published and the transition agenda counts projects rather than services, so the count of public services available online is not knowable from the record.
2026-01-08 — social-security filings and notifications moved to the portal by decree, and in May family-benefit claims had to be filed electronically.
2026-04-27 — opening a tourist establishment moved to an online prior notification, paid through the state's single payment reference.
2026-07-28 — an at-home identity-card service launched in one province at Kz 250,000 for at-home processing and Kz 10,000 for delivery alone, on a 48-hour issuance target, with requests through a web portal and a messaging number. The ministry called the fee a product of exclusivity, and no ministry publication naming the portal is on file.
2026-09-04 - the in-person counterpart to the missing online service inventory does publish numbers: the one-stop citizen service network stands at 16 subunits with eight more committed by 2027, and recorded 1.49m visits in the first half of 2026 and Kz2.47bn paid to the treasury. They are the secretary of state's own, with no service breakdown or online equivalent published.

Benin More than 230 public services had been moved online by July 2025, while the e-procurement platform was retired and its build tender failed.
Full record2025-07-02 — more than 230 public services had been moved online, electronic payment included, and more than 250 digital services are cited in the 2031 strategy material. The country was ranked a 2025 GovTech Maturity Index top performer, a reference index cited and not absorbed.
Against that, 2025-09-16 — the first tender to build a new e-procurement platform was declared unsuccessful, after a decree had already retired the previous system and portal for a replacement costed at 3.3bn FCFA on World Bank financing. The old platform is gone and the new one is not built.
2026-07-24 — a joint circular requires every state structure to move archive and procedure management onto digital tools, with returns stated as fiscal and environmental rather than as service improvement, and no deadline, budget, scope definition or compliance mechanism. 2026-07-31 — one university made its academic records platform the sole channel for certificates and transcripts, with no fee schedule, turnaround or appeal route published.
2026-09 — UnivServices.bj went live for students at four public universities with transport as its first service, catering, housing and health to follow.

Botswana Electronic services rose from 33 to 52 under a Digital Services Act in force with a compliance clock to November 2027, while the payslip portal went offline.
Full record2026-03-18 — the government services platform records electronic services increased from 33 to 52, including teacher registration, appointment scheduling and business facilitation. No usage, transaction or uptake figure is held for either end. The Digital Services Act came into force on 21 November 2025 with a 24-month compliance window running to November 2027, the clock confirmed in February.
2026-07-22 — against that, the government payslip portal is offline with no reopening date after the data-protection regulator's enforcement notice, a privacy notice, an impact assessment, multi-factor authentication and disaster recovery having been added since. It is the only government system on this ledger taken offline by the regulator.
Procurement has not moved: the electronic procurement system is still due at the beginning of 2027, and the authority still states it has no real-time data on procurement transactions, against a strategy approved in June 2025.

Burkina Faso A central portal carries 1,672 information sheets, 95 procedures and 183 institutions, against a ministry count of 146 procedures live of 272 platforms initiated.
Full record2026-05-05 — a central gateway launched with 1,672 information sheets, 95 online procedures and 183 public institutions, partly to counter fraudulent administrative websites. Its 95 do not reconcile with the ministry's count of 146 operational procedures out of 272 platforms initiated since 2023, reported against objectives contracts at 91%.
Volume is real: over 600,000 criminal-record certificates and nearly 70,000 nationality certificates issued through justice platforms, against internet access of 72% urban and 27% rural.
2026-09-07 - the enforcement system's first month is measured, and the gap is collection: 19,910 offences recorded between 1 and 31 August 2026 generating CFA146.559m in fines, of which CFA16.1m had been recovered at the 4 September review. A motorised police brigade was created on 2 September specifically to trace drivers who do not pay. No appeal or contested-notice figure is published.
2026-09-15 — FasoSign went live across three administration platforms.
The counterpart measure is where the platforms sit: the zero-data-abroad programme's own inventory counts 120 platforms hosted outside the national territory, with migration into the two national data centres begun.
2026-09-25 - the state airports company signed a memorandum to pilot an airport certification and operations platform.

No count of services available online, no service catalogue and no usage figure appears anywhere for the country.

Full recordThe consular platform carried entry visas online only at the window's opening; second-generation consular cards and travel documents launched in October 2025 and the platform now runs across 46 consular posts and two international airports.
2026-08-10 to 14 - the water utility trained staff ahead of rollout of a centralised platform replacing decentralised commercial management, with automated meter reading and expanded electronic payment channels; the account is the director general's and no customer count, cost or completion date is stated.

Cape Verde A single services portal went live on 24 February 2026, against an unmeasured target of 60% of vital services online by 2026.
Full record2026-02-24 - a single web and app gateway went live covering finance, civil registration, identity, tax, higher education, transport and social services, with departmental portals to migrate into it; no count of migrated services is published, and the Prime Minister's own account is that departmental platforms did not communicate with each other before that date. It follows the justice portal launched in December 2024 behind the identity platform, serving land, commercial, vehicle and criminal-record certificates, civil-status certificates, identity-card renewal and appointment booking.
The target of at least 60% of vital public services online by 2026 and 80% by 2030 was restated at a World Bank mission and remains unmeasured. The procurement platform was re-let at CVE 70,000,000 over four years on perpetual licensing, unlimited users, national deployment and full source-code control, its requirements redefined to extend it to local administration, and its use is compulsory for state purchasing in 2026.

No cost, financier, vendor, timetable or number of accounts closed is stated, and the architecture is explicitly regional rather than national.

Congo A task force was gazetted under the Prime Minister on 6 August 2026, with four priority services committed within two years.
Full record2026-08-06 - a decree created a task force under the Prime Minister with four organs: a strategic committee chaired by the Prime Minister seating thirteen ministers and meeting at least four times a year, a steering committee under the Vice-Prime Minister, a technical committee chaired by the digital-economy minister seating five agency directors-general, and an investment committee chaired by the finance minister, with interoperability of public information systems a standing objective. Its first listed task is to audit the level of digitisation across the administration, so no baseline is stated.
The roadmap commits civil status, passport, criminal record and company creation as digitised priority services within two years. The one service actually compulsory is certified electronic invoicing, mandatory for every economic agent.
