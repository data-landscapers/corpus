Angola

Published data-protection fines

Deliberação sobre processos contravencionais — Agência de Protecção de Dados, 11 October 2026

Angola's data protection agency publishes its register of fines: 15 sanctions between 2022 and 2026, from US$75,000 to US$525,000, with banks, telecoms operators, the national airline and the Luanda water utility among those penalised. The largest 2026 fine was for storing personal data in a cloud abroad without authorisation.

Benin

Data protection authority

IA et flux transfrontaliers en Afrique : L'appel de l'Apdp-Bénin à une gouvernance fiable des données — La Nation (Bénin), 3 September 2026

The president of Benin's personal data protection authority (APDP), Luciano Hounkponou, set out four measures for trusted cross-border data flows at a regulators' forum held with MTN Group's fintech summit in Johannesburg on 1–3 September 2026: stronger legal frameworks, secure and interoperable systems, shared governance and user education. He also called for closer cooperation between data protection and financial supervisors.

Data protection authority sworn officers

Seventeen officers of the data protection authority were sworn in before the Cotonou court of first instance on 14 September under the digital code, formally binding them to the obligations of their duties.

Burkina Faso

Authorisation of personal-data transfers abroad

Demande d’autorisation de transfert de données vers un pays étranger — Commission de l’Informatique et des Libertés (CIL), Burkina Faso, 1 September 2026

Burkina Faso's data protection authority, the CIL, publishes the form for applying to transfer personal data abroad. It asks for the foreign recipient, whether the destination country has a data protection authority, and the purpose, consent and security arrangements behind the transfer.

Autorité de Régulation de la communication et du traitement des données à caractère personnel : W. Louis Modeste Ouédraogo nommé président — leFaso.net, 20 October 2026

Burkina Faso appointed W. Louis Modeste Ouédraogo, previously president of the Conseil supérieur de la communication, to head the new regulator formed by merging that council with the data-protection commission CIL. The decree is dated 16 October 2026.

Cameroon

Personal data protection authority

Gouvernance des données en Afrique centrale : le Cameroun sans APDP opérationnelle — OBS-CC, 15 September 2026 Central Africa

Cameroon's data protection law of December 2024 creates a protection authority, the APDP, but its organising decree has not been found. The OBS-CC observatory checked more than 600 presidential acts on 14 September 2026 and found no such decree. A draft decree on the telecoms ministry's site predates the law and cites the wrong articles.

Cameroon’s AI Data Centre: Five Legal Questions Before the First Server Is Installed - CLG Global — CLG Global, 29 October 2026

A CLG Global analysis sets out five legal questions for Cybastion's planned US$75 million AI data centre in Douala. Cybastion announced the project in August 2026. The analysis notes that the data protection authority, which must authorise transfers abroad, awaits a presidential decree and is not yet operational.

Cape Verde

Serviço de Informações da República: Entre a defesa do Estado e os limites da democracia — A Nacao, 8 September 2026

Cabo Verde's prime minister delegated coordination of the intelligence service SIR to the interior minister by despacho no. 15/2026 in the Boletim Oficial. Congo

National personal data protection commission

Gouvernance : mobiliser les ressources publiques — Les Dépêches de Brazzaville, 17 October 2026

Opening the National Assembly's budget session on 15 October 2026, its president Isidore Mvouba asked the Congolese government to make the National Commission for the Protection of Personal Data operational promptly. DR Congo

Prosecution of offences committed through social media

RDC: la diffamation dans les réseaux sociaux, punissable par le code du numérique — ACP, 6 October 2026

A Kinshasa lawyer spoke to the press agency on 6 October 2026. He said the 2023 digital code punishes online defamation, with no immunity for journalists or influencers. He cited the September 2026 arrest of a TikTok chronicler. The arrest followed a broadcast about a singer's private life.

RDC : Des personnes poursuivies pour des infractions numériques détenues à Makala, annonce Guillaume Ngefa — LePotentiel, 24 October 2026

The justice minister spoke at a press briefing. He said several people prosecuted for social-media offences are held at Makala prison in Kinshasa. Egypt

Joint decision on social-media accounts for under-15s

«الأعلى للإعلام» و«الاتصالات» يلزمان منصات التواصل بحماية الأطفال دون 15 عامًا — Youm7, 17 September 2026

Egypt's media council and telecom regulator issued a joint decision obliging platforms to protect children under 15: no independent accounts for under-13s, an automatic and undisableable safe mode for those aged 13 to under 15, and age verification that resists circumvention while minimising the data collected for it. Platforms must review existing accounts that appear to belong to under-15s, offer a route to correct an age, and comply within three months.

مسار والمبادرة المصرية للحقوق الشخصية تطالبان بضمانات معلنة لحماية الأطفال على الإنترنت وصون حقوقهم — Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights, 29 September 2026

Egyptian rights groups Masaar and the Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights asked the media regulator and the telecoms regulator NTRA on 29 September 2026 to publish the full text and legal basis of their 17 September decision on children's social-media accounts, which bars independent accounts for under-13s. They also want public rules on what age-verification data is collected and kept, and no general biometric requirement.

Personal Data Protection Center

Egypt: Personal Data Protection Law Update — Baker McKenzie, 7 October 2026

Egypt's Personal Data Protection Center opened its electronic portal for accreditation, licence and permit applications in early October, weeks before the data-protection law's compliance grace period ends on 31 October 2026, on a law firm's account. Applying needs a Digital Egypt login tied to an Egyptian national ID, so foreign controllers and processors must act through an authorised local representative.

Eswatini

Data protection authority community outreach campaign

EDPA takes data protection services closer to Emaswati — Eswatini Positive News, 17 September 2026

Eswatini's Data Protection Authority is setting up information desks in major towns to give the public and businesses guidance on data-protection rights, responsibilities and registration. Gabon's data-protection authority APDPVP has authorised the ST Digital Gabon data centre and its mirror site and logged about 715 declarations of conformity, its president Joël Dominique Ledaga said in an interview. Ghana

Data Protection Commission

Full Text: Sam George's speech at Government Accountability Series — MyJoyOnline, 7 September 2026

Ghana's communications ministry gave a full public account of its January–August delivery, and the arithmetic is the interesting part: 141,954 registered accounts against 5,812 logged completions on the national coders programme, 8,578 fibre cuts projected for 2026 at roughly US$2,045 a repair, and a dig-once policy still awaiting Cabinet. It also records voice over LTE rising from about 7% of traffic to 22.43% and smartphone penetration at 71.8% in June 2026.

DPC begins checks on data protection compliance — Ghana News Agency, 4 October 2026

Ghana's Data Protection Commission said it would begin spot checks and enforcement from 5 October against organisations that process personal data without registering or meeting the Data Protection Act, 2012. Its executive director said prosecution would follow where necessary.

DPC strengthens enforcement after registering 2,575 data controllers | Ghana News Agency — Ghana News Agency, 13 October 2026

Ghana's Data Protection Commission said it had registered 2,575 data controllers in 2026, more than in any previous year, and was adding spot checks, inspections and complaint investigations.

United States health data-sharing agreement

President Mahama Defends Rejection of U.S. Health Compact, Calls Terms 'Humiliating' and a Threat to Sovereignty — Ghana News Global, 29 September 2026

Ghana's President John Mahama told the Council on Foreign Relations in New York that his Cabinet rejected a US health compact because it required Ghana to share pathogen profiles and medical records and exempted US-supplied medicines from Food and Drugs Authority inspection. Reuters reported that the deal, under negotiation since November 2025, would have provided US$109 million for health over five years.

U.S. seeks transition from health aid dependency to country ownership – Rolf Olson | Ghana News Agency — Ghana News Agency, 29 September 2026

The US chargé d'affaires in Ghana, Rolf Olson, told journalists in Accra on 29 September 2026 that the health memorandum of understanding Ghana rejected was meant to taper US assistance over several years towards country ownership. He said the US invested US$82.5 million in Ghana in the administration's first year and US$53 million in FY2026, and has signed 35 bilateral health MOUs, 24 in sub-Saharan Africa.

US seeks changes to rejected $109m health compact with Ghana – Mahama — MyJoyOnline, 10 October 2026

Ghana's President John Mahama said the United States has asked to reopen talks on the roughly US$109m five-year health compact that Ghana's Cabinet rejected, with a view to amending or removing the disputed clauses. Ghana objected to provisions requiring it to give the US its pathogen profile and medical records, to counterpart funding and to limits on its Food and Drugs Authority.

Ghana's Data Protection Commission directed an Accra hospital to report to the police criminal investigations department over an expired data protection registration certificate, after an enforcement operation on 12 October. How long the registration had lapsed was not stated.

Data Protection Commission clamps down on organisations breaching regulations, microfinance firm and health facility referred to police for prosecution — Graphic Online, 13 October 2026

Ghana's Data Protection Commission began field enforcement in Accra on 12 October 2026, finding two hospitals operating with expired certificates; a microfinance firm and a health facility were referred to police for prosecution. Kenya

Safeguards for deactivating and recycling telephone numbers

Procedures and technical safeguards for deactivation and recycling of inactive telecommunication numbering resources (CA.G.520:2026, Version 1.0) — Communications Authority of Kenya, 1 September 2026

The Communications Authority of Kenya published draft guidelines (CA.G.520:2026) on deactivating and recycling inactive phone numbers, open for comment until 11 September 2026 with a proposed start date of 19 September. The draft sets up a central database of deactivated and recycled numbers, quarterly reporting by operators, a *106# status check, and whitelisting for prisoners and incapacitated subscribers.

Kenya’s new phone number rules will make recycling costlier for telcos — TechCabal, 2 September 2026

Kenya's Communications Authority published draft procedures for deactivating and recycling mobile numbers: deactivation only after three consecutive months without revenue-generating activity, then three further months of contact attempts by message and the details given at registration, with at-risk numbers published in national media. The document is out for consultation, with memoranda due 11 September 2026.

SIM deactivation now backed by law, CA says — Daily Nation, 7 October 2026

Kenya's communications regulator told a National Assembly committee that SIM deactivation and number recycling now run under its 2026 procedures: notice to the subscriber's contact after three months' inactivity, a public notice and status check before recycling after six, and whitelisting for subscribers who are sick, imprisoned or deceased. It was answering a petition against the largest operator's recycling.

CA to set up central database to track recycled phone numbers — Daily Nation, 12 October 2026

Kenya's communications regulator said it will set up a central database of recycled phone numbers, which operators must update every three months. Banks, fintechs and bulk message senders would check it before sending account messages to a number.

High Court ruling on ride-hailing data retention

What High Court Ruling on Uber, Bolt and Other Ride-Hailing Taxi Mean for Kenya's Consumers — Consumers Federation of Kenya (COFEK), 2 September 2026

Kenya's High Court struck down a National Transport and Safety Authority rule requiring ride-hailing platforms to retain passenger and driver data for three years and hand it over on demand, finding it breached the right to privacy and the Data Protection Act 2019, the Consumers Federation of Kenya reported on 2 September 2026. The court, on a petition by Bolt, also blocked an 18% commission cap and suspended its declaration for 12 months so the government can redo the regulations.

Guidance Notes for Cross-border Data Transfers

Guidance Notes for Cross-border Data Transfers — Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (Kenya), 8 September 2026

Kenya's Office of the Data Protection Commissioner published Guidance Notes for Cross-border Data Transfers in September 2026, with a foreword by Data Commissioner Immaculate Kassait. The 63-page document includes a compliance checklist, an application form for binding corporate rules and standard clauses for controller-to-controller and controller-to-processor transfers. It prints no issue date.

Draft notice on additional categories of sensitive personal data

DRAFT Public Notice: Additional Categories of Sensitive Personal Data (Data Protection Act, 2019, s.47) — PDF — Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, 11 September 2026

Kenya's Office of the Data Protection Commissioner published a draft notice on 11 September 2026 adding political affiliation and trade union membership to sensitive personal data under section 47 of the Data Protection Act 2019. It applies only to data transferred from countries whose law already treats them as sensitive. Public comment is invited; no deadline is stated.

Matey v Safaricom ruling on access to data-disclosure records

Matey v Safaricom PLC (Constitutional Miscellaneous Application E043 of 2026) [2026] KEHC 13624 (KLR), Ruling, 18 September 2026 — High Court of Kenya at Nairobi (Milimani), Constitutional and Human Rights Division; text as reported by SheriaHub from Kenya Law, 18 September 2026

Kenya's High Court ruled on 18 September 2026 in Matey v Safaricom that Safaricom must, within 21 days, give a subscriber her subscriber details and records of every request for access to her communications, M-Pesa and transaction data, plus access logs, for March 2024 to January 2025. The court relied on the constitutional right of access to information, subject to the Data Protection Act.

Data-protection certificate as a condition of digital-lender licensing

Stakeholders push for data sovereignty as AI conference tackles power shortages, worker exploitation — Citizen Digital, 25 September 2026

At the Africa AI Stakeholder Meeting in Nairobi, Kenya's deputy data commissioner John Walubengo said an agreement with the Central Bank of Kenya requires digital lenders to hold a data-protection compliance certificate before being licensed. A Schneider Electric speaker said Kenya has 0.6% of global data-centre capacity, with local facilities about 37% utilised.

Kenya's Public Service Commission shortlisted 13 of 152 applicants to succeed Immaculate Kassait as Data Protection Commissioner at the end of her single six-year term, with interviews on 5 October 2026.

How Absa Bank's employee data leak brought down multi-billion business — The Standard, 12 October 2026

A former senior manager at a Kenyan bank testified, in a Sh1.5 billion data breach suit against it, that a credit manager disclosed a borrower's loan distress to a third party. He said the bank's legal department recommended no action because it saw no material risk of abuse.

Malawi

United States bilateral health agreement and its data-sharing agreement

US insists specimen data won't be shared — Nation Online, 1 September 2026

The US Embassy in Lilongwe said Malawian biological specimens will go to the United States only during public health emergencies and outbreaks, and that no personally identifiable information is shared. The agreement, signed on 11 February 2026 within a five-year US$936 million health package, requires Malawi to supply requested specimens within seven days. Malawian health advocates criticised the clause giving Malawi access to resulting products only after US domestic needs are met.

Detecting and countering misuse of AI: September 2026 — Anthropic, 1 September 2026

Togo's personal data protection authority (IPDCP) and Mali's APDP signed a cooperation agreement on 23 September covering information exchange, joint handling of complaints and compliance checks, and staff training. Their talks also touched on cross-border data transfers, cybersecurity and AI.

Mauritius

Data Protection Office guidance to management companies

Data Protection Office (Mauritius), Advice to Management Companies (MCs) - registration and Data Protection Officer obligations under the Data Protection Act 2017 — Data Protection Office, Mauritius, 1 September 2026

The Data Protection Office updated its guidance in September 2026. Every Management Company must register as a controller, and also as a processor where it handles personal data for managed entities. Each must name its own Data Protection Officer, and managed entities get no blanket exemption but may ask for a case-by-case assessment.

Mauritius Managed Entities Now Need Their Own Data Protection Registration | Acclime — Acclime, 26 October 2026

Mauritius's Data Protection Office issued updated guidance in September 2026, as described by an advisory firm. A management company must register as a controller for its own data and separately as a processor for entities it administers. Each managed entity needs its own registration and data protection officer.

Namibia

NDPC and NiDCOM collaboration on diaspora data-protection awareness

NDPC revenue generated

High Court rejection of implied consent for third-party contact data

Truecaller's Business Model and the Lagos State High Court's Rejection of the Implied Consent Argument — DNL Legal and Style, 19 September 2026

The High Court of Lagos State ruled on 14 September 2026, in Data Privacy Lawyers Association v Truecaller International LLP, that a Truecaller user's consent to upload contacts does not amount to consent from the non-users whose numbers are uploaded, declining to follow a 2023 Federal High Court decision. The court nonetheless refused the reliefs sought because damages were not established, applicants' counsel Olumide Babalola wrote.

Truecaller says Nigerians can remove their numbers after privacy ruling — TechCabal, 28 September 2026

Truecaller says it will not suspend or redesign its Nigerian service after the Lagos High Court dismissed a privacy suit on behalf of non-users, and points them to its unlisting portal. Judgment excerpts show the court held that a user's upload of contacts does not establish non-users' consent, and counsel for the applicants plans to appeal the refusal of relief.

Meta-funded data-protection capacity programme

Court dismisses $25 million privacy suit against Meta in Imo — Premium Times, 28 September 2026

Nigeria's Imo State High Court on 21 July 2026 dismissed a $25 million privacy suit against Meta, finding the applicant had not proved unlawful processing of her data, reported on 28 September 2026. Her case rested on a Nigeria Data Protection Commission order that was set aside under Meta's settlement with the commission. The court held that personal data falls under the constitutional right to privacy.

Lagos High Court judgment on Meta's behavioural advertising

Meta appeals Nigerian ruling restricting Facebook, Instagram Ad tracking — BusinessDay NG, 1 October 2026

Meta filed a notice of appeal on 30 September against a Lagos High Court judgment of 25 September that held its processing of Nigerian users' data for behavioural advertising unlawful. The court ordered it to stop, comply with the data protection Act within eight weeks and pay the naira equivalent of US$100,000 in damages.

NDPC and National Orientation Agency data-protection awareness MoU

Nigeria’s NDPC and NOA Sign MoU to Expand Data Protection Awareness — Tech Africa News, 6 October 2026

Nigeria's data protection commission and the National Orientation Agency signed a memorandum on joint public awareness work. It covers training for the agency's staff, coordinated campaigns across traditional and digital media, and guidance on applying the 2023 data-protection Act to the agency's own mandate.

Registration of data controllers and processors of major importance

Federal High Court Upholds Mandatory Data Protection Registration for PoS Operators — Andersen Nigeria, 9 October 2026

Nigeria's Federal High Court in Lagos ruled on 17 July 2026 that the Nigeria Data Protection Commission acted within its powers in designating point-of-sale operators as data controllers of major importance. Such operators must therefore register with the commission and pay its fees. A PoS agent had argued the requirement breached his constitutional right to privacy.

America First Global Health Strategy MOU

Nigeria-US health deal under scrutiny — Trust Radio, 12 October 2026

Nigeria's five-year health memorandum with the United States, signed on 19 December 2025 and reported as worth about $5bn, came under scrutiny after Ghana's president said Ghana rejected a similar deal over data concerns. The deal requires Nigeria to share health data and specimens; Atiku Abubakar's campaign demanded the full text, doctors faulted a clause letting the US keep using shared data after termination, and the health ministry did not respond.

Senegal
