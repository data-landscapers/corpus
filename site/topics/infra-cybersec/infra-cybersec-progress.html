Cybersecurity: progress report, 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-06

Edition of 2026-09-06-2  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
53 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Country Developments Progress
Algeria A 2025-2029 security strategy was presented in March 2026 and a sectoral incident-response centre established.
Full recordAn information systems security agency operates under the Ministry of National Defence with no establishing instrument held, its first attested act in March 2026. 2026-03-03 - it presented a 2025-2029 national strategy approved by the President, the text not held, reported in the trade press.

2026-06-15 - a sectoral incident-response centre for posts and telecommunications was established with Korean technical support, reported the same day; a child online-protection cell and green line opened on 1 June; and a cybersecurity memorandum with a Russian vendor was signed in November 2025 with no implementing project held.

2026-08-18 - a wilaya warned that unlicensed platforms are impersonating its institutions as fake ticketing storefronts built to harvest card details, with no takedown or prosecution published.
 Advanced
Angola A strategy, a council and a cyber centre were created by decree, none with an operating record, while an attack took the incumbent's services down for nine days.
Full recordThe instruments arrived together. A national cybersecurity strategy was gazetted on 3 December 2025 with six strategic objectives and a two-phase legislative reform, a consultative national cybersecurity council chaired by the President was created by decree the same day, and a national cybersecurity centre was constituted as a public institute with regulatory, supervisory, inspection and sanctioning powers, an emergency response team and a round-the-clock operations department, retaining 30% of the fines it levies. None has an operating record: no source records the council convening or the centre exercising any power, and in August 2026 the responsible minister was still describing the centre's launch in the future tense. The cybersecurity fund its statute presupposes is created by no instrument on file.

The operational test went badly. An attack on the incumbent from the early hours of 28 July 2026 took voice, messaging, mobile data and internet down nationwide until 5 August, nine days, with full restoration declared on 21 August bar one feature and automatic compensation to customers. Every account is the operator's own; no regulator finding, attacker, vector or outage cost is published.

Analysts argued afterwards that telecommunications should be designated critical infrastructure; no designation instrument exists.
 Mixed, a strategy, a council and a centre created on paper while the budget was cut and a national outage ran nine days
Benin The security stack has run since 2020 with a response team, public-key infrastructure and forensic laboratory, and a first national vulnerability report published 878 findings.
Full recordA national digital-security strategy has run since 2020 with a national response team, a national public-key infrastructure, a forensic-analysis laboratory and a critical-infrastructure protection policy, under the Malabo and Budapest conventions.

2025-06-25 — the first national vulnerability report covered 2021 to 2024: 878 recorded vulnerabilities of which 23% critical, 832 password breaches, and institutional compliance ranging from 6% to 87% with the public service worst hit at 326 cases, presented at the annual forum. Publishing it is a deliberate act of transparency that tempers the success narrative.

2026-06 — more than 230 services delivered online are framed by the agency as an expanding exposure, with about 40 priority structures under tailored oversight, while an expert roadmap to 2033 urges a sovereign cloud and a national cyber authority, neither of which exists. Enforcement reaches outward: the country was among those in a regional takedown producing 651 arrests. A vendor telemetry count of 631 ransomware detections in 2024 is the only exposure figure held, on an unpublished denominator.
 Advanced
Botswana A four-day national cyber drill was held and a vendor security portfolio entered the market; no national strategy or authority exists.
Full recordAn international police assessment flagged cyber threats against both institutions and individuals in Botswana (assessment).

2026-03 — a four-day national cyber drill was held, opened by the regulator's chief executive, where none was on file at the start of the window; the base separately records an inaugural drill in the same season.

2026-05-13 — an integrated security framework and cloud services became available to Botswana customers, on a company announcement with no customer, revenue or capacity figures.

The measured position is good and thinly sourced: 0.9% of consumer transaction attempts were flagged as suspected digital fraud in 2025, the lowest of the African countries analysed, on a vendor study paywalled beyond its opening paragraph.

What is missing is the institutional half. No national cybersecurity strategy document, date or version is on file, and the authority that would own one is created in law and not established, reported under legislation.

Burkina Faso The regulator allocated an object-identifier sub-arc to the national cybersecurity agency in February 2026.
Full recordThe regulator allocated an object-identifier sub-arc to the national cybersecurity agency, a technical building block for a public-key infrastructure, certificates and electronic signatures that underpins any future digital-identity authentication service. A national cyber-incident agency had been created in Council in July 2024.

The allocation is a foundation, not a service: no operating certification or signature service is reported, no incident response team is constituted, and no advisory from either body is on the record.
 Advanced
Burundi Regional central bank governors flagged financial-system cyber risk as needing stronger common measures; no national instrument or common measure exists.
Full record2026-07-27 — East African central bank governors flagged financial-system cyber risk as requiring stronger common measures. No common measure has been adopted and no baseline was held before it.

That single regional mention is the whole of the country's cybersecurity record on this ledger. No national strategy, no law, no computer emergency response team, no agency and no reported incident appears — at a point when an instant payment system, an online tax platform and a biometric identity programme have all entered service or are about to.

Cameroon The incident response team was equipped in January 2026, while DNSSEC validation sits at 56.60% against a target that has passed.
Full record2026-01-20 - intrusion-detection and prevention hardware and software were commissioned for the national incident response team at about FCFA 735m under the national connectivity project; the team ingests about 200GB a day and runs the national key infrastructure, having identified and blocked 19m threats in 2024. The national zone has been cryptographically signed since April 2025.

Against that, national DNSSEC validation stood at 56.60% in December 2025 with one operator at 2.75% and most others at 0%. A first-half 2026 target of 98% passed with no published figure, and the target is restated as 100% by end-2026. IPv6 is called essential with no target date, measured adoption rate or instrument. What victims lose is measured only by research: an average of 132,000 FCFA per online job scam against 58,000 FCFA in Chad, with 82% of the Cameroonian cases described being messaging-based social engineering, a survey of respondents rather than a reported-crime series.
 Mixed, the incident response team was hardened and the national zone stays signed while the DNSSEC validation target passed unmet and IPv6 has no target at all
Cape Verde A national security operations centre and incident response team were launched in October 2025, five years after the decree providing for them.
Full recordThe 2021 decree established the cybersecurity legal regime and created the national response team, with no budget line at any grain of the FY2024 or FY2025 volumes and the money sitting inside the state operator's trading account; the instrument itself is not held and is known only through the budget and operator records that recite it.

2025-10-30 - both bodies were launched inside the state's private technological network, built by the operator with World Bank involvement, the operations centre for real-time detection and the response team as a national alert-and-response point. No incident count, staffing figure or service catalogue has been published since.

A technical mission to South Korea covering data governance, service digitisation and cybersecurity produced no formal agreement.
 Advanced
Central African Republic The national cybersecurity agency appears in no estimates volume for a third year, and the 2026 programme budget carries FCFA 6,000,000 in total for network security and digital trust.
Full recordThe agency was created by the January 2024 cybersecurity law under joint supervision, to secure information systems, handle alerts and regulate the sector, with no establishment record. It appears nowhere in the 2024, 2025 or 2026 estimates under any wording; a search for the word cyber returns nothing across 602 pages of estimates and 183 pages of programme budget.

What is appropriated is FCFA 4,000,000 for network and information-system security and FCFA 2,000,000 for digital trust — FCFA 6,000,000 for the whole country for the year, against FCFA 1,600,000,000 transferred from the same ministry to the regulator.

The one capability the base can point to as delivered rather than announced is a cybersecurity and electronic-certification platform listed in a funder's delivered scope at the backbone inauguration, with no operator, uptake or certificate-issuance record, and no held source names it again. A 2022 appraisal found the country had no strategy, response team or operations centre and nothing has updated it in four years.

Chad A national cybersecurity strategy launched in December 2022 has no adopted text, and a tripartite audit and certification agreement covers the national data centre.
Full recordThe strategy is known only from a single relayed sentence; no document, launch date or adoption record is held (strategy).

The audit agreement was signed between the digital agency, the security agency and a private firm. A United States firm separately offered to audit and certify the same facility in May 2026 and no source connects the two mandates (agreement, security).

A comparative study of online job scams published in August 2026 is the only measure of harm in this ledger; it surveys respondents rather than reporting a crime series, and carries no sample size or national total (scams).
 No change
Comoros The digital agency's cybersecurity directorate is the competent authority under article 6 of the 2021 law; no response team exists.
Full recordThe directorate's own page names it the competent authority for the security of information systems and networks and for trust services, organised into a security-technologies department and a security-audit department. The law it stands on was promulgated on 18 January 2022 and its text names the same directorate.

Nothing on file records a budget, a headcount, an incident handled, an audit performed or a report published by it, at either end of the window.

No national computer emergency response team exists, searched at 31 August 2026: a designated authority is not an operating capability, and the 2018 strategy that costed a national cybersecurity apparatus has nothing on file saying what was built.
 No change, a designated authority with no operational record and still no incident-response team
Congo The agency gained a statutory levy-share basis in the 2026 finance law, while the national strategy is only now out to tender.
Full recordThe regulator dismantled two further telecommunications traffic-fraud sites at Massengo (operation).

The agency, created by law in October 2019, had FCFA 800,000,000 stated for 2025 operations on trade reporting alone, tied to no instrument and with no agency line in any appropriation or execution document. It now holds a statutory levy-share basis alongside the four resource streams of its founding statutes, and its largest operational mandate arrived through tax legislation: the invoicing decree and orders put it into joint homologation of every terminal, software and enterprise module, awarding the security label and taking 15% of homologation fees.

The strategy is out to open international tender with bids due 2 September 2026, seven years after the agency was created. An audit-provider qualification standard is a consolidated draft with no adoption instrument, and a national operations centre and response team are named as a roadmap programme with no standing-up instrument.
 Advanced
Cote d'Ivoire The agency commissioned a national root of trust and launched an administration security network, while the national strategy expired without a successor.
Full recordThe agency runs a national security operations centre, the public-administration security-officer network, the national public key infrastructure and auditor certification. The root of trust was commissioned in July 2026 as the trust anchor for every certification authority in the country, under the 2013 electronic transactions law and its 2014 and 2016 decrees, and the security-officer network was launched over 10 to 11 June 2026 for shared watch, early warning and incident coordination, with no membership figure or reporting obligation stated.

Against that, the 2021-2025 strategy expired and remains the text formally in force because no successor has been adopted, its 2026-2030 draft still awaiting the Council of Ministers. The 2024 cybercrime report recorded 12,100 complaints and 338 arrests; no 2025 edition is on file, so the most recent national figures are more than eighteen months old. The cybercrime platform is stated as created in the government's 2011-2025 security record alongside 11 forensic-police branches, a self-report with no caseload attached. And the only separable cyber-security appropriations in the budget went down: FCFA 1.618bn for the treasury information system in FY2026, roughly halved from FCFA 3.268bn.
 Mixed, a national root of trust and an administration security network entered service while the national strategy expired unsucceeded and the cybercrime report stopped
Djibouti A statutory cyber authority became operational in December 2025, and its response team took part in a regional exercise.
Full recordThere was no statutory cyber authority at the window's opening, only a pre-statutory label and a directorate inside the digital agency. A law carried in the official journal of 15 December 2025 created an autonomous public establishment under the Presidency, operational from December 2025 with a director-general named, after the bill advanced in November.

2026-06-26 to 30 - its incident response team took part in a regional exercise and is reported to have placed first in both simulation scenarios; the placing is the state daily's and the organiser has published no result. The 2024-2030 five-pillar strategy is unchanged.
 Advanced
DR Congo A presidential ordinance of 16 May 2026 creates the national cybersecurity agency.
Full recordOrdonnance 26-033, signed on 16 May 2026 and published in the Journal Officiel, creates the Agence Nationale de Cybersecurite under the 2023 Digital Code, with powers to audit public institutions and operators of vital importance and to certify and homologate cybersecurity products and services, and tasks it with standing up a national security operations centre and a CERT; covered entities must notify it of attacks and intrusions.

It is the clearest movement from legal framework to institution the base holds. Neither the operations centre nor the CERT is reported operating, and no incident report or advisory from the agency is on the record.

Egypt The second-edition cybersecurity strategy is being implemented with a third in preparation, and the country holds a full score on the global index.
Full recordThe base carries no dated statement of which strategy edition was in force at the window's opening. The second edition is being implemented through projects to protect digital infrastructure and build cyber defences, a third is being prepared, and the national emergency readiness centre is being developed to use artificial intelligence against cyber threats. No edition dates, text or publication is held: the whole position rests on one ministerial statement to a Senate committee.

The readiness centre operates under the telecommunications regulator and is a delivery partner in the national cyber-skills academy, with no incident statistics, mandate document, budget or reporting obligation held.

The global index standing of 100% and a first-tier place among twelve states was restated in June 2026, but both readings refer to the same 2024 edition, so it is a static reading rather than movement.

Full recordIt was created by the December 2024 law under the Presidency (creation).

No incident response team, sector designation, audit programme or national strategy exists anywhere in the base, so an agency named in a statute is the whole of the country's cybersecurity institutional record.

Eswatini A regional cybersecurity framework was finalised and approved at a subcommittee hosted in-country, and a national awareness month was opened by the prime minister.
Full recordA government mobile application testing laboratory was opened, funded and supported by the Republic of China (Taiwan) (opening).

The regional framework was finalised and approved by a central bank governors' subcommittee at a meeting in the country in July 2026 (framework).

Cybersecurity awareness month was opened by the prime minister in October 2025 (awareness).

Neither is a national instrument. The country has no cybersecurity statute — one was named as forthcoming a year ago — no incident response team on record, and no incident, caseload or capability figure anywhere in this ledger.

Enforcement has a unit behind it: a police digital forensic unit and cyber task team had made 87 arrests and submitted 528 dockets to the prosecutions director by October 2025, working on mobile money fraud, fake wallet schemes, online loan scams and rental scams. Over E8.5 million was involved in cyber-related crime in the twelve months to 30 September 2025.

Ethiopia Cybersecurity foundations are named in the national strategy, and the dominant state bank is moving to a zero-trust architecture.
Full recordThe December 2025 strategy makes the security agency's cybersecurity work one of the foundations of the national programme (Digital Ethiopia 2030).

The dominant state bank reported in August 2026 that it is moving its information-systems architecture to a zero-trust model supported by artificial intelligence (financial sector threats).

No national readiness assessment, incident statistics, emergency response team disclosure or sectoral audit is held, so the record is a stated intention and one institution's own account.
 Advanced
Gabon No national strategy is published, and a June 2026 attack collapsed 95 per cent of a utility's information systems.
Full recordA national cybersecurity strategy was named as an objective of the second national forum, by a vendor rather than by the agency or the ministry, and a regulator workshop of January 2026 restated resilience gaps without producing one (forum, workshop).

A security bill of material launched in June 2025 has published no asset count, coverage, review cycle or output in the fourteen months since, and its stated linkage target, a national security operations centre and incident response team, is not established anywhere in the base (method).

A national cybersecurity architecture is in design with twelve priority sectors defined and a strategic-asset inventory being mapped, on a single trade account with no counterparty primary on file (architecture).

A sabotage attack on 15 June 2026 collapsed 95 per cent of the electricity utility's systems and knocked out prepaid-meter top-up (recovery). Billing and payment were restored in the capital and seven interior centres by 4 August, on the company's own statement, with no forensic or damage-cost report on file.

A regional police operation concluded in August 2025 with 1,209 arrests and about US$97.4m recovered across eighteen countries, none of it attributed to Gabon (operation).
 Mixed, an architecture and an asset inventory are being built while no strategy exists and a utility lost most of its systems
Gambia The cybersecurity strategy for 2022 to 2026 runs out at the end of this year, with no successor, mid-term review or implementation report held.
Full recordIts four pillars are legal and regulatory strengthening, identification of critical information infrastructure, child online safety, and compliance with international standards and treaties, and its training and awareness uptake target was dated by 2023 (strategy).

The same document is the only source for the country's incident-response arrangements, and it describes an institutional design rather than a capability: a national response team operating a cybersecurity centre with a security operations centre inside it, a coordination directorate, a commission, an advisory board, sectoral focal points, sector response teams and a police cybercrime unit.

Nothing held states that any of those bodies is standing, and no incident report, budget line or annual report naming any of them exists.

Ghana The national response team runs a 24-hour service on a profile five years old, against GH¢19.3m of reported losses in 2025.
Full recordThe team is a unit of the cyber security authority with a constituency of eight sectoral response teams and the general public, affiliated to the Budapest Convention; its published profile is five years old and records its own policies as under review, with no incident statistics or annual report held (response team).

An apex coordination committee of eighteen institutions is chaired by the authority, with no terms of reference, membership list, meeting cadence or output held (committee).

Losses of GH¢19.3m are reported for 2025 on 14,425 reports and 3,286 confirmed incidents recorded from January to September, so the loss total and the counts are not on the same period and cannot be combined (white paper); the figures reach the base through a university white paper rather than a published authority series (white paper).

The minister says all central-bank-licensed entities will soon be designated critical information infrastructure, with no instrument, date or designation list published (designation).

Full recordIt is named as an established agency in a summit host profile and no founding instrument for it is held (agency).

No incident statistics, response team, sector designation or audit programme is published, so the agency that publishes the country's cybersecurity statute as an image scan is itself known only by name.

Guinea-Bissau A national cybersecurity strategy sits in a draft decree and the agency to run it is still only committed to.
Full record2025-01-22 — the government presented the results of a WARDIP cybersecurity feasibility study and committed to a National Cybersecurity and Data Protection Agency.

2025-07-10 — the Council of Ministers approved the draft decree for a national cybersecurity strategy. Neither instrument is confirmed gazetted or in force, and both predate the twelve-month window.

Nothing in the window moved either. No computer emergency response team, no cybercrime prosecution, no reported incident and no critical-infrastructure designation appears on this ledger, so readiness is a study, a draft strategy and a promised agency, with no operational capability recorded behind them.

Kenya A national cyber security agency was established as an autonomous state corporation, against 2.36 billion threat events in the quarter to June 2026.
Full recordIts mandate covers critical-infrastructure audit and certification, an operations centre and incident coordination, where cyber duties had been split across four bodies; the inaugural non-executive chair was announced on 24 August 2026, and whether the delegated legislation committee approved before the ministry announced it is contested (chair, approval, contest).

The regulator logged 2,355,938,192 threat events in April-June 2026, down 30.03% on the previous quarter, while web application attacks rose 43.68% to 17,406,495 with state systems and internet service providers the primary targets (regulator report).

The presidential website was defaced in July 2026 with a 5 BTC ransom demand, the second inside twelve months, with cause and attribution undisclosed (defacement, earlier breach).

KSh 457.2m was appropriated for cyber space management plus KSh 382m for programme cybersecurity (appropriation), a EUR 3m European-funded project runs to 36 months (project), and a bilateral partnership is running with no funding, deliverable or date stated (partnership, review).

The regulator estimates cybercrime losses of at least KSh 29bn a year, an estimate rather than a measurement (losses).

A national cybersecurity policy remains on the forward list with nothing drafted, so the agency and the appropriations precede the policy that would direct them (forward list).
 Mixed, a dedicated agency was established while web application attacks on state systems rose 43.68%
Lesotho An interim national response team launched in July 2026 and a security operations centre is being stood up, against no national strategy at the last review.
Full recordLesComCSIRT launched in Maseru on 29 July 2026 and operates on an interim basis, ahead of the statute intended to establish it (launch, account).

A national security operations centre was reported being stood up the same month, having been budgeted as a Government Security Operations Centre in the 2025/26 budget speech; no host, operator or date is published (centre, budget).

A capacity maturity review of March 2023 rated the strategy dimension at start-up and confirmed no official national cybersecurity strategy, and none has been published since (review).

Liberia The cybersecurity strategy is published and its scheduled council, act and operational deliverables are all unheld two years past their dates.
Full recordIts action plan dated deliverables from the fourth quarter of 2024; the cover reads July 2024 for a 2025-2029 period while every running header reads a draft for 2024-2029, and whether the posted file is the adopted final text is not established from the document (strategy).

The national cybersecurity council it scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2024 has no constituting instrument, membership or meeting record; the base holds no record of establishment rather than a stated non-delivery.

A House investigation was ordered in February 2026 into an alleged breach of about 2m citizens' data across the revenue, electricity and insurance bodies, with no findings published; the revenue authority denied the breach, and the probe cited the absence of a national cybersecurity strategy (investigation).

Madagascar A multisectoral national cybersecurity strategy was launched at a December 2025 workshop on a three-month delivery target, and no document has been published.
Full recordThe strategy was launched with a three-month delivery target and financed through the governance and identification programme; members of parliament flagged the 2014 cyber law as needing an update at the same workshop (launch).

The country is separately the only one of five studied to have rejected a foreign safe-city surveillance package (rejection).

The national computer emergency response team is planned, with no establishing instrument, staffing or premises on record (team).

A central bank digital reputation protection platform detected more than 10,000 electronic threats in the first half of 2026, of which 470 were confirmed and removed, with 20 digital identities protected (platform). Mauritius A critical information infrastructure regime came into force in June 2026 across six sectors, a threat-sharing platform is provided for, and a forensic laboratory is funded.
Full recordThe designation regulations are in force across financial and banking, public service, information technology and broadcasting, energy and water, and transport, with a twelve-month compliance window and non-compliance carrying up to twenty-five years' imprisonment and fines up to Rs 2m (regime, framework).

The omnibus bill would empower the central bank to establish a cyber threat intelligence sharing platform and to require financial institutions, and other institutions it specifies, to participate on terms it determines; participation is compulsory and the perimeter is set by the central bank rather than by the cyber authority (platform).

A cyber forensic laboratory is funded at Rs 13m alongside an information-security management system across the administration, with no site, staffing or opening date published (laboratory), against more than 4,000 reported cyber incidents since January 2026 (incidents).

Morocco A national cybersecurity strategy to 2030 sets four pillars and sixty actions, above a security directive in force since 2023 and a response centre issuing advisories.
Full recordThe strategy carries four pillars, eleven objectives, twenty-six initiatives and sixty actions, succeeding the country's first strategy of 2012; its workforce commitments carry no numbers, and the fiftieth-place ranking claimed for it is from a 2020 index edition (strategy, naming).

The base had carried a 2023-dated account of a separate strategy; the full document confirms one instrument rather than two (reconciliation).

The national response centre issued a June 2026 advisory naming Moroccan institutions among about 75,000 devices affected in about 200 countries (advisory); the base carries no dated record of the centre's creation (advisory), and the security directive covering state bodies and vital infrastructures has been in force since January 2023 (directive).

Mozambique A successor cyber strategy is in preparation and a national risk assessment rates nineteen of twenty-nine critical systems very high risk, against a regional index placing the country first among its language group.
Full recordA published study found public-service digital transformation running ahead of the regulatory framework meant to secure it (study).

The 2021-2025 policy ran six pillars and twenty-five projects; its successor is under revision, adding safeguards against artificial-intelligence risks, with no adoption date (revision, predecessor). Its period ended before its successor was drafted.

The incident response team was created in statute and seated in the ICT institute, which named consolidating it the immediate priority; fourteen national specialists hold an international maturity certification (team).

The first national cyber risk assessment, published in May 2025 on a foreign methodology, rates 19 of 29 systems across 18 institutions very high risk, 6 high and 4 moderate; it names no operator and no system, so it publishes a risk distribution without a register of what is at risk (assessment).

A Cyber Security Fund is provided for at articles 67 to 71 of the new statute, financed by a levy on licensed providers and state appropriations; its operating rules are left to a regulation not yet made (fund).

The ICT institute reports the country first among the Portuguese-speaking African countries in the 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index, up on 2020, with capacity development the pillar still to improve; no score or overall rank is given in the account held.

Namibia The national incident response team completed its three-year plan and publishes an annual landscape report, while detected vulnerabilities rose 39.8 per cent in a quarter.
Full recordNamibia launched DigiNam, its national public key infrastructure, with the communications regulator as root certification authority (launch), and the root authority is now live at the top of the country's digital trust chain (report).

The team operates under the regulator, was officially launched in April 2025, completed Phase 3 of its implementation plan in the 2025/26 year, and joined three international and regional bodies during 2025; about US$1m is allocated to cybersecurity for 2026/27 (team, surge, growth).

The 2025 landscape report records 1,701,883 attack events, up about 37 per cent on 2024, and 2,231,970 vulnerabilities, with five ransomware victims (report); the country scored 36.93 on an international cybersecurity index in 2024 against 11.47 in 2020, and the team states in its own text that it cannot fully execute its mandate absent a cybersecurity law (report, inaugural).

Incident management guidelines came into force in April 2026 setting out detection, reporting and coordinated response (guidelines), and detected vulnerabilities and events rose 39.8 and 56.7 per cent in the following quarter (quarter).

South Africa A bulk water utility supplying more than 16 million people was breached and moved to its recovery site, while the national CSIRT publishes no incident statistics.
Full record2026-05 — the national high-performance computing centre suffered unauthorised access and the deployment of crypto-mining malware, attributed in an August written reply to vulnerabilities in the legacy system, with ageing components no longer fully supported by their vendors. A remediation and resilience programme has since run across access controls, privileged-access management, network segmentation, patch management and monitoring, with an internal accountability review still running. No cost, data-loss finding or outage duration is stated and the account is the department's own.

2026-08-04 — the national CSIRT is operating, coordinating and routing reports rather than resolving them, on weekdays from 08:00 to 16:30 with no published out-of-hours capability. As at that date it published no incident statistics, advisories archive, annual report or data downloads.

The national measurement has not been repeated: the 2024 surveys remain the last, on breaches and on the skills gap. One firm's caseload put ransomware dwell time at 18 days in 2025 against 117 in 2024 — faster attack execution rather than improved defence, and not a national series.
