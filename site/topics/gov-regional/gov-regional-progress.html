Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Full recordClause 2.5.2 of the December 2025 memorandum has the two governments intending to negotiate an arrangement for exchanging data on long-term performance and for accountability to the United States Congress, and clause 4.7 makes failure to fulfil the specimen and data sharing arrangements grounds for changing or discontinuing assistance, although the memorandum creates no specimen arrangement (memorandum).

A rights organisation reading the seven signed memoranda puts Ethiopia among five whose text requires unilateral sharing of specimens and pathogen-sequencing data, and all seven as requiring broad audit access (assessment); no arrangement text is published, and a subset posted by the State Department in March 2026 was removed (records complaint).

Gabon A regional health-data roadmap was adopted in August 2026, and an airport passenger-data system entered its operational phase.
Full recordThe roadmap for cross-border health-data sharing among nine Central African states was adopted at a Libreville workshop of 18 to 21 August 2026, setting a six-week deadline for drafting an inter-country convention and a four-week deadline for a formal sharing mechanism, to be put to the health ministers of the seven states yet to sign the protocol (roadmap).

The advance passenger and passenger-name-record system moved into its operational phase on 14 August 2026 under the airport-security committee, with border risk-screening the stated purpose (passenger data).

Neither the roadmap nor the draft convention is published, and the convention terms, the retention rule for passenger data and any authorisation by the data-protection authority are not held.

Gambia A three-month customs roadmap with Senegal was agreed in July 2026 to exchange customs data by interface across three border posts.
Full recordThe roadmap installs a shared customs system at Karang, Keur Ayib and Selety within three months, deploys transit tracking, exchanges customs data by interface once the system is live, replaces transit agents with a single administrative document and reactivates an intelligence platform (roadmap).

It is the only cross-border data-sharing arrangement in the base, and no data-protection provision, retention rule or custodian is stated for the customs data it would exchange.

Ghana A United States health data-sharing agreement was refused in April 2026, and the medical regulator is pressing for an African verification exchange.
Full recordOn the data-protection regulator's account the five-year agreement covered not only health data sets but metadata, dashboards, reporting tools, data models and data dictionaries, gave ten United States entities access requiring no prior Ghanaian approval, required 25 years of citizens' health records, and settled disputes under United States law against US$109m subject to congressional appropriation (refusal).

The terms are the regulator's executive director quoted by a state news agency rather than a published text, and a further reported demand to integrate the national identity database with American systems in real time is attributed to another agency citing unnamed sources.

In the other direction the medical and dental council is pressing African health regulators for shared infrastructure to verify qualifications, licence status and disciplinary records of health workers moving across borders; nothing is built (proposal).

Kenya A United States health framework signed in December 2025 was frozen by injunction, with a final ruling reported due at end-October 2026.
Full recordIt runs five years with US$1,631,470,000 of planned United States support across FY2026 to FY2030 including US$175,000,000 for health data systems (framework); the US$2.5bn headline is that figure plus the country's own pledged increase in domestic health spending, not a competing number (framework, litigation).

The signed text makes audit information subject to Kenyan law and to the data-protection regulator's concurrence, and makes specimen testing and data sharing subject to Kenyan law rather than the template's five-day transfer; the subsidiary data sharing agreement has not been published.

The suspension of the data-transfer component was reversed on appeal in May 2026 (appeal). A rights organisation reading the seven signed memoranda places this country outside both the three committing to hand over data without explicit privacy safeguards and the five requiring unilateral specimen sharing (assessment).

In the other direction the country is one of the first three selected to implement shared cross-border rails for identity, payments and trusted data exchange, chosen on political commitment, regulatory alignment, technical capacity and system maturity; no national implementation plan, budget or timetable is published (continental pilot).

The data protection commissioner issued a guidance note on cross-border transfers in April 2026.

Lesotho The only cross-border arrangement on file is the health data-sharing instrument with the United States, whose text is withheld.
Full recordAfrican governments pushed back during 2026 on United States health agreements over data and power, this country's among them (reporting).

The data-sharing agreements attached to its own agreement have not been published, and the base holds no adequacy decision, transfer safeguard or domestic authorisation covering them (complaint).

Liberia A rights organisation's reading puts the country among three committing to hand over any data requested for an American audit without explicit privacy safeguards.
Full recordIt is also among two whose data-sharing arrangement is deferred to an unpublished appendix, and outside the five whose text requires unilateral specimen and pathogen-sequencing sharing; the signed memorandum carries no specimen clause (assessment).

Madagascar Transfers are governed by the 2014 law, whose authorising commission became effective in 2025 and whose regional context changed in June 2026.
Full recordThe statute of 2014 governs transfers of personal data abroad; article 20 makes them subject to authorisation (statute).

The commission that would grant such authorisations was confirmed effectively established in March 2026 (report).

Negotiations on an enhanced economic partnership agreement with the European Union concluded in June 2026 with a digital trade chapter and updated personal-data safeguards (summary). The chapter's own text is not held, and no transfer authorisation, adequacy finding or refusal has been published.

Full recordMalawi joined Mozambique, South Africa, Zambia and Zimbabwe in adopting a joint roadmap and action plan for customs-to-customs data exchange at a meeting in Pretoria on 24-26 August 2026 (roadmap).

Sections 38 to 40 of the Data Protection Act 2024 establish the cross-border transfer regime, and the authority's compliance guideline and checklist covers the obligations it puts on a controller. No adequacy decision, authorisation or refusal under it appears anywhere on file.

2025-12 - a continental validation workshop worked on cross-border data flow frameworks at African Union level. That is drafting above the state, not ratification or transposition by it, and nothing on file records either.

A regime whose gatekeeping step has never been exercised on the record is a rule and not yet a practice.

Mauritius Transfers run under the 2017 Act and the Convention 108+ protocol ratified in 2020; the European adequacy assessment is open.
Full recordThe Act of 2017 governs transfer abroad (Act), and the amending protocol to the Council of Europe data convention was ratified in September 2020, among the first ratifications outside Europe (ratification).

The national data strategy of January 2026 sets cross-border flows as a workstream (strategy).

Cabinet's February 2026 decisions record the social register being used for a connectivity measure rather than any transfer arrangement (Cabinet). No transfer authorisation, refusal or adequacy finding is held.

Morocco Continental data-exchange guidelines drafted under Moroccan chairmanship remain unpublished, a ledger-based pilot names the country, and Convention 108 has been ratified since 2019.
Full recordEleven member states convened in Rabat in July 2025 to draft the continent's first cross-border data exchange guidelines under Moroccan chairmanship of the agency council, and no guidelines were on record a year later (drafting).

Morocco is one of three pilot states for a cross-border data exchange on a distributed-ledger reference architecture owned by the continental trade secretariat, with no Moroccan implementing body, start date or budget named (pilot).

Convention 108 was ratified during 2019; the Council of Europe's 2012 opinion named two defects in the domestic law, its exclusion of public-safety and law-enforcement processing and a definition of sensitive data omitting sexual life and criminal convictions, and the base holds no record that either was closed (opinion).

Mozambique Mozambique joined a five-country roadmap for customs-to-customs data exchange (roadmap).
Full recordMozambique joined Malawi, South Africa, Zambia and Zimbabwe in adopting a joint roadmap and action plan for customs-to-customs data exchange at a meeting in Pretoria on 24-26 August 2026 (roadmap).

The agreement was signed at Washington in December 2025 and published as a treaty record (agreement).

Specimens, sequencing data and digital sequence information are to be shared within eight working days of a request, subject to approval by the national ethics, biosafety and security authority which shall not be unreasonably withheld, with advance consent to onward sharing with United States entities on after-the-fact notice and no cap on recipients (assessment, memorandum).

The terms are looser than a comparable country's on transfer time and tighter on consent. It is the country's only published cross-border data transfer instrument, and it runs in a jurisdiction with no data protection statute (complaint).

Namibia The government rejected a United States health data and specimen sharing proposal on sovereignty grounds (report).
Full recordThe government rejected a United States proposal to share health data and specimens, citing sovereignty (report), while funding talks ran against a deadline (report).

The ICT minister tabled the African Union Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection in Parliament for ratification in 2018, and the Union's own status list records the ratification (tabling). The domestic statute the Convention calls for is still a Bill.

The two ICT ministries signed a framework at Kasane in August 2026 covering digital transformation, cybersecurity, affordable connectivity and skills development, six months after the two countries' operators signed their own memorandum on cross-border connectivity (government, operators).

Regionally the regulator sits on the communications association's legal and policy committee, with artificial intelligence on its agenda.

Ratified without a domestic statute is the whole position: nothing on this base governs a transfer of personal data out of the country.

Full recordThe memorandum was signed in December 2025 with planned United States support of US$1,953,530,560 against Nigeria's US$2,975,634,499 (memorandum); a rights organisation's reading of seven signed memoranda puts Nigeria among five whose text requires unilateral sharing of specimens and pathogen-sequencing data and among two whose data-sharing arrangement is deferred to an unpublished appendix, with all seven requiring broad audit access (memorandum, assessment, complaint).
