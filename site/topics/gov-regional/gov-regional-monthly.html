Trans-Saharan integration projects with Niger

L'Algérie et le Niger lancent l'exploitation de la dorsale transsaharienne à fibre optique — Algérie Eco (reporting Algérie Presse Service), 6 October 2026 Niger

Niger-Algérie : lancement à Assamaka de l'exploitation de la dorsale transsaharienne à fibre optique — Agence Nigérienne de Presse, 6 October 2026 Niger

Niger and Algeria launched operation of the fibre link between In Guezzam and the Arlit-Agadez region at a ceremony at Assamaka, on the border, on 6 October, chaired by the two communications ministers. Algeria's account gives a starting capacity of 100 Gbps.

Smart Africa cross-border data exchange

Smart Africa moves cross-border digital ID interoperability toward live testing — Biometric Update, 19 October 2026 GhanaRwandaAfrica

Benin, Ghana and Rwanda have connected their national identity systems through the Smart Africa Data Exchange and are moving toward real-world testing of cross-border ID verification, Biometric Update reported on 19 October 2026. Pilots are planned for Benin-Rwanda air travel and Ghana-Benin land crossings. No date for live testing has been announced.

Benin, Ghana and Rwanda Take Major Step Towards Africa’s Single Digital Market Through Cross-Border Digital Identity Interoperability — Smart Africa, 20 October 2026 GhanaRwandaAfrica

Benin, Ghana and Rwanda completed Phase 1 of the Smart Africa Data Exchange cross-border digital identity pilot, announced on 20 October 2026. The three national systems were connected and verified identities end to end using synthetic data. Phase 2 prepares a controlled operational pilot for border identity checks, subject to national approvals and security assessments.

Rwanda, Ghana, Benin test cross-border digital identity verification — The New Times, 22 October 2026 RwandaGhana

Rwanda, Ghana and Benin completed the first phase of a Smart Africa pilot linking their national ID systems through the Smart Africa Data Exchange, verifying identities end to end with synthetic data in a test environment. Smart Africa said the exchange passes verification requests between national systems without storing records centrally, and does not change visa or immigration rules.

Cameroon

Yaounde Declaration on e-Governance and Artificial Intelligence in Africa

Déclaration de Yaoundé sur l'e-Gouvernance et l'Intelligence Artificielle en Afrique — E-Gov'A (Smart Click Africa and Digital Business Africa), 17 October 2026 Africa

The Yaoundé Declaration, adopted on 17 October 2026 at the close of the E-Gov'A conference, is a non-binding statement by governments, institutions and companies under a "Local First, African First" approach to e-government and AI. It sets an aim of hosting all critical or sovereign public data locally by 2030 and creates an Observatory of e-Government and AI in Africa.

Cameroon summit calls for interoperable DPI and sovereign infrastructure in Africa — Biometric Update, 23 October 2026

A second account of the Yaoundé Declaration, adding its call for interoperable digital identity, payments and data-exchange systems built on open standards, and for a once-only principle under which citizens are not asked again for information an administration already holds.

Yaoundé Declaration Targets 100% Local Hosting of Critical Government Data by 2030 — Business in Cameroon, 23 October 2026 Africa

The Yaoundé Declaration on E-Governance and Artificial Intelligence in Africa, adopted when the E-Gov'A conference closed on 17 October 2026, asks African governments to host all classified critical or sovereign public data inside their own borders by 2030, and where domestic capacity is lacking to prefer another African country over providers off the continent. The organisers, Smart Click Africa and Digital Business Africa, are to run an African Observatory for E-Government and AI; the hosting target is a recommendation and binds no government.

They decided this at their fourth extraordinary session in Libreville on 20-21 October 2026. The session also adopted a resolution on running the regional ICT Observatory.

The Central Africa Internet Governance Forum, held from 13 to 15 October 2026 with ECCAS, the AU Commission, UNECA and the ITU taking part, ended with a call for closer digital cooperation across the sub-region. A second cohort of the regional internet governance school was launched alongside it.

Numérique : clôture de l’édition 2026 du Forum sur la Gouvernance de l’Internet-Afrique Centrale - Dari Infos — Dari Infos, 15 October 2026 Central Africa

The 2026 Central Africa Internet Governance Forum closed in N'Djamena on 15 October after four days, with the adoption of the Declaration of N'Djamena. Its commitments include a joint CEEAC and ARTAC platform against cyberattacks by August 2027, fibre interconnection and laws on AI.

UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents

Ghana to host UN convention signing on digital cargo documents on October 26 — MyJoyOnline, 16 October 2026

Ghana will host the signing ceremony for the UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra on 26 October 2026. The Ghana Shippers' Authority says the convention gives electronic bills of lading and similar documents the legal standing of paper ones, but that the benefits depend on ratification and readiness.

26 countries sign Accra Convention to boost trade facilitation, digitalisation — MyJoyOnline, 28 October 2026

Twenty-six states signed the UN Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents in Accra on 26 October 2026, with Ghana first. Known as the Accra Convention, it gives electronic cargo documents the legal effect of paper. Road, rail and air documents can serve as documents of title. Ghana's trade minister said it will ratify; the Convention needs ten ratifications.

EAC Protocol on ICT Networks

Uganda MPs push EALA to pressure EAC states on ICT protocol ratification — Horn Economic Review, 2 November 2026 East Africa

Uganda's parliamentary Committee on Foreign Affairs said only Uganda and Rwanda have both signed and ratified the EAC Protocol on ICT Networks, Horn Economic Review reported on 2 November 2026. The protocol was adopted in 2013. The committee asked Uganda's members of the East African Legislative Assembly to press the other partner states to ratify it. It covers infrastructure sharing, cross-border connectivity, cybersecurity and common ICT policy.

COMESA Regional Digital Inclusion Strategy and Action Plan

PRE - EVENT PRESS STATEMENT - Digitalization in Eastern and Southern Africa (IDEA) — COMESA IDEA Programme, 30 October 2026 East AfricaSouthern Africa

COMESA announced on 30 October 2026 a regional meeting in Chongwe, Zambia, from 3 to 5 November. Member states will review a draft regional digital inclusion strategy, action plan and situational analysis. The work is funded by the World Bank under the IDEA programme.

Zambia: First Regional Validation Workshop on Digital Inclusion — Africa Newsroom (Ministry of Technology and Science, Zambia), 4 November 2026

Zambia, COMESA and the World Bank held the first regional validation workshop of the IDEA programme in Chongwe on 3-5 November 2026. Member states reviewed a draft regional digital inclusion strategy and action plan; validation is not reported. Zambia put its fibre backbone at over 42,000 km, against about 15,000 km in 2021.
