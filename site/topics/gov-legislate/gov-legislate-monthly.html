Algeria

Digitalisation law

الرقمنة في الجزائر.. شفافية وعصرنة (Digitalisation in Algeria: transparency and modernisation) — الشعب أونلاين (Echaab Online), 20 September 2026

A dossier in Algeria's Echaab newspaper on 20 September 2026 reviews the High Commission for Digitalisation's work since April 2024: a national digital transformation strategy published in May 2025, a national cloud and certified data centres, the IRIES secure network linking ministries, cybersecurity and network operations centres, and the national digital services centre opened in July 2026. It says a digitalisation law is in the final stages of study.

Draft 2027 finance law, e-payment and digital-platform provisions

Projet de loi de finances 2027: Des avantages fiscaux pour booster le E-paiement — L'Echo d'Algérie, 11 October 2026

Algeria's draft 2027 finance law proposes a 10% tax reduction until the end of 2028 for flat-rate and professional taxpayers who take at least half their receipts electronically. It would also require resident and non-resident e-commerce platforms to register with the tax authority and declare transactions yearly, on pain of a DA 1 million fine. The bill is not yet adopted.

L’Algérie prévoit de soumettre les plateformes numériques étrangères à de nouvelles obligations de TVA en 2027 — Algerian Business, 12 October 2026

Algeria's draft 2027 finance law proposes that foreign digital platforms selling services to customers in Algeria register with the tax authority, keep records for ten years and declare and pay VAT quarterly. Non-compliance would carry a fine of 5 million dinars after 30 days' notice and possible suspension of access to the platform. The provision is a draft, not yet adopted.

General authorisation regime for cloud services

Communiqué — retrait définitif de l’autorisation de cloud computing de DATAGIX — Autorité de régulation de la poste et des communications électroniques (ARPCE), 22 October 2026

Algeria's telecoms regulator ARPCE definitively withdrew, from 22 October 2026, the general authorisation it issued to DATAGIX in February 2024 to provide cloud hosting and data storage. The regulator cited breaches of the rules in force and told the company to contact it without delay.

Angola

National statistical system law

INE inicia recolha de contribuições para nova Lei Estatística — Angop (Angola Press Agency), 29 September 2026

Angola's statistics institute opened a public consultation, running to 2 October, on a revision of the national statistics law and a new national strategy for the development of statistics.

Cybersecurity bill

Nova Lei da Cibersegurança impõe registo e notificação de incidentes a operadores — PTI, 2 October 2026

Angola's cybersecurity law has been published as Lei n.º 9/26 of 28 September and is in force, revoking the 2017 law. Operators of critical infrastructure, essential services, electronic communications, data centres and cloud services must register with the National Cybersecurity Centre within 180 days, set up incident response teams and notify significant incidents to CERT.ao.

Electronic signature and certification regime

João Lourenço recebe autorização para estabelecer regime da assinatura electrónica — PTI, 8 October 2026

Angola's Law 12/26 of 2 October authorises the President to legislate the regime for electronic signatures and digital certification and the rules of the national public key infrastructure, within 90 days.

National cybersecurity centre

MINTTICS apresenta Estratégia Nacional de Cibersegurança no CyberSecur Summit 2026 — PTI, 12 October 2026

Angola's telecommunications ministry presented the National Cybersecurity Strategy, approved by Presidential Decree 256/25 of 3 December 2025, at the CyberSecur Summit in Luanda on 12 October 2026. Policy director Hecdiantro Mena said a national commission will be formed to prepare, within 180 days, the conditions for setting up the National Cybersecurity Centre. No budget is given.

Benin

Digital Code Article 550 on harassment through electronic communications

CPJ letter to President Wadagni: press freedom concerns in Benin — Committee to Protect Journalists, 1 September 2026

The Committee to Protect Journalists wrote to President Wadagni 100 days into his term asking that Benin's Digital Code stop being used against the press and that its Article 550 on electronic harassment be reformed. The letter cites the continued detention of two online journalists, prison sentences over Facebook posts after the December 2025 coup attempt, and repeated suspensions imposed by the media regulator HAAC.

Loi 2017-20 portant Code du numerique

Société : Votre patron peut-il lire vos messages WhatsApp professionnels ? — La Nation, 5 September 2026

Benin's La Nation explains how the country's Digital Code limits employers' access to staff WhatsApp messages. Owning a work phone or computer does not give an employer the right to read personal conversations; monitoring of accounts used only for work must serve a work purpose and follow the code's personal data rules, including article 379.

Authorisation of online media by the audiovisual regulator

Régulation des médias en ligne: 89 organes autorisés par la Haac — La Nation, 2 October 2026

Benin's media regulator HAAC signed conventions with 89 online media outlets in Cotonou on 1 October 2026, completing its authorisation process for websites offering online audiovisual and written-press services. The outlets now operate under the information and communication code and the digital code.

Burkina Faso

Decree on consular civil-status powers and civil status of citizens abroad

Conseil des ministres n26 du 10 septembre 2026 — Radiodiffusion Television du Burkina, 10 September 2026

Burkina Faso's Council of Ministers, meeting in Bobo-Dioulasso on 10 September 2026, adopted a decree on the civil-status powers of diplomatic and consular agents, replacing a 1963 decree. It adds deputy heads of mission as civil-status officers, allows delegation, including exceptionally to honorary consuls, and introduces mobile consular missions for citizens living far from a consulate.

Burundi

Ordonnance ministerielle n 540/906/2026 on SIM registration

Burundi SIM registration process faces challenges, scams persist — Connecting Africa, 2 October 2026

Burundi's mandatory SIM and eSIM registration, begun in mid-June, was about 70 per cent complete on the regulator's count. Users report long queues, few service points and slow systems, and scams continue; operators must keep customer data for at least five years.

Cameroon

Loi n. 2010/012 on cybercrime and cybersecurity

Cybersecurity as Industrial Policy: Protecting Cameroon's Digital Economy — Nkafu Policy Institute, 4 September 2026

A Nkafu Policy Institute brief argues that Cameroon should treat cybersecurity as industrial policy under its 2020–2030 development strategy. It cites a 156% rise in cyberattacks between 2020 and 2023, over $45m in fraud losses, 333,930 vulnerability-exploiting attacks in 2024, and 32,500 cybercrime legal requests handled by the ICT agency ANTIC in 2025. It describes the 2010 cyber law as outdated.

Public directory of licensed telecommunications operators

Cameroon tightens grip on telecoms — ITWeb Africa, 17 September 2026

Cameroon's Telecommunications Regulatory Board is compiling a public directory of licensed operators and has told every operator to complete an identification form on its website. The board says the directory will let consumers verify which companies are licensed, expose fraudulent operators and help agencies identify legitimate players.

Cameroon TRB Updates Telecom Operators Directory to Strengthen Transparency — Tech Africa News, 17 September 2026

Cameroon's telecommunications regulator gave operators until 30 September 2026 to file identification forms for a public directory that is to separate licensed and compliant providers from unlicensed ones. Nothing is yet published and no publication date for the directory itself is stated.

Central African Republic

Statistics law and its replacement

ICASEES : Evaluation à mi-parcours de la deuxième Stratégie Nationale de Développement de la Statistique — ICASEES, 10 October 2026

The Central African Republic's statistics institute ICASEES held a mid-term review of the second national statistics strategy (2024–2028) in Bangui from 7 to 9 October 2026, funded entirely by the UN Development Programme. The strategy, adopted in August 2023 with a budget of nearly 60 billion CFA francs, is to be updated; a bill replacing the 2001 statistics law has passed technical validation and awaits review by the official-texts commission.

Chad

CEMAC payment systems regulation as Chad's operative payments law

Instructions, Circulaires et Règlements — Banque des États de l'Afrique Centrale — Banque des États de l'Afrique Centrale (BEAC), 2 September 2026

The Bank of Central African States' register of payment-system instruments lists the CEMAC rules that apply in Chad: Regulation 03 of 21 December 2016 on payment systems, means and incidents, and Regulation 04 of 21 December 2018 on payment services. It also lists a 2018 instruction on interoperability and a 2025 instruction adopting the ISO 20022 standard for payments.

Loi 026/PR/2019 organising statistical activity

Politique d'accès — Archive Nationale des Données du Tchad (ANAD) — Institut National de la Statistique, des Études Économiques et Démographiques (INSEED), République du Tchad, 2 September 2026

Chad's national statistics institute, INSEED, sets the access conditions for microdata in its National Data Archive (ANAD). Users must keep respondents anonymous under the 2019 statistics law, may not copy or pass the data to others without INSEED's written consent, must cite a prescribed source line and must send INSEED two copies of any resulting report.

TEXTES RÉGLEMENTAIRES — INSEED-TCHAD — Institut National de la Statistique, des Études Économiques et Démographiques (INSEED), République du Tchad, 2 September 2026

Chad's national statistics institute, INSEED, lists the legal basis of the national statistical system: Law No. 026/PR/2019 of June 2019, which makes INSEED the system's central body with legal personality and management autonomy, and four application decrees of July 2019. The system also includes a statistics development fund, a national statistics council, sectoral statistical units and a statistics school.

Draft Labour Code covering digital platform workers

Travail : le Tchad change les règles entre employé et employeur — Le N'Djam Post, 11 September 2026

Chad's Council of Ministers adopted on 3 September 2026 a draft Labour Code replacing the 1996 law. Platform workers are presumed to be employees where at least two criteria of subordination are met, platforms must be more open about the algorithms that direct work, and social security cover is extended. The draft also raises the end-of-contract payment on fixed-term contracts from 5% to 7%.

Congo

Law No. 8-2001 on freedom of information and communication

Sicom 2026 : nécessité de faire progresser le cadre juridique des médias au Congo — Les Dépêches de Brazzaville, 17 September 2026

Opening the first Sicom media fair in Brazzaville on 17 September 2026, the Republic of Congo's prime minister, Anatole Collinet Makosso, said the 2001 law on freedom of information and communication must be updated for social networks, disinformation and artificial intelligence. No draft bill or timetable was announced.

Sicom 2026 : un bilan et des engagements pour l'avenir — Les Dépêches de Brazzaville, 21 September 2026

Congo's first information, communication and media fair closed at Kintélé on 19 September 2026 with 23 resolutions from participants. They call for rewriting the three core media laws to take account of digital broadcasting, online platforms and AI, and cover digital content, advertising and tax. These are recommendations from the profession, not government decisions.

Finance law 2026, article 13: registration and declaration of money transfers

Congo/Finances : Jean Claude Bazebi déterminé à assurer la transparence dans le secteur des transferts de fonds — Agence Congolaise d’information (ACI), 7 October 2026

The Republic of the Congo's money transfer regulator, ARTF, said on 5 October 2026 it will enforce article 13 of the 2026 finance law, which requires every money transfer operator to register on its digital platform and declare all transfers. Operators refusing to connect face a fine of 20 million FCFA. Electronic terminals will go first to Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire.

Transfert de fonds : l’usage des terminaux de paiement électronique entrera en vigueur dès janvier 2027 — Les Dépêches de Brazzaville (ADIAC), 8 October 2026

Congo's money-transfer regulator ARTF will require every money-transfer operator to use electronic payment terminals from January 2027, as the finance law provides, Les Dépêches de Brazzaville reported on 8 October 2026. The terminals record each transaction in real time for tax purposes; 200 of 1,000 ordered were shown to about a hundred transfer and exchange operators in Brazzaville.

Cote d'Ivoire

Net neutrality guidelines

Consultation publique relative à l’élaboration des lignes directrices en vue de la mise en œuvre de la neutralité du Net en Côte d’Ivoire (contribution Moov Africa CI, publiée par l'ARTCI) — ARTCI, 1 September 2026

Côte d'Ivoire's regulator ARTCI consulted on draft net-neutrality guidelines from 6 July to 6 August 2026 and published operators' responses in September. The draft allows zero rating only where it is unpaid and non-discriminatory, and would have internet providers declare their traffic-management practices each year; Moov Africa argued that strict non-discrimination would slow 5G services.

Banking regulation bill bringing financial technology firms into the perimeter

Reforme bancaire : La Cote d'Ivoire redessine la frontiere avec les fintechs — CADRECO, 10 September 2026

Cote d'Ivoire's banking reform folds digital finance into a wider legal architecture in which competition between banks, payment institutions, electronic money issuers, fintechs, Islamic finance and microfinance turns on licences, their obligations and their cost. The Council of Ministers adopted two bills in April as part of it.

Revision of the 2013 Postal Code (Loi 2013-702)

Côte d’Ivoire-AIP/ L’ARTCI mise sur l’innovation pour transformer le réseau postal en levier de développement — Agence Ivoirienne de Presse (AIP), 9 October 2026

The director general of Côte d'Ivoire's telecoms regulator, ARTCI, Lakoun Ouattara, said on World Post Day, 9 October 2026, that the postal network should adapt to e-commerce, digital addressing and last-mile delivery. He said the 2013 Postal Code, law 2013-702, is being revised to cover new uses and operators. No timetable for the revision is given.

DR Congo

Ordonnances-lois organising statistical activity

Le Président Tshisekedi fixe les conditions d'organisation de l'activité statistique en RDC - ACP — Agence Congolaise de Presse (ACP), 12 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's President Félix Tshisekedi signed an ordinance-law organising statistical activity, the 98th Council of Ministers reported on 11 September 2026. It governs the production, dissemination and archiving of official statistics and national censuses, names the Institut national de la statistique as principal statistical authority, and applies its confidentiality rules at once to operations under way.

RDC : le gouvernement adopte le projet de loi autorisant la ratification du nouveau cadre de l'activité statistique — Opinion Info, 12 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Council of Ministers on 11 September 2026 adopted a bill to ratify the ordinance-law organising official statistics, submitted by the planning minister. The law sets principles of professional independence and statistical confidentiality, and bars use of data collected for statistics for control, prosecution or sanctions outside cases the law provides.

RDC : le Sénat ratifie à l’unanimité l’ordonnance-loi portant organisation de l’activité statistique — Actualite.cd, 22 October 2026

The DRC Senate on 21 October 2026 ratified ordonnance-loi 26/015 of 7 September 2026 organising statistical activity, by 73 votes to none of its 109 members. The 95-article law gives the national statistics institute the power to issue the statistical visa, puts a council chaired by the Prime Minister at the head of the system, penalises breaches of statistical secrecy and puts the system's budget at about USD 192 million.

Private member's bill on non-consensual diffusion of intimate content

Violence numérique : une proposition de loi de protection des victimes annoncée à Kinshasa - ACP — Agence Congolaise de Presse (ACP), 12 September 2026

Christelle Vuanga, a member of the Democratic Republic of Congo's National Assembly, announced she will table a bill this parliamentary session amending the penal code and the 2023 digital code to protect victims of non-consensual sharing of intimate images. She proposes prison terms of five to ten years with fines, and heavier penalties where blackmail or minors are involved.

Décret 23/13 creating the successor regulator ARPTIC

[Enquête]- ARPTC, un policier illégal et défaillant — Ouragan.cd, 14 September 2026

A joint press investigation calls DR Congo's telecoms regulator ARPTC unlawful and ineffective, noting that the successor authority created by a 2023 decree has never been installed. It details failings on service quality and equipment approval.

Private member's bill on the protection of minors in the digital environment

Assemblée nationale: le député Alfred Dibandi initie une proposition de loi relative à la protection des mineurs dans l'environnement numérique — Actualite.cd, 21 September 2026

Alfred Dibandi Nzondomyo, a national deputy in the Democratic Republic of Congo, filed a private member's bill on protecting minors in the digital environment on 21 September 2026. He said it complements the Digital Code and child-protection law, with rules by age band for under-6s, under-11s and under-15s. The bill's text has not been published.

Bill amending the Code du numérique on minors' access to social platforms

DRC Senate Considers Social Media Ban for Children Under 13 — We Are Tech, 1 October 2026

The DR Congo Senate declared admissible on 28 September 2026 a bill by Senator Yvan Kazadi Kankonde amending the 2023 Digital Code to bar children under 13 from opening social media accounts. Those aged 13 to 15 would need verifiable parental consent, and platforms would have to verify age. The bill has gone to committee and is not law.

Bill revising the public procurement law

DRC Moves to Expand Public Procurement Access for MSMEs and Start-Ups — Ecofin Agency, 1 October 2026

The DRC government adopted on 30 September 2026 a bill to replace the 2010 public procurement law (Law No. 10/010), presented by Budget Minister Adolphe Muzito. It would widen the use of paperless procedures and open public contracts to small businesses and start-ups, and responds to heavy use of direct awards and weak contract monitoring.

Financial Regulatory Authority technology sandbox

Egypt's FRA Approves Two New Fintech Projects for Regulatory Sandbox — Tech Africa News, 1 September 2026

Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority granted preliminary approval for two further projects to enter its regulatory sandbox for non-banking financial technology.

One-time-password identity verification in consumer and MSME finance

الرقابة المالية تلزم شركات التمويل باستخدام رمز التحقق OTP خلال شهرين — Maspero (National Media Authority), 14 September 2026

Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority issued Decision 2735 of 2026, giving consumer finance and small-business finance companies two months to introduce one-time password verification of customers. A code must be sent to the customer's verified phone number when a finance contract is signed and when the finance is drawn, and the lender must keep a record of it.

Joint prior oversight of non-bank financial applications

بروتوكول بين "الأعلى للإعلام" و"الرقابة المالية" لتعزيز رقابة تطبيقات أسواق المالية غير المصرفية — Youm7, 16 September 2026

Egypt's Supreme Council for Media Regulation and the Financial Regulatory Authority signed a protocol on 16 September 2026 on oversight of apps and platforms in the non-bank financial markets. Before licensing any app that uses the names of FRA-supervised firms or carries on such activity, the council must obtain the authority's technical opinion, due within 15 days.

Comprehensive digital-safety law

«تنظيم الاتصالات»: حجب منصات التواصل في مصر حال مخالفة ضوابط حماية الأطفال — Fintechgate, 18 September 2026

Egypt's National Telecom Regulatory Authority said social media platforms that fail to comply with new child-protection rules could be suspended and blocked in Egypt, whatever their size. Its vice-chairman, Mohamed Ibrahim, said platforms have three months to comply, the rules cover existing accounts of under-15s, and age checks must collect only necessary data.

رئيس اتصالات النواب: قانون السلامة الرقمية أولوية بدور الانعقاد الثاني — Youm7, 4 October 2026

Ahmed Badawi, newly elected chair of the Egyptian House of Representatives' ICT committee, said a digital safety law tops the committee's agenda for the new session. He said parliament and government coordinated the bill over nine sessions with 14 ministers and that most of its articles regulate children's use of social media. Amendments to the information technology law are also planned.

Digital safety bill

اتصالات النواب: الانتهاء من قانون السلامة الرقمية خلال شهر — Al-Ahram, 5 October 2026

Egypt's parliamentary ICT committee chair Ahmed Badawi said the committee will open its new session with the digital safety bill and expects it finished within a month. The bill protects children on social media and adds rules for adults on online betting, defamation and disinformation; the committee held six hearings with 14 ministers and agency heads.

Moratorium on new fintech consumer-finance licences (FRA Resolution 43 of 2026)

Egypt’s Souhoola Secures FRA Approval for Fintech-Based Consumer Finance — Tech Africa News, 22 October 2026

Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority licensed Souhoola to provide fintech-based consumer finance through V-Lens, covering electronic identification and verification, e-KYC, electronic contracts and digital record-keeping. The application was approved because it predated FRA Resolution No. 43 of February 2026, which suspended new fintech consumer-finance licence applications for a year.

Equatorial Guinea

Taxation of foreign digital platforms

El Gobierno exige a Meta tributar en Guinea Ecuatorial y ordena centralizar la comercialización de Starlink en ORTEL — AHORAEG, 9 September 2026

Equatorial Guinea's vice-president, Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangue, gave the state operator ORTEL sole legal authority to sell Starlink satellite internet, under the state secretariat for artificial intelligence, and ordered contracts made outside official channels cancelled. He also tasked a commission with discussing payments to the state with Meta, which he said has done business in the country without paying.

Guinée équatoriale : contribution des plateformes numériques en discussion — wearetech.africa, 10 September 2026

Equatorial Guinea intends a mechanism letting the state capture part of the revenue large digital platforms generate in its market, a question sharpened where companies operate from abroad. The vice-president asked in Malabo for talks to be opened with one such platform and with comparable alternatives.

Press law revision

La Camara de los Diputados ha aprobado tres proyectos de leyes presentados por el Gobierno — Pagina Oficial del Gobierno de la Republica de Guinea Ecuatorial, 11 September 2026

Equatorial Guinea's Chamber of Deputies unanimously approved three government bills on 11 September 2026: reforms of the Mining and Hydrocarbons laws and a new press freedom law. The mining reform creates a national geological service, a mining registry and cadastre, a national mining management platform and a digital geological registry, with licences awarded by international tender as the general rule. The press law replaces a 1997 law that does not cover digital journalism.

La nueva Ley de Libertad de Prensa avanza al Pleno del Senado tras el consenso alcanzado por la comisión — AHORAEG, 8 October 2026

An Equatorial Guinea Senate committee approved by consensus its report on a new Press Freedom and Journalism Bill and sent it to the full Senate. The bill gives digital and audiovisual journalists the same protections and duties, sets a right of access to public information with deadlines and appeals, separates editorial oversight from spectrum management by ORTEL and creates a college of journalists.

Satellite broadband sales routed through the regulator

Equatorial Guinea: Starlink sales to run via ORTEL — Tech.Africa, 15 September 2026

All satellite internet sales are to run through the telecommunications regulator, under the supervision of the artificial intelligence state secretariat, and contracts citizens obtained outside official channels are to be cancelled, decided at a working session on 9 September.

Ley 7/2024 on social media and computer crime

El despacho que representa a S.E. Nguema Obiang Mangue explica las acciones contra Diario Rombe y otras tres personas — Oficina de Informacion y Prensa de Guinea Ecuatorial, 17 September 2026

Lawyers for Equatorial Guinea's vice-president Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangue said they lodged four criminal complaints at a Malabo court on 16 September 2026 against four people, including Mocache Massoko of the online paper Diario Rombe. The complaints rest on articles 2 and 60 of Law 7/2024 on social media and cybercrime; article 2 is invoked to reach conduct outside the country.

Reform of the Ley de Prensa y Medios Audiovisuales (1997)

El proyecto de ley de comunicación prohibirá contenidos que inciten a la violencia y vulneren los derechos de menores - Diario Bata — Diario Bata, 22 October 2026

Equatorial Guinea's draft communication law would ban content inciting violence, hatred or crime, or harming minors, across print, broadcast and digital media, and require streaming, podcast and platform providers to register and answer for what they carry. Broadcast licensees would have to give the regulator free monitoring of their output; the bill's legislative stage is not stated.

Eritrea

Bank of Eritrea Legal Notice No. 134/2026 on cash holdings

Eritrea's new cash restrictions — Martin Plaut, 18 September 2026

An Eritrean human-rights group reports that a central bank legal notice of 9 September caps the cash an individual may keep beyond 15 days at 25,000 nakfa, with higher limits for businesses, following a June order to deposit cash holdings. It notes that the country has no generally available cash machines or cards and thin branch access outside the main towns.

Eswatini

Immigration Bill traveller vetting system

New proposed law to screen foreigners entering country — Times of Eswatini, 10 September 2026

Eswatini's Immigration Bill would set up an information system to vet foreign nationals before entry and block those who fail requirements or are judged a risk. It was discussed at a consultation between Parliament and stakeholders in Ezulwini. The workshop heard that implementation would cost about E6 million.

Critical Infrastructure Protection Bill

Senators back E5m fines, 10-year jail terms for critical infrastructure offences — Independent News Eswatini, 19 October 2026

Eswatini's Senate adopted the ICT portfolio committee's report on the Critical Infrastructure Bill No. 16 of 2025 on 19 October 2026. It backs fines of up to E5 million for companies, and E1 million or up to 10 years' imprisonment for individuals, for offences against telecom towers, fibre and energy lines.

Ethiopia

National Data Management Proclamation (draft)

Council of Ministers Refers Key Bills to HPR on Governance, Data, Power and Defense — Ethiopian News Agency (ENA), 28 September 2026

The state news agency's account of the same Council session names the bill the National Data Governance Draft Proclamation, referred to parliament with the Council's unanimous backing.

Ethiopia's Council of Ministers Advances New Legal Frameworks for State Institutions, Data Sovereignty, Defence and Economy — Fana Media Corporation S.C., 28 September 2026

Ethiopia's Council of Ministers unanimously sent a draft national data proclamation to parliament, framed around data security and sovereignty, data sharing and interoperability across government, and support for AI research.

Gabon

Draft legal texts for digital transformation

Gabon Begins Validation of Digital Transformation Legal Framework — Tech Africa News, 2 October 2026

Gabon began a validation review of draft legal texts for digital transformation at Nkok on 1 October, to run about ten days. The texts cover digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, digital services and the institutional framework, under the World Bank-financed Gabon Digital programme.

Ordonnance n 0011/PR/2026 on social-media use through digital platforms

Gabon : amendes jusqu'à 50 millions et peines de prison pour les abus commis sur les réseaux sociaux — Gabon Media Time, 18 October 2026

Gabon promulgated by decree on 5 October 2026 a law ratifying its ordinance on social-network use, Gabon Media Time reported. Hosts or editors who fail to fight illicit content face a year in prison and fines of FCFA 5 million to 50 million, deepfakes are banned, and judges may order platform suspensions of up to 72 hours.

Gabon : l’identification formelle des utilisateurs de réseaux sociaux devient obligatoire — Gabon Media Time, 19 October 2026

Gabon's law 003/2026 of 5 October 2026, published on 9 October, requires individuals and legal entities using social networks and online platforms to formally identify themselves. Gabon Media Time reports that article 4 does not say what documents are needed, how identity will be checked or how existing accounts are treated. It also does not make the personal identification number mandatory for this purpose.

Gambia

Competition and Consumer Protection Commission Bill 2026

Parliament Commits Bill to Prohibit Anti-Competitive Practices - Mansa Banko Online — Mansa Banko Online, 15 September 2026

The Gambia's National Assembly took the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission Bill 2026 through second reading on 15 September and sent it to committee. Tabled by trade minister Mod K. Ceesay, it replaces the 2007 competition and 2014 consumer protection Acts, creates an independent commission with powers to investigate and impose penalties, and extends consumer protection to distance selling and digital content.

Ghana

Virtual Asset Service Providers Act 2025

Ghana: Technical Assistance Report - Regulation and Supervision of Crypto Markets and Activities (IMF Technical Assistance Report No. 26/78, September 2026) — International Monetary Fund, Monetary and Capital Markets Department (Parma Bains, Philipp Hochreiter); file as hosted by BitKE, 1 September 2026

An IMF technical assistance report on Ghana, dated September 2026, found that the crypto guidelines of the Bank of Ghana and the Securities and Exchange Commission fall short of full oversight, ahead of a licensing regime under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act 2025 due to start in December 2026. It puts annual crypto transactions at about US$21bn and recommends activity-based rules, final stablecoin rules and transitional licensing.
