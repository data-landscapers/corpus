Digitalisation of sub-national government: progress report, 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-02

Edition of 2026-09-02-3  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
37 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.
ICT infrastructure for local government · Digitalisation of local government records

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Country Developments Progress
Algeria The only account of municipal digitalisation on file is an academic one from 2025.
Full recordMunicipal digitalisation is in development on a year-precision academic account, and no later account is held.

Nothing moved at either end of the window. In a country that inaugurated a national data centre, launched a sovereign cloud, ordered every administration to interconnect within a month and put a national services portal into operation, the base holds not one statement by any authority about what a commune can do online.
 No change
Angola A provincial water utility begins installing 25,000-26,000 prepaid smart meters from 7 September 2026 on a stated USD 23 million budget.
Full recordBenguela's provincial water utility begins installing prepaid smart meters on 7 September 2026, targeting 25,000 to 26,000 by March 2027 on a stated USD 23 million budget drawn from a USD 191.3 million rehabilitation project across five municipalities (meters). It follows a USD 15 million registration exercise that produced a 70,000-client database, replacing estimate-based billing against arrears stated above 10 billion kwanzas (meters).
 Advanced
Botswana The Digital Services Act brings local authorities within its duties; no local-government ICT infrastructure plan of its own is held.
Full record2025-10-17 — the Act applies to all public bodies, local authorities among them, and requires a digital government plan and a centralised public-services platform.

2025-11-25 — the national Tier III data centre was launched as the platform beneath public services delivered through local offices.

The base holds no connectivity, equipment or systems inventory for district and council offices, and no separate local-government ICT budget line.
 Advanced, by inclusion in a national statute
Burkina Faso Two citizen service centres are under construction, the first inspected at 20% complete in August 2026, against a contract for twenty spaces over five years.
Full record2026-07-26 — two pilot citizen service centres are under construction, the first intended to bring postal, financial and digital services closer to the population, with architectural studies under way for a further city, eight regional capitals and fifty rural communes, against a state and postal-service contract for twenty spaces over five years, where a plan was being drafted with no site under construction.

2026-08-07 — the first is 20% complete on a ministerial inspection, one of twelve structuring digitalisation projects the ministry names, and the 20% figure covers this site and the supervision centre together rather than either alone. No location, service catalogue, cost, completion date or beneficiary count is published.

These are the physical answer to the rural access gap reported under digital divides: services delivered at a counter because they cannot be delivered down a line.
 Advanced
Burundi A USD 10 million contract was signed to extend rural 4G and equipment was handed to governors and communal administrators, from a base of non-functioning networks.
Full recordA country diagnostic records broadband deployment to commune level as achieved while the multiservice telecentre per commune and the intranet and e-government services were not, and describes the government network as not functioning effectively in most government units.

2026-03 — a USD 10 million contract was signed to extend 4G broadband in rural areas, co-financed 44 per cent by government and 56 per cent by the operator, tied to access to digital public services.

2026-08 — a development agency handed laptops, uninterruptible power supplies, printers and photocopiers to provincial governors and communal administrators, the most direct addition to equipment held by local government offices in the window. No count of communes reached by either is published.
 Advanced
Cameroon 230 councils, about 64%, are supported by the ICT agency, and a FCFA 92bn digitisation programme was presented to partners.
Full record2026-07-16 - agency figures put 230 councils, about 64% nationally, under its support, 48.6% of North West councils operating functional websites, 335 municipal portals developed and 145 authorities equipped.

2026-06-02 - a FCFA 92bn local-government digitisation programme was presented to technical and financial partners with no financier, no signed instrument and no start date. It follows a July 2025 digital master plan for about 394 decentralised authorities, against the government's own baseline that 68.5% lack structured computer networks and 57.8% employ no digitally trained staff.
 Advanced, on a FCFA 92bn programme with no financier, instrument or start date
Cape Verde A municipal council will open a services platform with 17 services by end-2026, funded by a UN joint office.
Full record2026-08-25 - a council presented a services platform for public presentation in October 2026 and operation by the end of that year, opening with 17 services on which residents can submit applications, track cases in real time, make payments and receive official documents in digital form with legal validity.

It is funded by a UN joint office, whose resident coordinator called 17 services a good starting point and asked the council to consider integrating further procedures in a second phase, while a minister argued for keeping and improving traditional counter services for those still facing difficulties with digital access, and for replicating the model in other municipalities if it succeeds. It is the only local-government digital instrument the base holds.

2025-12 - a tender was issued to supply, install and maintain structured cabling at twenty-three finance ministry sites across the three islands, bids due 16 February 2026. No award is on record.

Djibouti A pilot connecting 33 government bodies, 22 of them in the regions, is under way.
Full record2025-10 - the state fibre network reached Damerjog in Arta, adding a school and a sub-prefecture, after a stakeholder workshop covering the capital, Balbala and the five interior regions.

2026-02 - the supervision report puts the pilot at 33 ministries and agencies, eleven in the capital and 22 in the regions, under way but incomplete, with a generalisation phase targeting about a hundred more.

 Advanced
Egypt Public shop licensing moved onto one national platform with a three-month maximum approval time and deemed approval on silence.
Full recordThe service did not exist digitally at the window's opening. 2026-07-29 - it moved to a single national platform for the whole licensing process, with standardised procedures, real-time tracking, a maximum three-month approval time, immediate temporary operating permits, electronic licence issuance and deemed approval where an authority fails to respond, fees unified across participating agencies with the interior ministry cutting its own by half.

The Arabic record carries the enforcement half absent from the English accounts: penalties applied to unlicensed shops, agencies canvassing premises, and the stated object of building a complete database of every commercial premises in the country. It is the first consuming service of the digital treasury, and it is also a registration drive.

Eritrea The last assessment of sub-national office ICT found no computer at all in 25 of 55 sub-regional offices (sector note).
Full recordA sector assessment recorded a central ministry local network and internet server, six regional offices and 30 of 55 sub-regional offices connected by dial-up modem, no computer at all in 25 of 55 sub-regional offices, and no systematic maintenance arrangement (sector note).

It is the clearest authoritative assessment of ICT at sub-national government offices located, and it dates from 2002. Nothing later on the base measures the same thing.

The direction of travel over twenty-four years is therefore not something the base can state, which is itself the finding.

Malawi The first local-authority ICT policy and standards were validated in May 2026, and a successor governance project covers 32 councils to 2031.
Full recordCouncils had operated with no framework beyond the national digitalisation policy, a 2022 situational analysis having found weak ICT support, absent council projects and ICT excluded from procurement; the first dedicated local-authority policy plus standards were validated in May 2026 with councils being sensitised, and no adoption date is held (policy).

The successor project is an SDR 58.2m grant, about US$80m equivalent, ratified in July 2026 and closing in 2031, covering 32 councils with four entering for the first time, of which a US$12.8m subcomponent funds business-process re-engineering, software and hardware assessments and a performance dashboard (project, appraisal).

Its predecessor closed in June 2026 with its grant mechanism continuing (predecessor), so process re-engineering is funded ahead of equipment purchase.

South Africa A province launched an AI-evaluated infrastructure platform and opened a funding-matching platform to small business; no municipal-level system is on the ledger.
Full record2026-07-30 — a provincial government launched an infrastructure platform that evaluates projects automatically against treasury rules and a quality standard via an AI agent, reporting an evaluated pipeline of 52 projects worth R132bn with 11 worth over R20bn to be released to market, and memoranda signed with four national and academic bodies.

2026-08-12 — the same province opened free access for small and medium enterprises to a matching platform covering over 300 funders and 600 finance products. Digitalisation of local government records

Country Developments Progress
Angola One provincial government approved an integrated document and archive management system in December 2025, budgeting around weak internet and power.
Full record2025-12-18 — a provincial government approved integrated document and archive management with a citizen application for proposals and public-tender tracking, implementation from January 2026, budgeting explicitly around weak internet and power.

The stated six-month operationalisation target passed with nothing on file either way. It is the only sub-national digitalisation instrument on this ledger for any of the country's provinces, and no source records what the other twenty do with their records.

The design assumption is the useful part: a system specified for intermittent connectivity and power is the right shape for a country where mains electricity reaches few rural households, reported under energy.

Botswana The archives records management system reached its final phase, with a records unit sub-system rolling out to selected ministries.
Full record2026-02-24 — the final phase of the national archives records management system is live and its records management unit sub-system is rolling out to selected ministries and departments.

2025-10-17 — sections 17 and 18 of the Digital Services Act require public and private bodies, local authorities included, to keep records in secure electronic form and to digitise existing records to prescribed standards.

2026-02-26 — a national e-procurement system, its strategy approved in June 2025, is to cover the procurement chain across government, local authorities and state enterprises.

No council is separately named as having digitised its records.

Burkina Faso One portal per region launched in June 2026 across all seventeen regions, with an electronic document-archiving system.
Full record2026-06-16 — a portal for each of the seventeen regions launched, built jointly by the territorial-administration and digital ministries, with an electronic document-archiving system. Nothing existed before it.

It is an information and archiving channel rather than a transactional service: no procedure can be completed on it, and no usage, content or archiving volume is published. Complete coverage of every region on the day of launch is unusual on this ledger, and it is the coverage of a template rather than of a service.

2026-07-14 — an urban property-owner census began building a digitised, geo-referenced register with a unique identifier per parcel.

The urban development ministry launched a national informal settlement baseline inside the housing information system, a digital planning foundation for local authorities (baseline).

The capital adopted a people-centred smart city strategy in June 2026. Four town councils out of a country is where local records digitisation stands.
