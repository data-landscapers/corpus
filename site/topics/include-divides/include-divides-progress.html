Digital divides: progress report, 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-04

Edition of 2026-09-04-2  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
46 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Bridging of digital divides

Country Developments Progress
Algeria A universal-service programme reached 1,400 low-density areas in its first phase, with no target, budget or completion date.
Full recordThe programme's position was not held at the window's opening. By May 2026 it had reached 1,400 areas in a first phase, with no target, budget or completion date stated, against 76.9% internet penetration and 10.9m people offline in 2025.

The offline figure is a third-party compilation rather than an official series, and no rural coverage, affordability or usage-gap measure is published by the regulator or the ministry. So the country can state how many areas a programme reached and not how many people remain outside it, except on someone else's number.
 Advanced
Angola Satellite programmes reached six universities and more than 300 localities without mobile operators, while the universal service fund has published no accounts, grant or disbursement.
Full record2026-07-13 — the connectivity programme is stated at six universities and more than 300 localities where mobile operators are absent, with 20,000 students, against 27 locations and about 366,000 citizens stated to a United Nations body in November 2025. The counts are not the same measure and the base cannot net them; a separate white-space leg connected 3,972 students at two schools.

Alongside it, a commercial access hub opened in December 2025 with 37 firms in the integration process by April 2026, and a first startup was licensed to resell satellite access in July 2026.

The instrument built for this has done nothing on the record. The universal service fund and its 1% levy on telecom operators' gross revenue remain on statute, it may make non-repayable grants for non-commercial universal-service expansion and is barred from subsidising operating costs, and no accounts, grant or disbursement have been published for any year since 2010.
 Mixed, satellite connectivity reaching new places while the universal service fund has published nothing in sixteen years
Benin At least 75% of identification services were accessed digitally in 2025, while the regulator's usage report carries no disaggregation by gender or rurality.
Full record2026-02-02 — at least 75% of identification services were accessed digitally in 2025, with the unconnected served by an unstructured-menu channel and by in-person windows. That is the clearest adoption figure on this ledger and it is about one service family.

2026-05-19 — the regulator's 2025 report records rising digital usage alongside the mobile-money figures, with no disaggregation by gender or rurality in the account held.

So use is rising and nobody has published who is left out. The one intervention that reached the excluded directly was a charity's card distribution, reported under registration of the population, and it worked because it went to nine communes in person.
 Advanced, with the divide itself unmeasured
Botswana The universal fund handed over two school digital centres at P1.45m and P1.5m per site, while the single community wireless site has had no successor in fourteen months.
Full recordA two-day multistakeholder workshop in Gaborone mapped the creative industries to shape a more inclusive digital future (workshop).

2026-07-22 — two school digital centres were handed over in one district at P1.45m and P1.5m per site, thirty machines each, the second framed as addressing the rural divide. The fund is a 1% levy on mobile service providers, no programme total or school target is published, and the base carried nothing on its school programme before January 2026.

Against that, the community wireless site launched in June 2025 with tariffs of P2 to P10 and a free 45-minute daily window remains the only one, with no second site recorded in fourteen months.

Two laboratories and one hotspot is the whole of the published inclusion delivery, against an unresolved dispute about what the national penetration rate even is, reported under internet usage.
 Advanced
Burkina Faso White-zone equipment is ordered for delivery before end-2026, while real fourth-generation coverage fell from 53% to 33% and access runs 72% urban against 27% rural.
Full record2026-04-04 — service orders were signed and equipment ordered for the first white-zone tranche, most localities identified, with delivery targeted before end-2026, against a tender dossier for 750 localities finalised and the project not yet launched; a second lot of 750 localities was announced for 2027 with no separate financing line. The zero-white-zone-by-2027 framing sits against a 2030 horizon for full coverage of about 2,000 zones. The universal access fund co-finances half the state's FCFA 37.5bn white-zone contribution, levied at 2% of operator turnover, with no fund account published for any year from 2024 to 2026.

Against the build, real fourth-generation coverage fell from 53% to 33% on insecurity, on a figure whose earlier reading no held source dates. Internet access stands at about 72% urban against about 27% rural, not repeated or disaggregated by gender anywhere, and the only household survey instrument the base names dates from 2013.

One donor project closed: a 2021 to 2026 Sahel connectivity project serving more than 3,650 users including refugees published its report, with US$1.2m from one European state, on a pledge made in January 2025.
 Mixed, a white-zone programme moved into delivery while real coverage fell from 53% to 33% on insecurity
Burundi The broadband programme targets 80% territorial coverage and carries a digital villages component, with no achieved figure on file.
Full record2026-05-01 — the broadband programme is stated to target 80% of the territory, with a digital villages component under way and no completion date or coverage achieved on file. No baseline was held for either.

What is measurable sits elsewhere in this report and cuts the other way: tariffs more than doubled, the satellite alternative costs more than most can pay, and the regulator says the network fails mainly on electricity. A territorial coverage target says where a signal reaches; none of the three says whether anyone can afford or rely on it.
 Advanced, on a target rather than on an achieved figure
Cameroon A FCFA 55bn rural component targeting 328 unserved localities by 2028 went to tender, with no award on file.
Full record2025-11-09 - a limited national tender opened for the rural component of the national connectivity project: FCFA 55bn, 328 unserved localities targeted by 2028 in two phases of 120 sites plus an optional 208, with works expected by 30 December 2026. No award, contract or site count is held after the tender opened.

The domestic mechanism that would otherwise fund this is gone: the Special Telecommunications Development Fund stays abolished and universal-service money is not separately traceable in FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026, so rural connectivity now runs through donor projects rather than a domestic account.
 Advanced, on a tender with no award held, against a universal-service fund abolished before the window
Cape Verde The universal service fund is appropriated at CVE 122,073,633 for FY2026, identical to the escudo across three budgets.
Full recordThe fund is financed from operator contributions and the radio-spectrum fee, carried in the earmarked-revenue table and in no organic, functional or economic classification, and is appropriated at CVE 122,073,633 for FY2026, identical to the escudo across FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 - a fee-financed ceiling rolled forward rather than re-estimated.

The base holds the appropriation only: no expenditure, project, governing instrument or annual report for the fund from any date. So the one instrument the country has for connecting what the market will not connect cannot be shown to have spent anything.
 No change
Central African Republic The country's first free public access point was inaugurated in April 2026, while the universal service fund is still not operational, with implementation targeted for 2026.
Full record2026-04-24 — the President inaugurated what is presented as the country's first free public internet access point, with technical support from Russian cooperation. It is one site with no bandwidth, uptime, user or funding figure, carried by a pro-government outlet, and the item doubles as campaign copy for a local legislative candidate.

The fund that should be doing this work has never operated. The levy was provided for by a 2018 law and a February 2019 decree, its supervising committee's activities launched in July 2025, and effective implementation is named among the regulator's 2026 priorities; it is financed by a 2% levy on operators' previous-year turnover, with scope covering broadband in schools, health centres, town halls, libraries and post offices.

It has never published an amount collected, a balance or an allocation in the seven years since the levy took effect, and no collection figure, project award or operator selection is held.
 Advanced, one public access point opened while the universal service fund reached its eighth year without operating
Comoros Five mobile science caravans covering about ninety rural localities a year are being launched under the technology park pilot.
Full recordThe caravans were announced on 24 January 2026 as five mobile units covering about ninety rural localities a year and described as being launched at the technology park pilot five days later, with no caravan count, route or start date confirmed since.

They are the only named rural digital-divide instrument in the base. No universal service or rural access fund exists for this country, no rural coverage figure is published, and the measurement body that would report one was never created.

Full record2025-10 - the ministry reported 20 of 76 built-and-equipped sites fully operational at a mid-term review, with 56 in advanced stages, an additional US$3m allocated to upgrade sites to fourth-generation service and 16 rural localities across six departments commissioned. No later site count is held, so the position has not been restated in ten months and whether the end-2025 target was met is unestablished.

Rural coverage stands at 33% for third-generation and 21% for fourth-generation service, with half the rural population outside any coverage. The US$3,000,000 upgrade allocation is stated by the minister as the government's own, distinct from the project envelope, with the fiscal year unstated and no local-currency figure published.
 Mixed, twenty rural sites came into service and two towns were connected while the 76-site end-2025 target has not been restated in ten months
Cote d'Ivoire Territorial coverage rose to about 95% and the rural programme's first phase is stated complete, against a 575-locality target; the urban-rural gap stays unmeasured.
Full recordThe universal-service agency put territorial coverage at 82% with about 500 localities being connected and now at about 95%, with 500 villages connected under the 2025 emergency programme and 300 more localities planned for 2026. Both figures are the agency's or ministry's own.

The target was 575 localities and 825,675 residents by December 2025; no completion statement against it exists and the shortfall passes unremarked in every held account, including the thirty-town account of September 2025.

A feature-phone service platform stated for launch by the end of the first quarter of 2026 has nothing on file establishing it happened.

2026-08-28 - the ministry states the first phase of the rural connectivity programme complete, commissioned at Djebakaha in Korhogo, with phases two and three launched alongside it, the second at FCFA 18bn covering 240 further localities across Belier, Poro, Tchologo, Bagoue and Haut-Sassandra against a 575-locality programme target. The account dates that commissioning to August 2024 while reporting it as current, so the phase chronology is not relied on; no site count or subscriber figure accompanies the completion.
 Mixed, territorial coverage rose to about 95% while the rural connectivity programme missed its December 2025 target and a feature-phone platform did not launch
Djibouti Digital houses were announced across five inland regions, 3,000 to be trained by 2029 at a stated EUR 7m; no site is open.
Full recordThe initiative did not exist at the window's opening. 2026-05 - it was announced across five inland regions, with 3,000 to be trained by 2029 at a stated EUR 7m and no site open on record. Both figures are the programme's own.

It is the only instrument on this ledger addressed to the interior. DR Congo The fund signed subsidy conventions with four operators for 40 localities, against about 4.3m people with no mobile coverage.
Full recordThe fund was created by law in November 2020 and, per a named civil-society officer, still not available at the window's opening. 2026-08-08 - it signed subsidy conventions with the four operators for a first tranche of 40 localities covering about 258,000 people, with a second phase of 300 sites and a 2030 target of 3,000 localities and 16m people; passive infrastructure stands at fewer than 5,500 towers against an estimated need of at least 30,000. Ethiopia A 1.5% revenue levy funds universal access, and handset financing targets up to two million smartphones a year.
Full recordFrom March 2026 both operators contribute 1.5% of revenue to a universal access fund, about 2.5bn birr a year (access fund levy).

Financing deals with two banks target up to two million handsets a year for low-income customers on long-term instalments (handset financing).

Gambia D75 per gigabyte from January 2026 with the floor price gone, against 45.9 per cent internet use.
Full recordPrice moved by order: the tariff decision effective 1 January 2026 sets mobile data at D75 per gigabyte, ends the regulated floor and introduces rollover, on a ministerial directive of 16 October 2025 with the cost-of-service study still pending.

Full recordThe fund still operates and the base holds no levy receipts, accounts or disbursement figure at any date (fund).

The rural telephony project runs on a EUR 140.8m buyer's credit signed in May 2020 and has taken an upgraded mast design (credit); the last disbursement figure held is 60.2 per cent as at May 2022, with no site count, coverage figure or completion date (credit).

No community network regime exists; the regulator says an existing hotspot authorisation could found one, and its register shows four such authorisations each capped at three districts (community networks, access gaps).

South Africa Households with no telephone fell to 3.7% in 2025 from nearly 55% in 2002 (figures), while the zero-rating obligation runs late (acknowledgement).
Full recordThe share of households with no telephone at all fell from nearly 55% in 2002 to 3.7% — about 740,000 households — in 2025, with 93.8% holding a cellphone only and 2.5% holding both a cellphone and a landline (figures).

The communications minister acknowledged on 27 August 2026 that the zero-rating of public-benefit websites owed under the 2022 spectrum auction is moving slowly, five months before its deadline, while saying he remains confident it will be met (acknowledgement).

Device access and affordable access are different problems, and the base's evidence now separates them: near-universal handset ownership alongside an unmet obligation to make public-interest content free to reach.

Full recordAn operator remitted UGX 20.9bn at a handover ceremony; half the statutory 2 per cent levy goes to the consolidated fund and only half is retained, no fund balance or annual total is published, and the statutory basis is a named section of the 2013 communications Act (fund, contribution, remittance).

The foreign agency's withdrawal cut 83 per cent of its programmes (withdrawal); a multi-funder digital-economy fund lost its American component while the parts other funders backed continued, and no figure for the national share is stated (withdrawal).

Border free public wireless access was launched two weeks before the window opened (border).

The US$15m Swedish-funded rural inclusion programme closed on a 2019-2025 term, and its close-out is the only outcome record held for it: more than 3.4 million registered users of whom about 29 per cent were active, more than 21,000 agents recruited, 26 services piloted, and its own statement that technology alone could not close the usage gap (close-out, closure).
