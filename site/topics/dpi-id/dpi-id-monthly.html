Benin

Benin's ANIP, through the World Bank-financed WURI programme, called for expressions of interest in an international expert on digital public infrastructure strategy, to define a sustainable economic model and institutional framework for the identification agency. Benin's Ministry of Economy and Finance announced on 3 October 2026 that pensioners can complete their annual proof of life from a smartphone. After identifying with their personal identification number (NPI) and pension booklet number, they verify their fingerprints in an app and receive confirmation by SMS or email. Biometric kiosks in some public places offer an alternative.

Benin launched Preuve de Vie on 15 October 2026, a mobile app through which public-sector pensioners of the FNRB confirm they are alive using their NPI, pension booklet number and fingerprint, from Benin or abroad. Biometric kiosks taking the national ID card are an alternative, and the campaign runs to 15 November 2026.

By 21 October 2026, 15,189 pensioners of Benin's national pension fund (FNRB) had completed their annual proof of life remotely through the Preuve de Vie app, according to sources close to the presidency. Pensioners enter their national personal identification number (NPI) and pension book number and scan their fingerprints with the phone camera, or use biometric kiosks with a biometric ID card; the campaign runs to 15 November.

DR Congo

Biometric passport

Semlex to face criminal trial in Belgium over Congo passport scandal — Biometric Update, 1 September 2026

Fourteen defendants in the case of Semlex, the Belgian firm that made the Democratic Republic of Congo's biometric passports from 2015 to 2025, have been sent to a Brussels criminal court on bribery, money laundering and tax fraud charges. Investigators allege about $60 of each $185 passport went to a Dubai firm, nearly $60 million in all; 51 Congolese citizens are civil parties.

RDC: Jean-Claude Mputu dénonce la continuité Semlex-RAM — CongoQuotidien, 2 September 2026

Jean-Claude Mputu, spokesman for the Congo Is Not For Sale coalition (CNPAV), said the Democratic Republic of Congo's passport contract with Semlex, renewed in 2020 when the original five-year deal expired, has never been published. The renewal, meant to last a year, ran until 2025; the only concession won was a passport price cut from $185 to $100.
