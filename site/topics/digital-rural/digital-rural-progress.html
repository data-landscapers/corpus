Rural digital data capture: progress report, 2025-09-01 to 2026-09-02

Edition of 2026-09-02  ·  compiled by Claude Opus from the documents in the Corpus repository
↓ PDF
23 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.
Digitalisation of rural health clinics · Digitalisation of rural primary schools · Digitalisation of rural registry offices · Digitalisation of rural police stations

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Digitalisation of rural health clinics

Country Developments Progress
Botswana Phase one of the electronic medical record is live at 22 facilities in Ngami, with the remaining districts to follow.
Full record2026-03-05 — phase one of the centralised electronic medical record is rolling out at 22 Ngami facilities, extending to Okavango and the remaining 29 districts.

2025-11-20 — 223 clinics are connected under the village connectivity programme, with legacy systems active but not interfaced, on the minister’s answer to Parliament.

2026-01-26 — the health emergency project funds a logistics management system to track supplies to remote health posts.

No count of clinics with a working record system is published.
 Advanced
Burundi A laboratory system went into nine sites with automated transmission, and a mass campaign ran on digital household registration and barcode scanning.
Full record2026-05-18 to 29 — a laboratory information system was deployed to nine strategic sites, interconnecting ten diagnostic instruments to a central web platform with automated data transmission, with a national trainer pool formed to replicate across 64 instruments.

The 2025 insecticide-treated-net mass campaign was run on digital household registration and stock management with barcode and QR scanning and real-time tracking, the account carrying the challenges met and the lessons drawn as well as the design.

Both are digital capture reaching where patients are rather than reporting from where they were seen. Neither states how many of the sites are rural, which is the question this indicator asks and the record does not answer.
 Advanced
Comoros 58 of 74 public facilities, 78 per cent, capture stock in real time within about three months of go-live.
Full recordThe starting position is the strategy's own: the national health information system semi-electronic, the platform still being deployed, district health centres rarely connected and digital capture at primary and remote level still a gap.

2025-10 - the logistics rollout put 58 of 74 public facilities capturing stock in real time on Android within about three months of go-live, nearly 30,000 transactions recorded, remote islands still constrained by connectivity.

2025-10 - the measles and rubella campaign of 4 to 8 October became the first national campaign to use mobile data collection and geospatial tracking, 612 agents trained, targeting more than 101,330 children across the three islands.

Stock is captured at three-quarters of facilities; clinical data at that level is not.
 Advanced
Djibouti 69% of health facilities entered data directly by the end of 2024; the rest still submit on paper.
Full recordThe measured position predates the period and nothing inside it moves: by 31 December 2024, 69% of health facilities entered data directly into the reporting platform while others submitted on paper, and 23 of 62 facilities were connected to the logistics information system against a 65% target.

The health agency's own response records the system under review and resources still being sought to revamp it and ensure connectivity at national level.

2025-08-29 - the health ministry joined a regional ministerial meeting committing member states to community and cross-border surveillance, unified digital data repositories and interoperable laboratory systems - a commitment, not a deployment.
 No change
Eswatini A community health information system launched in September 2025 moved rural health motivators off paper to offline-capable digital capture.
Full recordThe health ministry launched the system with United Nations and World Bank partners, alongside a community-based health strategy (launch).

A year earlier the ministry's own annual report recorded facility data collection running electronically and on paper together (annual report).

The health agency's 2025 results report records optimisation of the district health information platform and enhanced interoperability across major programmes, while flagging still-fragmented legacy systems (results report).

Community health services are digitised on the base's own record. No count of clinics running electronically, and no figure for those still on paper, is held.
 Advanced
Ethiopia A matched study found measurably better maternal and child health delivery in woredas running the digitised community health information system (study).
Full recordA propensity-score-matching study published in May 2026, on household data collected in July and August 2024, found woredas implementing the electronic community health information system had higher modern contraceptive use by 13.0 percentage points, institutional deliveries by 8.9 points and full vaccination coverage by 14.6 points than non-implementing woredas (study).

The national health information system reached 72% of districts, with more than 2,975 institute, regional and woreda staff trained, alongside a new emergency-management system and event-based surveillance (report).

The health ministry's annual sector performance report for 2024/25 carries a dedicated digital health chapter (annual report).

The base holds district coverage, not health-post coverage, so how far digitisation reaches the last mile is not stated.
 Advanced
Kenya A county telemedicine network reached 170 public health facilities with 302 more due before end-2026, from a 35-facility pilot.
Full recordFifteen doctors at a central hub handle 450 to 600 patients a day (network).

It is the only rural clinical deployment in the base with a facility count and a throughput figure attached, and it is a county's rather than the health ministry's.
 Advanced
Lesotho A trial across 103 rural villages found village health workers on mobile decision support outperformed facility referral.
Full record2026-04-09 — the World Health Organization names DHIS2 and digital transformation among health priorities, the most current status statement held.

2026-02-12 — a cluster-randomised trial across 103 rural villages found community health workers using mobile decision support outperformed facility referral for hypertension care.

The evidence here is outcome evidence, which is rare in this frame; what it does not establish is how many clinics run any digital system, a figure the base does not hold.
 Advanced
Malawi The community health system reached 15 districts with 3,764 surveillance assistants trained, replacing paper at community level.
Full recordThe governing instrument is the ministry's digital health governance framework, which names the health information system as the flagship platform and mandates its integration with the national registration system.

2026-02 - a project began integrating climate data, the health management information system and the one-health surveillance platform with the world health body's early-warning system across four districts.

2026-06 - the community health information system was deployed in 15 districts with 3,764 health surveillance assistants and their seniors trained, replacing paper records at community level. Community level is where rural health data is captured, so this is the substantive move; the facility-level record system is a separate track.
 Advanced
Mozambique About US$40m is committed to digitising 63 health units within a wider plan covering about 863 of some 1,900, and a five-year health memorandum commits to electronic medical records from health-unit level.
Full record2026-04-15 — about US$40m is assured for digitising 63 health units, inside a wider plan covering roughly 863 of some 1,900 units nationally, with a pilot running at one Maputo hospital and extension planned to Quelimane and Beira. It is the largest funded expansion in the window, and the announcement does not break out how many of the target facilities are rural.

2025-12-15 — a five-year bilateral health memorandum worth about US$1.7bn commits to implementing an electronic medical record from the level of the health unit as part of strengthening health information systems.

Both are commitments. No count of rural units actually digitised, and no record-completeness or uptime figure for those that are, is published — so the base can state what is funded and not what is running.
 Advanced, on funding commitments rather than on delivered facilities
Namibia The e-health network reaches every hospital and 23 health centres, five of them and 18 clinics operational for real-time records.
Full recordThe health minister's budget speech reports e-health infrastructure installed at every hospital and 23 health centres, with five centres and 18 clinics already operational for real-time patient records (speech).

The ministry launched a ten-year National Digital Health Policy in February 2026 alongside an invitation to procure an electronic health system (policy).

A digital community system replaces paper-based community-health records with an offline-capable tool for community health workers, with officers trained for phased rollout in July 2026 (training).

Eighteen clinics running real-time records is the figure that matters, and it is the only one published.
 Advanced
Nigeria The primary health care agency operates a data-aggregation hub for primary health centre data, and nothing dated in the window moved it.
Full recordThe hub is the clearest primary statement the base holds of an existing instrument for digitising the primary health layer. It is an institutional description rather than a dated report: no facility count, coverage figure or data-quality statement is published, so how far it reaches rural clinics is unestablished.
 No change
Sierra Leone Satellite connectivity for 300 government health facilities launched its first site in July 2026, placing a national health dependency on one foreign operator with no public contract.
Full recordThe first site was launched at a paediatric centre on 27 July 2026 (launch, site).

It places a national health information dependency on a single foreign satellite operator with no public contract. No value, term, service level or exit arrangement is published, and no alternative transport is named for any of the 300 facilities.

That is the finding rather than the rollout: the country has chosen the fastest route to connecting its clinics and has published nothing about what happens if it stops.
 Advanced
South Africa Implementation partners were invited to deploy the electronic medical record across eight provinces, with one province already recognised for a primary-care deployment.
Full record2026-03-03 — public-health service providers were invited to implement the electronic medical record solution across eight provinces, held as an excerpt with no award, value or facility count on file.

2026-05-14 — one province deployed an electronic medical record across primary health care, with training, connectivity, cybersecurity and interoperability recorded as outstanding work. The outstanding list is the useful part: the record is installed and the conditions for using it are not met.

The national department carries a chief directorate for health systems digital information as the standing owner, on an undated page with no facility or coverage figure.
 Advanced
Zambia Satellite terminals were installed in rural public facilities and a government private fourth-generation network launched at a teaching hospital.
Full recordThe state buying foreign satellite capacity for the last mile sits alongside a government-owned private network build (terminals, network).

The private network launched at a teaching hospital; clinician accounts at the launch site are operator statements, and no baseline or measured outcome is held (launch, coverage).

Two connectivity strategies for the same problem — buying foreign satellite capacity and building a state network — running at once, with no source relating them.
 Advanced
Zimbabwe Satellite kits at rural health facilities are not activated because subscriptions are unpaid, on the health minister's own statement to the National Assembly.
Full recordThe minister told the National Assembly the subscriptions are unpaid (facilities).

The donation programme handed out kits with no cumulative total published by government, and the health minister's account is that they are not activated (kits, donation, record).

Hardware distributed and left unconnected for want of a subscription line is the sharpest delivery failure in this unit, and it is stated by the responsible minister in Parliament.

Digitalisation of rural primary schools

Country Developments Progress
Botswana Twelve primary-school ICT laboratories were commissioned in the year to March 2025, taking the national total to 14.
Full record2025-03-01 — the universal access fund commissioned twelve primary-school ICT laboratories in the year to March 2025, bringing the nationwide total to 14, and deployed 219 IT officers.

2026-06-01 — digital centres at Ditladi and Mabele primary schools were launched in November 2025 at P1.59m each.

2026-07-22 — a P1.5m digital centre with 30 laptops, connectivity and a smart board was handed over at Sankoyo primary school.

The fund publishes its laboratory count and no denominator: the base holds no total for the primary school estate the fourteen sit within.
 Advanced
Burundi Tenders opened for digital centres at ten rural and peri-urban learning centres and 585 computers across 39 central schools.
Full recordAn independent assessment describes school data as paper collection at the school followed by electronic entry at central level, with no demonstrated direct electronic capture at rural fondamental schools.

2026-06 — a tender opened for IT equipment and the operationalisation of a multimedia centre, digital centres and distance-learning platforms for a polytechnic training centre and ten rural and peri-urban learning centres, and for computers and tablets for data collection.

2026-08-11 — a national request for proposals opened for computer equipment in 39 central schools in three former provinces, its bill of quantities naming 585 computers and uninterruptible power supplies. Both are procurement rather than delivery: no installed count, no connectivity provision and no maintenance arrangement is stated for either.
 Advanced
Comoros 45 per cent of public primaries are on the school platform and eleven rural schools are receiving equipment kits.
Full recordThe baseline is a completion report's: the education ministry's own information system lacked sufficient data, collection was centralised with limited involvement of island-level structures, and no fully operational system existed.

2026-02 - the ministry put 45 per cent of public primary schools on the shared platform against every public secondary school, the first coverage figures the base holds and a gap that falls on the rural half of the estate.

2026-03 - computer equipment kits with solar power were delivered to four named rural primary schools on Ngazidja, with eleven schools planned across the three islands under a climate action project.

Solar power delivered with the computers is the measure of what rural connection means here.
 Advanced
Cote d'Ivoire Nine innovation and culture centres are operating in interior schools, with twenty more announced for 2026.
Full record2026-06-24 - the universal-service agency reported nine centres operating in interior schools with twenty more announced for 2026, having described the programme in February.

The first count on file is June 2026, so no direction can be read from the series itself. No opening dates, cost, equipment list or usage figure is published for any of the nine, and nothing states how they relate to the education ministry's own digitalisation work or who runs them once open.
 Advanced
Djibouti Every school location was validated by April 2026, the mapping reaching schools missing from administrative records.
Full record2025-12-16 - more than 80% of schools had been mapped, with imagery analysis being applied to locate the remainder, coordinates and pupil counts having previously been extracted by hand.

2026-04-27 - the country became the first to validate 100% of school locations on the connectivity mapping platform, addressing rural and hard-to-reach schools missing from administrative records or lacking precise coordinates.

What the data supports is planning rather than provision: the education system is deployed to public primary schools while rural electricity access remains limited and full adoption is still in progress. No count of connected schools is published.
 Advanced
Eswatini 402 of 965 mapped schools are connected, and the education ministry states most primary schools are still not.
Full record965 schools are mapped on the international schools-connectivity platform with 402 connected, and the education ministry states most primary schools remain unconnected (briefing).

The communications regulator handed 68 devices to the education ministry in December 2025 to support a learning-passport platform and pilot digital-learning demonstration sites in four schools, aimed particularly at rural areas (donation).

The education management information system is national in scale and keyed to a personal identification number, and most rural primary schools still collect on paper.

Connectivity is the binding layer, and it stands at 402 schools.
 Advanced
Ethiopia An open-source education information system was adopted, with initial implementation covering schools and woredas in four regions (launch).
Full recordEthiopia adopted DHIS2 for Education, an open-source education management information system, with initial implementation covering schools, woredas and zones in four regions and the explicit aim of operationalising the ministry's national system at local levels (launch).

A US$69m additional financing package approved in March 2026 integrates all 57 refugee primary schools into the national education system, including into the national education information system, with US$750,000 earmarked for enhancement and disaggregated reporting (programme paper).

The funded reform behind the rollout is the girls' education and learning programme, which names digitisation of the education information system as a reform strand with rollout to primary schools planned across 4,500 schools in 150 woredas (programme document).

The base holds plans and initial regions, and no count of primary schools actually reporting through the system.
 Advanced
Guinea A school connectivity plan targets 2,200 primary schools by the end of 2026, with 585 connected by January.
Full recordThe plan was launched on 5 November 2025 (plan) and 585 schools were connected by January 2026 (progress).

No later figure is published, so a programme with a hard end-of-year target is last measured at about a quarter of it with eleven months gone.
 Advanced
Kenya Three school-connectivity commitments totalling about EUR 19.6m and GBP 4.77m are unchanged, with no outturn or schools-connected update published.
Full recordOne commits EUR 9.8m (last-mile schools) and another EUR 9.8m to connect 1,000 schools reaching 369,400 children across all 47 counties with per-school cost cut about 60 per cent, again with no disbursement or update (school connectivity).

A bilateral programme committed GBP 4.77m to March 2027 with no outturn published (access programme).

The one delivered figure is a satellite operator's: 30 schools across 30 districts reaching more than 32,000 students and about 1,000 teachers at the start of the 2026 academic year, financial terms undisclosed (satellite schools).
 No change
Lesotho A school and clinic connectivity programme opened at a Butha-Buthe school in January 2026, after community hotspots in three rural places.
Full recordT-Connect Impact launched at Soofia International School in Butha-Buthe on 17 January 2026 (launch).

It followed the first community hotspots handed over at Matsieng, Thaba Bosiu and Mokhotlong in September 2025 (hotspots). No count of connected schools is published, no clinic is named, and no national programme for rural school connectivity is held.
 Advanced
Liberia About 90 of 156 public high schools are connected under a satellite school programme, with no funder, cost or completion date published.
Full recordThe count was given in July 2026 (programme, progress).

It is the only school-connectivity figure the base holds, it covers high schools rather than primary ones, and it runs on a satellite service the regulator had not then licensed.
 Advanced
Malawi 85 per cent of 9,000 schools have limited or no internet and about 900 have none, while a corporate programme has connected 36.
Full recordLack of electricity and of digital devices are named as the main barriers; a development bank is separately supporting connection of over 200 schools and the acceleration project targets 2,000 (analysis, targets).

The corporate programme has connected 36 schools with a further nine scheduled in 2026, equipped laboratories with televisions and tablets, zero-rated twelve local learning sites and states more than 32,000 learners and over 900 teachers benefiting (programme); all figures are the company's own (programme).
 Mixed, a corporate programme connecting 36 schools against 85 per cent of the country's 9,000 schools with limited or no internet
Mozambique 149 localities and 418 schools were connected under the ministry's programme, against a stated target of up to 4,000 schools.
Full recordThe connected counts and the targets are the communications ministry's own, given without a completion date: at least 300 localities, up to 4,000 schools and around 10,000 new access points (programme).

At 418 schools the programme is about a tenth of the way to its own ceiling. No completion date, per-school service level or count of schools still unconnected is published.
 Advanced
Namibia 21 of 188 schools in one region have workable connectivity, leaving 55,002 learners without digital teaching tools, against a national figure of 339 schools and 50 health centres lacking adequate connectivity.
Full recordThe regional education directors told the parliamentary ICT committee that 21 of Kavango West's 188 schools have workable connectivity, leaving 55,002 learners without digital teaching tools, and that the neighbouring region's 197 schools serving 86,282 learners are only marginally connected (committee); health officials told the same committee that clinics still struggle with unreliable coverage (committee, schools, clinics).

The ministry's national figure of 339 schools and 50 health centres lacking adequate connectivity dates from June 2025 with no later official figure; a research council account records 65.4 per cent of schools connected in 2023, 1,311 of 2,004, on a different basis that is not comparable (national, council).
 Regressed
Nigeria A state's 260 smart green schools at about ₦1.3-1.5bn each were promised open by January 2026 and many were incomplete, with roofs failed at three sites.
Full recordThe programme covers 260 schools, one in each ward, powered by solar and connected by satellite, at about ₦1.3-1.5bn per school from state allocations of ₦266.7bn in 2025 and ₦198bn in 2026 (investigation).

Full opening of all 260 was promised for January 2026; as of May many were incomplete and unequipped with pupils already resuming, roofs had failed at three sites, one school was demolished after failing integrity tests, and a ₦11.4bn contract for 22 schools was terminated with ₦1.23bn recovered.
 Stalled
Rwanda School internet connectivity advanced at a March 2026 dialogue against a government target of 80 per cent, and nothing has been published since.
Full recordConnectivity was reported at an education-technology dialogue in March 2026 against a government target of 80 per cent, and nothing has been published on it in the five months since (connectivity).

The education-technology integration appropriation grows in the 2026/27 finance law and is the one education-technology line that does (integration, prior).

The second education-technology line fell; its 2024/25 year had spent 252.4 per cent of a revised allocation, so the fall is against a base that was itself heavily overspent (second line, baseline).
 Mixed, school connectivity advancing while one of the two education-technology appropriations fell
Senegal The education ministry plans to connect more than 8,000 primary schools to the internet by satellite (plan).
Full recordThe education ministry plans to connect more than 8,000 primary schools to the internet by satellite (plan). It is a plan, with no schools connected on the record.
 Advanced
South Africa A provincial treasury launched a digital access programme for rural and under-resourced schools, and an off-grid containerised media lab entered service at one primary school.
Full record2026-04-14 — a provincial treasury launched a Digital Access Programme for rural and under-resourced schools in one district, with announced expansion to two further districts. No school count, budget or device figure is published, and the national digital education strategy of September 2025 is the policy frame it sits under.

2026-05-29 — a containerised off-grid media lab entered service at one primary school, with solar generation and battery storage, offering coding and robotics training to surrounding schools.

The off-grid design is the notable part. Against the rural electrification position reported under energy, a school computer laboratory that generates its own power is a workaround for a constraint the education programmes cannot themselves remove.
 Advanced
Zambia A target of 2,500 additional schools connected by end-2026, against one completed rural school connection.
Full recordThe auditor general found the education ministry's electronic census software set aside as non-functional and replaced by an in-house spreadsheet tool — the base's standing finding on data capture in schools, and a costly one.

2026-04 — the education ministry set a target of 2,500 additional schools connected to reliable internet by the end of 2026, prioritising rural and hard-to-reach areas. It is a target, not a rollout.

2026-08-06 — an operator connected a rural primary school's computer laboratory by satellite. One completed deployment against that target, with four months to run and no progress count published.
 Advanced
Zimbabwe A school connectivity and solar programme targets every school connected by December 2026, on the minister's own counts with no school total or verification of working terminals stated.
Full recordThe counts and the target are the minister's own; no school total, verification of installed and working terminals, or recurrent connectivity cost is stated (programme).

The recurrent cost is the point on which the rural health kits failed — hardware installed and subscriptions unpaid — and no source states who pays it for schools.

Digitalisation of rural registry offices

Country Developments Progress
Botswana Nine of fifteen hospital registration kiosks still operate, while online registration is reported complete and waiting on the platform.
Full record2026-08-01 — registration runs through 35 district offices and 17 on-site points at major health facilities and clinics, on a system described as partially automated and decentralised.

2026-03-06 — of fifteen hospital kiosks established for on-site birth and death registration, nine remain operational and six have closed, mainly for want of the two officers each was meant to have.

2026-02-24 — online registration of births, deaths and marriages was reported complete, pending the government platform’s launch.

The closures are staffing, not systems: what constrains rural registration on this record is people.
 Mixed, online registration complete while kiosks close
Burundi A digital civil-registration platform is piloted in a first group of communes, with thirteen communes stated to hold a biometric system.
Full record2026-05-06 — the interior ministry presented a digital civil-registration platform and biometric national identity card production, piloted in a first group of communes with intended extension to zone level.

2026-08-10 — the same directorate set out digitalisation in pilot communes and decentralisation to zone level, with planned interoperability with health facilities for births and deaths notified through hill chiefs, and a UN agency stated that thirteen communes already have a biometric registration system.

Thirteen communes is the only number on the record. Burundi has well over a hundred, so what is established is a pilot that has been running for at least a quarter and a national position that has not moved.
 Advanced
Comoros Field consultations reached Moheli's rural districts in August 2026, naming unregistered rural residents as the problem to solve.
Full recordThe law promulgated in September 2023 provides for computerised civil-status processing and sites registration centres at commune chefs-lieux, which is where a rural registry office is.

2025-10 - the study launch workshop validated the methodology, roadmap and governance for digitalising the system.

2026-08 - the project consulted midwives, village chiefs, cadis and mayors at Fomboni on Moheli about registration difficulties, continuing into the rural districts of Djando and Nioumachoi, with still-unregistered rural residents named as the core problem.

A design grounded in rural practice is worth more than one that is not, and it is still a design: no rural office is recorded as digitised, and no office count exists on file.
 Advanced
Djibouti A regional survey finds no electronic records in local civil-registration offices and paper-based transmission between levels.
Full recordThe standing position is set out in a country-comparative survey: no electronic records in local civil-registration offices for births or deaths, no central national civil-registration database, paper-based marital-status registration and fully paper-based transmission between registration levels and institutions, despite open-source registration software being in use centrally.

The one movement is administrative: civil-status administration was transferred to the Dikhil regional council at Mouloud in April 2026, with nothing said about equipment or records.

Archive digitisation at the population directorate is a central programme with no stated reach into regional offices.
 No change
Eswatini Short-code access to public services is planned so citizens without smartphones can transact, and 44 devices were handed to agricultural extension officers.
Full recordShort-code access is planned under the digital project specifically so citizens without smartphones can transact (access). In a country where data cost sits above the affordability threshold, that is the design choice that decides whether the rest of the programme reaches anyone rural.

Forty-four devices were handed to extension officers in August 2026, with no officer total, data platform, reporting standard or custodian for the collected field data stated (devices).

Field data collected on state-issued devices with no named custodian is a data-protection question the guidelines under validation do not yet reach.
 Advanced
Ethiopia Rural land registration runs on second-level certification and a national rural land administration information system (programme report).
Full recordThe land administration programme's completion report, covering 2011 to 2024, records the digitalisation of rural land administration through second-level land certification and the National Rural Land Administration Information System, endorsed as the national land registry system (programme report).

The captured text of that report reaches only its table of contents, so the number of woredas operating the system is not something the base can state, and its publication date is a proxy taken from the stated project period.

The base holds no separate account of registry offices outside land — civil, business or vehicle registration at woreda level — so this indicator rests on one sector.
 No change, a first statement of the position with no earlier figure behind it
Lesotho Thirty SMS-based birth registration stations were established across three districts in August 2026.
Full recordA national farmers portal was launched, placing every farmer in a single digital registry (launch). No enrolment figure is stated.

2022-05 — an assessment sets out the registry’s use cases and coverage.

2026-08-05 — thirty birth registration stations were established in government and church health facilities across Maseru, Berea and Leribe, on a low-technology SMS platform.

2026-02-26 — the 2026 work plan continues the identity infrastructure behind them.

The capture point chosen is the health facility rather than the registry office, and the platform is SMS rather than a terminal. No count of registry offices with a digital system is published.
 Advanced
Malawi Mobile registration reached 87 service points across five districts, taking registration to people rather than the reverse.
Full recordThe bureau's plan sets out district-office and rural service-point digitisation, with civil registration kits synchronising to the central register.

2026-02 - the integrated registration application had reached 87 service points across five districts, which is a mobile deployment rather than an office conversion.

2026-02 - the first phase of the birth registration campaign synchronised 970,400 of 1.1 million targeted child records into the central database across five districts. What the base does not hold is any count of registry offices, how many are connected, or what happens at one that is not.
 Advanced
Morocco 80 mobile identity enrolment units served more than 130,000 citizens in 2024, and registry extension to remote areas has no delivery figure.
Full recordThe mobile units are the base's only Morocco-specific rural digital-delivery instrument: 80 units serving more than 130,000 citizens in 2024, with no later figure held (units).

Extension of the registries to rural and remote areas via digital and remote applications was a component of the December 2024 project, with at least five programmes targeted by end-2024 and eight by 2028; that target date has passed with no delivery or coverage figure held (extension).
 No change, both figures are from 2024 and the stated extension target has passed
Mozambique Staff of every registry office in Nampula province are being trained to enter documents into electronic platforms, with technical teams deployed to the districts.
Full record2026-06-11 — the state administration minister confirmed training under way for the staff of every registry office in Nampula province to enter documents into electronic platforms, with technical teams deployed to the districts. It is a last-mile event distinct from the national electronic civil registration framework, and the first the base holds at province scale.

The duties behind it are statutory: the 2018 revision of the civil registration code governs the digitisation and notification obligations of registry offices, rural posts included.

One province is not a national position. No count of offices reached, documents entered or districts completed is published, and no other province has a comparable record.
 Advanced
Namibia Electronic birth notification runs across 153 facilities, and a mobile prototype lets a remote mother register a newborn by phone.
Full recordThe explanatory memorandum to the Civil Registration and Identification Bill describes the existing population registration system and legislates registration by electronic notice (memorandum).

Electronic birth and death notification operate across 153 facilities on the minister's account, up from 126 in December 2025 (coverage).

A mobile identity prototype was tested at Otjimbingwe so a remote mother could register a newborn by phone with a digital signature (prototype), and remote biometric enrolment has been tested through hand-held devices.

Over 21,000 San people were assisted under civil registration in 2025 (outreach). The registry reaches rural areas through hospitals and outreach rather than through offices.
 Advanced
Niger 173,109 birth certificates were issued in one region over three years with timely declaration at 88.04 per cent, and a birth-certificate pilot targets 21,202 under-fives elsewhere.
Full recordThe Diffa region issued 173,109 certificates between July 2023 and July 2026, with timely declaration at 88.04 per cent in 2025 against 56 per cent in 2022 (region); 263 village chiefs were trained as mobilisers and four communes computerised (region).

A pilot at Filingue targets 21,202 under-fives with no completion figure published (pilot).

Traditional chiefs as registration mobilisers is the delivery mechanism this unit relies on, and it is the same mechanism used to prepare the census.
 Advanced
Nigeria Civil registration went nationwide on a digital platform with paper discontinued from 1 July 2026, while none of 371 targeted health facilities went live.
Full recordThe country case on the registry platform records ward-level coverage and the platform as the operative system for civil registration and vital statistics.

Zimbabwe More than 20 of 209 civil registry sub-offices are computerised, the same figure the base held a year earlier, with ten more being equipped.
Full recordMore than 20 registry offices and sub-offices had been computerised by July 2025, allowing real-time data entry and faster document processing; one fully computerised sub-office sits 200 metres from the maternity ward at a Bulawayo hospital, so mothers can register a newborn before discharge (programme).

A year later the figure reported to a meeting of African registrars general was the same: all ten provinces and 63 districts computerised, but more than 20 of 209 sub-offices, under a tenth of the local layer (registrars).

Ten further sub-offices across eight districts are now being computerised, digitalised and solar-powered under a China-funded, UNICEF-managed project (project).

The sub-office layer is where rural registration happens, and it is the layer that did not move.
