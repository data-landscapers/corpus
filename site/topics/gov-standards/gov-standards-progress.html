46 countries. Each row below is carried verbatim from that country's own progress report, which answers a fixed frame of indicators over the period; nothing is written here. A country not listed under an indicator has No evidence on it in its own report.
National interoperability standards · National quality standards · Adoption of international standards

Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

National interoperability standards

Country Developments Progress
Algeria A national information-security referential covers 14 domains; no interoperability standard is held.
Full recordThe clearest national technical standard the base holds is an information-security referential covering 14 domains including asset management, access control, cloud-services security and incident handling. It is security, not interoperability.

The addressing side does have one: the national addressing procedures guide sets a data model and procedural standard.

No whole-of-government interoperability framework, message standard or conformity test appears anywhere, and the national interoperability system this ledger holds separately is a system rather than a standard.
 No change, a first stated position with no earlier account behind it
Benin The interoperability framework binds public and private bodies providing a public service, on named standards, with its coordinating structure reporting to the President.
Full recordThe framework has been in force since March 2020, binding public and private bodies that provide a public service, with four levels of challenge, five common infrastructure components and named standards. Its governance clause states that the coordinating structure should report directly to the President — an explicit answer to the question of who outranks a ministry that declines to connect.

Nothing amended it in the window. The referentials consulted on in July 2026, reported under the interoperability roadmap, are the first attempt to add architecture standards on top of it, and no conformance regime, certification scheme or published register of compliant systems exists.
 No change
Botswana The national interoperability framework is being aligned under an externally funded programme; the framework itself is not published.
Full record2025-10-17 — the Digital Services Act requires a standardised framework for secure exchange of information, an information gateway and an interoperability standard.

2026-02-17 — a programme running since August 2024 through Expertise France, ESTDEV and HAUS is improving and aligning the framework, its standards, guidelines and tools.

No standard, framework document or conformance regime is published, and no list of the systems the framework covers.
 Advanced, under external assistance
Burkina Faso A procedures manual for ministerial information-systems directorates was validated in September 2025, under a decree requiring three sequential consents before a public platform goes live.
Full record2025-09-23 — a harmonised reference framework for ministerial information-systems directorates was validated at a workshop, against a regulatory duty that had stood on every public service for more than twenty years with no manual in existence. Formal approval by joint ministerial order is not recorded as signed, and signature would settle it.

The governing instrument is unchanged: the platforms decree requires a prior favourable technical opinion before development or acquisition, a security non-objection before go-live, and assignment of the full source code and current documentation by the supplier or in-house team before a platform enters service, with the same regime restated in 2026. The source-code assignment is the unusual clause and it is what makes the in-house builds reported elsewhere durable rather than vendor-locked.
 Advanced
Burundi Health standards were defined at a national workshop in April 2026 and the education system was assessed against continental norms in June; no cross-government standard is adopted.
Full record2026-04-07 to 10 — a national health information workshop defined common technical standards, naming HL7 FHIR, with priority data-exchange flows and mediation for the country's fragmented health platforms.

2026-06 — Burundi self-assessed its education management information system against the continental EMIS norms and standards at a three-day technical workshop; no score or action plan is published.

Both are sectoral. The cross-government instrument that would sit above them, the Concept type des systemes d'information des institutions publiques, was presented for validation in December 2024 and has not been adopted.
 Advanced
Comoros An interoperability framework and a government enterprise architecture are named as components of the national e-government plan; neither is published.
Full recordThe citizen portal's concept note is the only record in the base that names either. It places the Cadre d'Interoperabilite and the Architecture d'Entreprise Gouvernementale inside the Plan National e-Gouv, alongside the emergent-Comoros plan and the 2028 digital strategy.

No profile, schema, register of standards or conformance requirement is on file, and no ministry is named as owning one. The note's own request is a census: it asks government entities to report the e-government platforms they have built, so that a database of them can be assembled before the portal is deployed.

A government that is still counting its own platforms has not yet begun to set standards between them. The page carries no date; it is dated here by the day it was retrieved.
 No change, a framework named in the national e-government plan with no standard published
Cote d'Ivoire Five public administrations had joined the national interoperability platform by 22 July 2026, against a target of 100 interoperable services by the end of the year.
Full record2026-07-22 - a workshop reported that five public administrations had joined the platform, twelve structures were named for phase one, and the aim was to make those five services operational before 31 July 2026 and to reach 100 interoperable services by the end of 2026 out of 31 administrations judged mature enough to connect. The platform's director general states it is already available; the minister named a governance project making information-system heads accountable for their own developments, and a commitment to interconnect at least 700 public services within three years.

The standards those connections are to be made against are the general interoperability framework the 2017 ordinance requires. Five services against 700 is the distance, and nothing published gives a conformity test any of the five had to pass.
 Advanced
Djibouti The national interoperability framework sets the standards layer; no technical specification or conformance register is held.
Full recordThe framework defines a technical interoperability layer and a semantic layer, and provides for governance-level standards and specifications maintained by the state information-systems agency.

What is not on file is the layer below it: no published technical specification, data-exchange schema, application-programming interface catalogue or conformance register.

The one system in the period described as exchanging data to a stated pattern is the national mobile identity service, where partner administrations query authentication server-to-server without direct access to the biometric database - an architecture stated by its owner rather than a published standard.
 No change
Egypt The one sectoral exchange standard held is the smart-metering adoption of 2025; no whole-of-government standard is on the base.
Full recordThe base holds one exchange standard and it is sectoral: a 2025 national standard adopting the international smart-metering data-exchange standard. Another is a specification rather than a standard: a national electronic receipt technical specification setting the document element, data structure and mandatory fields for education-service return receipts exchanged with the tax authority.

No whole-of-government interoperability framework, message standard or conformity test appears anywhere, and the ministry's own programme page names linking government digital systems as the strategic objective without one.
 No change, a first stated position with no earlier account behind it
Eswatini No national interoperability standard is published. The ICT ministry's own report records the e-government interoperability framework under review.
Full recordThe ministry's report to Parliament for 2024/25 records GovStack-model application development and a review of the electronic-government interoperability framework; the framework itself is not held (report).

The draft data governance policy proposes Mchumanisi as the exchange platform, which is an architecture rather than a standard (proposal).

Where standards have been adopted they are sectoral: the health information exchange runs a master patient index with deterministic and probabilistic matching.

More than half of ministries lack meaningful system interoperability, which is the condition a standard would exist to change. Two of the three sources staged for this indicator did not reach the base, so what is held here is thinner than the search behind it.
 No change, a framework under review and none published
Ethiopia The government interoperability framework remains the standing instrument, listed as approved and not superseded (repository entry).
Full recordThe interoperability framework of record is the Ethiopian Government Interoperability Framework, carried as approved in the innovation and technology ministry's own guidelines and standards repository (repository entry).

Digital Ethiopia 2030, approved in November 2025, commits to interoperable digital systems (approval), but names no standard and supersedes no framework.

The repository entry is a document-library listing rather than the framework text, so the base holds that the framework exists and is current without holding what it requires.
 No change
Gambia A national enterprise architecture was validated in May 2026 as the interoperability blueprint, and is not adopted, funded or implemented.
Full recordIt was validated under an international open-standards initiative (validation).

A blueprint validated and not adopted is the same position the data policy is in, and both sit behind an identity system and a data centre that are already running, so the standards layer is arriving after the systems it would govern.
 Advanced
Ghana A national data exchange hub was named again in July 2026 for standardised sharing between institutions, with no design, cost or timetable.
Full recordA hub was launched in 2009 and stalled on funding and unclear mandates (the 2009 system).

The communications minister named a National Data Exchange Hub the government plans to establish, without saying whether it revives that system or replaces it (re-announcement).

The interoperability legal base is being rebuilt through the harmonisation bill in parallel, so a standard, a platform and a statute are all announced and none is in force.
 No change, an old system re-announced with nothing new behind it
Kenya Two health interoperability implementation guides were published in August 2026, both served as continuous builds rather than authorised publications.
Full recordA core guide stands at version 1.0.0 and a patient summary guide at 0.1.0, and both pages state they are not authorised publications; no gazettement, conformance requirement or implementer list is held (core guide, patient summary).

Four foundational digital-health governance documents were launched at a health summit on 21 August 2026, together establishing the architecture for governing, connecting and using health data that three decades of collection had left without a shared framework; the documents are not individually named in the record and no commencement or compliance date is stated (foundational documents).
 Advanced
Lesotho A Government Enterprise Architecture and Interoperability Framework has been under development since the 2025/26 budget, with no further detail published.
Full recordThe budget speech recorded the framework as under development, aimed at improving government efficiency and service delivery and at avoiding fragmented and costly infrastructure deployment across government (budget speech).

No draft, adoption date, standards register or list of the systems bound by it has been published in the eighteen months since.
 Advanced
Madagascar The national interoperability framework is the standing technical standard and gained an institutional basis in law in December 2025.
Full recordThe framework, at version 1.0.2, was reviewed for official publication in November 2022 and sets the general provisions, definitions, scope and foundational principles of state interoperability (framework).

The platform implementing it launched its first stage in April 2026, with the first usage conventions signed by participating institutions (launch).

The capture stops before the framework's own chapters on interoperability levels, governance model, architecture and operational directives, so the base holds the standard's principles and not its technical content.
 Advanced
Malawi The labour market system is being built around the international statistical data-exchange standard, the first such adoption on file.
Full recordThe government's own readiness assessment finds a government-wide interoperability platform and data-exchange standard absent, which is the position at the window's opening and is not formally superseded.

2026-02 - the enterprise service bus was named one of five core programme priorities, with 25 e-services at advanced-stage onboarding onto the national exchange platform.

2026-04 - the country's first integrated labour market information system was built around SDMX, the international statistical data-exchange standard, across six government and labour institutions. One sector adopting an international standard is not a national profile, and it is the only concrete standard the base can name.
 Advanced
Mauritania A public digital service standards framework is under development with German support, and the state agency proposed to operate interoperability has never been created.
Full recordA three-day standards and interoperability workshop was run by the ministry in March 2026 with German support; no framework or standard has been published (workshop).

The agency intended to operate national interoperability was proposed in July 2021 as a public establishment under the digital ministry with about fifty staff, EUR 220,000 to set up and EUR 1.4m a year to run, with a draft creation decree (proposal). A June 2023 open-data strategy proposed reviving that decree to name the same body national interoperability operator, and no adoption, decree or launch is on record (strategy).
 Advanced
Mauritius The e-Government Interoperability Framework dates from 2002 and no successor is held, twenty-four years on.
Full recordCabinet noted a Digital Service Framework on 28 August 2026 setting mandatory principles and standards for every government digital service, from procurement through maintenance (framework). It is noted, not yet issued, and no compliance date is stated.

Version 1.0 was published in November 2002 and remains the standing interoperability instrument (framework, case study).

The interoperability commitments of the 2025-2029 blueprint have produced no published instrument. A country running a mobile identity wallet, an instant payment switch and a Tier IV sovereign facility is doing so on an interoperability framework older than any of them.
 Advanced
Mozambique An electronic government systems interoperability regulation was reviewed in December 2025, stated to set standards for integrating public information systems.
Full recordThe review rests on a single account of institute remarks and the regulation text is not held (review).

No standards register, conformance requirement or list of systems bound by it is published, so the base records that an interoperability rule exists and cannot say what it requires.
 Advanced
Namibia An interoperability framework was drafted in 2016 on the Estonian model and never gazetted; the standards that exist are in signatures and payments.
Full recordA government expression of interest carries the fullest dated official account: a framework drafted by the Office of the Prime Minister's public service information technology management department in 2016, modelled on Estonia's data-exchange layer, with no gazetted framework, adoption date or implementing platform since (timeline).

Where technical standards have been gazetted they are sectoral. The electronic signature regulations of December 2025 set the rules for signature interoperability (signatures), and a determination of July 2026 sets operational and cybersecurity standards inside the national payment system (payments).

A governmental interoperability solution is in service from the Office of the Prime Minister, and an inter-ministry data exchange platform has no record since September 2025.

Ten years from a draft framework, the exchange runs and the standard for it is unpublished.
 Advanced
Nigeria A government enterprise architecture was handed to the technology agency in April 2026 and piloted at four agencies, alongside a digital trustmark.
Full recordThe architecture was built over about two and a half years with a foreign development agency, is hosted by the government infrastructure company and was piloted at the identity commission, customs, immigration and the technology agency (handover, takeover).

A digital trustmark has been in issue since October 2025 (trustmark). Neither carries a published conformance standard, adoption count or list of bound systems.
 Advanced
Rwanda A government-wide enterprise architecture framework stands as the functional equivalent of an interoperability framework, and only an excerpt of version 2.1 is held.
Full recordThe framework document was acquired as an excerpt in August 2026, so it is present in the base as an artefact rather than as a readable standard (framework).

A reference study of interoperability challenges in the public sector is cited rather than absorbed (study). The gap the country's own analysts name most often is the data-sharing platform rather than the standard, which is recorded separately.
 Advanced
Senegal An inventory of state information systems was launched at a July 2026 workshop alongside interim diagnostic results, and the inventory carries no scope, deadline or publication commitment.
Full recordInterim diagnostic results were presented and the inventory launched at a workshop on 29 July 2026, implemented by the state digital agency with donor programme funding (architecture, projects, agenda).

The inventory is the load-bearing commitment: without it there is no list of the systems any interoperability platform would have to reach. It is now under way and carries no scope, deadline or publication commitment.
 Advanced
Somalia The e-government strategy records that no interoperability framework exists; the certificate system shows what standard building blocks look like in one service.
Full recordThe strategy for 2025 to 2029 states the federal government lacks an enterprise architecture and interoperability framework, and prescribes standardised solutions with open interfaces, a data-exchange hub and a national enterprise service bus (strategy).

The high school certificate system connects registration, identity verification and consent, payments and a digital registry so exam results are retrieved automatically and certificates issued digitally (system).

One service built to a pattern is not a published national standard, and nothing states that the next service must follow it.
 Advanced
South Africa The Minimum Interoperability Standards and the government-wide enterprise architecture have an update dated September 2026 and nothing published against it.
Full recordThe standing instruments are the Minimum Interoperability Standards and the government-wide enterprise architecture. The only thing the window records about them is a date: the quarterly reform report carries an update due in September 2026.

Nothing is published, gazetted or consulted in the twelve months, and no conformance requirement, testing regime or department-level compliance figure appears anywhere on the ledger — which matters more than the slipped date, because a standard nobody tests against is a document rather than a standard.
 No change
Sudan A request for proposals for the instant payment system sets real interoperability requirements; the government platform names the principle without publishing a standard.
Full recordVersion 5.0 of the central bank's request for proposals, dated 7 April 2026, requires interoperability with the existing interbank settlement system and real-time programming-interface integration, alongside enterprise security and compliance standards (document).

The ministry's government-institution platform product page names process engineering, smart governance and interoperability as design principles and lists 23 integrated institutions (page).

A procurement specification binds one supplier and a product page binds nobody. No published national interoperability standard exists for other systems to build to.
 Advanced
Uganda A renewed enterprise architecture and interoperability framework was delivered in May 2026, and the government data exchange sits at a 37 per cent agency-integration baseline.
Full recordNeither renewed document is held, and the rollout is not reported (framework, renewal).

The exchange's integration baseline comes from the authority's five-year strategy; two figures for it use different denominators and are not reconciled (exchange, case study).

The single sign-on service is described in an undated agency deck, proxy-dated at ingest, so its figures must not be read as current (sign-on).
 Advanced
Zambia Data interoperability standards are mandatory for every ministry, province and spending agency, with an annual compliance audit schedule.
Full recordThe state ICT institute issued public service ICT data interoperability standards that bind every ministry, province and spending agency, and carry an annual compliance audit schedule (standards).

Together with the electronic government statute they set the data management and governance requirements for government data (scope).

No audit finding, compliance rate or exemption is published, so whether the standards are met is not established - and officials pursuing the statute's amendment still name fragmented, vendor-locked systems as the problem.
 No change
Zimbabwe An enterprise architecture blueprint was handed over in May 2026 and a civil registration and health platform is in build; no national interoperability standard is published.
Full recordThe enterprise-architecture framework built over 2023 and 2024 was handed to government ICT directors in May 2026, with a government service bus announced for once-only data sharing across ministries (handover, priorities).

The registrar-general's ICT deputy told the regional registrars' meeting on 18 July 2026 that a secure platform to exchange birth and death notifications is being implemented, with health systems joined through standardised interfaces and automated validation against the national population register; middleware design is due in the fourth quarter of 2026 and a three-province pilot in the first half of 2027 (presentation).

A national health information exchange platform went to formal procurement on 29 May 2026, with bids closing on 22 June (procurement).

No published national interoperability standard or data-exchange specification is held.

National quality standards

Country Developments Progress
Algeria The standardisation body published a draft national work programme for 2026; no count of standards in force is held.
Full recordThe body was created as a public establishment by decree of 21 February 1998, taking standardisation over from the industrial property institute.

2026-02 - it published a draft national standardisation work programme for 2026. It is explicitly a draft work programme, not a record of adopted standards, and the base holds no count of standards in force, no adoption rate and no conformity-assessment output.

The national accreditation and conformity network this ledger holds separately is the other half of the quality infrastructure, and neither is measured.
 Advanced
Botswana The standards bureau publishes an annual standards-development programme, under a statute in force since 1995.
Full record1995 — the Standards Act establishes the Botswana Bureau of Standards and the Standards Council.

2026-04-16 — issue 341 of the annual standardisation work programme was published, setting the pipeline of standards-development projects.

2025-11-21 — a national quality standard for school uniforms was developed and published, on the minister’s account to Parliament.

No ICT or data standard from the programme is separately identified in the base.
 Advanced
Burundi A national quality policy was adopted in February 2022, and the standards bureau signed a mutual-recognition protocol with a neighbour in August 2025.
Full record2022-02-02 — the Council of Ministers adopted the Politique Nationale Qualite as the government's base instrument for establishing and supervising the national quality infrastructure system, setting its objectives, roadmap and timetable. The policy text itself is not held.

2025-08-29 — the standards and quality control bureau signed a memorandum with the Congolese control office covering exchange of information on standards and quality assurance, metrology, conformity assessment and pre- and post-shipment inspection, with progressive mutual recognition of conformity certificates. No official page carrying the protocol was located, so it is held through trade press.
 Advanced
Cameroon Quality-of-service monitoring platforms were handed to the regulator in January 2026, replacing manual inspection.
Full recordAt the window's opening the regulator issued sanctions off manual inspections, fining two operators in July 2025, with no dedicated monitoring platform on record.

2026-01-07 - platforms were handed over by the national connectivity project and the World Bank, and field inspections of three operators followed at Douala on 13 and 14 April 2026.

No sanction is on record since the platforms were handed over, and no measurement out of them has been published, so whether the new capability has changed either enforcement or the reported quality is unestablished.

2026-08-13 - a finance ministry order reclassified the regulator from the third to the second category of public establishments, set on its average realised budget across the 2022, 2023 and 2024 financial years, affecting its institutional standing and pay structure; the order text and the budget figures behind it are not published.
 Advanced
Central African Republic A spectrum-monitoring system was delivered in June 2025 and a quality-of-service package remains under contract, with a publication site financed and not live.
Full recordA spectrum contract signed in October 2024 completed in June 2025, and a quality-of-service package signed the same month carries no completion date, unchanged at the February 2026 revision of the procurement plan. The plan's actual-amount column is unpopulated throughout, so no contract value is published.

2026-02-02 — the minister stated the regulator had obtained financing for a website publishing operators' quality-of-service data. No site was recorded live on a third test in July 2026, and the regulator still publishes no statistics of any kind.

If delivered it would close the standing absence of any independent measurer in this country: every connectivity figure in this report comes from an operator, a ministry or an international estimate.
 Advanced
Chad The regulator ran the fifteenth cycle of its service-quality audit and inaugurated strategic regulatory platforms described as a shift to continuous data-driven regulation.
Full recordThe fifteenth audit cycle ran September to October 2025 with findings reported in February 2026; the audit reports themselves are not held, and the regulator's own entity page states no penalty it imposed is established, against a held source recording a 2023 fine (audit, upgrades, fibre).

The platforms were inaugurated by the minister in June 2026, described by the regulator as a shift from compliance-based to continuous data-driven regulation; no cost, supplier, data-retention rule, legal basis or output is on record (platforms).

Fifteen audit cycles with no established penalty, and a new data-driven regulatory apparatus with no legal basis stated, are the two halves of regulation here.
 Advanced
Comoros The 2024 quality-of-service audit is still the only edition, and the regulator has never published a statutory annual report.
Full recordThe 2024 audit measured both operators against twenty-three quality indicators over three months and was presented at a Moroni workshop, with a quarterly bulletin requested by the minister and none produced. Its finding was coverage gaps requiring operators to redouble their efforts, and eight years after market liberalisation it is the first published quality measurement in the base. No 2025 or 2026 audit and no quarterly bulletin is on file.

An annual activity report has been required by statute since promulgation on 2 February 2024, and the regulator's own site states that none has yet been published - an empty series the regulator states itself.
 Stalled
Congo The regulator's two-month deadline for completing subscriber identification expired around end-2025 with no result on file.
Full recordRegistration is mandatory. A survey of 18 localities found cards sold pre-activated and without identity documents in twelve named towns, with only two localities fully compliant, and the regulator gave the two operators two further months to complete identification.

The two-month deadline expired around end-2025 and no compliance result, sanction, follow-up survey or extension is on file. Operators blamed distributors' resellers and undertook to sanction them, and the base holds no evidence any sanction followed.

Quality enforcement went the other way: 2026-04 - the regulator served formal notices on both mobile operators after drive tests in 20 interior localities, with a six-month clock and per-department scores published, one operator absent from fourth-generation service in five named localities against eleven interior localities now covered where none was at end-2024, the next campaign falling due around October 2026.

Full recordAuditor certification is running against the state security reference framework, with 88 auditors certified at an Abidjan-Plateau ceremony and a further certification announced for experts bringing information systems into conformity.

The reference framework's own text is not held, so what an audit against it tests is not checkable from anything on file. No register of certified auditors, no list of systems audited and no conformity finding has been published, and nothing states whether certification is a condition of anything.

DR Congo The regulator installed service-quality monitoring equipment and the minister ordered operators to return units consumed without justification (order).

 Advanced
Egypt A 2025 national standard adopts the international smart-metering data-exchange standard, on a default to international standards.
Full recordThe body is the sole official one for preparing and issuing national standards, founded by presidential decree in 1957.

A ministerial decision of 2022 provides that where no national standard exists, an international or foreign standard applies, from a named list - a default that makes the national catalogue a supplement rather than a gate.

2025-10 - the register carries a national standard adopting the international smart-metering data-exchange standard, giving electricity metering a common exchange basis.
 Advanced
Equatorial Guinea A service-quality regulation of 2018 is confirmed in force through the regulator's own index, with only its title and date held.
Full recordThe index also lists an inspection and sanctions regulation of 2020 and a broadband-market update of February 2026, neither captured separately (regulatory index).

So the country has published quality-of-service rules and the base cannot read them; no measurement, inspection outcome or sanction under any of the three is held.
 Advanced
Eritrea Standardisation rests on the 1995 proclamation establishing the standards institution as sole national organ (proclamation).
Full recordThe Eritrean Standards Institution was established by proclamation in 1995 as an autonomous body with legal personality and the sole national organ for standardisation, quality assurance and metrology, with powers to prepare compulsory Eritrean standards, affix conformity marks, test and certify import and export products, and inspect (proclamation).

Nothing in the window amended that proclamation, and the base holds no Eritrean standard made under it that bears on digital systems, data formats or interoperability.

The institution exists and its output in this field is not on the record.

Eswatini Nineteen new national standards were designated in 2025 and certification cottage factories began construction in December.
Full recordThe standards authority is constituted by an Act assented in 2001; a 2025 account of the new designations cites a later Standards and Quality Act of 2023 as the enabling statute, and that Act is not held (Act).

Nineteen new national standards were designated across electricity, fuel facilities, liquefied petroleum gas, food production, beverages, climate change adaptation and household consumer products (designations).

Construction began in December 2025 on certification cottage factories at Lugongolweni under a scheme aimed at conformity for the smallest producers (factories).

No count of certified organisations, and no conformity-assessment accreditation, is published.
 Advanced
Ethiopia National livestock data standards were endorsed in July 2026 and cascaded through training of trainers to more than 300 experts and collectors.
Full recordThe standards were developed with the agriculture ministry, endorsed, and then cascaded on a training-of-trainers model (livestock data standards).

They underpin a livestock information system drawing on five priority databases, which repositions animal identification from an export tool to a national asset register and links it to disease surveillance and vaccination records (the same account).

The account is the implementing organisation's own, after four years with a team embedded in the ministry, and is uncorroborated in the base.

Gabon At a departmental capital the incumbent sells data bundles on a network whose coverage comes and goes while validity runs down normally.
Full recordReporting of 25 August 2026 sets out prepaid credit and bundle validity running against a service that intermittently is not there, and puts the regulator's inaction to the president of its regulatory council (service quality).

No regulator decision, quality-of-service determination or sanction is published, so the obligation is asserted in reporting and nowhere else in the base.
 Regressed
Ghana The standards authority Act of 2022 is in force, and a metrology institute project entered its second phase.
Full recordThe founding statute came into force on 30 June 2022, establishing the standards authority and its mandate for national standardisation, conformity assessment and metrology, and was amended by the accreditation service Act of 2023.

2026-03-19 - the authority and a German partner institute held a steering committee on the second phase of a project to modernise the metrology system toward a self-sustaining, internationally recognised national metrology institute.

The Act's operative text is not held - the legal database's viewer failed on two attempts and only the citation and history page was captured - so what the mandate obliges in detail rests on the citation rather than the text.
 Advanced
Kenya Equipment vendor and public access centre licence conditions were gazetted in August 2026, and a distributor licence made mandatory before type approval.
Full recordThe vendor conditions carry a twelve-month minimum warranty, returns, after-sales support and record-keeping duties, and both categories were gazetted in the same notice after public consultation, effective 30 days from it and published for free download (gazette notice, consumer protection).

A separate distributor licence is mandatory before equipment type approval and customs clearance, at KSh 1m or three years for non-compliance; fee, holding threshold and grandfathering are unestablished and the gazette notice is not held (distributor licence).
 Advanced
Lesotho Quality-of-service rules have been in force since 2023, and the regulator's own testing puts compliance at 75 per cent with speeds far below target.
