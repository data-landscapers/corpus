National social card

Vers une base de données unifiée : La carte sociale nationale prend forme — Eco Times DZ, 6 September 2026

Algeria's High Commission for Digitalisation held the fifth meeting of the working group defining the data and indicators for a national social card on 3 September 2026. Algeria Opens Tender to Extend Mobile Coverage to 3,000 Underserved Areas — Tech Africa News, 9 September 2026

Algeria's Postal and Electronic Communications Regulatory Authority launched a competitive tender on 7 September to extend mobile wireless coverage to 3,000 sparsely populated areas, under its universal service mandate in Law no. 18-04 of 10 May 2018.

Algeria's post and telecommunications minister signed an order on 3 September 2026, published in the Official Journal on 13 September, setting the timetable for the tender to provide mobile coverage to sparsely populated localities under the universal service scheme. The tender was to be launched on 6 September and advertised on 7 September, with 45 days to bid and results due 30 days after bids are opened.

Universal-service mobile coverage of 201 sites (Mobilis specification)

Arrêté du 4 août 2026 portant approbation du cahier des charges relatif à la fourniture du service universel des communications électroniques pour la couverture par un réseau public de télécommunications mobiles de deux cent un (201) sites, confiée à l'opérateur « ATM Mobilis S.P.A » — Journal officiel de la République algérienne (Secrétariat général du Gouvernement), 23 September 2026

Algeria's Ministry of Post and Telecommunications approved, by order of 4 August 2026 published in the Official Journal on 23 September 2026, a universal-service specification giving ATM Mobilis the task of providing 4G mobile coverage at 201 sites, funded site by site from the universal-service fund. The regulator ARPCE pays a fixed amount per site on acceptance; late delivery draws a penalty of up to 10%. Per-site amounts are in annexed tables.

Public computer rooms and free internet points

Angola's telecoms minister, Mário Oliveira, said in Benguela on 16 September 2026 that the Angola Digital project, promoted by the communications development fund FADCOM, now has about 180 free public internet access points in 18 of the country's 21 provinces. Identity-card universalisation plan

Angolan Government issues more than 17 million citizen's identity cards — Angola Newswire (reporting Angola Press Agency), 24 September 2026

Angola has issued 17,166,000 national identity cards at home and abroad, the national director of registries, Aristides Marques, said in Luanda on 24 September 2026. Daily production capacity has risen from 12,000 to 60,000 cards, cards are now printed in all 21 provinces, and the target is a card for every registered adult by December 2026. A draft law would make the card the only valid identity document.

Politique d'identification et d'inclusion: 1 422 cartes d'égalité des chances aux personnes handicapées — La Nation (Bénin), 23 September 2026

Benin's identification agency ANIP handed 1,422 equal-opportunity cards for persons with disabilities to the Ministry of Family and Social Action in Cotonou on 18 September 2026. The cards, provided for under a 2017 law, give access to rights in health, education, employment and transport. ANIP issued 4,718 cards in 2024 and 2025 and has about 4,000 applications for 2026.

Bridging Digital Dividends: Safeguarding Disabled Persons Online, Offline — Cameroon Tribune, 2 September 2026

Egypt

Post-distribution monitoring of refugee cash assistance

Year-End Post-Distribution Monitoring Report of UNHCR's Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance to Refugees in Egypt — UNHCR Egypt, 28 February 2025

UNHCR gave multipurpose cash to an average of 24,335 refugee and asylum-seeker households in Egypt during 2024, paid every two months through Egypt Post offices, and raised transfer values by about 32% over the year. Its December survey reached 393 households, and 99% said the cash let them buy items or services they could not afford before.

Equatorial Guinea

Refugee legal framework

Equatorial Guinea | Rights Mapping and Analysis Platform — United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), Rights Mapping and Analysis Platform, 2 September 2026

UNHCR's rights-mapping platform records that Equatorial Guinea is party to the 1951 Refugee Convention, its 1967 Protocol and the 1969 OAU Convention but has no domestic law for refugee status determination, so asylum seekers have no formal procedure to apply. It acceded to the Kampala Convention on displaced persons in 2019 and lists five asylum seekers.

It is run by the transport ministry with the road transport council, police, emergency services and the Anti-Corruption Commission; reports can be anonymous and are tracked by a reference number.

Refugee women's SIM registration and mobile use

Closing the digital divide for refugee women means understanding their full experience of connectivity — GSMA Mobile for Humanitarian Innovation (Zainab Moallin), 10 September 2026 UgandaTanzaniaMauritaniaGlobal

The GSMA published research on 10 September 2026 on refugee women's mobile use, drawing on fieldwork with Venezuelan refugees in Colombia and in Mauritania's Mbera camp. It reports that refugee women in Ethiopia are twice as likely to have SIMs registered in someone else's name, that in Uganda about a quarter use the internet against half of men, and that in Tanzania identity rules push women to register SIMs through intermediaries.

Kenya

Children exposed to unwanted sexual content online

When the danger comes through a child’s phone: One in five children exposed to sexual content online — Daily Nation, 5 September 2026

A UNICEF report finds about one million internet-using Kenyan children aged 12–17, 20.1%, have been exposed to unwanted sexual content online, the third-highest rate of 21 countries surveyed. Most victims knew their abusers, WhatsApp was the platform most used, and about 1% of cases are reported to police.

Kenya is extending its digital learning initiative to 10,384 public junior schools, distributing smartboards and laptops, digitising curriculum content and training teachers. PUL Condemns Government’s Assault on Freedom of Information and Transparency; Calls for Immediate Withdrawal of Confidentiality Policy Undermining the FOI Act - Liberia Public Radio — Liberia Public Radio, 24 October 2026

Malawi

Malawian farmers help shape digital plant-health services — NIBIO (Norwegian Institute of Bioeconomy Research), 4 September 2026

Farmers, extension workers, agro-dealers and policymakers tested Malawi's digital plant-health service and proposed changes before wider rollout. Morocco

Disability data and access measures

Morocco disability rate rises to 8.7% as access gaps persist — Hespress English, 3 September 2026

Morocco's third national disability survey puts disability prevalence at 8.7% of the population, up from 6.8% in 2014, according to preliminary results presented in Rabat on 3 September 2026. The survey covered 20,412 households; only 20% of disabled people reported internet access, and their employment rate was 12.7%. Officials said broader detection tools account for part of the increase.

86% من المغاربة يستخدمون منصات التواصل و'واتساب' في الصدارة — Assabah, 4 September 2026

Sonergia's seventh annual social media barometer for Morocco found 86% of the population using social platforms in 2026, five points above 2025. WhatsApp leads at 83%, followed by Facebook at 64%, while TikTok rose eight points to 32%. Only 39% of Moroccans trust news circulating on the platforms outside official media, against 61% who express some distrust.

Nigeria

Smartphone ownership

Nigeria Smartphone Study: A Nationwide Analysis of Smartphone Ownership and Digital Application Usage — KPMG Nigeria; Orange Group, 1 September 2026

KPMG Nigeria and Orange Group's smartphone study, published in September 2026 from 13,251 respondents in 12 cities, puts smartphone ownership at 75% in 2025, up from 64% in 2023. Android runs on 96% of smartphones and WhatsApp on 95%; OPay is installed on 69% and PalmPay on 29%, and over a third of mobile subscribers were still on 2G in May 2026.

Financial inclusion

How Nigeria can bring 29m adults into financial system — NIMASA director Ani — The Sun Nigeria, 4 September 2026

Odunayo Ani, finance director at Nigeria's maritime agency NIMASA, told a Lagos conference that about 28.9 million adults, 26 per cent, remain outside formal finance, citing EFInA's 2023 survey. She named distance, documentation demands, high charges and the digital divide as barriers, and said banking agents rose from 83,560 in 2019 to about 1.92 million in 2024.

Access to Financial Services in Nigeria (A2F) 2023 Survey Report — EFInA (Enhancing Financial Innovation & Access), 18 July 2024

EFInA's eighth Access to Financial Services in Nigeria survey, based on 26,930 interviews, puts formal financial inclusion at 64% in 2023, against 57% in 2020. The full report was published in July 2024.

Report: digital finance usage hits 64% — The Nation, 17 September 2026

EFInA's 2026 Access to Financial Services in Nigeria survey, released in Abuja and reported on 17 September 2026, puts use of digital financial services at 64 per cent and financial exclusion at 21 per cent. Mobile money use rose from 12 per cent in 2023 to 38 per cent, while 53 per cent of adults in the poorest wealth quintile remain excluded and 92 per cent of agricultural workers are paid in cash.

Nigeria's Financial Inclusion Reaches 79% As EFInA's A2F 2026 Survey Points To Next Challenge — Independent Newspaper Nigeria, 22 September 2026

EFInA's ninth Access to Financial Services in Nigeria survey, released on 16 September 2026, found that 79% of adults, about 94.2 million people, use a financial product or service. Formal inclusion reached 73%, above the 70% target of the National Financial Inclusion Strategy. The Central Bank of Nigeria said the focus now shifts to usage, affordability, trust and financial health.

Nigeria hits 73% financial inclusion target, but 60.4 million adults remain vulnerable — Nairametrics, 5 October 2026

The 2026 Access to Financial Services survey by EFInA and Nigeria's statistics bureau found formal financial inclusion at 73% of adults, above the 70% national target, and use of digital financial services at 64.4%, up from 45% in 2023. Only 30.7% of formally included adults are financially healthy, leaving about 60.4 million adults vulnerable. The survey covered 18,679 adults between April and June 2026.

TikTok sued over Nigeria blackout — ITWeb Africa, 6 October 2026
