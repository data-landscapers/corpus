Progress values. Advanced — a system entered service, a stage was completed or an instrument was made. Stalled — a stated target passed without delivery. Regressed — an instrument was withdrawn or neutralised, or a reported position worsened. Mixed — the indicator's instruments moved in different directions in the period; the clause after the comma names which moved which way. No change — the base holds a standing position and nothing in the period touched it. No evidence — the base holds nothing on this indicator at all. A value may carry a qualifying clause after a comma, as in Advanced, regulations still pending.

The place reports do not share one window; the period above is the range they span.

Ethiopia OpenAgriNet launched in February 2026 on open standards with consent-based data governance, the clearest sectoral operationalisation of open-data principles in the window (launch).

The open-data policy guideline was recorded as still in draft in 2018 (supervision report) and the base holds no later statement that it was adopted, so the policy instrument itself has not moved in eight years.

Gambia The 2024-2027 open data strategy records no registry, no legal framework and no portal, with targets set for 2026.
Full recordThe strategy is unusually plain about its own starting point: The Gambia has no data registry, no open-data legal framework and no portal. Its targets fall in 2026.

Nothing in the base records any of the three arriving. The strategy is the instrument this indicator asks for; the portal it provides for is a separate question the ledger carries as its own row.

Ghana The open data initiative dates to 2012 and its national data sharing policy, drafted under a 2018-2019 revamp, is still described as being finalised.
Full recordThe initiative dates to 2012 and is led by the technology agency; a draft national data sharing policy incorporating open data was produced under a 2018-2019 revamp and is still described as being finalised. The draft policy itself failed to fetch twice and is not held, so the agency's own description is what stands.

2026-03 - the statistical service announced an upgraded public statistics catalogue for release that month, with improved discovery, navigation and exploration tools.

A catalogue upgrade is not a policy. No dataset count, licence, publication schedule or reuse figure for the initiative appears on this ledger.
 No change, a fourteen-year-old initiative whose policy is still described as being finalised
Guinea-Bissau No open data policy exists; the access to information bill remains in development and no portal is on the record.
Full recordThe base holds no Guinean open data policy, licence or portal.

The adjacent instrument is the access to information bill the ledger carries as in development. Publication that does happen is done by institutions for their own purposes: the state budget volume and the fund's national summary data page are the base's only routine releases.

Kenya A state-run data marketplace is proposed at KSh 396m to build and operate, offering more than 1,000 datasets over five years.
Full recordIt would be overseen by a proposed national council, with pricing deferred to a later phase and no valuation method stated (marketplace, draft policy).

The proposal is to sell state data rather than to open it, and it sits inside the same draft policy that leaves the personal and non-personal boundary undefined, which is the question a marketplace would have to answer first.

Lesotho The government’s own digital policy records that open government data legislation has yet to be promulgated.
Full record2024-12-30 — the National Digital Policy states Lesotho has yet to promulgate legislation on open government data, the most current statement the base holds.

2025-09-24 — the Prime Minister told a leaders’ symposium that Lesotho had adopted a national digital framework and was piloting digital public infrastructure, working with regional and European partners to validate a data governance policy.

The absence was searched on 31 August 2026: no portal, no policy, no dataset inventory. A data management policy was signed in June 2026 and is not an open-data instrument.

Liberia No adopted open-data policy; the statistics office publishes public-use microdata under access conditions instead.
Full recordThe anonymised public-use dataset for the 2024 women's empowerment and nutrition survey was released on the statistics office's own microdata portal under stated access conditions, with the confidentiality basis cited as the 2004 statistics Act rather than an open-data instrument.

That is open-data practice in the absence of a policy. The base holds no adopted national open data policy and no government open-data licence.

Libya A draft national open-data policy was issued in November 2025 alongside the open government data platform; no adopted text has followed.
Full recordThe identity authority launched the platform on 27 November 2025 with a draft national open-data policy and a National Technical Guide covering standards and metadata for adoption by government entities (launch).

Nine months on, the base holds no adopted policy, no adopting decision and no list of the entities that have taken the guide up (workshop account).

Madagascar Three of four known open-data platforms were reachable in August 2026 and no whole-of-government open-data policy or mandate is held.
Full recordA capture of August 2026 confirms institution-specific resources rather than a national programme: a licensed parliamentary open-data interface, a gated national-statistics microdata platform and an unconfirmed general portal (capture, microdata, portal).

The finance ministry's own portal does not resolve. No open-data policy, public-sector information reuse instrument or publication mandate is held for the country.

Malawi A national open data portal launched in September 2025; the only open data policy covers extractives.
Full recordThe country's sole document titled an open data policy is scoped to extractive-industry data and runs to 2027. Nothing on file sets a default, a licence or a release calendar over government-held data generally.

2025-09 - the open government secretariat announced a new public-facing open data portal for budget, project and service-delivery data. The announcement does not link to the live portal, and no dataset count is stated.

2026-03 - a government circular mandated use of the national electronic procurement system across all ministries and agencies from 1 April 2026, which standardises how procurement data is captured before it is published.
 Advanced, a portal launched while the only policy instrument stays scoped to one sector
Mali The statistics institute publishes an open data licence agreement and worked through migration to a second-generation portal platform in June 2026.
Full recordBoth are the institute's own, covering its data rather than government data generally (licence, migration).

No dataset count, portal address or open-data policy instrument is held, so what has moved is one institution's terms of use and not a government position. The institute also publishes a microdata catalogue (catalogue).

Mauritius The national open data policy of May 2017 stands unamended; a statistics platform launched in 2025 is the practical movement.
Full recordThe policy was published in May 2017 and is still the governing instrument (policy).

The statistics office launched a data platform with United Nations development agency support on World Statistics Day 2025 (launch).

The 2026 national data strategy is the first instrument to revisit the policy's ground (strategy). No dataset count, licence statement or publication mandate under the 2017 policy is held.

Morocco The open-data programme has four published procedure volumes and a four-strand action plan, with a steering committee of ten bodies established in December 2019.
Full recordThe published procedure set runs to manuals for open-data officers and data managers from April 2021, a data-inventory methodology from February 2023 and a standards and formats manual from December 2024 (inventory methodology, standards manual).

A steering committee of ten bodies was established in December 2019 and a readiness assessment carried out in 2020, after which a four-strand action plan covering legal, technical, organisational and usage work was adopted (governance). The assessment report itself is not held.

Namibia No open data policy exists. The Access to Information Act is gazetted and uncommenced, and applications for the Information Commissioner opened in February 2026.
Full recordThe Act is gazetted and not commenced — section 86 leaves commencement to the Minister and no commencement notice is held — but the National Assembly invited applications in February 2026 for the Information Commissioner and Deputy under the appointment regulations (invitation).

The closest thing to a publication regime is spatial: the 2015 policy provides for public access and a metadata catalogue (policy), and the statistics agency tendered in October 2025 to redesign the public geoportal that serves it (tender).

The agency also runs a microdata catalogue and data portals page.

An open data policy would say what every public body must publish. Nothing on this base does, and the statute that would compel disclosure has waited four years for a commencement notice.

Niger A renovated nutrition information portal launched in September 2025, on an access-to-information charter whose implementing decree was never made.
Full recordThe statistics institute's director launched the renovated nutrition information portal on 18 September 2025, described as an interactive digital library giving access to nutrition reports, studies and surveys.

The ministry also recorded the handover of the household survey on access to information technology on 13 May 2026 and its distribution to the regulator and the state broadcaster.

The nearest thing to an open-data instrument is the 2011 charter of access to public information, which establishes a general right of access and free electronic delivery where available; article 35 leaves its implementing decree to a later act that has not been made. No open-data licence, catalogue or machine-readable release policy is on file.

Nigeria A national data action plan for 2025 to 2028 was launched in December 2025 above the 2018 open data policy.
Full recordThe action plan launched on 15 December 2025 is a coordinated national data roadmap, the first national data instrument since 2018; the base holds the statistics bureau's catalogue record because the published document carries no extractable text.

The 2018 open data policy remains the only national instrument expressly titled one and still governs the national portal and agency publication obligations.

Neither has a compliance report, review or dataset inventory behind it, so whether agencies meet their publication obligations is still unestablished.

Rwanda The 2017 data revolution policy expired in 2022 with no replacement, and the 2025 data-sharing policy puts open data outside its own scope.
Full recordCabinet approved the National Data Revolution Policy on 26 April 2017 for the period to 2022 (communiqué). It has expired.

The data governance framework published in November 2025 carries a public access classification within a wider package of classification, metadata, quality and incident-management guidance (framework) — a classification, not a publication duty.

The data-sharing policy that sits beside it states open data falls outside its scope, so nothing on the base obliges any body to publish.

Nothing else in the base names an open-data instrument, portal or dataset catalogue. The nearest published artefact is the citizen's budget, which is a document rather than a portal. The absence is dated and searched rather than merely unexamined.

Senegal A national geospatial portal launched in November 2025 and the statistics agency extended its open-data platform to the regions.
Full recordThe geospatial portal publishes official base and thematic data to the public, with a standards-conforming metadata and cataloguing editor, a map library offering consultation, download and print-order services, and thematic data organised in five poles covering agriculture, the blue economy, social services, environment and infrastructure — the clearest open-data portal launch in the window, against a dormant government data portal.

The statistics agency's own route reached the regions: a four-day workshop closed at Louga on 24 June 2026 training about thirty participants from the region's data-contributing services in the exchange standard, multidimensional modelling and use of the platform.

The commitment behind them is a strategy measure rather than a charter: the 2023-2028 data strategy commits to a legal framework adapted to data opening and to a national publication platform; no standalone open-data law or decree was found.
