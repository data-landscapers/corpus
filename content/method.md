# Method

*This page is a first pass, written to be replaced. It is `content/method.md`; `scripts/method.py` converts it and wraps it in the site chrome, and nothing else on the site depends on its structure. Write it as you would write anything — headings, sub-headings, prose.*

The Corpus is a compiled view over an intelligence base on data governance and digital transformation in Africa. It holds what has been published and could be verified, and it says plainly where it holds nothing.

## What becomes a source

Only primary or first-hand evidence: official announcements, regulations, filings, court records, company statements, datasets, on-the-record reporting, primary documents, published academic work. A source need not break news — a dated explainer, a methodology note or a reference report is admissible.

Second-hand syntheses are treated as leads rather than sources. An AI summary or a news digest has already compressed and paraphrased its inputs, so ingesting one would launder its errors into an authoritative-looking page and break the audit trail. They are mined for the primaries they cite; the primaries are ingested and the synthesis discarded.

Source bodies are stored verbatim, never paraphrased, and are not republished here. Every record links to the publisher.

## How figures are dated

Every time-varying figure is written with the date it was true — "ranked 156th (2020)", never "ranks 156th". This is a phrasing rule rather than a freshness threshold, which means staleness stays visible on the page instead of being tracked somewhere else.

The event date is not the publication date. Where a source re-reports an older announcement, the event date is established from the primary or marked as unknown, and a secondary outlet's own date never becomes the event's.

Money is carried in the currency the announcing party used, and any US dollar figure is a dated conversion. Without that, one commitment becomes three different-looking numbers.

## What the base does not claim

**A gap in the evidence is not a gap in the world.** Where nothing is held, the page says so, dated. An absent heading means no evidence on file for that place and period, not that nothing happened.

**Coverage is uneven on purpose.** The base goes deep where there is a question being asked of it and stays thin elsewhere. Thin coverage of a country nobody is writing about is the intended state, not a backlog.

**Capture is not endorsement.** Holding a source, or profiling an organisation, implies no view of it.

## Editions

A published file is never revised. The dated CSVs and PDFs on this site are citable artefacts: a new edition is cut when the content changes, and the old one is retained at its own address rather than overwritten. If a figure has moved, the current edition says so; the earlier one still says what it said.

## Corrections

Errors are corrected in public. Where a correction changes a finding rather than a typo, it is published as a new version with its own date and a statement of what was wrong.
