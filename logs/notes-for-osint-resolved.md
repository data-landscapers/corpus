**6** (2026-08-14) — **The UNITEL outage has two incompatible durations in the base.** `2026-07-30-...unitel-restores-services...` documents restoration in stages — localised from 11:45 on 29 July, full 2G/3G national coverage about 23:00 on 30 July, messaging and electronic airtime back on 31 July, i.e. three to four days. `2026-08-10-angola-unitel-cyberattack-customer-reaction` frames the same event as running 28 July to 5 August, nine days, for about 20.8 million customers. They may be reconcilable — full restoration against residual degradation, and the held source does note card terminals still unreliable four days in — but nothing in the base states which. CORPUS has kept the better-evidenced staged account in `AGO-monthly.md` and has not adopted the nine-day framing. *(Raised by BUILD, 2026-08-14. Was note 12.)*

*(**Resolved — strike it, 2026-08-16. The base now states which, and it is the nine-day account; CORPUS was on the wrong side and the residue is CORPUS's to fix, not OSINT's.** Two sources ingested on 2026-08-08 settle it. `2026-08-06-angola-unitel-completes-restoration-of-mobile-services` is a press note the operator sent Angop stating that voice, messaging, mobile data, internet access and third-party services carried over its network were all restored on **Wednesday 5 August, nine days after the attack of 28 July**; `2026-08-06-unitel-restores-core-services-after-nationwide-cyberattack` is the operator's English market update saying the same, and dates the attack to the early hours of 28 July. `2026-08-04-angola-unitel-attack-continues-to-affect-business-luanda` corroborates from the demand side — a week in, Multicaixa Express was unusable and Luanda bank branches were queueing one to two hours. So the 2026-07-30 source CORPUS preferred was documenting an **intermediate stage, not completion**, which is precisely the "full restoration against residual degradation" reading this note offered as a possibility. Nothing is owed in OSINT: the base holds the operator's own account, dated, and the two records are a stated `keep both` under `INGEST.md` §2. **The open item is in CORPUS**: `outputs/reports/AGO/ledger.csv` has already adopted nine days on `AGO-infra.cybersec-critical-national-infrastructure-designation`, while `AGO-monthly.md` still opens on "A four-day national mobile outage from 28 July" and states "Restoration ran four days" — so the monthly contradicts the ledger it is rendered from. That is a BUILD correction and it is tracked as such.)*

*(**Added on the move, 2026-08-16: the CORPUS residue is closed too, so this note leaves nothing behind on either side.** The annotation above closed the OSINT half but left an open item in CORPUS, and a note carrying an open item does not belong in a resolved file. **8** (2026-08-14) — **OSINT's `index/` was built from CORPUS's workroot and holds `raw/` only. It needs one rebuild in an OSINT session, and CORPUS's build is stopped until it gets one.** `index/meta.json` records **10,178** files over roots that include `wiki/`, `sweep/` and `budget-archive/`; a walk of those roots in OSINT counts **13,580**. A build run in OSINT could not have produced 10,178 — that is `raw/` alone, which was all that resolved in CORPUS's workroot — so the index on OSINT's disk was written by a CORPUS run on 2026-08-13, the timestamp `files.jsonl` and `links.jsonl` still carry. It is missing all 2,410 `wiki/` files, 632 `budget-archive/`, 310 `sweep/`, 23 `new-budget/`, 12 `reviews/`, 9 `queries/` and 6 `new/`. This is the write across the boundary that `vault_lib._assert_own_index()` was added on 2026-08-14 to make impossible: it landed the day before the guard existed, and a guard cannot undo what preceded it. **Anything in OSINT that asks the index about the wiki is reading a hole.** CORPUS's own reads were never wrong — `slug_urls()` takes only `folder == "raw"` rows, and for those the index is complete — but CORPUS now *stops* rather than reads: its workroot exposes all eight roots as of today, so it walks 13,580 against a meta of 10,178, finds the index stale, and raises `ForeignIndex` rather than rebuild a tree that is not its own. The repair is `python scripts/build-index.py` in an OSINT session, about 77 seconds. *(Found by CC reviewing the workroot change, 2026-08-14.)*

*(**No longer urgent, same day.** This note was written while CORPUS still read OSINT's index and was stopped by it. CORPUS now builds its own, so nothing here waits on the rebuild and nothing in CORPUS is wrong in the meantime. The note stands because the index on OSINT's disk is still a partial one that OSINT's own lint and queries will read as complete, but it is OSINT's hygiene now, at OSINT's convenience.)*

*(**Resolved — strike it, 2026-08-16.** The rebuild happened, in OSINT, this morning. `index/meta.json` is stamped `2026-08-16T11:50:59` over all eight roots — `raw`, `wiki`, `new`, `new-budget`, `budget-archive`, `reviews`, `sweep`, `queries` — with 11,044 files and 98,272 links, taking 629 seconds. The hole this note described is closed: `wiki/` is in the index and anything in OSINT asking the index about the wiki now gets an answer. The file count is below the 13,580 the note projected, which is expected — `budget-archive/` has since been moved out of the tree, taking its 632 files with it. `vault.db` was not rebuilt and that is note 4's business, not this one's.)*
