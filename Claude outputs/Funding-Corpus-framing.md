# Funding Corpus — framing

*Drafted 17 November 2026 from the Purpose note and Bill's answers of the same day. To be read before any one-pager or proposal is written.*

## The ask

- **Applicant.** Data Landscapers Ltd, a UK company. It is not a charity, so each funder's eligibility rules must be checked; the tracker records this.
- **What it buys.** The running costs of Corpus and the OSINT collection that feeds it. Corpus and OSINT are presented as one system in two halves: public reports and data, built from a private evidence base that is kept private for copyright reasons.
- **How it is paid.** One payment up front, with an expense report every quarter that attaches invoices. It is renewed once the money has been spent and accounted for.
- **Not in the budget.** Bill's own time. His income is covered elsewhere, so every dollar goes to the public good. Expect funders to ask what happens if one person steps back; option B answers that.

**Option B: core costs plus a data administrator or analyst.** This is option A plus a part-time post. Size and location are to be decided. An analyst based in Africa would also give Corpus a presence on the continent.

**Credits.** Bill would accept Anthropic API credits if no strings are attached; credits would reduce the cash ask. Exa offered only a small one-off gesture, so it stays a cash line.

## The case

- **Who it is for.** African government officials, and analysts working with African governments and regional organisations.
- **What it is.** A research assistant that makes them more productive. It does not take decisions for them.
- **The line to lead with.** You can't afford not to take this approach. Every country and topic gets a nightly, sourced and dated evidence base, at a running cost of under USD 900 a month.
- **Scale today.** Reports on all 54 African countries, five regions and a topic taxonomy, measured against 123 indicators. A finance dataset of non-state commitments since 2015. Over 21,000 primary documents catalogued. Everything published under CC BY 4.0, with the code under MIT.
- **Status.** An application to be listed as a Digital Public Good has been submitted and is pending.
- **Track record.** Bill's portfolio of past work in the field (data-landscapers.io/portfolio). Close colleagues in a number of African countries, though no formal partners.

## Evidence of use

Daily analytics have been kept since 29 September 2026, covering 49 days with some gaps.

- **October 2026.** 964 page views. Reports viewed covered 59 country and region codes.
- **1 to 16 November 2026.** 526 page views. The best day so far was 16 November, with 36 users and 56 pages.
- **Most viewed.** The data-centres dataset, the Corpus home page, the maturity pages and the finance pages.

The users figure is a sum of daily users, not a count of unique people, so it must not be quoted as unique users. The numbers are early and small. Lead with endorsements; describe the analytics as early, growing use, give the months they cover, and update them before each submission.

**Endorsements available.** A think-tank chief executive, researchers and a consultant. None of them yet has a record of Corpus changing a decision, so this case is about adoption, not proven outcomes.

## Approach

- **Introducers.** The chief executive of an INGO, a consultant who works closely with the African Union, and a well-known academic and consultant in global digital transformation.
- **First prospects.** Mozilla, IDRC and Co-Develop. None has been contacted yet.
- **Excluded.** Funders Bill has criticised in public, including Gates.
- **Deadline.** Money in place by mid-2027.
- **Outputs.**
  1. A prospect tracker: fit, eligibility, typical grant size, deadlines and route in.
  2. A one-page concept note, written so friends can forward it as it is.
  3. Full proposals, tailored to each funder.
